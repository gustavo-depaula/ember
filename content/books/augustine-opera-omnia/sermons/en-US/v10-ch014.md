# Sermon 348/A: On the Pelagian Heresy, from a Sermon to the People

**1.** Those two petitions: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^1], and: *Lead us not into temptation*[^2]—when they are put to the Pelagians, what do you think they answer? I shuddered, my brothers, when I heard it. I indeed did not hear it with my own ears, but my holy brother and fellow bishop, our Urbanus, who was a presbyter here and is now bishop of Sicca, when he had returned from the city of Rome and had there contended with a certain man holding such views, or related that he had contended, when he was being pressed by the weight of the Lord's Prayer—for he was pressing him and saying—what do you think he answered?

**2.** You see, brothers, what a malign heresy. You see how you all shudder. Beware lest you be captured. For I know the cleverness and the evasions of impious men turned away from the truth, and, because they have already fallen into their own opinions, unwilling to be conquered; see, I beseech you. For behold, he found something to say, that we say *Lead us not into temptation* for this reason, lest something befall us which we do not have in our power, according to the temptation of the body. Was it for this, then, that the Lord said: *Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation*[^3]? Did he say this: *Watch and pray*, lest you break your foot, or lest your head ache, or lest you run into some loss? He was not saying this. But what was he saying? What he said to Peter: *I have prayed for you, that your faith may not fail*[^4]. *I have prayed*, he says, *for you*; God says to a man, the Lord to a servant, the master to a disciple, the physician to the sick: *I have prayed for you*. That what may not fail? What? your hand, your foot, your eye, your tongue, some paralysis, that is, a dissolution of the members? No, but *that your faith may not fail*. According to these men, we have it in our power that our faith may not fail.

**3.** Why is God asked for us, that he may grant us to ask of the eternal majesty what these men say we ought not to ask, but to have in our power? Our blessings, my brothers, our blessings which we make over you, they empty out, they drain, they shatter. You have heard me, I believe, my brothers, when I say: Turned to the Lord, let us bless his name, may he grant us to persevere in his commandments, to walk in the right way of his instruction, to please him in every good work, and other such things. Altogether, they say, this whole thing is established in our power. Then we wish such things for you in vain. Let us defend ourselves and you, lest both we bless without cause and you subscribe Amen without cause. My brothers, your Amen is your subscription, it is your consent, it is your assent. Lest perhaps some of them condemn both us and you, let us defend ourselves from the apostle Paul: let us see whether he wished such things for his people—hear what he said—as we pray over you. Hear what he said in a certain place. I say a brief thing. What do you say, O new heretic, whoever you are who hear me, if you are present? What do you say? That we have it so in our power not to sin, that we can fulfill this without the help of divine grace? Do you say this? Then we have it in our power not to sin without the help of God? What is it, then, that the Apostle says, writing to the Corinthians? *We pray to God, that you may do no evil*[^5]. You have attended, you have heard, you have received; and because it is most manifest, without doubt you have understood what the Apostle prayed. *We pray*, he says, *to God, that you may do no evil*. He could have said: We admonish you, that you may do no evil; we teach you, that you may do no evil; we command you, we charge you. Which indeed, if he said it, he would say something certain, because our will too does something; for our will does not do nothing. It alone does not suffice. Yet he preferred to say: *We pray*, that he might commend grace itself, that they might understand that, when they do no evil, it is not by their own will alone that they avoid evil, but by the help of God that they fulfill what is commanded.

**4.** Therefore, brothers, when something is commanded, recognize the choice of the will; when what is commanded is prayed for, recognize the benefit of grace. For you have both in the Scriptures: both it is commanded and it is prayed for. What is commanded, this is prayed for. See what I say. It is commanded that we understand. How is it commanded that we understand? *Do not be like the horse and the mule, which have no understanding*[^6]. You have heard that it is commanded; ask that you may be able to fulfill what is commanded. You say: How? Hear Scripture. What is commanded you? *Do not be like the horse and the mule, which have no understanding*. Because it is commanded, you have recognized the will. Hear that it is prayed for, that you may recognize grace: *Give me understanding, that I may learn your commandments*[^7]. It is commanded that we have wisdom: because it is commanded, I read it. Hear: *You who are unwise among the people, and foolish, be at some time wise*[^8]. Now what does that one say? You see how God commands us to be wise. Then is wisdom in our power? I have already said, I have heard the precept, I have recognized the will. Hear the prayer, that you may be able to recognize grace. It is about wisdom, which is commanded us. Let us hear what the apostle James says: *But if any of you lacks wisdom, let him ask of God, who gives to all abundantly*[^9]. Continence is commanded us. Where is it commanded? The Apostle to Timothy: *Keep yourself continent*[^10]. It is a command, it is a precept; it must be heard, it must be done. But unless God helps, we are left behind. We try indeed to do it by will, and the will strives at something; let not the power presume, unless the weakness be helped. For surely it is commanded: *Keep yourself continent*. Hear another place of Scripture: *And when I knew*, he says, *that no one can be continent unless God grants it, and this very thing was a matter of wisdom, to know whose gift this was*. And what, he says, did I do? *I approached the Lord and besought him*[^11]. What need is there to run through many things, my brothers? Whatever is commanded us, it must be prayed that it be fulfilled: but not so that we let ourselves go and lie on our backs like sick men and say: Let God rain food upon our faces, so that we wish to do nothing at all; and when the food has rained upon our mouth, let us say: Let God even swallow for us. We too ought to do something. We ought to be eager, we ought to try, and to give thanks in that wherein we have been able, to pray in that wherein we have not been able. When you give thanks, you take heed lest you be condemned as ungrateful; but when you ask for what you do not yet have, you take heed lest you remain empty, because you are hindered.

**5.** Consider these things, then, my brothers: whoever approaches you and says to you, "What, then, do we do? We have nothing in our power, unless God gives all. Then God will not crown us, but will crown himself"—you now see that he comes from that vein. It is a vein, but it has poison; for it has been struck by the serpent, it is not healthy. For this Satan does today, how he may cast out of the Church by the poisons of heretics, just as then by the poisons of the serpent he cast out of paradise. Let no one say that that man was absolved by the bishops. It is absolved, but the confession, as a kind of correction, is itself absolved, because the things he said before the bishops seemed catholic, but what he wrote in his books the bishops who absolved did not know. And perhaps he has been corrected. For we ought not to despair of a man, who perhaps preferred to be joined to the catholic faith and fled to its grace and help. Perhaps this has happened. Yet it is not the heresy that has been absolved, but a man denying the heresy.
