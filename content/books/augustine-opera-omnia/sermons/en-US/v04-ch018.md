# Sermon 108

**1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ both came to men, and departed from men, and is going to come to men. And yet he was here when he came, nor did he withdraw when he departed, and he is going to come to those to whom he said: *Behold, I am with you even to the consummation of the age* [^1]. According to the form of a servant, then, which he took for us, at a certain time he was born, and was killed, and rose again, and *now dies no more, nor will death any longer have dominion over him* [^2]: but according to his divinity, by which he is equal to the Father, *he was in this world, and the world was made through him, and the world did not know him* [^3]. About this you have just now heard the Gospel, what it admonished us, making us cautious, and wanting us to be unencumbered and ready to await the last things: so that after the last things, which in this world are to be feared, there may follow a rest which has no end. Blessed are they who shall have been made partakers of it. But then they will be secure, who now are not secure: and again then they will fear, who now refuse to fear. For this expectation and on account of this hope we have been made Christians. Is not our hope away from this world? Let us not love the world. From the love of this world we have been called, that we may hope for and love another world. In this we ought to abstain from all unlawful desires, that is, we ought to have our loins girded: and to glow and shine in good works, that is, to have our lamps burning. For the Lord himself said to his disciples in another place of the Gospel: *No one lights a lamp and puts it under a bushel, but on a lampstand, that it may give light to all who are in the house* [^4]. And to show whence he was speaking, he added and said: *So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good deeds, and glorify your Father who is in heaven* [^5].

**2.** Therefore he wanted our loins to be girded, and our lamps burning. What is, the loins girded? *Turn away from evil* [^6]. What is to shine? what is to have lamps burning? This is: *And do good* [^7]. What is it that he added and said: *And you, be like men awaiting their lord when he comes from the wedding* [^8]: except what follows in that psalm: *Seek peace, and follow it* [^9]? These three—that is, abstinence from evil, and the doing of good, and the hope of the eternal reward—are recalled in the Acts of the Apostles, where it is written that Paul taught them *about self-control, about justice, and the hope of eternal life* [^10]. To self-control pertains: *Let your loins be girded* [^11]. To justice pertains: *And lamps burning* [^12]. To the expectation of the Lord pertains the hope of eternal life. So then: *Turn away from evil*, this is self-control, these are the loins girded: *And do good*, this is justice, these are the burning lamps: *Seek peace and follow it*, this is the expectation of the age to come: therefore: *Be like men awaiting their lord when he comes from the wedding*.

**3.** But having these precepts and promises, why do we seek on earth good days [^13], where we cannot find them? For I know that you seek them, when either you are sick, or you are in tribulations, which abound in this world. Since when one's age sinks toward its end, even an old man is full of complaints, and of no joys. Among all the tribulations by which the human race is worn down, men seek nothing but good days, and they want a long life, which here they cannot have. For even a long human life is constrained by such brevity, compared to the breadth of that whole age, as a single drop compared to the whole sea. What, then, is a man's life, even that which is called long? They call a life long which in this world is short: and, as I said, groans abound even to decrepit old age. This whole is little and brief: and yet how it is sought by men? With how much diligence, how much labor, how much care, how much watchfulness, how much effort do men seek to live here long and grow old? But this very living long, what is it, except to run to the end? You had yesterday, you want to have tomorrow too. But when this and tomorrow have passed, you have less of it. Therefore you wish the day to dawn, that there may approach you the day to which you do not want to come. You hold some feast for your friends, you hear there well-wishers say to you, May you live many years: you want what they said to come. What? That years and years may come, and you do not want the end of the years to come? Your zeals are contrary: you want to walk, and you do not want to arrive.

**4.** Nevertheless, as I said, if so great care is in men, that with daily, great, and perpetual labors they desire to die later; with how great care must we act, that we may never die? About that no one wants to think. Daily good days are sought in this world, where they are not found: and no one wants to live so as to come there where they are found. Therefore the same Scripture admonishes, and says: *What man is there who wants life, and loves to see good days* [^14]? Scripture so questioned, as though to know what would be answered it: knowing that all men would seek life, and good days. For their desire it questioned, as though it were answered from the heart of all, I want it: so it said: *What man is there who wants life, and loves to see good days?* Just as even at this hour, in which I speak to you, when you heard me say: *What man is there who wants life, and loves to see good days?* you all answered in your heart, I do. For I too who speak with you want life and good days: what you seek, this I too seek.

**5.** Just as, if gold were necessary to us all, and I, wanting to come to the gold along with you, and it were somewhere in your field, in a place subject to your power, and I saw you seeking gold, and said to you, What do you seek? you would answer me, Gold. And I would say to you, You seek gold, and I seek gold: what you seek, I too seek; but you do not seek it where we can find it. So then hear me, where we can find it: I take nothing from you, I show you the place: rather let us all follow him who knows where what we seek is. So now too, because you desire life and good days, we cannot say to you, Do not desire life and good days: but we say this, Do not here seek in this world life and good days, where they cannot be good. Is not life itself like death? But the days themselves hasten and pass; because today excluded yesterday: tomorrow is born for this, to exclude today. The days themselves do not stand still: why do you want to stand still with them? Therefore your desire, by which you want life and good days, I not only do not repress, but even more vehemently kindle. Indeed seek life, seek good days: but let them be sought where they can be found.

**6.** For do you want to hear with me the counsel of him who knows where good days are, and where life is? Hear it not from me, but with me together. For a certain one says to us: *Come, sons, hear me* [^15]. And let us run together, and stand, and prick up our ears, and understand with the heart the Father who said: *Come, sons, hear me; the fear*, he says, *of the Lord I will teach you* [^16]. And what thing he wants to teach, and for what thing the fear of the Lord is useful, follows: *What man is there who wants life, and loves to see good days* [^17]? We all answer, We want it. Let us hear what follows. *Restrain your tongue from evil, and let your lips not speak guile* [^18]. Now say, I do. Just now when I said: *What man is there who wants life, and loves to see good days?* we all answered, I do. Come, let someone answer me, I do. Then: *Restrain your tongue from evil, and let your lips not speak guile*. Now say, I do. So you want good days and life; do you not want to restrain your tongue from evil, and your lips not to speak guile? Eager for the wage, sluggish for the work. And to whom not working is the wage paid? Would that in your own house you would pay the wage even to one working. For I know that to one not working you do not pay. Why? Because to one doing nothing you owe nothing. And God has set forth a wage. What wage? *Life and good days*, which we all desire, and to which days we all strive to come. He is going to give the promised wage. What wage? *Life and good days*. And which are the good days? Life without end, rest without labor.

**7.** He has set forth a great wage: with so great a wage set forth, let us see what he has commanded. For now, kindled by the wage of so great a promise and by love of the wage, let us prepare our strength, our sides, our arms for his command. As though he were going to command us to carry great burdens, perhaps to dig something, perhaps to raise some engine? He has not commanded you a laborious thing, but the member which among all the members you move quickly, this he has charged you to restrain: *Restrain your tongue from evil*. It is no labor to raise a structure; and is it labor to hold the tongue? *Restrain your tongue from evil*. Do not speak a lie, do not speak accusations, do not speak calumnies, do not speak false witness, do not speak blasphemies: *Restrain your tongue from evil*. See how you are angry, if anyone speaks evil of you. As you are angry at another, when he speaks evil of you: so be angry at yourself, when you speak evil of another. *Let your lips not speak guile*. What is within in your heart, let this be said outside. Let not one thing cover the breast, and the tongue utter another. *Turn away from evil, and do good*. For how shall I say to him, Clothe the naked, who still wants to strip the clothed? For he who oppresses his fellow citizen, how does he receive the pilgrim? Therefore in order, first *turn away from evil, and do good*: first gird your loins, and then light the lamp. And when you have done this, securely await *life and good days. Seek peace, and follow it* [^19]: and then with a good face you will say to the Lord: I have done what you commanded, repay what you promised.
