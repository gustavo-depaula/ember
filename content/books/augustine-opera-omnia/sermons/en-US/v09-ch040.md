# Sermon 330: On the Feast of the Martyrs

**1.** The solemnity of the blessed Martyrs, and the expectation of Your Holiness, demands a sermon from us. For we understand that we ought to discourse on what is suited to this day. This you want, this we want: may he do it in whose hand are both we and our sermons; may he grant the ability, who bestowed the will. For in this the martyrs blazed: kindled with the love of invisible things, they despised the visible. What did he love in himself, who despised even himself, lest he lose himself? For they were temples of God, and they felt the true God dwelling in them; therefore they did not worship false gods. They had heard, had drunk in thirstily, and had handed over to the inmost marrow of the heart, and in a way had made part of their own flesh, what the Lord said: *If anyone wants to come after me, let him deny himself. Let him deny himself,* he says, *and take up his cross, and follow me*[^1]. From this I want to say something, and your attention terrifies me, the duty commands.

**2.** What is it, I ask you: *If anyone wants to come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross, and follow me?*[^2] We understand what is: *Let him take up his cross;* let him sustain his tribulation: for *take up* is to bear, to endure. Let him patiently, he says, receive all that he suffers for my sake. *And follow me.* Where? Where we know that he went after the resurrection. For he ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father. There he has set us too. Meanwhile let hope go before, that the reality may follow. How hope ought to go before, those know who hear, Lift up your heart. But it remains to seek, as much as the Lord helps, and to examine, and, with him opening, to enter, and, with him granting, to find, and to bring forth to you what we have been able to find: what it is that he says: *Let him deny himself.* How does he deny himself who loves himself? So indeed reason has it, but human reason: a man says to me, How does he deny himself who loves himself? But God says to a man, Let him deny himself, if he loves himself. For by loving himself, he loses himself: by denying himself, he finds himself. *He who loves his soul,* he says, *will lose it*[^3]. He commanded who knows what he commands, because he knows how to counsel who knows how to instruct, and he knows how to restore who deigned to create. *He who loves, let him lose.* It is a sad thing to lose what you love. But sometimes even the farmer loses what he sows. He brings it out, scatters it, casts it away, buries it. Why do you marvel? This despiser and loser is a greedy reaper. What was done, winter and summer proved; the joy of the reaper showed you the counsel of the sower. So *he who loves his soul will lose it.* Let him who seeks fruit in it sow it. This, then, is, let him deny himself, lest by perversely loving it he lose himself.

**3.** For there is no one who does not love himself; but the right love is to be sought, the perverse to be guarded against. For whoever, having forsaken God, loves himself, and has forsaken God by loving himself, remains not even in himself, but goes out even from himself. He goes out an exile from his own breast, by despising the inner things, by loving the outer. What did I say? All who do evil things, do they not despise their own conscience? But he sets a limit to his iniquity, whoever blushes at his conscience. So because he despised God in order to love himself, by loving outside what he himself is not, he despised even himself. See, hear the Apostle bearing witness to this thought: *In the last times,* he says, *dangerous times will be at hand*[^4]. What are the dangerous times? *Men will be lovers of themselves*[^5]. This is the head of the evil. Let us see, then, whether they remain even in themselves, by loving themselves; let us see, let us hear what follows: *Men will be,* he says, *lovers of themselves, lovers of money*[^6]. Where are you who loved yourself? Surely you are outside. I ask you, are you money? Surely you who, neglecting God, loved yourself, by loving money deserted even yourself. First you deserted yourself, afterward you lost yourself. For the love of money made you lose yourself. You lie for the sake of money: *The mouth that lies kills the soul*[^7]. Behold, when you seek money, you have lost your soul. Bring out the balance of truth, not of greed: bring out the scale, but of truth, not of greed; bring it out, I beg you, and place on one side money, on the other the soul. Now you weigh, and out of greed you apply fraudulent fingers; you want the side that has the money to sink. Place it, do not weigh it: you want to commit fraud against yourself; I see what you are doing. You want to prefer money to your soul; to lie for its sake, to lose the soul. Place it, let God weigh; let him who knows not how to be deceived, nor to deceive, himself weigh. Behold, he himself weighs; see him weighing, hear him reporting: *What does it profit a man, if he gains the whole world?*[^8] It is the divine voice, the voice of one weighing, and not deceiving; reporting, admonishing. You were placing money on one side, and on the other the soul; see where you placed the money. What does he who weighs answer? You placed the money: *What does it profit a man, if he gains the whole world, but suffers the loss of his soul?*[^9] But you wanted to weigh the soul against gain: weigh it against the world. You wanted to lose it, that earth might be acquired by you: this has more weight than heaven and earth. But you do this, because by forsaking God, and loving yourself, you have gone out even from yourself; and other things now, which are outside, you value more than yourself. Return to yourself: but again, having returned to yourself, turned upward, do not remain in yourself. First from the things that are outside return to yourself, and then give yourself back to him who made you, and sought you lost, and found you a fugitive, and turned you, turned away, back to himself. Return, then, to yourself, and go to him who made you. Imitate that younger son; for perhaps you are he. I speak to the people, not to one man; although all can hear me, I speak not to one, but to the human race. Return, then, be that younger son, who, living on his substance prodigally dispersed and lost, was in need, fed swine, wearied with hunger took breath, recalled to memory his father. And what does the Gospel say of him? *And returning to himself*[^10]. He who had forsaken even himself, returning to himself, let us see whether he remained in himself. *Returning to himself, he said: I will arise.* So he had fallen. *I will arise,* he says, *and will go to my father*[^11]. Behold, now he denies himself, who has found himself. How does he deny himself? Hear: *And I will say to him, I have sinned,* he says, *against heaven, and before you.* He denies himself. *I am no longer worthy to be called your son*[^12]. Behold what the holy martyrs did. They despised the things that were outside; all the enticements of this world, all errors and terrors, whatever pleased, whatever terrified, they despised it all, they trampled it all. They came even to themselves, and attended to themselves; they found themselves in themselves, they displeased themselves: they ran to him by whom they might be formed, in whom they might live again, in whom they might remain, in whom might perish what they themselves had begun to be by their own selves, and might remain what he himself had founded in them. This is to deny oneself.

**4.** This the apostle Peter could not yet grasp, when, to our Lord Jesus Christ foretelling his passion, he said: *Far be it from you, Lord, this shall not happen*[^13]. He feared lest the Life should die. Just now, when the holy Gospel was read, you heard what blessed Peter answered the Savior foretelling his passion for us, and in a way promising it. The captive was contradicting the Redeemer. What are you doing, Apostle? how do you contradict? how do you say: *This shall not happen?* So the Lord is not to suffer? The word of the cross is a stumbling block to you: to those perishing it is folly. You are to be redeemed, and you contradict the merchant? Let him suffer: he knows what he is doing, he knows why he has come, he knows how he seeks you, he knows how he finds you. Do not teach your master; seek from his side your price. You rather hear the one correcting: do not you want to correct; it is perverse, it is backward. Hear what he says: *Get behind me*[^14]. And because he said it, I say it; I will not be silent about the word of the Lord, nor do I do insult to the Apostle. The Lord Christ says: *Get behind me, Satan.* Why Satan? Because you want to go before me. Do you not want to be Satan? Get behind me. For if you go behind me, you follow me: if you follow me, you will take up your cross, and you will not be my counselor, but my disciple. For why were you frightened, when the Lord foretold his death? why were you frightened, except that you too feared to die? By fearing to die, you did not deny yourself; by wrongly loving yourself, you denied him. But afterward the blessed apostle Peter, after he had thrice denied the Lord, blotted out that fault by weeping: strengthened by the Lord rising, built up, he died for him whom, fearing to die, he had denied; by confessing he found death, but by finding death he laid hold of life. And behold, now Peter does not die; all fear has passed away, no tears for him any longer, all things have withdrawn, he remains blessed with Christ. For he trampled all the enticements outside, the threats and terrors: he denied himself, took up his cross, and followed the Lord. Hear too the apostle Paul denying himself: *Far be it from me,* he says, *to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world is crucified to me, and I to the world*[^15]. Hear him still denying himself: *I live,* he says, *now not I.* An open denial of himself: but now follows a glorious confession of Christ, *but Christ lives in me*[^16]. What is it, then, Deny yourself? Do not you yourself live in yourself. What is, Do not you yourself live in yourself? Do not do your own will, but his who dwells in you.
