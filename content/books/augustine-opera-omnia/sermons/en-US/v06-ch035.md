# Sermon 213: On the Handing Over of the Creed

**1.** The Apostle says: *It will be that everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved*[^1]. To this salvation you run, you who have given your names for baptism; salvation, not of a short time, but of an eternal one; salvation, which is not common to us and the beasts, nor to good and evil men. For you see, and it is certain to us all, that the salvation of the present time, for obtaining or repairing which men labor much, not only men have, but also beasts, great and small: from dragons and elephants to flies and little worms this salvation extends. Then men themselves have this salvation, both those who call upon God and those who blaspheme God; whence the holy Psalm says: *Men and beasts you will save, Lord, since your mercy is multiplied, O God; but the sons of men will hope under the covering of your wings*[^2]. This salvation, then, through the manifold mercy of God, reaches even to the lowest animals: *But the sons of men*, who pertain to the Son of man, *will hope under the covering of your wings*. This we do in this life: now we hope for what we may afterward receive. And what does the Psalm itself promise? *They will be made drunk with the abundance of your house, and you will give them to drink of the torrent of your delight: for with you is the fountain of life*[^3]. The fountain of life is Christ: that we might now taste something of him, he was made man; but his abundance, which satisfies the angels and all the heavenly ministries, is kept for us. But this is for afterward: but now, that we may be able to come to it, let us call upon God that we may be saved, according to the Apostle who said: *Everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved*[^4]. The Prophet had said this before[^5]: but Paul the apostle said that the time had now passed when there should be fulfilled what is written: *Everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord will be saved*. I have already said by what salvation, lest perhaps you now say: Why are even those who call upon the name of the Lord not saved? He will be saved. Then the Apostle himself added: *But how will they call upon him in whom they have not believed? or how do they believe him whom they have not heard? But how will they hear without a preacher? Or how will they preach, unless they be sent? As it is written: How beautiful are the feet of those who announce peace, who announce good things!*[^6] No one, then, can be saved who has not called upon him: no one can call upon him unless he has first believed. Because, then, this is the order, that you first believe, afterward call upon him, today you receive the Creed of faith, in which you may believe; but after eight days the Prayer, in which you may call upon him.

**2.** The Creed, then, is the rule of faith briefly comprised, to instruct the mind, and not to burden the memory; let it be said in few words, whence much may be acquired. The Creed (Symbolum), then, it is called, in which Christians recognize one another: this is what I will first briefly pronounce to you. Then, as much as the Lord deigns to grant, I will open it to you; that what I want you to hold, you may also be able to understand. This is the Creed. And after the Creed: It is not much, and it is much; there is no need for you to count the words, but to weigh them. *I believe in God the Father almighty*. See how quickly it is said, and how much it is worth. He is God, and he is Father: God by power, Father by goodness. How happy are we, who have found God our Father! Let us believe, then, in him, and promise ourselves all things from his mercy, because he is almighty: therefore we believe in God the Father almighty. Let no one say: He cannot forgive me my sins. How can the almighty not do so? But you say: I have sinned much. And I say: But he is almighty. And you: I have committed such sins, from which I cannot be freed and cleansed. I answer: But he is almighty. See what you sing to him in the Psalm: *Bless*, he says, *my soul, the Lord, and do not forget all his recompenses, who is propitious to all your iniquities, who heals all your diseases*[^7]. For this his omnipotence is necessary to us. For to the whole creation it was necessary for this, that it might be created: he is almighty to make greater and lesser things, almighty for heavenly and earthly things, almighty to make immortal and mortal things, almighty to make spiritual and corporeal things, almighty to make visible and invisible things, great in great things, and not small in the least; finally, almighty to make all things that he willed to make. For I tell how many things he cannot do: he cannot die, he cannot sin, he cannot lie, he cannot be deceived; so many things he cannot do, which, if he could, he would not be almighty. Believe, then, in him, and confess: *For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation*[^8]. Therefore, when you have believed, it behooves you to confess, when you give back the Creed. Receive now what you may hold, and afterward give back, and never forget.

**3.** After this, what? *And in Jesus Christ.* *I believe*, you say, *in God the Father almighty, and in Jesus Christ his only Son, our Lord.* If the only Son, then equal to the Father: if the only Son, then of the same substance as the Father: if the only Son, then of the same omnipotence as the Father: if the only Son, then coeternal with the Father. This in himself, and with himself, and with the Father. For our sake, what? to us, what? *Who was born of the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary.* Behold by what he came, who, to whom: through the virgin Mary, in whom the Holy Spirit worked, not a man, a husband; who made the chaste one fruitful, and kept her intact. So, then, the Lord Christ was clothed with flesh, so he who made man was made man: by taking up what he was not, not by losing what he was. *For the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^9]. The Word was not turned into flesh, but the Word, remaining, by taking flesh, ever invisible, was made visible when he willed, and dwelt among us. What is, among us? Among men, made one of the number of men: one and only, the only Son to the Father. To us, what? To us too the only Savior, for no one besides him is our Savior; and to us the only Redeemer, for no one besides him is our Redeemer: not with gold, not with silver, but with his own blood.

**4.** Therefore let us look at those transactions of his by which we were bought. For when it had been said in the Creed: *Born of the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary*, now what did he suffer for us? It follows: *Under Pontius Pilate crucified and buried.* What? The only Son of God our Lord crucified? The only Son of God our Lord buried? A man was crucified: God was not changed, God was not slain; and yet according to the man he was slain: *For if they had known*, says the Apostle, *they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*[^10]. He both showed him to be the Lord of glory, and confessed him crucified: because if someone tears even your tunic, your flesh being unharmed, he does you an injury, nor do you so cry out for your garment as to say: You have torn my tunic, but: You have torn me, you have rent me, you have made threads of me. You speak these things whole, and you speak the truth, and he who injured you took nothing from your flesh. So too the Lord Christ was crucified. He is the Lord, he is the only Son to the Father, he is our Savior, he is the Lord of glory: yet he was crucified, but in the flesh, and buried in the flesh alone. For where he was buried, and when he was buried, then and there there was no soul, the flesh alone lay in the tomb; and yet you confess *Jesus Christ his only Son our Lord, who was born of the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary.* Who? Jesus Christ, the only Son of God, our Lord. *Under Pontius Pilate crucified.* Who? Jesus Christ, the only Son of God, our Lord. *And buried.* Who? Jesus Christ, the only Son of God, our Lord. The flesh alone lies, and you say: our Lord? I say it, plainly I say it: because I behold the garment, and I adore the one clothed. That flesh was his garment; because: *Though he was in the form of God, he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*, not losing the form of God, *made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man*[^11].

**5.** Let us not despise the flesh alone: when it lay, then it bought us. Why did it buy us? Because it did not always lie: *For on the third day he rose from the dead.* This follows in the Creed; when we have confessed his passion, we confess also his resurrection. In the passion what did he do? he taught what we are to endure. In the resurrection what did he do? he showed what we are to hope for. Here the work, there the reward: the work in the passion, the reward in the resurrection. Nor, because he rose from the dead, did he remain here: but what follows? *He ascended into heaven.* And where is he now? *He sits at the right hand of the Father.* Understand the right hand, lest you seek there a left: the right hand of God means eternal felicity; the right hand of God means ineffable, inestimable, incomprehensible blessedness and prosperity. This is the right hand of God, there he sits. What is, There he sits? There he dwells: for "seats" are spoken of where each one dwells. For when holy Stephen saw him, he was not lying who said: *He sits at the right hand of the Father*. For how does Stephen say it? *Behold, I see heaven opened, and the Son of man standing at the right hand of God*[^12]. Because the one saw him standing, was the other perhaps lying who said that he then *sits at the right hand of the Father*? He sits, then, it is said: he remains, he dwells. How? as you do. In what posture? Who will say? Let us say what he taught, let us say what we know.

**6.** What? *Thence he will come to judge the living and the dead.* Let us confess the Savior, that we may not fear the Judge: for he who now believes in him, and does his precepts, and loves him, will not fear when he comes to judge the living and the dead; not only will he not fear, but he will wish him to come. For what is happier for us than when he comes whom we desire, when he comes whom we love? But let us fear, because he will be our judge: he himself will then be our judge, who now is our advocate. Hear John: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us; but if we confess our sins*, he says, *he is faithful and just, who will forgive us our sins, and cleanse us from all iniquity. These things I have written to you, that you may not sin: and if anyone has sinned, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous; and he is the propitiation for our sins*[^13]. If you had a case to plead before some judge, and you instructed an advocate, were taken up by the advocate, he pleaded your case as he could; and if he had not finished it, and you heard that that judge was to come, how much would you rejoice, because he could be your judge, who was a little before your advocate? And now he himself prays for us, he himself intercedes for us[^14]; we have him as advocate, and do we fear him as judge? Rather, because we have sent ahead our advocate, let us securely hope for the judge to come.

**7.** What pertains to Jesus Christ, the only Son of God, our Lord, has been passed over in the Creed; there follows: *And in the Holy Spirit*, that the Trinity may be completed, Father and Son and Holy Spirit. But of the Son much has been said, because the Son took up man: the Son, the Word, was made flesh, not the Father, not the Holy Spirit. But the flesh of the Son the whole Trinity made: for the works of the Trinity are inseparable. So, then, receive the Holy Spirit so as not to believe him less than the Son, or less than the Father. For the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, the whole Trinity, is one God: nothing there differs, nothing is varying, nothing defective, nothing contrary to another; always equal, invisible and unchangeable, Father and Son and Holy Spirit. May the Trinity free us from the multitude of sins.

**8.** Now what follows pertains to us. *In the holy Church.* The holy Church is we: but I did not so say "we," as though, behold, those of us who are here, who have just now heard me. As many of us as are here, by the favor of God faithful Christians in this church, that is, in this city, as many as are in this region, as many as are in this province, as many as are also across the sea, as many as are in the whole world; since from the rising of the sun to its setting the name of the Lord is praised[^15]; so does the catholic Church stand, our true mother, the true spouse of that Bridegroom. Let us honor her, because she is the matron of so great a Lord; and what shall I say? Great is the Bridegroom's singular condescension: he found a harlot, he made her a virgin. That she was a harlot she ought not to deny, lest she forget the mercy of the one who freed her. How was she not a harlot, when she fornicated after idols and demons? Fornication of the heart was in all: in few of the flesh, in all of the heart. And he came, and made her a virgin: he made the Church a virgin. In faith she is a virgin: in the flesh she has few virgins, consecrated ones; in faith she ought to have all virgins, both women and men; for there ought to be chastity and purity and holiness. For do you wish to know how she is a virgin? Hear the apostle Paul, hear the friend of the bridegroom, zealous for the bridegroom, not for himself. *I have betrothed you*, he says, *to one husband.* He was speaking to the Church: and to what Church? To wherever those letters could reach: *I have betrothed you, to present you to one husband as a chaste virgin to Christ. But I fear*, he says, *lest, as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, so your minds be corrupted from the chastity which is in Christ*[^16]. Where you fear to be corrupted, there you are a virgin. *I fear*, he said, *lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his cunning.* Did that serpent lie bodily with Eve? And yet he extinguished the virginity of her heart. *This I fear*, he said, *lest your minds be corrupted from the chastity which is in Christ.* The Church, then, is a virgin. She is a virgin, let her be a virgin: let her beware of the seducer, lest she find a corrupter. The Church is a virgin. You will perhaps say to me: If she is a virgin, how does she bear children? or if she does not bear children, how did we give our names, that we might be born of her bowels? I answer: She is both a virgin, and she bears; she imitates Mary, who bore the Lord. Did not holy Mary both bear and remain a virgin? So the Church both bears and is a virgin; and, if you consider, she bears Christ: because those who are baptized are his members. *You are*, says the Apostle, *the body of Christ and members*[^17]. If, then, she bears the members of Christ, she is most like Mary.

**9.** Unto the forgiveness of sins. If this were not in the Church, there would be no hope: if the forgiveness of sins were not in the Church, there would be no hope of a future life and of eternal liberation. We give thanks to God, who gave this gift to his Church. Behold, you are about to come to the holy font: you will be washed by the saving baptism, you will be renewed by the laver of regeneration; you will be without any sin, coming up from that laver. All things that pursued you in the past will there be blotted out. Your sins will be like the Egyptians pursuing the Israelites; pursuing, but only as far as the Red Sea[^18]. What is, as far as the Red Sea? As far as the font consecrated by the cross and blood of Christ. For what is red, is ruddy. Do you not see how the part of Christ is ruddy? Ask the eyes of faith: if you see the cross, attend also to the blood: if you see what hangs, attend to what it shed. The side of Christ was pierced by a lance, and our price flowed out[^19]. Therefore baptism is signed with the sign of Christ, that is, the water in which you are dipped, and you pass through as it were in the Red Sea. Your sins are your enemies: they follow, but only as far as the sea. When you have entered, you will escape, they will be blotted out; as, the Israelites escaping over the dry ground, the water covered the Egyptians. And what did the Scripture say? *Not one of them remained*[^20]. You have sinned many things, you have sinned few; you have sinned great things, you have sinned small; what does it matter, when *not one of them remained*? But because we are going to live in this age, where no one lives without sin, therefore the forgiveness of sins is not in the washing of holy baptism alone, but also in the Lord's and daily Prayer, which after eight days you are going to receive. In it you will find, as it were, your daily baptism; that you may give thanks to God, who gave this gift to his Church, which we confess in the Creed; so that, when we have said: *The holy Church*, we may add: *the forgiveness of sins.*

**10.** After these, *the resurrection of the flesh.* This is now the end: but the resurrection of the flesh will be an end without end; but afterward there will be no death of the flesh, no pain of the flesh, no distresses of the flesh, no hunger and thirst of the flesh, no afflictions of the flesh, no old age and weariness of the flesh. Do not, then, shudder at the resurrection of the flesh: see its goods, forget its evils. Absolutely, whatever there is of fleshly complaints now, then there will not be there: we shall be eternal, equal to the angels of God[^21], we shall have one city with the holy angels. We shall be possessed by the Lord, we shall be his inheritance, and he himself will be our inheritance; since we now say to him: *Lord, the portion of my inheritance*[^22], and of us it was said to his Son: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance*[^23]. We shall possess, and be possessed: we shall hold, and be held. What shall I say? we till, and are tilled; but we till as God; we are tilled as a field. That you may know that we are tilled, hear the Lord: *I am the true vine, you are the branches, my Father is the husbandman*[^24]. If he is called a husbandman, he tills a field. What field? He tills us. And the husbandman of this visible earth can plow, can dig, can plant, can water, if he finds water, can he rain? can the husbandman give increase, bring forth the shoot, fix the root in the earth, push it up into the air, add strength to the branches, load it with fruits, adorn it with leaves? Yet our husbandman, God the Father, can do all these things in us. Why? Because we believe *in God the Father almighty.* Therefore hold what we have both set before you, and, as God has deigned to give, have expounded.

**11.** In eight days you are to give back what today you have received. Let your parents, who receive you, teach you, that you may be found ready, and how you should keep watch at cockcrow, at the prayers which you celebrate here. Now the Creed itself begins to be offered you here, that you may diligently hold it: let no one tremble, let no one, by trembling, fail to give it back. Be secure, we are your fathers, we do not have the canes and rods of the grammarians. If anyone errs in a word, let him not err in the faith.
