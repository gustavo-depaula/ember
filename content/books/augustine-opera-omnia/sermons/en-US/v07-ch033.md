# Sermon 258: Another Sermon Which He Gave at the Greater Basilica on the Same Day

**1.** What we have sung to God: *This is the day which the Lord has made*[^1], from this let us speak what the Lord may grant. Here surely the prophetic Scripture wished us to understand something. What a day, not common, not visible to the eyes of the flesh! Not that day which rises and sets, but a day that could know a rising and not know a setting! Let us see what the same Psalm had said above: *The stone which the builders rejected, this has been made the head of the corner. By the Lord it was made, this is wonderful in our eyes.* And it follows: *This is the day which the Lord has made*[^2]. Let us take the beginning of this day from the cornerstone. Who is the cornerstone which the builders rejected, except the Lord Christ whom the teachers of the Jews rejected? For the teachers of the Jews, skilled in the law, rejected him, saying: *This man is not from God, who breaks the Sabbath*[^3]. You have already said: *This man is not from God, who breaks the Sabbath. The stone which the builders rejected, this has been made the head of the corner.* How the head of the corner? Why was Christ called the cornerstone? Because every corner makes peace between two walls from different directions. The apostles came from the circumcision, they came from the nation of the Jews, from there too came those crowds that went before and followed his beast, saying what is in this very Psalm: *Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord*[^4]; from there came so many churches of which the apostle Paul says: *I was unknown by face to the churches of Judea that are in Christ, but they only heard that he who once persecuted us now preaches the faith that he once ravaged, and in me they glorified God*[^5]—Jews, but clinging to Christ like the apostles, coming and believing in Christ and making one wall. There remained the other part, the Church coming from the gentiles. They found one another; peace in Christ, unity in Christ who made both one. He is the *day which the Lord has made.* Understand the whole day to be head and body, the head Christ, the body the Church. This is the *day which the Lord has made.*

**2.** Recall the first condition of the world. *Darkness was over the abyss, and the Spirit of God was borne over the water. And God said: Let there be light, and there was light. And God divided the light from the darkness, and called the light day, but the darkness he called night*[^6]. Think of the darkness of these people before they came to the forgiveness of sins. Darkness, then, was over the abyss, before these sins had been forgiven. But *the Spirit of God was borne over the waters.* These went down into the waters, the Spirit of God was borne over the waters, the darkness of sins was driven out, *this is the day which the Lord has made.* To this day the Apostle says: *For you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^7]. Surely he did not say: You were darkness in the Lord? *Darkness* in yourselves, *light in the Lord.* But *God called the light day*, because his grace does what is done. They by themselves could be darkness, they could not become light unless the Lord had made it. Because *this is the day which the Lord has made*, not the day itself, but the Lord.

**3.** Was not Thomas a man, one of the disciples like a man from the crowd? His fellow disciples said to him: *We have seen the Lord.* And he said: *Unless I touch, unless I put my finger into his side, I will not believe.* The Evangelists announce to you and you do not believe? The world believed them and the disciple does not believe. Of them it was said: *Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^8]. Their words go out, reach to the ends of the earth, the whole world believes, all announce to one and he does not believe. It was not yet the day which the Lord had made. Still there was darkness in the abyss; in the depth of the human heart, there was darkness there. Let him come, let the head of this day come and say, patient, gentle, not angry because a physician: Come, he says, come, touch this and believe. You said: *Unless I touch, unless I put my finger, I will not believe.* Come, touch, *put your finger, and do not be unbelieving but believing.* Come, *put your finger.* I knew your wounds, I kept for you my scar. But plainly, putting in his hand, he completed his faith. For what is the fullness of faith? That Christ be believed neither only a man nor only God, but man and God. This is the fullness of faith, because *the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us*[^9]. Therefore this disciple, when the scars and members of his Savior were offered to him to be touched, when he had touched, exclaimed: *My Lord and my God*[^10]. He touched a man, he came to know God, and touched the flesh, he looked to the Word, because *the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us.* This Word suffered its own flesh to be hung on the wood; this Word suffered nails to be fixed in its flesh; this Word suffered its own flesh to be pierced through with a lance; this Word suffered its own flesh to be laid in the tomb; this Word raised its own flesh, offered it to the gaze of the disciples to be seen, presented it to their hands to be handled. They touch, they exclaim: *My Lord and my God. This is the day which the Lord has made.*
