# Sermon 229/E: A Tractate on the Monday of Easter

**1.** The Lord's passion and resurrection show us two lives: one that we endure, the other that we desire. For he is able to give us that one, who deigned to endure this one for us. For this shows how greatly he loves us, and he willed that we should believe that he will give us his own good things, who willed to have our evil things in common with us. We were born, and he was born; because we are to die, he died. These two things we knew in this life of ours, the beginning and the end, to be born and to die; by being born to begin our labors, by dying to depart to uncertainties. These two things we knew, to be born and to die: this abounds in our region. Our region is earth; the region of the angels is heaven. Our Lord, then, came to this region from another region; to the region of death, from the region of life; to the region of labor, from the region of happiness. He came bringing us his good things, and patiently bore our evil things. He carried his own good things hidden, he bore our evil things openly; the man appeared, God lay hidden; weakness appeared, majesty lay hidden; the flesh appeared, the Word lay hidden. The flesh suffered: where was the Word, when the flesh was suffering? The Word was not silent, for it was teaching us patience. Behold, the Lord Christ rose on the third day; where is the insulting of the Jews? Where is the insulting of the raging and frenzied chief men of the Jews who killed their physician? Recall, dearly beloved, what you heard when his passion was read. *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross, and we will believe him. If he is the Son of God, let him save him*[^1]. He heard these things, and was silent; he prayed for those who said them, and did not show himself. For in another Gospel it is written that he cried out for them, and said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^2]. He saw there those who would be his own, he saw those who would presently believe in him; he willed that they be forgiven. Our Head was hanging on the cross, but it recognized its members on the earth.

**2.** When the book of the *Acts of the Apostles* was read, there you heard how, when those who had gathered marveled that the Apostles and those who were with them were speaking in the tongues of all nations which they had not learned, by the inspiration and teaching of the Holy Spirit whom they had received, Peter the apostle addressed them as they stood amazed at the miracle, and explained to them that they indeed had done that evil through ignorance, in that they killed the Lord; but God fulfilled his own disposition[^3], that for the whole world innocent blood might be poured out, and the sins of all believers be blotted out; because he died in whom no sin could be found. The bond of our sins was held against us, the devil held the handwriting against us[^4]; he possessed those whom he had deceived, he held those whom he had conquered. We were all debtors, since all are born with an inherited debt; blood without sin was poured out, and it blotted out the bond of sin. Those, then, who had believed when Peter spoke, in the *Acts of the Apostles,* said in their dismay: *What shall we do, brothers? Tell us*[^5]. For they despaired that so great a wickedness could be forgiven them. And it was said to them: *Do penance, and let each of you be baptized in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, and your sins are forgiven you*[^6]. What sins? All of them. How far does "all" reach? Even this so great a sin, that you killed Christ. For what more wicked thing could you have done than to kill your Creator, created for your sake? What graver thing could a madman do than to be killed by his physician? Yet even this is forgiven; it was said to them: all are forgiven. You raged, and poured out innocent blood; believe, and drink what you poured out. Those, then, were there who in their despair said: *What shall we do?* and they heard that, by believing in him whom they killed, they could receive pardon for so great a crime. There they were, he saw them; he saw before his cross those whom he foresaw before the foundation of the world[^7]. For these he said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^8]; they were killing the physician; the physician was making, out of his own blood, a medicine for those who killed him. Great mercy and glory; what is not forgiven them, when it was forgiven them that they killed Christ? Therefore, dearly beloved, no one ought to doubt that in the bath of regeneration absolutely all sins whatsoever, the least and the greatest, are altogether forgiven; for there is a great example and proof of it. There is no graver sin than to kill Christ; and when even this is forgiven, what offense will remain in one who, believing, has been baptized?

**3.** But let us consider the resurrection of Christ, dearly beloved; for just as his passion signified our old life, so his resurrection is the sacrament of the new life. Therefore the Apostle says: *We have been buried together with Christ through baptism into death, so that as Christ rose from the dead, so we too might walk in newness of life*[^9]. You believed, you were baptized; the old life died, it was killed on the cross, buried in baptism. The old life, in which you lived ill, was buried; let the new rise. Live well: live so as to live; live so that, when you have died, you may not die. Consider, dearly beloved, what the Lord said in the Gospel to the man whom he healed: *Behold, you have been made well; now sin no more, lest something worse befall you*[^10]. By this sentence we had been shut in, and reduced to great straits; but his mercy never abandons us. He gave the baptized a prayer, because here one does not live without sin, that we may say daily: *Forgive us our debts*[^11]. There are debts: there is that general bond, and we do not cease to become debtors. We say from whom they are daily forgiven us; but not for that reason ought we to rest, as though secure, in disgraceful acts, in crimes, in misdeeds. Sins ought not to be our friends; we have vomited them up, let us hate them; let us not in dog-like fashion return to our vomit[^12]. And if they creep in, let them creep in upon us unwilling, not upon us loving or eagerly seeking them; for whoever wishes to keep friendship with sins will be an enemy to him who came to blot out sins, when he himself had no sin. My brothers, consider what I say: the friend of the disease is the enemy of the physician. If you were sick in body, and the physician came to you in keeping with his profession, tell me what he would want in coming to you; what would he want, but to heal you? Since, then, he is your friend, he must of necessity be the enemy of the fever; for if he loved your fever, he would not love you. He hates your fever, then; against it he entered your house, against it he climbed up to your bedroom, against it he came to your bed, against it he felt your pulse, against it he gave you instructions, against it he composed and applied medicines; all against it, all for you. If he, then, is wholly against the fever, wholly for you, you alone, by loving the fever, will be against yourself. You will answer me, I know, you will answer me and say: Who is there that loves a fever? I too know it: the sick man does not love the fever, but he loves what the fever asks for. What is it the physician said when he came in to you, armed with his art against your fever? He says to you, for example: Do not drink cold water. Do not drink cold water—you heard it from the physician, the enemy of your fever. When the physician has left, the fever says: Drink cold water. When the fever says this to you, you must say: that is the burning of the fever. The silent persuasion speaks to you, forces dryness into your throat, makes cold water a delight; remember what the physician said, do not drink. But when the physician is absent, the fever is present. What had the physician said? Do you want to conquer it? Do not yield to it. If you join yourself to the physician against the fever, you will be two; if you consent to the fever, the physician is conquered, but to the harm of the sick man, not of the physician. But far be it that the physician Christ should be conquered in those *whom he foreknew and predestined*; for these he *also called, and those whom he called, these he also justified; and those whom he justified, these he also glorified*[^13]. Let vices be restrained, let lusts be checked, let the devil and his angels be tormented with envy. *If God is for us, who is against us?*[^14]

**4.** But as for what Christ showed by the resurrection of his body, begin by living well to act spiritually. But that very thing, that is, the very property, the very reality, the very incorruptibility of the flesh, do not now hope for; it is the wages of faith, and the wages are paid when the day is finished. Now let us labor in the vineyard, and await the end of the day; for he who hired us to labor does not abandon us, lest we faint. He feeds the laborer at his work, who prepares to give him his wages when the day is finished; so now too the Lord feeds us as we labor in this age, not only with food for the belly, but also with food for the mind. If he did not feed us, I would not be speaking; because he feeds by the word, we do this, who preach him not to your bellies but to your minds. Hungering you receive, feasting you give praise; what is it you cry out, if no food reaches your minds? But what are we? His ministers, his servants; for it is not what is ours, but what is from his storehouse, that we dispense to you. From there we too live, since we are fellow servants. And what do we minister to you: his bread, or himself the bread? Whoever had hired a man to labor in his vineyard could give him bread, not himself. Christ gives his very self to his laborers; he offers himself in the bread, he keeps himself for the wages. We must not say: If we eat him now, what shall we have in the end? We eat, but he is not finished; he restores the hungry, but he himself does not fail. He feeds us now as we labor, for whom the wages remain whole. For what better thing are we to receive than himself? If he had anything better than himself, he would give that: but nothing is better than God, and Christ is God. Attend: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; this was in the beginning with God*[^15]. Who grasps this? Who gathers it? Who beholds it? Who contemplates it? Who thinks of it worthily? No one. *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^16]. To this he calls you, that as a laborer you may toil. *The Word was made flesh.* He himself calls you: your praise will be the Word, the Lord will be your wages.
