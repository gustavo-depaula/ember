# Sermon 140

**1.** What is it that we heard, brothers, the Lord saying: *He who believes in me, does not believe in me, but in him who sent me* [^1]? It is good for us to believe in Christ; especially because he himself openly said too that which you heard, that is, that he himself had come as light into the world, and that he who believes in him will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life [^2]. It is good, then, to believe in Christ. It is a great good to believe in Christ; and a great evil not to believe in Christ. But because Christ the Son is whatever he is from the Father, but the Father is not from the Son, but is the Father of the Son; he commends faith indeed in himself, but recalls the honor to its author.

**2.** For hold this firm and fixed, if you want to persevere Catholics, that God the Father begot God the Son without time, and made him from the Virgin in time. That birth exceeds times; this birth enlightens times. Yet both births are wonderful: that one without a mother, this one without a father. When God begot the Son, he begot him of himself, not of a mother; when the mother begot her son, a virgin begot him, not of a man. Of the Father he was born without beginning; of the mother he was born today at a certain beginning. Born of the Father he made us; born of the mother he remade us. Born of the Father, that we might be; born of the mother, that we might not perish. But the Father begot one equal to himself, and the whole that the Son is, he has from the Father. But that he is God the Father, he does not have from the Son. So we say that the Father is God of no one, the Son God of God. Therefore everything that the Son does wonderfully, everything that he says truly, he attributes to him from whom he is; nor can he be other than the one from whom he is. Adam was made a man; he could be other than he was made. For he was made just, and could be unjust. But the only-begotten Son of God, what he is, this cannot be changed; he cannot be turned into another thing, cannot be lessened, what he was cannot not be, cannot not be equal to the Father. But plainly he who gave all things to the Son being born, gave to one not in need; without doubt the Father gave to the Son also that very equality with the Father. How did the Father give? Did he beget him less, and add to his form, to make him equal? If he had done this, he would have given to one in need. But now I have said to you, what you ought most firmly to hold, that is, that the whole that the Son is the Father gave, but to one being born, not in need. If he gave to one being born, not in need, he gave without doubt equality too, and by giving equality begot one equal. And though the one is one person, the other another; yet the one is not one thing, the other another thing; but what the one is, this the other too is. Not who the one is, this the other too; but what the one is, this the other too.

**3.** *He who sent me*, he says, you heard: *He who sent me*, he says, *himself gave me a commandment what I should say, and what I should speak; and I know that his commandment is eternal life* [^3]. It is the Gospel of John, hold it. *He who sent me, himself gave me a commandment what I should say, and what I should speak; and I know that his commandment is eternal life*. O if he grants that I say what I want! For my poverty and his abundance make straits for me. *He himself*, he says, *gave me a commandment what I should say, and what I should speak; and I know that his commandment is eternal life*. Seek in the Letter of this evangelist John what he said of Christ. *Let us believe*, he says, *in his true Son Jesus Christ. He himself is the true God, and eternal life* [^4]. What is: *The true God, and eternal life?* The true Son of God is true God, and eternal life. Why did he say: *in his true Son?* Because God has many sons, therefore he had to be distinguished, by adding that he was the true Son. Not only by saying that he is the Son; but by adding, as I said, that he is the true Son; therefore he had to be distinguished, on account of the many sons whom God has. For we are sons by grace, he by nature. We made by the Father through him: he is this which the Father is; are we too that which he is, God?

**4.** But someone off to the side, not knowing what he says, says: For this reason it was said: *I and the Father are one* [^5], because they have between themselves a concordant will, not because the nature of the Son is the same that is the nature of the Father. For even the Apostles (and this he said, not I), for even the Apostles are one with the Father and the Son. A horrible blasphemy! Even the Apostles, he says, are one with the Father and the Son, because they obey the will of the Father and the Son. Did he dare to say this? Then let Paul say: I and God are one. Let Peter say it, let any Prophet say it: I and God are one. He does not say it. Far be it that he say it! He knows himself to be another nature, a nature to be saved; he knows himself to be another nature, a nature to be enlightened. No one says: I and God are one. However much he makes progress, however much he excels in holiness, on however great a height of virtue he is exalted, he never says: I and God are one; because if he has virtue, and therefore says this; by saying this, what he had, he lost.

**5.** Believe, then, the Son equal to the Father, but yet the Son from the Father, but the Father not from the Son. Origin with the one, equality with the other. For if he is not equal, he is not the true Son. For what do we say, brothers? If he is not equal, he is less; if he is less, I ask the nature to be saved, believing badly, how he was born less. Answer: does the less one grow or not? If he grows, then the Father too grows old. But if what he was born, this he will be; if he was born less, he will be less too; with his own loss he will be perfect, with the loss of the Father's form perfect born, never going to come to the Father's form. So, impious ones, you assign the Son; so, heretics, you blaspheme the Son. What, then, does the Catholic faith say? The Son God from God the Father; the Father God not from God the Son. But the Son God equal to the Father, born equal, not born less; not made equal, but born equal. What he is, this the one too who was born. Was the Father at some time without the Son? Far be it! Take away "at some time," where there is no time. Always the Father, always the Son. Without beginning of time the Father, without beginning of time the Son; never the Father before the Son, never the Father without the Son. But yet because the Son is God from God the Father, but the Father is God, but not from God the Son; let not the honoring of the Son in the Father displease us. For the honoring of the Son gives honor to the Father, does not lessen his own divinity.

**6.** Because, then, I was saying what I had set forth: *And I know*, he says, *that his commandment is eternal life* [^6]. Attend, brothers, to what I say: *I know that his commandment is eternal life*. And we read in this same John of Christ: *He himself is the true God, and eternal life* [^7]. If the commandment of the Father is eternal life, and Christ the Son is himself eternal life; the commandment of the Father is the Son himself. For how is the commandment of the Father not that which is the Word of the Father? Or if you take the commandment given by the Father to the Son carnally, as though the Father said to the Son: This I command you, this and that I want you to do; with what words did he speak to the one Word? When he gave a commandment to the Word, did he seek words? Because, then, the commandment of the Father is eternal life, and the Son himself is eternal life, believe and accept, believe and understand, because the Prophet says: *Unless you believe, you will not understand* [^8]. Do you not grasp it? Be enlarged. Hear the Apostle: *Be enlarged, be not bearing the yoke with the unbelieving* [^9]. Those who do not want to believe this before they grasp it, are unbelievers. But because they wanted to be unbelievers, they will remain unskilled. Let them believe, then, that they may understand. Utterly the commandment of the Father is eternal life. Therefore the commandment of the Father is the Son himself, who was born today; a commandment not given in time, but a commandment born. The Gospel of John exercises minds, files and pares away the flesh, that we may relish of God not carnally, but spiritually. Let these things, then, suffice you, brothers; lest in the length of disputation the sleep of forgetfulness creep upon you.
