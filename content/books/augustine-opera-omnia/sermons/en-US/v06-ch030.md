# Sermon 209: In Lent

**1.** The solemn time has come, when I should admonish your Charity concerning the soul, to be more attentively thought upon, and the body, to be chastised. For these are the forty most sacred days throughout the whole world, which, as Easter draws near, the whole world, which God reconciles to himself in Christ[^1], celebrates together with a devotion to be proclaimed. If any enmities, which either ought not to have been born, or ought to have died quickly, and yet up to this time, whether by negligence, or by stubbornness, or by an immodest but proud shame, could last among brothers; let them at least now be ended. Those upon which the sun ought not to have gone down[^2], let them at least, after many risings and settings of the sun, even they at length at some time be extinguished by their own setting, and be no further renewed by any rising. The negligent man forgets to end enmities; the stubborn man is unwilling to grant pardon when he is asked; the proudly shamefaced man disdains to ask pardon. By these three vices enmities live: but they kill the souls in which they do not die. Let memory watch against negligence, mercy against stubbornness, humble prudence against proud shame. Let him who recalls himself a neglecter of concord shake off torpor by waking up: let him who desires to be an exactor of his debtor think himself a debtor of God: let him who blushes to ask that a brother forgive him conquer evil shame by good fear: so that, the harmful enmities being ended, they being dead, you may live. All this love does, which *does not act perversely*[^3]. Love, my brothers, insofar as it is present, let it be exercised by living well; but insofar as it is less, let it be obtained by asking.

**2.** But that our prayers may be aided by fitting supports, since on these days we ought to have them more fervent; let us also bestow alms more fervently. To them let there be added what is withdrawn from us by fasting and by abstaining from our usual foods. Although he ought to have more bountiful alms who, on account of some necessity of his body and habit of food, cannot abstain, so that he may add to the poor what he withdraws from himself: but let the pious man give to the poor for this reason, that he does not withdraw from himself; so that, since he can less aid his prayers by the chastisement of the body, he may enclose a more abundant alms in the heart of the poor, which can pray for him. From the holy Scriptures this is the most wholesome counsel, to be embraced: *Enclose*, it says, *alms in the heart of the poor, and it will pray for you*[^4].

**3.** We admonish also those who abstain from meats not to avoid, as though unclean, the vessels in which they were cooked. For so speaking the Apostle says: *All things are clean to the clean*[^5]. For what is done in observances of this kind by sound doctrine is done not for the cause of avoiding uncleanness, but of reining in desire. Whence those too who so restrain themselves from meats as to seek out other foods both of more difficult preparation and of greater cost, greatly err. For this is not to undertake abstinence, but to change luxury. How shall we tell these men to give to the poor what they withdraw from themselves, by whom the usual food is so left aside that in providing another the expense is increased? Be, then, on these days both more frequent in fasting, and more sparing in spending on yourselves, and more bountiful in bestowing on the needy. These days also demand continence from marital intercourse: *For a time*, says the Apostle, *that you may give yourselves to prayer: and again return to it, lest Satan tempt you because of your incontinence*[^6]. This is not hard and difficult, for a few days, for the faithful married, which holy widows have undertaken from a certain point of life to the end, which holy virgins do for their whole life. And in all these things let devotion be fervent, let pride be suppressed. Let no one so rejoice in the good of bounty as to lose the good of humility. For all the other gifts of God do not make anything profit, unless there be present the bond of love.

[^1]: See 2 Cor 5:19
[^2]: See Eph 4:26
[^3]: 1 Cor 13:4
[^4]: Sir 29:12
[^5]: Titus 1:15
[^6]: 1 Cor 7:5
