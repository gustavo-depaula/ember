# Sermon 334: On the Feast of the Martyrs

**1.** This indeed is the voice of all the good faithful Christians, but especially of the glorious martyrs: *If God is for us, who is against us?*[^1] The world raged against them, the peoples meditated vain things, the princes gathered together as one: new tortures were devised, and ingenious cruelty invented incredible punishments. They were overwhelmed with reproaches, charged with false crimes, shut up in intolerable prisons, torn with hooks, slain with iron, exposed to beasts, burned with fires, and the martyrs of Christ said: *If God is for us, who is against us?* The whole world against you, and you say: *Who is against us?* They answer you: And what is the whole world, when we die for him through whom the world was made? Let them say, let them say, let us hear, let us say together: *If God is for us, who is against us?* They can rage, they can curse, they can slander, they can drive us with false reproaches, finally they can not only kill the body, but even tear it; and what will they do? *For behold God is my helper, and the Lord is the upholder of my soul*[^2]. Tell me, blessed martyr: your body is torn, and you say, It does not pertain to me? Yes, I said it. Why? tell us why. Because *the Lord is the upholder of my soul.* My body is restored through my soul. Does my hair not perish, and the head perish? My hair does not perish. But by dogs your body is torn. And what is it to me? Even if by dogs my body is torn, yet by the Lord it is raised up. The world is the slayer of my body, but *the Lord is the upholder of my soul.* And what harm does it do me, since the Lord is the upholder of my soul, that the world is the slayer of my body? What have I lost? what have I forfeited? When the Lord is the upholder of my soul, he will be also the restorer of my body. What will be lacking to me, if the enemy tears my limbs, when God numbers my hairs? Christ, exhorting his martyrs, that they should dread nothing from the enemy persecutors: *The hairs,* he says, *of your head are all numbered*[^3]. Shall I, then, fear for the loss of my limbs, when I have received security about the number of my hairs? So let us say, let us say from faith, let us say in hope, let us say with most ardent love: *If God is for us, who is against us?*

**2.** Behold the king against you, and you say, *who is against us?* Behold all the people against you, and you say: *Who is against us?* From where do you prove it, O glorious martyr; from where do you prove to me what you say: *If God is for us, who is against us?* For it is manifest that if God is for you, who is against you? But prove that God is for you. Do I then not prove it? Behold I teach it: *Who did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all*[^4]. This which follows, you heard when the Apostle was read. For when he had said: *If God is for us, who is against us?* as though it were said to him: Prove that God is for you: at once he brought a great proof, at once he introduced the Martyr of martyrs, the Witness of witnesses; namely him whom the Father did not spare, his own Son, but handed him over for us all: hence the Apostle proved that he had truly said: *If God is for us, who is against us? Who even did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all. How has he not with him given us all things?*[^5] When with him he gave us all things, he also gave us himself. Surely the raging of the world does not terrify me, to whom the maker of the world has been given? Let us rejoice that Christ has been given to us, and let us fear no enemies of Christ in this world. For see who has been given to us: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^6]. He is Christ, he is the only-begotten Son of God, he is coeternal with the begetter. *All things were made through him*[^7]. How have the things which were made through him not been given to us, when he himself has been given to us, through whom all things were made? And that you may know that it is he: *The Word became flesh, and dwelt among us*[^8]. Desire and seek the life of Christ given to you; and until you reach it, hold as a pledge the death of Christ. For, promising us that he would live with us, he could give us no greater pledge than to die for us. Your evils, he says, I bore, shall I not render my goods? He promised, he made a bond, he gave a pledge; and do you doubt to believe? He promised, when he walked here among men; he made a bond, when he wrote the Gospel. To his pledge daily you say, Amen. You have received the pledge, daily it is paid out to you. Do not despair, you who live on the pledge.

**3.** Or is perhaps an injury done to the only-begotten Son, when it is said that he has been given to us, as though he were to be our possession? Plainly he is to be. For what if someone today gives you a pleasant and fertile estate, where it would delight you always to dwell because of the pleasantness, from which you could easily support yourself because of the fertility; would you not embrace the gift, and give thanks to the giver? In Christ we are to abide. How will he not be our possession, where we will abide, and from where we will live? Let Scripture say this too, lest we seem by our conjectures to have usurped anything against the discipline of the word of God. Hear what someone says to him, who knew that *if God is for us, who is against us? The Lord,* he says, *is the portion of my inheritance*[^9]. He did not say: O Lord, what, do you give me some inheritance? Whatever you give me is cheap. You be my inheritance, I love you, I love you wholly, with my whole heart, my whole soul, my whole mind I love you. What will be to me whatever you give me besides yourself? This is to love God freely, to hope God from God, to hasten to be filled by God, to be satisfied by him. For he himself suffices you; besides him nothing suffices you. Philip knew this, when he said: *Lord, show us the Father, and it suffices us*[^10]. When, then, will be what the Apostle says at the end: *That God may be all in all*[^11], that he himself may be to us whatever here without him we desire, and in desire for which for the most part we sin against him? In place of all things he himself will be to us, when God will be all in all. You sin against God to eat, you sin against God to be clothed, you sin against God to live, you sin against God to be honored. And when shall I number all things? Do not sin against God for the sake of these things. For the sake of food do you sin against God? food eternal will God be to you. For the sake of clothing do you sin against God? with immortality God will clothe you. For the sake of honor do you sin against God? your honor will be God. For the sake of love of temporal life do you sin against God? eternal life God will be to you. Do not sin against him for the sake of anything. For you ought to love him freely, who himself can satisfy you in place of all things.
