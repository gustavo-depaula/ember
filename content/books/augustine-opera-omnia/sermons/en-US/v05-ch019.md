# Sermon 162/A

**1.** It is good to speak of love to those who love it, by which whatever is loved is loved well. For according to the Apostle there is in love a most surpassing way. It was just now read, we heard: *I show you a surpassing way*. Then he related many gifts, and indeed excellent ones, not to be lightly esteemed; yet he said that they profit men nothing, who do not have love. Among the gifts he mentioned: to speak with the tongues of men and of angels; to have all prophecy, all knowledge, all faith, so as to move mountains; to distribute all one's goods to the poor, to hand over one's body to be burned[^1]. All these are great and divine things, but only if they are set in the foundation of love, and rise from the root of love. But that many of these gifts were in many who did not have love, we would not dare to say, unless we were taught by examples—not of any men whatever, sought from anywhere, but from the holy Scriptures themselves, in which whoever does not have faith cannot have love. But among the chief things that were named, either prophecy or faith appears there as great. What, then, of the rest? If anyone having prophecy profits nothing by it, if he does not have love, and anyone having faith cannot reach the kingdom of God unless he has love, what shall we say of the rest? To speak with tongues—what is that compared to prophecy and faith? To distribute all one's goods to the poor—what is that compared to prophecy? And to hand over one's body to be burned? Very often rash and headstrong people do this. Therefore those two are the great things there, about which it is truly to be wondered at, if we can find any man having prophecy and not having love, or having faith and not having love.

**2.** Of prophecy the book of *Kingdoms* gives us an example. Saul was a persecutor of holy David. When he was persecuting him, and sent satellites to seize him for punishment, those who were sent to lead David away to be killed found him among the prophets, where was also holy Samuel, the son of the barren Anna, whom she had asked from the Lord to conceive, had received from the Lord, and had given, once born, to the Lord. At that time, then, in which David was, there was Samuel, that most excellent of the prophets; for by him David was anointed. So when David was suffering persecution from Saul, he fled to Samuel, just as now, for example, one who perhaps suffers some persecution outside flees to the Church. So he had fled there, where there was not only Samuel, the most excellent of all the prophets, but also many other prophets. Among them, when they were prophesying, came those sent by Saul, who, as I said, were to seize him for death. The Spirit of God leaped upon them, and those who had come to lead a holy and just man of God to the sword, and to drag him from the midst of the prophets, began to prophesy; suddenly filled with the Spirit of God, they were made prophets. Perhaps this was due to the innocence of these men; for they had not come of their own will to seize him, but were sent by their king. And perhaps they had indeed come to that place where David was, but were not going to do what Saul commanded; perhaps they too were going to remain. For these things happen even today. Sometimes an officer is sent by a great power to seize someone from the church; he does not dare to act against God, and, lest he himself incur the sword, he remains there where he was sent to seize. So someone, wondering, might say that these became prophets suddenly because they were innocent; the prophecy itself bore witness to their innocence. Those sent came, but were not going to do what that evil man commanded. Let us believe this of these. Others were sent; and the Spirit of God leaped upon them too, and they too began to prophesy. Let us count these too with the former by the merit of innocence. A third group was sent; this happened to them too. Let them all have been innocent. When they delayed, and what Saul commanded was not done, he himself came too. Surely he too was not innocent? Surely he too was not sent by some power, and not perverse by his own will? And yet the Spirit of God leaped upon him too, and he began to prophesy[^2]. Behold, Saul prophesies, having prophecy, but not having love. He was made a kind of vessel, to be touched by the Spirit, not to be cleansed by the Spirit.

**3.** For the Spirit of God touches some hearts unto prophesying, and yet does not cleanse them. And if it touches and does not cleanse, is the Spirit itself perhaps defiled? For it belongs to the divine substance to touch all things, to be nowhere defiled. Nor wonder, if this light which is poured from heaven, touching all unclean things scattered everywhere, is nowhere darkened by the unclean. Nor only this which comes from heaven, but even that which is sent from a lamp touches whatever you lead the light to; and perhaps whoever passes through a sewer, he himself, if he touches it, is polluted; but if he carries a lamp, the splendor of the lamp passes over all things, nowhere contracts a stain. If God could grant this to corporeal lights, can he himself, the true and eternal and unchangeable light, be anywhere defiled? Or can the light of God be lacking anywhere, of which it was said, *It reaches mightily from end to end, and disposes all things sweetly*[^3]? It touches, then, what it wishes, and cleanses what it wishes; it does not cleanse whatever it touches, but touches what it cleanses. The Spirit, then, did not cleanse Saul, the persecutor of God, but yet touched him unto prophesying. Caiaphas, the chief priest, was a persecutor of Christ; and yet he spoke prophecy, when he said, *It is expedient that one man die, and the whole nation not perish*. The Evangelist, following, expounded the prophecy and said, *But this he did not say of himself, but, since he was high priest, he prophesied*[^4]. Caiaphas prophesied, Saul prophesied; they had prophecy, but did not have love. Did Caiaphas have love, who persecuted the Son of God, whom love led to us? Did Saul have love, who persecuted the one by whose hand he had been freed from his enemies, not only envious, but also ungrateful? Therefore we have proved that there can be in someone prophecy, and not love. But prophecy cannot profit such men, according to the Apostle: *If I do not have love*, he says, *I am nothing*[^5]. He did not say: Prophecy is nothing, or faith is nothing; but: I myself am nothing, if I do not have love. Having great things, he is nothing; though he have great things, he is nothing; for those great things which he has, he has not unto help, but unto judgment. It is not a great thing to have great things, but to use great things well is a great thing; but he does not use them well who does not have love. For one does not use anything well except a good will; the will cannot be good where love is not.

**4.** What of faith? Do we find someone having both faith and not having love? There are many who believe and do not love. Nor need men be enumerated; we find that the demons believed what we believe, and did not love what we love. For when the apostle James was arguing against those who thought it sufficed for them to believe, and were unwilling to live well—which is not done except from love (for the good life pertains to love, nor can one having love live badly; because this very living well is nothing else than to be filled with love)—when, then, certain people boasted that they had believed in God, and were unwilling to live well, and fittingly to so great a faith as they had received, he compared them to demons, saying, *You*, he says, *say that God is one. You believe well; but the demons too believe, and tremble*[^6]. If, then, you only believe, and do not love, you still have something in common with the demons. Peter said, *You are the Son of God*; and it was said to him, *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^7]. We find that the demons too said, *What have we to do with you, Son of God*[^8]? The apostles confess the Son of God, the demons too confess the Son of God; the confession seems equal, the love unequal. They believe and love; those believe and fear; love awaits a reward, fear punishment. Therefore we find that anyone can have even faith, and not have love. Let no one, then, boast of any gift of the Church, if perhaps he is eminent in the Church by some gift attributed to him, but let him see whether he has love. For the same apostle Paul spoke, and enumerated many gifts of God in the members of Christ, which is the Church; and he said that proper gifts are attributed to each member, and that it cannot happen that all have one gift. Nor yet will anyone remain without a gift; *Apostles*, he says, *Prophets, Teachers, interpreters, speakers in tongues, those having powers of healings, those having helps, governings, kinds of tongues*[^9]. These things were said; and we see some things in some, other things in others. Let no one, then, grieve that what he sees granted to another is not granted to himself; let him have love, let him not envy the one who has it, and with that one he has what he does not have. For whatever my brother has, if I do not envy it, and have loved it, it is mine. In myself I do not have it, but in him I have it; it would not be mine, if we were not in one body and under one head.

**5.** The left hand, for example, has a ring in the body, and the right does not; has it then remained without adornment? Look at the individual hands, and you will see one has it, the other does not; look at the framework of the body, to which both hands cleave, and see that the one which does not have it, has it in the one which has it. The eyes see where one goes, the feet go where the eyes look ahead; nor can the feet see, nor the eyes walk. But the foot answers you: I too have light, but not in myself, but in the eye; for the eye does not see for itself, and not see for me. The eyes too say: We too walk, not in ourselves, but in the feet; for the feet do not carry themselves and not carry us. The individual members, then, distributed to their individual and proper offices, carry out what the soul commands; yet all, set in one body and holding unity; nor do they arrogate to themselves what the other members have, if perhaps they do not have those members; nor do they think foreign to themselves what they have together in one body. Finally, brothers, if some trouble befall any member of the body, what members will deny it their help? What seems so extreme in a man as the foot? And in the foot itself, what so extreme as the sole? And in the sole itself, what so extreme as the very skin, by which the earth is trodden? Yet this extreme thing is so held by the framework of the whole body, that if in that very place a thorn is trodden on, all the members run together to the aid of pulling out the thorn; at once the knees are bent, the spine is curved—not the one that was pricked, but the one that holds the whole back; one sits down, that the thorn may be pulled out; now sitting down, that this may be done, belongs to the whole body. How tiny is the place in trouble! So small a place is it, as much as a thorn could prick; and yet the trouble of that extreme and tiny place is not abandoned by the whole body; the other members feel no pain, and in that one place all feel pain. Hence the Apostle himself gave an example of love, exhorting us so to love one another as the members in the body love one another: *If one member suffers*, he says, *the other members suffer with it; and if one member is glorified, all the members rejoice together. You are the Body of Christ and members*[^10]. If the members love one another which have a head on earth, how ought the members to love one another whose head is in heaven? Surely they do not even love one another, if they are forsaken by their head; but since that head is so the head, and is so exalted, and so set in heaven at the right hand of the Father, that yet he labors on earth—not in himself, but in his members—so that he says at the end, *I was hungry, I thirsted, I was a stranger*, when it is said to him, *When did we see you hungry and thirsty*? and as though he answers: I, the head, was in heaven, but on earth my members thirsted—finally he says, *When you did it to one of my least, you did it to me*, and again, to those who did not do it, *When you did not do it to one of my least, neither did you do it to me*[^11]—to this head we are joined only through love.

**6.** For thus, brothers, we see the individual members acting their own work in their offices, so that the eye sees but does not work; the hand works but does not see; the ear hears but does not see or work; the tongue speaks but does not hear or see; and although they are individually distinguished and separated in their offices, yet bound together by one framework of the body, they have something common in all. The offices are diverse, the health is one. This, then, is in the members of Christ what health is in the members of the body—love. The eye is placed in a better location, on high, and as it were set on a citadel for counsel, whence it may look out, whence see, whence point out; great honor in the eyes, both in location, and in a more ardent sense, and in agility, and in a certain power that the other members do not have. Accordingly men very often swear by their eyes rather than by any other member. No one has said to another: I love you as my ears; and the sense of the ears is next and nearest to the eyes. What shall I say of the rest? Daily men say: I love you as my eyes. And the Apostle, signifying that greater love is held in the eyes than in the other members, when he said that he was loved by the Church of God, said, *For I bear you witness, that, if it could be done, you would have plucked out your eyes and given them to me*[^12]. Nothing, then, in the body is loftier than the eyes and more honored; and nothing in the body perhaps more extreme than the smallest toe of the foot. Since these things are so, yet it is more profitable in the body to be a toe, and to be sound, than to be an eye, and in disturbance to be bleary; for health, which is common to all the members, is more precious than the offices of individuals. So you see in the Church a small man having some small gift, and yet having love; another perhaps eminent in the Church by some greater gift, and yet not having love. Let that one be the extreme toe, let that one be the eye; that one belongs more to the framework of the body, who could obtain health. Finally, it is troublesome to the rest of the body, whatever is sick in the body; and effort is indeed given by all the members, that what is sick may be healed, and very often it is healed. But if it is not healed, and conceives so great a rottenness that it cannot be healed, it is taken thought for all the members that it be cut off from the framework of the body.

**7.** Let some Donatus, then, have been as it were an eye in the body, let it have been so; we do not know what he was, but let him have been wholly such as he is said to be; what did the excellence of his honor and glory profit him? He could not keep health, because he did not have love. Finally, these men are so rotted that they had necessarily to be cut off; and those whom they say they have are worms of rottenness; cut off, they are worms, nor can they admit health. For a member admits health only as long as it is not cut off from the body; for from the other sound parts health flows to the place of the wound; but when the member where the wound is has been cut off, by what way and whence health may come to it is not found. Therefore they are compared to cut-off shoots, and the gospel reading runs together with the apostolic reading. For there too the Lord, that we might abide in him, commended to us most of all nothing but love: *I am*, he says, *the vine, you are the shoots, my Father is the husbandman. Every shoot that bears fruit in me, he prunes it, that it may bear more fruit; but the one that bears no fruit in me, he will cut it off*[^13]. Fruit comes from love itself, because fruit comes only from the root. But the Apostle says, *That, rooted and grounded in love*[^14]. There, then, is the root, whence all fruit rises. He who has begun to dissent from the root, although he seems to remain a while, has either been cut off secretly, or is even openly to be cut off; for in no way can he bear fruit. They were once in unity. They were cut off. Whence were they cut off? From unity. But you, they say, are cut off. What shall we do? I say: You are cut off; you say: You are cut off. Let God judge. Have we then deferred the question and sent it to the judgment of God? Plainly not. In many matters we do this, where the sentence of God does not yet appear; but where it does appear, let us use it, not defer it. I bring forth Scripture, and I see who is cut off from where. For if Scripture bore witness to the party of Donatus, to some Church established in some part of the world, as the party of Donatus is established in the part of Africa, let them say we are cut off, and let them say they are rooted. But if Scripture bears witness only to the Church spread throughout the whole world, why do we seek a man as judge of our suit? We have God; he does not yet preside in judgment, he already presides in the Gospel.

**8.** Crispinus the heretic has just now been judged. But what does he say? Surely I was not overcome by a gospel sentence? Thus asserting that he was not conquered, because the proconsul judged against him, not Christ. If, then, he holds the judgment of a man of little account, why did he appeal from the proconsul to the emperor? He himself demanded the judgment of that very proconsul; he himself said: Hear me, I am not a heretic. The one whose judgment you demanded, does his judgment displease you? Why? Because he judged against you. If he judged for you, he would judge well; because he judged against you, he judged badly. Before he judged, he was a good judge, to whom you said: I am not a heretic, hear me. But the proconsul judged, he says, according to the laws of the emperors, not according to the laws of the Gospel. Granted he did so, granted the proconsul judged according to the laws of the emperors; if, then, the emperors judge badly against you, why did you appeal from the proconsul to their judgment? Were the laws of the emperors already against you, or not yet? If not yet, the proconsul did not judge according to them; if they already were, will the emperors judge for you against their own laws? Then I ask you: These laws of the emperors which are against you—what happened? Teach me. For it is manifest, and not denied, that there are many laws of the emperors against them. Whence did this happen? Whence was this done? Did we perhaps persecute, and say many evil things to the emperors about you? This indeed they say to those whom, wretched and unskilled, they deceive. For they utterly conceal the cause, as it was conducted at that time, from those they wish to deceive. But, however much they conceal it, it is dug out, laid open, made public, brought to the knowledge even of the unwilling and the refusing. Closing their eyes, and unwilling to see the light, let the light itself strike them. Let them not be permitted to dissemble manifest things; let them not be permitted to be turned from things open; let them not be permitted to cover the plain. Let us press them with manifest truth. You demanded the emperor's judgment. You lie, he says. The public records exist. The Donatists themselves of the party of Majorinus, who was first ordained against Caecilian, went to the proconsul Anulinus at that time, and delivered accusatory documents in the name of Caecilian, sealed in leather, saying that they had against Caecilian the crimes which they had written in that document, and asking that he send their very accusation to the imperial court, to the emperor. There exists the report of the proconsul Anulinus writing to the emperor Constantine, that men of the party of Majorinus came to him with documents about the accusation of Caecilian, asking that he send those same documents to the emperor; and he says he did what they asked. The emperor wrote to bishop Miltiades and Mark, transferring to them the ecclesiastical case, and removing it from himself. In the same letters the emperor writes that he had sent the documents sent by Anulinus; and in those very letters it is not known what those documents are, but in the report of Anulinus it is learned, which today is contained in the public codices. Then the same Constantine writes to Anulinus, that he direct the parties to Rome for the episcopal judgment. Anulinus too reports at the end that he had sent the parties. You, then, went to those before the emperor; you brought the cause of the Church to a human power. He was better than you: for you brought it to the emperor, he to the bishops. The cause was pleaded in the episcopal judgment, they accusing first. Sentence was given for Caecilian. They, not content with the ecclesiastical judgment, began to murmur, again went to the same emperor, after the episcopal judgment seeking an imperial judgment. He gave another ecclesiastical one at Arles; from that judgment too they appealed to the emperor himself. Overcome by their importunity, he himself too wished to take up the cause and examine it. He took it up, examined it, judged Caecilian most innocent; and now all the orders of the emperors are against them. What wonder? The one whose judgment you demanded, do you dare to refuse his sentence? Why did you wish to bring the judgment to him? Did you have a Church in Africa; did you not have one in the whole world? But where were they going, who had already cut themselves off from it? They no longer cleaved to the Church; but the emperor cleaved to it, to whom the judgment was brought. Accordingly he most gently wished the bishops to judge; and afterward he yielded to them, that he himself too should judge. Hence the laws are against you; see whether they are not against you. First you yourselves were there; you the first accusers, you the last appellants, you the final murmurers. Yet surely, he says, was I overcome from the Gospel? You were overcome by that judgment which you yourself chose.

**9.** But we do not refuse the sentence of the Gospel: plainly, even if he did not say it, we would read it, we would dig it out, we would show it. Let the Gospel be recited; let us see where the Lord Jesus Christ says the Church is. Surely both our ears and our hearts ought to be open to him; let us hear him, let him tell us where the Church is. If he says his Church is in Africa, let us all flow together into the party of Donatus; if he says his Church is in the whole world, let the cut-off members return to the body; for the branches are not so broken that they cannot be grafted in again. You have the apostle Paul saying, *But you say: The branches were broken off, that I might be grafted in. Well. They were broken off because of unbelief; but you stand by faith. Do not be high-minded, but fear. For if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you*[^15]. For the Jews were broken off, as natural branches, and the gentiles were grafted in, as a wild olive into the olive. From these grafted branches, and from this grafted wild olive, we all are partakers of the olive. But, just as the Apostle had threatened the proud branches of the wild olive, such these men have become by being proud, that with those natural ones cut off before, they too by pride deserve to be cut off. But what does the Apostle say? *And they*, he says, *if they do not remain in unbelief, will be grafted in*[^16]; and you, if you do not remain in faith, will be cut off. Let no one, then, be proud in the vine, let no one despair apart from the vine: if you are proud in the vine, watch that you not be cut off; if any are placed apart from the vine, let them not despair, let them dare to be grafted in. For they are not to be grafted in by their own hand. For he says, *God is able to graft them in again*[^17]. Let them not say: And how can it be done, that a cut-off thing, a broken branch, be grafted in again? You rightly say it cannot be done, if you ask human ability, not if you ask the divine majesty. For what? What has already been done by the Lord, can any of the husbandmen do it? He took a wild olive and grafted it into an olive; and the wild olive grafted into the olive gave not bitter berries, but the olive. Let someone do this: let him graft a wild olive into an olive; he will see nothing come forth but the berries of the wild olive. God, then, was able—not to graft the olive into the wild olive, but to graft the wild olive into the olive, and to make the wild olive a partaker of the fatness of the olive, so that, stripped of its bitterness, it might be clothed with fatness; and will he not be able to graft you, cut off through pride, by humility? You exhort me well, he says, but first show me cut off; lest perhaps you ought to exhort yourself to come to me, not me to be grafted into you. I dare to say: Hear me; and yet I fear to say: Hear me; for I fear lest he despise a man. Rather, I exhort that he despise a man; for if he despised a man, he would not be of the party of Donatus; for Donatus was a man. Therefore, if we speak our own words, let us be despised; if we speak the words of Christ, let him be heard, who is not heard in vain, and not despised in vain; for he is heard unto reward, not heard unto punishment. Let us hear him: let the Lord himself tell us.

**10.** He showed the Church in innumerable places; but yet let me mention some one. After the resurrection, you know, brothers, he showed himself to his disciples, demonstrated his scars, offered himself to be handled, not only to be seen. But they, holding and handling and recognizing, yet still hesitated for joy, as the Gospel teaches us, which it is fitting to believe, which it is wicked not to believe. But the Lord, to those still hesitating for joy and doubting, brought firmness from the Scriptures, and said, *These things I said to you, while I was still with you, that all things must be fulfilled which are written in the Law and the Prophets and the Psalms about me. Then he opened their understanding, that they might understand the Scriptures, and said to them: Because so it behooved Christ to suffer, and to rise on the third day, and that repentance and the forgiveness of sins be preached in his name through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^18]. There you are not, there I am. Why do you await a man to judge about you from a tribunal? Hear Christ from the Gospel. *Through all nations*, he says, *beginning from Jerusalem*. Are you there? Do you commune with all nations? Do you commune with that Church which is spread through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem? If you commune, you are there, you are in the vine, you are not cut off; for that is the vine which grew and filled the whole world, the body of Christ, the Church of Christ, whose head is in heaven. But if you commune only with Africans, and from Africa you secretly send, where you can, men to console pilgrims, do you not find that you have remained in a part, cut off from the whole? What did you say in the proconsul's judgment? I am a Catholic. It is his own voice; it is recited from the *Acts*. Catholic, hold the whole; for "holon" is "whole," and hence the Church was called Catholic, because it is throughout the whole. Surely it was not called "according to a part," and not Catholic? For "meros" is "part," "holon" is "whole"; from the Greek word it was called "Catholic," "according to the whole." Do you, then, commune with the universal? No, he says. Then you are in a part; how are you a Catholic? There is much difference between the whole and a part, whence "Catholic" takes its name. For you took your name from the party of Donatus; the Catholic took its name from the whole world. But we say it is in the universal, and does God perhaps not say so? I have mentioned the Gospel, I have recited from the Gospel. *Through all nations*, he says, *beginning from Jerusalem*. Did it not come from there to Africa? For if it began from Jerusalem, by filling all things it came to you, not by drying up. Who is there who says: A stream was led from a fountain, that it might come to me; it dried up on the way, and reached me? If it dried up on the way, by what way did it reach you? Surely by filling all things it reached you. Ungrateful stream, why do you blaspheme the fountain? Unless it flowed, you would not be filled. But I fear lest you have dried up; for every stream cut off from the fountain must necessarily dry up. From dryness they speak harsh things against the Church; they would speak gentle things, if they were watered. I am a Catholic. What is, "Catholic"? A man from Numidia? I ask at least the Greeks. "Catholic" is surely not a Punic word, but a Greek word; seek an interpreter. You err deservedly in language, you who do not agree with all languages.

**11.** When the Spirit came from heaven and filled those who had believed in Christ, they spoke in all tongues; and this was the sign, at that time, of having received the Holy Spirit, if anyone spoke in the tongues of all. Is the Holy Spirit not now given to the faithful? Far be it that we believe this, otherwise we will have no hope. And they too surely confess that the Holy Spirit is given to the faithful; and we say this, we believe this, we assent that this happens most of all and only in the Catholic Church. But let them be Catholics, there the Holy Spirit is given; let us be Catholics, here the Holy Spirit is given; now let us not ask what difference there is, who are Catholics; it is manifest that the Holy Spirit is given. Why do those who receive the Holy Spirit not now speak in the tongues of all, except because then in a few was figured what would afterward be shown in all? For what did the Holy Spirit foretell, moving the hearts of those whom it then filled, and teaching them all tongues? Scarcely does a man learn two or three, either through teachers, or through the regions in which he dwells, by some habit; at most three or four tongues. They spoke in all tongues, who were filled with the Holy Spirit; and indeed they spoke suddenly, not learning them gradually. What, then, did the Spirit then show? Tell me, why does it not now do this, except because by signifying something it did it? What did it signify, but that the Gospel would be in all tongues? I dare to say, the Church too now speaks in all tongues; for in all tongues it cries out the Gospel; and what I was just now saying about the members, this I say of the tongues too. And just as the eye says: The foot walks for me, so the foot too says: The eye sees for me; so I too say: My tongue is Greek, my tongue is Hebrew, my tongue is Syriac; for one faith holds all, for one framework of love encloses all. What was demonstrated through the Lord, this was foretold before through the Prophets: *Their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^19]. Behold how far the Church has grown, which is called Catholic from the whole. And see that all tongues went through all lands: *There are no speeches nor words whose voices are not heard*[^20].

**12.** This Church, then, I hold, you do not hold it; if, therefore, you are cut off, acknowledge whence you are cut off. Return, and be grafted in, lest you wither and be cast into the fire. The Prophets speak, the Apostles speak, the Lord speaks of the Church spread throughout the whole world: against you all these pronounce sentence. From the proconsul to the imperial judgment: from the Gospel, to where? Or perhaps to Donatus? Will Donatus judge against Christ, or does Christ judge Donatus? What will Donatus say to you? I preached my Christ from Africa. What will he say? Or: I set myself in Christ's place, and I succeeded Christ? It remains for him to say this, because he dared to cut men off from the body, because he succeeded Christ. Behold the sentence of Christ, behold the Gospels. *Through all nations*, he says, *beginning from Jerusalem*. It began from Jerusalem: there the Holy Spirit came; there the apostles were, when it came upon them; thence the Gospel began to be preached, thence to be spread through all nations, thence afterward it came to Africa. To where it came afterward, did it forsake them? Rather it did not forsake, if they are not unwilling. For we too are Africans; surely the Gospel which came to Africa remains here among Catholic Africans, just as it remains among all nations. For through all nations too there are heretics, some there, some here; and the Africans who are among those nations are not known. They were cut off from the vine. For the Catholic Church knows all; they themselves do not know themselves. For the vine, from which the shoots are cut off, knows all the shoots, both those that remain in it and those that are cut off from it. For the Catholic Church is spread everywhere. Those shoots, where they were cut off, there they remained; they could not reach other parts and still other parts. But that Church, spread everywhere, everywhere holds its own, everywhere laments the cut-off; it cries to all, that they return and be grafted in. Its cry is not heard, but yet the breasts of love do not cease to flow with exhortation. It is anxious for the cut-off; it cries in Africa to the Donatists, it cries in the East against the Arians, against the Photinians, against others and still others. For because it is spread everywhere, everywhere it finds those against whom to cry; because they were in it, and were cut off from it. The shoots began to be fruitless, and were cut off; if they do not remain in unbelief, they will again be grafted in. Hear these things, brothers, with fear, that you not be proud; with love, that you may even pray for them. Let us turn to the Lord.
