# Sermon 265/D: A Discourse on the Fortieth Day, the Ascension of the Lord

**1.** We heard, when the holy Gospel was read, and marveling we believed, and believing we marveled, that the Lord appeared after the resurrection of the dead, that he offered himself as a lesson to those who would die, an example to those who would rise. He appeared to those who despaired, who, when they were terrified, *thought they were seeing a spirit*[^1]. There is a malicious heresy, which even today thinks this, which the disciples then thought: the Manichees say that the Lord Christ was a spirit, not a body, and that all that was done was in a figure of a body, and that he appeared by movements of members rather than was. These let us address a little, with you bearing with it; because perhaps they too lie hidden among you, let not this opportunity of the reading pass them by.

**2.** What do you say, whoever you are, Manichee, what do you say? Christ, he says, was a spirit, did not have flesh, but appeared as if flesh. I admit it for the moment, I admit the contradictor, that I may make him, if I can, a believer. This surely you say, that Christ was seen to be a spirit, not a body? This, you say. This, I say, the disciples too thought beforehand. I am not, then, greatly angry that you so erred: but you are plainly to be condemned, because, when they were corrected, you remained in the error. Christ was a spirit, did not have flesh? I have admitted the contradictor, hear the teacher; hear, I say, the teacher, not me, but himself. Go, and say, and boast, and preach, and teach, and penetrate houses, and lead captive *little women laden with sins*[^2]; do it, and say: Christ was a spirit, did not have flesh and bones. Hear him saying: *Why are you troubled, and why do thoughts arise in your heart? See my hands and my feet; handle and see, that a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have*[^3]. Why do you contradict? Are you a Christian? If you are a Christian, hear Christ saying: *Why do thoughts arise in your heart? See my hands and my feet; handle and see, that a spirit*—that is, what you think me to be—*does not have flesh and bones, as you see me to have.* Do you still contradict? If you still contradict, see whether perhaps it is no evil to think Christ was a spirit, even if he had true flesh. If it were no evil, the Lord would have left his disciples in that very error. Do not despise the wound which such a physician took care to heal; if those thoughts, as it were thorns, did not harm the Lord's field, the farmer would not uproot them with diligent hand. But the disciples were corrected, the Manichees were perverted; that thought made a passage through the disciples' hearts as a foreigner; it possessed the hearts of the Manichees as a mistress, because it invaded as an enemy.

**3.** Let it be cured, brothers, if anyone perhaps doubts about it; let him hear the truth, lay aside contention. Christ, the Word, soul, and flesh. Any man, soul and flesh: Christ, Word and man. If Word and man, Word and soul and flesh. They are not three persons, the Word and soul and flesh; because you too are not two, soul and flesh. You, soul and flesh, are one man; he, Word soul and flesh, is one Christ. But sometimes he speaks according to that which is the Word, and yet Christ himself speaks; sometimes he speaks according to that which is soul, and yet Christ himself speaks; sometimes he speaks according to that which is flesh, and yet Christ himself speaks. Let us prove these things by examples of the divine words. According to the Word hear: *I and the Father are one*[^4]. According to the soul hear: *My soul is sorrowful unto death*[^5]. According to the flesh hear: It was fitting for Christ to suffer, and to rise on the third day. Where to rise, except in that which could fall? There he rose, where he died. Seek death in the Word: it could never be. Seek death in the soul: it never was, where there was no sin. Seek death in the flesh: there plainly it was; and therefore there was a true resurrection, because there was a true death. There death was. Why was it, where there was no sin? There was punishment without fault, that in us both fault and punishment might be loosed.

**4.** Why do you marvel that Christ died, when Christ utterly did not sin? He wished to pay for you what he did not owe, that he might free you from the debt. By right the devil possessed the deceived human race; he possessed what he had captured, he had captured what he had deceived. Christ brought in mortal flesh blood to be poured out, by which the bond of sins might be blotted out. He would still hold the guilty, if he did not kill the innocent. But now see how justly it is said to him: You killed one who owed nothing, give back the debtors. *Behold*, he says, *the prince of this world comes, and in me he will find nothing.* How nothing? Do you not have a soul, do you not have flesh? are you not also the Word? Are all these nothing? Far be it. Nothing of his own, because no sin. He is the prince of sinners: the prince of sinners *will find nothing in me.* I did not sin, I drew nothing from Adam, who came to you from a virgin. I added nothing, because I had no one to whom to add, and by living justly I committed nothing. Let him come, and, if he can, let him find something of his own in me. But he *will find* nothing of his own *in me*; I have no sin: born innocent, I led an innocent life. Let him come, *he will find nothing.* Why, then, do you die, if he will come, and *will find nothing?* And he gives the reason why he dies: *Behold, the prince of the world will come, and in me he will find nothing.* And as though we said: Why, then, do you die? he answered: *But that all may know*, he says, *that I do the will of my Father, rise, let us go from here*[^6], to the passion, because of the will of the good Father, not because of the debt of the evil prince.

**5.** Why, then, do you marvel? Surely Christ is life: why did life die? Neither did the soul die, nor did the Word die; the flesh died, that in it death might die. Suffering death, he killed death; he set bait for the lion in a snare. A fish, if it wished to devour nothing, would not be caught on the hook. The devil was eager for death, the devil was greedy for death. The cross of Christ was a mousetrap: the death of Christ, rather the mortal flesh of Christ, was as it were the bait in the mousetrap. He came, he drank it in, and was caught. See, Christ has risen: where is death? Now in his flesh is said what will be said in ours at the end: *Death is swallowed up in victory*[^7]. It was flesh, but there was no corruption. The nature remaining, the quality is changed: the same substance, but no failing there now, no sluggishness, no corruption, no need, nothing mortal, nothing such as we are wont to know as earthly. He was touched, handled, felt, but was not killed.

**6.** Hear still more. He ascended into heaven, he was escorted by the eyes of the disciples; he sends them gazing, makes them witnesses. It is said to them: *Why do you stand? This Jesus, who has been taken up from you, will so come.* What is, So? So, in that form, in that flesh: *They will see him whom they pierced*[^8]. *He will so come, as you saw him going into heaven*[^9]. Surely they saw, surely they touched, they handled: they strengthened their faith both by gazing and by touching: they escorted with their gaze the one ascending into heaven: with attentive ears they heard the witnessing voice of the angel foretelling Christ to come. Yet, now all these things being completed in them, that they might become witnesses of Christ, and bravely endure all things for the preaching of the truth, and contend against falsehood even to blood, neither that vision alone, nor that handling of the Lord's members, granted it. But who granted them this? Hear the Lord himself: *But you, sit in the city, until you are clothed with power from on high*[^10]. You have seen, and have touched; but you cannot yet preach and die for what you have seen and touched, until *you are clothed with power from on high.* Let men now go, and ascribe to their own strength, if they can do anything. He was Peter, and was not yet established on the rock; he was not yet clothed with power from on high; because *no one can receive, unless it has been given him from heaven*[^11].

**7.** This, then, brothers, let the truth itself persuade us; let no one glory in his own strength, let no one be lifted up over the choice of his will. For sinning you are fit alone: for doing right you need a helper. Say: *Be my helper, do not forsake me*[^12]; woe to you, if he forsakes you. When he leaves you to yourself, to whom does he leave you but to a man? Do you not, then, dread, when you hear: *Cursed is everyone who puts his hope in a man*[^13]? See, the Lord Christ, as I said, Word soul and flesh; there God, there you too; and one Christ. From where there you? By what merit, by what free choice did the Lord take up human nature, was the Word clothed with human nature? What merit of that human nature itself went before? Or will you perhaps say that, I know not where, Christ lived well, and by living well merited to be taken up by the Word, and to become one with the Word, and to be born of a virgin? Far be it, far be it: take this away from the minds of Christians, O Lord our God. We see him *as the only-begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth*[^14]. For the Word had no means by which to die for you: it was fitting for Christ to die for you, and in the Word there was no means by which to die for you; because, a simple life, without flesh and blood, without any changeability, *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^15]. How far from death! Therefore what mercy! Mary surely of the human race: a virgin, but a human being; holy, but a human being. But the Lord, the only-begotten Word, took up for you, what he might offer for you. But he took up for you, only from you; because the means by which to die for you, he did not have in himself. Neither did you have the means by which to live, nor he the means by which to die. O great exchange! Live from his, because he died from yours.
