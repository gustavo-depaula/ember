# Sermon 341: On the Fact That Christ Is Understood in Three Ways in Scripture

Against the Arians

**1. 1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ, brothers, as far as we have been able to observe the sacred pages, is understood and named in three ways when he is proclaimed, whether through the Law and the Prophets, or through the apostolic letters, or through faith in the deeds that we come to know in the Gospel. The first way is according to God and that divinity coequal and coeternal with the Father before the assumption of the flesh. The second way is when, the flesh having been assumed, he is now both the same God who is man and the same man who is God, according to a certain property of his excellence by which he is not made equal to other men but is the mediator and head of the Church—so he is read of and understood. The third way is, in a certain manner, the whole Christ in the fullness of the Church, that is, head and body, according to the fullness of a certain perfect man, in which man we are individual members. This is proclaimed to believers and offered to the prudent to be recognized. We cannot recall or explain in so short a time all the testimonies of the Scriptures—they are so many—by which we might prove all these three kinds; yet we cannot leave them all unproven, so that, with certain testimonies brought to mind, the rest, which we are not permitted to mention because of the constraints of time, you may now be able to observe and find for yourselves in the Scriptures.

**2. 2.** To the first way of presenting our Lord Jesus Christ the Savior, the only Son of God, through whom all things were made, belongs that most noble and most splendid passage in the Gospel according to John: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; the same was in the beginning with God; all things were made through him, and without him was nothing made. What was made, in him was life; and the life was the light of men, and the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not comprehended it*[^1]. These words are wonderful and astonishing, and before they are understood, they are to be embraced. If food were set before your mouth, one person would take this part of the food, another that part; yet one food would reach all, but not the whole food to all. So now a kind of food and drink of words is set before your ears; and yet the whole reaches all. Or perhaps, while I speak, does one person take one syllable for himself, another the other? Or one person one word, another a different word? If that were so, I would have to speak as many words as there are people I see, so that single words might reach all. And indeed I easily say more words than there are people here; yet all reach all. Is the word of a man, then, not divided into syllables so that all may hear it—while the Word of God is cut into pieces so as to be everywhere? Do we think, brothers, that these sounding and passing words are in any respect to be compared to that Word which remains unchangeably? Or have I, because I have said these things, compared them? But in whatever way I wished to convey it to you, so that what God displays in corporeal things might help you toward believing the things you do not yet see concerning spiritual words. But let us now pass on to better things. For words sound and pass away. Concerning spiritual realities, think of justice. When one person thinking of justice is set in these western regions, and another thinking of justice is set in the East, how does it come about that the one thinks of the whole of justice, and the other of the whole? That the one sees the whole, and the other the whole? For whoever sees justice, according to which he does something, himself acts justly. He sees within, he acts without. From where does he see within, if nothing is present to him? Because he is himself set in one place, will another's thought not reach that same place? But when you, set here, see with your mind the same thing that one set so far away sees, and the whole shines for you and the whole appears to him—because the things that are divine and incorporeal are everywhere whole—believe that the Word is whole in the Father, whole in the womb. Believe this of the Word of God, who is God with God.

**3. 3.** But hear now another presentation, another way of presenting Christ which Scripture proclaims. For the things I have said were said before the flesh was assumed. But now hear what Scripture now proclaims: *The Word*, it says, *was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^2]. For he who had said, *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; the same was in the beginning with God: all things were made through him, and without him was nothing made*[^3], would proclaim to us the divinity of the Word in vain if he kept silent about the humanity of the Word. For in order that I may see that, this is done with me here; in order that he may purify me to contemplate that, he himself comes to the aid of my weakness. By taking from human nature human nature itself, he was made man. He came with the beast of the flesh to the one who lay wounded on the road[^4], so that he might shape and nourish our little faith by the mystery of his incarnation, and might make our understanding serene to see what he never lost through what he took up. For he began to be man; he did not cease to be God. This, then, is the proclamation of our Lord Jesus Christ insofar as he is the mediator, insofar as he is the head of the Church: that God is man and man is God, when John says, *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^5].

**3. 4.** Hear now both in that very well-known passage of the apostle Paul: *Who, though he was in the form of God*, he says, *did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*[^6]. This is: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^7]. How did the Apostle say, *He did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*, if he is not equal to God? But if the Father is God and he is not God, how is he equal? Where, then, that one says, *The Word was God*, there this one says, *He did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*. And where that one says, *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*, there this one says, *But he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^8]. Pay attention: through this, then, that he was made man, through this that *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*, through this *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*. For what did he empty? Not so as to lose his divinity, but so as to put on humanity, appearing to men as what he was not before he was man. So by appearing he emptied himself, that is, keeping back the dignity of his majesty and offering the flesh as the garment of humanity. Through this, then, that *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant* (not taking the form of God; for when speaking of the form of God, he did not say he took it, but, *Though he was established in the form of God, he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*; but when it came to the form of a servant, *Taking*, he says, *the form of a servant*)—through this, then, he is the mediator and head of the Church, through whom we are reconciled to God, through the mystery of his humility and passion and resurrection and ascension and the judgment to come, so that those two future things may be heard, since God has spoken once[^9]. Where are the two heard? Where he renders *to each according to his works*[^10].

**4. 5.** Holding this, then, do not be amazed at the questions of men, which spread like a cancer, as the Apostle said[^11]; but guard your ears and the virginity of your mind, as those betrothed by the friend of the bridegroom to present a chaste virgin to the one husband, Christ. For your virginity is in the mind. Bodily virginity is in few in the Church; virginity of mind ought to be in all the faithful. The serpent wishes to corrupt this virginity, of which the same Apostle says: *I betrothed you to one husband, to present you as a chaste virgin to Christ. And I fear lest, as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, so also your senses may be corrupted and fall away from the chastity that is in Christ Jesus*[^12]. *Your senses*, he says, that is, your minds. For this is the more proper meaning. For the senses are understood also of this body—of seeing, hearing, smelling, tasting, touching. The Apostle feared that our minds would be corrupted, where the virginity of faith is. So now go forward, O soul, guard your virginity, to be made fruitful afterward in the embrace of your bridegroom. Hedge your ears, then, as it is written, with thorns[^13]. The question of the Arians troubled the weak brothers of the Church; but in the Lord's mercy the catholic faith prevailed. For he did not abandon his Church; and even if for a time he troubled it, he troubled it for this reason, that it might always make supplication to him by whom it would be confirmed upon the solid rock. And still the serpent murmurs and does not keep silent. By a certain promise of knowledge he seeks to cast down from the paradise of the Church the one whom he does not permit to return to that paradise from which the first man was cast down.

**5. 6.** Attend, brothers. What was done in that paradise is done in the Church. Let no one seduce us from this paradise. Let it be enough that we fell from there; let us, having had the experience, at least be corrected. He is the serpent who always suggests iniquity and impiety. He sometimes promises impunity, just as he promised there too, saying, *Shall you indeed die?*[^14]. He suggests such things so that Christians may now live badly. Will God, he says, destroy them all? Will God condemn them all? He says: I will condemn; I will pardon those who have changed themselves; let them change their deeds, and I change my threats. He, then, is the one who murmurs and mutters and says: Behold where it is written, *The Father is greater than I*[^15]; and you say he is equal to the Father? I accept what you say, but I accept both, because I read both. Why do you accept the one and not want the other? For you have read both with me. Behold, *The Father is greater than I*; I accept it, not from you but from the Gospel; and accept from the Apostle that the Son is equal to God the Father. Join both, let both agree; because he who spoke through John in the Gospel, he himself spoke through Paul in the Letter. He cannot be at odds with himself, but you do not want to understand the harmony of the Scriptures, since you yourself love to quarrel. But, he says, I prove from the Gospel: *The Father is greater than I*. And I prove from the Gospel: *I and the Father are one*[^16]. How is each true? As the Apostle teaches us: *I and the Father are one. Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*. Hear: *The Father is greater than I: but he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^17]. Behold, I show why he is greater; you show in what he is not equal. For we read both. He is less than the Father insofar as he is the Son of man; equal to the Father insofar as he is the Son of God; because *the Word was God*. The mediator, God and man; God equal to the Father, man less than the Father. He is, then, both equal and less: equal in the form of God, less in the form of a servant. So tell me, from where is he equal and less? Is he equal in one part and less in another part? Behold, apart from the assumption of the flesh, show me him as equal and as less. From where you are going to demonstrate it, I wish to see.

**6. 7.** Attend to foolish impiety, which is wise according to the flesh, as it is written: *To be wise according to the flesh is death*[^18]. Stop here. I still set aside, and I do not yet speak of the incarnation of our Lord Jesus Christ, the only Son of God; but as though what has already been done were not yet done, I attend with you: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; the same was in the beginning with God*[^19]. I attend with you: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*[^20]. There show me both the greater and the lesser. What will you say? Will you divide God by qualities, that is, by certain corporeal or animal affections, in which we perceive one thing to be different from another? I can indeed say so naturally; but whether you too understand it so, God will see. So, as I had begun to say, before the assumption of the flesh, before the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, show me the lesser, show me the equal. Is God one thing and another, so that on one side the Son is less than him, and on another side equal to him? As if we should say: There are certain bodies; you can say to me: It is equal in length, but it is less in strength. For very often two such bodies are found, that they are equal in stature of length, but in strength one is less, the other greater. Are we, then, going to think of God and his Son as such bodies? Are we going to think of him who was wholly in Mary, wholly with the Father, wholly in the flesh, wholly above the angels? May God turn such thoughts away from the hearts of Christians! Again, perhaps you will think this, so as to say: Equal in both strength and length, but they are unlike in color. Where is there color except in corporeal things? But there is the light of wisdom. Show me the color of justice. If these things have no color, you would not say those things of God, if you had the color of shame.

**6. 8.** What, then, will you say? Are they equal in power, but the Son less in prudence? God is unjust, if he gave equal power to a lesser prudence. If they are equal in prudence, but the Son is less in power, God is envious, who gave a lesser power to an equal prudence. But in God everything that is said is one and the same. For in God power is not one thing and prudence another, fortitude one thing and justice or chastity another. Whatever of these you say of God, it is not understood as one thing and another, and nothing is said worthily; because these belong to souls, which that light in a certain way floods and affects according to their qualities; just as when this visible light rises upon bodies. If it is taken away, there is one color for all bodies—which should rather be called no color. But when, brought in, it has illumined the bodies, although it is itself of one kind, yet according to the diverse qualities of the bodies it sprinkles them with a diverse brightness. These affections, then, belong to souls, which are well affected by that light which is not affected, and are formed by that which is not formed.

**7. 9.** Yet we say these things of God, brothers, because we find nothing better to say. I call God just, because in human words I find nothing better; for he is beyond justice. It is said in the Scriptures: *The Lord is just, and he has loved acts of justice*[^21]. But there it is also said that God repents[^22], there it is also said that God does not know[^23]. Who would not shudder? Does God not know, does God repent? Yet for this reason Scripture has wholesomely descended even to those words which you shudder at, lest you should think that those things which you consider great are said worthily. And so, if you ask: What, then, is said worthily of God? Someone perhaps may answer you and say that he is just. But another, understanding better than this one, may say that even this word is surpassed by his excellence, and that even this is said unworthily of him, although it is said fittingly according to human capacity; so that when that man wishes to prove from the Scriptures, because it is written, *The Lord is just*, he is rightly answered that it is set down in the same Scriptures that God repents; so that, just as he does not take this according to the custom of those who speak, as men are wont to repent, so let him also understand that what is called just does not befit his pre-eminence; although Scripture has set this down well, so that through whatever words the mind may be led step by step to that which cannot be spoken. You call God just indeed: but understand something beyond justice, something other than you are accustomed to think even of a man. But the Scriptures called him just, and for that reason they also called him repenting and unknowing, which you no longer wish to say. As, then, you understand those things which you now shudder at as said because of your weakness, so also these things which you esteem highly were said for the sake of some stronger firmness. But whoever transcends even these and begins to think of God worthily, as much as is granted to a man, will find a silence to be praised with the heart's ineffable voice.

**8. 10.** Therefore, brothers, because in the Lord virtue is the same as justice (whatever you say of him, you say this same thing, while yet you say nothing worthily), you cannot say that the Son is equal to the Father in justice and not equal in virtue, or equal in virtue and not equal in knowledge; because if he is equal in any one thing, he is equal in every thing; because all the things you say there are the same and are worth the same. It is enough, then, that you cannot say how the Son is unequal to the Father, unless you posit certain diversities in the substance of God. And when you have posited them, the truth casts you out, and you do not approach that sanctuary of God where he is seen most purely. But since you cannot call him equal on one side and unequal on another, because there are no parts in God; nor can you say he is equal on one side, less on another, because there are no qualities in God; according to God you cannot call him equal except equal in every way; from where, then, can you call him less, except because he took the form of a servant? And so, brothers, always observe this. If you have received a certain rule in the Scriptures, the light itself will show you all things. Wherever you find the Son called equal to the Father, take it according to his divinity. But according to the form of a servant which he assumed, take him as less; according to what is said, *I am who I am*[^24]; and according to what is said, *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*[^25]; so you will hold both what is in his nature and what is in his mercy. I think enough has been said about that way too, in which our Lord Jesus Christ our Savior, made the head of the Church, the mediator through whom we are reconciled to God, is presented in the Scriptures as God and man.

**9. 11.** The third way is how the whole Christ is proclaimed according to the Church, that is, head and body. For head and body are one Christ: not because he is not complete without the body, but because he has deigned to be complete with us also, who is also always complete without us, not only in that he is the Word, the only-begotten Son equal to the Father, but also in the very man whom he took up, and with whom he is at once God and man. Nevertheless, brothers, in what way are we his body, and one Christ with us? Where do we find this, that head and body are one Christ, that is, the body with its head? The bride speaks as though singly with her bridegroom in Isaiah: certainly one and the same speaks; and see what he says: *As on a bridegroom he has set a crown upon me, and as a bride he has clothed me with ornament*[^26]. As bridegroom and bride: he calls the same one bridegroom according to the head, bride according to the body. Two are seen, and he is one. Otherwise, how are we members of Christ? The Apostle saying most plainly: *You are the body of Christ and his members*[^27]. We are all together the members and the body of Christ; not only we who are in this place, but also throughout the whole earth; nor only we who are at this time, but—what shall I say? From righteous Abel down to the end of the age, as long as men beget and are begotten, whoever of the righteous makes his passage through this life, whoever is now (that is, not in this place, but in this life), whatever there will be of those born hereafter—all this is one body of Christ; but individually they are members of Christ. If, then, all are the body, and individually members, there is surely a head whose body this is. *And he is*, it says, *the head of the body, the Church, the firstborn, himself holding the primacy*[^28]. And because it also says of him that he is always *the head of every principality and power*[^29], this Church, which is now on pilgrimage, is joined to that heavenly Church, where we have the angels as fellow citizens; to whom we should shamelessly claim that we are going to be equal after the resurrection of bodies, had not the Truth promised, saying, *They shall be equal to the angels of God*[^30]; and one Church is made, the city of the great King.

**10. 12.** So, then, Christ is sometimes presented in the Scriptures in such a way that you understand the Word equal to the Father. Sometimes in such a way that you understand the mediator, when *the Word was made flesh*, to dwell *among us*[^31]; when that Only-begotten through whom all things were made *did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, becoming obedient unto death, even death on a cross*[^32]. But sometimes in such a way that you understand head and body, with the Apostle himself most plainly expounding what was said of man and wife in Genesis: *They shall be*, it says, *two in one flesh*[^33]. See him expounding it himself, lest we seem to dare to say anything by our own conjectures. *For they shall be*, he says, *two in one flesh*; and he added: *This is a great mystery*. And lest anyone should still think it was about a husband and wife according to the natural coupling of the two sexes and bodily mixture: *But I say*, he says, *in Christ and the Church*[^34]. According to this, then, that what was said is taken in Christ and the Church—*They shall be two in one flesh; now no longer two, but one flesh*. And as bridegroom and bride, so head and body: because the head of the woman is the man. Whether, then, I say head and body, or say bridegroom and bride, understand one thing. And therefore the same Apostle, when he was still Saul, heard: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^35], since the body is joined to the head. And when, now a preacher of Christ, he suffered from others what he as a persecutor had done: *That I may fill up*, he says, *what is lacking of the afflictions of Christ in my flesh*[^36]; showing that what he suffered belonged to the afflictions of Christ. This cannot be understood according to the head, which now in heaven suffers nothing of the kind; but according to the body, that is, the Church; which body, with its head, is one Christ.

**11. 13.** Show yourselves, then, a body worthy of such a head, a bride worthy of such a bridegroom. That head cannot have any but a worthy body; nor does that great man marry any but a worthy wife. *That he might present to himself*, it says, *a glorious Church, having no spot or wrinkle or any such thing*[^37]. This is the bride of Christ, having no spot or wrinkle. Do you not wish to have a spot? Do what is written: *Wash yourselves, be clean, take away your wickedness from your hearts*[^38]. Do you not wish to have a wrinkle? Be stretched out on the cross. For it is not enough only that you be washed, but also that you be stretched out, that you may be without spot or wrinkle. For through the washing sins are taken away; through the stretching-out comes the desire for the age to come, on account of which Christ was crucified. Hear Paul himself, washed: *Not*, he says, *by works of justice which we have done, but according to his mercy he has saved us, through the washing of regeneration*[^39]. Hear the same one stretched out: *Forgetting the things that are behind*, he says, *stretched out toward the things that are ahead, I follow with intent toward the prize of the heavenly calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^40].
