# Sermon 64/A: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 10:16: "Behold, I Send You as Sheep," and What Follows; on the Birthday of the Martyrs

**1.** The solemnity of the martyrs, dearly beloved, by which we celebrate the memorial of their passion, is set before us for imitation, so that, if perhaps any harsh temptation befall, we may persevere to the end, that we may be able to be saved, as just now we heard read together from the Gospel: *He who perseveres to the end, he will be saved*[^1]. And the end of this age perhaps is far off, perhaps is near; but the Lord willed it to be hidden when it is to be, so that men, always ready, may await what they do not know when it is to come. But that end of the age, as I said, whether near or far, the end of each individual man, by which he is compelled to pass from this life into another according to his merit, cannot be far off, on account of the brevity of this mortality. For his own end surely each of us ought to prepare himself: for the last day will in no way harm one who, thinking of every day as the last, so lives that he dies secure; provided that he so dies, that he not die forever. Thinking of these things, the holy martyrs (how they heard the voice of the Lord saying: *Behold, I send you like sheep into the midst of wolves!*) with what firmness were they strengthened, that they did not fear this! For from this it appears how many were the wolves and how few the sheep, because not wolves were sent into the midst of sheep, but sheep into the midst of wolves. For the Lord did not say: Behold, I send you like lions into the midst of beasts of burden; but, saying sheep into the midst of wolves, he sufficiently shows the fewness of the sheep, the flocks of wolves. And whereas one wolf is wont to disturb a flock however large, the sheep that were sent into the midst of innumerable wolves went, and did not fear; because he who sent them did not abandon them. For why should they fear to go among wolves, with whom was the lamb, who conquered the wolf?

**2.** For in that reading we heard: *When they hand you over, do not think what you are to say; for it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you*[^2]. Whence he says in another place: *Behold, I am with you even to the consummation of the age*[^3]. Were those who then heard this voice of the Lord going to be here even to the consummation of the age? But the Lord regarded not only that those would pass away, but also that the rest, and we ourselves, and those who will be after us in this life, would succeed them; and he saw all in his one body. This voice, then, by which he says: *I am with you even to the consummation of the age*, not only they, but we too have heard; and if then we did not hear in our own knowledge, we heard in his foreknowledge. Wherefore, that secure, as sheep among wolves, we may hold the precepts of him who admonishes us to be *simple as doves, cunning as serpents*[^4]: simple as doves, that we harm no one; cunning as serpents, that we beware lest we be harmed. But you cannot beware lest you be harmed, unless you have understood in what you may be harmed. For there are those who fight it out, resisting for the sake of temporal things: and when you reprove them for resisting so greatly, when they ought rather, as the Lord himself commanded[^5], not to resist evil, they answer that they are doing what was said: *Cunning as serpents.* Let them attend, then, to what the serpent does, how it exposes the coils of its body to the blows of those striking, for the sake of its head, that it may guard that in which it feels it has its life; how it despises the rest, in which it is long, lest its head be cut off by the pursuer. So if you wish to imitate the cunning of the serpent, guard your head. But it is written: *The head of the man is Christ*[^6]. See, then, where you have Christ, because Christ dwells in you through faith: *That Christ dwell*, he says, *through faith in your hearts*[^7]. So that your faith may remain whole, set the rest against the persecutor, that what you live by may persevere unharmed. For Christ himself the Savior, the head of the whole Church, our Lord, sitting at the right hand of the Father, can no longer be struck by persecutors; yet, having compassion on us, and showing himself to be in us, he cried out from heaven to that Saul, who afterward became the apostle Paul: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^8] And no one was touching him; but for his members trodden underfoot on earth, as their head he cried out from above. But if Christ dwells through faith in the Christian heart, then, that the faith itself may be safe, that is, that Christ may remain in the believer, whatever the persecutor can strike or take away must be despised, that those things rather may perish for the faith, not the faith for those things.

**3.** Imitating this cunning of the serpent, since the head of the man is Christ[^9], the martyrs set against the persecutors, for Christ as for their head, whatever they possessed mortally, lest they die there where they lived. They held this precept of the Lord admonishing them to be cunning as serpents; lest, when they were commanded to be beheaded, they should then believe they were losing their head, but, the head of the flesh cut off, should keep their head, Christ, whole. For with whatever savagery the torturer assails the members of the body, with whatever cruelty, the sides plowed and the entrails torn apart, he reaches to all the inner parts of the flesh, he cannot reach to our head, which he is not even permitted to see. He reaches it indeed, if he wishes, not by raging against us, but by believing what we believe. But how could women, that they might merit the crown of martyrdom, imitate this cunning of the serpent? For Christ was called the head of the man, but the head of the woman is the man[^10]. For they did not suffer for their husbands, when, that they might suffer, they overcame even the blandishments of their husbands calling them back. Because they too through that same faith are members of the Church; and through this Christ, who is the head of the whole Church, is the head of all his members. So the whole Church is called both woman and man: for it has been called a single virgin. The Apostle says: *I have fitted you to present you as a chaste virgin to one husband, Christ*[^11]. And it is understood as a man, when the same Apostle says: *Until we all attain to the unity of faith, to the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the age of the fullness of Christ*[^12]. If, then, it is a woman, its husband is Christ: if it is a man, its head is Christ. Since, then, the head of the woman is the man, and the husband of the Church is Christ, when even women suffered for Christ, they fought by the cunning of the serpent for their head. Let us, then, guard against the persecutors our head, let us imitate the cunning of the serpent; and for our very persecutors let us groan to God, that we may also keep the innocence of doves.
