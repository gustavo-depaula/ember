# Sermon 216: On the Giving Back of the Creed

**1.** The first beginnings of our ministry, and your conception, by which you begin to be begotten in the womb of faith by heavenly grace, must be aided by the mouth: that both our sermon may address you wholesomely, and your conception may usefully console us. We instruct by sermons, do you advance by manners. We scatter the sermon of the word, do you render the fruit of faith. Let us all, according to the calling by which we have been called by the Lord, run in his way and path; let no one look back. For Truth, which neither deceives nor can deceive, openly declares: *No one who puts his hand to the plow and looks back will be fit for the kingdom of heaven*[^1]. That you desire this, that you strive toward this with all the efforts of your mind, your very name, by which you are called "competentes" (those who seek together), shows. For what else are "competentes," but "those seeking together" (simul petentes)? For as "condocentes," "concurrentes," "considentes" sound nothing other than teaching together, running together, sitting together; so too the word "Competentes" is composed from nothing other than seeking together and striving for some one thing. And what is that one thing which you seek or desire, except what someone, his fleshly desires cast away and the terrors of the age overcome, cries fearlessly? *If camps stand against me, my heart will not fear; if war rise up against me, in this I will hope.* And, expressing what that is, he added and said: *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this I will seek; that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life.* And, explaining the blessedness of this region and dwelling, he joins and expresses: *That I may behold the delight of the Lord, and be protected by his temple*[^2].

**2.** You see, my recruits, to what delight of the Lord you will come, when you cast away the delight of the age? If you despise the world, you will have a clean heart, and you will see him who made the world: and as he conquered[^3], so you too in his grace will conquer this world. Which surely you will soon overcome and trample, if you presume not on your own strength, but on the help of the most merciful God. Do not despise yourselves, because it has not yet appeared what you will be. But know that, when it has appeared, you will be like him[^4], when what you will be has appeared. But know that you will see him, not such as he came to us in the fullness of time, but such as, ever remaining, he created us. Strip off the old man, that you may put on the new[^5]. The Lord begins a pact with you. You have lived for the age, you have given yourselves to flesh and blood, you have borne the image of the earthly man. As, then, you have borne the image of the one who is of the earth, so also from now on bear that of the one who is of heaven[^6]. It is a human manner of speaking, since therefore the Word was made flesh, that, *as you presented your bodies as weapons of iniquity to sin, so now you may present your members as weapons of righteousness to God*[^7]. For your destruction your assailant was armed against you with your own javelins: for your salvation, let your protector in turn be armed against him with your members. He will harm you nothing, if, when you take your members from him, he does not hold them. He will rightly leave you, if your vow and will dissent from him.

**3.** Behold, with the auction and merchandise of faith, the kingdom of heaven is offered to you for sale: look upon and gather together the resources of your conscience, gather with one mind the treasures of your heart. And yet you buy freely, if you recognize the welcome grace which is offered to you. You spend nothing, and yet great is what you acquire. Be not cheap to yourselves, you whom the Creator of all things, and yours, esteems so dear, that for you he daily sheds the most precious blood of his Only-begotten. You will not be so cheap if you distinguish the precious from the cheap; if you serve not the creature, but the Creator; if your lower things have not had dominion over you, that, immaculate, you may be cleansed from the capital and great sin[^8]; if in you the seed of the word of God, which even now the heavenly husbandman scatters in the field of your heart, be not either, as on the way, trampled by the passing of the unworthy, or, as among rocky places, by the stolidity of your most hard conscience, already sprouting, choked, or among the thorns of your desires suffocated by pernicious stings[^9]. If you shrink from the sterility of so ruinous and damnable an earth, a fruitful and most rich earth will surely receive you, and with great joy you will render to your sower and waterer a hundredfold multiplied abundance; or if perhaps you are unequal to this, you will pay the interest of a sixtyfold fruit: but if you do not attain even to this, even a thirtyfold yield will not be ungrateful to you; because all will be received into the heavenly granaries, all will be stored away in eternal rest. From the fruits of all that heavenly bread will be made, and every laborer working in the Lord's vineyard without deceit will be abundantly and wholesomely filled by it: while the glory of so great a sower, waterer, and irrigator, and the giver of the increase himself, is spread abroad by the gospel preaching[^10].

**4.** Draw near to him, then, in the crushing of the heart: since he is near to all who have crushed their heart, and he himself will save you, humble in spirit[^11]. Draw near eagerly, that you may be enlightened[^12]. For you are still in darkness, and darkness is in you. But you will be light in the Lord[^13], who *enlightens every man coming into this world*[^14]. You were conformed to the age, be reformed to God. May you at last be wearied of the Babylonian captivity. Behold, Jerusalem, that heavenly mother, comes to meet you on the ways, cheerfully inviting, and beseeches you to be willing to have life and to love to see good days[^15], which you never had, nor will ever have in this age. For there your days were failing like smoke: days for you to be increased and diminished, to grow and fail, to ascend and vanish. You who have lived to sin many and evil years, desire to live to God: not many years to be at some time ended, and running to perish in the shadow of death; but good ones, and near to the truth of a living life; where you will weary with no hunger, no thirst; because your food will be faith, your drink wisdom. For now in the Church you bless the Lord in faith: but then in the sight you will be most abundantly watered from the fountains of Israel.

**5.** But in this pilgrimage meanwhile let your tears be your bread day and night; while, when men say to you daily: *Where is your God?*[^16] you cannot show to the fleshly *what eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man*[^17]. Until you come and appear before the eyes of your God, do not fail. For he himself too will come to pay the promises, who freely professed himself a debtor: who borrowed nothing from anyone, and deigned to be a debtor by his own promise. We owed; and we owed as much as we had sinned. He came without debt, because without any sin; he found us oppressed by a ruinous and damnable usury, and, paying what he had not seized, mercifully freed us from the everlasting debt. We had committed the fault, we awaited the punishment; he, made not a partner of our fault, but made a partaker of the punishment, willed to be at once the forgiver both of fault and of punishment. For he it is who will rescue the souls of believers from usuries and iniquity[^18], and from the heart, in each of those who say: *I believe to see the good things of the Lord in the land of the living*[^19]. This land is to be desired not with an earthly or dead, but with a heavenly and living heart. For it is that of which, in another psalm, someone burning with love of it and cheerfully singing says: *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^20]. To which they tend who vitally mortify their members upon the earth: not the members by which the framework of this world's body consists, but those members by which the manhood of the soul is wretchedly weakened. Most openly enumerating and designating them, Paul the apostle, the Vessel of election, says: *Mortify your members which are upon the earth; fornication, uncleanness, disturbance, evil desire, and covetousness, which is the service of idols*[^21]. Behold what in this land of the dying you ought to mortify, you who desire to live in that land of the living. So let us become members of Christ, that we do not take them and make them members of a harlot[^22]. For what is baser, what is more disgraceful, than that very harlot, which is fornication, placed first, and covetousness last? Which he truly called the service of idols; because not only in the luxury of the body, but also in the flux of the soul, you ought to recognize and avoid it, lest you incur that perdition by the threatening and chaste Bridegroom and just Judge, to whom it is said: *You have destroyed everyone who fornicates away from you*[^23]. How much more justly, how much more usefully, in the chastity of the heart toward him, will each one of you cry: *For me, to cleave to God is good*[^24]. This cleaving that love affords, of which it is likewise said: *Love without dissimulation; hating evil, cleaving to the good*[^25].

**6.** Behold where your stadium is, behold where the wrestling of those contending, behold where the running of those who run, behold where the boxing of those who strike. If you wish to dash down the most pernicious wrestler of the faith with the arms of your strength, lay low the evil, embrace the good. If you wish so to run that you may lay hold, flee the unjust, follow on after the just. If you wish so to box that you do not beat the air, but strike the enemy manfully, chastise your body and bring it into servitude[^26], that, abstaining from all things and lawfully contending[^27], you may triumph as partakers of the heavenly prize and the incorruptible crown. What we do in you by the adjured name of your Redeemer, this complete by the scrutiny and tribulation of your heart. We resist by prayers to God and by rebukes the deceits of the inveterate enemy: do you persist by vows and by the contrition of your heart, that you may be rescued from the power of darkness, and transferred into the kingdom of his glory[^28]. This is now your work, and this your labor. We heap upon him worthy curses for his wickednesses: do you rather, by your turning away and pious renunciation, declare against him a most glorious contest. He is to be ground down, bound, shut out, the enemy of God and yours, and rather of himself. For his fury is shown both insolent against God, and nefarious against you, and pernicious to himself. Let him pant everywhere for slaughter, set snares, sharpen his manifold and deceitful tongues: all his poisons, the name of the Savior being invoked, pour out from your hearts.

**7.** Whatever by most criminal suggestions, whatever by most shameful enticements he sent in, now will be drawn out, now made public. Now his captivity, by which he tyrannically possessed you, will be laid waste. The yoke by which he monstrously pressed you down will be turned away from you, and laid upon his neck: only give your Redeemer your consent for your own liberation. Hope in him, all you assembly of the new people, and you people who are being born, whom the Lord has made, strive that you may be wholesomely brought to birth, lest you be deadly miscarried. Behold the womb of mother Church, behold, that she may bear you and bring you forth into the light of faith, she labors in her groaning. Do not by your impatience shake the maternal bowels and narrow the doors of your birth. People who are being created, praise your God: praise, you who are being created, praise your Lord. Because you are nursed, praise: because you are fed, praise: because you are nourished, advance in wisdom and age. He too admitted these delays of a temporal birth, who neither fails by shortness of time, nor advances by any length of time, but from eternal days excluded all narrownesses and time. Do not here, as a kindly nurse of the little one admonishes, *become children in your minds, but be children in malice, in your minds be perfect*[^29]. As "competentes," competently grow up in Christ, that you may grow up youthfully into the perfect man[^30]. Gladden, as it is written, your father by the advancement of your wisdom, and do not sadden your mother by your failing[^31].

**8.** Love what you will be. For you will be sons of God, and sons of adoption. This will be given you freely, and conferred freely. In which you will more bountifully and abundantly abound, the more grateful you are to him from whom you received these things. Strive toward him, who knows who are his. But then he will not disdain to know you among those who are his, if, naming the name of the Lord, you depart from unrighteousness[^32]. You have, or had, parents of your flesh in the age, who begot you for labor and for punishment and death: but because, by a happier orphanhood, each one of you can say of such ones: *My father and my mother have forsaken me*[^33]; recognize, Christian, that Father who, when those forsook, took you up from the womb of your mother, to whom a certain faithful one faithfully says: *From the womb of my mother you are my protector*[^34]. The Father is God, the mother the Church. Far otherwise will you be begotten by these than you were begotten by those. These births, not labor, not misery, not weeping, not death; but ease, felicity, joy, and life will receive. Through those a lamentable generation; through these a desirable generation. Those, in begetting us, beget us unto eternal punishment, on account of the old fault: these, in regenerating, make neither the punishment nor the fault remain. This is that regeneration *of those who seek him, who seek the face of the God of Jacob*[^35]. Seek humble things: which, when you have found, you will come to a secure height. Your infancy will be innocence, your boyhood reverence, your adolescence patience, your youth virtue, your maturity merit, your old age nothing other than a gray-haired and wise understanding. Through these joints or steps of age, you are not unrolled, but, remaining, you are renewed. For the second will not succeed so that the first falls away, nor will the rise of the third be the perishing of the second, nor is the fourth now born so that the third dies; the fifth will not envy the fourth that it may remain, nor will the sixth bury the fifth. Although these ages do not come at once, yet in a pious and justified soul they persevere equally and concordantly. These will lead you to the seventh, perennial rest and peace. Yet, six times, as we read, freed from the necessities of deadly age, now in the seventh the evils will not touch you[^36]. For those things will not contend which will not be, nor will those prevail which will not even dare. There is secure immortality, there immortal security.

**9.** And whence these things, except from the change of the right hand of the Most High, who will bless your sons in you, who will set your borders as peace[^37]? Be roused, then, to these things, you who are grown together, you who are set apart; grown together with the good, set apart from the evil: the elect, the beloved, the foreknown, the called, those to be justified, those to be glorified[^38]: that, growing, growing young, and growing old in faith and in the maturity of strength, not in the corruption of the members, in a fruitful old age[^39], you may tranquilly announce the works of the Lord, who has done great things for you, who is mighty[^40]: because great is his name, and of his wisdom there is no number[^41]. You seek life; run to him who is the fountain of life: and, the darkness of your smoky desires being put to flight, you will see light in the light of his Only-begotten, your most merciful Redeemer, and most resplendent enlightener. If you seek salvation, hope in him who saves those who hope in him[^42]. If you pursue drunkenness and delights, he will not deny even these. Only come and adore, fall down and weep before him who made you[^43]: and he will make you drunk with the abundance of his house, and give you to drink of the torrent of his delights[^44].

**10.** But beware, lest the foot of pride come upon you; and watch, lest the hands of sinners move you[^45]. First, lest it happen, pray that he cleanse you from your hidden things: second, lest it rush in and cast you down, asking that he spare you from those of others[^46]; lying, rise; rising, stand; standing, stand by; standing by, persist. Bear the yoke no longer: rather break their bonds, and cast their yoke from you[^47], lest again you be held by the yoke of servitude. *The Lord is at hand; be anxious for nothing*[^48]. Eat now the bread of sorrow: the time will come when, after the bread of sadness, the bread of gladness will be ministered to you. But the desert of the latter is the endurance of the former. Your turning away and flight earned the bread of mourning: be converted, be pricked, and return to your Lord. He is ready to bestow the bread of joy on one pricked and returning; if you do not dissemble nor defer to pray, tearfully laden with hardship for your flight. In so great crowds of those troubling you, then, clothe yourselves in sackcloth, and humble your soul in fasting. To humility is rendered what is denied to pride. And you indeed, when you were being scrutinized, and the persuader of that very flight and desertion was duly rebuked in the omnipotence of the dread Trinity, were not clothed in sackcloth: but yet your feet stood mystically in the same.

**11.** The vices and the fleeces of the goats are to be trodden down: the garments of the goats on the left are to be torn apart. With the first robe the merciful Father will come of his own accord to meet you, who, to drive away your pestilent hunger, did not delay to sacrifice even the fattened calf[^49]. On his flesh you will be fed, with his blood you will be given drink: by whose shedding sins are forgiven, debts pardoned, stains wiped away. Eat as the poor, and then you will be satisfied: that you too may be of those of whom it is said: *The poor will eat and be satisfied*[^50]: and, wholesomely satisfied, belch forth his bread and glory. Run to him, and be converted: for he it is who converts the turned-away, pursues the fugitives, finds the lost, humbles the proud, feeds the hungry, looses the fettered, enlightens the blind, purges the unclean, refreshes the wearied, raises the dead, and rescues those possessed and captured by the spirits of wickedness. From whom, because we have now proved you free, rejoicing with you we admonish you, that the health which has appeared in your body may be preserved in your hearts.
