# Sermon 313/D: A Treatise on the Feast of Cyprian

**1.** We celebrate today the holy solemnity of that martyr who sent many martyrs before himself by his eloquence, and led out many after himself by his example. What, then, worthy of so great a matter shall we offer to so great a man, except that he not wait to be praised by us, but not cease to pray for us? For we are, even if not in the merit in which he was here, certainly in that life in which he too labored. For he led a mortal life, and now, leading it . . . he merited to obtain the immortal. But this manner of leading the mortal life, and of coming to the immortal, was not prescribed to him by himself, but by the leader, king, commander, forerunner, helper, savior, deliverer, the crowner of all martyrs, namely the Lord our God and Savior Jesus Christ, the only Son of God that we might be created, and the Son of man that we might be re-created; by him who knows not how to lie, who neither deceives nor is deceived, the rule was prescribed in which is set the manner of leading the mortal life and coming to the eternal. This blessed Cyprian knew and taught: nor did he only teach, but he also did it; thereby showing that he did not deceive those whom he taught, because by teaching he lived, and by living he did it. What, then, did the Lord do? What manner did he appoint of leading the mortal life, and the merit of obtaining the immortal life? Just now we heard, when the holy Gospel was read: *He who wishes to follow me,* he says, *let him deny himself*[^1]. As though it seemed obscure, the heavenly teacher added still, and says: *He who loves his soul will lose it; and he who loses it for my sake will find it*[^2]. This is the manner of leading the mortal life, of obtaining the immortal life; about this manner, with the favor of the one whose solemnity we keep, and his praying for us, let Your Charity hear something from us. [For great is the reward set before us, most beloved brothers.] Surely we celebrate the solemnity of the most blessed martyr: to this festivity the whole multitude of brothers and sisters gathers, and rejoice celebrating the birthday of the martyr. If it is a birthday, he was born; that he might be born, he was conceived. Where was he conceived? In this life in which he was born. This life we all know to be full of tribulations: that life in which blessed Cyprian was reborn here, we know well; yet we celebrate his birthday. And who of us is there, who would dare in the smallest part to compare his own birthday, which he celebrates in his house, to these birthdays of the blessed martyrs? Whoever does this, will judge himself a sacrilegious man.

**2.** So let us see what it is, "let him deny himself"; for great is the reward set before us, most beloved brothers. We have heard the confession of the most blessed martyr Cyprian: *I worship one God, who made heaven and earth, the sea, and all that is in them*[^3]. God is silent, but the works of God speak: behold in what God, behold in what sort of God—indeed, not in what sort and such, but in God—behold in whom Cyprian believed. What, then, is it, "let him deny himself"? Deny yourself. What is, deny yourself? Are you compelled to deny God? deny yourself, and do not deny God. Do not love this temporal life as yours, and labor against eternal life; rather yield to eternal life, that you may be made eternal: deny yourself, that you may confess God; deny yourself, O man, that you may be made an angel; deny yourself, O mortal man, that, having confessed God, you may merit to live forever. Behold, you love the temporal life: you do not want to deny it, and you want to deny God. God, whom you denied, whom you refused to confess, withdraws from you; and you will have the temporal life, which you refused to deny. So let us see how long you are going to have this life. Behold, tomorrow, and after tomorrow another tomorrow, and after many tomorrows the end comes. And where will you go? where will you go out? Surely to God, whom you denied. O wretched, unhappy man! both you denied God, and the temporal life, like it or not, you lost. For this life, most beloved brothers, whether we will or not, passes, runs; let us, then, deny ourselves in this temporal life, that we may merit to live forever. Deny yourself, confess God. Do you love your soul? lose it. But you say to me: How do I lose what I love? You do this in your house. You love wheat, and you scatter the wheat, which you had stored in the granary with such diligence, which with such labor of harvest and threshing you had purged; now stored, now cleaned, when the time of sowing comes you will bring it out, scatter it, bury it, so as not to see what you scatter. Behold, by loving wheat, you scatter wheat; by loving life, pour out life; by loving your soul, lose it; because when you have lost it for God's sake in this time, in the future you will find it unto eternal life. So by loving life, pour out life.

**3.** It is hard, it is troublesome, it is a sad thing; I sympathize with you, because the Lord our God too sympathized with us. For he showed himself in you, and you in himself, when he said: *My soul is sorrowful even to death*[^4]. He suffered for us, let us suffer for him; he died for us, let us die for him, that we may live forever with him. But perhaps you hesitate to die, O mortal man, who at some time will die, because you were born mortal. Do you want not to fear death? Die for God. But perhaps you fear to die because death is a sad thing. Consider the harvest: the time of sowing has cold; but if the farmer refuses to be saddened by the cold in winter, he will not rejoice in summer. Consider whether you are therefore lazy to sow, because the sadness of cold is in the sowing. Heed the Psalm: *Those who sow in tears will reap in joy. Going they went and wept, casting their seeds*[^5]. So we were singing. What we sang, let us do: let us sow our souls in this time, like wheat in winter, that we may reap them unto eternal time, like wheat in the summer time. So the holy martyrs, so all the righteous, laboring on earth, weeping cast their seeds: for this life abounds in weeping. And what follows? *But coming they will come in exultation, carrying their sheaves*[^6]. Your seed, the shedding of blood; your sheaf, the crown received.

**4.** This our martyr believed, this he taught before he did it, this he did because he had already taught it. Those to whom blessed Cyprian spoke, he taught by speech alone; we hold his teaching doubled; we hold the sermon in writing, the example in memory. So let him exhort us, and pray for us; let him obtain for us from the Lord such a will as he himself spoke of in his passion, saying: *A good will, which knows God, cannot be changed.* And when the threatening judge said to him: *Take counsel for yourself,* that judge seemed to hate, not to love, the man, when he said to a mortal man: *Take counsel for yourself;* that is, take counsel for yourself, that you may now live a few days, and die forever. But holy Cyprian did not attend to the judge, a man, having power on earth; but he attended to the judge God, who made heaven and earth. So blessed Cyprian, unless he denied himself, unless, loving his soul, he lost it, that by losing he might find it, would have said to him: I take counsel indeed, and I give thanks, because you give me room for taking counsel, or a delay for thinking; or surely: Now receive my devotion, I undertake the rites of the princes. He would have said this, he would not have denied himself; and by perversely loving his soul, he would not have lost it: that is, he would not have sown what he might reap. But now, despising the cold of winter, thinking of the gladness of summer, he answered the man, as an adversary whom he saw, convicting the one whom he did not see. For what the devil was doing through that judge, the judge himself was ignorant of, but holy Cyprian knew. He answered him, saying: *Do what has been commanded you.* This "command" the holy martyr was attending to more deeply, mindful of the Lord his God, who, standing before Pontius Pilate, when that one said inflamed: *Do you not know that I have power to release you, and power to kill you?* answered him, truthful and the truth: *You would have no power over me, unless it were given you from above*[^7]. Truly, brothers, it was not because the emperor had commanded this, that holy Cyprian suffered, but because he had commanded it who was bestowing such a martyr upon us. So, mindful of the Lord his God, he answered the judge: *Do what has been commanded you; in so just a matter there is no deliberation.* About doubtful matters one usually takes counsel: if I still take counsel, I still doubt. *In so just a matter there is no deliberation.* What is this just matter? It is just that I follow the martyrs, whom by speaking I made to go before me to martyrdom; it is not just that I forsake those who have gone before me. For if I do not do what I taught, with them crowned the rest will grow cold. It is just, then, that I fulfill what I taught, and by fulfilling teach what I have fulfilled. This blessed Cyprian did. *In so just a matter there is no deliberation;* he received the sentence, he merited the crown. The judge saw whom he afflicted, he did not see where he sent him; because, to see it, he was not worthy. What, then, shall we do about so great gifts of God, most beloved brothers, except what the martyr himself said? For at the last, when the judge said: *It is my pleasure that Tascius Cyprian be punished by the sword,* Cyprian said: *Thanks be to God.* And so let us too, set in this solemnity, seeing these things with the eyes of faith, and hoping that we too will come where he hastened, all say: Thanks be to God. Turned to the Lord.
