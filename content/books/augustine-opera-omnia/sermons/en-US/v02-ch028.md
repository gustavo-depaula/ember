# Sermon 40

**1** Often, brothers, we have sung with the Psalmist: *Wait for the Lord, act manfully; and let your heart be strengthened, and wait for the Lord*[^1]. What is *Wait for the Lord*? That you receive then, when he gives; that you not demand then, when you wish. The time of giving is not yet; he waited for you, wait for him. What is it that I said, He waited for you, wait for him? If you already live justly, if you are already turned to him, if your past deeds displease you, if it has now pleased you to choose the good, new life—do not hasten to demand. He waited for you, that you might change your evil life: wait for him, that he may crown your good life. For if he too did not wait, there would be no one to whom he might give. Wait, then, because you have been waited for.

**2** But you who do not want to be corrected, O whoever you are here who still do not want to be corrected—as though there were one: I ought rather to have said, Whoever you are here. But you who are here, who have not resolved to be corrected, I will speak as though to one: Whoever you are who do not want to be corrected, what do you promise yourself? Do you perish by despairing, or by hoping? Whoever you are who perish by despairing, you say this in your soul: My iniquity is upon me, I waste away in my sins; what hope of living is there for me? Hear the prophet saying: *I do not want the death of the impious; only let the impious turn back from his most wicked way, and live*[^2]. You who perish by hoping, you say this in your soul: God is good, God is merciful, he forgives all things, he does not render evil for evil. Hear the Apostle saying: *Do you not know that the patience of God leads you to repentance?*[^3]

**3** What, then, remains? If we have obtained anything with you, if what I said has entered your heart, I see what you answer me: It is true, I neither despair, so as to perish by despairing; nor do I hope wrongly, so as to perish by hoping. I do not say to myself, "My iniquity is upon me, I now have no hope." Nor do I say to myself, "God is good, he renders evil to no one." I say neither that nor this: "The prophet presses me, the Apostle presses me." And what do you say? For a little while longer I will live as I wish. These are the ones who weary us: they are very many, they are troublesome. For a little while longer I will live as I wish; afterward, when I have corrected myself, surely what the prophet said is true: *I do not want the death of the impious; only let the impious turn back from his most wicked way, and live*[^4]. When I have turned, he will blot out all my evils; why do I not add something to my pleasures, and live as long as I wish, as I wish, intending afterward to turn to God?

**4** Why do you say this, brother? Why? Because God promised indulgence if I change myself. I see, I know, God promised indulgence; through the holy prophet he promises it, and through me, his least servant, he promises it; it is true what he promises, he promised it through his only Son. But why do you want to add evil days to evil days? Sufficient for the day is its own malice. Yesterday was an evil day, today too is evil, tomorrow too is evil. Or do you think they are good days, when you satisfy your pleasures, when you nourish your heart in luxuries, when you plot against another's chastity, when you sadden your neighbor by fraud, when you deny things entrusted to you, when you swear falsely for a coin? When you provide yourself a good lunch, do you therefore think you are passing a good day? How can it be that a day is good, when the man is evil? Do you want to add evil days to evil days?

**5** I beg, he says, let a little be allowed me. Why? Because God promised me indulgence. But no one promised you that you will live tomorrow. Or read to me, as you read the prophet, the Gospel, the Apostle, that when you turn, God blots out all your iniquities—read to me where tomorrow was promised you, and live tomorrow badly. Although, my brother, I ought not to have said this to you. Your life will perhaps be long: if it will be long, let it be good. Why do you want to have a long life and a bad one? Either it will not be long—and that long life ought to delight you which has no end; or it will be long—and what evil will it be, that you have lived well for a long time? Do you want to live a long time badly, and not well? And yet no one promised you tomorrow. Correct yourself, hear the Scripture: *Do not delay to turn to God*[^5]. These words are not mine: but they are mine too. If I love them, they are mine; love them, and they are yours. This discourse which I now speak is the holy Scripture's: if you despise it, it is your adversary. But hear the Lord saying: *Agree with your adversary quickly*[^6]. Let all hear, I recite the words of the divine Scripture. O evil deferrer, O evil seeker of tomorrow, hear the Lord saying, hear the holy Scripture foretelling. Of this passage I am a watchman. *Do not delay to turn to God, nor put it off from day to day*[^7]. See whether he did not see them, see whether he did not look upon those who say: Tomorrow I live well, today I will live badly. And when tomorrow comes, you will say this. *Do not delay to turn to God, nor put it off from day to day. For his wrath will come suddenly, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you*[^8]. Did I write this? Can I blot it out? If I blot it out, I fear to be blotted out. I can keep silent about it: I fear to keep silent. I am compelled to preach: terrified, I terrify. Fear with me, that you may rejoice with me. *Do not delay to turn to God*. Lord, see that I say it: Lord, you know that you terrified me, when your prophet was read. Lord, you know my fear in that chair, when your prophet was read. See, I say: *Do not delay to turn to God, nor put it off from day to day. For his wrath will come suddenly, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you*. But I do not want him to destroy you.

**6** Do not say to me: I want to perish: because I do not want it. Better, then, is my "I do not want" than your "I want." If your lethargic father were sick in your hands, and you, a young man, were present at the sick old man, and the physician said: Your father is in danger; this sleep is a kind of lethal heaviness; watch him, do not let him sleep; if you see him sleeping, rouse him; if it is too little to rouse him, pinch him; if this too is too little, prick him, lest your father die—you, a young man, would be a burden to the old man. He, dissolved into the sweet sickness, would go, would close his eyes pressed by that heaviness, you would cry against it to your father: "Do not sleep." But he: "Let me be, I want to sleep." And you: "But the physician said: If he wants to sleep, let him not sleep." But he: "I beg you, let me be, I want to die." But I do not want it, says the son to the father. To whom? Surely to one wishing himself to die. And yet you want to defer your father's death, and to live a little longer with your dying old father. The Lord cries to you: Do not sleep, lest you sleep forever: wake up, that you may live with me, and have a Father whom you do not bury. You hear, and you are deaf.

**7** What have I done as a watchman? I am free, I do not burden you. I know some will say: What did he mean to say to us? He terrified us, burdened us, made us guilty. Rather, I wished to free you from guilt. It is foul, it is base: I will not say evil, I will not say perilous, I will not say destructive; it is base that I should deceive you if God does not deceive me. The Lord threatens death to the impious, to the most wicked, to defrauders, to the criminal, to adulterers, to seekers of pleasures, to despisers of him, to those who murmur about the times and do not change their ways: the Lord threatens them with death, threatens them with hells, threatens them with everlasting ruin. What do they want, that I should promise what he does not promise? See, a procurator gives you security: what does it profit you, if the head of the household does not accept it? I am a procurator, I am a servant: do you want me to say to you: Live as you wish, the Lord will not destroy you? The procurator has given you security: the procurator's security is worth nothing. Would that the Lord gave it to you, and I made you anxious! For the Lord's security is valid, even if I am unwilling; but mine is worth nothing, if he is unwilling. But what is our security, brothers, whether mine or yours, except that we hear the Lord's commands intently and diligently, and faithfully await his promises? In these we are wearied, because we are men: let us implore his help, let us groan to him. Let our prayers be not for worldly things that pass away, transitory, and vanishing like a vapor: but let our prayers be for the very fulfilling of justice, and for sanctification for the name of God: not for conquering a neighbor, but for conquering lust: not for healing the flesh, but for subduing avarice. For these let our prayers be: let them help us within as we struggle, that they may crown us as we conquer.
