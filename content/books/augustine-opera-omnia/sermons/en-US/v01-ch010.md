# Sermon 9: A Treatise of Saint Augustine on the Ten Strings

A Sermon Delivered at Chusa

**1.** Our Lord and God, merciful and compassionate, long-suffering and very merciful and truthful—as bountifully as he dispenses mercy in the present age, so severely does he threaten judgment in the age to come. The words I have spoken are written and are contained in the divine authorities, for *The Lord is merciful and compassionate, long-suffering and very merciful and truthful*[^1]. It greatly delights all sinners and lovers of this world that *the Lord is merciful and compassionate, that he is long-suffering and very merciful*. But if you love one so very merciful, fear there also the last thing he says, *And truthful*. For if he said nothing else but, *The Lord is merciful and compassionate, and very merciful*, you would, as it were, turn yourself to security and impunity and to the license of sins. You would do what you wished, you would use the world, either as much as was permitted you or as much as your lust commanded you. And if anyone, admonishing you well, were to rebuke and terrify you, that you might restrain yourself from the immoderate flow of going after your desires and abandoning your God, you would stand up in the midst of the rebuker's words, with a shameless brow, as though having heard the divine authority, and you would read from the Lord's book: "Why do you terrify me about our God? *He is merciful and compassionate and very merciful*." Lest people say such things, he added one word at the end, where he says, *And truthful*. And he shook off the joy of those presuming wrongly, and brought in the fear of those who grieve. Let us rejoice at the mercy of the Lord, but let us fear the judgment of the Lord. He spares, but he is not silent. He is silent, but he will not always be silent. Hear while he is not silent in the word, lest you have no leisure to hear when he will not be silent in judgment.

**2.** For now you are permitted to settle your case. Before the last judgment of your God, settle your case. There is nothing on which you may presume when he comes, nor will you bring false witnesses by whom he may be deceived, nor will you employ an advocate with fraudulent circumventions and wordy arts, nor will you in any way scheme to be able to corrupt the judge. What, then, do you do before such a judge, whom you can neither corrupt nor deceive? And yet there is something you may do. For then he himself will be the judge of your case, who now is the witness of your life. We have cried out, and we have praised. Let us settle our case. He who is the witness of your works, he himself is the witness of these voices. Let them not be empty, let them be turned to groaning. It is the time to be reconciled with the adversary quickly. So long-suffering is God, seeing daily unjust things and not punishing, yet judgment will come quickly. For after the manner of human life that is long which to God is short. But what comforts even the world itself and the human race, that it seems far off? Is it that, if the last day of the whole human race is far off, the last day of each person is far off? This I say: from Adam many years have rolled on, many years have flowed, and hereafter will flow, not indeed so many, yet up to the end of the world years will pass, as those too passed. What remains seems long, although it will not be as much as has passed, and yet from the past time that has gone by the end of the remaining time is to be hoped for. There was once a certain day that was called today. From that until this today, has not whatever was future become past? It is held as if it had not been. So will be whatever last thing remains. But let this too be long, let it be extended, as much as you have thought, as much as you have said, as much as you have imagined, as much as Scripture does not form for you but your imagining fashions; lengthen the day of judgment as much as you wish—will you nevertheless extend into the distance your last day, that is, of your life, on which you are to go out from this body? Let your old age be certain for you, if it can be. But for whom can it be? Is it not that, from the moment a man begins to be able to live, he can already also die? The possibility of death the beginning of life makes. In this earth and in the human race that one alone cannot yet die, who has not yet begun even to live. An uncertain day, then, is to be hoped for as though daily. But if an uncertain day is to be hoped for as though daily, let your case be settled with the adversary, while he is with you on the way. For this life is called the way, through which all pass. And this adversary does not depart.

**3.** But who is this adversary? This adversary is not the devil, for Scripture would never exhort you to be reconciled with the devil. There is, then, another adversary which a man makes for himself as an adversary. For that one, if he were the adversary, would not be with you on the way. He is with you on the way for this, that he may be reconciled with you. For he knows that unless you are reconciled on the way, he will hand you over to the judge, the judge to the officer, the officer to prison. These are the words of the Gospel; those who have read or heard them recall them with us. Who, then, is the adversary? The word of God. The word of God is your adversary. Why is it an adversary? Because it commands contrary things, which you do not do. It says to you, *One is your God*[^2], worship one God. You wish, the one God being abandoned as though the lawful husband of the soul, to commit fornication with many demons, and, what is graver, not as though openly forsaking and repudiating, as apostates do, but as though remaining in your husband's house, you admit adulterers. That is, as though a Christian, you do not leave the Church, you consult astrologers or soothsayers or augurs or sorcerers. As though, an adulterous soul, you do not depart from your husband's house, and, remaining in his marriage, you commit fornication. It is said to you, *Do not take in vain the name of the Lord your God*[^3], do not suppose Christ to be a creature because for your sake he took up a creature. And you despise him who is equal to the Father and one with the Father. It is said to you that you should observe the sabbath spiritually, not as the Jews observe the sabbath with fleshly idleness. For they wish to be at leisure for their trifles and luxuries. For a Jew would do better to do something useful in his field than to be seditious in the theater. And their women would do better on the sabbath day to make wool than all day long to dance shamelessly on their balconies. But to you it is said that you should observe the sabbath spiritually, in hope of the future rest which the Lord promises you. For whoever, for the sake of that future rest, does whatever he can, although what he does seems laborious, yet if he refers it to faith in the promised rest, he does not yet have the sabbath in fact, but he has it in hope. But you wish to rest for this, that you may labor, when you ought to labor for this, that you may rest. It is said to you, *Honor your father and your mother*[^4]. You inflict insult on parents, you who do not wish to suffer it from your children. It is said to you, *You shall not kill*[^5]. But you wish to kill your enemy; and perhaps you do not do it because you fear a human judge, not because you think of God. Do you not know that he is a witness of thoughts? While he lives whom you wish to die, he holds you a murderer in your heart. It is said to you, *You shall not commit adultery*[^6], that is, do not go to any other besides your wife. But you demand this of your wife and are unwilling to render this to your wife. And whereas you ought to precede your wife in virtue, since chastity is a virtue, you fall under one assault of lust. And you want your wife to be the conqueress; you lie conquered. And whereas you are the head of your wife, she precedes you to God, whose head you are. Do you want your house to hang head downward? *For the man is the head of the woman*[^7]. But where the woman lives better than the man, the house hangs head downward. If the man is the head, the man ought to live better and precede his wife in all good deeds, that she may imitate the man, may follow her head. As the head of the Church is Christ, and the Church is commanded to follow its head and to walk in the footsteps of its head, so each household has the man as its head and, as it were, the woman as its flesh. Where the head leads, there the body ought to follow. Why, then, does the head wish to go where it does not wish the body to follow? Why does the man wish to go where he does not wish his wife to follow? In commanding these things, the word of God is an adversary. For people do not wish to do what the word of God wishes. And why should I say that the word of God is an adversary because it commands? I fear that I myself may be an adversary to some because I speak these things. What does it matter to me? Let him who terrifies make me strong to speak, that I may not fear the complaints of people. For those who are unwilling to keep chastity for their wives—and such abound—are unwilling that I should say these things. But, willing or unwilling, I am going to say them. For if I do not exhort you to be reconciled with the adversary, I shall remain in a quarrel with him. He who commands you to do them, himself commands us to speak them. If by not doing what he commands you to do, you are his adversaries, then by not saying what he commands us to say, we shall remain his adversaries.

**4.** Did I dwell long on the rest, which I mentioned above? For this we presume of your Charity, that you worship one God. This we presume of the Catholic faith which is in you, that you believe the Son of God to be equal to the Father. Nor do you take in vain the name of the Lord your God, so as to think the Son of God to be a creature. Since every *creature is subject to vanity*[^8]. But you believe him to be equal to the Father, God from God, the Word with God, the Word God, through whom all things were made, light from light, coeternal with him who begot him, one with him who begot him. But you believe that this Word took up a creature, took up mortality from the Virgin Mary, and suffered for us. These things we read, we believe, that we may be saved. Nor did I dwell on this, that whatever you do, you should do it for the sake of future hope. For I know that the minds of all Christians think about the age to come. For whoever does not think about the age to come, nor is a Christian for this, that he may receive what God promises at the end, is not yet a Christian. Nor did I dwell on that where the word of God says, *Honor your father and your mother*[^9]. For many honor their parents, and rarely do we find parents complaining of the wickedness of their children, although they are not lacking; but yet, since it so rarely happens, it had to be admonished so briefly. Nor on that where it was said, *You shall not kill*[^10], did I wish to dwell. For I do not believe there is here a crowd of murderers. But that evil, creeping widely, has taken hold of more, and in it that adversary is more vehemently provoked, who therefore cries out that he may at some time be a friend. There are daily complaints, although the women themselves now do not even dare to complain of their husbands. So custom, invading all things, is observed in place of law, so that perhaps it has even been persuaded to the women that this is permitted to men, not permitted to women. For they are accustomed to hear that women have been brought to court who perhaps were found with slaves. That a man was brought to court because he was found with his maidservant, they have never heard, although it is through sin. In an equal sin not divine truth but human perversity makes the man seem more innocent. And if perhaps today someone has had a sharper wife murmuring more freely, to whom it now seemed that it was permitted to the man, and she has heard in the Church that it is not permitted to the man—if, then, he has had his wife, as we said, murmuring more freely and saying to him, "What you do is not permitted. We heard it together. We are Christians. What you demand of me, render to me. I owe you fidelity, you owe me fidelity, we both owe fidelity to Christ. And if you deceive me, you do not deceive him whose we are, you do not deceive him who bought us"—hearing these and suchlike things which he is not accustomed to, while he is unwilling to become healthy in himself, he becomes mad against me. He is angry, he curses. Perhaps he even says, "How did it happen that he came here, or that my wife went out to church on that very day?" And I believe he says this in his thought, for he does not dare to break out freely into voice, nor even before his wife alone. For perhaps if he broke out and said this, she could answer and say, "Why do you curse him to whom a little while ago you were acclaiming? Surely we are spouses. If you are at variance with your own tongue, how will you be able to live in concord with me?" We, brothers, look to your dangers, we do not attend to your wishes. For a physician too, if he attends to the wish of the sick man, never cures him. What ought not to be done, let it not be done. What God forbids, let it not be done. Whoever believes God hears from him what we say. Certainly it would be better for some who are unwilling to be corrected, that either we should not have come here if we were going to speak so, or, since we have already come, that we should not say these things.

**5.** The day before yesterday I remember I said to your Holiness that, if we were harpers or exhibiting something of this kind to the populace for the pursuits of your frivolity—which we now ask that you abandon—you would have held us to give you a day, and each one according to his small measure would contribute a fee to us. Why should we walk about delighted with vain songs, profiting nothing, sweet for a time, bitter afterward? For by such base songs human minds, enticed, are enervated and fall, flowing down from virtue into baseness. And on account of those very basenesses they afterward feel pains, and with great bitterness digest what they drank with temporal sweetness. Better, then, that we sing to you things bitter for a time, which afterward may grow sweet in you. Nor do we demand any fee, except that you do what we say—rather, that you not do it, if we do it. But if he who fears no one says it to all, and through whom it comes about, in his name and in the glory of his mercy, that we too fear no one, then let us all who have heard, let us all do it, let us all be reconciled with our adversary.

**6.** Suppose me to be a harper—what more could I sing to you? Behold, I bear a psaltery, it has ten strings. This you sang a little while ago, before I began to speak. You were my choir. Did you not a little while ago sing, *O God, I will sing to you a new song, on the psaltery of ten strings I will play to you*[^11]? Those very ten strings I now strike. Why is the voice of the psaltery of God bitter? Let us all play on the psaltery of ten strings. I do not sing this to you which you do not do. For the decalogue of the law has ten commandments. Which ten commandments are so distributed that three pertain to God, seven pertain to men. Three to God, which I have already said: One is our God, and we ought to make no likeness, and not to commit fornication after God who is one, because God Christ the Son of God is one with the Father. And therefore he ought not to be taken in vain by us, so that we think him made, that is, some creature, through whom all things were made. But because the one God himself is Father and Son and Holy Spirit, in the Holy Spirit, that is, in the gift of God, everlasting rest is promised to us. Thence we have now received a pledge. For thus the Apostle says, *Who has given us the Spirit as a pledge*[^12]. If we have received a pledge, that we may begin to be tranquil in the Lord and in our God, that we may be gentle in our God, may be patient in God, we shall also be, in him from whom we received the pledge, at rest forever. Which will be the sabbath of sabbaths, on account of that very rest pertaining to the gift of the Holy Spirit. The third commandment, then, about the sabbath, which we have spoken of, which the Jews celebrate fleshly, let us recognize spiritually. For because it is called the Holy Spirit, therefore God sanctified the seventh day, when he made all his works, as we read written in Genesis. You do not have sanctification named there, except on that day where it is said, *God rested from his works*[^13]. For God was not wearied, that it should be said, *God rested from his works*, but in that word he promised rest to you who labor. And because he made all things very good, and so it is said, *God rested*, that you may understand that you too after good works shall rest, and shall rest without end. For all the things above that are said, that is, the days above, have an evening. This seventh day, where God sanctified rest, has no evening. There it is said, "Morning was made, that the day itself might begin." It was not said, "Evening was made, that the day might be ended," but it was said, *Morning was made*[^14], that the day might come to be without end. So, then, our rest begins as though in the morning, but it is not ended, because we shall live forever. To this hope, whatever we do, if we do it, we observe the sabbath. This is the third string of this decalogue, that is, of the ten-stringed psaltery: in three strings the commandments pertaining to him.

**7.** If it were said to us, *You shall love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul and with all your mind*[^15], and nothing were said about our neighbor, it would not be a ten-stringed instrument, but a three-stringed one. But because the Lord added, *And you shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^16], and joined on, saying, *On these two commandments hang the whole Law and the Prophets*[^17], the whole Law is in two commandments, in the love of God and the love of neighbor. To two commandments, then, that is, to the love of God and of neighbor, the decalogue pertains. To the first commandment three strings pertain, because God is Trinity. But to the other commandment, that is, to the love of neighbor, seven strings: how one should live among men. For the very number seven, as of seven strings, begins from the honor of parents. *Honor your father and your mother*[^18]. For to his parents a man opens his eyes, and this life takes its beginning from their friendship. But whoever does not show honor to his own parents, whom will he be able to spare? *Honor your father and your mother*. And the Apostle says, *Honor your father and mother, which is the first commandment*[^19]. How first, when it is the fourth commandment, except because in the number seven it is first? For the love of neighbor it is first in the second table. For therefore two tables of the Law were given. For God gave his servant Moses on the mountain two tables, on which two stone tables were written the ten commandments of the law—which is the psaltery of ten strings—three on one table pertaining to God, seven on the other table to the neighbor. On the other table, then, the first is: *Honor your father and your mother*. The second: *You shall not commit adultery*. The third: *You shall not kill*. The fourth: *You shall not steal*. The fifth: *You shall not bear false witness*. The sixth: *You shall not covet your neighbor's wife*. The seventh: *You shall not covet your neighbor's property*. These let us join to those three pertaining to the love of God, if we wish to sing on the psaltery of ten strings a new song.

**8.** Let your Charity attend, that I may say what the Lord suggests. The people of the Jews received the law. They did not observe these things in the decalogue. And whoever obeyed, obeyed out of fear of punishment, not out of love of justice. He carried the psaltery, he did not sing. For to the singer it is a pleasure, to the fearful a burden. Therefore the old man either does not do it, or does it out of fear, not out of love of holiness, not out of delight in chastity, not out of the temperance of love, but out of fear. For he is the old man, and the old man can sing the old song, not the new. But that he may sing the new song, let him be a new man. And how he can be a new man, hear—not me, but the Apostle—saying, *Put off the old man, and put on the new*[^20]. And lest anyone should think, when he said, *Put off the old man and put on the new*, that something was to be laid down and something to be taken up when he was commanding about changing the man, he added and said, *Wherefore, putting away lying, speak the truth*[^21]. This is what he says, *Put off the old man and put on the new*. This he said: Change your ways. You loved the world, love God. You loved the trifles of iniquity, temporal pleasures; love your neighbor. If you do it out of love, you sing a new song. If you do it out of fear, you do it nevertheless, you indeed carry the psaltery, but you do not yet sing. But if you neither do it, you cast away the psaltery itself. It is better even to carry it than to cast it away. But again, it is better to sing with pleasure than to carry with a burden. Nor does one come to the new song unless already singing with pleasure. For he who carries it with fear is still in the old. And note what I say, brothers. He has not been reconciled with his adversary, who still acts out of fear. For he fears lest God come and condemn him. For chastity does not yet delight him, justice does not yet delight him, but, dreading the judgment of God, he refrains from deeds. He does not condemn the desire itself, which rages in him. What is good does not yet delight him. He does not yet have sweetness there to sing the new song, but out of the oldness he fears punishments. He has not yet been reconciled with his adversary.

**9.** For such people are generally tripped up by such a thought, so that they say to themselves, "If it could be, God would not threaten us, would not say through his Prophets such things as terrify people, but he would come to give pardon to all, to forgive all; he would come afterward, he would send no one into hell." Now, because he is unjust, he wants God unjust. God wants to make you like himself, and you try to make God like yourself. Let God, then, please you as he is, not as you wish him to be. For you are perverse, and you want God such as you are, not such as he is. But if he please you such as he is, you are corrected, and you direct your heart to that rule from which now, an alien, you are distorted. Let God please you such as he is, love him such as he is. He does not love you such as you are, but hates you such as you are. Therefore he has mercy on you, because he hates you such as you are, that he may make you such as you are not yet. May he make you, I said, such as you are not yet. For he does not promise you that he will make you such as he is. For you will be such as he is, but in a certain mode, that is, an imitator of God as an image, but not such an image as the Son is. For even images among men are different. The son of a man has the image of his father, and is what his father is, because he is a man as his father is. But in a mirror your image is not what you are. For your image is one thing in your son, another in the mirror. In your son your image is according to equality of substance, but in the mirror how far it is from the substance! And yet it is a certain image of you, although not such as in your son according to substance. So in the creature, the image of God is not what is in the Son, who is what the Father is, that is, God the Word of God through whom all things were made. Receive, then, the likeness of God, which through evil deeds you lost. For just as on a coin the image of the emperor is one thing and another in his son—for it is an image and an image, but it is one way impressed on the coin, held another way in the son, the image of the emperor one way on a gold piece—so you too are the coin of God, the better for this, that with understanding and with a certain life you are the coin of God, so that you may know whose image you bear and to whose image you were made, for the coin does not know that it has the image of the king. Therefore, as I had begun to say, God hates you such as you are, but loves you such as he wishes you to be, and therefore he exhorts you to be changed. Be reconciled with him, and begin first to will well and to hate yourself such as you are. Let this be for you the beginning of reconciliation with the word of God, that you begin first to hate yourself such as you are. When you too have begun to hate yourself such as you are, as God hates you such, you now begin to love God himself such as he is.

**10.** Attend to a sick man. The sick man hates himself sick as he is; thence he begins to be reconciled with the physician. For the physician too hates him such as he is. For therefore he wants him to be well, because he hates the feverish man, and the physician is a persecutor of the fever that he may be the deliverer of the man. So avarice, so lust, so hatred, desire, luxury, so the frivolity of spectacles, are fevers of your soul. You ought to hate them along with the physician. Thus you are reconciled with the physician, you strive with the physician, and you gladly hear what the physician commands, you gladly do what the physician commands, and, health now advancing, even the commandments begin to delight. How burdensome is food to the sick when they are being refreshed! And the sick reckon the hour of their refreshment worse than that of the onset of their illness. And yet they force themselves, being reconciled with the physician, and, however unwilling and struggling, they conquer themselves to take something. With what eagerness, when well, will they take greater things, of which, when sick, they scarcely take smaller! But whence did this happen? Because they hated their fever and had been reconciled with the physician, and physician and sick man together persecuted the fever. When, therefore, we too say such things, we hate nothing but your fevers; rather, in us the word of God itself hates your fevers, with which you ought to be reconciled. For what are we, except people to be freed along with you, to be healed along with you?

**11.** Now do not look at me, but at the word of God. Do not be angry at your medicine: for I have found no other way to cross over. I have come to the fifth string, I who touch the psaltery of ten strings. Was I going to pass over the fifth? Rather I am going to strike it constantly. For in it I see almost the whole human race lying, in it I see more labor. In striking it, what do I say? Do not commit adultery after your wives, because you do not want your wives to commit adultery after you. Do not go where you do not want them to follow. In vain do you try to excuse yourselves, when you say, "Do I go to another's wife? I go to my maidservant." Do you want her to say to you, "Do I go to another's husband? I go to my manservant"? You say, "It is not another's wife to whom I go." Do you want it to be said to you, "It is not another's husband to whom I go"? Far be it that she should say this. For it is better that she grieve over you than that she imitate you. For that chaste and holy woman and truly Christian, who grieves over her fornicating husband, and grieves not on account of the flesh but grieves on account of love—she is unwilling that you should do it not because she herself does not do it, but because it is not expedient for you. For if she does not do it for this reason, that you should not do it, then if you do it, she will do it. But if she owes that to God, if she owes to Christ that which you demand, and renders it because he commands, then even if the husband commits fornication, she shows chastity to God. For Christ speaks in the hearts of good women, speaks within where the husband does not hear, because he is not even worthy, if he is such. He speaks, then, inwardly and says, and consoles his daughter with words of this kind: "You are tormented by the wrongs of your husband; for what has he done to you? Grieve, but do not imitate him so as to do evil; rather let him imitate you in good. For in that which he does evil, do not think him your head, but me." For if even in that which he does evil he is the head, the body will follow its head, and both go over a precipice. But that the body may not follow its evil head, let it hold itself to the head of the Church, Christ. Owing her chastity to him, paying her honor to him, whether the husband is absent, whether the husband is present, she does not sin; because he to whom she owes it that she may not sin is never absent.

**12.** These things, then, my brothers, do, that you may be able to be reconciled with the adversary. Nor are the things I say bitter, or, if they are bitter, let them cure. If this draught is bitter, let it be taken. Because the inward parts are in danger, it is bitter; let it be drunk. Better a slight bitterness in the throat than an eternal torment in the inward parts. Change yourselves, then. Whoever of you were not doing this good of chastity, now do it. Do not say, "It cannot be done." It is foul, my brothers, it is shameful, that a man should say that what a woman does cannot be done. It is a wickedness that a man should say, "I cannot." What a woman can, can a man not? For what, does she not bear flesh? She was first deceived by the serpent. Your chaste wives show you that it can be done, which you are unwilling to do and say cannot be done. But perhaps you will say that she can do it more easily because there is much guarding over her: the commandment of the law, the husband's diligence, the terror also of the public laws. There is also a great defense in her modesty and shame. Many guards make a woman more chaste; let his very manliness make the man chaste. For therefore there is more guarding over a woman, because there is more weakness. She blushes before her husband; do you not blush before Christ? You are freer, because you are stronger. Because you conquer easily, therefore you are entrusted to yourself. Over her are both the diligence of her husband, and the terror of the laws, and the custom of behavior, and greater modesty. And over you is God, God only. For you easily find men like yourself, before whom you do not fear to blush, because many do it. And so great is the perversity of the human race that sometimes it is to be feared that the chaste may blush among the shameless. Therefore I do not cease to touch this fifth string, on account of that very perverse custom and ruin of the whole human race, as I said. If anyone among you commits murder—which God avert—you wish to drive him from his homeland, and at once, if it can be done, to shut him out. If anyone commits theft, you hate him and are unwilling to see him. If anyone bears false witness, you abominate him, nor does he seem to you a man. If anyone covets another's property, he is reckoned a robber and unjust. But if anyone has wallowed with his maidservants, he is loved, kindly received, the wounds are turned into jokes. But if anyone arises who says that he is chaste, that he does not commit adultery, and it is known that he does not, he blushes to approach those not like himself, lest they insult, lest they mock, lest they say he is not a man. To this human perversity has sunk, that a man bound by lust is held to be a man, and a man who is conqueror of lust is not held to be a man. Those triumphing exult, and they are not men; they lie prostrate, and they are men! If you were watching in the amphitheater, would you watch in such a way that he would seem to you the stronger who lay under the beast, than he who slew the beast?

**13.** But because you make light of the inner battle, and the outer battles delight you, therefore you do not wish to belong to the new song, where it is said, *Who teaches my hands for the fight and my fingers for war*[^22]. For there is a war that a man wages with himself, struggling against evil desires, bridling avarice, beating down pride, smothering ambition, slaying lust. These battles you wage in secret, and you are not conquered in the open. For this your hands are taught for the fight, and your fingers for war. This is not in your spectacles. In those spectacles the hunter is not the same as the harper; the hunter does one thing, the harper another. In the spectacle of God it is one. Touch the same ten strings, and you slay the beasts. You do both at once. You touch the first string by which one God is worshiped: the beast of superstition has fallen. You touch the second by which you do not take the name of the Lord your God in vain: the beast of the error of the abominable heresies that thought this has fallen. You touch the third string where, for the hope of future rest, you do whatever you do: the love of this world, more cruel than the other beasts, is slain. For on account of the love of this world people labor in all their business. But you, in all your good works, labor not for the love of this world, but for the everlasting rest which God promises. See how you do both at once. You both touch the strings and slay the beasts. That is, you are both harper and hunter. Do not such spectacles delight you, where we earn not the eyes of the producer, but the eyes of the Redeemer? *Honor your father and your mother*: you touch the fourth string so as to show honor to your parents, the beast of impiety has fallen. *You shall not commit adultery*: you touch the fifth string, the beast of lust has fallen. *You shall not kill*: you touch the sixth string, the beast of cruelty has fallen. *You shall not steal*: you touch the seventh string, the beast of rapacity has fallen. *You shall not bear false witness*: you touch the eighth string, the beast of falsity has fallen. *You shall not covet your neighbor's wife*: you touch the ninth string, the beast of adulterous thought has fallen. For it is one thing not to do anything of the sort besides one's wife, another not to desire another's wife. Therefore there are two commandments: *You shall not commit adultery*, and, *You shall not covet your neighbor's wife*. *You shall not covet your neighbor's property*: you touch the tenth string, the beast of cupidity has fallen. So, all the beasts falling, you live secure and innocent in the love of God and in human society. Touching the ten strings, how many beasts you slay! For there are many heads under these heads. On each string you slay not single beasts, but herds of beasts. So, then, you will sing the new song with love, not with fear.

**14.** Do not say to yourself, when perhaps you wish to do something wantonly, "I do not have a wife, I do what I will; for I do not sin after my wife." You now know your price, you now know what you approach, what you eat, what you drink—rather, whom you eat, whom you drink. Abstain yourself from fornications. Lest perhaps you say to me, "I go to the brothel, I go to a harlot, I go to a prostitute, nor do I violate that commandment which said, *You shall not commit adultery*, because I do not yet have a wife nor do anything after her; nor do I violate that commandment where it was said, *You shall not covet your neighbor's wife*. I who go to the public house, into what commandment do I run?" Do we not find a string to touch? Do we not find a string by which word to bind this runaway? Let him not run away, he has wherewith to be bound. But let him love, and there will be no binding, but an adornment. In those very ten strings we find it. For the ten commandments are referred to those two, as we have heard, that we should love God and neighbor, and those two to that one. And the one is, *What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to another*[^23]. There the ten are contained, there the two are contained.

**15.** But you say, "If I commit theft, I do what I am unwilling to suffer; if I kill, I do what I am unwilling to suffer from another; if I do not show honor to my parents when I wish it to be shown to me by my children, I do what I am unwilling to suffer; if I am an adulterer and attempt something of the sort, I do what I am unwilling to suffer—for if anyone is asked, he says: I do not want my wife to do anything of the sort; if I covet my neighbor's wife, I do not want anyone to covet mine—I do what I am unwilling to suffer; if I covet my neighbor's property, I do not want mine to be taken away—I do what I am unwilling to suffer. But when I go to a harlot, to whom do I do what I am unwilling to suffer?" What is graver, to God himself. Let your Holiness understand. For *What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to another*[^24] pertains to two commandments. How does it pertain to two commandments? If you do not do to a man what you do not want to suffer from a man, it pertains to the commandment of neighbor, to the love of neighbor, to the seven strings. But if what you do not want to suffer from a man, you wish to do to God himself, what is this? Do you not do to another what you do not want to suffer? Has a man become dearer to you than God? "How, then," he says, "do I do it to God himself?" You corrupt yourself. "And whence do I do injury to God, because I corrupt myself?" Whence does he do injury to you who perhaps wishes to throw a stone at your painted panel on which panel is your image, set up idly in your house for your vain honor, neither feeling nor speaking nor seeing? If anyone throws a stone at it, does not the insult go to you? But when you corrupt in yourself the image of God, which you are, through fornications and through the dissipations of lust, you attend that you have not gone to anyone's wife, you attend that you have done nothing after your wife, you who do not have a wife. And do you not attend whose image you have violated through the unlawful lusts of fornication? Finally, God, who knows what is useful to you, who truly so governs his servants for their advantage, not for his own—for he does not need servants as a help, but you need the Lord as a help—this Lord himself, then, who knows what is useful to you, granted a wife, nothing more. This he ordered, this he commanded, lest through unlawful pleasures his temple, which you have begun to be, should collapse. Is it I who say this? Hear the Apostle: *Do you not know that you are the temple of God, and the Spirit of God dwells in you?*[^25] This he says to Christians, this he says to the faithful: *Do you not know that you are the temple of God, and the Spirit of God dwells in you? If anyone corrupts the temple of God, God will destroy him*[^26]. You see how he threatens? You do not want your house corrupted; why do you corrupt the house of God? Surely you do not do to anyone what you do not want to suffer? There is, then, no way to escape. He is held who thought he was not held. For all the sins of men pertain either to the corruption of shameful deeds, or to the crimes of doing harm. Because you cannot harm God by crimes, you offend him in shameful deeds, you offend him in corruptions, in yourself you do injury to him. For you do injury to his grace, to his gift.

**16.** If you had a slave, you would want your slave to serve you. Serve a better Lord, your God. Your slave you did not make; both you and your slave he made. Do you want him to serve you, with whom you were made, and are you unwilling to serve him by whom you were made? Therefore, when you want your slave, a man, to serve you, and you yourself are unwilling to serve your Lord, you do to God what you are unwilling to suffer. Therefore that one commandment contains two, those two contain ten, those ten contain all. *Sing,* therefore, *on the psaltery of ten strings a new song*[^27]. But that you may sing a new song, be new men. Love justice: it has its own beauty. Therefore you are unwilling to see it, because you love something else. For if you loved nothing else, you would certainly see it. Why do you see it, when you demand it? Why do you praise faith, when you demand it of your slave? What a beautiful thing is faith! But it is beautiful then, when it is demanded of the slave; then it is seen, when it is sought from another. When it is demanded of you, it is not seen. Gold you see, faith you do not see. As gold shines to the eyes of the body, so faith shines to the eyes of the heart. You open the eyes of the heart to it, when you want your slave to show it to you. And if he shows it to you, you praise the slave and proclaim him and say, "I have an excellent slave, I have a great slave, I have a faithful slave." What you praise in your slave, you do not show to your Lord, and the more wickedly because you want yourself to have a better slave than God has. God commands your slave to be good toward you. As he commands your wife that, even if you commit adultery, she should not commit adultery, so he commands your slave that, even if you do not serve your God, he should serve you. But see that all this avails for your admonition, not for your ruin. For that slave, in that he serves you, who are unworthy, worthily—that is, in that he serves you, unworthy, well and faithfully, and loves you purely—that he owes to God, not to you. It is just, then, that you too should attend that you are under a Lord, to whom he too attends in serving you. Fulfill, then, what was said, *What you do not want done to yourself, do not do to another*[^28]. But when you say "another," attend to both, both the neighbor and God. Sing on the psaltery of ten strings, sing a new song, be reconciled with the word of God, while it is with you on the way. *Be reconciled with your adversary quickly*[^29], lest you come in discord before the judge. If you do what you hear, you have been reconciled with him. But if you do not do it, you still quarrel with him, and have not yet settled it until you do it.

**17.** But that you may be reconciled, abstain yourselves from detestable corruptions, from detestable inquiries, from astrologers, from soothsayers, from diviners, from augurs, from sacrilegious rites; abstain yourselves, as much as you can, from frivolous spectacles. If any delights of the world creep into the soul, exercise yourselves in mercy, exercise yourselves in almsgiving, in fasts, in prayers. For by these the daily sins are purged, which cannot but creep into the soul on account of human frailty. Do not despise them because they are smaller; but fear, because they are more. Attend, my brothers. They are small, they are not great. There is no beast like a lion, to break the throat with one bite. But often even many small beasts kill. If anyone is thrown into a place full of fleas, does he not die there? They are indeed not greater, but human nature is weak, which can be slain even by the tiniest beasts. So too small sins: you attend that they are small; beware, because they are more. How tiny are the grains of sand! If more sand is put into a ship, it sinks it so that it perishes. How small are the drops of rain! Do they not fill rivers and cast down houses? Therefore do not despise these things. But you will say, "And who can be without these?" Lest you say this—because truly no one can—God, merciful, seeing our frailty, set remedies against them. What are the remedies? Of almsgiving, of fasts, of prayers: these are the three. But that you may say what is true in prayer, almsgiving must be perfectly fulfilled. What is perfect almsgiving? That from that which abounds to you, you give to him who does not have, and that whoever harms you, you forgive him.

**18.** But do not think, brothers, that adulteries are to be committed daily, and to be cleansed by daily almsgiving. For other greater crimes daily almsgiving does not suffice to cleanse them. It is one thing where you change your life, another where you bear your life. Those things are to be changed, so that if you were an adulterer, do not be an adulterer; if you were a fornicator, do not commit fornication; if a murderer, do not be a murderer; if you went to an astrologer and to the other sacrilegious plagues, now cease. Or do you think that these, unless they cease to be done, can be cleansed by daily almsgiving? I call those daily sins which are either easily committed through the tongue, as is a harsh word, or when someone slips into immoderate laughter, or into trifles of this kind that are daily. In things themselves permitted there are sins. With one's own wife, if the manner of intercourse due for begetting children is exceeded, it is now a sin. For to this a wife is taken: for that too the marriage tablets indicate, where it is written, *For the sake of begetting children*. When you wish to use your wife more than the necessity of begetting children compels, it is now a sin. And such sins daily almsgiving cleanses. In food itself, which is certainly permitted, if you perhaps exceed the measure and take more than is necessary, you sin. These that I speak of are daily, but yet they are sins, and they are not light because they are more. But because they are daily and very many, the ruin of their multitude is to be feared, even if not of their magnitude. Such sins, brothers, we say can be cleansed by daily almsgiving. But do almsgiving, and do not cease. Attend to your daily life teeming with these very sins, the small ones, I say.

**19.** And when you do almsgiving, do not do it proudly, nor pray as the Pharisee there. But yet what did he say there? *I fast twice on the sabbath, I give tithes of all that I possess*[^30]. And the blood of the Lord had not yet been shed! We have received so great a price for ourselves, and we do not even pay out as much as the Pharisee. And you have the Lord in another place openly saying, *Unless your justice abound more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter the kingdom of heaven*[^31]. Therefore they give tithes; you, if you give a hundredth, boast that you have done something great. For you attend to what another does not do, not to what God commands you to do. You measure yourself by comparison with the worse, not by the command of the better. Not because that one does nothing, do you therefore do something great. But because there is rejoicing at certain least works of yours—because so great is your barrenness that there is rejoicing at small things—as though secure, you flatter yourselves about the least grains of almsgiving, and forget the heaps of sins. You perhaps brought forth something small, I know not what, which another either did not have or did not bring forth though he had it. Do not attend to who after you does not do it, but to what God commands you to do. Finally, why, in those worldly desires, do those whom you have surpassed not suffice you, but you wish to be rich, equal to those richer than you? You do not attend to how many poorer ones you transcend: you wish to surpass the richer. But in almsgiving a measure is observed. Here it is said, "How long now do I do it?" and there it is not said, "How many rich am I richer than?" The wants of innumerable beggars are not attended to, the later multitudes of the poor are not regarded, but the fewness of the rich who go before is set before the eyes. Why in a good work is not that Zacchaeus attended to, who gave half his goods to the poor? But we are forced to wish that at least that Pharisee be attended to, who gave tithes of all that he possessed.

**20.** Do not spare perishable treasures, vain treasures. Do not, under the image of piety, increase money. "I keep it for my children": a great excuse. He keeps it for his children. Let us see. Your father keeps it for you, you keep it for your children, your children for their children, and so through all, and no one is going to do the commandments of God. Why do you not rather spend everything on him who made you from nothing? He who made you, himself feeds you from the things he made, himself feeds your children too. For you do not entrust your children better to your patrimony than to your Creator. And people indeed lie. Avarice is evil. They wish to cloak themselves with the name of piety and to whitewash themselves, so that, as it were on account of their children, people may seem to keep what they keep on account of avarice. For that you may know that it generally happens so, it is said of someone, "Why does he not do almsgiving? Because he keeps it for his children." It happens that he loses one. If he was keeping it for his children, let him send his portion after him. Why does he hold it in the world, and leave him out of his mind? Render to him what is his, render what you were keeping for him. "He is dead," he says. But he has gone ahead to God, his portion is owed to the poor. It is owed to him to whom he has gone. It is owed to Christ: for to him he has gone. And he himself said, *What you did to one of these least, you did to me, and what you did not do to one of these least, you do not do to me*[^32]. But what do you say? "I keep it for his brothers." If he were living, he would not be going to divide with his brothers. O dead faith! For your son is dead. Whatever you say, you owe to the dead what you were keeping for the living. "My son is dead, but yet I keep my son's portion for his brothers." Do you so believe that he is dead? If Christ did not die for him, he himself is dead. But if there is faith in you, your son lives. He lives entirely. He did not depart, but went ahead. With what face will you come to your son who has gone ahead, to whom, going ahead, you do not send his portion into heaven? Or cannot it be sent into heaven? It can entirely. Hear the Lord himself saying, *Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven*[^33]. If, then, that treasure is better kept in heaven, is it not then to be sent to your son, when, if it is sent, it will not perish? Will it be held here where it can perish, and not sent there where Christ is the keeper? Surely the things you hold here, and are unwilling to send after your son, to whom do you entrust them? To your agents and procurators. To your agents you entrust the portion of him who has gone ahead, and to Christ, to whom he has gone ahead, you do not entrust it? Is your procurator suitable to you, and Christ less suitable?

**21.** You see, brothers, that it is a lie that people say, "I keep it for my children." It is a lie, my brothers, it is a lie. People are avaricious. Or at least let them be forced to confess what they are unwilling, since they are ashamed to be silent about what they are. Let them pour out, let them vomit in confession what they carry. The breast is pressed by the surfeit of iniquity. Let confession vomit it out, but let it not, in the manner of a dog, return to the vomit. Be Christians. It is very little to be called a Christian. How much do you give to actors? How much do you give to hunters? How much to base persons? You give to those who kill you. For through those very exhibitions of pleasures they slay your souls. And you go mad over who gives more. If you went mad over who keeps more, you would not be endurable. To go mad over who keeps more belongs to avarice. To go mad over who gives more belongs to wastefulness. God wants you neither avaricious nor wasteful. He wants you to lay out what you have, not to throw it away. You contend over who conquers in the worse, you do not take pains over who is better. And would that you did not give over who is worse. And you say, "We are Christians." For the favor of the populace you throw away your goods, against the commands you hold your goods. Behold, Christ does not command, Christ asks, Christ is in want. *I was hungry,* says Christ, *and you did not give me to eat*[^34]. He chose to be in want for your sake, that you might have where to sow the earthly things he gave, and reap eternal life. Do not be slothful and wrongly secure. Amend your ways, redeem your sins. And when you have done these things, give thanks to God, from whom you have received the ability to live well. And so give him thanks that you do not insult those who do not yet live well, but exhort those very people by your conduct. By doing these things, you have perfect justice, as far as it can be in this life. Conducting yourselves in good works, in prayers, in fasts, in almsgiving on account of the small sins, and abstaining from those great sins, you are reconciled with the adversary, and secure you say in prayer, *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^35]. For you have something to be forgiven you daily, who have something to forgive daily. So, walking secure on the way, you will not fear the robberies of the devil, because Christ made himself the way and the great road, and leads to the homeland. There is all security, all rest, where even the very works of mercy will cease, because there will be no want of the wretched there. It will be that sabbath of sabbaths, that what we here desire, there we may find. Amen.
