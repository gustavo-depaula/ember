# Sermon 318: On Stephen the Martyr

**1.** Your Holiness awaits to know what has been placed today in this place. They are the relics of the first and most blessed martyr Stephen. You heard, when the reading of his passion was read from the canonical book of the Acts of the Apostles, how he was stoned by the Jews, how he commended his spirit to the Lord, how also at the last, his knees bent, he prayed for those stoning him. His body from that time until these times lay hidden; but recently it appeared, as the bodies of the holy martyrs are wont to appear, by the revelation of God, when it pleased the Creator. So some years ago, when we were young men set at Milan, the bodies of the holy martyrs Gervasius and Protasius appeared. You know that Gervasius and Protasius suffered long after the most blessed Stephen. Why then theirs first, and his afterward? Let no one dispute: the will of God seeks faith, not a question. But the truth was revealed to him who showed the things found themselves. For by preceding signs the place was shown; and as it had been revealed, so too it was found. Many received relics from there, because God willed it, and they came here. So both the place and the day are commended to Your Charity: each to be celebrated in honor of God, whom Stephen confessed. For we in this place have not made an altar to Stephen, but from the relics of Stephen an altar to God. Such altars are pleasing to God. Do you ask why? Because *precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^1]. They were redeemed by the blood, who shed their blood for the Redeemer. He shed it, that their salvation might be redeemed: they shed it, that his Gospel might be spread abroad. They returned the favor, but not from their own: for that they might be able to do this, he gave it; and that there might come to be what could be done by them, he gave it. By showing favor, he gave the occasion. It came to pass, they suffered, they trampled the world.

**2.** It was a small thing for them to despise the delights of the world; they conquered punishments, threats, torments. For to despise what delights, for the confession of God, is very great: but it is less to despise what delights, than to conquer what troubles. Suppose it said to someone, Deny Christ, and I give you what you do not have: he despised what delights, and did not deny. But that persecutor added: You do not want to receive what you do not have? I take away what you have. Gain does not taste so sweet as loss grieves: because it is easier not to eat than to vomit. He did not acquire, he did not eat: what he had acquired he lost, what he had eaten he vomited. In not eating the gullet is cheated, in vomiting the stomach is overturned. So he is stronger in the confession of Christ who did not fear losses, than he who despised gains. But what kind of losses? The loss of money, the loss of patrimony, the loss of all the things he had. But the enemy had not yet drawn near. The things perished, which lay outside. If they were not loved while they were had, they did not sadden when they were lost. And to say it briefly, when they are lost, they leave as much grief as, when they were had, they could be loved. But for the persecutor of those times, when the saints were being killed, it was too little to say: I take away what you have. I torture, he says, I bind, I kill. He who did not fear this, conquered the world. They carried the contest for the truth to the highest, who contended even to these things. This is what he says in the Epistle to the Hebrews: *For you have not yet resisted to the point of blood in your struggle against sin*[^2]. They are the perfect, who contended against sin to the point of blood. What is "against sin"? Against a great sin: against the denial of Christ. You know how Susanna contended against sin to the point of blood. But lest women alone have consolation from this, and men seek something of their own number, such as appeared in Susanna: you know how Joseph contended against sin to the point of blood. The case is similar. And she had as her false witnesses those very men, to whom she refused to consent, lest she sin; and he had that very woman, to whom he refused to consent. To both, those to whose sin there was no consent, bore false witness; and those who heard believed: but they did not conquer God. She is freed, he too is freed. What, if they had died too, would they not be more freed, when secure they would be crowned? Why did I say, secure they would be crowned? Because no trial would remain. For although Susanna was freed, she was still to be tried; and Joseph himself, freed, was still to be tried. From where, to be tried? because *human life on earth is a trial*[^3]. To death the whole is trial: after death only blessedness; but of the saints, whose death is precious in the sight of God. So both she against sin, that is, against adultery, and he against such a sin, contended to the point of blood. It is a greater sin to deny Christ, than to commit adultery. Adultery is of the flesh, to lie down unlawfully: adultery of the heart is to deny the truth. In faith, in mind, there ought chastity to be. There first our parent Eve was corrupted. Do you want to know in that corruption the greatness of the iniquity? Attend in us, who were born of it, the greatness of the calamity. As witness of this my word I will cite holy Scripture: *From a woman,* it says, *was the beginning of sin made, and because of her we all die*[^4]. What she received as punishment, this the martyrs despise for victory. God threatened them with death, lest they sin: the enemy threatened the martyrs with death, that they might sin. They sinned, that they might die: the martyrs died, lest they sin. From where punishment was inflicted on them, from there glory was raised up for these.

**3.** So they contended, and conquered. The earlier ones conquered: but they did not cut off the bridge by which they crossed, and forbid our approach. It lies open to whoever pleases: nor is such a persecution as they suffered to be wished for; but the trial of human life is daily. Sometimes a faithful man falls sick, and there the tempter is. There is promised him, for his health, an unlawful sacrifice, a harmful and sacrilegious amulet, a wicked incantation, a magical consecration is promised, and it is said to him: This man and that were in worse danger than you, and so escaped; do it, if you want to live; you will die, if you do not do it. See whether it is not, You will die, if you do not deny Christ. What the persecutor said openly to the martyr, this the hidden tempter says to you sidelong. Make for yourself this remedy, and you will live: is this not, Sacrifice, and you will live? If you do not do it, you will die: is this not, If you do not sacrifice, you will die? You have found an equal fight, seek an equal palm. You are in bed, and you are in the stadium; you lie, and you wrestle. Remain in the faith; and while you are wearied, you conquer. So you have, dearly beloved, no small consolation, a place of prayers. Let the martyr Stephen be honored here: but in his honor let the crowner of Stephen be adored.

[^1]: Ps 116:15 (115:15)
[^2]: Heb 12:4
[^3]: Job 7:1
[^4]: Sir 25:24 (25:33 Vulgate)
