# Sermon 163/B

**1.** Recall the reading of the apostolic epistle that mentions: *Brothers*, he says, *if a man is overtaken in any offense, you who are spiritual, instruct such a one in a spirit of gentleness, looking to yourself, lest you too be tempted. Bear one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ. For he who thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing, seduces himself. But let each one prove his own work, and then he will have glory in himself alone, and not in another. For each one will bear his own burden. But let the one who is catechized in the word share in all good things with the one who catechizes him. Do not err, God is not mocked; for what a man sows, that will he also reap; for he who sows in the flesh, from the flesh will reap corruption; but he who sows in the spirit, from the spirit will reap also eternal life. But in doing good let us not grow weary: for in its own time we shall reap untiringly. And so, while we have time, let us work good toward all, but most of all toward those of the household of faith*[^1].

**2.** Thus far it was recited from the Apostle's epistle: thus far I have been to you as a reader. But, my brothers, if the reader is understood, for what is a disputant necessary? Behold, we have heard, we have understood: let us do, and let us live. And what need is there to burden your memory? hold these things, and from there think on them. Or does it perhaps trouble any of you how that is to be understood which he says, *Bear one another's burdens*; and a little after he says, *Each one will bear his own burden*? For you say in your hearts—those of you, however, who noticed this: How do we bear one another's burdens, if *each one will bear his own burden*? How will *each one bear his own burden*, if we bear one another's burdens? I confess, it is a question. *Knock, and it will be opened to you*[^2]; knock by attending, knock by studying, knock also for us by praying, that we may say something worthy of you; thus by knocking you help us, and quickly this question will be resolved. Would that, as quickly as it is resolved, each one might as effectively do what he understood! In the weight of infirmity we bear one another's burdens; in the reckoning of devotion each one will bear his own burden. What is it I have said? All men—what are we but men, and through this weak, and not at all without sin? For in this we bear one another's burdens. For if you grow weary at your brother's sin, and he at yours, you neglect one another, and you truly commit a great sin. But if what he cannot bear, you bear, and what you cannot bear, he bears, you bear one another's burdens; and because you bear one another's burdens, you fulfill the most sacred law of love. For this is the law of Christ; the law of love is the law of Christ. He came for this reason, because he loved us; and there was nothing he might love, but by loving he made us lovable. You have heard what is, *Bear one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ*. What, then, is, *Each one will bear his own burden*? Each one will render an account for his own sin; no one will render an account for another's sin. Each one has his own case; he will render to God his own account. But even the very superiors, who render an account for the flocks of Christ, render an account for their own sin, if they neglect the flocks of Christ.

**3.** Therefore, brothers, *if a man is overtaken in any offense, you who are spiritual*—whoever you are who are spiritual—*instruct such a one in a spirit of gentleness*. And if you cry out, within love. You exhort, you flatter, you rebuke, you rage; love, and do whatever you wish. For a father does not hate his son: and yet a father, if there is need, flogs his son; he inflicts pain, that he may guard his health. This, then, is, *In a spirit of gentleness*. For if a man is overtaken in any offense, and you say: It does not pertain to me; and I say to you: Why does it not pertain to you? and you answer me: Because *each one will bear his own burden*; I will answer you: Surely you gladly heard, and understood: *Bear one another's burdens*. Therefore, if a man is overtaken in any offense, you who are spiritual, instruct such a one *in a spirit of gentleness*. For his own sin he himself will render an account, because each one will bear his own burden; but if you neglect his wound, you will render a bad account for the sin of your negligence; and through this, if you do not bear one another's burdens, you will have a bad account in that each one will bear his own burden. Do this, that you bear one another's burdens, and God spares you in that each one will bear his own burden. For if you bear another's burden, when he is overtaken in any offense, so as to instruct him in a spirit of gentleness, you will come to that place where you heard, *Each one will bear his own burden*; and with a good conscience you say to God, *Forgive us our debts*[^3]. Therefore, brothers, remember: *If a man is overtaken in any offense* . . . Do not take it in passing: *A man*. For he could have said: If anyone is overtaken, if whoever is overtaken; he did not say this, but said, *A man*. But it is very difficult that a man not be overtaken in some offense: for what is man?

**4.** But those spiritual ones, whom he admonished to instruct in gentleness the man who has been overtaken by an offense, perhaps said in their hearts: Let us bear the burdens of those who are overtaken by offenses; because we do not have anything in ourselves that they could bear. Hear, that you ought not to be secure, the following words; see: *Looking to yourself, lest you too be tempted*. Lest the spiritual be proud, lest they exalt themselves; although, if they are spiritual, they will not exalt themselves; I fear lest they exalt themselves for this reason, because they are carnal; yet let even the spiritual one attend, lest he too be tempted. For is he not a man because he is spiritual? Because he is spiritual, does he not carry a *corruptible body* that *weighs down the soul*[^4]? Because he is spiritual, has he ended this life, which is wholly *a temptation upon the earth*[^5]? It is well said to him, wholly well: Looking to yourself, lest you too be tempted. And when he had admonished those—that is, the spiritual—he at once brought in that general sentence: *Bear one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ*[^6]. What is, *one another's*? Let the carnal bear the burden of another carnal one, let the spiritual bear that of another spiritual one. *Bear one another's burdens*, do not neglect one another's sins. Among those with whom you have confidence, reprove; among those with whom you have not confidence to reprove, admonish; and if this is necessary, that no one sin, pray, ask. Or have I perhaps humbled you, because I said, ask? Hear the Apostle: *But commanding*, he says, *we also ask, that you not receive the grace of God in vain*[^7]. The physician, if he finds strength in the sick man, rebukes; but if he does not find strength, and fears lest under the bitterness of the rebuke he perhaps fail, he supplicates, asks, that he may hear, do, and live. It is agreed, then, that the Apostle said this—*Bear one another's burdens*—because he had admonished the spiritual one, and had said, *Looking to yourself, lest you too be tempted*; lest this spiritual one arrogate so much to himself as to think that he has no burden which ought to be borne up by another.

**5.** Hear him again, on account of pomposity, on account of swelling, on account of puffing-up, hear him again: *He who even thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing, seduces himself*[^8]. It could not be said better: he seduces himself. The devil is not to be accused in all things; for sometimes the man himself is his own devil. For why is the devil to be guarded against? Surely for this reason, lest he seduce you. Are you not, then, your own devil, when you seduce yourself? What then? *But let each one prove his own work, and then he will have glory in himself alone, and not in another*[^9]. When you do something good, if it pleases you for this reason, because another praises you; but if another did not praise you for it, you would fail in doing a good work, destitute of the voices of the praiser, you have glory in another, not in yourself. If he praises, you do it; if perhaps your good work displeases a foolish man, you do not do it. Do you not see men pouring out their goods to actors, and holding out nothing to the poor man, how they are praised by the mouths of many? Is what they do therefore good, because they are praised? Wake up at last: *The sinner is praised in the desires of his soul*. You all cried out, because you know the holy Scripture from which we mentioned the testimony; let those who do not know it hear too. The holy Scripture said, the holy Scripture foretold: *For the sinner is praised in the desires of his soul, and he who does unjust deeds is blessed*[^10]. Now then, if the sinner is praised in the desires of his soul, and he who does unjust deeds is blessed, seek praisers. Evil desires tear you apart; commit iniquities daily to fulfill your desires, and seek praisers. Believe me, you will find none but flatterers or seducers. How flatterers, how seducers? I must render an account of my words. Flatterers are those who know you do evil, and yet praise you; but those who praise you doing evil for this reason, because they think what you do is good, are not flatterers, because they praise from the soul, but are seducers, because by the frequency of their praises they seduce you to do those evils, and do not allow you to breathe. For going thus into the empty winds, you think what you do is good: you cast away your goods, you empty your house, you leave your children naked. Those praises made you mad: you run, you move your hands, you receive applause, you put it to your mouth; you pour out your house, and you gather wind. How, you say, are those my seducers, who praise me from the soul? They are your seducers for this reason, because by erring they were first their own. Will he then labor to put ladders up to you, so as not to seduce you, who seduces himself? *Therefore the sinner is praised in the desires of his soul, and he who does unjust deeds is blessed*. Beware of such a praiser, avoid a blesser of this kind. Rather, do good yourself. But, you will say, I will displease that man, if I do it. And displease him, and please God; for if you displease him, and please God, you will have glory in yourself, and not in another. But the evil detract from the good, and the lovers of this age curse the despisers of the age, speak reproaches, seek what to reproach; as soon as something evil is said, they at once believe it: if something good is said, they are unwilling to believe; and your heart is troubled, so that you fail in doing good, because no praiser or flatterer or deceiver has been found, and the testimony of your conscience does not suffice you, in the theater of your breast, under the eyes of God. Why are you troubled, I beseech you, why are you troubled? Because many evil things are said about me. Is this what you say? You would not be troubled in the ship of your heart, unless Christ slept there.

**6.** You heard, when the Gospel was read, a great storm broke out, and the ship was tossed, and was being covered by the waves[^11]. Why? Because Christ was sleeping. When does Christ sleep in your heart, except when you forget your faith? The faith of Christ in your heart is as Christ in the ship. You hear reproaches, you are wearied, you are troubled: Christ sleeps. Awaken Christ, awaken your faith. You have something to do even when troubled; awaken your faith. Let Christ wake, and speak to you: Do reproaches trouble you? Which did I not first hear for you? This Christ says to you, thus your faith addresses you; hear it, and see that it thus addresses you; unless perhaps you have forgotten that Christ suffered for us[^12], and before he suffered such great evils for us, he heard reproaches. He cast out demons, and it was said to him: You have a demon[^13]. Of him it was said through the Prophet, *The reproaches of those who reproach you fell upon me*[^14]. Awaken Christ, then, and he will say to you in your heart, *When men detract from you, and say all evil against you for my sake, rejoice and exult, for your reward is abundant in heaven*[^15]. Believe what was said, and a great tranquillity comes to be in your heart. If, then, a man thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing, he seduces himself; but let each one prove his own work, and then he will have glory in himself, and not in another. Whether he praise or reproach, you have glory in yourself, because your glory is your God in your conscience; and you will be like the wise virgins, who carried oil with them in vessels, so as to have glory not in another, but in themselves. For those who did not carry oil with them begged from them, and their lamps were going out, and they said, *Give us of your oil*[^16]. What is, *Give us of your oil*, but: Praise our works, because our conscience does not suffice us? What seemed obscure in the apostolic reading, as the Lord granted, I have expounded. The rest is plain: it does not seek an expositor, but a doer. But that we may do what we have heard, let us ask him without whose help we can do nothing good; since he himself said to his disciples, *Without me you can do nothing*[^17]. Let us turn to the Lord . . .  
Truly I say to your Charity, that we cannot bear the longing for our own and their complaints even by letters; but because this which you ask the holy old man too has now commanded, I thus conclude the sermon. The birthday of blessed Cyprian is surely now drawing near; on account of that solemnity you wished to be violent in keeping me here: therefore, we who are eager for the word, it is good that we fast also in body.
