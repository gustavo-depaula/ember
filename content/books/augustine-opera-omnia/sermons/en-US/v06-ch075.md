# Sermon 229/F: A Tractate on the Monday of Easter

**1.** Some saw the resurrection of the Lord; others did not believe it when it was announced; and they are reproached by the Lord himself, now present, because they did not believe those who had seen and announced it. How great is the gracious favor shown to the gentiles, and to those born long afterward! What did God grant to those by whom the churches of Christ are now filled? The holy Apostles walked with the Lord, heard the word of truth from his mouth, saw him raising the dead; and they did not believe that the Lord had risen. But we, who were born long after, never saw his bodily presence, heard no word from the mouth of his flesh, beheld with these eyes no miracle done by him; and yet we believed, hearing the writings of those who at that time were unwilling to believe. A most recent event, announced to them, they did not believe: they wrote what we might read; we have heard, and we believe. As for the fact that the Lord Jesus did not will to appear to the Jews, he did not judge them worthy to see the Lord Christ after the resurrection; he showed himself to his own, not to strangers. And while his own preached, strangers believed; and those who were strangers became his own. For many of them, as is read in the *Acts of the Apostles,* many of those who crucified the Lord, who were defiled by the pouring out of his blood; many of those who said: *His blood be upon us and upon our children*[^1], afterward believed the Apostles as they announced. His blood was indeed upon them, but to be washed clean, not to be destroyed; upon some to be destroyed, upon others to be cleansed; just upon those to be destroyed, merciful upon those to be cleansed. And now is the faith of all? Just as then some of the Jews believed and some did not believe, so now too among the gentiles; some have believed, others do not believe. *Not all have faith*[^2]. But those who have faith believe by grace; let them not boast of themselves, it is the gift of God. Did the Lord choose us because we were good? He did not choose the good, but those whom he willed to make good. We were all in the shadow of death, we were all held bound in the mass of sin coming from Adam. With the root corrupted, what kind of fruit could be born from the tree of the human race? But he who would heal vices came without vice; and he who came to cleanse sins came without sin.

**2.** Do not pay heed to the Jews who are now chaff, that is, of that threshing floor which was then threshed. For if we consider, my brothers: from the Jews come the Prophets, from the Jews the Patriarchs, from the Jews the Apostles, from the Jews the Virgin Mary, who bore Christ; from the Jews afterward Paul, who believed, and so many thousands baptized in a single day; from the Jews the countless churches of Christians. But that wheat has now been stored away in the barn: with the chaff the devil will play. There are faithful Jews, and unbelieving Jews. Where were they first condemned? In the first one, in the very father of them all, Jacob, who was also called Israel. Jacob means "Supplanter"; Israel, "Seeing God." When he returned from Mesopotamia with his sons, an angel wrestled with him, bearing the person of Christ; and while he wrestled, though far surpassing him in strength, the angel as it were yielded to him, and Jacob prevailed. So too the Lord Christ yielded to the Jews: they prevailed when they killed him. By great strength he was conquered: where he was conquered, there he conquered for us. What does it mean, where he was conquered, there he conquered for us? Because by that whereby he suffered, he poured out the blood with which he redeemed us. So, then, it is written, Jacob prevailed against him. And yet Jacob himself, who was wrestling, recognized the mystery. The man who wrestled prevailed against the angel; and when he had said to him: *Let me go,* the one who had prevailed said: *I will not let you go unless you bless me*[^3]. O great mystery! The one conquered blesses, who by suffering sets free; then that very thing was the whole blessing. *What are you called? he said to him. He answered: Jacob. You shall not be called Jacob,* he says, *but you shall be called Israel*[^4]. The imposition of so great a name is a great blessing. Israel means, as I said, "Seeing God"; the name of one, the reward of all. Of all: but of the faithful and the blessed, both of Jews and of Greeks. For the Apostle calls all the nations Greeks, because among the nations the Greek tongue is exalted. *Glory,* he says, *and honor*—these are the Apostle's words—*glory and honor and peace to everyone who works good, to the Jew first and to the Greek; wrath and indignation, tribulation and anguish, upon every soul of one who works evil, to the Jews first and to the Greeks*[^5]. To the good Jews, good; to the evil, evil; to the good gentiles, good; to the evil, evil.

**3.** Let not the Jews exalt themselves and say: But behold, Jacob is our father; he prevailed against the angel, and was blessed by the angel. We say: People of Israel, look at yourself there; you are not Israel; you are so called, but you are not; the name is mistaken in you, the crime remains in you. But he says to me: Behold, Jacob is my father, behold, Israel is my father; behold the name, where is the crime? Read there, there I have found you. For there it is written: *And he touched the breadth of Jacob's own thigh, and it withered, and he was limping*[^6]. Jacob, one man, both blessed and lame. In what is he blessed, and in what is he lame? If you have believed in Christ, recognize yourself as blessed; if you have denied Christ, recognize yourself as lame; for you are of those of whom the Prophet says: *And they have gone limping from their paths*[^7]. Of whom were the holy women, to whom the Lord first showed himself when rising? Were they not of the Jews? Of whom were the Apostles, who, even though they did not at first believe the women who announced it, yet afterward heard him, and acknowledged him as he reproached them, and clung to their Master? Were they not of the Jews? Behold Israel, blessed. But in many things lame, in few things blessed; for the breadth of the thigh is the multitude of the race. He did not simply say: He touched the thigh; but: *the breadth of the thigh.* Where there is breadth of the thigh, without doubt there is multitude of the race. And what wonder? I recognize the few grains, I marvel at the heap of chaff; but I see what is owed to the barn, what to the flame. And let them hear now, while they still live; let them correct their limping, let them come to the blessing.

[^1]: Matt 27:25
[^2]: 2 Thess 3:2
[^3]: Gen 32:26
[^4]: Gen 32:27-28
[^5]: Rom 2:10-9
[^6]: See Gen 32:25, 31
[^7]: Ps 18:45 (17:46)
