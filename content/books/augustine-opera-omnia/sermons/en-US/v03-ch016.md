# Sermon 62/A: On the Centurion's Servant

**1.** The faith of this centurion announces the faith of the Gentiles, like a grain of mustard seed, a humble and fervent faith. His servant, as you heard, was sick, and lay paralyzed in the house; but this centurion asked the Savior for the healing of his servant. But the Lord promised that he himself would go to heal his servant. But he, as I said, fervently humble, and humbly fervent: *I am not*, he says, *worthy, Lord, that you should enter under my roof*[^1]. He said himself unworthy that the Lord should enter under his roof; and yet he would not have said these words, unless the Lord had already entered his heart. Then he added: *Only say the word, and my servant will be healed*[^2]. I know whom I address: speak, and what I wish is done. He added also a most sweet and most true likeness. *For I too*, he says, *am a man*, you are God: I *am under authority*, you above all authorities: *having soldiers under me*, you the angels too; *and I say to this one, go, and he goes: and to this one, come, and he comes: and to my servant, do this, and he does it*[^3]. Your handmaid is the whole creation: it is enough that you command, what you command is done.

**2.** And the Lord: *Amen I say to you, I have not found such faith in Israel*[^4]. You know that the Lord took flesh from Israel, of the seed of David, whence was the Virgin Mary, who bore Christ; and to them he came, showed them the face of his flesh, the mouth of his flesh sounded in their ears, the form of his body lay before their eyes. His presence was rendered to the Jews: it was promised to the fathers, and rendered to the sons. And yet this centurion was a foreigner, was of the Romans, served as a soldier there; and he preferred his faith to the faith of the Israelites, so that he said: *Amen I say to you, I have not found such faith in Israel.* What do we think he praised in this man's faith? Humility. *I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof*[^5]. This he praised: and because he praised this, by this he entered. The humility of the centurion was the door of the Lord entering, that he might more fully possess one whom he already possessed.

**3.** So the Lord gave great hope to the Gentiles by this occasion: we were not yet, and already we were foreseen, already foreknown, already promised. For what does he say? *Therefore I say to you, that many from the East and West will come*[^6]. Whither will they come? Where they may believe. There they come: to come is the very believing. He believed, he came; he apostatized, he departed. *They will come*, then, *from the East and West*; not into the temple of Jerusalem, not into some middle part of the earth, not up some mountain; and yet they come to the Jerusalem temple, and to a certain middle part, and a certain mountain. The Jerusalem temple is now the body of Christ: whence he had said: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up*[^7]. The middle place to which they come is Christ himself: he is in the middle, because he is equal to all; whatever is set in the middle is common to all. They come to the mountain, of which Isaiah says: *In the last times the mountain of the Lord will be manifest, prepared on the summit of the mountains; and it will be exalted above all hills, and all nations will come to it*[^8]. This mountain was a small stone, and by growing it filled the world: for so Daniel saw it[^9]. Approach the mountain, ascend the mountain; and you who ascend, do not descend. There you will be safe, there fortified: the mountain of your refuge is Christ. And where is Christ? At the right hand of the Father: for he ascended into heaven. He is very far off: who ascends? who touches? If he is far from you, how do we say truly: *The Lord be with you?* Both he sits at the right hand of the Father, and he does not depart from your hearts.

**4.** Turning to the centurion: *Go, as you have believed, be it done to you; and the servant was healed in that hour*[^10]. As he believed, so it was done. *Say the word, and he will be healed*; he said the word, and he was healed. *As you have believed, be it done to you;* the worst sickness departed from the servant's members. With what wondrous ease the Lord commands the whole creation! For he does not labor to command. Or is the Lord of creation such that he commands the angels, and does not deign to command men? Would that men were willing to serve! But blessed is he whom he commands within, not in the ear of the flesh, but in the ear of the heart, where he corrects, directs. For that the Lord commands all things, understand it from this, that in his command he did not pass over even the little worms. He commanded a worm, and it gnawed the root of the gourd, and the prophet's shade perished. *He commanded*, says Scripture, *the morning worm, and it gnawed the root of the gourd, and the shade perished*[^11]. The morning worm is Christ; the twenty-first psalm, on his passion, has thus: *For the morning receiving*[^12]. In the morning he rose, gnawed the Jewish shade. Therefore, coaxing his bride in the *Song of Songs: Until the day breathe, and the shadows be removed*[^13]. Do you keep the Sabbath carnally? Do you abstain from the flesh of animals that do not chew the cud, or do not have a cloven hoof? Do you offer God sacrifices of victims from the herds? You do none of these. Why? Because the gourd is gnawed, because the shade has ceased, the sun is opened. Invoke refreshment, lest you labor in the heat of the precepts. Here it ends.
