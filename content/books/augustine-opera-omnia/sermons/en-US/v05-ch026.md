# Sermon 164/A

**1.** There are those who think that alms are to be given only to the righteous, but that to sinners such things ought not to be given. In this error the sacrilegious Manicheans hold the first place, who believe that in any food whatever the members of God are held mingled and bound, which they reckon must be spared, lest they be polluted by sinners and entangled in more wretched knots. This madness is perhaps less worthily refuted than it offends the sense of all the sound, if it is merely set forth. But some, thinking nothing of the sort, suppose for this reason that sinners are not to be fed, lest we strive against God, whose indignation is declared in them, as though by this he could even be angry with us, because we wish to help those whom he wishes to punish. They even bring forward testimonies of the holy Scriptures, where we read, *Give mercy, and do not receive a sinner, and to the ungodly and sinners render vengeance. Do good to the humble man, and do not give to the ungodly. For the Most High too hates sinners, and to the ungodly will render vengeance*[^1]. Not understanding how these words are to be taken, they clothe themselves in a detestable cruelty. Hence we ought, on this matter, brothers, to address your Charity, lest by a perverse thought you not consent with the divine will.

**2.** That mercy is to be imparted to all, Paul the apostle teaches most openly, saying, *Untiringly*, he says, *while we have time, let us work good toward all, but most of all toward those of the household of faith*[^2]. Whence indeed it appears clearly enough that in works of this kind the righteous are to be preferred. For whom else should we understand as *those of the household of faith*, when elsewhere it is openly set down, *The righteous lives by faith*[^3]? Yet to other men too, even sinners, the bowels of mercy are not to be shut, not even if they bear a hostile mind against us, our Savior himself saying and admonishing, *Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you*[^4]. Nor is this passed over in silence in the Old Books. For there it is read, *If your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he thirsts, give him drink*[^5], which the Apostle too used in the New[^6].

**3.** Nor are the things we set down above for this reason false, because they too are divine precepts: *Give mercy, and do not receive a sinner*[^7]. For those things were said for this reason: that you should not do good to any sinner because he is a sinner, but that you should do good to the one who hates you, not because he is a sinner, but because he is a man. So you will keep both precepts, neither slack to take vengeance, nor inhuman to help. For everyone who rightly rebukes a sinner—what else does he wish, but that he not be a sinner? He hates, then, in him what God too hates, so that what man made may be destroyed, and what God made may be freed. For man made the sin, but God made the man himself. And when we say these two names, "sinful man," they are surely not said in vain. Because he is a sinner, rebuke him; and because he is a man, have mercy. Nor will you wholly free the man, unless you persecute the sinner. To this office all discipline keeps watch, as it is fitting and suited to each one who governs—not only to the bishop governing his people, but also to the poor man governing his house, the rich man governing his household, the husband governing his wife, the father governing his offspring, the judge governing his province, the king governing his nation. All these, when they are good, surely wish well to those they govern, and according to the power imparted by the Lord of all, who governs even those who govern, they give effort that those whom they govern may both be preserved as men, and perish as sinners. So they fulfill what is written, *Give mercy and do not receive a sinner*—lest they wish that to be saved in him which is that he is a sinner; *and to the ungodly and sinners render vengeance*—and in this, that the very thing by which they are ungodly and sinners be blotted out in them; *do good to the humble man*, on account of this, that he is humble, *and do not give to the ungodly*, on account of this, that he is ungodly, *for the Most High too hates sinners, and to the ungodly will render vengeance*[^8]—who nevertheless, because they are not only sinners and ungodly, but also men, *makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^9]. Thus mercy is to be shut to no man, impunity for no sin is to be relaxed.

**4.** Hence, then, it is especially to be understood how almsgiving is not to be despised which is bestowed on any poor by the right of humanity, since indeed the Lord relieved the want of the poor even from those purses which he filled from the wealth of others[^10]. But if perhaps someone says that neither those weak and beggarly persons whom the Lord rather commanded to be invited[^11], nor those to whom he was accustomed to distribute from the purses, were sinners; and therefore it does not follow that, on account of these gospel testimonies, even sinners are commanded to be received or fed by the merciful; let him attend to what I have already mentioned above—that surely those are sinners and most of all wicked who hate and persecute the Church, of whom, nevertheless, it is said, *Do good to those who hate you*[^12]. And this is built up by the example of God the Father, *who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^13]. Let us not, then, receive sinners because they are sinners, but yet let us treat those very ones, because they are also men, with human consideration. Let us persecute in them their own iniquity, let us have mercy on the common condition. And so, *untiring, while we have time, let us work good toward all, but most of all toward those of the household of faith*[^14].
