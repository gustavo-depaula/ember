# Sermon 177

**1.** The theme of our sermon is the apostolic reading. *We brought nothing*, he says, *into this world, but neither can we take anything away: but having food and covering, with these we are content. For those who wish to become rich fall into temptation and a snare, and into many foolish and harmful desires, which plunge men into destruction and perdition. For the root of all evils is greed, which some craving have strayed from the faith, and engrafted themselves onto many pains*[^1]. A worthy matter to make you attentive to hear, and us prompt to speak. By these words greed is set before our eyes: it is accused, let it not be defended; rather, accused, let it be condemned, lest its defender be condemned with it. But somehow greed so acts in the hearts of men that all—or, to speak more truly and cautiously, nearly all—say in words that it is guilty, and in deeds wish to have it received as a guest. Many said against it many and great and grave and true things—poets and historians and orators and philosophers, and every kind of letters and professions said many things against greed. But it is a great thing not to have it, and much more not to have it than not to be silent about its vices.

**2.** But what is the difference between philosophers, for example, accusing greed, and the Apostles accusing the same thing? What is the difference? If we attend, we learn something that nothing has as its own but the school of Christ. Behold what I just mentioned: *We brought nothing into this world, but neither can we take anything away, having food and covering with these we are content*—many said this. This too: *The root of all evils is greed*—there were those who said it. But what follows, none of them said: *But you, O man of God, flee these things; pursue rather righteousness, faith, love, with those who call upon the name of the Lord from a pure heart*[^2]. Such things none of them said. Far from the rattling cheeks is the solidity of devotion. Wherefore, dearly beloved, since there are outside our society those who both accused greed and despised it; lest they seem great to us or men of God, therefore: *But you, O man of God*. Lest they be in any way compared, we ought first to distinguish and hold this—that we do what we do on account of God. For if the worship of the true God be taken away, any lover of greed whatever is rejected. Yet the truer rule of devotion ought to strike upon us a greater care. For it is base, and exceedingly shameful and grievous, if worshipers of idols were found tamers of greed, and the worshiper of the one God be subjugated by greed, and become the chattel of greed, whose price is the blood of Christ. The Apostle added, and said to Timothy, *I testify before God who gives life to all things, and Christ Jesus who gave testimony under Pontius Pilate, the good confession* (you see how far this is from them) *that you keep the commandment irreproachable until the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, which in his own times the blessed and only Powerful one will show, the King of kings and Lord of lords, who alone has immortality and dwells in inaccessible light, whom no man has seen, nor can see, to whom be honor and glory unto ages of ages*[^3]. Of this household we have been made, into this household we have been adopted; his sons we are, not by our merits, but by his grace. It is too grave, and too horrible, that greed should hold us on earth; when we say to him, *Our Father, who are in heaven*[^4]. By the longing for him let all things become cheap; and let the things among which we were born not be born to us, because for his sake we were reborn. Let these be for the use of necessity, not for the affection of love: let them be like the lodging of a traveler, not like the estate of a possessor. Refresh yourself and pass on. You are making a journey, attend to the one to whom you come, because great is he who comes to you. By departing from this road, you make place for the one coming; this is the condition of lodgings; you yield, that another may succeed. But if you wish to come to the most secure place, let God not withdraw from you, to whom we say, *You have led me through the paths of your righteousness, for the sake of your name*[^5]: not for the sake of my merit.

**3.** The road of mortality, then, is one thing, the road of devotion another. The road of mortality is common to all who are born, the road of devotion is not common to all; for that one all the born walk; this one only the reborn. To that one belong to be born, to grow, to grow old, to die. On account of this food and covering are necessary. Let the provisions of this road be sufficient. Why do you burden yourself? Why do you carry so much on a short road—not what may help you to finish this road, but rather what may, when this road is finished, more grievously burden you? For it is too pitiable, what you wish to happen to you: you burden yourself, you carry much, money presses you on this road, and after this road greed presses you. For greed is an uncleanness of the heart. You take nothing out of this world that you loved: but you take the vice you loved. If you perseveringly love the world, he who made the world does not find you clean. Let there be, then, for temporal use a moderate amount of money, let there be a provision for the journey, with that limit established which is written: *Without love*, he says, *of money a sufficient measure is enough for present needs*[^6]. See before all what he prefixed: *Without love*, he says; so put your hand to it that you loosen your heart from it. For if you wish to bind your heart with love of money, you engraft yourself onto many pains; and where will be, *But you, O man of God, flee these things*[^7]? For he did not say: Leave them, and forsake them; but: *Flee*, as from an enemy. You were seeking to flee with the gold; flee the gold: let your heart flee, and the use is secure. Let there be no cupidity, yet let devotion not be lacking; there is something for you to do with gold, if you are the master of the gold, not the slave. If you are master of the gold, you do with it what is good: if you are a slave, it does with you what is evil. If you are master of the gold, the clothed one praises the Lord because of you: if you are a slave of the gold, the stripped one blasphemes God because of you. But cupidity makes you a slave, love makes you free. Hence a slave, if you do not flee. *But you, O man of God, flee these things*. In this matter, if you do not wish to be a slave, be a fugitive.

**4.** You have heard what to flee, you have also what to pursue. For you do not flee in vain, or so leave that you lay hold of nothing. *Pursue*, then, *righteousness, faith, devotion, love*[^8]. Let these make you rich. These riches are within: the thief does not come to them, unless an evil will gives him place. Fortify the inner chest—that is, the conscience. These riches no robber, no most powerful enemy, no rushing enemy or barbarian, finally no shipwreck will be able to take from you; from which, if you come out naked, you come out full. For he was not truly empty, although he seemed to have nothing outside, who said, *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^9]. Praiseworthy fullness, immense riches: empty of gold, full of God; empty of every transitory possession, full of his Lord's will. Why do you seek gold with such great labors and pilgrimages? Love these riches, and now be filled; their fountain is not hidden, if the heart lies open; the key of faith opens the heart, and both opens and cleanses where you may put it. Do not seem narrow to yourself: your riches, your God, when he enters, he himself will enlarge.

**5.** Therefore, *without love of money a sufficient measure is enough for present needs*. Why *present needs*? Because *we brought nothing into this world, neither can we take anything away*[^10]: therefore *present needs*, not future. But what is it that deceives a man toward the reckoning of greed? What if I live long? He who gives life, does he not give whence you may live out your life? Finally there are incomes: why is a treasure too sought? Something comes in from business, something from craft, something from a price: let it suffice, let it not be treasured up; lest, where you put your treasure, there your heart too remain, and, that it may be on high, you hear in vain, and answer falsely. For when you answer that most sacred word and subscribe with your voice, are you not within accused by your very heart? However pressed and oppressed, does your heart not say to you within: You put me under the earth, why do you lie? Does it not say to you: Am I not there, where your treasure is? Therefore you lie. Or does he lie who said, *For where your treasure is, there will your heart be too*[^11]? You say: It will not be there. Truth says: It will be there. But it will not be there, because I do not love. Prove it by deeds. You do not love, but you are rich. You attend well indeed, and you distinguish yourself; from the one who is rich, you distinguish the one who wishes to be rich. For there is much difference between being rich and wishing to be rich. It is a just distinction, it cannot be denied. There it is means, here it is cupidity.

**6.** For the Apostle himself does not say: Those who are rich, but: *Those who wish to become rich*, who wish to become so, *fall into temptation and a snare, and many harmful desires*: by wishing to become so, not by being so. Therefore he says, *desires*. For there is desire in a man who wishes to attain what he does not have. For no one desires what he has. Greed is indeed insatiable; and yet even in those who have much, there is to be spoken of a desire, not of what they have, but of what they wish to have. He has this estate, he desires to have another which he does not have; but when he has had this too, he will desire another; yet he will not desire what he had, but what he did not have. By wishing, then, to be rich, he desires, burns, thirsts, and, as if by the disease of dropsy, by drinking more thirsts more. A wonderful similitude this is in the disease of the body: a greedy man is wholly a dropsy in the heart. For dropsy in the flesh is full of fluid, is endangered by fluid, and is not satisfied by fluid: so the dropsy in the heart, the more it has, the more it lacks. When it had less, it wished less, rejoiced in fewer things, was gladdened by tiny coffers: but because it has been filled, it has been distended, has been made most abundant (inheritances come daily); it drinks and thirsts. If I have this, I will be able to have that; I can little, because I have little. When you have this too, you have more to wish: want has been increased, not power.

**7.** But I do not love, he says, what I have, that I may have my heart on high. I plainly agree; if you do not love, your heart can be on high. For why should not a free heart be on high? But see whether you do not love: report it faithfully to yourself, not accused by me, but questioned by yourself. Plainly, he says, I do not love: I am indeed rich, but because I now am, and do not wish to be, lest I fall *into temptation and a snare, and many foolish and harmful desires, which plunge a man into destruction and perdition*: a grave evil, horrible, dangerous, deadly—I now am, he says, rich, I do not wish to be. You are now rich? Now, he says. You do not wish to be? No, he says. If you were not, would you be unwilling? I would be unwilling, he says. Now, then, because you are, and the word of God found you rich outside, made you rich within, receive what was said to the rich. For this which was said in these words—*We brought nothing into this world, but neither can we take anything away: having food and covering, with these we are content. For those who wish to become rich fall into temptation*, and the rest; *those who wish*, he says, *to become rich*—was said as it were to the poor. Did these words of the Apostle find you poor? Say them, and you are rich; say in your heart, from your heart: I brought nothing into this world, but neither can I take anything away; having food and covering, with these I am content. For if I wish to become rich, I will fall into temptation and a snare. Say it, and stand where you were found. Do not engraft yourself onto many pains; lest, when you wish to strip yourself, you be torn. But were you found rich? There are other words too, which let us recite: let not the one found rich think nothing was said to him. To the same Timothy the Apostle speaks, but speaks to a poor man. For Timothy was poor, as Paul was. What, then, is he going to say to Timothy on this matter, to a poor man, that may pertain to those who are found rich? Hear what: *Command*, he says, *the rich of this world*: for there are rich of God too; and the true rich are none but the rich of God, such as Paul himself was, who said, *For I have learned, in whatever state I am, to be content*[^12]. Give me a rich man of the world who says this: *I have learned, in whatever state I am, to be content*: but the greedy man is not content. Therefore: *command*, he says, *the rich of this world*. What shall I tell them? Do not wish to be rich? They are now found rich: let them hear what was said to them, where it is the chief point, *not to be proudly wise*. For this riches are had and much loved. A nest of pride is gathered, where it may be nourished and grow; and what is worse, it will not, but remain. Therefore before all, *not to be proudly wise*. That a man may understand, be wise, consider himself mortal, and the equal of a poor mortal. For the earth received both naked, death awaits both, the fever fears neither. The poor man has it on an earthen bed, but neither does the rich man frighten it away as it comes by a silver couch. Therefore: *Command the rich of this world not to be proudly wise*. Let them acknowledge the poor as their equals: the poor are men and men too; the garment is unlike, but the skin is alike: and if a rich dead man is embalmed with spices, there will not be no rottenness, but only later; it rots later—does it not rot? But let us suppose both do not rot: yet both do not feel it. *Command the rich of this world not to be proudly wise*. Let them not be proudly wise: and truly they will be such as they wish to seem: without love they will possess, they will not be possessed.

**8.** But see what follows: *Not to be proudly wise, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches*[^13]. You love gold; make it, if you can, certain, that you may not fear to lose it. You have gathered means; give yourself, if you can, security. *Nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches*. It comes, it goes away; now it was, now it perishes, now it is feared lest it perish. Let them not, then, hope in the uncertainty of riches. He took away hope from there. Where did he fix it? *But in the living God*. There fix your hope, there the anchor of your heart, that the storm of this age may not tear you from there and destroy you: *in the living God, who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy*[^14]. If *all things*, how much more himself? And truly he himself will be to us all things to enjoy. For it does not seem to me said, *Who gave us all things abundantly to enjoy*, except himself. For it seems one thing to use, another to enjoy. For we use according to necessity, we enjoy according to pleasantness. Therefore he gave these temporal things to use, himself to enjoy. If, then, himself, why was it said *all things*, except because it is written, *That God may be all in all*[^15]? Therefore there the heart for enjoying, that the heart may be on high. Loosen yourself from here, but bind yourself there: it is dangerous for you in these storms to remain without a bond.

**9.** *Not to hope in the uncertainty of riches*: yet not nowhere, *but in the living God, who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy*[^16]. What is so "all things" as he who made all things? For these all things would not be made by him, unless he knew them. Who would dare say: God made this which he did not know? He made what he knew. He had it, then, before he made it: but he had it in wondrous ways, not as he made it, temporal and transitory, but as a craftsman makes. He has within what he works outside. There, then, are all things—chief, immortal, indefectible, permanent—and God himself *all in all*: but to his holy ones, to his just ones, he himself will be *all in all*. He himself, then, suffices, alone suffices, of whom it was said, *Show us the Father, and it suffices us*; but, *so long a time*, he says, *I am with you, and you have not known me? He who has seen me has seen the Father too*[^17]. God is all—Father and Son and Holy Spirit. Deservedly he alone suffices. If we are greedy, let us love him. If we desire wealth, let us desire him. He alone will be able to satisfy us, of whom it was said, *Who satisfies your desire with good things*[^18]. Does this not suffice the sinner? Does this so great, so great a good not suffice the sinner? By wishing to have more, he lost all: because *the root of all evils is greed*[^19]. And in Adam himself the root of all evils was greed. For he wished more than he received, because God did not suffice him. Deservedly through the Prophet he rebukes the sinful soul fornicating away from him, and says, *You reckoned that, if you departed from me, you would have something more*. But just as that younger son—behold, you have fed swine; behold, you have lost all; behold, you remained in want, and late, weary, you returned[^20]. Now understand that what the Father was giving you, he himself kept more safely: *You reckoned that, if you withdrew from me, you would have something more*. O sinful soul, filled with fornications, made foul, made colorless, made unclean, and yet loved! Return, then, to the beautiful one, that you may return to beauty: return, and say to him who alone suffices you, *You have destroyed everyone who fornicates away from you*[^21]. What, then, suffices, but what follows? *But for me to cleave to God is good*[^22]. Therefore the heart on high, not on the earth, not in the most beggarly treasure, not in a place of rottenness. *For the root of all evils is greed*. And in Adam himself the root of all evils was greed. For he wished more than he received, because God did not suffice him.

**10.** Attend, then, O rich man, to what you are to do with what you have. You are no longer proudly wise: rightly. You do not hope in the uncertainty of riches: most excellently. But you hope *in the living God, who furnishes us all things abundantly to enjoy*: praiseworthily. Do not, then, be slothful in the things that follow: *Let them be rich in good works*. Let us see these, and what we do not see, let us believe. You said: I have gold, but I do not love it; but the not-loving is within you; if I deserve anything of you, prove it to me too; what you do not hide from your God, prove it to your brother too. Whence, he says, shall I prove it? From what follows: *Let them be rich in good works, give easily*. Be rich for this, that you may give easily. For the poor man wishes to give, and cannot: with him there is difficulty, with you facility. Let this profit you, that you are rich, because when you wish to do it, at once you do it. *Give easily, share*. Do they lose it? *Treasure up for themselves a good foundation for the future*. And lest we love that very gold and silver and estates, and the things that seem beautiful in the means of men, even in that age, and desire such things there, when it is said to us: Migrate there, put your treasure there: he admonished us against carnal thoughts, and said, *That they may lay hold of the true life*[^23]; not gold, which remains on earth, not perishable means, not transitory goods, but the true life. How, then, do we migrate, when this does not pass over there, nor will we have there what we transfer from here? In a way the Lord our God wishes us to be merchants, he makes exchanges with us: what abounds here we give, what abounds there we receive; just as very many make merchandise for shipment: they give one thing in one place, and where they come they receive another. For example, he says to his friend: Receive from me gold here, and give me oil in Africa; and he migrates and does not migrate: he has already transferred what he gave, but he did not receive this. He receives what he desires. Such is this exchange, my brothers, as our trade is. What do we give, and what do we receive? We give what we cannot carry with us, even if we wished, and which therefore perishes. Here let what is less be given, that what is greater may be found there. We give earth, and receive heaven; we give temporal things, and receive eternal; we give perishable things, and receive immortal; finally we give what God gave, and receive God himself. Let us not, then, be slothful in this exchange of things, in this rich and ineffable commerce. Let it profit us that we are here, let it profit us that we were born, let it profit us that we are on pilgrimage. Let us not return destitute.

**11.** Let not the moth of evil thought enter the chest of the heart. Let it not be said: I will not give, lest tomorrow I have nothing. Do not think much about future things: rather, think much about future things, but about the far-off future: *Let them treasure up*, he says, *for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*. Nor in such a way as the Apostle said: *that there be refreshment for others, but for you distress, but from what each one has*[^24]. Only do not love, keep, treasure up, lean upon what is stored; this is to hope in uncertain things. How many rich men slept rich, and rose up poor. For on account of these thoughts, when he had said, *Without love a sufficient measure of money is enough for present needs*, on account of the evil thoughts that say: If I do not have a treasure, who will give to me, when I begin to be in want? Then: There abounds whence I may live, there suffices whence I may live; but what, if a slander strikes me: whence shall I redeem myself? what, if it be necessary for me to litigate: whence shall I lay out expenses? (as long as you can narrate and reckon all the evils that can befall the human race, very often one calamity disturbs the reckoning of the one counting, and the whole that was being counted not only perishes, but will not even remain in the fingers)—therefore against that little worm of thought, against that malignant moth, God set in his Scripture, as certain perfumes are wont to be set on garments, lest they be moth-eaten. What? You were thinking lest you lack on account of this and that, and you were counting many calamities. Did you not fear one great one? Attend to what follows: *Without love a sufficient measure of money is enough for present needs*, for he himself said: *I will not leave you, I will not forsake you*[^25]; you feared I know not what evils, therefore you were reserving money: hold me as your surety. This God says to you. Not a man, not your equal or you yourself, but God says to you, *I will not leave you, I will not forsake you*. If a man promised, you would believe; God promises, and do you doubt? He promised, wrote it, made the bond: be secure. Read what you hold, you hold the bond of God, you have held the debtor himself, from whom you asked that your debts be released. Amen.
