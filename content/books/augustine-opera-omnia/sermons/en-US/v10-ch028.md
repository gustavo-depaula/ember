# Sermon 354: Delivered to the Continent: in Which He First Strengthens Them Against Detractors and the Envious, and Then Commands Them to Beware of Pride

**1. 1.** The Lord admonished this, when the Gospel was read, that whoever believes in him believes in the one who sent him[^1]. That the Savior of the world was sent to us, the truest faith holds: since Christ himself preaches Christ, that is, the body of Christ spread throughout the whole world. For he was in the heavens, and to the persecutor raging on earth he said: *Why do you persecute me?*[^2] Where the Lord so expressed that he is here too in us. So the whole grows: because just as he is here in us, so we too are there in him. This the joining of love makes. He who is our head is the Savior of his body. Christ, then, preaches Christ, the body preaches its head, and the head protects its body. And therefore the world hates us, as we have heard from the Lord himself[^3]. For he was not saying this to the apostles, who were few; that the world hated them; and that they ought to rejoice when men detracted from them and said all evil against them, because on account of these things their reward would be greater in heaven[^4]; the Lord did not say it to them alone, when he said these things: but he said it to his whole body, he said it to all his members. Whoever has wished to be in his body and a member of his, let him not wonder that the world hates him.

**1. 2.** But the Sacrament of his body many receive: but not all who receive the Sacrament are going to have with him also the place promised to his members. Almost all, indeed, by the Sacrament, call his body, because all feed together in his pastures: but there will come one who will divide, and place some at the right hand, others at the left. And each part will say: *Lord, Lord, when did we see you, and minister to you?* or: *Lord, when did we see you, and not minister to you?* Each part will say it: yet to one he will say: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*; to the other: *Go into the eternal fire, which was prepared for the devil and his angels*[^5].

**2. 2.** We ought not, then, to reckon as our enemies only, dearest ones, whoever with a good conscience we are members of Christ; we ought not to think those alone our enemies who are openly outside. For much worse are those who are seen within, and are outside. For they love the world; and therefore they are evil. And indeed they think of us those things which they themselves love, and in the as it were prosperities of this world over which we groan, they envy us. There they think us happy, where we are in peril. But our inner happiness they have neither known; because they have not tasted it. But that whatever the world smiles upon us temporally is more a danger than an adornment for us, they do not know; because they do not know how to distinguish these joys.

**2. 3.** Whence we exhort your love, especially because we see you who have a higher purpose to have gathered more frequently, that is, who in the very body of Christ, by his gift, not by your merits, hold a more excellent place, having a conscience which has been given by God. For that very conscience comes under the suspicion of our evil and envious ones. Yet for this it is bitten, that it may be proved. For if in the very profession of continence we seek the praises of men, we fail at the rebukes of men. When you are a chaste servant of God, behold, the world perhaps suspects you of being unchaste, and bites, and reproaches, and gladly dwells on your detractions; for to a malevolent soul what it suspects most basely tastes as it were sweetly; but if you wished to undertake continence on account of human praises, you have failed at human rebukes and lost the whole of what you had purposed for yourself.

**3. 3.** But further, if you know how to say with the Apostle, *Our glory is this, the testimony of our conscience*[^6]; the rebukes not only do not lessen your reward, but even make it more ample. Yet pray for the one who rebukes, lest he die at the cost of your reward. For from this too we are tested, dearest ones, because if we did not have enemies, there would be none for whom we might pray according to the precept of our Lord saying: *Love your enemies, and pray for those who hate you*[^7]. Whence do we prove, whence do we question our heart, whether we are able to do this, if we experience no enemy, no rebuker, no detractor, no slanderer? You see, then, that the evil too are necessary to the good. We are in a certain way in the goldsmith's furnace, that is, in this world. If you are not gold, you burn along with it. If you are gold, the evil man is your chaff. If you too are chaff, you will be smoke together.

**4. 4.** Nevertheless, first know this, dearest ones, that in the body of Christ the more excellent members are not the only ones. For the conjugal life is praiseworthy, and has its own place in the body of Christ: just as in our own body too not only those parts have a place which are more excellently placed, as the senses occupied the upper part of the body in the face; but unless the feet carried it, whatever is lofty would lie on the ground. Whence the Apostle says: *And the parts of us which are dishonorable are the more necessary. God tempered the body, that there should be no divisions in the body*[^8]. But we know that the members of Christ who lead a conjugal life, if they are members of Christ, that is, if they are faithful, if they either hope for or await the age to come, if they know why they bear the sign of Christ, just as we know that they defer honor to you; we know that they judge you to be better than they themselves are. But as much as they honor you, so much ought you too to render mutual honor to them. For if there is holiness in you, fear lest you lose it. Whence? Through pride. The holiness of the chaste perishes one way, if he becomes an adulterer; it perishes another way, if he becomes proud. And I dare to say, those leading a conjugal life, if they keep humility, are better than the proud chaste. For let your love consider what I say: attend to the devil—is adultery or fornication to be charged against him in the judgment of God? He does none of these things, who has no flesh. Pride and envy alone send him into the eternal fire.

**5. 5.** In the servant of God, then, upon whom pride has crept, envy is at once there. The proud man cannot be without envy. Envy is the daughter of pride: but this mother does not know how to be barren; wherever she is, she at once gives birth. But that it may not be in you, consider this, that in the time of persecution not only Agnes was crowned a virgin, but also Crispina, a married woman: and perhaps, what is not doubted, some of the continent then failed, and many of the married fought, and conquered. Whence not in vain does the Apostle say to all the members of Christ: *Esteeming one another superior to himself, and in honor preventing one another*[^9]. For if you consider these things, you will not be great in your own eyes. For you ought rather to consider what is lacking to you, than what is present to you. What you have, beware lest you lose: what you do not yet have, beseech that you may have. In how many things you are less, you must consider; not in how many things you are greater. For if you consider how much you have surpassed another, fear swelling. But if you consider how much is still lacking to you, you groan; and when you groan, you are healed, you will be humble, you will walk more safely, you will not be cast headlong, you will not be puffed up.

**6. 6.** And would that all could think of the one love. For it alone both conquers all things, and without which all things avail nothing, and which wherever it is draws all things to itself. It is that which *is not envious*. Do you seek the cause? Attend to what follows: *It is not puffed up*[^10]. First in vices is pride, as I had begun to say, then envy. For envy did not give birth to pride, but pride gave birth to envy. For nothing envies except the love of excellence. The love of excellence is called pride. Since, then, in order pride is first, but envy follows: the Apostle, in the praises of love, did not wish to say first, *It is not puffed up*, and afterward, *It is not envious*; but first he said, *It is not envious*; afterward, *It is not puffed up*. Why this? Because when he had said, *It is not envious*, as though you were going to seek the cause why it is not envious, he added: *It is not puffed up*. Therefore, if it is not envious because it is not puffed up; if it were puffed up, it would be envious. Let this grow in you, and the soul is made solid, because it is not puffed up. *Knowledge*, says the Apostle, *puffs up*[^11]. What then? Ought you to flee knowledge, and choose to know nothing rather than be puffed up? Why do we speak to you, if ignorance is better than knowledge? why do we discourse to you? why do we distinguish these things? why do we admonish you of what you know, bring in what you do not know, if knowledge is to be guarded against, lest it puff up? Therefore love knowledge, but set love before it. Knowledge, if it is alone, puffs up. But because *love builds up*[^12], it does not permit knowledge to be puffed up. There, then, knowledge puffs up, where love does not build up: but where it builds up, it is made solid. There is no swelling, where the rock is the foundation.

**7. 7.** But how greatly does swelling, that is, elation, tempt us, so that on account of this vice even so great an Apostle said there was set against him a sting of the flesh, an angel of Satan, by whom he was buffeted? He who is buffeted, his head is struck, lest it be raised up: for there too the fear from knowledge was of swelling, that is, of elation. For he says: *Lest in the greatness of my revelations I be lifted up.* There, then, elation was to be feared, where the revelation of great things was: *Lest in the greatness of my revelations I be lifted up, there was given me a sting of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me. On account of which I three times asked the Lord to take it from me: and he said to me: My grace suffices you; for power is perfected in weakness*[^13]. The sick man asks that what the physician applies for health be taken away when the sick man wishes. The physician says: No; it bites, but it heals. You say: Take away what bites. The physician says: I do not take it away, because it heals. Why did you come to the physician? To be healed, or not to suffer trouble? The Lord, then, did not hear Paul according to his will, because he heard him according to health. For it is not a great thing to be heard by God according to one's will; it is not great. Do not think it great, when anyone prays, as something great, if he is heard. Seek what he prays, seek in what he is heard. For do not hold it great to be heard according to will: hold it great to be heard according to benefit. According to will the demons too were heard, and were permitted to go to the swine which they had asked for[^14]. According to will even their prince the devil was heard; from whom Job to be tempted was not denied, that the one might be proved, the other confounded[^15]. According to will the Israelites too were heard; and while the food was still in their mouth, you know what befell them[^16]. Do not, then, hold it great to be heard according to will. Sometimes God when angry gives what you ask, and God when favorable denies what you ask. But when you ask of him those things which God praises, which God commands, which God promises in the age to come; ask securely, and bend yourselves, as much as you can, to prayers, that you may receive. For those things are granted when God is favorable: those are bestowed not by his anger, but by his mercy. But when you ask temporal things, ask with measure, ask with fear: commit it to him, that, if they profit, he give; if he knows they hurt, he not give. But what hurts, what profits, the physician knows, not the sick man.

**8. 8.** There are, then, humble continent ones, there are proud ones. Let not the proud promise themselves the kingdom of God. The place to which continence leads is more excellent: but *he who exalts himself will be humbled*[^17]. Why do you seek a loftier place by the appetite of loftiness, which you can attain by the keeping of humility? If you exalt yourself, God casts you down: if you cast yourself down, God lifts you up. It is the sentence of the Lord: nothing can be added or taken away. But so far do continent men often grow proud, that they are ungrateful not only to any men whatever, but even to their parents, and are exalted against their parents. Why? Because those begot, these despised marriage. Whence would those be ungrateful who had despised marriage, unless those had begotten? But the son is better than his married father, because he himself did not take a wife: and the daughter is better than her married mother, because she did not seek a husband. If more proud, in no way better: if better, without doubt more humble. If you wish to find yourself better, question your soul, whether you see swelling there. Where there is swelling, there is emptiness. The devil, where he has found something empty, contrives to make a nest.

**9. 9.** Finally, my brothers, I dare to say, it is expedient for proud continent ones to fall, that in that very thing in which they exalt themselves they may be humbled. For what does it profit one in whom there is continence, if pride lords it? He has despised that from which man is born, and craves that from which the devil fell. You have despised marriage, you have done well; you have chosen something better: but do not be proud. From marriage man is born, from pride the angels fell. If I consider your good things one by one, you are better than your father, who have despised marriage; and you are better than your mother, who have despised marriage. For virginal holiness is better than conjugal chastity. If these two are compared together, that is better than this: who doubts it? But by adding two others, pride and humility; in these two I ask you, and answer me about these: Which is better, pride or humility? You answer: Humility. Join it to virginal holiness. But let pride not only not be in your virginity, but not remain even in your mother. For if you hold pride, and your mother humility, the mother will be better than the daughter. Again I will compare you. A while ago, when I considered them one by one, I had found you better: now when I consider them two by two, I do not doubt to prefer a humble woman to a proud virgin. And how to prefer? See how I prefer her whom a while ago I was comparing. Good is conjugal chastity, better is virginal integrity. I was comparing two goods, not an evil and a good; but I was distinguishing a good and a better. But further, when I set those two, pride and humility, can we say: Good is pride, but better is humility? But what do we say? Pride is an evil, humility a good: and pride a great evil, humility a great good. If, then, of these two one is evil, and the other good: the evil is joined to your greater good, and the whole becomes evil: the good is joined to your mother's lesser good, and it becomes a great good. The mother will have a lesser place in the kingdom of heaven, because she was married, than the daughter, because she is a virgin. For the daughter virgin a greater place, the mother married a lesser place, yet both there: as a shining star, a dim star, yet both in heaven. But if your mother is humble, you proud: she will have some place, but you no place. And who finds another place who there has no place, except with him who fell from there, and cast down the one standing? From there the devil fell, from where he cast down the man standing. He cast down the one standing: but Christ, coming down, raised up the one lying. But from where your Lord raised you up, attend. He raised you up by humility, made obedient unto death, he humbled himself[^18]. Your prince humble, and you proud? The head humble, and the member proud? Far be it. He who loves pride does not wish to be of the body of the humble head. But if he will not be, let him see where he will be. I do not wish to say it, lest I seem to have terrified too much. Rather would that I had terrified, and accomplished something. Would that he who was such, or she who was such, may be so no longer. Would that I had poured in these words, and not poured them out. The whole is to be hoped from the mercy of God: because he who terrifies, saddens; he who saddens, consoles; but only if the one who is saddened is amended.
