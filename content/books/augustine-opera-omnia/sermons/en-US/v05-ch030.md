# Sermon 167/A

**1.** *Our struggle is not against flesh and blood*; because not only does a man persecute you, but the devil through him, and before he harms you in the body, he kills you in the mind. *Our struggle is not against flesh and blood*, men against men, who are flesh and blood, *but against principalities and powers, the rulers of these darknesses*[^1]; because, as Christ governs and rules those who are light, so the devil casts down and instigates to every evil those who are darkness. This, then, the Apostle exhorts us: that we pray not against the evil man, but against the devil who works with him, and do whatever we can, that the devil be driven out and the man be freed. For just as, if against someone set in battle from the adversary's side an armed man comes sitting on a horse, he is angry not at the horse but at the rider, and as much as he can, he desires to do this—to strike the rider and possess the horse; so it must be done concerning evil men, and we must labor with all our strength not against them, but against the one who instigates them, so that, while the devil is conquered, that unhappy one whom he had begun to possess may be freed.

**2.** *He who says that he abides in Christ ought himself also to walk as he walked*[^2]. What is this way in which Christ walked? What is it but love, of which the Apostle says, *I show you a yet more surpassing way*[^3]? If, then, we wish to imitate Christ, we ought to run by that very way by which Christ, even hanging on the cross, deigned to walk. For he was fixed on the cross, and, running the way of love, he supplicated for his persecutors. Finally he said thus: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^4]. And let us too, then, continually supplicate this for all our enemies, that the Lord grant them an amendment of morals and the forgiveness of sins.

[^1]: Eph 6:12
[^2]: 1 John 2:6
[^3]: 1 Cor 12:31
[^4]: Luke 23:34
