# Sermon 235: On the Monday of Easter Week

**1.** Yesterday, that is, last night, the resurrection of the Savior was read from the Gospel, and read from the Gospel according to Matthew. Today, however, as you heard the reader announce, the resurrection of the Lord was recited to us as the Evangelist Luke wrote it down. As you must often be reminded, and ought to hold in memory, it should not disturb you that one Evangelist says something, if another leaves something out, because even the one who leaves out what another says, says something that the other had left out. For some things the individual ones say, which the other three do not say; some things two say, which others do not say; some things three say, and one does not say. But so great is the authority of the holy Gospel that, because in them one Spirit was speaking, what one has said is true as well. This, then, which you have just heard, that the Lord Jesus, after he had risen from the dead, found two of his disciples on the road, talking with each other about the things that had happened, and said to them: *What are these words that you are speaking with one another*[^1], and so on, the Evangelist Luke alone said. Mark touched on it briefly, that he appeared to two on the road, but what they said to the Lord, or what the Lord said to them, he left out.

**2.** What then has this reading brought us? Something great, if we understand. Jesus appeared. He was seen by the eyes, and was not recognized. The Master was walking with them on the road, and he himself was the road. And they were not yet walking on the road; for he found that they had strayed from the road. For when he had been with them before the passion, he had foretold everything: that he would suffer, would die, would rise on the third day. He had foretold everything, but his death was their forgetfulness. They were so disturbed when they saw him hanging on the wood, that they forgot the teacher, did not await the one who would rise, and did not hold to the one who promised. *We*, they say, *were hoping that he was the one to redeem Israel*[^2]. O disciples, you were hoping! So you no longer hope? See, Christ lives. Is hope dead in you? Truly Christ lives. The living Christ found the hearts of the disciples dead; to whose eyes he appeared and did not appear. He was both seen and hidden. For if he was not seen, how did they hear him questioning, how did they answer when he questioned? On the road he walked with them as a companion, and he himself was the guide. They surely saw, but did not recognize. For *their eyes*, as we have heard, *were held, so that they should not recognize him*[^3]. They were not held so that they should not see, but *held so that they should not recognize him*.

**3.** Come now, brothers, where did the Lord wish to be recognized? In the breaking of the bread. We are safe: we break bread, and we recognize the Lord. He did not wish to be recognized except there, for our sake, who were not to see him in the flesh, and yet were to eat his flesh. Whoever, then, you are who are faithful, whoever you are who are not called Christian in vain, whoever you are who do not enter the church without cause, whoever you are who hear the word of God with fear and hope, let the breaking of the bread console you. The Lord's absence is not absence. Have faith, and he whom you do not see is with you. Those men, when the Lord was speaking with them, neither had faith, because they did not believe that he had risen, nor hoped that he could rise. They had lost faith, they had lost hope. Dead, they walked with the living one; dead, they walked with life itself. Life was walking with them, but in their hearts there was not yet life. And so you too, if you wish to have life, do what they did, that you may recognize the Lord. They received him as a guest. For the Lord was like one going to a distant place, but they held him. After they came to the place where they were heading, they said: *Stay here with us now, the day has declined toward evening*[^4]. Hold the guest, if you wish to recognize the Savior. What unbelief had taken away, hospitality restored. The Lord, then, presented himself in the breaking of the bread. Learn where you should seek the Lord, learn where you should have him, learn where you should recognize him. When you eat. For the faithful know something that they understand better in this reading than those who do not know.

**4.** The Lord was recognized by them; and after he was recognized, he nowhere appeared. He was absent from them in body, while he was held by faith. For this reason our Lord absented himself in body from the whole Church, and ascended into heaven, so that faith might be built up. For if you know only what you see, where is faith? But if you believe even what you do not see, when you see it you will rejoice. Faith is built up, because the form will be restored. What we do not see will come, it will come, brothers, it will come. See how it finds you. For it will come, though men say: Where is it, when is it, what is it like? when will it be? when is it to come? Be certain, it will come. Not only will it come, but even if you do not wish it, it will come. Woe to those who did not believe, and great joy to those who believed. The faithful will rejoice, the unfaithful will be confounded. The faithful will say: Thanks to you, Lord; we heard the truth, we believed the truth, we hoped for the truth, we behold the truth. But the unfaithful will say: Where is what we did not believe? where is what, when those things were read, we thought to be lies? So it will come about that punishment is rendered for confusion, reward for gladness. *For those* will go *into eternal burning, but the just into eternal life*[^5]. Turning to the Lord, etc.

[^1]: Luke 24:17
[^2]: Luke 24:21
[^3]: Luke 24:16
[^4]: Luke 24:29
[^5]: See Matt 25:46
