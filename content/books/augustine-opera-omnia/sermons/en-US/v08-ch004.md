# Sermon 276: On the Feast of the Martyr Vincent

**1.** In the passion that was recited to us today, my brothers, there is clearly shown a ferocious judge, a bloody torturer, an unconquered martyr. In his body, furrowed with various punishments, the torments had already failed, and yet the limbs still held out. So much impiety, convicted by so many miracles, persisted; so much weakness, harried by so many sufferings, did not yield; let the divinity that was at work, then, be acknowledged. For how could corruptible dust hold out against such monstrous torments, unless Christ dwelt in it? For in all these things he is to be acknowledged, he is to be glorified, he is to be praised, who both in the first calling gave faith, and in the last passion gave strength. Do you wish to know that both were given as a gift? Hear the apostle Paul: *To you*, he says, *it has been granted for Christ's sake, not only to believe in him, but also to suffer for him*[^1]. Both of these the levite Vincent had received—he had received them and possessed them. For if he had not received them, what would he have? He had confidence in speech, he had endurance in suffering. Let no one, then, presume upon his own heart when he utters speech; let no one trust in his own strength when he undergoes temptation: because both that we may speak good things prudently, our wisdom is from him; and that we may bear evil things bravely, our endurance is from him. Recall the Lord Jesus Christ in the Gospel admonishing his disciples; recall the King of martyrs equipping his cohorts with spiritual arms, pointing out the battles, supplying the aids, promising the rewards; who, when he had said to his disciples: In this world you will have affliction, at once added, that the terrified heart might be consoled, saying: *But take heart, I have overcome the world*[^2]. Why then do we wonder, dearly beloved, if Vincent conquered in him by whom the world was conquered? *In this world,* he says, *you will have affliction;* so that, if it presses, it may not overwhelm; if it assaults, it may not defeat.

**2.** The world brings forth a double battle line against the soldiers of Christ. Take note, brothers. A double battle line, I said, the world brings forth against the soldiers of Christ. For it flatters, in order to deceive; it terrifies, in order to break. Let our own pleasure not hold us, let another's cruelty not terrify us; and the world is conquered. To both approaches Christ comes to meet, and the Christian is not conquered. If in this passion human endurance is considered, it begins to be incredible; if the divine power is recognized, it ceases to be wonderful. So great a cruelty was raging in the martyr's body, and so great a tranquillity was brought forth in his voice; so great a harshness of punishments was raging in his limbs, and so great a security was sounding in his words, that in a wondrous way it might be thought, while Vincent was suffering, that some other Vincent was being tortured, or at least that another was speaking. And truly, dearly beloved brothers, so it was: it was utterly so; another was speaking. For Christ promised this also to his witnesses in the Gospel, whom he was preparing for contests of this kind. For he says thus: *Do not plan beforehand how or what you are to speak. For it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you*[^3]. So the flesh was suffering, and the Spirit was speaking. And as the Spirit spoke, not only was impiety convicted, but weakness too was strengthened.

**3.** So many torments made the martyr more illustrious to us. For, pierced by a manifold variety of wounds, he did not abandon the fight, but renewed it more fiercely. You might think that the flame hardened him rather than burned him; and that, like a potter's furnace receiving soft clay, it made him into a hard vessel of pottery. Our martyr could say to Dacian: My flesh no longer cares for your fires, because: *My strength is dried up like a potsherd*[^4]. And since it is truly written: *The potter's furnace tests the vessels, and the testing of tribulation tests righteous men*[^5]; Vincent was tested and refined by that fire: but Dacian truly burned and crackled. For if he was not burning, why was he smoking? For what were the words of the angry man but the smoke of one burning? So, while our martyr had refreshment in his heart, the other was bringing the flames to bear from without; but he himself, kindled by the torches of his fury, was burning within like an oven, and was consuming his indweller, the devil. For through Dacian's furious voices, through his fierce eyes and threatening looks and the movements of his whole body, that inner indweller of his was shown; and through these visible signs he was betrayed, as through the bursting cracks of his little vessel, which he had filled. The torments did not torment our martyr as much as madness was devastating that man.

**4.** But now, brothers, all those things have passed away—both the wrath of Dacian and the punishment of Vincent. Yet the punishment of Dacian and the crown of Vincent remain. Then, leaving aside for the moment the bounds of future recompense, let us show the glory of the martyrs even in this world. What region today, what province across the sea, as far as either the Roman empire or the Christian name extends, does not rejoice to celebrate the birthday of Vincent? But who today would have heard even the name of Dacian, had he not read the passion of Vincent? And that the Lord preserved the martyr's body with such great care—what else did it show, except that he governed him living, whom he did not abandon lifeless? Living, then, Vincent conquered Dacian; he conquered him also dead. Living, he trampled the torments; dead, he swam across the seas. But the one who guided the dead corpse amid the waves was the same who, amid the iron claws, gave up his unconquered soul to God. The torturer's flame did not bend his heart, the water of the sea did not sink his body. But in all these things there is nothing else than that *precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^6]. To which glory may the Lord bring us under his protection, to whom is honor and dominion forever and ever. Amen.

[^2]: John 16:33
[^3]: Matt 10:19-20
[^4]: Ps 22:15 (21:16)
[^5]: Sir 27:5
[^6]: Ps 116:15 (115:15)
