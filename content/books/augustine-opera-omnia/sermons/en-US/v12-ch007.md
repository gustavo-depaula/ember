# Sermon 204C

**1.** The coming of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ in the flesh began to be revealed to the nations at the very beginning of his birth from the Virgin, when magi came from the east to worship him. This day became a most solemn feast, with its due holiness, for the Church, especially the Church that grew from the gentiles and joined the second wall, a more abundant structure, to the cornerstone. Thus he, our peace, made both one, forming the Church from the circumcised and the uncircumcised. Hence he was called the cornerstone, joining in himself those coming from different directions, to transform both in one body for God, as the teaching of the prophets, the Gospel, and the apostles bears witness. When the Lord was born of the Virgin—the day we celebrated very recently—the shepherds, informed by angels according to the Evangelist Luke, came to the manger of the infant Word. Those shepherds were Israelites: here was one wall prefigured, running ahead from the circumcision to the cornerstone. A few days later, on this day, the magi whom the Evangelist Matthew mentions are believed to have come from the east and recognized that little child's greatness. Here the other wall, running from the hopelessness of the uncircumcised, specifically figured us and doubled our feast with a joy especially our own.[^1]

**2.** For they saw a completely unfamiliar star in heaven, not a few days earlier but almost two years earlier, as they disclosed to Herod when he questioned them. Hence *he killed the infants two years old and under, according to the time*, as it is written, *he had ascertained from the magi*. Having marveled at this star, as we understand, they also deserved to learn through a subsequent revelation whose it was: the king of the Jews'. When he was born and they had learned this too through his grace, they were able to arrive today to worship him. Certainly these foreigners from far away, entirely outside that kingdom, did not think they owed such great honor to a king of the Jews of the sort who ordinarily reigned there. They had learned that someone had been born in worshiping whom they did not doubt they would obtain the salvation that comes from God. He was not even of an age to attract human flattery. No royal throne lay beneath his knees; no purple shone from his limbs, no crown from his head. No display of attendants, no terror of an army, no fame of glorious battles had drawn these men from distant lands to him with such an eager desire to worship. A newborn child lay in a manger, tiny in body and contemptible in poverty. Yet something great lay hidden in the little one, something those men, the firstfruits of the nations, had learned not from the earth that carried him but from heaven that proclaimed him. They came from so far away in such supplication to what they did not see by looking and whose favor they longed to obtain by worshiping. The queen of the South indeed had come from the ends of the earth to hear Solomon's wisdom, moved not by the height of his kingdom but by the light of his mind. Yet Solomon possessed teaching that, through his words spread far and wide, could stir eager minds to learn. Thus she had come not to worship a hidden God but to hear a speaking man. But here the one who had given Solomon that wisdom, who was God's power and wisdom, clothed in the weakness of flesh, was still silent on earth because of his tender age, yet already shone in heaven because of his boundless majesty.[^2]

**3.** Why are you afraid, Herod? Why is your malicious mind troubled? If you cared for your salvation, the one born is he through whom you may see God's kingdom, not one whom you should envy for a human kingdom. Why do you rage? Why do you kill infants? Why, guilty yourself, do you pursue an innocent age? No one will be safe from this king's sword through cruelty, but through devotion. Herod's madness, then, is detestable, but the Jews' foolishness is more astonishing. For their judgment, by divine arrangement, that star withdrew for a little while from the sight of the magi seeking Christ. For the star that appeared to show the place where the child was could certainly also have pointed out the city. But that Christ was to be born in Bethlehem of Judah had to be brought forth from the holy books by the Jews when questioned: ready to read, blind to understand, hard to believe. Let us too, then, leave them in the oldness of the letter and, helped by their books, recognize with these foreigners that Christ must be worshiped.[^3]

[^2]: Matt 2:16; cf. Luke 2:7, 16; cf. Ps 19:1 (18:2 Vg); cf. 1 Kgs 10:1; Matt 12:42; cf. 1 Cor 1:24.
[^3]: Cf. Matt 2:16; cf. Matt 2:5; cf. Rom 7:6.
