# Sermon 31

**1** The psalm that is sung to the Lord seems to suit the holy martyrs, but if we are members of Christ, as we ought to be, let us understand that it pertains to us all: *Those who sow in tears will reap in joy. Going, they went and wept, casting their seeds. But coming, they will come in exultation, carrying their sheaves*[^1]. Going where, and coming whence? Sowing what in tears? What are the seeds? What the sheaves? Going into death, coming from death. Going by being born, coming by rising again. Sowing good works, reaping the eternal reward. The seeds, then, are ours, whatever good we do; our sheaves, what we will receive in the end. If, then, the seeds are good, the good works, why with tears, since God loves a cheerful giver?

**2** Here first see, dearly beloved, how these words pertain especially to the blessed martyrs. For none spent so much as those who spent themselves, as the apostle Paul says: *And I myself will be spent for your souls*[^2]. For they spent themselves, by confessing Christ, and by fulfilling, with his help, what was said: *You have sat at a great table; know that you must prepare such things*[^3]. What is the great table, except that from which we receive the body and blood of Christ? What is *Know that you must prepare such things*, except what blessed John expounds: *As Christ laid down his life for us, so we too ought to lay down our lives for the brothers*[^4]? See how much they spent. But did they perish, when from the Lord they received security even concerning a hair? Does a hand perish, where a hair does not perish? Does a head perish, where a hair does not perish? Where an eyelid does not perish, does the eye perish? Having received, then, this great security, they spent themselves. Let us, then, sow with good works while there is time, the Apostle saying: *He who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly. Tirelessly*, he says, *while we have time, let us work good toward all, but most of all toward those of the household of faith*[^5]. And again: *In doing good*, he says, *let us not fail; for in its own time we will reap*[^6]. He who fails in the sowing will not rejoice in the harvest.

**3** Why, then, in tears, since all our good works ought to have cheerfulness? And indeed of the martyrs it can be said that they sowed in tears. For they fought bravely, and were in great tribulations. For that Christ might console their tears, he transferred them and transfigured them into himself and said: *My soul is sorrowful unto death*[^7]. Yet, my brothers, it seems to me that our Head had compassion on his weaker members, lest perhaps the weak members should despair of themselves, and, as human frailty is, be disturbed at the approach of death, and say that they did not belong to God—for if they belonged, they would rejoice. Therefore Christ first said: *My soul is sorrowful unto death. Father, if it can be, let this cup pass from me*[^8]. Who says this? What power, what weakness? Hear who says it: *I have power to lay down my soul and I have power to take it up again. No one will take it from me, but I lay it down and take it up again*[^9]. This power was sorrowful, when it did what it would not do unless it willed. For he did it by power, not by condition, because he himself willed it, not because the Jew was able. He transfigured into himself, then, the weak members of his body. And perhaps of them it was said: *Those who sow in tears will reap in joy*[^10]—that is, of the weaker. For that great herald of Christ was not sowing in tears when he said: *For I am now being offered, and the time of my dissolution is at hand. I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith. For the rest there remains for me a crown of justice*[^11]—a crown from the sheaves. *There remains for me*, he says, *a crown of justice, which the Lord, the just judge, will render to me on that day*. As if to say: He will render me the harvest, to whom I expend myself as the sowing. These words, as far as we understand, brothers, are of one exulting, not of one weeping. Was he in tears when he said these things? Was he not like the cheerful giver, whom God loves? Let us, then, refer these words to the weak, that even those who sowed in tears may not despair, because, even if they sowed in tears, the grief and groaning will pass. Sadness passes with an end, and gladness comes without end.

**4** Yet, dearly beloved, see how it seems to me that what was said pertains to all: *Those who sow in tears will reap in joy. Going, they went and wept, casting their seeds. But coming, they will come in exultation, carrying their sheaves*[^12]. Hear, if with the Lord's help I am able to explain how it pertains to all: *Going, they went and wept*. From the time we are born, we go. For who stands still? Who, from the time he entered life, is not forced to walk? The infant is born, and by growing he walks. Death is the end. To the end we must come, but with exultation. For who does not weep here on this evil road, when the infant himself begins from there? Surely the infant, when he is born, is poured out from the narrowness of the womb into the breadth of this world, comes forth from darkness to light. And yet, coming from darkness to light, he can weep, he cannot laugh. For such is this life, that when one rejoices here, fear lest it deceive. When one weeps here, pray that you may escape. And tribulation passes, and tribulation comes. And men laugh, and men weep. And what men laugh at is to be wept over. But one weeps over his loss, another weeps over his distress because he is set in prison, another weeps because he has lost some one of his dearest, now dead. One for this, one for that. The just man, for what? First, for all these things. For the just man truly weeps over those who weep barrenly. He weeps over those who weep, he weeps over those who laugh, because both those who weep over vain things weep emptily, and those who laugh over vain things laugh to their own harm. He weeps everywhere; therefore he himself weeps the more.

**5** But *they will come with exultation, carrying their sheaves*[^13]. You see, then, that the just man, when he does a good work, is cheerful. Yes, plainly he grows cheerful. *For God loves a cheerful giver*[^14]. When, then, does he weep? When by praying he commends his good works. The prayers of the saints the psalm wished to commend, the prayers of pilgrims, the prayers of those laboring on this road, the prayers of those who love, the prayers of those who groan, the prayers of those who sigh for the eternal homeland, until they are satisfied by seeing it, who now are set in mourning. For indeed, my brothers, *as long as we are in the body, we are away from the Lord*[^15]. The pilgrimage does not long for the homeland without a tear. If you long for what you do not have, pour out tears. For whence will you say to God: *You have set my tears in your sight*[^16]? Whence will you say to God: *My tears have become my bread day and night*[^17]? They have become my bread: they have consoled me groaning, they have fed me hungering. *My tears have become my bread day and night*. Whence? *While it is said to me daily: Where is your God?*[^18] What just man did not have these tears? He who did not have them did not grieve that he was a pilgrim. With what face does he come to the homeland who, absent, did not sigh? Is it not said to us daily: Where is your God? Learn, my brothers, learn to be among the few. Live well, walk the way of God, and see that you hear: *Where is your God?* You will be happy when you hear this, unhappy if you say it. For when we defend the Christian faith and it is answered us, "See, the name of Christ is preached everywhere, why do evils abound?" what else is said but: *Where is your God?* He who hears it groans, because he who says it perishes.

**6** There are the tears of the devout, there are the tears of the saints, which their prayers indicate. He works well, and is cheerful. And he weeps that he may work well, he weeps because he has worked well. By weeping he demands the good work, by weeping he commends the good work he has done. Frequent, then, are the tears of the just, but on this road. Surely not in the homeland? Why not in the homeland? Because *coming, they will come in exultation, carrying their sheaves*[^19]. Happiness comes; surely the tear does not return? But moreover those who here weep emptily, laugh emptily, scattered by their desires, when they are cheated they groan, when they cheat they exult. They too weep on this road, they too weep, but not in exultation. *But coming, they will come in exultation, carrying their sheaves*. What do they gather, who sowed nothing? Rather they do gather, but what they sowed. Because they sowed thorns, they gather fire, and they go, not from weeping to laughter like the saints: *Going, they went and wept, casting their seeds, but coming, they will come in exultation*—but those, from weeping to weeping, from weeping mixed with laughter to weeping without laughter. For what will happen to them? Where do they go when they have risen again? What, except what the Lord said: *Bind their hands and feet, and cast them into the outer darkness*[^20]? Come, what then? Will there be darkness and no pain? Will they perhaps grope but not suffer? Will they not see, but not be tormented? Far be it. There will not be darkness only; not only is the sight taken from them in which they rejoiced, but they are also given something over which to groan forever. For lest you should despise darkness, O whoever you are, you disgraceful man, who are accustomed, on account of your evil deeds and your lascivious adulteries, not only not to dread darkness but to seek it, who are accustomed to rejoice the more when the lamp is put out—you will not have such darkness there to rejoice in, where you may take delight, where you may regale yourself with the pleasures of the flesh. The darkness itself will not be so. But how will it be? *There will be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^21]. The torturer without ceasing, the tortured without ceasing. Neither is the one who tortures wearied, nor does the one who is tortured die. There will be, then, eternal tears for those who have lived so. There will be eternal joys for the saints, when *coming, they will come in exultation, carrying their sheaves*. For at the time of harvest they will say to their Lord: "Lord, with your help we did what you commanded; render what you promised."
