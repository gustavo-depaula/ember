# Sermon 204: On the Epiphany of the Lord

**1.** A few days ago we celebrated the birthday of the Lord; today we celebrate the Epiphany, which by a Greek word signifies "manifestation," and refers to what the Apostle says: *Without doubt great is the sacrament of piety, which was manifested in the flesh*[^1]. So both days pertain to the manifestation of Christ. For on that one he was born a man from a human mother, who without beginning was God with the Father; but he was manifested to the flesh in the flesh; because the flesh could not see him as he was in the spirit. And on that day, indeed, which is called his birthday, the Jewish shepherds saw him; but on this present day, which is properly called "Epiphany," that is, "manifestation," the Gentile Magi adored him. To the former the angels announced him, but to the latter the star. The angels inhabit the heavens, and the stars adorn them: to both, then, the heavens declared the glory of God[^2].

**2.** For to both the cornerstone was born, that, as the Apostle says, *he might create the two in himself into one new man, making peace, and reconcile both in one body to God through the cross*[^3]. For what is a corner, except the joining of two walls which come from different directions and there in some way find the kiss of peace? For the circumcision and the uncircumcision, that is, the Jews and the Gentiles, were enemies to each other, on account of two things diverse and contrary between them: on the one side the worship of the one true God, on the other of many and false ones. So, since the former were near and the latter far, he brought both to himself, who reconciled both in one body to God, as the same Apostle consequently adds: *through the cross, slaying the enmities in himself. And coming he announced peace to you*, he says, *who were far off, and peace to those who were near; because through him we both have access in one Spirit to the Father*[^4]. See whether the Lord Jesus did not point out both the two walls, coming from the diversity of enmities, and the cornerstone, to whom both drew near from different directions, in whom both agreed, that is, both those of the Jews and those of the Gentiles who believed in him, as though it were said to them: Both you from close by and you from afar, *draw near to him, and be enlightened, and your faces will not blush*[^5]. *For it is written: Behold, I lay in Zion a cornerstone, chosen, precious; and he who believes in him will not be confounded*[^6]. Those who heard and obeyed, both came from here and from there, held to peace, ended the enmities; the firstfruits of both were the shepherds and the Magi. In them the ox began to know its owner, and the ass its master's manger[^7]. From the Jews the horned animal, where the horns of the cross were prepared for Christ; from the Gentiles the long-eared animal, whence it had been foretold: *A people whom I did not know served me; in the hearing of the ear it obeyed me*[^8]. For the owner of the ox and the master of the ass himself lay in a manger, and provided common nourishment for both. Because, then, peace had come to those who were far off and peace to those who were near, the shepherds, the Israelites, as it were found near, on the day on which Christ was born came to him, saw, and exulted; but the Magi, the Gentiles, as it were found far off, so many days being interposed from that on which he was born, today arrived, found, adored. So it behooved us, that is, the Church which is gathered from the Gentiles, to join the celebration of this day, on which Christ was manifested to the firstfruits of the Gentiles, to the celebration of that day on which Christ was born of the Jews, and to keep the memory of so great a sacrament with a twofold solemnity.

**3.** When these two walls are considered, one of the Jews, the other of the Gentiles, cleaving to the cornerstone, keeping the unity of the spirit in the bond of peace[^9], let not the multitude of reprobate Jews offend the mind, among whom were those building, that is, wishing to be teachers of the law, but, as the Apostle says: *Understanding neither what they say nor concerning what they affirm*[^10]. For through this blindness of mind they rejected the stone, which was made the head of the corner[^11]. But it would not have been made the head of the corner, unless to two peoples coming from different directions it offered, by the coupling of grace, a peaceful joining. Let there not be considered, then, in the Israelite wall, the persecutors and killers of Christ, as though building the law and destroying faith, rejecting the cornerstone and constructing ruin for the wretched city. Nor let there be considered there that numerous multitude of Jews scattered through the lands, to the testimony of the divine letters which they everywhere carry without knowing. For in them Jacob limps, whose breadth of thigh was touched and dried up, by which would be signified the multitude of the race limping from his paths[^12]. But in the holy wall, which from them drew near to the peace of the cornerstone, let there be considered those in whom Jacob was blessed. For the same is both blessed and lame: blessed in the sanctified, lame in the reprobate. Let there be considered in that wall those whose abundance went before and followed the little ass of the Savior, crying: *Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord!*[^13] Let there be considered those who from there were chosen as disciples and made apostles. Let there be considered Stephen, in the Greek tongue called by the name of "crown," and the first after the Lord's resurrection to be crowned with martyrdom. Let there be considered also, from those very persecutors, so many thousands of believers, when the Holy Spirit came[^14]. Let there be considered the churches of which the Apostle says: *But I was unknown by face to the churches of Judea which were in Christ; only they were hearing that he who once persecuted us now announces the faith which he once devastated; and they glorified God in me*[^15]. In this way let the Israelite wall be considered, and joined to the wall coming from the Gentiles, which is now conspicuous; and so the cornerstone, first laid in the manger, raised up to the summit of heaven, the Lord Christ, is found not foretold in vain.
