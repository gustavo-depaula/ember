# Sermon 215: On the Giving Back of the Creed

**1.** The Creed of the most sacred mystery, which you received together and have each today given back, is the words in which the faith of mother Church is made solid and firm upon the stable foundation, which is Christ the Lord. *For no one can lay another foundation than that which has been laid, which is Christ Jesus*[^1]. You have received, then, and given back, what you ought always to retain in mind and heart, what you should say on your beds, what you should think in the streets, and what you should not forget amid your meals; in which, even sleeping in body, you should watch in heart. For, renouncing the devil, his pomps and his angels, withdrawing your mind and soul from them, it behooves you to forget the things of the past, and, the old age of your former life despised, to renew with the new man your very life too by holy manners; and, as the Apostle says, forgetting the things that are behind, and stretching out to the things that are before, to follow on to the prize of the upward calling of God[^2]; and to believe what you do not yet see, that you may deservedly be able to come to what you have believed. *For what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, we await it with patience*[^3].

**2.** This faith, then, is also the rule of salvation, that we believe *in God the Father almighty, the Creator of all things, the king of the ages, immortal and invisible*. For he is God almighty, who in the first beginning of the world made all things out of nothing, who is before the ages, and who made and rules the ages. For he is not increased by time, nor stretched out by place, nor enclosed or bounded by any matter; but with himself and in himself remains full and perfect eternity, which neither human thought can comprehend, nor the tongue narrate. For if the gift which he promises to his saints, *neither eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man*[^4]; how can the very one who promises it either the mind conceive, or the heart think, or the tongue narrate?

**3.** We believe also *in his Son Jesus Christ, our Lord*, true God of true God, the Son of God the Father, God, but not two gods; for he and the Father are one[^5]; and through Moses he intimates this to the people, saying: *Hear, Israel, the commandments of life: the Lord your God is one God*[^6]. But if you wish to think how the eternal Son was born of the eternal Father without time, the prophet Isaiah refutes you, who says: *His generation, who shall declare?*[^7] So the birth of God from God you will be able neither to think nor to narrate; you are only permitted to believe, that you may be able to be saved, as the Apostle says: *For he who draws near to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of those who seek him*[^8]. But if you desire to know his nativity according to the flesh, which for our salvation he deigned to take up, hear, and believe him *born of the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary*. Although even this very *nativity of his, who shall declare?* For who can worthily estimate that God for the sake of men willed to be born a man, that a virgin conceived without male seed, gave birth without corruption, and after the birth remained in integrity? For our Lord Jesus Christ deigned to enter the womb of the Virgin, filled the members of a woman, immaculate, made the mother fruitful without corruption, came forth formed by himself, and preserved the bowels of his mother intact; that her of whom he deigned to be born he might endow both with the honor of a mother and with the holiness of a virgin. Who thinks this? who narrates it? Therefore even this *nativity of his, who shall declare?* For whose mind suffices to think, whose tongue to utter, not only that in the beginning was the Word, having no beginning of being born, but also that *the Word was made flesh*[^9], choosing a virgin whom he would make his mother, making a mother whom he would keep also a virgin; the Son of God with no mother conceiving, the Son of man with no man sowing; bringing fruitfulness to the woman by coming, not taking away integrity by being born? What is this? who could tell it? who could be silent? And, wonderful to say: what we are not able to utter, we are not allowed to keep silent; by sounding we proclaim what we do not grasp even by thinking. For so great a gift of God we cannot speak forth, since we are little ones for narrating its greatness; and yet we are compelled to praise it, lest by keeping silent we remain ungrateful. But thanks be to God, because that which cannot be fittingly said can be faithfully believed.

**4.** Let us believe, then, *in Jesus Christ, our Lord, born of the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary*. For blessed Mary herself, the one whom she bore by believing, she conceived by believing. For when, the son being promised her, she had asked how it would be, since she did not know a man; surely the only manner of knowing and bearing that was known to her was the one which she herself had not experienced, but had learned from other women by nature's frequent course, namely, that a man is born of a man and a woman; she received the answer from the angel: *The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you; therefore what will be born of you holy will be called the Son of God*[^10]. When the angel had said these things, she, full of faith, and conceiving Christ in mind before in womb: *Behold*, she said, *the handmaid of the Lord, be it done to me according to your word*[^11]. Be it done, she says, a conception in the virgin without male seed; let him be born of the Holy Spirit and an intact woman, in whom may be reborn of the Holy Spirit an intact Church. Let the holy one who is born of a human mother without a human father be called the Son of God; since he who was born of God the Father without any mother wondrously, it behooved that he should become the son of man; that, born in that flesh, he should as a little one go forth through closed bowels, in which, raised up, he should as a great one enter through closed doors. These things are wonderful, because they are divine; ineffable, because also unsearchable; the mouth of man does not suffice for explaining, because neither does the heart of man for investigating. Mary believed, and in her what she believed came to pass. Let us too believe, that what came to pass may be able to profit us also. Although, then, even this nativity is wonderful: yet think, O man, what for your sake your God, the Creator for the creature, took up; that God, remaining in God, eternal living with the eternal, the Son equal to the Father, for guilty ones and for sinful servants did not disdain to put on the form of a servant. For neither was this rendered to human merits. For for our iniquities we rather deserved punishments: but if he had marked iniquities, who could endure?[^12] For the ungodly, then, and for sinful servants, the Lord, a servant and a man, deigned to be born *of the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary*.

**5.** This perhaps seems little, that for men God, for sinners the righteous, for the guilty the innocent, for captives the king, for servants the Lord, clothed with the flesh of humanity, came, was seen on earth and dwelt with men[^13]: *moreover, crucified, dead, and buried.* Do you not believe? You say perhaps: When did this happen? Hear at what time: *Under Pontius Pilate.* For your indication the name of the judge too is set down, lest you doubt even of the time. Believe, then, that the Son of God was *crucified under Pontius Pilate, and buried. But greater love than this no one has, than that one lay down his soul for his friends*[^14]. Do you think, no one? Absolutely no one. It is true, Christ said this. Let us question the Apostle, and let him answer us: Christ, he says, *died for the ungodly*[^15]. And again he says: *When we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son*[^16]. Behold, then, in Christ we find a greater love, who handed over his soul not for friends, but for his own enemies. How great the love of God toward men, and what an affection, so to love even sinners, that for love of them he died. *For God commends his love*, the words are the Apostle's, *in us, because, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us*[^17]. Believe this, then, you too, and for your salvation do not blush to confess it. *For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation*[^18]. Finally, lest you should doubt, lest you should blush, when you first believed, you received the sign of Christ on your forehead as on the house of modesty. Consider your forehead, that you may not dread another's tongue. For he who, says the Lord himself, *is ashamed of me before men, the Son of man will be ashamed of him before the angels of God*[^19]. Do not, then, blush at the ignominy of the cross, which God himself for your sake did not hesitate to take up. Say with the Apostle: *But far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^20]. And the same Apostle answers you: *I did not judge that I knew anything among you, except Jesus Christ, and him crucified*[^21]. He who was then crucified by one people is now fixed in the hearts of all peoples.

**6.** But you, whoever you are who wish to glory rather in power than in humility, receive consolation, have exultation. For he who was crucified under Pontius Pilate and buried, *on the third day rose from the dead.* Perhaps here too you doubt, perhaps you tremble. When it was said to you: Believe him born, believe him to have suffered, crucified, dead, and buried; as of a man you believed it more easily. Now, because it is said: *On the third day he rose from the dead*, do you doubt, O man? To bring forth one example out of many, attend to God, think on the Almighty; and do not doubt. For if he could make you, when you were not, out of nothing; why could he not raise from the dead his own man, whom he had already made? Believe, then, brothers: where it is a matter of faith, there is no need to use a long speech. This faith alone is what distinguishes and separates Christians from all men. For that he died and was buried, even the pagans now believe, and the Jews then saw; but that *on the third day he rose from the dead*, neither pagan nor Jew admits. So the resurrection of the dead distinguishes from the unbelieving dead the life of our faith. For the apostle Paul too, when he wrote to Timothy: *Remember*, he says, *that Christ Jesus rose from the dead*[^22]. Let us believe, then, brothers; what we believe was done in Christ, let us hope will be in us. For it is God who promises: he does not deceive.

**7.** After he rose from the dead, then, *he ascended into the heavens, he sits at the right hand of the Father.* Do you perhaps still not believe? Hear the Apostle: *He who descended*, he says, *is the same who also ascended above all the heavens, that he might fill all things*[^23]. See lest him whom you are unwilling to believe rising, you feel judging. *For he who does not believe is already judged*[^24]. For he who now *sits at the right hand of the Father* as advocate for us, *thence will come to judge the living and the dead.* Let us believe, then, that whether we live or die, we may be the Lord's[^25].

**8.** Let us believe, then, *also in the Holy Spirit.* For he is God, because it is written: *God is a Spirit*[^26]. Through him we received the *forgiveness of sins*, through him we believe the *resurrection of the flesh*, through him we hope for *life eternal*. But see lest by counting you suffer error, and think that I have said three gods, because I have named one God a third time. There is in the Trinity one substance of deity, one virtue, one power, one majesty, one name of divinity; as he himself said to the disciples, when he had risen from the dead: *Go, baptize the nations*, not in many names, but *in* the one *name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit*[^27]. Believing, then, the divine Trinity and the threefold unity, beware, most beloved, lest anyone seduce you from the faith and truth of the catholic Church. *For whoever announces to you otherwise than what you have received, let him be accursed.* Hear the Apostle, not me, who says: *But even if we, or an angel from heaven, announce to you otherwise than what you have received, let him be accursed*[^28].

**9.** You surely see, dearest ones, even in the very words of the holy Creed, how, in the conclusion of all the rules which pertain to the sacrament of faith, there is added as it were a certain supplement, that it should be said: *Through the holy Church.* Flee, then, as much as you can, the diverse and various deceivers, whose sects and names, because of their multitude, it is now long to recount. For I have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now[^29]. One thing I commend to your breasts, that from him who is not catholic you turn away your mind and hearing in every way: that you may be able to lay hold of the *forgiveness of sins* and the *resurrection of the flesh* and *life eternal* through the one, true, and *holy* catholic *Church*; in which is learned the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God, to whom is honor and glory forever and ever.
