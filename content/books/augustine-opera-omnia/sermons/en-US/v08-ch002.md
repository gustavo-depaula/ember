# Sermon 274: On the Feast Day of the Martyr Vincent

**1.** We have watched a great spectacle with the eyes of faith: the holy martyr Vincent conquering everywhere. He conquered in words, he conquered in punishments; he conquered in confession, he conquered in tribulation; he conquered when burned by fire, he conquered when sunk beneath the waves: at the last he conquered when tortured, he conquered when dead. When his flesh, in which was the trophy of Christ the conqueror, was thrown from the little boat into the sea, it silently said: *We are cast down, but we do not perish*[^1]. Who granted this endurance to his soldier, except the one who first shed his blood for him? To whom it is said in the Psalm: *For you are my endurance, O Lord; O Lord, my hope from my youth*[^2]. A great contest brings about a great glory; not human or temporal glory, but divine and everlasting. Faith fights; and when faith fights, no one overcomes the flesh. For even though it is mangled, even though it is torn apart, when does one perish who has been redeemed by the blood of Christ? A powerful man cannot lose what he has bought with his gold—and shall Christ lose what he has bought with his blood? But let all this be referred not to the glory of man but to the glory of God. From him truly comes endurance—true endurance, holy endurance, religious endurance, upright endurance; Christian endurance is the gift of God. For many robbers too are tortured most patiently; and, not yielding and overcoming their torturer, they are afterward punished with eternal fire. The cause distinguishes the martyr from mere endurance—indeed, from the obstinacy of the wicked. The punishment is alike, but the cause is unlike. With the voice of the martyrs we have sung (for Vincent had said these words in his prayers): *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^3]. His cause was set apart: because he contended for the truth, for justice, for God, for Christ, for the faith, for the unity of the Church, for undivided love. Who granted him this endurance? Who? Let the Psalm tell us. For there it is read, there it is sung: *Shall not my soul be subject to God? For from him is my endurance*[^4]. Whoever thinks that Saint Vincent could do these things by his own strength is very much mistaken. For whoever presumes that he can do this by his own strength, even if he seems to conquer by endurance, is conquered by pride. To conquer well is to conquer all the engines of the enemy. While he plies his enticements, he is conquered by self-restraint; he inflicts punishments and torments, he is conquered by endurance; he suggests errors, he is conquered by wisdom. At the last, when all these have been conquered, he suggests to the soul: Well done, well done! How much you have been able to do! How much you have contended! Who is to be compared with you? How well you have conquered! Let the holy soul answer him: *Let them be confounded and put to shame, who say to me, Well done, well done!*[^5] When, then, does the soul conquer, except when it says: *In the Lord shall my soul be praised; let the meek hear, and be glad*[^6]? For the meek know what I say, because in them the Word dwells, in them the example dwells. For one who is not meek does not know the savor of what is said: In the Lord shall my soul be praised. For everyone who is not meek, but proud, harsh, puffed up, wishes to be praised in himself, not in the Lord. But one who says: *In the Lord shall my soul be praised,* does not say: Let the nations hear, and be glad; let men hear, and be glad: but: *Let the meek hear, and be glad.* Let those hear to whom it has savor. For Christ was meek: *Like a sheep he was led to the slaughter*[^7]. Meek for this reason, because like a sheep he was led to the slaughter. *Let the meek hear, and be glad.* For they have a savor for what is said: *Taste, and see how sweet the Lord is: blessed is the man who hopes in him*[^8]. We have heard a long reading, and the day is short: with a long sermon we too ought not to detain your patience. We know that you have listened patiently, and that by long standing and listening you have suffered along with the martyr, as it were. May he who hears you love you and crown you.
