# Sermon 242/A: In the Days of Easter, on the Resurrection of Bodies

**1.** That our Lord Jesus Christ rose from the dead on the third day the holy Gospels testify, and the whole world now confesses it in the holy Creed. The prophets, who did not see such things, foretold them as future; for they did not see, but foresaw in the spirit. And I think the others ought now to blush at this, who, since they do not dare deny the resurrection of Christ, deny that ours will be. For they say: He rose, as one to whom alone it was permitted to rise with the very flesh which he condescended to take. Does it therefore follow that our flesh too rises, because his rose? His power and strength is surely far distant from us. To whom it is answered: Far from you is the divinity of Christ, but the weakness of Christ has drawn near to you. With himself God, for you man; from what is his he made you, from what is yours he suffered for you. If, then, he made you from what is his, he rose in what is yours. For the Word did not have flesh: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him*[^1]. And man was made through him: but afterward he through whom man was made was made man; and, lest man should perish, Christ died. But Christ rose. What rose in him? *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^2]. The Word took up what it was not, but never lost what it was. The Word, then, remained. What rose? The Word. Why did it fall? to rise. Why did the Word die? to come to life again. But we say it died in the flesh which it took up, not in the divinity in which it remained. Therefore, in the respect in which Christ rose, he did himself an injury, he gave you an example; for that he should rise was a humiliation, was an injury. Return to the Word, return to that, *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*, and see what it is for him to rise; for he who raised his own flesh will also raise yours; for he willed to raise his own precisely so that you would not disbelieve that yours will rise.

**2.** But the men who argue against this say: The flesh of Christ rose, because it was for three days in the tomb, and did not see corruption; it neither wasted away, nor rotted, nor was dissolved into dust; but ours, when perhaps the tombs are opened, scarcely are bones found there, dust is found; whatever was flesh is all reduced to rottenness, all dissolved into dust; will that, then, be able to rise, which could not keep its own integrity? Consider, O man who argue these things, consider, I say, that there are bones in the tomb; if nothing else, there are at least bones. There is in the tomb also the dust of the body, in that place where the bosom of the earth received it. Return to your origin, and inquire what you were when you were sown. In the womb it poured out our first beginnings; remember: compare a man buried, and a man sown. Surely we all know that we are mortal. As, then, we consider the bowels of the earth, in which the body lies sown to rise, so let us consider men sown in their mothers' wombs, from where this composition of members has risen. Where lay hidden those five senses of the body? where were, in that fluid, the eyes and the tongue and the ears and the hands? From where did these distributed offices of members proceed? who created them? who formed them? Was it not God? That same God, then, who was able to bring the formed one out of the womb, he wills that from things done you should consider things to be done, and believe that he can bring you living out of the tomb. That men are sown in these ways, that men, I say, are formed in these ways in their mothers' wombs, are daily miracles; but they have lost their wonder through their too constant occurrence, and through that very familiarity have grown cheap. But a thing that is to be once—that is, the resurrection that is to be once—daily testimonies attend. Nature cries out, Scripture rebukes, let the future thing be believed; let it be believed, brothers, that this body of ours will rise, the good to glory, the bad to punishment.

**3.** For what, I beseech you, you who do not want to believe in the future resurrection of the flesh, what moves you? what displeases you in the body? *Who ever hated his own flesh?* says the Apostle[^3]. What displeases you in the body? If all the fitting-together of the body were described, would not the one who hears grow dull, and would not the one who describes be exhausted? What displeases you in the body? I say it: corruption, mortality. But the things that please you will be there, the things that displease you will not be. Hear the Apostle: *An animate body is sown, a spiritual body will rise; it is sown in dishonor, it will rise in glory; it is sown in weakness, it will rise in power*[^4]. Hear more fully: *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality*[^5]. What pleases you comes back to life, what displeases you perishes. Do not, then, be ungrateful to your Redeemer, by not believing what he promised; but do what he commands, that you may receive what he promised. For your Redeemer can do everything, because he is God. If it displeases you that the body be raised, let the body now displease you too. Why do you cherish what displeases you? why do you protect what displeases you? why do you nourish what displeases you? why do you wish it unharmed? Or does it please you? Then give thanks, and believe the resurrection. Bodies, then, will rise, because Christ rose; but they will not have any need, because Christ too, when he rose, ate by power, not by need. There will be no hunger there; we will not stand there with trembling and say: *Give us this day our daily bread*[^6]; we will always have the eternal bread. But it is always at hand; we will not pray for rain on its account, we will not tremble at a dry sky; because our bread will be he who made the sky. And there will be no fear there, no toil, no sorrow, no corruption, no need, no weakness, no weariness, no sluggishness. All these will not be: but the body will be. For all these evils that we feel in the body befell us through sin, did not arise through our condition. For from the beginning, through the man who sinned, we received a bad inheritance from our sinful father; but another inheritance came to us, of him who took up ours, and promised his own inheritance. We had death through fault, he took up death without fault; he was slain who was not a debtor, and he wiped out the bond of the debtors. Let there be in you, then, a mind full of faith in the resurrection. Not only are the things already done in Christ preached, but also the things to come are promised to Christians about him.

[^1]: John 1:1-3
[^2]: John 1:14
[^3]: Eph 5:29
[^4]: 1 Cor 15:44, 43
[^5]: 1 Cor 15:53
[^6]: Matt 6:11
