# Sermon 181

**1. 1.** The most blessed apostle John, writing wholesomely and truly, says among other things, *If we say that we have no sin, we lead ourselves astray, and the truth is not in us. But if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, and cleanse us from all iniquity*[^1]. By these words the blessed John taught—rather, the Lord Jesus himself, not silent through John—that no one in this flesh, in this corruptible body, on this earth, in this evil age, in this life full of temptations, no one lives here without sin. The sentence is absolute, nor does it need an expositor: *If we say that we have no sin*. For who is there who has no sin? As Scripture says, *Not even the infant whose life is of one day on the earth*[^2]. Such a little one committed no sin, but drew it from his parents. Therefore no one can in any way say that he had no sin. But the faithful man came through faith to the washing of regeneration, and all was forgiven him; he now lives under grace, lives in faith, has been made a member of Christ, has been made a temple of God; and yet, even as he has been made a member of Christ and a temple of God, if he says that he has no sin, he leads himself astray, and the truth is not in him; he plainly lies, if he says: I am righteous.

**2. 2.** But there are certain inflated wineskins, full of the spirit of elation, not huge in greatness, but swollen with the disease of pride, who dare to say that men are found without sin. They say, then, that the righteous in this life have no sin at all. But the heretics, the Pelagians, the same as the Caelestians, are those who say this. And when it is answered them: What is it you say? Does this man, then, live here without sin, and have no sin at all, neither in deed, nor word, nor thought? They answer out of that wind of pride with which they are full; which wind would that they would end, would be deflated and silent—that is, would become humble, not exalted; they answer, I say: Plainly these holy men, faithful to God, can have no sin, neither in deed, nor word, nor thought. And when it is said to them: Who are these righteous, who are without sin? they answer and say: The whole Church. I could marvel, if I found one, two, three, ten, as many as Abraham sought. For Abraham descended from fifty even to ten[^3]; but you, heretic, answer and tell me the whole Church. Whence do you prove this? I prove it, you say. Prove it, I beg you. For you bring me great joy, if you can teach that the whole Church plainly, in each of its faithful, has no sin. I prove it, you say. Tell me whence. The Apostle speaks. What does the Apostle say? *Christ*, he says, *loved the Church*. I hear, and I recognize the words to be the Apostle's. *Cleansing it by the washing of water in the word, that he might present to himself a glorious Church, not having spot or wrinkle, or any such thing*[^4]. We have heard great thunders from the great cloud. For the cloud of God is the Apostle. These words sounded, and made us tremble.

**3. 3.** But tell us, before we ask how the Apostle said these words; tell us, I say, whether you are righteous, or not. They answer: We are righteous. Then you have no sin? Through all days, through all nights you do nothing evil, you say nothing evil, you think nothing evil? They do not dare to say: Nothing. But what do they answer? We indeed are sinners; but we speak of the saints, not of ourselves. This I ask you: Are you Christians? I do not say: Are you righteous? Are you Christians? They do not dare to deny: We are Christians, they say. Are you, then, faithful? Are you baptized? We are baptized, they say. Have all your sins been forgiven you? They have been forgiven, they say. How, then, are you sinners? It is enough for me whence to refute you. You are Christians, you are baptized, you are faithful, you are members of the Church, and you have spots and wrinkles? How, then, is the Church at this time without spot and wrinkle, when you are its wrinkle and spot? Or, if you do not wish the Church to be any but the one without spot and wrinkle, with your wrinkles and spots cut yourselves off from its members, cut yourselves off from its body. But why should I still say that they separate themselves from the Church, when they have already done this? For they are heretics, they are already outside: with all their cleanness they have remained outside. Return, and hear; hear, and believe.

**3. 4.** Perhaps you are going to say, in your swollen and inflated heart: Could we possibly say that we are righteous? It was surely necessary, on account of humility, that we say we are sinners. On account of humility, then, you lie? You are righteous, you are without sin: but on account of humility you say you are a sinner. How shall I receive you as a Christian into a witness against another, whom against yourself I hold a false witness? You are righteous, you are without sin, and you say you have sin. You are, then, a false witness against yourself. God does not accept your lying humility. Inspect your life, see your conscience. Are you, then, righteous, but can only say that you are a sinner? Hear John; he himself repeats to you what he also truly said above: *If we say*, he says, *that we have no sin, we lead ourselves astray, and the truth is not in us*[^5]. You have no sin, and you say you have sin; the truth is not in you. Because John did not say: *If we say that we have no sin*, humility is not in us; but he said: *we lead ourselves astray, and the truth is not in us*. Therefore we lie, if we say we have no sin. If John feared a lie, do you not fear a lie, so that, being righteous, you say you are a sinner? How, then, shall I receive you as a witness in another's cause, who lie in your own? You make the saints guilty, while against yourself you say false testimony. What will you do to another, you who defame yourself? How will another avoid your slander, when you make yourself guilty by the lie of your tongue?

**4. 5.** Again I question you in another way: Are you righteous, or a sinner? You answer: A sinner. You lie, because what you do not believe in your heart that you are, this you say with your mouth. Therefore even if you were not a sinner, you will begin to be one while you lie. For you say: For the sake of humility we say we are sinners; for God sees that we are righteous. When, then, on account of humility you lie, if you were not a sinner before you lied, by lying you become what you had avoided. The truth is not in you, unless you so say you are a sinner that you also acknowledge that you are one. But truth itself is that you say what you are. For how is there humility, where falsehood reigns?

**4. 6.** Finally let us omit John's words: behold, in the body of the Church, which you say has no spot or wrinkle or any such thing, and is without sin, behold the hour of prayer will come, the whole Church is going to pray: and you indeed are outside; come to the Lord's prayer, come to the balance, come, say: *Our Father, who are in heaven*. Follow: *Hallowed be your name. Your kingdom come. Your will be done, as in heaven, so on earth. Give us this day our daily bread*. Follow, and say: *Forgive us our debts*. Answer, heretic, what are your debts? Did you perhaps receive money on loan from God? No, he says. I will not question you further about this: for the Lord himself is going to expound what the debts are that we ask to be released for us. Let us, then, say what follows: *As we too forgive our debtors*. Let the Lord expound this: *For if you forgive men their sins* (therefore your debts are your sins), *your Father too will forgive you your sins*[^6]. Return, then, heretic, to prayer, if you have grown deaf against the true reason of faith. *Forgive us our debts*, do you say, or not? If you do not say it, even if you are present in body, you are yet outside the Church. For it is the prayer of the Church, it is a voice coming from the teaching of the Lord. He himself said: *So pray*; to the disciples he said: *So pray*; to the disciples he said it, to the Apostles he said it, and to us, whatever kind of little lambs we are, he said it; to the rams of the flock he said: *So pray*. See who said it, and to whom he said it. The Truth to the disciples, the Shepherd of shepherds to the rams: *So pray: Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*. The King to the soldiers, the Lord to the servants, Christ to the Apostles, the Truth to men spoke, the Sublimity to the humble spoke. I know what goes on in you: I weigh you, I report from my balance, I plainly say what goes on in you. For this I know more than you. Say: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*.

**5. 7.** I question you, O righteous man, holy man, man without spot and wrinkle; I question you, I say: This prayer—is it of the Church, of the faithful, or of the catechumens? Surely it is of the regenerate—that is, the baptized; finally, what surpasses all, it is of sons. For if it is not of sons, with what face is it said: *Our Father, who are in heaven*? Where, then, are you, O righteous and holy ones? Are you in the members of this Church, or are you not? You were there, but now you are not there. And would that, already cut off, having received the reason, they would hear and believe. Therefore if the whole Church says: *Forgive us our debts*, the one who does not say it is reprobate. And we indeed, when we say: *our debts*, until we receive what we ask, are reprobate, because we are sinners; but what you do not do, we do—that is, by confessing our sins we are cleansed; provided we do what we say: *As we too forgive our debtors*. Where are you, then, heretic, Pelagian or Caelestian? Behold, the whole Church says: *Forgive us our debts*. It has, then, spots and wrinkles. But by confession the wrinkle is stretched out, by confession the spot is washed away. The Church stands in prayer, that it may be cleansed by confession; and as long as one lives here, so it stands. And when each one goes out of the body, all is forgiven him, which he had such as to be forgiven, as debts; because by daily prayers too they are forgiven: and then he goes out cleansed, and the Church treasures up pure gold into the treasures of the Lord; and through this, in the treasures of the Lord the Church is without spot and wrinkle. And if there it is without spot and wrinkle, what is to be prayed for here? That pardon be received. He who gives pardon wipes off the spot; he who forgives stretches out the wrinkle. And where is our wrinkle stretched out? As it were on the stretching-frame of the great fuller, on the cross of Christ. For on that very cross—that is, on that very stretching-frame—he poured out his blood for us. And you faithful know what testimony you bear to the blood you have received. For you surely say: Amen. You know what the blood is that was shed for many unto the remission of sins. Behold how the Church is made without spot and wrinkle, well cleansed, stretched out as it were on the stretching-frame of the cross: but this can be wholly done here. The Lord presents to himself a glorious Church, not having spot or wrinkle. He does this here, he presents it there. For he does this, that we may not have spot or wrinkle. Great is he who does it, he cures well, he is a most learned craftsman. He stretches us out on the wood, and makes us without wrinkle, whom by washing he had made without spot. He himself who came without spot and without wrinkle was stretched out on the stretching-frame; but for our sake, not for his own, that he might make us without spot and wrinkle. Let us ask him, then, to do it, and after he has done it, to lead us to the granaries, and there store us, where there will be no press.

**6. 8.** You, then, who were speaking, are you without spot and wrinkle? What do you do here in the Church, which says: *Forgive us our debts*? It confesses that it has debts to be released. Those who do not confess do not for that reason not have them; but for that reason they will not be released to them. Confession heals us, and a cautious life, a humble life, prayer with faith, contrition of heart, tears not feigned, flowing from the vein of the heart, that our sins be forgiven us, without which we cannot be. Confession, I say, heals us, the apostle John saying: *If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, and cleanse us from all iniquity*[^7]. But not because I say that we cannot be here without sin ought we to commit murders, or adulteries, or the other deadly sins, which slay at one stroke. Such things the Christian of good faith and good hope does not do; but only those which are wiped off by the daily brush of prayer. Humble and devout, let us say daily: *Forgive us our debts*; but only if we do what follows: *As we too forgive our debtors*[^8]. This compact with God, this is a true compact and a fixed condition. You are a man, and you have a debtor, and you too are a debtor. You come to God, who has debtors and is not a debtor, that you may ask your debts to be released to you. But he says this to you: I have no debts, you have debts; for you owe me: but your brother too owes you. You are my debtor, you have a debtor too. You are my debtor, because you sinned against me; you have your brother as a debtor, because he sinned against you. What you do with your debtor, I too do with mine; that is, if you forgive, I forgive; if you hold, I hold. You hold against yourself, who do not forgive another. Let no one, then, say that he is without sin: but yet we ought not for that reason to love sin. Let us hate them, brothers; even if we are not without sins, yet let us hate them: and most of all let us abstain from crimes; let us abstain, as much as we can, from light sins. I, says someone, have no sins. He deceives himself, and the truth is not in him. Let us plainly pray that God forgive; but let us do what is said, let us too forgive our debtors. When we forgive, it is forgiven us too. We say this daily, and daily we do it, and daily it is done in us. We are not here without sin, but we shall go out from here without sin.
