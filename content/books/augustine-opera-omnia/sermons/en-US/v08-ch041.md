# Sermon 300: On the Feast of the Maccabean Martyrs

**1.** The glory of the Maccabees has made this day solemn for us: their wondrous sufferings, when they were read, we not only heard but also saw and beheld. These deeds were done long ago, before the incarnation, before the suffering of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ. They arose in that first people, in which the prophets arose, who foretold these present things. And let no one suppose that, before there was a Christian people, there was no people belonging to God. On the contrary—so to speak, as the truth holds, not the custom of names—even that people then was Christian. For Christ did not begin to have a people only after his suffering; rather, his people was that begotten from Abraham, to whom the Lord himself, bearing witness, says: *Abraham longed to see my day; and he saw it, and was glad*[^1]. So from Abraham was born that people which served in Egypt, and which, by a mighty hand freed through Moses the servant of God from the house of bondage, led through the Red Sea with the waves drawn back, trained in the desert, made subject to the law, was settled in the kingdom. Thence, as I said, the prophets arose; thence these martyrs flourished. Christ indeed had not yet died: but Christ, who was to die, made them martyrs.

**2.** This, then, is first to be commended to your love, that, when you admire those martyrs, you do not think they were not Christians. They were Christians: but they preceded by their deeds the name of Christians that was later made known. But, you may say, as if there were no confession of Christ for them—they were not compelled by an impious and persecuting king to deny Christ, as the later martyrs, when they were compelled not to do so, attained a like glory. For the later persecutors of the Christian people were forcing those they persecuted to deny the name of Christ: those, standing most steadfastly in the name of Christ, suffered such things as we have heard, when they were read, that they suffered. So to these more recent martyrs, whose thousands have made the earth crimson, it was commanded and said by the persecutors, Deny Christ. Not doing so, they suffered such things as these also suffered. But to these it was said, Deny the law of Moses. They did not do so: they suffered for the law of Moses. These for the name of Christ, those for the law of Moses.

**3.** Some Jew arises and says to us: How do you reckon these, ours, as your martyrs? With what imprudence do you celebrate their memory? Read their confessions: see whether they confessed Christ. To him we answer: Truly, since you are one of those who did not believe in Christ, and, broken off from the olive, with the wild olive succeeding, have remained outside, dry[^2]—what will you say, one of the faithless? They did not openly confess Christ, because the mystery of Christ was still veiled. For the Old Testament is the veiling of the New Testament, and the New Testament is the unveiling of the Old Testament. See then concerning the unbelieving Jews, your fathers, but in evil your brothers, see what the apostle Paul says of such. *Even until now, as long as Moses is read, a veil is laid upon their hearts. But that same veil remains in the reading of the Old Testament, not being lifted, because in Christ it is done away. When you pass over*, he says, *to Christ, the veil will be taken away*[^3]. *The veil*, he says, *remains in the reading of the Old Testament, not being lifted, because in Christ it is done away*: not the reading of the Old Testament, but the veil that is laid there. The reading of the Old Testament, in short, is not done away, but is fulfilled by him who said: *I have not come to abolish the law, but to fulfill it*[^4]. So the veil is done away, that what was obscure may be understood. This was surely shut up, because the key of the cross had not yet come.

**4.** Look then at the Lord's suffering, set before your eyes him hanging on the wood, and like a lion, when he willed, lying down[^5], and, that he might slay death, dying not of necessity but of power. Attend to this very thing: see how on the cross he said: *I thirst*[^6]. And when the Jews, not knowing what was being done through them, what was being fulfilled by the hands of those who did not know, bound a sponge with vinegar to a reed, and gave it him to drink, he, having drained the vinegar, answered: *It is finished. And bowing his head he gave up his spirit*[^7]. Who sets forth on a journey as he departed this life? With what truth, with what power, he who had said: *I have power to lay down my soul, and power to take it up again. No one takes it from me; but I lay it down of myself, and I take it up again*[^8]. He recognizes the kingdom of the living who has worthily considered the power of the dying. Now this he had said through the prophet to the Jews themselves: *I slept*[^9]. As if to say: Why do you boast of my death? why, as if you had conquered me, do you vainly glory? *I slept*. I slept, because I willed it; not because you raged. I fulfilled what I willed: you remained in your crime. So, having received and drained the vinegar, he said: *It is finished*. What is finished? What was written about me. What was written about him? *They gave me gall for my food, and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink*[^10]. So, looking around at all that had been done in his suffering—already they had shaken their heads before the cross, already they had given gall, already they had numbered the bones of him hanging and stretched out, already the garments were divided, already over the seamless tunic they had cast lots—having looked around and in a manner reckoned all that the prophets had foretold of his suffering, there remained I know not what that was lacking: *And in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink*. That this little that remained might be added, he said: *I thirst*. Having received what was lacking, he answered: *It is finished*. Having said this, *bowing his head he gave up his spirit*[^11]. Then the foundations of the earth were shaken, then with the rocks split the hidden things of the underworld lay open, then the tombs gave back the dead; and—to say why we have said all this—because now the time had come that in the mystery of the cross all that was veiled in the Old Testament should be unveiled, the veil of the temple was torn[^12].

**5.** So from that time Christ began, after the resurrection, to be preached most openly. The prophetic things that had been foretold began to be most manifestly fulfilled in him; the martyrs began most steadfastly to confess him. The martyrs confessed openly him whom then the Maccabees confessed in secret: these died for Christ in the revealed Gospel; those died for the name of Christ in the veiled law. Christ has both, Christ helped both as they fought, Christ crowned both. Christ has both in his service, like some most powerful man advancing with a train of attendants, some going before, some following. So rather behold him presiding in the vehicle of the flesh: both those who go before, they attend him; and those who follow, they are devoted. For that you may know, and know plainly, that those dying for the law of Moses died for Christ, hear Christ himself, O Jew, hear; and let your heart at last be opened, let the veil be taken from your eyes. *If you believed Moses, you would believe me also*[^13]. Hear this, receive this, if you can. If the veil has been taken from me, see. *If you believed*, he says, *Moses, you would believe me also: for he wrote of me*[^14]. If Moses wrote of Christ, then he who truly died for the law of Moses laid down his soul for Christ. *Of me*, he says, *he wrote*. Whom the tongues of those confessing served, him the pen of those writing the truth served. How will you be able to understand the pen of Moses, you who bound vinegar on a reed? Would that one day you might drink his wine, to whom by blaspheming you still offer vinegar.

**6.** So the Maccabees are martyrs of Christ. Therefore not inappropriately, nor unfittingly, but most suitably, their day and their feast is celebrated rather by Christians. What such thing have the Jews known how to celebrate? A basilica of the holy Maccabees is said to be at Antioch: in that city, namely, which is called by the name of the very king who persecuted them. For they endured Antiochus, the impious persecuting king, and the memory of their martyrdom is celebrated at Antioch; so that the name of the persecutor and the memory of the one who crowns them sound together. This basilica is held by Christians, was built by Christians. So we have their memory to celebrate, we hold it: among us their sufferings have been imitated by thousands of holy martyrs throughout the world. Let no one then hesitate, my brethren, to imitate the Maccabees; lest, in imitating the Maccabees, he think he is not imitating Christians. Let the desire of imitation fervently burn in our hearts. Let men learn to die for the truth. Let women learn, from the great patience, the unspeakable courage of that mother; she knew how to keep her sons. She knew how to have what she did not fear to lose. They, each one, felt it in himself; she, beholding, suffered in them all. Made the mother of seven martyrs, she was seven times a martyr: not separated from her sons in beholding, and added to her sons in dying. She saw them all, she loved them all. She bore in her eyes what she bore in flesh; nor was she only not frightened, but she even exhorted.

**7.** Antiochus the persecutor reckoned this woman as a mother like other mothers. Persuade, he says, your son, lest he perish. And she: Plainly I will persuade my son to life, by urging him to death: you wish to persuade death, by sparing. But what an address, how loving, how motherly, how poised in ambiguity between the spiritual and the carnal! *Son, have mercy on me. Son*, she says, *have mercy on me, who carried you nine months in the womb, who gave you milk for three years, and brought you to this age: have mercy on me*[^15]. All were awaiting the words that should follow: Consent to Antiochus, do not desert your mother. She, on the contrary: Consent to God, do not desert your brothers. If you as it were desert me, then you do not desert me. There I shall have you, where I shall not fear to lose you any longer. There Christ will keep you for me, whence Antiochus shall not take you. He feared God, he heard his mother, he answered the king, he clung to his brothers, he drew his mother after him.
