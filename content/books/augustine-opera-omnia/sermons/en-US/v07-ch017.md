# Sermon 244: In the Days of Easter

**1.** From the Gospel according to John the resurrection of the Lord began to be recited today. We have heard, and with the eyes of faith we have seen, the devout woman's affection toward the Lord Jesus. She was seeking Jesus, but yet still sought him as the body of a dead man, and yet loved him as a good master. She did not understand that he had risen from the dead, she did not believe it; and because she saw the stone removed from the tomb, believing that the body she sought had been taken away from there, she announced to the disciples a thing to be grieved over. Two ran, of whom one was Peter, the other John. For he it is whom Jesus loved, surely above the rest: for he loved them all as the Lord. They ran, to see whether the woman spoke the truth, that the body had been taken away from the tomb. They came, they looked, they did not find the body, and they believed. But what did they believe? What they ought not to have believed. So when you heard: *And they believed*, perhaps you thought they believed what they ought to have believed, that is, that the Lord had risen from the dead. They did not believe this, but what the woman had announced. Finally, that you may know they believed this, the Evangelist at once added, and said: *For they did not yet know the Scriptures, that he must rise from the dead*[^1]. Where is faith? where is the truth so often attested? Did not the Lord Jesus himself, before the passion, several times tell them that he was to be handed over, to be killed, and to rise again? He was still speaking to the deaf. Already Peter had said to him: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*. Already he had heard: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, for flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven. And I tell you that you are Peter, and on this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it*[^2]. Such faith was swallowed up when the Lord was crucified. For Peter believed him the Son of God only until he saw him hanging on the wood, until he saw him fixed with nails, until he saw him dead, until he saw him buried. Then he lost what he was holding. Where is the rock? where is the firmness of the rock? The rock was Christ himself, but Peter was from the rock. Therefore the rock had risen, to make Peter firm: for Peter would have perished, unless the rock lived.

**2.** Afterward, however, when the Lord said to the woman: *Mary*, turning she recognized him, and called him master: *Rabboni.* The Lord's resurrection was manifested to this woman. What, then, does it mean: *Do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to my Father*[^3]? A question wonderful in many ways. First, because he forbade himself to be touched, as though he could be touched badly by one touching him. Then, because, giving the reason why he did not wish to be touched and forbade it, he says: *For I have not yet ascended to my Father*: as if he said: Then you will touch me, when I have ascended to my Father. He was set on earth and forbidden to be touched, and could she touch him sitting in heaven? For I had said, What does it mean: *Do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to my Father?* I add more: When he rose, as he himself says, and the other Evangelists, and we have already heard, when the holy readings were read, he appeared to his disciples; and when they thought him a spirit, he said to them: *Why are you troubled, and why do thoughts arise in your heart? See my hands and my feet. Handle, and see*[^4]. Surely he had now ascended? He had not yet ascended to his Father, and he said to his disciples: *Handle, and see.* Where is: *Do not touch me?* Here perhaps someone is going to say: He willed to be touched by the men, he did not will to be touched by the women. If he shrank from a woman, he would not have been born of a woman. Nevertheless, whatever this is that can make some kind of question, so that it is said that the Lord, before he ascended to the Father, willed to be touched by men, did not will to be touched by women, the Evangelist Matthew speaks against it. For he himself narrated that women met the Lord rising again, among whom this Mary too was, and that they held his feet. The question is constricted in many ways, as to what it means: *Do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to my Father.* All that I have said, I have said for this, that the difficulty of the question might be increased: you see it strong, and as it were insoluble. May the Lord help me, that it may be solved. He who has condescended to propose it, let him condescend to expound it. Pray with me for the effect: your ears to me, your heart to him. What he condescends to suggest to me, I will share with you. Whoever understands better, let him teach me: I am so a teacher that I am not unteachable. But whoever does not understand better, let him hear from me what he understands.

**3.** The Lord Jesus, as we have heard, as it appears, the disciples thought a man, and according to this they weighed their faith: they did not raise it higher. On earth they walked with Christ. What he had been made for our sake, this they knew: what made us, they did not know. He himself is Christ both the maker and the made. See the maker: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him*[^5]. See the made: *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^6]. We see Jesus, then, but with the faith of the apostles already preached to us. What we know, they did not yet know. I do no injury. I do not dare to say that they were ignorant: but yet I see them confessing ignorance. They did not know, afterward they learned, what we already know. That Christ is both God and man, that Christ is both the maker of things and made among things, that Christ is both the creator of man and a created man, we know, they did not yet. God, Christ is equal to the Father: he is as great as he, he is such as he, he is what he is; he is not who he is. He is what the Father is; because the Father is God, and he is God; the Father is almighty, and he is almighty; the Father is unchangeable, and he is unchangeable: he is what the Father is. He is not who the Father is; because the Father is the Father, this one the Son. Whoever knows this, for him Christ has ascended to the Father: whoever does not know this, for him Christ has not yet ascended to the Father; he is a little child with him, he is on earth with him, he is not yet for him equal to the Almighty. For the one making progress he ascends; with him who makes progress he ascends. What, then, does it mean: *Do not touch me*? Touch signifies faith. For by touching, one draws near to the one who is touched. See that woman who was suffering from a flow of blood. She said in her heart: *I will be healed, if I touch the hem of his garment.* She drew near and touched, she was healed. What does it mean, She drew near and touched? She came close and believed. That you may know she touched by believing, the Lord said: *Someone touched me.* What does it mean, *Someone touched me*; except, Someone believed in me? And that you may know that this is, *Someone touched me*, namely Someone believed in me; *The disciples answered, and said to him: The crowds press you, and you say, Who touched me?* If you were walking alone, if the crowd had made you space for walking, if no one were beside you, you would rightly say: *Someone touched me.* The crowd presses you, you mention one who touched. And he repeated: *Someone touched me.* For first he had said: *Who touched me?* and afterward: *Someone touched me*[^7]. You know, because you say: The crowds press you. Someone touched me. This crowd knows how to press, it does not know how to touch. It is settled that he wished to signify this by saying: *Who touched me? Someone touched me.* So that we believe that touch to be the faith of the one touching, or rather the approach of the one believing. What, then, does it mean: *Do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to my Father?* What you see me to be, this you think me to be: *I have not yet ascended to the Father.* You see me a man, you think me a man: I am indeed a man, but let your faith not stop here. Do not touch me so as to believe me only a man. *For I have not yet ascended to my Father.* I ascend to my Father, and touch me: that is, make progress, understand me equal to the Father, and then touch, and you will be saved. *Do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to my Father.* You see that I have come down, you do not yet see that I have ascended. *For I have not yet ascended to my Father.* I emptied myself, *taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man*[^8]. This was crucified, this buried, this raised again. But that, *Since he was in the form of God, he did not think it robbery to be equal to God*[^9], you do not yet see. That I have ascended you do not yet see. Do not, by touching the earth, lose heaven: do not, by remaining in the man, fail to believe in the God. *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father.*

**4.** Let the Arian come forward: first let the Photinian come forward. We answer the Photinian: Do not touch. What does it mean, Do not touch? Do not believe so: Christ has not yet ascended to the Father for you. Let the Arian come forward. I, he says, believe Christ to be God, but less. Neither for you has he yet ascended to the Father. When he has ascended to the Father, stretch yourself out, that you may touch: stretch yourself out, touch God. And I, he says, profess God; but of another nature, and another substance; created, not the one through whom all things were created; made, not in the beginning the Word without time. So you are still below; he has not yet ascended to the Father for you. Do you want him to ascend to the Father for you? Believe: *Since he was in the form of God, he did not think it robbery to be equal to God*[^10]. It was not robbery, because it was nature. Robbery is usurped, nature is recognized. *In the form of God he did not think it robbery to be equal to God.* So he was born, and always born; both born and always born, and born without beginning. What do you say, Arian? There was a time when the Son was not. Do you see that he has not yet ascended to the Father for you? Do not touch, do not believe so. There is no time between the Father and the Son. The Father begot, the Son was born: without time he begot, without time he was born, he through whom the times were made. Touch so, and he ascends to the Father for you. The Word, but coeternal with God: the Wisdom of God, but without whom the Father never was. Your flesh has an answer for you, and will chat with you, will say to you in the darkness: How was he born? The darkness speaks with you. Let it be expounded to me, you cry: I cry, let it be expounded to me. What do you want expounded to you? Was he born, or not born? For he would not be the Son, unless born. If, then, he was born, there was a time when he was not. This is false: you speak earth, you speak from earth. Expound to me, then, he says, how he was born, if he always was. I do not expound it, I do not expound it; I cannot. I do not expound it: but for myself I set up the prophet: *His generation, who shall declare it?*[^11]
