# Sermon 133

**1.** It is set before us, with the Lord's help, to discuss this most recent passage of the Gospel, and it is no small question, lest the truth be in peril, and falsehood glory. But neither can the truth perish, nor falsehood prevail. But what question this reading has, hear a little; and, made attentive by the question set forth, pray that we may be sufficient for the resolution. There was a feast day of the Jews, Tabernacles: these are the days, as far as appears, which they observe even today, when they call them booths. For they have this solemnity from the building of tents; since a tent is called *skenopegia*, Tabernacles is the building of a tent. These feast days were kept among the Jews: and it was called one feast day, not because it was done on one day, but because it was carried out in a continuous festivity; like the feast day of Passover, like the feast day of unleavened bread, and yet, as is manifest, that feast day is kept through several days. So this solemnity was in Judea; the Lord Jesus was in Galilee, where he was reared, where he had kinsmen and relatives too, whom Scripture calls brothers. *His brothers said*, then, to him, as we heard read: *Pass over from here, and go into Judea, that your disciples too may see your works which you do. For no one does anything in secret, and himself seeks to be in the open. If you do these things, manifest yourself to the world*. Then the Evangelist adds: *For not even his brothers believed in him*. If, then, they did not believe in him, they added envious words. *Jesus answered them: My time has not yet come; but your time is always ready. The world cannot hate you: but it hates me, because I bear witness about it, that its works are evil. You go up to this feast day. I do not go up to this feast day, because my time is not yet fulfilled*. Then follows the Evangelist: *When he had said these things, he himself remained in Galilee. But when his brothers had gone up, then he himself too went up to the feast day, not openly, but as it were in secret* [^1]. This far the question extends, the rest is open.

**2.** What, then, is asked? What moves us? What is in peril? Lest the Lord, or rather, to say it more openly, lest Truth itself be believed to have lied. For if we want it thought to have lied, the weak man will receive authority for lying. We have heard people say that he lied. For those who think he lied, say this: He said he would not go up to the feast day, and he went up. First, then, as far as in the short time we can, let us see whether he lies who says something and does not do it. For example, I said to a friend: Tomorrow I will see you; a greater necessity arose, which detained me; not for that did I speak falsely. For when I promised, I felt what I said. But when something else greater arose, which hindered the fulfillment of my promise, I did not want to lie, but I could not fulfill the promise. Behold, as I judge, I have not labored to persuade, but only admonished your prudence, that he does not lie who promises something and does not do it, if, so that he should not do it, something else arose, which hinders his promise, not which convicts him of falsehood.

**3.** But he who hears me says: Can you say this of Christ, that either he could not fulfill what he wanted, or did not know the future? You do well, you suggest well, you rightly admonish: but, O man, share with me the anxiety. Him whom we do not dare to call less able, do we dare to call lying? I indeed, as far as I judge, as far as for my weakness I can judge, choose that a man be deceived in something, than that he lie in something. For to be deceived pertains to weakness, to lie to iniquity. *You hate*, he says, *Lord, all who work iniquity*. And at once: *You will destroy all who speak a lie* [^2]. Either iniquity and the lie are worth the same; or it is more: *You will destroy*, than *you hate*. For not he who is hated is at once punished with destruction. Let that question be true, whether sometimes it is necessary to lie; for I do not now examine it: it is full of hiding places, it has many recesses; there is no leisure to cut them all, and to come to the living. Therefore let its cure be deferred to another time; for perhaps without our sermon it will be healed by divine help. But what I deferred, what I want to treat today, attend and distinguish. Whether one must sometimes lie, this I said is a difficult and most hidden question, this I defer. But whether Christ lied, whether the Truth said anything false, this we have undertaken today, admonished from the gospel reading.

**4.** But what the difference is between being deceived and lying, I say briefly. He is deceived who thinks that what he says is true, and because he thinks it true, therefore he says it. But this which the one deceived says, if it were true, he would not be deceived: if not only it were true, but he also knew it to be true, he would not lie. He is deceived, then, because it is false, and he thinks it true; but he says it only because he thinks it true. There is error in human weakness, but it is not in the soundness of conscience. But whoever thinks something to be false and asserts it as true, he himself lies. See, my brothers, distinguish, you who have been nourished in the Church, instructed in the Lord's Scriptures, not untaught, not rustic, not ignorant. For among you are learned and erudite men and those well instructed in various letters: and you who have not learned those letters which are called liberal, it is greater that you have been nourished in the word of God. If I labor in explaining what I feel, help me both by attention of hearing and by prudence of thinking. Nor will you help, unless you are helped. Whence let us pray for one another in turn, and await common help together. He is deceived, who, when it is false, thinks that what he says is true: but he lies who thinks something to be false, and says it as true, whether that be true, or false. Note what I have added. Whether that be true, or false, yet he who thinks it false, and asserts it as true, lies: he aims to deceive. For what does it profit him, that it is true? Meanwhile he himself thinks this false, and says it as though it were true. It is true in itself, that which he says, in itself it is true: with him it is false, his conscience does not have this which he speaks; he thinks one thing in himself to be true, he brings forth another outside as the truth. The heart is double, not simple; he does not bring forth what he has there. The double heart was long ago condemned. *Deceitful lips, in heart and heart they have spoken evil things*. It would suffice for him to say: *In heart they have spoken evil things*; whence is: *deceitful lips*? What is deceit? When one thing is done, another held out. *Deceitful lips*, not a simple heart; and because not a simple heart, therefore *in heart and heart* [^3]; therefore twice *heart*, because the heart is double.

**5.** Do we suppose, then, of the Lord Jesus Christ, that he lied? If it is less to be deceived, than to lie; him whom we do not dare to say is deceived, do we dare to say lies? But he neither is deceived, nor lies: but altogether, as it is written (for it is understood of him, of him it ought to be understood): Nothing false is said to the king, and nothing false will go out of his mouth [^4]. If he meant any man as king, let us set Christ the king before a man king. But if, what is more truly understood, it is Christ of whom he spoke (for to him nothing false is said, because he is not deceived; from his mouth nothing false proceeds, because he does not lie); let us seek how we may understand the gospel passage, and not, as though by heavenly authority, build a chasm of lying. But it is more absurd to seek to expound truth and to prepare a place for lying. What do you teach me, I ask you, who expound this passage to me? What do you want to teach me? I do not know whether you dare to say: Falsehood. For if you dare to say this, I turn away my ears, and stop them with thorns, so that if you try to crowd in upon me, I will depart even pricked, without the Gospel expounded. Tell me what you want to teach me, and you have resolved the question. Say, I beg you: behold I am present; my ears are open, my heart is ready, teach. But I ask what? I do not go through many things. What are you going to teach? Whatever doctrine you are going to turn over in the midst, whatever sinews you are going to apply in the dispute, say this alone, I ask one of two things: Are you going to teach me truth, or falsehood? What do we think he will answer, lest he depart; lest, while he gapes and tries to express words, I forsake him at once? what will he promise, except truth? I hear, I stand, I await, I most intently await. Behold, he who promised that he would teach me truth, insinuates falsehood about Christ. How, then, will he teach truth, who is going to call Christ a liar? If Christ lies, am I to hope that you are going to tell me the truth?

**6.** See another thing. What does he say? Christ lied. Where, I ask you? Where he said: *I do not go up to the feast day* [^5], and he went up. I indeed would want to examine this passage, lest perhaps Christ did not lie. Rather, because I do not doubt that Christ did not lie, this passage either I will examine and understand, or, not understanding, I will defer. Yet I will not say that Christ lied. Suppose I did not understand it: I will depart not knowing. For it is better with devotion not to know, than with madness to judge. Yet let us try to discuss it, lest perhaps, with him helping who is the Truth, we may find something, and we be found something, and that something will not in the truth be a lie. For if by seeking I find a lie, I find not something, but nothing. Therefore let us seek where you say Christ lied. Because he said, he says: *I do not go up to the feast day*, and he went up. Whence do you know that he said it? What if I say, rather not I, but someone; for far be it that I say this; what if another says: Christ did not say this? Whence do you convict him, whence are you going to prove it? You are going to open the book, find the reading, show it to the man, rather with great confidence of breast thrust the book at the one resisting: Hold, attend, read, you carry the Gospel. What, then, I ask you, why do you trouble me a little? Do not press, say it more plainly, more tranquilly. Behold, I carry the Gospel, and what of it? He: The Gospel says that Christ said what you deny. And therefore will you believe that Christ said this, because the Gospel says it? Plainly so, he says. I much wonder, how you say that Christ lies and the Gospel does not lie. But lest perhaps, when I say Gospel, you attend to the book, think parchment and ink to be the Gospel, see what the Greek name means: the Gospel is "good news," or "good announcement." Does the messenger, then, not lie, and he who sent him lie? This messenger, namely the Evangelist, that we may say the name too, this John who wrote this, did he lie about Christ, or speak the truth? Choose what you will, I am ready to hear you for either. If he lied, there is nothing by which you prove that Christ said those things. If he spoke the truth, from a fountain of falsehood truth does not flow. Who is the fountain? Christ: let John be a little stream. The little stream came to me, and you say to me: Drink securely; and while you frighten me about the fountain itself, while you say there is falsehood in the fountain, you say to me: Drink securely. What do I drink? What did John say? That Christ lied? Whence did John come? From Christ. Will he who comes from him tell me true things, when he from whom he comes was lying? I have read plainly in the Gospel: *John reclined upon the breast of the Lord* [^6]; but I think that he drank the truth. What did he drink? What, except what he belched forth? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him nothing was made. What was made, in him is life, and the life was the light of men, and the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it*; yet it shines, and if I perhaps have something of obscurity, nor am able to comprehend it perfectly, it shines. *There was a man sent from God, whose name was John; he came to bear witness about the light, that all might believe through him. He was not the light* [^7]: who? John; who? John the Baptist. For of him the Evangelist John says: *He was not the light*; of whom the Lord says: *He was a lamp burning and shining* [^8]. But a lamp can both be lit and put out. What then? Whence do you distinguish? About what place do you ask? He to whom the *lamp* bore witness, *was the true light*. Where John added: *true*, there you seek a lie. Hear still John the Evangelist belching forth what he drank: *And we saw*, he says, *his glory*. What did he see? What glory did he see? *Glory as of the Only-begotten of the Father, full of grace and truth* [^9]. See, then, see whether we ought not to restrain weak or rash disputations, and presume nothing false about the truth, give to the Lord what is owed; let us give glory to the fountain, that securely we may be filled. *But God is truthful: but every man is a liar* [^10]. What is this? God is full: every man is empty; if he wants to be filled, let him approach the full one. *Approach him, and be enlightened* [^11]. Further, if man is empty, because he is a liar, and seeks to be filled, and with haste and eagerness runs to the fountain, wants to be filled, he is empty. But you say: Beware of the fountain, there is a lie there. What else do you say than: There is poison there?

**7.** Now, he says, you have said everything, now you have restrained me, now you have chastised me. Tell me how he did not lie, who said: *I do not go up*, and went up. I will say it, if I can; let it not be too little for you, that even if I have not set you in the truth, yet I have restrained you from rashness. Yet I will say it, which you too now, if you remember the words I commended, I think you recognize. The very words resolve the question. That feast day was kept for many days. *To this*, namely today's, *feast day*, he says, this surely today's when they were hoping, he did not go up; but when he himself was arranging it. In short, attend to what follows: *When he had said these things, he himself remained in Galilee*. Therefore he did not go up *to this feast day*. For his brothers wanted him to go first; therefore they had said to him: *Pass over from here into Judea* [^12]. They did not say: Let us pass over, as though they would be his companions; or: Follow us into Judea, as though they would go first; but as though they would send him ahead. This he willed, that they should go before: this he avoided, commending the weakness of man, hiding his divinity; this he avoided, just as when he fled into Egypt [^13]. For it was not from inability: but this too was of the truth, that he might give an example of taking care; lest any servant of his say: I do not flee, because it is shameful; when perhaps it would be expedient to flee. About to say to his own: *When they persecute you in this city, flee to another* [^14]; he himself offered this example. For when he willed, he was seized; when he willed, he was born. This, then, lest they go before, and announce that he was coming, and ambushes be prepared: *I do not go up*, he says, *to the feast day*. He said: *I do not go up*, that he might be hidden; he added: *this*, lest he lie. He brought in something, he took away something, he deferred something; yet he said nothing false, because nothing false proceeds from his mouth. In short, after he said these things: *But when his brothers had gone up*, the Gospel says; attend, read what you were offering me; see whether the reading itself does not resolve the question, see whether I took from elsewhere what I should say. This, then, the Lord awaited, that they should go up first, lest they announce that he would come. *After his brothers had gone up, he himself too then went up to the feast day, not openly, but as it were in secret* [^15]. What is *as it were in secret?* There he acts as it were in secret. What is: *as it were in secret?* Because not even this was in secret. For he did not truly try to lie hidden, who had it in his power when he should be seized. But in that concealment, as I said, he offered an example of taking care against the ambushes of enemies, to the weak disciples, who did not have the power, when they did not want it, of not being seized. For he too went up openly afterward, and taught them in the temple, and some said: *Behold, here he is, behold, he teaches. Surely our rulers said that they wanted to seize him: behold, he speaks openly, and no one lays a hand on him* [^16].

**8.** But now if we attend to ourselves, if we think of his body, because we too are himself. For even if we ourselves were not himself, it would not be true: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me* [^17]. If we ourselves were not himself, it would not be true: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me* [^18]? Therefore we too are himself, because we are his members, because we are his body, because he is our head [^19], because the whole Christ is head and body. Perhaps, then, he foresaw us, that we would not keep the feast days of the Jews, and this is: *I do not go up to this feast day* [^20]. Behold neither Christ, nor the Evangelist lied: of which two if it were necessary to choose one, the Evangelist would pardon me, in no way would I set the truthful one before Truth itself; the one sent before him by whom he was sent I would not prefer. But thanks be to God, as I judge, what was obscure has been laid bare. Your devotion will help toward God. Behold, as I could, I have resolved the question, both about Christ and about the Evangelist. Hold the truth with me, friend, embrace love without contention.
