# Sermon 350/F: On Almsgiving Done to All

**1.** {There are those who think that alms are to be provided only to the just, but that nothing of the kind ought to be given to sinners. In this error the Manichees hold the first place of sacrilege, who believe that in every food the members of God are held mixed in and bound together, which they judge must be spared, lest they be polluted by sinners and entangled in more wretched knots. This insanity is perhaps refuted less worthily than it offends the sense of all sound men, if it is merely set forth. But some, holding nothing of the kind, think that sinners are not to be fed for this reason, lest we strive against God, whose indignation is declared against them, as though from this he could even grow angry with us, because we wish to help those whom he himself wishes to punish. They even bring forward testimonies of the holy Scriptures where we read: *Give mercy and do not receive the sinner, and to the impious and to sinners render vengeance*; *do good to the humble and do not give to the impious, because the Most High too hates sinners and to the impious will render vengeance*[^1]. Not understanding how these words are to be taken, they are clothed with detestable cruelty. Whence we ought to address your love briefly on this matter, brothers, lest by a perverse thought, when you do not understand the divine will in the divine books, you consent to human depravity.

**2.** For that mercy is to be imparted to all, the apostle Paul, teaching most plainly: *Therefore, untiringly, while we have time*, he says, *let us work good toward all, but especially toward those of the household of faith*[^2]. Whence indeed it appears clearly enough that in works of this kind the just are to be preferred. For whom else should we understand by *those of the household of faith*, when in another place it is plainly set down: *The just man lives by faith*[^3]? Yet the bowels of mercy are not to be closed to other men, even sinners, nor even if they bear a hostile mind against us, our Savior himself saying and admonishing: *Love your enemies, do good to those who hate you*[^4]. Nor is this passed over in silence in the old books; for there it is read: *If your enemy is hungry, feed him; if he thirsts, give him drink*[^5], which testimony the Apostle too used[^6]. Nor for that reason, however, are those false which we set down above, because they too are divine precepts: *Give mercy and do not receive the sinner*. For those were said for this reason, that you may not do good to any sinner for the reason that he is a sinner, but that you may do good to him who hates you, not because he is a sinner, but because he is a man. So you will hold both precepts, neither remiss in punishing nor inhuman in helping. For everyone who rightly rebukes a sinner, what else does he do but wish him not to be a sinner? He hates, then, in him what God too hates, that what man made may be destroyed and what God made may be freed. For sin man made, but man himself God made. And when we say these two names, "man" and "sinner," they are surely not said in vain. Because, then, he is a sinner, correct; and because he is a man, have mercy. Nor will you altogether free the man, unless you persecute the sinner.

**3.** Every discipline keeps watch for this office, as it is fitting and adapted to each one who governs, not only the bishop governing his people, but also the poor man governing his household, the rich man governing his family, the husband governing his wife, the father governing his offspring, the judge governing his province, the king governing his nation. All these, when they are good, surely wish well to those whom they govern, and, according to the power imparted by the Lord of all, who governs even those who govern, give effort that those very ones whom they govern be both preserved as men and perish as sinners. So they fulfill what is written: *Give mercy and do not receive the sinner*, lest they wish this in him to be safe, that he is a sinner; *and to the impious and to sinners render vengeance*, that this very thing, that they are sinners and impious, may be blotted out in them; *do good to the humble*, because he is humble, *and do not give to the impious*, because he is impious, *because the Most High too hates sinners and to the impious will render vengeance*; who yet, because they are not only sinners and impious, but also men, *makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^7]. Thus to no man is mercy to be closed, to no sin is impunity to be relaxed.}

**4.** For what do we do in rebuking but punish sins, and when anyone turns to God by repenting, what else does he do but rebuke and punish himself? In no way, then, do you stray from the work of mercy when you persecute in anyone that which you yourself also mercifully desire to be persecuted in yourself. See what the prophet declares about the future preachers of the kingdom of heaven: *The saints shall exult in glory, they shall rejoice in their beds, the exultations of God in their throats and two-edged swords in their hands, to execute vengeance among the nations*[^8]. And lest anyone should think that by such swords blood is shed and slaughters of bodies are made, as though meeting such thoughts by way of exposition, when he had said *to execute vengeance among the nations*, he added what kind of vengeance: *rebukes*, he says, *among the peoples*[^9]. This the *swords in their hands* do, that is, given into their power to use. For they are two-edged with the grief of present things and the fear of future things. *Who is there*, says the Apostle, *who gladdens me, except the one who is made sorrowful by me?*[^10] Behold the grief of present things. And *when I come*, he says, *I will not spare. Or do you wish to receive a proof of Christ who speaks in me?*[^11] Behold the fear of future things. Again, in another place he so commends the governance of the Lord himself over men: *For if we judged ourselves*, he says, *we would not be judged by the Lord. When we are judged, we are corrected by the Lord, lest we be condemned with the world*[^12]. In the blow of correction is the grief of present things, in the threat of damnation the fear of future things. These are the two-edged swords; this persecution is owed to the sinner, that mercy be not denied to the man.

**5.** But it can seem a wonder to those who attend little, and perhaps incredible, how a sinner may be received and fed for this very reason that he is a sinner. For it is one thing when anyone feeds a man because he either knows or believes him to be just, looking to that reward of which it was said: *He who receives a just man in the name of a just man will receive the reward of a just man*[^13]; but another when anyone so feeds any man whatever as man feeding man, serving that general command where the Lord says: *Whatever good you wish men to do to you, do this also to them*[^14]. But there are those who love the very sins in sinners and on account of these spend whatever they bestow on them, so that you see altogether a kind of work contrary to that work which is shown to the just because they are just. For just as the just, endowed with praiseworthy piety, hasten to provide the service of kindness to the servants of God, by whom they too may be received into eternal dwellings[^15], so on the contrary the sacrilegious and impious seek out kinds of wickedness in men, in buying which, as it were, they squander their temporal goods, and with whom afterward they may come into everlasting torments. Between these two kinds a certain middle place is held, in which neither for the sake of justice nor for the sake of sin, but for the sake of the very fellowship of nature, kindness is shown by man to man. This middle kind both the pious use knowingly and even the wicked are sometimes touched by.

**6.** Since, then, it is one thing to minister necessary things to a dispenser of the kingdom of heaven, as Onesiphorus did to Paul[^16], another to hold out alms to a beggar, as that man received who sat at the Beautiful Gate[^17], another to distribute rewards for disgraceful acts, as actors, charioteers, and huntsmen are enriched by madmen, the Church frequents that first and middle kind of giving, but condemns, rebukes, amends this last, which is contrary to that first and best. But from this very contrary our slothful ones are to be roused, who scarcely break bread for the hungry Christ, when theatrical givers scarcely leave bread for their own children. But on account of that middle kind, by which kindness is owed by man to man, it can come about that even a son of Gehenna, moved by some compassion, may feed a dispenser of God, and that a son of the Church, if he finds him failing from hunger, may feed even a huntsman. But neither did that one love justice, but he could not altogether despise the common condition of mortality, nor did this one receive the sinner, but he did not deny mercy to the man.

**7.** Of that first kind of good work the Lord speaks thus: *He who receives a just man in the name of a just man will receive the reward of a just man, and he who receives a prophet in the name of a prophet will receive the reward of a prophet, and he who gives a cup of cold water to one of these least ones only in the name of a disciple, amen I say to you, he will not lose his reward*[^18], and that of which I mentioned something a little before: *Make for yourselves friends from the mammon of iniquity, that they too may receive you into the eternal dwellings*[^19]; from there too is that: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which was prepared for you from the foundation of the world, for I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*, and the rest. But they: *When did we see you hungry?* To whom he: *When you did it for one of these least ones of mine, you did it for me*[^20]. But of that kind of mercy in which the misery of no man is to be despised—although to a sinner, for the reason that he is a sinner, no mercy is owed—he admonished us thus, when he said to a certain man by whom he had been invited along with the others: *When you make a feast, do not call your friends by whom you too can be invited, but call the lame, the blind, the weak, the beggars, who have nothing with which to repay you. But it shall be repaid you in the resurrection of the just*[^21]. Whence we can also notice from the custom of the Lord himself that the disciples supposed he had ordered that Judas, his betrayer, prepare something to be given to the poor on the feast day, since he himself kept the moneybags, when he said to him: *What you do, do quickly*[^22]. For from where would they suspect this, if the Lord had never taught such almsgiving even by doing it? For of that first kind of good works, in which one serves the just on account of the merit of justice, to that same one rather such services were ministered by others. For from where did those moneybags gather money except from the offerings of those who served him? And most plainly certain religious women too are named in the Gospel, who, accompanying his circuit with devoted service, ministered to him from their substance[^23].

**8.** {From this, then, it is especially to be understood how the almsgiving is not to be despised which is expended on any poor people whatever by the right of humanity, since indeed the Lord relieved the need of the poor from those moneybags which he filled from the resources of others. But if perhaps someone says that neither those weak and beggars whom the Lord ordered to be invited, nor those to whom he was wont to give out from the moneybags, were sinners, and that therefore it does not follow that, on account of the gospel testimonies, even sinners are commanded to be received or fed by the merciful, let him attend to what I already mentioned above, that surely those are sinners, and especially wicked, who hate and persecute the Church, of whom nevertheless it is said: *Do good to those who hate you*, and this is confirmed by the example of God the Father *who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^24]. Let us not, then, receive sinners because they are sinners, but yet let us treat those very ones, because they are also men, with human consideration. Let us persecute in them their own iniquity, let us have mercy on their common condition, and so, *untiringly, while we have time, let us work good toward all, but especially toward those of the household of faith*[^25].}
