# Sermon 236: In the Days of Easter

**1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ, as the Apostle says, *died for our offenses, and rose for our justification*[^1]. As by his death we are sown, so by his resurrection we sprout. For by his death is signified the death of our life. About this matter, hear the Apostle: *We have been buried*, he says, *with Christ through Baptism into death, so that as Christ rose from the dead, so we too may walk in newness of life*[^2]. He had nothing to amend on the cross: because he ascended the cross without sin. Let us be amended on his cross, and there let us lay down what we have wrongly contracted, so that we may be able to be justified by his resurrection. For you ought to distinguish thus: *He was handed over for our offenses, and rose for our justification.* He did not say: He was handed over for our justification, and rose for our offenses. In his being handed over, offense sounds; in his resurrection, righteousness sounds. Therefore let offense die, and let righteousness rise.

**2.** This hope, this gift, this promise, this so great grace, when Christ died, his disciples lost from their mind, and in his death they fell away from hope. See, his resurrection was being announced to them, and the words of those announcing it seemed to them like nonsense. The truth had become as it were nonsense. If at any time the resurrection is preached in this age, and it seems to someone to be nonsense, do not all say that he has great torment? Do not all detest, recoil, turn away, shut their ears, refuse to hear? See what the disciples were when Christ was dead: what we shudder at, that they were. The rams had this evil that the lambs shudder at. Then these two to whom he appeared on the road, and whose eyes were held that they should not recognize him—where their heart was, their words indicate; and what is going on in the mind, the voice is the witness, but for us: for to him even the heart lay open. They were speaking among themselves about his death. He joined them as it were a third traveler; and on the road he began to converse with them on the road, he mingled in conversation. He asks what they were saying among themselves, though he knew everything; that he might provoke them to confession as one who did not know. And they say to him: *Are you the only stranger in Jerusalem, and do you not know what has happened there in these days, concerning Jesus of Nazareth, who was a great prophet?* No longer Lord, but prophet. For this they thought he had been, when he was dead. They still honored him as a prophet; they did not yet recognize the Lord, not only of the prophets, but also of the angels. *How*, they say, *our elders and the chief priests handed him over to the condemnation of death. And behold, it is now the third day since these things were done. But we were hoping that he was the one to redeem Israel*[^3]. Is this all your trouble? You were hoping, have you now despaired? You see that they had lost hope. He began, therefore, to expound the Scriptures to them, so that they might recognize Christ there, where they had abandoned Christ. For they had despaired of Christ, because they had seen him dead. But he opened the Scriptures to them, so that they might recognize that, if he had not died, he could not be Christ. He taught about Moses, he taught about the Scriptures that follow, he taught about the prophets, what he had said to them: *That it was fitting for Christ to die, and so to enter into his glory*[^4]. They heard, they rejoiced, they sighed; and, as they themselves confessed, they burned: and they did not recognize the present light.

**3.** But what a mystery, my brothers! He enters in to them, he becomes their guest; and he who was not recognized along the whole road is recognized in the breaking of the bread. Learn to receive guests, where Christ is recognized. Do you not know that if you have received any Christian, you have received him himself? Does he not himself say: *I was a guest, and you received me?* And when it is said to him: *Lord, when did we see you as a guest?* he answers: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^5]. When therefore a Christian receives a Christian, members serve members; and the head rejoices, and reckons as given to itself what has been bestowed on its member. Here, then, let Christ be fed when he hungers, let him receive drink when he thirsts, let him be clothed when he is naked, let the stranger be received, let the sick be visited. This the necessity of the journey requires. So must we live in this pilgrimage, where Christ is in need. He is in need in his own, he is full in himself. But he who is in need in his own, and is full in himself, leads the needy to himself. There will be no hunger, no thirst, no nakedness, no sickness, no pilgrimage, no toil, no sorrow. I know that these things will not be there, and I do not know what will be there. For these things, which will not be there, I know: but that which we are to find there, eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man. We can love it, we can desire it, we can sigh after so great a good in this pilgrimage: but to think of it worthily and to explain it in words we cannot. Certainly I cannot. Therefore, my brothers, seek who can. If you can find him, draw me too along with you as a disciple. This I know, that *he who is able*, as the Apostle says, *to do more than we ask or understand*[^6], will lead us there, where there comes to pass what is written: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you*[^7]. Our whole business will be the praise of God. What will we praise, if we do not love; and we will love that which we will see? For we will see the truth, and the truth itself will be God, whom we will praise. There we will find what we sang today, *Amen*, It is true: *Alleluia*, Praise the Lord.

[^1]: Rom 4:25
[^2]: Rom 6:4
[^3]: Luke 24:18-21
[^4]: Luke 24:26
[^5]: Matt 25:35, 38, 40
[^6]: Eph 3:20
[^7]: Ps 84:4 (83:5)
