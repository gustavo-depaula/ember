# Sermon 91: On the Words of the Gospel, Matthew 22:42-46: Where the Lord Asked the Jews Whose Son They Said the Christ Was

**1. 1.** The Jews, when questioned, as we now heard from the Gospel when it was read out, how our Lord Jesus Christ was the son of David, whom David himself had called his Lord, could not answer. For in the Lord they knew this, what they saw. For the son of man appeared to them: but the Son of God was hidden. Hence it is that they both believed he could be overcome, and mocked him hung on the wood, saying: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross, and we believe in him*[^1]. They saw one thing, they did not recognize another. *For if they had recognized him, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*[^2]. Yet they knew Christ to be the son of David. For even now they hope that he himself is to come. It is hidden from them that he has come, but it is hidden from them willingly. For if they did not recognize him hanging, they ought not to have failed to recognize him reigning. For in whose name are all nations called and blessed, but in his whom they think was not the Christ? For he himself is the son of David, surely of the seed of David according to the flesh, the son of Abraham. But if it was said to Abraham: *In your seed all nations shall be blessed*[^3], and they see all nations now blessed in our Christ; why do they await what has already come, and not fear what is to come? For our Lord Jesus Christ himself said he was a stone, using prophetic testimony to assert it of himself. And a stone of such kind that if anyone strikes against it, he is shattered; but on whomever that stone comes, it crushes him[^4]. For when it is struck against, it lies low: lying low it shatters the one striking, coming on high it crushes the proud. The Jews, then, have already been shattered by that striking: it remains that by his glorious coming they be even crushed, unless perhaps while they live they recognize him, that they may not die. For God is patient, and daily invites them to faith.

**2. 2.** But since the Jews could not answer the Lord, who put a question and said whose son they said the Christ was; and they answered, The son of David: by adding and putting the question: *How then does David in the spirit call him Lord, saying: The Lord said to my Lord, Sit at my right hand, until I put your enemies under your feet? If, then, David,* he says, *in the spirit calls him Lord, how is he his son*[^5]? He did not say: He is not his son; but: *How is he his son? How,* when he says it, is the word of one seeking, not of one denying: as though he said this to them, You say well indeed that the Christ is the son of David, but David himself calls him Lord; the one he calls Lord, how is he his son? The Jews would say, if they had been instructed in the Christian faith, which we hold; if they did not close their hearts against the Gospel, if they had wanted to have spiritual life in themselves, they would answer, instructed in the faith of the Church, to this question, and would say, Since *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^6]; behold how he is the Lord of David. But because *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^7]; behold how he is the son of David. But, not knowing, they were silent, nor at least with closed mouth did they open their ears, so that, what they could not answer when questioned, they might know when taught.

**3. 3.** But because it is a great thing to know the mystery, how the Lord of David is also the son of David: how one person is man and God: how in the form of a man he is less than the Father, in the form of God equal to the Father: how again he says both: *The Father is greater than I*[^8]; and: *I and the Father are one*[^9]: because it is a great sacrament, that it may be grasped, morals must be formed. For to the unworthy it is closed, to those who earn it it is opened. It is not stones, nor crowbars, nor fists, nor heels by which we knock at the Lord. Life knocks, to life it is opened. With the heart it is sought, with the heart it is asked, with the heart it is knocked, to the heart it is opened. But this heart, which rightly asks, rightly knocks and seeks, must be devout. First, to love God freely; for this is devotion: and not to set for oneself, outside him, a reward that one would await from him. For nothing is better than he. And what dear thing does he ask of God, to whom God himself is cheap? He gives earth, and you rejoice, lover of earth, and become earth. If you rejoice when he gives earth, how much more ought you to rejoice when he gives you himself, who made heaven and earth? God, then, must be loved freely. For the devil, not knowing what was being done within holy Job, brought a great charge against him, saying: *Does Job worship God for nothing*[^10]?

**4. 4.** Therefore if the adversary brought this charge, we ought to fear lest this be charged against us. For we have business with a great slanderer. If he seeks to feign what is not, how much more to charge what is? Yet let us rejoice, because the judge is such that he cannot be deceived by our accuser. For if we had a man as judge, the enemy would feign for him whatever he wished. No one is more cunning than the devil in feigning. For even now he himself contrives all false charges about the saints. So, since his accusations cannot prevail with God, he scatters them among men. And what does this profit him, when the Apostle says: *This is our glory, the testimony of our conscience*[^11]? Yet do you think that he contrives no false charges by any cunning? He knows what evil he does thereby, unless the vigilance of faith resists him. For he scatters evil things about the good for this reason, that the weak may think no one is good, and give themselves to be snatched by lusts and dissipated, saying within themselves: For who is there who keeps the commandment of God? or who is there who keeps chastity? While he thinks no one does, he himself becomes no one. This, then, the devil does. But Job was such a man that he could contrive nothing about him: for his life was known and exceedingly clear. But because he had many riches, he charged this, which, even if it were so, could be in the heart, could not appear in his morals. He worshiped God, he did alms; and with what heart he did them, no one knew, not even the devil: but God knew. God bears witness to his servant: the devil slanders the servant of God. He is permitted to be tempted, Job is proved, the devil is confounded. Job is found to worship God freely, to love freely: not because he gave something, but because he did not take away himself. For he says: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it is done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^12]. The fire of temptation came; but it found gold, not stubble: it took away the dross, it did not turn him into ashes.

**5. 5.** Because, then, to understand the sacrament of God, how Christ is both man and God, the heart must be cleansed; and it is cleansed by morals, by life, by chastity, by holiness, by love, and by faith that works through love (and this which I speak is all, as it were, a tree, which has its root in the heart: for deeds proceed only from the root of the heart; where, if you plant cupidity, thorns proceed; if you plant love, fruits proceed): at once the Lord, after this question was put to the Jews, when they were unable to answer, went on to speak of morals, to show why they were unworthy to understand what he asked. For the wretched, proud men, when they could not answer, ought surely to have said: We do not know; Master, tell us. They were silent at the question, nor did they open their mouth to inquire. And at once the Lord, concerning their pride: *Beware,* he says, *of the scribes, who love to preside in the synagogues, and love the first place at banquets*[^13]. Not because they receive it, but because they love it. For here he accused their heart, and he could not be an accuser of the heart unless he were an inspector of the heart. For it is fitting that to a servant of God who has some honor in the Church the first place be given: because if it is not given, it is bad for the one who does not give it; yet it is not good for the one to whom it is given. It is fitting, therefore, that in the congregation of Christians the superiors of the people sit more eminently, that they be distinguished by the very seat, and their office appear sufficiently: yet not that they be puffed up by the seat; but that they consider the burden for which they are to render an account. But who knows whether they love this or do not love it? This matter is of the heart, it can have no judge but God. The Lord himself was admonishing his own, lest they fall into such leaven: which in another place he calls: *Beware of the leaven of the Pharisees and Sadducees*[^14]. And when they thought he said this because they had not brought bread; he answered them: *Has it slipped your mind, how many thousands were satisfied from five loaves? Then they understood,* he says, *that he was calling their teaching leaven*[^15]. For they loved these temporal things: but as for the eternal, they neither feared the evil things nor loved the good things. With closed heart they could not understand what the Lord asked.

**6. 6.** But what should the Church of God do, that it may be able to understand what it first deserved to believe? Let it make the mind capable of receiving what will be given. That this might happen—that is, that the mind might be capable—the Lord our God suspended his promises, he did not take them away. He suspended them for this reason, that we might extend ourselves: for this reason we extend ourselves, that we might grow: for this reason we grow, that we might attain. See the apostle Paul extended toward the suspended things. *Not that I have already received, or am already perfect. Brothers, I do not consider that I have laid hold of it: but one thing, forgetting the things behind, stretched out toward the things that are ahead, according to my intention I follow toward the prize of the heavenly calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^16]. He was running on earth: the prize was hanging from heaven. On earth, then, he was running: but in the Spirit he was ascending. See him, then, extended, see him hanging toward the suspended things. *I follow,* he says, *toward the prize of the heavenly calling of God in Christ Jesus.*

**6. 7.** We must walk, then, and the feet need not be anointed, nor beasts of burden sought, nor a ship provided. Run by affection, walk by love, ascend by love. Why do you seek the way? Cling to Christ, who by descending and ascending made himself the way. Do you want to ascend? Hold the one ascending. For by yourself you cannot be lifted up. For *no one ascends into heaven except the one who descended from heaven, the Son of man, who is in heaven*[^17]. If no one ascends except the one who descended, and he himself is the Son of man, our Lord Jesus; do you too want to ascend? Be a member of him who alone ascends. For he, the head with the rest of the members, is one man. And since no one can ascend except the one who in his body has become a member of him; it is fulfilled, that *no one ascends except the one who descended.* For you cannot say: Behold, why did Peter, for example, ascend, why did Paul ascend, why did the Apostles ascend, if *no one ascends except the one who descended?* It is answered you: Peter, Paul, and the other Apostles, and all the faithful, what do they hear from the Apostle? *But you are the body of Christ, and members in part*[^18]. If, then, the body of Christ and the members are of one, do not make two. For he left father and mother, and clung to his wife, that they might be two in one flesh[^19]. He left the father, because here he did not show himself equal to the Father: but *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^20]. He left also his mother, the Synagogue, from whom he was carnally born. He clung to his wife, that is, to his Church. When he himself recalled this testimony too, he showed that the marriage could not be separated: *Have you not read,* he says, *that God who made them from the beginning, made them male and female? They will be,* he says, *two in one flesh. What, then, God has joined, let no man separate*[^21]. And what is: *Two in one flesh?* It follows, and he says: *Therefore now not two, but one flesh*[^22]. *No one ascends except the one who descended*[^23].

**7. 8.** For that you may know that the bridegroom and the bride are one man, according to the flesh of Christ, not according to the divinity—for according to the divinity, what he is, we cannot be; because he is creator, we creation; he the maker, we the made; he the founder, we the founded: but that we might be one with him in him, he willed to be our head, by taking flesh from us, in which he might die for us—that you may know, then, that this whole is one Christ, he said through Isaiah: *As to a bridegroom he bound a miter on me, and as a bride he clothed me with an ornament*[^24]. He himself the bridegroom, he himself the bride. He plainly the bridegroom in the head, the bride in the body. *For they will be,* he says, *two in one flesh:* and *now not two, but one flesh*[^25].

**7. 9.** And so, as those who pertain to his members, that we may understand this sacrament, as I said, brothers, let us live devoutly, let us love God freely. He himself, who showed the form of a servant to those on pilgrimage, keeps the form of God for those who arrive. From the form of a servant he laid the road, from the form of God he founded the homeland. Because, then, it is much for us to grasp this, but it is not much to believe this: for *unless you believe,* says Isaiah, *you will not understand*[^26]: let us walk by faith as long as we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord, until we arrive at sight, where we will see face to face[^27]. Walking by faith, let us do good works. In good works let the love of God be gratuitous, let the love of neighbor be beneficent. For we have nothing to bestow on God: but because toward the neighbor we have something to bestow, by bestowing on the needy one we shall earn the abundant one. Thence let each one bestow on another what he has: whatever more he has, let him lavish on the needy. One has money: let him feed the poor, clothe the naked, build a church, work whatever good he can with money. Another has counsel: let him govern his neighbor, drive away the darkness of doubt with the light of devotion. Another has teaching: let him pay it out from the Lord's storeroom, let him minister food to his fellow servants, let him strengthen the faithful, recall the erring, seek the lost, do what he can. There is something that even the poor may bestow: one may lend his feet to the lame, another offer his own eyes as guides to the blind; another may visit the sick, another bury the dead. These things are in all, so that it is altogether difficult to find anyone who does not have something to bestow on another. And that last and great thing which the Apostle says: *Bear one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ*[^28].
