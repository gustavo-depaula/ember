# Sermon 293/B: On the Birthday of Saint John the Baptist

**1.** Today we celebrate the birthday of a great man. Do you want to know how great? *No one,* Scripture says, *has arisen greater among those born of women*. The one born of a virgin said this about him; he bore this testimony to his witness. The judge pronounced this sentence about his herald; thus the Word wished to honor his voice, as you know and heard today also in the morning sermon.

**2.** Christ is the Word and John the voice, because of Christ it is written: *In the beginning was the Word*. But John, speaking of himself, said: *I am* the voice *of one crying in the wilderness.* The word belongs to the heart, the voice to the ear. When the voice strikes the ear without carrying a word to the mind, it has an empty sound but no useful fruit. For a word to be born in my heart it needs no voice; but for what is already born in my heart to be carried to your heart, it takes up the voice's ministry. Therefore, the word can precede the voice but cannot proceed without a voice. The voice is created not to generate a word that did not exist, but to reveal what existed and was hidden. Let us therefore see in Christ and John what we have said about word and voice. Seek Christ. *In the beginning was the Word.* Where was he? *And the Word was with God. In the beginning with God.* How far before us, how far above us! *And the Word became flesh* to dwell among us. How would we know this unless we heard the voice? For Christ was walking among people already clothed in mortal flesh, yet people came to John and asked him: *Are you the Christ?* But to show himself the voice, he made known the Word he preceded. He rejected unowed honor and pointed his finger toward Christ. For to those saying, *Are you the Christ?* John replied: *See the Lamb of God; see the one who takes away the world's sin*. Hear him; recognize him. I precede him; I announce him. Remember what he said: *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness: Prepare the Lord's way*—not mine, but the Lord's. For when I cry out, I announce him, because the herald's voice is the judge's arrival. But when the one I announce comes and rests in your heart, *he must increase, but I must decrease*. For you know, beloved, that when a word takes up the voice's help and flows through the paths of the ears into the region of the heart, that word grows in the heart while the voice fades in the ear. The sound that struck the ear does not remain, whereas the meaning remains without limit because it has descended into the mind. Why is this? Because *he must increase, but I must decrease.*

**3.** John baptizes, and Christ baptizes too. John was told: *The one upon whom you see the Spirit descending like a dove and remaining upon him is the one who baptizes in the Holy Spirit and fire.* You know this, brothers; it happened when Christ was baptized. And see, now throughout the whole world he is the one who baptizes. Christ's baptism has increased everywhere; but John's baptism, although it has a mystery in the remembrance of the past, has no ministry in present celebration. John's baptism ceased; Christ's baptism increased. Therefore, he also says: *He must increase, but I must decrease*. We find this statement in both their births and their sufferings. For although John said of John—that is, John the Evangelist of John the Baptist—although he said of him, *There was a man sent from God whose name was John; he came as a witness to bear witness to the light,* although he came forward to bear witness to the light, nevertheless he was born today, brothers, when night grows and day begins to diminish. Christ, however, was born at the winter solstice, as you know, when night's losses begin turning into light's gain, because we were *once darkness but now light in the Lord*. Why were they born this way? Because *he must increase,* but John *decrease.* In their sufferings too, John is beheaded by the sword, Christ exalted upon the wood; Christ is raised, John laid low. John is beheaded to diminish; Christ is stretched out to increase. *He must increase,* but John *decrease.* Not without reason, I think, the parents' ages too are chosen: an elderly woman bears John, a young virgin Christ; hopeless barrenness bears the one, untouched virginity the other. Finally, a growing young woman bears Christ, declining old age John.

**4.** But what is this, my brothers? How great is this man's dignity, that an angel announced his coming birth to his parents, as also the Lord Christ's! Why did he deserve this? Because *no one has arisen greater among those born of women*. For as you know, the angel Gabriel is sent to the priest Zechariah; Gabriel is sent to the Virgin Mary. He promises each a son and receives an answer from each. Zechariah answered the angel promising a son: *How shall I know? For I am old, and my wife is barren and advanced in her days*. Mary too answered: *How will this happen, since I do not know a man?* Both despair according to nature's law; they did not yet know, I think, that with the gift of God's grace, nature's law ceases. Therefore, both answer out of doubt; yet he is punished and she blessed. He is told: *See, you will be silent*; Mary is told: *Blessed are you among women*. Zechariah loses his voice; Mary conceives the Word. After this the Word becomes flesh in the virgin, and the voice is born from one who is silent. John, born, restores his father's voice; the father, speaking, gives his son a name. Everyone marvels, everyone is astonished, and mingling murmurs among themselves, they say to one another: *What do you think this child will be?.* Let us now speak from the Gospel. *For the Lord's hand was with him.* What do you think he will be, who began this way? He is still so small and already so great. If this one, who has just begun, will be great, what will the one who always existed be? The one whom John, still enclosed in the hidden place of his mother's womb, recognized as he too lay hidden in the chamber of the virgin's womb, and greeted by movements because he could not yet do so by words—what, then, will he be? Do you want to know what he will be? I will say it briefly; hear the Prophet: *He,* he says, *will be called the Lord of the whole earth*.

**5.** Therefore, celebrating in festive gatherings the birthday of blessed John, the Lord's forerunner, a great man, let us ask the help of his prayers. For because he is the bridegroom's friend, he can also obtain for us that we may belong to the bridegroom and deserve to find his grace. But if we want to find his grace, let us not wrong his birthday. Let the remnants of sacrilege cease; let the pursuits and places of vanity cease. Let the things customarily done not be done—no longer indeed in demons' honor, but still according to demons' custom. Yesterday after evening the whole city blazed with stinking flames; smoke covered the entire air. If you pay little attention to religion, at least consider the common harm. We know, brothers, that children do these things, but their elders ought to have forbidden them. For someone says: *The one who does not forbid sin when he can commands it.* Indeed, brothers, in the Lord's name the Church advances year by year; these things diminish, and every diminution certainly tends toward nothing. But they have not yet been so completely eliminated that we may safely be silent. Nor will we be able to be silent until oldness and newness reach their proper ends: so that the old superstition may be finished and the new religion perfected. Through our Lord Jesus Christ, to whom be honor and glory with God the almighty Father and with the Holy Spirit forever and ever. Amen.
