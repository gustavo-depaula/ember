# Sermon 114a

**1.** We have heard a most healthful precept from the holy Gospel, that we should forgive the sin to a brother who has sinned against us. Lest it suffice to have done this once, but however often he sins it is to be forgiven, if he asks pardon. So he says this: *If he sins against you seven times in a day, and seven times in a day turns to you saying: I repent, forgive him* [^1]. If you understand "seven times in a day," then however often; for the number seven is wont to be set for the whole. Whence is that: *The just man falls seven times and rises* [^2]; that is, however often he has been humbled by some tribulation, he is not deserted, but freed from all his tribulations. Whence is that: *Seven times a day I will praise you* [^3]; for this is *seven times a day*, which is always. So this, *seven times a day*, which is said elsewhere: *His praise shall always be in my mouth* [^4]. For we do not sound the praises of the Lord with the tongue only, and when we are silent do not praise; clearly in all our good thoughts, in all our acts and good conduct we praise him, from whom we rejoice to have received these things. For we see even the Apostles asking that faith be increased for them [^5]. Did they themselves give the first beginnings of faith to themselves, and ask the increases from the Lord? Far be it. So they asked this, that he who began might himself perfect, according to the Apostle saying: *Because he who began a good work in you will perfect it unto the end* [^6]. And what we have just now sung, what else does it show, dearly beloved? *Lead me*, he says, *Lord, in your way, and I will walk in your truth* [^7]. He does not say: Bring me to your way; for this too he himself does; but not, when he has brought, let him forsake. It is too little, then, to have brought to the way, unless there follow having led in the way, and having led through to the homeland. Since, then, we have all good things from God, in all our good works, when we think of the giver of all goods, we praise God without end: but when we praise God, if we live well, without end, let us bless the Lord at all times, and may his praise for this reason be always in our mouth [^8]. *Seven times*, he says, *a day I will praise you*: by the number seven signifying the whole:

**2.** Therefore if *your brother sins against you seven times in a day, and coming says: I repent, forgive him* [^9]. Let it not be a weariness to you, always to pardon the penitent. If you are not a debtor, you would be a troublesome exactor with impunity; but since, being a debtor, you have a debtor, and you are the debtor of one who has no debt, see what you do with your debtor: for this God will do with his. Hear and fear: *Let my heart rejoice*, he says, *that I may fear your name* [^10]. If you rejoice when it is forgiven you, fear so as to forgive. For how much you ought to fear, the Savior himself shows, setting forth in the Gospel that servant with whom the master made a reckoning, and found him a debtor of a hundred thousand talents: *He ordered him to be sold, and all that he had, and to be repaid*. He, falling at the feet of his master, began to beg, that he might receive a delay, and deserved remission. But he, going out from the face of his master, all the debt relaxed for him, found himself too his debtor, his fellow servant, who owed him a hundred denarii, and, gripping his throat, began to drag him to payment. When it was forgiven him, his heart rejoiced, but not so as to fear the name of the Lord his God. The servant said to the fellow servant, what the servant had said to the master: *Have patience with me, and I will repay you*. But he: No, today you repay. It was reported to the householder; and, what you know, he not only threatened him that he would not afterward remit, if he found him a debtor, but folded the whole that he had relaxed back upon his head, and ordered him to repay whatever he had bestowed [^11]. How, then, is it to be feared, my brothers, if there is faith in us, if we believe the Gospel, if we do not think the Lord a liar? Let us fear, let us observe, let us beware, let us forgive. For what do you lose, by that which you forgive? You give pardon, not money.

**3.** Although even in dispensing money itself you ought not to be dry trees. In dispensing money you bestow on the needy: in giving pardon you forgive the one sinning; both the Lord sees, both he rewards, both he commended in one place: *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you; give, and it will be given you* [^12]. But you neither pardon nor dispense: you hold anger, you keep money. Beware, anger, where you cannot be freed by money: *Treasures will not profit the unjust* [^13]. This is not my saying, but a divine one: those who have read know it. I read it that I might say it, I believed it that I might speak it: *treasures will not profit the unjust*. They seem to profit, but they will not profit. Perhaps in the present: perhaps, if yet they profit anything; but in that day they will not profit. Let them be held, and they will not profit; let them be despised, and they will profit. You use justice well then, if you love it: because, if you do not love it, you will not have it. Fortitude, temperance, chastity, love, and the other goods of the soul you use well, if you love them: money you use well then, if you do not love it. In short, money, if it is loved, let it be kept in heaven; if it is to be feared lest it be lost, let it be guarded in a safer place. For neither does your servant keep faith with you in preserving money, while your Lord deceives you. Do you not hear him saying: *Lay up for yourselves treasure in heaven?* Behold, he did not command you to lose it, but to make it migrate: *Lay up for yourselves treasure in heaven, where the thief does not approach, nor moth corrupt; for where your treasure is, there is your heart too* [^14]. On earth you lay up treasure, on earth you set your heart. What will become of your heart on earth? It wastes away, rots, turns to ash. Lift up on high what you love, and there love it. And do not think you will receive back the same that you set down: for you set down mortal things, you will receive immortal; you set down temporal things, you will receive eternal; you set down earthly things, you will receive heavenly; in short, you dole out what your Lord gave you, and you will receive a reward from that same Lord of yours.

**4.** But you will say: How am I to set it in heaven? by what engines am I to ascend into heaven with my gold and silver? Why do you seek engines? Migrate. Your porters are the poor, and through the wearing down of the world they have been made porters. In short, you make a transfer: here you give, and there you receive. Surely if you give here, you will receive there, and from the one to whom you give, from him you will receive. Let not now some ragged beggar come to your mind; but let this come to your mind: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me* [^15]. In the poor man receives the one who made the poor man: from the rich man receives the one who made the rich man; for what he gave, from it he receives; from his own you give to Christ, not from your own. For why do you boast, that here you found many things? Recall how you came. Everything here you found; and, having found many things, if you use them badly, you have swelled with pride. Did you not come forth naked from your mother's womb [^16]? Give, then, give, lest you lose what you have. If you give, you are to find it there: if you do not give, you are to leave it here; yet, whether you give or do not give, you are to migrate. But sometimes—even if a trifling excuse, and one to be reproved and rejected from the ears of the faithful—yet greed has some excuse, refusing to dole out to the poor from what abounds. For it says to itself: If I give, I will not have it; and giving much, I will be in need: and afterward I will seek one from whom I myself may receive. It ought to abound for me, not only for food and clothing, both for my house and my household, but also on account of good fortune, that I may have what to expend on a slanderer, that I may have from which to redeem myself: human affairs are full of mishaps; I ought to keep for myself, from which I may be able to free myself.

**5.** You say these things, when you want to keep money; what will you say, when you do not want to give pardon to the one sinning? If it irks you to dole out money to the needy, dole out pardon to the penitent. What do you lose, if you give? I know what you lose, I know what you let go; I see it, but you let it go for your own good: you let go anger, you let go indignation, you let go from your heart hatred of your brother. Shall these remain there, where you will be? Anger, indignation, abiding hatred, what does it make of you? What evil does it not make of you? Hear Scripture: He who *hates his brother is a murderer* [^17]. So even if he sins against me seven times in a day, shall I forgive him? Forgive. Christ said this, the truth said it, to whom you sang: *Lead me, Lord, in your way, and I will walk in your truth* [^18]. Do not fear, he does not deceive you. But there will be, you will say, no discipline; all sins whatever will always be unpunished; for it always delights to sin, when the one who sins thinks you will always pardon. It is not so. Let discipline both keep watch, and benevolence not sleep. For why do you think you render evil for evil, when you give discipline to the one sinning? Far be it: you render good for evil; and then you do not do well, if you do not give it. Sometimes indeed even discipline itself is tempered with gentleness: it is given. But is it not one thing to extinguish it by negligence, another to temper it with gentleness? Let discipline keep watch: pardon, and strike. See the Lord himself, hear the Lord himself, think of him to whom we daily beggars say: *Forgive us our debts* [^19]. And do you suffer weariness, when a brother constantly says to you: Forgive me, the penitent? How often do you say this to God? are you silent from this supplication in any prayer? Do you want God to say to you: Behold, yesterday I pardoned, the day before I pardoned, for so many days I pardoned; how often do I still pardon? Do you not want him to say to you: Always you come with those words, always you say: *Forgive us our debts*, always you beat your breast; and like hard iron you are not corrected. But because we were dealing with discipline, does not the Lord our God forgive us, because from faith we say: *Forgive us our debts?* And yet, although he forgives us, what was said of him? what is written of him? *For whom God loves he rebukes*. But perhaps with words? *He scourges every son whom he receives* [^20]. Lest the sinful son, scourged, be indignant at being rebuked, even the Only-begotten himself, without sin, deigned to be scourged. So give discipline, but from the heart let go anger. For so the Lord himself says, when he dealt with that debtor, to whom he folded back the whole debt, because he was inhuman to his fellow servant: *So*, he says, *your heavenly Father will do to you too, if each of you does not forgive his brother from your hearts* [^21]. Where God sees, there forgive; from there do not lose love, exercise healthful severity; love and strike, love and beat. For sometimes you flatter, and rage. How do you flatter and rage? Because you do not reprove sins; and those sins are going to kill the one whom by sparing perversely you love. Your word, sometimes harsh, sometimes hard, which will wound, attend to what it will do. Sin lays waste the heart, demolishes the inner parts, suffocates the soul, destroys the soul: have mercy, strike.

**6.** Set before your eyes, dearly beloved, that you may understand more plainly what I say, two men. Some heedless little boy wanted to sit where they knew a serpent lay hidden in the grass. If he sat, he would be bitten, and would die: two men knew this. One says: Do not sit there. He is despised; he will go to sit, he will go to perish. The other says: This one does not want to hear us; he must be rebuked, held, dragged away, struck with a slap: whatever we can do let us do, lest we lose the man. Another says: Let him be, do not strike, do not offend, do not hurt. Which of these is merciful? The one sparing, that the man may die by the serpent; or the one raging, that the man may be freed? And so understand it: those who are subject to you, you also correct; impose discipline on their conduct, keep benevolence. From the heart forgive, within let there be no anger; because that anger, when fresh, is a thin and as it were contemptible splinter; fresh anger disturbs the eye, like a splinter in the eye: *My eye is disturbed in anger* [^22]; but that splinter is nourished by suspicions, strengthened by length of time; this splinter is going to come to a beam. Inveterate anger will be hatred; now where there is hatred, there will be murder: *He who hates his brother is a murderer* [^23], he says. And sometimes men having hatred in their heart reprove the angry. Do you hold hatred, and reprove the angry one? *You see the splinter in your brother's eye, you do not see the beam in your own* [^24]. Let the sermon be concluded. Let the Lord be invoked, that he may deign to grant what he commands: *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you; give, and it will be given you* [^25].
