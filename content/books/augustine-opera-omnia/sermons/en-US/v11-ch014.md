# Sermon 352/A: (Dolbeau 14)

**1.** The Lord Jesus, the founder of the holy Gospel, as it were by a law of mercy wishing no one to perish, since for that reason he came to save what had perished, cried out as if in an assembly of the human race, as we have heard: *Since the times are fulfilled and the kingdom of God has drawn near, repent and believe in the Gospel*[^1]. This voice the founder of the world uttered, and the world heard it. Let what he said be done, who came to come to our aid, lest he come to judge those who despise him. Let it be done, while the voice of mercy sounds, before the tribunal of justice comes. He to whom it was said in the psalm: *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, Lord*[^2], himself kept the order of his help. First he came to dispense mercy, about to come to exercise judgment. For not judgment, but *mercy and judgment I will sing to you, Lord.* What greater mercy can be desired, asked, demanded, than that God did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all, that with him he might give us all things? What greater mercy than to come to die not for the just, but for the impious? What greater mercy than to come and not condemn those worthy to be condemned, but free those unworthy to be freed?

**2.** But let not this mercy be despised, my brethren. *The Lord is merciful and compassionate.* Add yet: *long-suffering.* Add yet: *and very merciful.* Fear what follows: *and truthful*[^3]. Far be it from us to despise the merciful one, that we should experience the truthful one! What need, then, is there for us to cry out? Let us rather hear the one who cries out: *Since the times are fulfilled.* For *the fullness of times came,* as the apostle says, and in the fullness of times *God sent his Son made of a woman, made under the law, that he might redeem those who were under the law, and that we might receive the adoption of sons*[^4]. Commending this fullness of times, the Lord said: *The times are fulfilled;* he came, awaited by few, found by many. Therefore *the times are fulfilled: repent.* How long ago it is that this is cried out, and would that it were sometime heard: *Since the times are fulfilled and the kingdom of God has drawn near, repent and believe in the Gospel!* Renounce and be converted. This is *repent and believe in the Gospel:* renounce dead works, believe in the living God. What does it profit to believe without good works? It was not the merit of good works that brought you to faith, but faith begins, that good works may follow. This is said to the holy Church in the Song of Songs: *Come, my bride, you shall come and pass through from the beginning of faith*[^5]. But who believes God, unless he has renounced the devil? Therefore, that you may renounce, *repent; believe,* that you may be saved.

**3.** This was said to the Jews, said to the idolatrous gentiles. Is it still to be said to Christians? It is still to be said to Christians who daily read that *the times are fulfilled,* still to be said to Christians that *the kingdom of heaven has drawn near,* still to be said to Christians: *Repent and believe in the Gospel?* Clearly, the one to whom I say "Believe in the Gospel" is not yet a Christian. No one, I suppose, hears me in this multitude who does not yet believe in the Gospel. Many hear me who are not yet made faithful by baptism, and still catechumens: not yet born, but already conceived. But when or how would they be conceived in the womb of mother Church, unless they were marked with some sacrament of faith? Many also hear me who are baptized and, neglecting in themselves the grace of God, love to sin. And to these what shall I say: *The times are fulfilled?* They answer: "We know." *The kingdom of God has drawn near?* They answer: "Long ago we heard it, long ago we believed it." What, then, shall I say? Since I am speaking to men who love to sin, despising in themselves the grace of the redeemer, what shall I say? *Repent.* I shall say it clearly, even if they do not permit me to say: *The times are fulfilled,* since they know it; *The kingdom of heaven has drawn near,* because they know this too; the very last that was set down: *Believe in the Gospel,* they do not permit me to say to them, because they have already believed; but let them hear: *Repent,* because they have not yet done so. From here, then, let us speak to the catechumens and to the negligent faithful. Let the diligent hear, since they too cannot be lacking among the members of Christ. Let them not think me wordy and superfluous, if they have love: it is a common business; they wish me to speak also to those whom they desire to be corrected together with them.

**4.** Let those, then, hear me saying not: *The times are fulfilled, the kingdom of heaven has drawn near, believe in the Gospel,* but saying: *Repent.* The catechumen answers me: "Why do you say to us: *Repent?* Let me first be faithful, and perhaps I will live well, and I will not be a penitent." I answer such men: That you may be faithful, *repent.* For those coming to baptism come through repentance. For unless by repenting they condemn their old life, they will not, as candidates, come to the new life. That I might say this, I have not dared beyond measure, nor usurped anything to myself unwonted to the Scripture of God. Let the catechumens hear the Acts of the Apostles. When the apostles, filled with the Holy Spirit, were speaking to the crowds, because they were speaking in all tongues, the crowds, astonished and made more attentive, heard from the apostles the word of God. All, because they were men of diverse tongues, *were pricked to the heart,* and because they were Jews, they repented of having crucified Christ, and, troubled about their own salvation and fearing certain damnation for so great a crime, they said to the apostles: *What shall we do, brethren?*[^6] And the apostles: *Do penance, and let each of you be baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ*[^7]. You have heard how those not yet baptized were bidden to come to baptism through penance. This, then, we too will say to the catechumens. For we have recalled the testimony of the Scriptures, lest they think we say to them impudently that, before they are faithful, we wish them to be penitents.

**5.** But now let those who are faithful and live badly not think that I say to them impudently: *Repent.* Now, then, I will speak to both: Change your life, lest you lose your life. Condemn past sins, fear future evils and hope for good things. The evil man first does not contradict himself, that he should hope for good things, who is not good. You hope for a good: be what you hope for. First hear the one who commands, and then demand of the one who promises. God has become your debtor not by borrowing, but by promising. *For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been his counselor, or who has first given to him, and it shall be repaid him?*[^8] What did he receive from you, who gave you all? Therefore he deigned to become a debtor, because he promised, and so to become a debtor that he even made a bond. This bond is the Gospel, it is Scripture, in which God made himself your debtor not by borrowing, as I said, but by promising. Therefore hear: *Repent, believe in the Gospel.* Hear the command, and then demand the bond. "Tomorrow," he says. For he did not find what to say to me, set before his own face and now displeasing to his own eyes. How much more, then, to the purer eyes of God! When, then, he had been displeased with himself in his evil ways, "Tomorrow," he says, "I will amend; do not press me, let today too pass. Why do you hasten? Tomorrow I will amend. Why do you press me on the day?" Because the day does not depend on you. All contention with such men is about delay. Since they say: "Let today pass as yesterday did. Tomorrow I shall be corrected, changed, amended, I shall fulfill what is commanded: I shall repent," that is: the whole matter turns on delay. Tomorrow is no great thing. But see, lest, just as you wished today to be like yesterday, so you wish tomorrow to be like today. Although I could say: Why not today, but tomorrow, you who do not know what will befall you tomorrow? But see, let us bear with it. Do not make tomorrow like today. "For what," he says, "if I do? Do I not hold what is written: *On whatever day the wicked and impious man is converted, I will forget all his iniquities?* It is said, and divinely said." I confess: it is divinely written. How well you have remembered God's bond! You hold what is owed you, you forget what you owe.

**6.** Impious men hear these things, not knowing the law, exiles from the grace of God, unworthy of the mercy of God, because it is said in our Scripture: *On whatever day the wicked man is converted, I will forget all his iniquities,* and they say to us: "You make men sin, who promise them such great impunity when suddenly converted." Do we dare not to promise what God promised? "Not a good law," they say, "which makes sinners abound." I would wish to know whether the one who speaks does not judge himself a sinner. And he is angry at God who waits, the very man whom God awaits. What if God did not promise this, who by his patience leads to repentance? If he did not promise this, would not more become sinners through despair? Let your love attend a little. Who is found not a sinner? The flesh weak, the age feeble, the sense narrow, the mind fearful, the nature ignorant of things—into how many sins does it hurl the incautious and negligent? How few can appear and exist who are not convicted of being sinners? Take away this mercy, take away this promised indulgence, take away in this most turbulent sea of iniquities the harbor of pardon, let there be nowhere for those tossed about to take refuge: do they not through despair add sins to sins, and say to themselves: "I am now a sinner, there is no mercy upon sinners, damnation is necessarily owed; why do I not do what I wish, why do I not fulfill my lusts, why do I not at least now do whatever delights me, since I am to lose this life and receive none afterward?" So desperate are gladiators, whence too a proverb has arisen: Back away from the wounded one. For the wounded one seeks where to die, and does not hesitate to do evil, because he hopes for no indulgence. He lets the reins go very loose to his desires, he is carried where he can, he desires that whatever pleases him be lawful for him: therefore he perishes by despairing. But you too, do not perish by hoping. Two voices, both contrary, both dangerous, through which men perish, but yet God despised neither voice: he provided for both this and that. What are those two voices? "Why should I not sin, why should I not do whatever I wish? When I am converted, all is forgiven me." Such perish by hoping. The other voice, on the other side, contrary indeed, but equally dangerous. "Why should I not do whatever I wish, I to whom no mercy is owed, to whom no indulgence will be given? I will do whatever I wish." They perish by despairing.

**7.** Which shall I accuse first: perverse hope or unbelieving despair? Why do you perish by hoping, why do you perish by despairing? Let those hear a few words who wish to perish by hoping: *Return, transgressors, to the heart*[^9]. Hear God saying, because you say: "Tomorrow, tomorrow," *Do not delay to be converted to the Lord*[^10]. To you I say, who prepare to be converted to the Lord, but put it off; to you I say, for whom I fear lest hope destroy you: hear not me, but him in whom you hope and by hoping too much in whom you perish: *Do not delay to be converted to the Lord, nor put it off from day to day. For suddenly his wrath will come, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you*[^11]. Where is what you were saying: "I put it off to tomorrow and tomorrow to another tomorrow, since God promised me indulgence, saying: *On the day the sinner is converted*; for he did not say: If today he is converted, but: *On whatever day he is converted?*" God answers you: "I promised you indulgence, I did not promise you a tomorrow." Or do you perhaps wish to join and couple together two promises, what God promised and what the astrologer promised? For God promised indulgence, the astrologer perhaps promised you a tomorrow. Watch out, wretch: the astrologer is mistaken, and God condemns you. How many, suddenly snatched away, were permitted neither to make satisfaction to God nor to accuse the astrologers.

**8.** Let him too hear who had perished by despairing the things he was saying: "I have now committed such great evils that I hope for no indulgence. It remains to fulfill my lusts, to satiate my delights, that at least here I may have rest, which there I will not have." Hear you too the voice of God, not your own: "Despairing one, return to hope." Hear Paul saying: *Therefore I obtained mercy—I who persecuted the Church of God—that in me Christ Jesus might show all his long-suffering, for the instruction of those who shall believe in him unto eternal life*[^12]. Therefore you have a pattern: do not despair, hear the bond which a little before the one who hoped badly was reciting, and from it let the despairing one be corrected. What were you hearing? Whence did he flatter himself, that he put it off from day to day? "God, God to me: *On whatever day the wicked and impious man is converted, I will forget all his iniquities.*" Read and return. You were sitting on the bank, or carrying a noose in despair, you longed to bring death upon yourself, perhaps, having nothing to live on, you wished to die: return, you have whereby to live: see, the bread of God. *I do not wish the death of the impious, so much as that he turn back and live*[^13].

**9.** Therefore, brethren, there is no reason for us to complain about God, there is nothing in which he displeases the perverse, although he cannot displease any but the perverse. The straight does not please the perverse; the crooked cannot be fitted to the straight. For there is nothing to be said. You were saying: "See, Christians grant impunity: they make sin abound." I have given the reason, that men would perish more by despairing, if no harbor of indulgence were set before the one tossed about. Both, then, God did not forsake, both those who hope badly and those who despair badly. For the sake of those who despair badly he gave the harbor of indulgence; for the sake of those who hope badly he made the day of death uncertain. There is nothing to be said, but there is something to be done. Let them say to me, then: "What shall we do?", who are moved by the word of truth; let them say not with their voices, but with their hearts: "What, then, shall we do?" What shall I answer, but what we have heard: *Repent?* What more shall I say? *Repent.* You are a catechumen: repent and you will be renewed; you are a bad baptized person, not a badly baptized one: repent and you will be healed. I do not find what to say to this; you know what to do. Turned . . .
