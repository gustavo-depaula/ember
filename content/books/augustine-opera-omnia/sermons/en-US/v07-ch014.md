# Sermon 242: In the Days of Easter, on the Resurrection of Bodies, Against the Gentiles.

**1. 1.** On these holy days dedicated to the resurrection of the Lord, as far as we can by his gift, let us treat of the resurrection of the flesh. For this is our faith, this gift was promised to us in the flesh of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the example went before in him. For he willed not only to foretell to us what he promised for the end, but also to demonstrate it. Those indeed who were then with him saw, and though they were terrified, and believed they were seeing a spirit, they grasped the solidity of the body. For he spoke not only with words to their ears, but also with his appearance to their eyes: and it was too little to offer himself to be seen, unless he also offered himself to be handled and felt all over. For he said: *Why are you troubled, and why do thoughts arise in your heart?* For they supposed they were seeing a spirit. *Why are you troubled*, he says, *and why do thoughts arise in your heart? See my hands and my feet: handle, and see, that a spirit does not have bones and flesh, as you see me to have*[^1]. Against this evidence men argue. For what else would men do, who savor the things that are of men, but argue about God against God? For he is God, they are men. But God *knows the thoughts of men, that they are vain*[^2]. In a carnal man the whole rule of understanding is the custom of seeing. What they are accustomed to see, they believe: what they are not accustomed to, they do not believe. Beyond custom God works miracles, because he is God. Greater miracles indeed are that so many men are born daily who did not exist, than that a few have risen who did exist: and yet these miracles are not grasped by consideration, but have grown cheap through their constant occurrence. Christ rose, it is a settled matter. It was a body, it was flesh, he hung on the cross, he sent forth his soul, it was laid in the tomb. He who lived in it showed it alive. Why do we marvel? Why do we not believe? It is God who did it: consider the author, and remove the doubt.

**2. 2.** Men ask, then, whether this corruption of the body, which they feel in their flesh, will be in the resurrection of the dead. We say it will not be. They answer us: If there will be no corruption, why will there be eating? Or if there will be no eating, why did the Lord eat after the resurrection? Just now, as the Gospel was read, we heard that when he showed himself alive to the eyes and hands of his disciples, it seemed to him too little to demonstrate the bodily evidence: but he added: *Have you here anything to eat? And they offered him a piece of broiled fish, and a honeycomb: and he ate, and gave the remains to them*[^3]. So it is said to us: If the corruption of the body will not rise, why did the Lord Christ eat? You have read that he ate; have you read that he hungered? That he ate was of power, not of need. If he desired to eat, he would be in need. Again, if he could not eat, he would be less able. Did not the angels too, when they were received as guests by our fathers, eat, and yet were not corruptible?

**2. 3.** Again they say: Will the flaws that were in the human body, with which a man dies, rise? We answer: The flaws will not rise. And it is said to us: Why, then, did the Lord rise with the scars of his wounds? What do we say to this, except that this too was of power, not of necessity? He willed so to rise, he willed so to show himself to certain doubters. In that flesh the scar of a wound healed the wound of unbelief.

**3. 4.** Still they argue, and ask of us: The little ones who die, will they rise as little ones? or will the age of those coming back to life be full, whose age was small when they died? This indeed we do not find defined in the Scriptures. Incorruptible and immortal bodies are promised to rise. But if the small age is restored, if the tiny stature is recalled, is weakness too therefore recalled? If they will be small, will they lie there, and not be able to walk? Yet it is taken as more credible and more probable and more reasonable that full ages will rise, so that what was going to accrue in time may be rendered by gift. For we are not going to believe that even gasping and bent old age will rise. In short, take away corruption, and add what you will.

**3. 5.** But, you say, how will an earthly body be in heaven? For the philosophers of the gentiles, those very great ones, whose either insane or at least merely human opinions I have already conveyed to you (for they sought these things not by the Spirit of God, but by the conjecture of the human heart); from this most of all they make a question, they treat subtly of the measures of weights and the order of the elements: and they say—what we too see—that the world is so arranged that the earth is lowest as it were at its bottom, the second, water, is poured over the earth, the third, air, comes, the fourth, ether, covers all. That uppermost element, which they call ether, they say is fire, liquid and pure, that from it the stars are formed, that nothing earthly can be there, since the order of weights does not admit it. If we were to say to them that our bodies will live in a new earth, and will not be in heaven, we speak boldly and rashly, indeed unfaithfully. For we ought to believe that we will have such bodies that wherever we wish, whenever we wish, there we will be. For if we answer, to solve the question about the order of weights, that we will live on earth, there arises for us the question about the very body of the Lord, with which he ascended into heaven.

**4. 6.** You heard what just now sounded freshly from the Gospel in our ears: *Lifting up his hands he blessed them. And it came to pass, while he blessed them, he withdrew from them, and was carried up into heaven*[^4]. Who was carried up into heaven? The Lord Christ. Which Lord Christ? The Lord Jesus. What, are you going to separate the man from God, and make one person of God, another of man, so that there is now no longer a Trinity, but a quaternity? As you, a man, are soul and body, so the Lord Christ is Word, soul, and body. But the Word did not withdraw from the Father: and he came to us, and did not abandon the Father; and in the womb he took flesh, and ruled the world. What, then, was lifted up into heaven, except what was taken up from the earth? that is, that flesh, that body, of which, speaking to the disciples, he said: *Handle, and see, that a spirit does not have bones and flesh, as you see me to have*[^5]. Let us believe this, brothers: and if we resolve the arguments of the philosophers with difficulty, let us hold without difficulty of faith what was demonstrated in the Lord. Let them chatter, let us believe.

**5. 7.** But an earthly body, they say, cannot be in heaven. What if God wills it? Answer against God, and say: God cannot. Do you not, whatever pagan you are, say that God is almighty? Is it not read in the book of Plato, as I showed yesterday, that the uncreated God said to the gods made by him: *Since you have come to be, you cannot indeed be immortal and indissoluble; yet you will not be dissolved, nor will any fates of death destroy you; nor will they be stronger than my counsel, which is a greater bond for your perpetuity than those by which you have been bound together?* God brought everything to his own will, who can do even what is impossible. For what else is it to say, You cannot be immortal, but I make it that you do not die; except, even what cannot happen, I make happen?

**6. 8.** Yet I want to argue something also about the diversity of weights. I ask you, tell me, Earth is earth, Water is water, Air is air, Ether, that is heaven, and that liquid fire is heaven. These four, as it were step by step, they constructed and built the world, that is, of these four the world is built. Ask what is at the bottom, it is earth: what above, it is water: what above the water, it is air: what above the air, it is heaven, it is ether. What of solid bodies that are held and handled? I do not mean liquid ones, which slip and flow; I mean handleable bodies—from where are they? Are they to be assigned to earth, or to water, or to air, or to ether? You will answer, To earth. So is wood an earthly body? Plainly earthly. It is born in earth, it is nourished in earth, it grows in earth. It is handleable, not fluid. Return with me to that order of weights. Earth is at the foundation: follow the order. What above the earth? Water. Why does wood float on water? It is an earthly body: if you bring it back to that order of weights, it ought to be under the water, not above. We find water in the middle between earth and wood: earth below, water above, and above the water again earth, since wood is earth. You have lost that order, hold the faith. Therefore earthly bodies are found above the element that is second in the order of elements, when wood floats and is not sunk.

**7. 9.** Attend to something else, at which you may marvel more. The very heaviest bodies, and yet earthly, which, as soon as they are let go upon water, at once sink, and reach the lowest depths, like iron, finally like lead. For what is heavier than lead? Yet the hand of the craftsman comes to the lead, makes from it some hollow vessel, and the lead floats on the water. Will God, then, not give to my body what the craftsman gives to lead? Then, where do you place the water itself? Return to the order of the elements. Surely you will answer that water is above the earth. Why, then, before they run on the earth, do rivers hang in the clouds?

**7. 10.** Recall your consideration and thought from there to the things I am going to say, if with the Lord's help I am able. What is moved more easily, what is driven more swiftly, a heavier body or a lighter one? Who would not answer, A lighter one? For lighter bodies are moved more easily, driven more swiftly: heavier ones with more difficulty and more slowly. Surely you have fixed the rule, surely you have considered, and having looked around at everything, you have answered that lighter bodies are moved more easily and driven more swiftly than heavier ones. It is so, you say. Answer me, then. Why does the very light spider move itself slowly, and the heavy horse run swiftly? Let me speak of men themselves: a larger body of a man is heavier; a shorter body, which has less weight, is lighter. So indeed it is, but only if another carries it. But if the man himself carries his own body, the strong man runs, the thin man with feebleness scarcely walks. Weigh a thin man and a robust one; the one with feebleness scarcely weighing a few small pounds, the other with bodily health carrying many pounds in his flesh: try you to lift both; the strong one is heavy, the emaciated one is light. Let the carrier withdraw, let the walker appear; leave them to themselves, let them move their own bodies; I see the thin one scarcely moving a step, I see the strong and robust one running. If health avails this much, what will immortality avail?

**8. 11.** God, then, will give a wondrous ease, a wondrous lightness. Not without cause are those bodies called spiritual. They are not called spiritual because they will be spirits, not bodies. For these that we now have are called animate bodies: and yet they are not souls, but bodies. As now these are called animate, and are not souls: so those are called spiritual, but are not spirits, because they will be bodies. Why is it called a spiritual body, beloved, except because it will serve at the nod of the spirit? Nothing in you will contradict you, nothing in you will rebel against you. There will not be there what the Apostle laments: *The flesh desires against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh*[^6]. There will not be there: *I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind*[^7]. These wars will not be there: there will be peace, perfect peace will be there. Where you wish, you will be: but you will not withdraw from God. Where you wish, you will be: but wherever you go, you will have your God. In whom you will be blessed, with him you will always be.

**8. 12.** *The promise of the resurrection is confirmed by so many other promises of God already fulfilled.* Let no one, then, deceive, let no one argue, let no one rave with his own suspicion: what God promised us, let us hold most certainly will come. When Christ was being seen, my brothers, when he was thought a spirit, to persuade that he was a body, he offered himself not only to the eyes to be seen, but also to the hands to be handled. To whom, to show the truth of faith in the body, he condescended even to take food, not from necessity, but from power: yet while they still trembled for joy, he applied a strengthening of the heart from the holy Scriptures; and he said to them: *These are the words that I spoke to you, while I was still with you: that all things must be fulfilled which are written in the Law of Moses, and the Prophets, and the Psalms about me. Then he opened their minds*, as the Gospel says, which was just now read, *that they might understand the Scriptures; and he said to them, That so it is written, and so it was fitting for Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day, and that repentance and the forgiveness of sins be preached in his name among all nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^8]. That we did not see, but this we do see. When those things were being promised, they were not yet seen. The apostles saw Christ present: but they did not see the Church spread through the whole world: they saw the head, and believed about the body. We have our turns, we have the grace of our dispensation and distribution: for believing by the most certain documents, the times have been distributed to us in one faith. They saw the head, and believed about the body: we see the body, let us believe about the head.
