# Sermon 319: On Stephen the Martyr

**1. 1.** May the Lord grant me to say a few things healthfully, who granted to holy Stephen to say so many things bravely. He began so to speak to his persecutors, as though he feared them: *Men, brothers and fathers, hear*[^1]. What more gentle? what more clement? He won over the hearer, that he might commend the Savior. He began softly, that he might be heard for a while. And because he had been accused of this, that he had spoken words against God and the Law, he expounded that very Law to them, that he might be the preacher of that Law, of which he was accused of being the destroyer. This we both heard and you heard: many words of ours are not necessary, because you have heard many. This only I exhort, for the building up of Your Charity, that you may know that holy Stephen sought the honor of Christ, that you may know that the holy Martyr was the witness of Christ, that you may know that he did then so great miracles in the name of Christ. For this you healthfully know, that holy Stephen did miracles in the name of Christ, but that the Lord Christ did no miracle in the name of Stephen: that you may distinguish the servant from the Lord, the worshiper from God, the adorer from the one to be adored. For when you distinguish, then he loves you. For he did not shed his blood for himself, but shed it for Christ.

**2. 2.** See to whom he commended his soul. *Behold,* he says, *I see the heavens opened, and the Son of man standing at the right hand of God*[^2]. He saw Christ, confessing Christ, about to die for Christ, about to go on to Christ; and at the last, when the blows of stones grew thick, and hard wounds were hurled from hard hearts, he saw himself near, not to destruction, but to his departure; he saw his soul now about to depart, and commended it. To whom? To him whom he saw, to him whom he worshiped, to him whom he served, to him whose name he preached, to him for whose Gospel he laid down his soul, to him he commended his very soul. *Lord,* he says, *Jesus, receive my spirit*[^3]. You have made me a victor, receive me into the triumph. *Receive my spirit.* They persecute, do you receive: they cast out, do you take in. Say to my spirit: *Enter into the joy of your Lord*[^4]. For this is: *Receive my spirit.*

**2. 3.** But his spirit, into what did Jesus receive it? into what dwelling? into what heaven of heaven? Who comprehends it? who can unfold it.

**3. 3.** Do you want to hear it in brief? Hear Christ himself: *Father, I will that where I am, they also may be with me*[^5]. To be where Christ is, what thought can comprehend it? whose speech suffices to unfold this? Let it be commended to faith, let it not be awaited from the tongue. You heard, when the Gospel was read: *Where I am, there will my minister be also*[^6]. Read the Greek manuscript, and you will find "deacon." For what the Latin rendered "minister," the Greek has "deacon"; because truly "deacon" in Greek is "minister" in Latin: just as "martyr" in Greek is "witness" in Latin; "apostle" in Greek, "one sent" in Latin. But we are now accustomed to use the Greek names for the Latin. For many manuscripts of the Gospels have it thus: *Where I am, there will my deacon be also.* Think this said, because this is said: *Where I am, there will my deacon be also.* So rightly his deacon: *Lord Jesus, receive my spirit*[^7]. You promised that the Gospel would be read, I preached the Gospel: *Where I am, there will my deacon be also.* I stood forth to you as your deacon, I ministered to you my blood, I laid down for you my soul; render to me your promise.

**4. 4.** And for the Jews, for those stoning him, for the bloody hearts, for the cruel souls, how did he pray? He bent the knee. So great the humility of Stephen, so great the guilt of that people. For himself, standing, he asked; for them he bent the knee. Did he prefer them to himself? Far be it: it is not to be believed. He loved his enemies: but of the neighbor it was said: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^8]. Why then did he bend the knee? Because he knew that he was praying for the wicked; and the more malignant they were, the more difficult it was for him to be heard. The Lord hanging on the cross: *Father,* he says, *forgive them*[^9]: Stephen under the stones, with knee bent: *Lord, do not lay this offense to their charge*[^10]. He followed the footsteps of his pastor, like a good sheep: a good lamb followed the Lamb, whose blood took away the sin of the world. He fulfilled what the apostle Peter says: *Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, that we may follow his footsteps*[^11].

**5. 5.** See the man following the footsteps of his Lord. Christ on the cross: *Father, into your hands I commend my spirit*[^12]: Stephen under the stones: *Lord Jesus, receive my spirit*[^13]. Christ on the cross: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^14]: Stephen under the stones: *Lord Jesus, do not lay this offense to their charge*[^15]. How could this man not be there where was the one he followed, where was the one he imitated?

**6. 6.** He triumphed, he was crowned. His body lay hidden for so long a time, it came forth when God willed, it illumined the lands, it did so great miracles, dead it makes the living dead, because he is not even dead. So this I commend to Your Charity, that you may know that his prayers obtain many things, yet not all things. For we find even in the booklets that are presented, that he had difficulties in obtaining things, and yet afterward received the benefit, the faith of the suppliant not failing. There was no ceasing, there was praying, and afterward God gave it through Stephen. There are words of Stephen praying, and it was answered him: She for whom you pray is not worthy, she did this and that. And yet he insisted, asked, received.

**7. 6.** He gave us to understand, that in whose name he did these things before he laid down his flesh, in his name his prayers do them, that benefits may be obtained for those to whom he knows they ought to be given.

**7. 7.** But he prays as a servant. A certain angel was being worshiped along with John. The angels are such before God, that if we are good, and have perfectly merited him, we will be made equal to the angels: *They will be,* he said, *equal to the angels of God*[^16]. The angel was showing many miracles to holy John the Evangelist; troubled by the miracles, he adored him. The man adored the angel; and the angel to the man: *Rise, what are you doing? Adore him: for I am the fellow servant of you and of your brothers*[^17].

**8. 7.** If so great humility appeared in an angel, how great do you think it ought to be in a martyr, as it is? Let us not, then, believe Stephen to be proud, when we think that he does what he does by his own power. Through the fellow servant let us receive benefits, to the Lord let us give honor and glory. What more shall I say to you, and speak at length? Read the four verses which we have written in the chamber, read them, hold them, have them in heart. For we wished to write them there for this reason, that whoever wishes may read them, may read them when he wishes. That all may hold them, therefore they are few: that all may read them, therefore they are written publicly. There is no need to seek a manuscript: let that vault be your manuscript. We have indeed come forth somewhat earlier than usual: but because a long reading was recited, and the heat is heavy, let us defer to the Lord's day the booklet of God's benefits through him, which we were to read today.
