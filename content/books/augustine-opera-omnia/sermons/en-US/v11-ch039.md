# (Erfurt 6 = Sermon 313/G) (Erfurt 6)

**1** I give thanks to the Lord our God because, that I might keep this day with you, he deigned to grant it both to me and to you. We are celebrating the solemnities of a most renowned martyr in a most devoted gathering. The ornament of confession, the ruler of the Africans and teacher of the Church, a most true and most sincere martyr, both instructor and ruler, teaching what he was going to do, doing what he had taught, sending many before himself by his precept, he drew many after himself by his example. But who wrought this in him, except he who made him? For God made him both man and faithful and martyr: man when he created him, faithful when he called him, martyr when he crowned him. Such martyrs we venerate without scruple; for the sufferings are renowned and clear to all which they bore for the Lord. This blessed Cyprian too was sent as a sheep into the midst of wolves; by his word he chastised the wolves, and as a shepherd he answered for the sheep and poured out his blood for the sheep. He held the simplicity of the dove and the cunning of the serpent: by the simplicity of the dove he harmed no one, by the cunning of the serpent he guarded his own head. When serpents are struck, they set all the coils of their members against the blow in defense of the head and guard with great zeal where they have life. How did holy Cyprian guard his head? *The head of a man is Christ*[^1]; he guarded his head, then, when, setting his body against the persecutors, he did not deny Christ.

**2** . . . let us celebrate the sincerity of our devotion eager and joyful; let this suffice us, brethren! Let us not persecute with cups the martyrs whom the pagans persecuted with stones! By God's favor I see this place full of the eagerness of your gathering. Are we not even now eating—do not question your bellies, but your minds!—are we not even now drinking? Our food and drink ought to be that of the inner man. For the outer man, when burdened, weighs down the inner; whence the Lord says, *Let not your hearts be weighed down with carousing and drunkenness*[^2], because by drunkenness bodies are filled and hearts are weighed down. How greatly we ought to avoid this, when we celebrate the birthdays of the martyrs! For in order that these martyrs might attain to those crowns, they despised such things as are now longed for and celebrated, when men set about to celebrate their solemnities. For if we love the martyrs, let us follow in the martyrs' footsteps! Imitate the one you love: this is the fruit of love, the footprint of imitation.

[^1]: See 1 Cor 11:3
[^2]: See Luke 21:34
