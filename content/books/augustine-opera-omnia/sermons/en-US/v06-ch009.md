# Sermon 192: On the Birthday of the Lord

**1.** Today *truth has sprung up from the earth*[^1], Christ was born of flesh. Rejoice solemnly, and, admonished also by today, think on the everlasting day; with firmest hope long for the eternal gifts; presume, with the power you have received, to be *children of God*[^2]. For your sake the maker of times was made subject to time, for your sake the Founder of the world appeared in the flesh, for your sake the Creator was created. Why do you still, being mortal, take delight in mortal things, and try, if it could be done, to hold onto a fleeting life? A far brighter hope has shone forth on earth, that to those of earth life might be promised in heaven. That this might be believed, a more incredible thing was bestowed beforehand. He who was God was made man, that he might make gods of those who were men: not losing what he was, he himself willed to become what he had made. He himself made what he should be, because he added man to God, he did not lose God in man. We marvel at the virgin's childbearing, and we try to persuade the unbelieving of the new manner itself of being born: that in an unsown womb the seed of offspring arose, and that bowels free from the embrace of flesh brought forth the son of a man, whose father they did not bear a man; that the integrity of virginity remained both closed in conception and uncorrupted in the birth. Wondrous is this power, but more to be wondered at is the mercy, that he who could be born thus willed to be born. For he who was born the only Son to the mother was already the only Son to the Father: and he himself was made in the mother who had made for himself the mother; everlasting with the Father, of today from the mother: after the mother, made of the mother, before all things, of the Father not made: without whom the Father never was, without whom the mother never would have been.

**2.** Rejoice, virgins of Christ, the mother of Christ is your companion. You could not bear Christ, but for the sake of Christ you chose not to bear. He who was not born of you was born for you. Yet if you remember his word, as you ought to remember, you too are his mothers, because you do the will of his Father. For he himself said: *Whoever does the will of my Father, he is my brother and sister and mother*[^3]. Rejoice, widows of Christ: to him who made virginity fruitful you have vowed the holiness of continence. Rejoice also, you chastity of marriage, all you who live faithfully with your spouses: what you have lost in the body, preserve in the heart. Where now the flesh cannot be intact from intercourse, let the conscience be a virgin in faith, according to which the whole Church is a virgin. In Mary holy virginity bore Christ: in Anna widowhood, aged, recognized the little Christ: in Elizabeth marital chastity and aged fruitfulness served Christ. All the ranks of the faithful members conferred on the Head what by his grace they were able to confer. Therefore, because Christ is truth and peace and righteousness, conceive him by faith, bring him forth by works; that what the womb of Mary did in the flesh of Christ, your heart may do in the law of Christ. But how do you not belong to the Virgin's childbearing, when you are the members of Christ? Mary bore your Head, the Church bore you. For she too is both mother and virgin: a mother in the bowels of love, a virgin in the integrity of faith and piety. She bears peoples, but they are members of one, whose body and spouse she herself is, bearing in this too a likeness to that Virgin, because in the many she also is the mother of unity.

**3.** And so let us all, of one mind, with chaste minds and holy affections, celebrate the day of the Lord's birth: on which day, as we began this sermon, *truth has sprung up from the earth*[^4]. For now what follows in the same psalm has also come to pass. For he who sprang up from the earth, that is, was born of flesh, since he came from heaven and is above all[^5], without doubt, when he ascended to the Father, *righteousness also looked down from heaven*. For this righteousness he himself commends by his words, in promising the Holy Spirit: *He*, he says, *will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment. Of sin, because they did not believe in me: of righteousness, because I go to the Father; and you will see me no longer*[^6]. This is the righteousness that looked down from heaven. For *his going forth is from the height of heaven, and his circuit even to its height*[^7]. But lest anyone despise truth because it sprang up from the earth, when, like a bridegroom, he *came forth from his chamber*[^8], that is, from the virginal womb, where the Word of God was joined to the human creature by a certain ineffable union: lest, then, anyone should despise this, and, though born wonderfully and wonderful in words and deeds, should nonetheless, on account of the likeness of sinful flesh, believe Christ to be nothing more than a man: when it had been said, *Like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber, he rejoiced like a giant to run his course*; it was at once subjoined, *His going forth is from the height of heaven*. What you hear, then, *truth has sprung up from the earth*, is condescension, not condition; mercy, not misery. That truth might spring up from the earth, it descended from heaven: that the bridegroom might come forth from his chamber, his going forth is from the height of heaven. Hence it is that he was born today, on the day on which no day is the least in the earth, from which, however, the days take their increase. He, then, who was bowed down and lifted us up chose the smallest day, but the day from which the light grows: by his very coming thus, exhorting us also silently, as by the sound of a great cry, that we who became poor for his sake[^9] may learn to be rich in him; that we for whose sake he took *the form of a servant*[^10] may receive liberty in him; that we for whose sake he sprang up from the earth may possess heaven in him.
