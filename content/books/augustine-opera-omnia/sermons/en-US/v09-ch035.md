# Sermon 325: On the Feast of the Twenty Martyrs

**1.** On the solemn day of the holy Martyrs the sermon owed is to be rendered. As we are about to speak of the glory of the martyrs, briefly to speak of the just cause of the martyrs, let the prayers of the martyrs help us. For this Your Holiness ought to recall through these solemnities, first, lest we think that we confer anything on the martyrs, because we celebrate their most solemn days. They do not need our festivals, because in heaven they rejoice with the angels: but they rejoice with us, not if we honor them, but if we imitate them. Although even that we honor them profits us, not them. But to honor and not to imitate is nothing other than to flatter mendaciously. For this, then, these festivals are established in the Church of Christ, that through them the congregation of the members of Christ may be admonished to imitate the martyrs of Christ. This is altogether the usefulness of this festival, there is no other. For if God is set before us to be imitated, human frailty answers, that it is too much for it to imitate him to whom it cannot be compared. If next the example of our Lord Jesus Christ himself is set before us for imitation, who for this reason, though he was God, was clothed with mortal flesh, that he might impart a precept to men bearing mortal flesh, and show an example; of whom too it is written: *Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, that we may follow his footsteps*[^1]: yet here too human frailty still answers, What likeness have I and Christ? He, though flesh, yet Word made flesh. *For the Word became flesh, that it might dwell among us*[^2]: he took up flesh, he did not lose the Word; he received what he was not, he did not lose what he was. *For God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself*[^3]. What likeness, then, have I and Christ? To take away, then, all the excuses of unbelieving weakness, the martyrs built us a paved road. For it had to be built with stone slabs, on which we might walk securely. They made it by their blood, by their confessions. Finally, despising their bodies, to Christ coming to win the nations, sitting as it were on that beast of burden, they spread their bodies like garments. Who is there who is ashamed to say, I am unequal to God? Plainly unequal. Am I unequal to Christ? Even to the mortal Christ I am unequal. Peter was what you are, Paul was what you are, the Apostles and Prophets were what you are. If you are loath to imitate the Lord, imitate the fellow servant. The throng of servants has gone before, the excuse of the lazy is taken away. Lastly he still says: I am unequal to Peter, unequal to Paul. Are you unequal to the truth? Rusticity is crowned, vanity is not excused. Lastly, are you unequal to boys? are you unequal to girls? are you unequal to holy Valeriana? If you are still loath to follow, do you not want to cling to Victoria? For so the series of the holy twenty Martyrs was recited to us. It began with the bishop Fidentius, and closed with the faithful woman holy Victoria. The beginning from faith, the end at victory.

**2.** See, then, brothers: so celebrate the passions of the martyrs, that you think of imitating the martyrs. They, that they might have a fruitful punishment, chose the cause. For they attended to the Lord saying, not *Blessed are those who suffer persecution;* but: *Blessed are those who suffer persecution for the sake of righteousness*[^4]. Choose the cause, and do not care about the punishment. But if you do not choose the cause, both here and in the future you will find punishment. Let not the punishments and penalties of evildoers, of the sacrilegious, of the enemies of peace, and of the enemies of truth move you. For they do not die for the truth: but they die for this, that the truth may not be announced, that the truth may not be preached, that the truth may not be held, that unity may not be loved, that love may not be cherished, that eternity may not be held. O worst cause! therefore a fruitless punishment. Do you not attend, you who boast of your punishment, that there were three crosses, when the Lord suffered? Between two thieves the Lord suffered: the punishment did not distinguish, but the cause distinguished. So the voice of the martyrs is in that Psalm: *Judge me, O God*[^5]. He does not fear the judgment: for he has nothing that the fire may consume in him; where all is gold, why is the flame dreaded? *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^6]. Surely he did not say: Distinguish my punishment? It would be said to him, The thief suffered punishment. Surely he did not say: Distinguish my cross? There the adulterer too was shut up. Surely he did not say: Distinguish my chain? There thieves too are bound. Surely he did not say: Distinguish my wound? By the sword the wicked too were slain. Since, then, he saw all things common to good and bad in their sufferings, he cried out, and said: *Judge me, O Lord, and distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy.* If you distinguish my cause, you crown my patience. Let these things suffice for Your Charity, by way of exhortation, in this holy place; since the days are short, and there still remain for us in the greater basilica things to do with Your Charity.

[^1]: 1 Pet 2:21
[^2]: John 1:14
[^3]: 2 Cor 5:19
[^4]: Matt 5:10
[^5]: Ps 43:1 (42:1)
[^6]: Ps 43:1 (42:1)
