# Sermon 229/M: A Tractate on the Friday of Easter

**1.** Your Charity knows that these readings of the holy Gospel, witnesses of the Lord's resurrection, are celebrated at the yearly solemnity. Just as, then, the reading renews the memory, so the exposition of the reading renews the memory; and so, with the Lord's help, we are going to say what you are accustomed to hear every year. But if the reading must be renewed in memory, which can be read at another time too in the Gospel, how much more the sermon, which is heard only once a year? The Lord appeared to his disciples after his resurrection at the sea of Tiberias, and found them fishing, catching nothing. Fishing by night they caught nothing; the day dawned, and they caught; because they saw Christ as the day, and at the word of the Lord they cast their nets, that they might catch. Now we find two catches of fish made by the disciples of Christ, at the word of Christ; the first, when he chose them, and made them apostles; the other now, after he rose from the dead. Let us compare them, if you please, and carefully attend to what difference there is between them; for it pertains to the building up of our faith. Earlier, then, when the Lord found fishermen, whom he had not yet found, even through the whole night they caught nothing, they labored in vain. He bade them cast their nets: he did not say, To the right; he did not say, To the left; but: *Cast,* he says, *your nets*[^1]. They cast them . . . so that they loaded two boats, so that they were almost sunk by the multitude of fish; and then there was so great a multitude of fish that the nets were breaking[^2]. So that catch went; what, then, of this one? *Cast,* he says, *the nets on the right side*[^3]. Before the resurrection the nets are cast indiscriminately; after the resurrection the right side is chosen. Then, in the first catch the boats are weighed down, the nets are broken; in this last one, after the resurrection, neither is the boat weighed down, nor is the net broken. In the first catch no number of fish is given; in this one, after the resurrection, a fixed number of fish is given. Let us bear with the first, then, that we may come to the last. What did I say, Let us bear with the first? Behold the nets, the nets of the word, the nets of preaching, behold the nets. Let the psalm say: *I announced and spoke; they were multiplied beyond number*[^4]. It is certain, because it is happening now: the Gospel is announced, Christians are multiplied beyond number. If all lived well, they would not weigh down the boat; if heresies and schisms were not dividing, the net would not be broken. But why were there two boats in the first catch? Recall, brothers: they are those two walls, the circumcision and the uncircumcision, which meet there in the cornerstone, and find the kiss of peace[^5]. But in the last catch is perfect unity; for it is the right side, it has nothing on the left. This is the holy Church, which now is in the few who labor among the many evil; this same Church will be in that fixed and definite number, where no sinner any longer is found; for it is the right side, it has nothing on the left. And the fish will be great: because all will be immortal, all will live without end. What is so great as that which has no end? And it pertained to the Evangelist that you should renew in memory the first catch. For why did he add: *And though the fish were so great, the nets were not torn*[^6]? As though to say: Recall that first catch, where the nets were torn. Here will be the kingdom of heaven, no heretic will bark, no schismatic will set himself apart; all will be within, will be in peace.

**2.** And all, how many will they be? A hundred and fifty-three? Far, far be it from us to say that, even in this people who stand here before me, there will be so few who will be in the kingdom of heaven, where there are thousands, countless thousands, whom John saw clothed in white robes. This he said: *They came from every tribe and tongue, a number that no one can number*[^7]. What, then, does this number mean? I am teaching some, reminding others; let those who have not heard learn; let those who have heard and forgotten recall; let those who hold in memory what they heard be confirmed by my reminding. The reckoning of this number begins from seventeen: it is the seal of all the saints, of all the faithful, of all the just who are to be in the kingdom of heaven. Seventeen: ten because of the law, seven because of the spirit of grace. Impose the law: no one fulfills it, no one keeps it. Add the help of the Spirit: what is commanded is done, because God helps. What does the Law say? *You shall not covet.* *Sin, taking occasion through the commandment, deceived me,* he says, *and through it killed me; and the Law entered in, that offense might abound*[^8]. Add the Spirit: *The fullness of the Law is love*[^9]. But whence comes love? *The love of God has been poured forth in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^10]. That the Spirit is commended by the number seven, he knows who reads: but let him hear who reads carelessly, or perhaps who cannot read. God commends the Holy Spirit thus through the prophet Isaiah: *The Spirit,* he says, *of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and fortitude, of knowledge and piety, the spirit of the fear of God*[^11]. This is the sevenfold Spirit, who is also invoked over the baptized. The Law is the Decalogue: for ten precepts were written on tablets, but still on stone ones, because of the hardness of the Jews. After the Spirit came, what does the Apostle say? *You are our letter, written not with ink, but with the Spirit of the living God; not on tablets of stone, but on the fleshly tablets of the heart*[^12]. Take away the Spirit, the letter kills; for it makes guilty, it does not free the sinner. On account of this the Apostle says: *For we are not,* he says, *sufficient to think anything of ourselves as from ourselves; but our sufficiency is from God, who has also made us fit ministers of the New Testament, not of the letter, but of the spirit. For the letter kills, but the Spirit gives life*[^13]. Join, then, the seven to the ten, if you wish to fulfill righteousness. When you are bidden to do something through the law, ask the Spirit to help.

**3.** We have commended the seventeen: but how far they are from a hundred and fifty-three! Yet many know what we are going to say: let those who know bear with the delays. When two walk on the road, one swift and one slow, it is in the power of the swift one that they walk together, if he waits for the other. So he who knows what I am going to say is the swift one; but let him wait for his slow companion, until he too is instructed about the seventeen. They become a hundred and fifty-three, if those very seventeen are counted in such a way that you begin from one, and add them all, until you arrive at seventeen. For if you begin to say: One, two, three, four, you arrive at ten, and you will find nothing in your hand but ten. But if you count thus: one and two are now three; and three, now six; and four, now ten; and five, now fifteen; and six, now twenty-one; and seven, now twenty-eight; and eight, now thirty-six; and nine, now forty-five; and ten, now fifty-five. Behold, now we are near, and there is hope of arriving, because we have gone so far. Add, then, eleven, it makes sixty-six; add twelve, it makes seventy-eight; add thirteen, it makes ninety-one; add also fourteen, it makes a hundred and five. Now the slow follow the swift. Add, then, also fifteen, it makes a hundred and twenty; add sixteen, it makes a hundred and thirty-six; add seventeen, it makes a hundred and fifty-three. There, then, will be all who run to seventeen, who keep the law of God, with the help of the Spirit of God.
