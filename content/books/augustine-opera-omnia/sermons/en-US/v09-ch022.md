# Sermon 313/H: On the Day of Saint Eulalia

**1.** On the solemnities of the martyrs holy readings suited to them are recited, which commend to us that their victories were foretold beforehand, and were fulfilled through the help of him who had promised these things. *If the world hates you,* says the Lord, *know that it hated me first. If you were of the world, the world would love what is its own*[^1]. Of this world the Lord Christ never was, because he was not a sinner. But his disciples were of the world: but, that they might not be of the world, he chose them out of the world, who never was of the world. For openly he said this to them in another place: *I have chosen you out of the world*[^2]. I chose what I made, not what I found. For whose merits did he find but evil ones, when he came? But those whom he found evil, he made good. He made the redeemed out of captives, servants out of the redeemed, brothers out of servants, members out of brothers.

**2.** Since, then, they had been made his members, what were they to suffer, except what he himself suffered? By his own example he showed what they ought to do. Do we think it was said to the disciples alone, *the world hates you*[^3], or to all Christians? But now all Christians are the world itself, that is, the whole world. What, then, has remained of the world, to hate us? Yet whatever has remained hates us. Whoever are pagans, without doubt hate Christians. We do not hate them, but seek them. Whoever are reprobate Jews, like the chaff of that threshing-floor which was winnowed by the cross, they too hate us. Whatever there is in the human race of heretics, which has gone out of the Church, they all hate us. And however many and however great they are, if the remnants are counted against us, so they hate us. How the whole world rages! Behold, those who suffered as martyrs, suffered before the whole world. Who were they, to endure the great roaring lion, unless Christ helped? Wherever they went, wherever they passed, they were cursed, seized, stoned, struck, burned with fire, exposed to beasts, smitten with the sword. Behold, those against whom there was raging are crowned; those who raged, where are they?

**3.** But that which the Lord says: *The hour will come, when whoever kills you will think he is rendering service to God*[^4], does not pertain to those martyrs about whom this is whose solemnity we celebrate. For this holy Eulalia, of the province of Spain, a holy and strong woman, who by her affection overcame her sex, like holy Crispina, like blessed Cyprian, like many other holy martyrs, like the Eight, like the Twenty, and all their companions, believed out of the multitude of the gentiles, and were killed by the pagans. How then can it be said, that there was fulfilled in them what the Lord says: *The hour will come, when whoever kills you will think he is rendering service to God?* They are not the ones of whom it was said; because those who killed them thought they were rendering service not to God, but to idols. So they are killed, that they may not perish: they are humbled, that they may be exalted; they die, that they may live. So it was done. Therefore after the fragrance of the ointments the rest too ran, the Twenty, and the Eight, some this way, some that: Cyprian, Crispina, Eulalia. And who could number them all? A few grains were sown, and made so great a harvest, and filled the granaries of Christ.

[^1]: John 15:18-19
[^2]: John 15:19
[^3]: John 15:18
[^4]: John 16:2
