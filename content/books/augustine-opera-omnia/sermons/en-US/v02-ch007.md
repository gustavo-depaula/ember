# Sermon 23/A

**1** We are happy if we also do what we hear and sing. For our hearing is a sowing, but our work is the fruit of the seed. A field in which wheat is sown and which produces thorns ought to expect not the barn but the flame. So too those who hear good things and do evil should not expect for themselves the barn of the kingdom of heaven, but that fire of which it is said: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^1]. Having said this by way of preface, I would warn your Charity not to enter the church fruitlessly, hearing such great goods and not doing good. But according to the goodness of the sower and of the seed of the word of God, so in your habits and in your life, as in good soil, let the most abundant fruit of good habits arise, and may you hope that the farmer will come, preparing for you a barn into which you may be put. See, we have sung: *We will confess to you, O God, we will confess and call upon your name*[^2]. To confess to God, what is it but to be humbled before God, not to claim any merits for oneself? Since *by his grace we have been saved*, as the Apostle says, *not by works, lest perhaps anyone be exalted; for by his grace we have been saved*[^3]. For no good life went before, which he should love and cherish from above, and say, "Let us help, let us come to the aid of these men, because they live well." Our life displeased him, all that we were doing in ourselves displeased him, but what he himself did in us did not displease him. And so what we did he will condemn, what he himself did he will save. He will condemn the evil deeds of men, and will save the men themselves. For the men themselves did not make themselves, but made evil deeds. What God made in them—because God made man to his own image and likeness—is good. But what man himself, by free choice turned away from his founder and creator and turned toward wickedness, did badly, this God condemns, that he may free the man; that is, God condemns what man made, and God frees what God himself made.

**2** We were not good, then. And he had mercy on us, and sent his Son to die, not for the good but for the evil, not for the just but for the impious. For indeed *Christ died for the impious*[^4]. And what follows? *For scarcely will anyone die for a just man, for perhaps someone might dare to die for a good man*[^5]. Perhaps someone is found who would dare to die for a good man. But who would be willing to die for an unjust man, for an impious man, for an iniquitous man, except Christ alone, so just that he might justify the unjust? We had, then, my brothers, no good works, but all evil ones. Since, then, the deeds of men were such, his mercy did not desert men. And though they were worthy of punishment, he, in place of the punishment that was owed, gave the grace that was not owed. And he sent his Son to redeem us, not with gold, not with silver, but with the price of his own poured-out blood, the spotless lamb led to the sacrifice for the spotted sheep—if even spotted, and not wholly corrupted. We have received, then, this grace. Let us live worthily of the very grace we have received, lest we do injury to so great a grace. So great a physician came to us, he forgave all our sins. If we want to be sick again, we will be not only ruinous to ourselves, but also ungrateful to the physician.

**3** Let us, then, follow his ways which he showed us, especially the way of humility, which he himself became for us. For he showed us the way of humility by precepts, and made it by suffering for us. For he would not suffer unless he humbled himself. Who would kill God, unless God humbled himself? For Christ is the Son of God, and the Son of God is surely God. He is the Son of God, the Word of God, of whom John says: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^6]. Who would kill the one through whom all things were made, and without whom nothing was made? Who could kill him, unless he humbled himself? But how did he humble himself? John himself says: *The Word became flesh and dwelt among us*[^7]. For the Word of God could not be killed. That what could not die might therefore die for us, *the Word became flesh and dwelt among us*[^8]. The immortal one took up mortality, that he might die for us, and by his death kill our death. This the Lord did, this he bestowed on us. The great one was humbled, humbled he was killed, killed he both rose and was exalted, that he might not leave us, dead, in the underworld, but exalt us in himself in the resurrection of the dead, whom he has now exalted in the faith and confession of the just.

**4** Therefore he gave us humility as our way. If we hold to it, we will confess to the Lord, and not without cause do we sing: *We will confess to you, O God, we will confess and call upon your name*[^9]. For you shamelessly call upon his name, to whom you do not confess. First confess, that you may prepare a dwelling for the one you call upon. For your heart is full of wickedness. Confession pours out the filth you carry within, and cleanses the house into which he may come whom you call upon. For whoever calls upon him before he confesses wishes to do injury to the one he calls upon. For if you do not dare invite some holy man into your house unless you have first cleansed your house, lest his eyes suffer injury, do you dare to call upon the name of God into your heart full of wickedness, unless you have first poured out by confession the iniquity within? Therefore confession, my brothers, humbles us, and the humbled it justifies, and the justified it exalts. For if we are proud, God resists us; if we are humble, God exalts us; since *he resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble*[^10], and: *He who exalts himself will be humbled, but he who humbles himself will be exalted*[^11]. Let us turn to the Lord.
