# Sermon 240: In the Days of Easter, on the Resurrection of Bodies, Against the Gentiles

**1.** During these days, as your Charity recalls, the Gospel readings relating to the resurrection of the Lord are solemnly read. For all four Evangelists could be silent neither about his passion nor about his resurrection. Now, because the Lord Jesus did many things, they did not all write everything down: but one wrote these things, another those; yet with the greatest harmony of truth. The Evangelist John also relates that many things were done by the Lord Jesus Christ that were written down by none of them. As many things were done as then needed to be done; as many were written as now needed to be read. But to show that all four Evangelists, in what they all say together and do not leave out—that is, about either the passion or the resurrection of Christ—did not say things contrary to one another, is a very laborious task. For some have thought them to be contrary to one another, when they themselves were contrary to their own soul. And therefore care has been taken by those who were able, with the Lord's help, to show that they are not contrary to one another. But, as I said, if I were to show this to you, and wished to discuss these things among the people, the multitude of hearers would be overwhelmed by tedium before the knowledge of the truth was revealed. But I know your faith, that is, the faith of this whole multitude, and of those who are not here today, and yet are faithful; I know their faith to be so certain about the truth of the Evangelists that they have no need of my exposition. He who knows how to defend these things is more learned, not more faithful. He has faith, he has the ability to defend the faith. Another does not have the ability and resource and learning to defend the faith, but he has the faith itself. But he who knows how to defend the faith is necessary for those who falter, not for those who believe. For in the defense of the faith the wounds of doubt or unbelief are cured. He, then, who defends the faith is a good physician: but in you there is no disease of unbelief. When does he know how to cure what you do not have? He knows how to apply the medicine, but in you there is no fault. *The healthy do not need a physician, but those who are ill*[^1].

**2.** *The resurrection is proved against unbelievers from the omnipotence of God.* Nevertheless, it is not my counsel to keep silent toward you about what can be said more readily for the occasion, and heard more conveniently. About the resurrection itself, the example of which the Lord sent ahead in himself, that we might know what we ought to hope for in our bodies too at the end of the age, many people argue many things; some faithfully, some unfaithfully. Those who argue faithfully wish to know more diligently what they should answer unbelievers: but those who argue unfaithfully reason against their own souls, by arguing against the power of the Almighty, saying, How can it happen that a dead man rises? I say, It is God who does it, and you say, It cannot happen? I do not say, Give me a Christian, give me a Jew: but, give me a pagan, a worshiper of idols, a slave of demons, who does not say that God is almighty. He can deny Christ, he cannot deny that God is almighty. Whom you, then, believe—I speak as it were to a pagan—whom you believe to be almighty God, that same one I say is the raiser of the dead. If you say, It cannot happen, you detract from the Almighty. But if you believe him almighty, why do you reject me when I say these things?

**3.** If we were to say that the flesh will rise to hunger, to thirst, to be sick, to toil; to be subject to corruptions; you would rightly not have to believe. For this flesh now has these necessities or calamities. And from where is this? The cause is sin. In one we all sinned, and we are all born to corruption. The cause of all our evils is sin. For it is not without cause that men suffer these evils. God is just, God is almighty: we would in no way suffer these things, if we did not deserve them. But while we were in the punishments to which we came from our sins, our Lord Jesus Christ willed to be in our punishments without sins of his own. By enduring punishment without fault, he loosed both the fault and the punishment. He loosed the fault by forgiving sins: he loosed the punishment by rising from the dead. This he promised, and he willed us to walk in hope: let us persevere, and we will reach the reality. The flesh will rise incorruptible, the flesh will rise without flaw, without deformity, without mortality, without burden, without weight. What now causes you torment, will afterward be your adornment. Therefore, if it is good to have an incorruptible body, why do we want to despair that God will do this?

**4.** The philosophers of this world, who were great and learned, and better than the rest, held that the human soul is immortal: nor did they merely hold it, but defended it with as many arguments as they could, and left their very defenses written down for posterity. There are books, they are read. I said that these philosophers were better than others in comparison with worse ones; because there were philosophers who said to man that, when he was dead, no life remained afterward. To such people the former are surely to be preferred. And in the respect in which they were better—although in many things straying from the truth—yet in the respect in which they were superior, they had come near to the truth. These, then, who held and said that human souls are immortal, sought the causes of the evils of men, of the troubles and errors of mortals, as far as men could; and they said, as they could, that certain sins, I know not what, had gone before in another life, on account of which sins these bodies deserved, as a prison for the soul. Then it was asked of them, what afterward, when a man is dead, what will he be. And here they wore out their wits; and they labored, as far as they could, to give an account to men, either to themselves or to others: and they said that the souls of men who live badly, unclean with the worst conduct, when they have departed from their bodies, are at once turned again into other bodies, and pay here the penalties that we see; but those souls that lived well, when they have departed from their bodies, go to the upper regions of the heavens, rest there in the stars and these conspicuous lights, or in whatever heavenly and hidden recesses, forget all past evils, and again take delight in returning to bodies, and come again to suffer these things. This, then, they wished to be the difference between the souls of sinners and the souls of the just: that the souls of sinners, soon, at once, when they depart from their bodies, they say, are turned again into other bodies; but the souls of the just are long in rest; not always, however, but again they take delight in bodies, and from the highest heavens, after so great a righteousness, make a downfall to these evils.

**5.** This very great philosophers said. Beyond this the philosophers of this world could find nothing, of whom our Scripture says: *God has made foolish the wisdom of this world*[^2]. If the wisdom, how much more the folly? If the wisdom of the world is folly with God? Yet there is a certain folly of this world that reaches to God, of which the Apostle says: *Since in the wisdom of God the world did not know God through wisdom, it pleased God through the folly of preaching to save those who believe.* And he says: *Since the Jews ask for signs, and the Greeks seek wisdom: but we preach Christ crucified; to the Jews indeed a stumbling block, to the gentiles folly; but to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the power of God and the wisdom of God*[^3]. The Lord Christ came, the wisdom of God: heaven thunders: let the frogs be silent. What truth said, this is true. What it said—that the human race is indeed in evil because of sin—is manifest. But whoever believes in the Mediator, who has been set in the middle between God and men (between the just God and unjust men, a just man in the middle, having his humanity from below, his righteousness from above; and therefore in the middle: one thing from here, one from there: because if both were from there, he would be there; if both were from here, he would lie with us, and would not be in the middle): whoever, then, believes in the Mediator, and lives faithfully and well, will indeed depart from the body, and will be in rest; but afterward he will receive the body, not for torment, but for adornment, and will live with God forever. There is no reason for it to delight him to return: because he has his body with him. Therefore, beloved, since I have set before you today what even the philosophers of this world say, whose wisdom God reproved as if it were true folly, tomorrow, with the Lord's help, we will be able to expound.

[^1]: Matt 9:12
[^2]: 1 Cor 1:20
[^3]: 1 Cor 1:21-24
