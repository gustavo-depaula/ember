# Sermon 260/C: On the Lord's Day of the Octave of Holy Easter

**1.** I do not doubt that it is neither new nor unheard of, but manifest to your faith, that as we are born of our parents carnally, so we are born of God the Father and the Church our mother spiritually. Yet the same Lord God is both our creator from those parents, and our re-creator from himself and the Church. In that generation the bond of sin is drawn, in this it is loosed. There we are begotten, that we may succeed parents who are to die; here, that we may cling to those who so remain. And so, if the sons of men, who precede by being born, rejoice with genuine love at the brothers being born in the house, rather rejoicing over partners of light from the same blood than envying sharers of the inheritance; how much more and more sincerely ought we to rejoice together, when the same sons of men, reborn through the grace of holy baptism, become sons of their Creator, since we are born to that inheritance which is both whole for all its possessors, and entire for each? *The Lord*, he says, *is the portion of my inheritance*[^1]. If, then, as the prophet David says, our inheritance is God himself, and, as the apostle John says: *God is love*[^2]; and as the apostle Paul says: *Love is not jealous*[^3]; the more partners and companions we see born to us to obtain such an inheritance, the more with abundant love we rejoice, to whom that very love is set forth to be possessed. For where the inheritance itself is love, he shows that he is not an heir, who does not love the coheir. And so the things which, with the Lord's help, we are going to say to those whose eighth day of baptism is being completed today, embrace the more gladly, the more you exult that they are joined to you by new kinship; that the catechumens too, whom mother Church has already conceived by a certain sacred sign, may with desire of the new light press her womb, and hasten to be perfected and born.

**2.** The solemnity especially of these very octaves, which throughout the whole world has wholesomely subjected the nations everywhere to the name of Christ, is most devoutly celebrated by all who are reborn through his baptism. What, then, it means, and what is the reason of so great a mystery, let us try, with the Lord's help, to recall in a few words. Let your Christian learning consider with me whether it agrees with the rules of our faith. For who does not know that the earth was once cleansed from iniquities by the flood, and that the sacred sign of holy baptism, by which through water all the sins of a man are blotted out, was even then foretold, where the ark, fashioned of unrotting wood, by which the Church was figured, contained only eight men? What, then, the number eight of men attests in the waters of the flood, by which sins were extinguished, this the number eight of days attests in the waters of baptism, by which sins are blotted out. For deeds that signify something are compared to the sounds of our mouth; as, then, one and the same thing can be said by manifold words and tongues, so one and the same thing is wont to be signified not only by voices but also by figurative deeds, many and various, without any alteration of itself. For which reason, not because there are eight men there, but here eight days, is one thing and another announced, but the same thing in one way and another, by the difference of signs, as by the diversity of letters.

**3.** By the number eight, then, are prefigured the things that pertain to the age to come, where by no rolling of times anything either fails or makes progress, but it perseveres continually in stable blessedness. And since the times of this age slip away by the number seven of days repeated in a circuit, that is rightly called as it were the eighth day, on which, when the saints have arrived after the temporal labors, they distinguish action or rest by no alternation of light and night; but for them there will be a perpetually waking rest, and an action not slothfully but tirelessly at leisure. But as for the saints, when the time of seven has passed, there is the eighth and eternal happiness, so for the impious, the same sevenfold revolution being completed, there is the eighth, the judgment of punishment. From which, desiring to be freed, that man in the sixth Psalm, which is inscribed with the title *for the eighth*, groaning alleges his weakness, and says: *Lord, do not rebuke me in your indignation, nor chastise me in your anger*[^4]. Again in the eleventh Psalm, where likewise the inscription is *for the eighth*, it is shown that for the very reward of eternal life all the adversities of this age are to be endured; lest, when iniquity begins to abound, love grow cold, and he who has not persevered to the end cannot be saved. For when anyone has wished to rest in other men, and in many, in whom he least thought it, has found falsehoods and deceits and empty and proud boasting, it is good for him to look to the eighth and everlasting day, where his secure gladness will be wounded by the fellowship of no wicked one, and to say, groaning here and praying with tears: *Save me, Lord, for the holy one has failed, for the truths are diminished from the sons of men*[^5]; for so the Psalm itself begins. But let him pray in sure hope according to the last words of the same Psalm; for so it ends: *You, Lord, will preserve us*, he says, *and will guard us from this generation forever*[^6]; as it were beginning from the seventh, and leading on to the eighth, *from glory to glory as by the Spirit of the Lord*[^7].

**4.** For what is it that elsewhere too he promises through the prophet *peace upon peace*[^8], except that the Sabbath too, which is signified by the seventh day, although it is contained in the same temporal rolling of days, has surely a rest, which has been promised to the saints on this earth; where no storm of this age troubles them, resting in their God after good works? Which, to signify it so long before, after he had made all things very good, on the seventh day he himself too rested. Or is it for another reason that it is written in the book of holy Job: *Six times I will rescue you from necessities, and in the seventh evil will not touch you*[^9]? But for this reason that day no longer has an evening, because, without any incursion or clouding of sadness, which is often spread over from the mingled company of malignant men, it carries the saints across into the eighth day, that is, everlasting blessedness. For it is one thing to rest in the Lord still amid the times themselves, which is signified by the seventh day, that is, the Sabbath; but another to transcend all times, and to be set in the artificer of the times without any end now, which is signified by the eighth day, which, by not rolling with the rest, declares that it has the mark of eternity. For with these seven continually rolling by the repetition of succession, the circuits of all times are completed. But the friends of this world are not figured by the significations of these days; for they do not desire the rest of the spiritual Sabbath in the seventh, from which their attention too could be stretched into the eighth, eternity; but, given to those transitory celebrations, deserting the Creator they slip into worshiping the creation, and become impious. Hence consequently that man who sings for the eighth added; and when he had said: *You, Lord, will preserve us and will guard us from this generation forever*, he at once joined: *The impious walk in a circuit*[^10]; subject, namely, to temporal reasonings, not knowing how to savor the eternal.

**5.** And in these days, indeed, in which a certain signification is figured, the same eighth is found which is the first. For the Lord's day is itself called the first of the Sabbath; but it itself passes away as the first, the second succeeding. But in that eternity which this eighth and first signifies, there is both a first eternity, which, deserting by sinning in the origin of our first parents, we came into this mortality; and as it were a last, an eighth, which after the resurrection, the last enemy, death, being destroyed, we seek again, that this corruptible may put on incorruption, and this mortal may put on immortality; and the returning son may receive the first robe, which, after the labor of the long pilgrimage, and the feeding of swine, and the other troubles of mortal life, and the sevenfold circuits of times, is rendered to him as that last and as it were eighth. Not undeservedly, then, did our Lord himself too, on the first and the same eighth day, the Lord's day, condescend to demonstrate in his own flesh the example also of bodily resurrection; he who now no longer dies, and death will no longer have dominion over him. To this exaltation of his we ought to tend through humility. For setting it before two disciples who were asking, and desiring to sit one at his right and the other at his left, he said: *Can you drink the cup that I am going to drink?*[^11] That they might understand that the way to the height is to be begun from the valley of weeping; and that they would not be made worthy to take the summit of heaven, unless they first did not disdain the reproach of the cross.

**6.** For the very last verse too of the eleventh Psalm, over which the title *for the eighth* stands out, *Lord, according to your height you have multiplied the sons of men*[^12], is fittingly taken as said to the same Lord Jesus Christ, who in himself, the Son of God and Son of man, making sons of God of the sons of men, multiplies, according to his height, those who in this earth are few because of the abundance of sins, and like grains on the threshing floor because of the multitude of chaff, which alone seems to be everywhere—those very ones lying hidden and almost not appearing—into the heavenly Jerusalem, calling the things that are not as though they were. And from what height? Because *blindness in part has come upon Israel, that the fullness of the gentiles might enter, and so all Israel might be saved*[^13]; it was exclaimed by the Apostle saying: *O the height of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God!*[^14] Let us not, then, seek the ruinous heights of temporal sublimities. For we are dead, and our life is hidden with Christ in God; when Christ who is our life appears, then we too will appear with him in glory. Therefore let our attention persevere toward the eighth, where, every circuit of times being completed, the impious walk. Since our way of life precedes in hope into heaven, let us live with Christ, in Christ, equal to the angels of God, and made partakers of that eternity, who did not refuse to become a partaker of our mortality. For as in the octaves of the sacred signs after seven days there is the eighth, so in the sacred sign of Pentecost after seven weeks, which we conclude on the forty-ninth day, the same eighth is added, that fifty may be completed; one in the smaller number, and the same one in the greater. For eternity, of which this eighth day has the sign, can neither be increased nor diminished; and there it is always today, since nothing succeeds to something passing away. And that today is neither begun from the end of yesterday, nor ended at the beginning of tomorrow, but is always today; since all past things passed away without it setting, and all future things will come without it being born.

**7.** Forgetting, then, the things that are behind, and stretching yourselves out to the things that are before, following according to the intention toward the prize of the upward calling, dearest brothers and sons, even when you have laid aside the seals of the sacred signs, always bear in your heart the hope of this everlasting day; and so change your shining garments—by which the bright shoot is written in your memory, in the newness of your life, as it were by a visible word—that you do not change what they indicate, gleaming with the light of faith and truth; and let it be stained with no filth of perverse conduct: that on that day we may not be found naked, and may pass from the glory of faith into the glory of vision without any difficulty. But when, as is solemnly done today, you have been mingled with the people from these railings by which your spiritual infancy distinguished you from the rest, cling to the good; and remember that *bad conversations corrupt good morals; be sober*, just, *and do not sin*[^15]. *For I betrothed you to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ; and I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his cunning, so your senses too be corrupted from the chastity of God, which is in Christ*[^16]. The friendship which is of this world commits adultery against souls, and makes them fornicate away from the one and true and lawful spouse, from whom you have received the ring of the Holy Spirit. Beware of the wide way, *which leads to destruction, and many are those who walk by it*[^17]; do not fail in the narrow way, whose end is eternal breadth. And if ever, as in human life it must happen amid the waves of this age, you fall into various temptations, and are barked at all around by the iniquities of many men, with the perseverance of faith and the joy of hope and the ardor of love sing *for the eighth*, and say: *Save me, Lord, for the holy one has failed; for the truths are diminished from the sons of men; each one has spoken vain things to his neighbor*[^18].

**8.** But you, sons *of man*, now made sons of God, *how long heavy of heart?* Do not love vanity and seek a lie[^19], and you will give no place to the devil. Nor let each of you, when amid the tumults of temptations and scandals he has clung unshaken to Christ, and, embracing the word of God, has stood immovable, think himself the only grain, because, walled around on every side by chaff, he does not see the partners of his barn. Let him consider that even in those times, before the blood of Christ, the price of the world, had been poured out on the earth, when holy Elijah said: *I am left alone*; the answer was: *I have left for myself seven thousand men, who have not bent their knees before Baal*[^20]. Which seven thousand, when they were still being threshed with him on the threshing floor of this age, did not yet touch one another in the mass; and therefore each one seemed to himself to be alone. More abundant is the mass in all the nations, which will be kept for the Lord, and will be guarded from this generation and forever; because by a hidden counsel, according to his height, he will multiply the sons of men.
