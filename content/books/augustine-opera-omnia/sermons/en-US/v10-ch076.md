# Sermon 389: A Tractate on Obtaining the Heavenly Bread, or on Giving Alms and Mercy

**1.** The Lord admonishes us, by the Gospel reading, to speak to you about obtaining the heavenly bread. For this earthly bread is necessary for the earth, because our flesh is earth. But it could not be that our flesh should have its bread, and our soul not have its bread. For our soul too, in a certain want in this age, needs its bread, just as the flesh needs its own. For who does not need bread? God alone, then, the bread, does not need bread: for he is the bread of our soul, who needs no other bread, but, sufficient to himself, nourishes us too. Manifest, therefore, is the heavenly bread by which our soul is fed.

**2.** But how to come to it, that we may be filled with that of which now we scarcely gather crumbs, lest we perish in this famished desert: how, then, to come to the fill of this bread of which the Lord says, *He who eats this bread will not hunger, and he who drinks the drink which I shall give will never thirst*[^1], promising a certain fill and satiety without disgust: how, then, we may come to this satiety of bread, set far from that satiety in this famine, there is need of counsel. And if we neglect this counsel, we knock at that bread in vain. Indeed, this counsel which I am about to give—or rather to recall, for I will not say of myself what I learned with you—this counsel, then, which I am about to give: I do not say that whoever despises it knocks in vain, but that whoever despises it does not knock at all. For to follow and act on this counsel, this is to knock. For what do you suppose, my brothers—that God truly has, as it were bodily, a certain hard door which he shuts against men, and therefore said to us, *Knock*, that we should come and beat on the door until by knocking we reach the ears of the head of the household set in some hidden place, and he commands it to be opened to us, saying: Who is it that knocks? Who is it that makes a weariness for my ears? Give him what he asks and let him depart from here. It is not so. Yet there is something similar. Surely, when you knock at someone's door, you act with your hands. There is something for you to do with your hands, when you knock at the Lord's. Act altogether with your hands, knock with your hands. If you do not do this, I do not say, you knock in vain, but I say, you do not knock. Therefore you will not deserve it, therefore you will not receive it, because you do not knock. How, he says, do you want me to knock? Behold, I ask daily. You do well to ask, you do excellently, for this too was said: *Ask, and it will be given you; seek, and you will find; knock, and it will be opened to you*[^2]. All has been said: Ask, seek, knock. You ask by praying, you seek by knocking, you knock by giving out. Let not your hand, then, be at rest.

**3.** The Apostle, when he admonished the people about alms, says: *In this I give counsel. For this is profitable for you, who began not only to do, but also to will, from the year before*[^3]. And Daniel said thus to King Nebuchadnezzar: *Take my counsel, O king, and redeem your sins by alms*[^4]. If, then, it is counsel that we receive when we are commanded or admonished to give something of what we have to the needy, let us not be proud when we give. For if it is counsel that you have received, it profits you more than the one to whom you gave. Let us not, then, exalt ourselves over our works, and as it were please ourselves that we bestow benefits on those to whom we give. He who wishes to take counsel wishes to take what may profit himself, and he who gives counsel gives advice. If he gives advice, it profits the one to whom he gives advice. The poor man receives from you as a suppliant—because if it is not fitting for you to give proudly, how much more is it unfitting for him to receive proudly—he receives humbly, he receives giving thanks. Yet do you know and remember not only what you give, but also what you receive. For if this poor man should take the liberty of answering you, if he perhaps sensed you being proud over him, and said to you: Why are you proud, why are you lifted up? Because you gave to me? What did you give me? Bread. This bread, if you put it in your house and despised it, would go to mold, from mold to rottenness, from rottenness to earth: earth would return to earth. And you, who to my outstretched hand, that it might receive, stretched out your hand to give—remember whence your hand was made, and whence mine. You put earth from earth into earth. Then what do I do with your bread? I eat it, I restrain the trouble of hunger. I receive a benefit, I am not ungrateful. Yet do you think what the Lord the Savior himself admonished you, that *everything that enters the mouth goes into the belly and is cast out into the privy*[^5]. And again, what did the apostle Paul say to you? *Food for the belly and the belly for food: but God will destroy both this and these*[^6]. Bread, then, as I said, is earth from earth into earth, that earth may be propped up and earth restored. You think of what you gave, you do not think of what you are going to receive. See, then, that I do not bestow more on you by receiving than you on me by giving. For if there were no one to receive from you, you would not give out earth and buy heaven. I knock at your door and you hear me, you command something to be handed to me whereby my hunger may be sustained, and the trouble it brings upon me restrained. You did well. Do not hear me knocking, if you dare. If you are not going to ask, despise the one who asks. Despise me, if you ask nothing from him who made both you and me. But if you are going to ask for that which you give me—because you heard me—you bring it about that you are heard. Give thanks to him who made you buy so precious a thing at so cheap a price. You give what perishes in time, you receive what abides forever. You give what, if you did not give, you would after a little while throw away; you receive what you will enjoy forever. You give that whereby the hunger of men may be sustained, you receive that whereby you may be a companion of the angels. You give that whereby a man may not hunger for a time, soon to suffer hunger again, you receive that whereby you will never suffer hunger, never famine and thirst. When, then, you see what you give, what you receive, do not give, if you dare. Let us see who is struck with the greater loss: I, to whom you do not give earth, or you, who will not reach him who made heaven and earth. If, then, we have received counsel, let us act for our own sake, and let no one say that he bestows on the poor man: for he bestows on himself more than on the poor man.

**4.** If we think the truth, my brothers, and are wise according to the words of our Lord—for if we are wise otherwise, we perish—if we live not by our own counsel but by his, then we truly live. If we have anything to give to the poor, if we do not give it, we leave it here, or perhaps while we live we lose it here. For how many have suddenly lost all the goods they were most studiously storing up! In one enemy onslaught all the treasures of the rich perished. No one said to the enemy: I am keeping it for my children. Surely you see that if there is in them any faith—for we must speak of those Christians who suffered this; for of those ignorant of God there is no mention: they lost in this life what they held for something great, and they hoped for no other life: darkness without, darkness within: poverty in the chest, greater want in the conscience; of these, then, as I said, we must not speak, but rather of those in whom there is some Christian faith: therefore I said, some, not robust, not full, because if it were robust and full, they would not have spurned the Lord's counsel—yet certainly, dearest ones, when they saw their houses empty, or perhaps were not even allowed to see the very emptiness of their houses, when they were led away captive from there, when, as they departed, the flame followed: when, then, they considered themselves empty, how they repented that they had not heard the Lord's counsel. For what did our Lord Jesus Christ, what did he say to that rich man who sought counsel about laying hold of eternal life? What did he say to him? Did he say to him: Lose what you have? Plainly even if he said this: Lose temporal things that you may acquire eternal ones. Yet he did not say to him: Lose what you have. For he saw him to be a lover of his own things. He did not say: Lose, but said: Move to where you may not lose. Do you love your treasures? Do you love your money? Do you love your riches? Do you love your estates? Whatever you love, you have on earth. There you have what you love, where you may lose it and perish. I give counsel: move to heaven. Here, if you have it, you will lose what you have, you will perish with that which you lose; but there, if you have it, you have not lost it, but you will follow it where you sent it. I give counsel, then: *Give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven*. You will not remain without treasure, but what you have on earth with anxiety, you will have in heaven with security. Move, then. I give counsel for keeping, not for losing. *You will have*, he says, *treasure in heaven, and come, follow me*[^7], and I will lead you to your treasure. It is not an expense, but a gain. Let men awake, let them hear—even those who have learned by experience—what they should fear, let them do that whereby they need not fear, let them move to heaven. What sort of counsel is it that you have set your grain on the earth? A friend who knows the nature of grain and earth teaches your ignorance: What have you done? You have set your grain on the earth, in the lower parts; the place is damp, what you set there rots, you will lose your labor. What, then, shall I do? Move it to the upper parts. You hear your friend giving counsel about your grain, and you despise God giving counsel about your heart. You fear to set your grain on the earth, and you lose your heart on the earth. Behold, the Lord your God, when he gives you counsel about your treasure, gives it to you also about your heart. *For where*, he says, *your treasure will be, there will be also your heart*[^8]. Lift, he says, your heart to heaven, lest it rot on the earth. It is the counsel of one who wants to save you, not to destroy you. If, then, it is so, how those who did not do it repent! What do they now say to themselves? We would have in heaven what we lost on earth. The enemy invaded the house; would he invade heaven? He killed the servant who was guardian; would he kill the Lord the keeper, to whom no thief approaches, nor does moth corrupt? How many say: There we would have it, there we would store our treasures, where, secure, we would after a little while follow them. Why did we not hear our Lord? Why did we despise the Father admonishing, and feel the enemy invading? Many, then, repent. For a certain man—what is truly said to have happened—a man not rich, but yet rich with the fat of love even out of slender means, when he had sold a solidus, as is usual, for a hundred folles, ordered that out of the price of the solidus a part be given out to the poor. It was done. There that ancient enemy, that is, the devil, brought it about that the man repented of his good work, and by murmuring blotted out what by obeying he had rightly done. He sent in a thief, and took away the whole from which the little had been given to the poor. The devil awaited the voice of one blaspheming; he found one praising. He awaited a faltering to happen; he found a confirmation. The enemy indeed wanted the man to repent, and he did repent. But see of what. Wretched me, he said, that I did not give it all! For this I lost, that I did not give. For I did not put there, where no thief approaches. So if this is counsel, let us not be slow in so good a counsel. If we must move what we have, let it be to that place from which we may not lose it. What are the poor to whom we give, but our porters, through whom we may move from earth to heaven? You give to your porter; he carries to heaven what you give. How, he says, does he carry it to heaven? Behold, I see that he consumes it by eating. He transports it not by holding it but by eating. Or has it escaped you: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom. For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*? And: *When you did it for one of these least of mine, you did it for me*[^9]. If you did not despise the one begging before you, attend to whom what you gave has reached. *When you did it for one of these least of mine, you did it for me*. Christ received what you gave; he received it, who gave you the means to give; he received it, who in the end will give you himself.

**5.** For this too, brothers, I have at times admonished your love, which I confess moved me most in the Scripture of God, and which I ought rather often to admonish. I ask you to think on what our Lord Jesus Christ himself says—that at the end of the age, when he comes to judgment, he will gather all nations before his sight and divide men into two parts, setting some on the right, others on the left, and will say to those on the right: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world*; but to those on the left: *Go into the eternal fire which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^10]. Seek the causes either of so great a reward or of so great a punishment: *Receive the kingdom*, and: *Go into the eternal fire*. Why are these to receive the kingdom? *I was hungry and you gave me to eat*. Why are these to go into the eternal fire? *For I was hungry and you did not give me to eat*[^11]. What is this, I ask? I will look at those who are to receive the kingdom, because they gave, as good faithful Christians, not despising the words of the Lord and hoping with confidence for the things promised. They did this because, if they had not done it, this barrenness would surely not have suited their good life. For perhaps they were chaste, not cheats, not drunkards, abstaining from evil works. If they did not add this, they would remain barren. For they would have done, *Turn away from evil*, and not done, *And do good*[^12]. Yet even to them he does not say: Come, receive the kingdom: for you lived chastely, you cheated no one, you oppressed no poor man, you invaded no one's boundary, you deceived no one by swearing. He did not say these things, but: *Receive the kingdom*. Why? *For I was hungry and you gave me to eat*. How much this excels, when the Lord was silent about the rest and named this alone! Again to those: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*. How many things he could say against the impious, if they asked: Why do we go into the eternal fire? Why do you ask, adulterer, murderer, cheat, sacrilegious one, blasphemer, unbeliever? None of these things, but: because *I was hungry, and you did not give me to eat*. I see that you too are moved and amazed. And truly it is a wonderful thing. But I gather, as I can, the reason of this wonderful thing, which I will not hide from you. It is written: *As water extinguishes fire, so alms extinguish sin*[^13]. Again it is written: *Shut up alms in the heart of the poor man, and it will entreat the Lord for you*[^14]. Again it is written, what I mentioned a little before: *Hear my counsel, O king, and redeem your sins by alms*[^15]. And there are many other proofs of the divine word, by which it is shown that alms avail much for extinguishing and blotting out sins. Accordingly, to those whom he is going to condemn—or rather first to those whom he is going to crown—he will reckon these alms alone, as if to say: It is difficult, if I examine you and weigh you and scrutinize your deeds most diligently, not to find something for which to condemn you; but: *Go into the kingdom: for I was hungry and you gave me to eat*. You go into the kingdom, then, not because you did not sin, but because you redeemed your sins by alms. Again to those: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*. And they, like guilty men long ago accused, trembling late, attending to their sins—when would they dare to say that they were condemned undeservedly, that this sentence was undeservedly pronounced against them by so just a judge? Considering their consciences, considering all the wounds of their conscience, when would they dare to say: We are condemned unjustly? Of whom it was said before in *Wisdom*: *Their iniquities will accuse them to their face*[^16]—without doubt they will see that they are justly condemned for their crimes and outrages. And as if he said to them: Not for the reason you think, but because *I was hungry and you did not give me to eat*: for if, turned away from all those deeds of yours and turned to me, you had redeemed all those crimes and sins by alms, those very alms would now free you and absolve you from the guilt of so great crimes: for *blessed are the merciful, for to them mercy will be shown*[^17]; but now: *Go into the eternal fire: for there is judgment without mercy for him who has not shown mercy*[^18].

**6.** Let me commend to you, my brothers: give earthly bread, and knock at the heavenly. The Lord is bread. *I am*, he says, *the bread of life*[^19]. How will he give to you who do not give to the needy? Another is in need toward you, you are in need toward another. And when you are in need toward another and another is in need toward you, that one toward whom you are in need is in need toward the needy, but is in need of no one. Do what may be done concerning you. For not as friends are wont in a way to throw their benefits in each other's teeth—I bestowed this on you, and the answer comes: And I this on you—does God will us to bestow on him because he too bestowed on us: he needs no one. Therefore he is the true Lord: *I said to the Lord: You are my God, because you have no need of my goods*[^20]. Since, then, he is Lord, and the true Lord, and has no need of our goods, yet, that we too might do something toward him, he deigned to hunger in his poor: *I was hungry*, he says, *and you gave me to eat. Lord, when did we see you hungering? When you did it for one of these least of mine, you did it for me*. And likewise to those: *When you did not do it for one of these least of mine, neither did you do it for me*[^21]. Briefly, then, let men hear and worthily consider how great is the merit of having fed Christ hungering, and what sort of crime it is to have despised Christ hungering. Repentance of sins indeed changes a man for the better; but not even it seems to be of any profit, if it is barren of works of mercy. The Truth testifies to this through John, who said to those coming to him: *Brood of vipers, who showed you to flee from the wrath to come? Bring forth, then, fruits worthy of repentance. And do not say: We have Abraham for our father. For I tell you that God is able from these stones to raise up sons to Abraham. For now the axe is laid to the roots of the trees. Every tree, then, not bearing good fruit will be cut down and cast into the fire*[^22]. Of this fruit he says above: *Bring forth fruits worthy of repentance*. Whoever, then, does not bear these fruits thinks in vain that by a barren repentance he earns the repentance of his sins. But what these fruits are he consequently shows. For after these words of his, *the crowds asked him, saying: What, then, shall we do?*—that is, what are these fruits which you exhort and frighten us to bring forth? *And answering he said to them: He who has two tunics, let him give to him who has none, and he who has food, let him do likewise*[^23]. What is more manifest, my brothers, what more certain, what more express? What else, then, does it mean, which he says above: *Every tree which does not bear good fruit will be cut down and cast into the fire*, except what those on the left are to hear: *Go into the eternal fire: for I was hungry, and you did not give me to eat*? It is too little, therefore, to depart from sins, if you neglect to cure the past, as it is written: *Son, have you sinned? Do not add again*. And lest he think himself secure by this alone: *And concerning the past*, he says, *pray that they be forgiven you*[^24]. But what will it profit to pray, if you do not make yourself worthy to be heard by not bringing forth fruits worthy of repentance, so that, like a barren tree, you are cut down and cast into the fire? If, then, you wish to be heard when you pray for your sins: *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you; give, and it will be given to you*[^25].
