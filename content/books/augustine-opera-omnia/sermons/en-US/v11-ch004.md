# Sermon 299/A: Augm. (Dolbeau 4)

**1.** The festival of the holy day that gathers us together today, well known to your ears and minds and deeds, we commemorate as sharers in the same rejoicing with you and joyful with you. The birthday of the apostles Peter and Paul has shone forth, a birthday not that entangled them in the world, but that freed them from the world. For by human weakness man is born to hardship; by Christian love the martyrs are born to a crown. And this day, on account of their merit, is set before us as a festival, both for the solemnity of celebrating and for the holiness of imitating, so that, recalling the martyrs' glory, we may love in them what those who killed them hated, and, loving their virtue, may honor their passion. In virtue the merit was won, in the passion the reward was repaid in the crowning. One day of two martyrs and two apostles: as far as we have received by the tradition of the Church, the two did not suffer on one day, and yet the two suffered on one day. Today Peter suffered first, today Paul suffered later: the merit matched the passion, love kept the day. This was wrought in them by him who was in them, who suffered in them, who suffered with them, who helped them as they contended, who crowned them as they conquered. But since we have said that a day of solemnity is set before us, not idly nor for celebrating a carnal joy, but for imitating a spiritual crown—for all wish to be crowned, but few to contend—let us, according to the order of the passions, not the order of the readings, hear from the Gospel the merit of Peter, and hear from the apostolic epistle the merit of Paul.

**2.** The Gospel was just now read, just now we heard: *The Lord said to Peter: "Simon Peter, do you love me?" He answered: "I love you," and the Lord: "Feed my sheep." And again the Lord: "Simon Peter, do you love me?" And he: "I love you, Lord," and again the Lord: "Feed my sheep"*[^1]. He asks a third time nothing other than what he had asked twice. To ask a third time pertained to him; to answer a third time was now wearisome to Peter. For *Peter was saddened,* says the Gospel, *because the Lord asked him a third time, and he said: "Lord, you know all things, you know that I love you." And the Lord: "Feed my sheep"*[^2]. He who asks what he knows wishes to teach something. What, then, did the Lord wish to teach Peter, asking a third time what he knew? What do we think, brethren, but that love might take away weakness, and that Peter might know that he must confess three times through love, who had denied three times through fear? Peter's merit, that he fed God's sheep, would never have been crowned with true martyrdom, if he had fed his own sheep. Not without cause did the Lord three times add *my sheep,* unless there were to be those who would wish to boast of martyrdom while feeding their own sheep. The apostolic soul, the catholic soul, simple, humble, subject to God, not seeking its own glory but his, that *he who boasts may boast in the Lord*[^3], feeds for the shepherd and in the shepherd is a shepherd. Heretics feed their own sheep, but they impose the Lord's mark on their sheep not for the sake of truth but for the sake of defense. For there are many—as we know, for everything is full of examples—who, fearing to lose their possessions, affix the titles of certain powerful men, so that by this deed one may possess and another may terrify. Because, then, they do not see their own name glorified throughout the world, they have imposed the name of Christ on their sheep, and would that they were bought and not plundered! One bought them, the others stole them. He bought them who redeemed them from the devil, who gave his blood as the price: a worthy price indeed, by which the whole world might be bought. Perhaps more was given than we were worth, but the buyer was a lover. They possess the sheep, then—I will not now say their own, but those they wish to be their own—lost servants; by their thefts they have imposed the Lord's mark. But the true Lord does not rest; in his other servants he utters true voices to the sheep, that they may recognize the shepherd's voice and return. Let them return to the flock, let them return in safety: we receive the sheep, but we do not corrupt the mark.

**3.** Perhaps some of the brethren wondered that, since we have great zeal for gaining and recovering our brothers from the ruin of error, in the sermons we gave earlier we said nothing about the heretics; and it has been reported to us that those wretched and pitiable men too have said that silence was imposed on us by the terror of the Circumcellions. It is indeed true that they do not cease to try to terrify us from preaching the word of peace; but if the wolves terrify us, what shall we answer to him who says: *Feed my sheep*[^4]? They bare their teeth to tear; we bare our tongue to heal. We speak plainly and do not keep silent, and we say the same things, often the same things: let them hear what they do not wish to hear, let them do what they ought to do. To the unwilling, indeed, we are importunate; but if for this reason we are loved among the willing, so that we are endangered among the unwilling, we will have in the name of Christ a confidence that perseveres to preach the word of God, aided by your prayers. For we believe that, when you hear of our dangers, how we dwell amid the fury of brigands, you pray for us: this our mutual love shows us. For we have not entered into your hearts, but he who is in you shows it to us, because he is in us too. This indeed I urge, that when you pray for us, you pray this: that God may guard above all our salvation, which according to him is eternal salvation. About this temporal salvation, let him do what he knows is expedient for us and for his Church. For we have heard from the same master, shepherd, prince, and head of shepherds: *Let us not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul*[^5]; we have heard also from the voice of the psalm a most healthful prayer: *Do not hand me over, Lord, to the sinner from my desire*[^6]. For each one is handed over to the sinner from his own desire to his harm; the martyrs were not handed over to sinners from their own desire, the apostles whose birthdays we celebrate today were handed over to sinners, but first the Lord of the martyrs and apostles was handed over to sinners: all these were handed over to sinners, but not from their own desire. Who, then, are those who are handed over to sinners from their own desire, but those who, for the sake of some worldly desire, consent to their persecutors? For instance—not to depart from this example in which we dwell—if, when they threaten and rage with cudgels, swords, and burnings, we keep silent out of desire for this life, we will have been handed over to sinners from our own desire, and we live dead, having the health of the flesh, losing the soul of love. But that we may live well, let us love both you, that you be not seduced, and them, that we may gain them. They threaten: let us reprove; they rage: let us pray; they drive us off: let us teach.

**4.** For we have heard also the merit of Paul, so that now I may speak also of his merits in the order I had promised; he was saying to his disciple, about to announce to him his own coming passion and to take fear from him by his own example: *I testify before God and Christ Jesus, who is to judge the living and the dead, and by his manifestation and his kingdom.* He bound him by this testimony and added: *Preach the word, be urgent in season and out of season*[^7]. Hearing this, we too, according to our small measure, are in season for you, out of season for them. Yet the word of God, the preaching of peace, we do not cease to bring forth and repeat in the name of Christ, *in season and out of season.* He is in season who offers bread to the hungry; he is out of season who forces food on the sick: to the one food is offered, on the other it is pressed; to one the refreshment is welcome, to the other bitter, but love forsakes neither. Let us, then, take in their examples the merits of the apostles; but let us not only not fear the passions, but even, if it be necessary, undergo them. Hear what the same apostle says: *For I am already being offered up,* or *poured out as a libation*—some manuscripts have *poured out,* some *offered up*: to be poured out and to be offered up both pertain to sacrifice—: he knew his passion to be a sacrifice to God. Not those who were killing him, but he himself was offering such a sacrifice to the Father, the priest who had said: *Do not fear those who kill the body*[^8]. *The time,* he says, *of my dissolution is at hand*[^9]. What, then, O Paul, do you hope for in that dissolution? O toiling one, for what rest? *The time,* he says, *of my dissolution is at hand.* What have you done? What do you hope for? *I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith*[^10]. Whence did he keep the faith, but because he was not terrified by his persecutors from preaching the word of God *in season and out of season?* How wicked, then, it is for us, when we are afraid, not to keep the faith, especially to him who teaches us both to love the better things and to fear the greater!

**5.** Whatever sweetness this life can have, it is not paradise, it is not heaven, it is not the kingdom of God, it is not the fellowship of the angels, it is not the company of those citizens of the heavenly Jerusalem. Let the heart be borne upward, let the earth be trodden underfoot by the flesh. The Lord taught us to despise transitory things, to love eternal things; he taught us, he cared for us, and he cares, because he deigns to: for the physician came not to find the healthy but to the sick. The cup of the passion is bitter, but it heals all diseases thoroughly; the cup of the passion is bitter, but the physician drank it first, lest the sick man hesitate to drink. Let it be drunk, if he so wills: for his will toward us is better than ours; he is more prudent than we, knowing better than we ourselves what is good for us, and knowing better than we what is being done in us. As with a sick man and a physician, the one suffers and does not know what ails him, the other examines another's suffering and pronounces the truth. A man asks another what is being done in him, and desires from one outside testimony about his own inner parts. And if the art of a human physician can do this, how much more the power of the Lord! For from this very day of today I am admonished to set forth an example of this matter. Before the Lord's passion and as the Lord's passion was at hand, Peter himself, whose day we celebrate today, was sick and did not know what was being done in him: he did not at all know the inner parts of his own weakness. He had presumed that he would die with the Lord: he dared more than he could. The sick man presumed the coming passion, the physician pronounced the coming denial. What wonder, then, if in that weakness the physician's judgment was found truer than the sick man's? It came, as it were, to the access of a greater fever, and he could not follow the passion. Therefore let this cup be drunk, if he gives it who knows what he gives and to whom he gives it. But if he does not wish it to be drunk, let him heal in another way, provided only he heals. Let us, however, be subjected without fear to the physician's hands, wholly certain that he will apply nothing that is not good for us.

**6.** For the debt that Paul demanded, he claimed back as if by merit. And what merit? *I have finished the course, I have fought the good fight, I have kept the faith.* These you have done: what do you hope for? *For the rest there remains for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will render to me on that day*[^11]. *The righteous judge will render it.* But that he might render it, the merciful Father brought it about. For what kind of Saul, who afterward was Paul, did Christ find him—was he not languishing and in peril, frenzied by a kind of madness against the Jews? Is he not that Saul who was present when Stephen was stoned, who guarded the garments of those stoning him, that he might stone in the hands of all? Is he not the one who, having received letters from the chief priests, was going wherever he could, to bind Christians and lead them to punishment? Did he not, while he went breathing threats and slaughter, as we read, get called, cast down by a heavenly voice, recalled by a word to the Word? That the Lord should so call him, then, what merits of his had gone before? I do not say what had gone before to be crowned, but what not to be damned. He made the persecutor of the Church a preacher of peace; he loosed all his sins; he set him in that place, so that through him the sins of others might be loosed. These were gifts of mercy, not debts of merits. Hear Paul himself, not at all ungrateful to the grace of God, hear him recalling and proclaiming these things: *I who formerly,* he says, *was a blasphemer and a persecutor and insolent, but I obtained mercy*[^12]. Did he say there, "it was rendered to me"? For if he said: *I who formerly was a blasphemer and a persecutor and insolent, but* it was rendered to me, what was to be rendered for those deeds, but damnation? But he says: *I obtained mercy.* Punishment was not rendered to me, that afterward a crown might be rendered. See, brethren, to whom punishment was owed, a crown is owed. *I formerly was,* he says, *a blasphemer and a persecutor and insolent.* You see the merit: punishment was owed. Therefore punishment is not rendered; he obtains mercy in place of punishment. But having received mercy, not ungrateful, he fights the good fight, finishes the course, keeps the faith. He made the forgiver of sins his debtor. *There remains for me,* he says, *a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will render to me on that day.* He does not say "gives," but *will render;* if he will render, he owed it. I dare to say outright: if he will render, he owed it. And had he received a loan, that he should owe? He owes a crown, he renders a crown, made a debtor not from any loan of ours but from his own promise. For when he crowned his merits, he was crowning his own gifts.

**7.** Therefore, dearly beloved brethren, therefore God is a debtor, because he is a promiser. Accordingly, if someone promises us something, when we meet him that he may give it, we use this word: "Render what you promised me." We demand of a debtor when we say "Render," but yet we confess his benevolence when we say "Render what you promised," not "what you received." He promised, then, both to us all and to the whole world; he promised certain things, and those very things are great. Not to say many things, he promised Christ, the passion of Christ, the blood of Christ for us; he promised through his prophets, he promised through his books, he promised the Church spread throughout the world, he promised victories to the martyrs, he promised the Church the breaking of idols, he promised at the end judgment and eternal life. Not to recall many things—for it is difficult to enumerate all his promises—let us for now consider these things I have said. He promised Christ: *Behold, a virgin shall conceive and bear a son, and you shall call his name Emmanuel, which is interpreted, God with us*[^13], and the rest that you know, which it is long to recall. He promised concerning his passion, his resurrection, his glorification: these have come to pass. He promised that there would be martyrs for his name, strong in their passions, victors in perseverance. The world rages, it was promised to rage, not that the seed might be trampled, but that the harvest might be sown; the blood of the martyrs was poured out everywhere; the harvest of the Church filled the world: these things have come to pass. The Church itself was promised in the Scriptures as about to reign, but in reality it was not yet shown. The apostles preached it, they sowed everywhere; not yet had come what was said: *And all the kings of the earth shall adore him, all the kings shall serve him*[^14], not yet had it come, but it was held in a pledge. For God so willed that human weakness be made secure about his promise, that he not only said it but also wrote it. To the believing he spoke, for the doubting he made provision; and all was held in a kind of bond, in holy Scripture, not yet in the experience of fulfillment. The kings too believed; for it was in God's bond: *All the kings of the earth shall adore him, all the nations shall serve him.* Now that all the nations too may serve, the Church has been spread wide. You have this too in the bond: *And in the idols of the nations there shall be a regard*; you have in the bond: *Lord God, my refuge, to you the nations shall come from the end of the earth and shall say: Truly our fathers worshiped lies, idols, which did not profit them*[^15]. Not idols indeed, but for the sake of idols demons and men raged, killed the martyrs, made them triumph over themselves: it is repaid to Babylon what she did.

**8.** For a certain impious city is described, spread through all lands as a kind of consensus of human impiety, and this is mystically named Babylon in the Scriptures. Again, a certain city is a pilgrim in this earth among all nations in the consensus of piety, and this is named Jerusalem. Now both cities are mingled; at the end they will be separated. And divine Scripture addresses both in many places, and in one place it says to Jerusalem: *Repay her double for what she did, repay her*[^16], that Jerusalem might repay Babylon double. What is this double? How do we understand the double being repaid to the city of Babylon? She, for her idols, killed Christians, but she could not kill Christ, our God. She ravaged the flesh of Christians, but she did not harm the spirit, she did not touch our God. The double is repaid to her, in her men and in her gods. For they killed men, but they could not kill our God. But now in Jerusalem men, their faithlessness slain, are received; the images are broken. They seek their own and do not find them: for out of pagans Christians are made. He who is not what he was has been slain. As Paul, who was first Saul, though the preacher lives, yet the persecutor has been slain. Formerly the Christians sought where to hide themselves while the pagans raged; now the pagans seek where to hide their gods. And when these are broken, their defenders still are unwilling to be silent, but yet they murmur in part, as though, if they ever dared, they could do anything but what he who possesses us promised, or as though, when they did it, they did it by their own power. See, Christians were seized, they confessed Christ, and they were killed. Let someone confess Mercury, swear by Mercury; when he sees one guard, even a cloaked one, "I did not do it, I was not present, I did not sacrifice; where did you see me?" But those holy servants of God: "Were you in the assembly of the Christians?" "I was." We read the confessions of the martyrs, we exult in the joy of the examples. These things came to pass, repaid by the Lord because promised. Formerly they were held in Scripture, now they are also displayed. And these things I have said about the idols have been displayed and are being displayed. And the Church, spread throughout the world, has now held almost all nations, and those it does not hold, it will hold; it grows daily, the Christian people is everywhere increased in the name of Christ.

**9.** Few among Christians live well, many among Christians live badly. But these few are few in comparison with their own chaff; in comparison, I say, with their own chaff they are few. This threshing floor will be winnowed, there will be a huge heap of chaff, but the shining mass of the saints will also appear. The chaff will go into the fire, the wheat into the barn, but for now both are everywhere. Whence is this? Through those who sowed, brethren, whose memory we celebrate today, through them God displayed to them what he promised them, and through them what he promised us. What did he promise them? *For the rest there remains for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will render on that day.* What did he promise us? *In your seed all the nations shall be blessed*[^17]. How was it done through these men? *Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^18]. What will the heretics recite against these things? I suppose that they too celebrate the birthday of the apostles today; they affect indeed to celebrate this day, but they do not dare to sing this psalm.
