# Sermon 382

**1.** Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ commands us something and promises us something. What he commands is here. What he promises is elsewhere. What he commands is finite, because it is temporal. What he promises is not finite, because it is eternal. What he commands is the work. What he promises is the wage. Here let your holiness notice how great his mercy is toward us, that he has set the toil here with an end, the wage in heaven without an end. And therefore we ought first to toil here and afterward receive the wage in heaven, rather than to wish to receive the wage here and afterward toil. For of certain men the Lord says: *Amen I say to you, they have received their reward*[^1]. But you are perhaps slow to receive the wage from your toil. With what face do you ask what God promises and do not do what God commands? First do the precept, and so demand the promise. First, I say, hear the one who commands, and then demand of the one who promises. For he commands us to love our enemies. *Love*, he says, *your enemies, do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who persecute and slander you*. A heavy precept, but a grand reward. *Love your enemies*. It is the work. *Do good to those who hate you, and pray for those who persecute and slander you*. And lest you be terrified at the toil, he immediately added the wage: *That you may be*, he says, *sons of your Father who is in heaven, who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^2].

**2.** Attend to the Lord himself, who commanded this, what he did. After so many things which the impious Jews committed against him, who were repaying him evil for good, did he not, hanging on the cross, say: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^3]? He prayed in the man, who heard with the Father. For even now he prays in us, prays for us, is prayed to by us. He prays in us as our priest, prays for us as our head, is prayed to by us as our God. When, then, hanging on the cross he prayed, he saw and foresaw: he saw all his enemies, but he foresaw that many of them would be friends. Therefore he begged pardon for all. They raged, he prayed. They said to Pilate, *Crucify*[^4]; he cried out, *Father, forgive*. And he hung upon the harsh nails, but he did not lose his gentleness. They raged, they barked around him, they wagged their heads, which were not sound, and, as if around one supreme physician set in the midst, the frenzied raged on every side. He hung and healed. He hung and yet bestowed. He did not come down, because of his own blood he was making remedies for the frenzied. Finally, after the resurrection he healed those whom, hanging, he endured most insane. Behold why Christ came: not to lose what he had found, but *to seek and to save what had perished*[^5], so that by loving raging enemies he might make believing friends.

**3.** But lest perhaps you say it is too much for you to imitate your Lord, who for you *suffered, leaving you an example that you might follow in his steps*[^6], attend to Stephen, your fellow servant. He was a man like you, born of the lump of sin like you, redeemed at the price at which you too were, a deacon, as you too know. When he was being struck by the Jews with a hail of stones, not only did he not threaten, but on top of that he begged pardon for those who stoned him. *Lord*, he says, *do not lay this sin to their charge*[^7]. As though he said: Let me die in the flesh, let not these perish in the mind. They cast stones, he sent prayers ahead.

**4.** For, that your holiness may know how much the prayer of the holy martyr availed, run back with us to that young man named Saul, who, when holy Stephen was being stoned, himself guarded the garments of all who stoned him, so that he seemed to stone as it were by the hands of all. Afterward, as you know—for I know to whom I speak, I speak to my brothers, I speak to the sons of my Father, I a fellow disciple speak to disciples—afterward *he received letters from the chief priests, that whatever followers of the Christian way he found, men and women, he might bring them bound to Jerusalem*, to be tortured and punished. *While he was going on the way, suddenly a light from heaven shone around him. He fell, and heard a voice saying to him: Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me? It is hard for you to kick against the goad*, because you will wound not the goad but the feet with which you kick. *But he: Who are you, Lord?* Because you have once fallen, you have recognized the Lord: *Who are you, Lord?* And he: *I am Jesus of Nazareth, whom you persecute*[^8]. Why do you rise up against me to your own harm, and not rather humble yourself to your own good? Saul, Saul, what is there between me and you? Against the great evils you commit against me, I should indeed long since have destroyed you, but my Stephen prayed for you. The raging man was struck down, the believing man was raised up. The wolf was struck down, the lamb was raised up. The persecutor was struck down, the preacher was raised up. I will say it plainly, I will say it more expressly: the *son of perdition*[^9] was struck down, the *vessel of election*[^10] was raised up. For if holy Stephen had not prayed thus, the Church would not have Paul. But for this reason Paul was raised up from the earth, because, bowed to the earth, holy Stephen was heard.

**5.** But against these things, so great and such as they are—thus, you, whoever you are, who do not love. Bend your knee, dash your forehead to the earth, cry out and say: God, kill the wicked man. Even you who pray thus, that a man may die, pray, being wicked, against a wicked man, and now you have been made two wicked men. You cry out and say: God, kill the wicked man. He will answer you: Which of you? Let your holiness notice; I speak of things known. A judge, being a man, does not himself kill the accused, but commands, and the executioner kills. And you, when you say: Lord, kill my enemy; you make yourself the judge, and you seek to have God for the torturer. God will answer you too: I will not be the torturer of the sinner, but his liberator, *because I do not will the death of the sinner, but that he be converted and live*[^11]. Before you came when invited, did you not blaspheme me? did you not provoke me by evil works? did you not wish to blot out my name from the earth? did you not despise me in my precepts or in my servants? If then I had killed you, my enemy, whom would I now make my friend? Indeed, then, God says to you: I will teach you, that you may imitate me. Hanging on the cross I said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^12]. I taught this to my soldiers, I taught it to the holy martyrs. Be you too my soldier against the devil. Otherwise you will in no way fight unconquered, unless you have prayed for your enemies.
