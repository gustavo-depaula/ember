# Sermon 313/B: Delivered in the Same Place and on the Same Day, on the Feast of the Aforesaid Martyr

**1.** We have sung a psalm: *Blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth*[^1]. Thanksgiving is owed to the gifts of God. *Blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth.* It is surely a voice of thanksgiving, and a worthy thanksgiving. And when does human thanksgiving suffice for such divine gifts? When in this place the most blessed martyr shed his sacred blood, I do not know whether there was here so great a throng of raging men, as there is now a multitude of those praising. Again I say—for it delights me to see the people in the house of the Lord gathering most religiously in this place, and to compare times with times—hence again I say and repeat, and commend to your minds with all the devotion I can: when in this place the most blessed martyr shed his sacred blood, I do not know whether there was here so great a throng of raging men, as there is now a multitude of those praising. But even if there was, there was, *blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth.* When they killed, they believed they had conquered; they were being conquered by the dying, and rejoiced. If they were being conquered, surely they raged. So the throng of the raging departed, the multitude of the praising succeeded. Let it say, let the multitude of the praising say: *Blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth.* To whose teeth? To the teeth of enemies, to the teeth of the impious, to the teeth of the persecutors of Jerusalem, to the teeth of Babylon, to the teeth of the enemy city, to the teeth of the throng raging in crimes, to the teeth of the throng persecuting the Lord, deserting the Creator, turning itself to the creation, worshiping the thing made by hand, despising him by whom it was made. *Blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth.*

**2.** It is the voice of the martyrs: it is surely the voice of those who chose to be killed for the name of Christ, rather than to live by denying Christ. If, then, those wanted to kill, these were killed; those did what they wanted, these suffered; how then: *Blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth?* What kind of thanksgiving is it: *Blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth?* Because the persecutors wanted not to kill, but to devour, that is, to transfer into their own body. They were pagans, they were impious, they were worshipers of demons and idols; they wanted to make us this, when they desired to devour us. Consider what we do with food, when we eat. What surely do we do, but transfer it into our own body? It was the body of the impious: those devoured them, who consented with them to impious things; without doubt they passed into their body. The martyrs, then, when these insisted that Christ be denied and the idol adored, resisted constantly, spurned the idol, confessed Christ, and by not consenting did not pass into their body. Let them say, let them say, let them gloriously say, let them happily say, let them truly say: *Blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth.* The net is faithlessness, the net is impiety, the net is the denial of Christ. The nets are stretched out. You hear the hunters. If you want to beware of the hunters, despise those who terrify. You know what hunters do. On one side they stretch nets, on the other side they frighten the beasts, which they drive into the nets. Do you fear the evil by which you are terrified? Worse is that to which you flee. So the holy martyrs, seeing where the hunters had stretched the nets—for the persecutor threatened death for this, that the Savior be denied—suffered, but by suffering were not caught. With what fat prey, with what richness of hunting would impious Babylon be fed, if the Lord were denied by Cyprian the bishop! With what fat, with what hunting, with how excellent a prey would impious Babylon be fed, if by Cyprian the bishop, the teacher of the nations, the frustrater of idols, the betrayer of demons, the winner of pagans, the strengthener of Christians, the inflamer of martyrs; if, then, by so great and so good a man the Lord were denied, with what hunting would impious Babylon rejoice! *Blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth.* Let them have raged, let them have persecuted, tortured, imprisoned, bound, struck, burned, exposed to beasts; Christ was not denied, the confessor of the Lord was crowned. They lost their savagery, the martyrs found glory. *Blessed be the Lord,* let the Christian people say, let it say it altogether, it is fitting that it say: *Blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth.* Now let this place say it: it is filled with a people of those confessing, it is filled with a people of those adoring the one true God; let this place say it: for then this crop was being sown, when that place was being watered with the blood of the martyr. Do not marvel, O earth, at your fertility, if you were watered to sprout this.

**3.** So *blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth.* For by what strength of ours were we snatched from the teeth of the impious? We arrogate nothing to ourselves, we attribute this not to our own power. *Blessed be the Lord, who has not given us as prey to their teeth.* For what were we, when we were terrified, the weak by the strong, the lowly by the lofty, the needy by the rich, the destitute by the abundant? What were we, unless our help were in the name of the Lord, of him *who made heaven and earth*[^2]? Exult, exult, Jerusalem: exult you too, not given to the teeth of the hunters; exult you too: you too have teeth. *Your teeth are like a flock of shorn ewes*[^3]. You too have teeth, O holy Jerusalem, city of God, Church of Christ, you too have teeth. To you it is said in the Song of Songs: *Your teeth are like a flock of shorn ewes coming up from the washing, all of which bear twins, and there is no barren one among them*[^4]. Well, well, that you did not fear the teeth of Babylon. The teeth of Babylon were the powers of the age; the teeth of Babylon were the teachers of unlawful rites. To these teeth you were not given. Acknowledge your teeth: do you what they wanted to do. Convert: you too have teeth. *Your teeth are like a flock of shorn ewes.* What is: *of shorn ewes?* Of those laying down their worldly burdens. What is: *of shorn ewes?* Of those laying down their fleece, as the loads of the worldly burden. Those were your teeth, of whom it is written in the Acts of the Apostles, that they sold all their goods, and laid the prices of their things before the feet of the Apostles, that it might be distributed to each, as there was need. You received the fleeces of your shorn ewes. That flock came up from the washing of holy baptism. They all bore, because they fulfilled the two commandments. You remember, you have recalled, like men instructed you cried out, when I recalled the two commandments; but what they were, I did not say, and yet I received the sign of your heart by your voice. You recognized them. Yet I will say them for the sake of those who come to church more rarely. The Lord says, the most truthful Teacher says, the prince of martyrs says: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind; and you shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two commandments hang the whole Law and the Prophets*[^5]. So your teeth therefore conquered, because they bore such twins. To you having such teeth it is said, to you it is said, O Church, in the figure of the most blessed Peter: *Rise, kill and eat*[^6]. *Rise,* it was said to Peter—when the vessel let down from heaven in the figure of animals carried all the nations—to the hungry Peter, that is, to the eager Church: *Rise,* what do you hunger for? *Rise,* food is prepared for you. You have teeth: *Kill and eat.* Kill what they are, make them what you are; kill what they are, turn them into what you are. You heard well, having such teeth, you killed well, you ate well. The very judges, whom you did not fear, you drew to yourself; the very powers of the age, whom you did not fear, you converted to yourself; those raging you spurned, those honoring you made. It was fulfilled, what was promised to your Lord: *And all the kings of the earth will adore him, all nations will serve him*[^7].

**4.** These things the persecutors did not believe, when they raged. How many of those very persecutors, who saw the most blessed Cyprian shedding his blood, bending his knees, offering his neck to the executioner, saw it here, watched it here, here exulted over so great a spectacle, here, here insulted him dying! How many of them, which I do not doubt, afterward believed! It is not to be doubted, it is to be believed without doubt. The Jews, the killers of Christ, who when he hung shook their heads insulting, and against him said the words they wanted, afterward exulting believed in the very Lord whom they crucified. For the voice of the Physician hanging on the cross could not be empty, making for the frenzied a medicine of health out of his own blood. So that voice could not be empty and vain: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^8]. It was plainly not empty. There was there a multitude of those for whom this voice resounded from the mouth of Truth. For afterward, when the miracle was done, the Holy Spirit coming from heaven, when the Apostles spoke with the tongues of all nations, marveling at the sudden miracle, pricked at once, turned to him whom they killed, believing they drank the blood which raging they had shed. This plainly, even concerning the blessed Cyprian, the holy martyr of Christ, is by no means to be doubted, that very many of those who watched him slain impiously believed in his Lord, and perhaps even themselves, by imitating, shed their blood for the name of Christ. Lastly, about those let it be uncertain; about those who were then present at this place, who saw holy Cyprian struck in this place, let it be uncertain whether they believed. Surely all these, or almost all, whose voices I hear exulting, are the children of those insulting.
