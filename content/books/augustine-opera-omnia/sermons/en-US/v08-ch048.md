# Sermon 305/A: On the Birthday of Saint Lawrence,

Delivered at Carthage in the Restored Basilica, on the Third Day Before the Ides of August

**1.** On account of the hearer's weariness the sermon ought to have been withheld, but on account of homage to the martyr it ought to be offered; so then, with the Lord's help, it will be measured so as to be neither burdensome, nor perhaps too scanty for what is sufficient. There has shone forth a most solemn day at Rome, which is celebrated by a great gathering of the people; we join ourselves, though absent in body, yet present in spirit, to our brethren in one body, under one head. For not only where the tomb of his body is, is there the memory of his merit: devotion is owed everywhere; the flesh is laid in one place, but the victorious spirit is with him who is everywhere. Blessed Lawrence was in body, as we have received, a young man, grave in mind, whom a fresher age much commended, an unfading crown more. He was a deacon, lower than the bishop in office, equaled to the apostle in crown. And this feast of all the glorious martyrs was instituted in the Church for this, that, to their imitation, those who did not see them suffering may be drawn by faith, and reminded by the feast. For perhaps it would slip from the hearts of men if the yearly course did not repeat it. And not the fervent feasts of all the martyrs can be held everywhere, for they would not fail on any day; for not even one day can be found in the course of the year on which martyrs were not crowned in various places. But the more fervent feasts, if they were continuous, would bring weariness; intervals, however, renew affection. Let us only hear what is commanded, attend to what is promised; on the feast of any martyr let us so prepare our heart for his festivity that we be not separated from his imitation.

**2.** For he was a man, and we are men; by whom he was made, by him we too; with what price he was bought, with it we too. So a Christian man ought not to say: Why I? Rather he ought not to say: Not I; but: Why not I too? You heard blessed Cyprian, both the example and the trumpet of martyrs: *In persecution one is crowned by soldiering, in peace by conscience*[^1]. So let no one think the time is lacking to him; the time of suffering is not always at hand, but the time of devotion always is. Nor let anyone think himself weak, where God works the strength; lest, while he fears for himself, he despair of the very worker. Therefore in the examples of the martyrs God willed all ages to be, and both sexes: old men were crowned, young men were crowned, youths crowned, boys crowned, men crowned, women crowned. And among women every age was crowned; nor did a woman say: I am unequal in sex to vanquishing the devil. She rather attended to the foe to be cast down, by whom she had been cast down, and to be overcome by the faith to which she had consented in being seduced. Did even the women presume on their own strength? For it was said to every man: *For what have you that you did not receive?*[^2] The glory of the martyrs, then, is the glory of Christ going before the martyrs, filling the martyrs, crowning the martyrs. Yet, although there be peace at one time, persecution at another, is hidden persecution lacking to any time? It is never lacking: that lion, that dragon, neither always rages, nor always lies in wait, but always persecutes. When the savagery is open, the snares are not hidden; when the snares are hidden, the savagery is not open; that is, when he roars as a lion, he does not creep as a dragon; when he creeps as a dragon, he does not roar as a lion; yet, because he is either a lion or a dragon, he always persecutes. When the roaring is silent, beware the snares; when the snares are exposed, in the roaring avoid the lion. And both the lion and the dragon are avoided, if the heart is always kept in Christ. Whatever in this life is to be feared, is going to pass away; but in the other life, both what is to be loved does not pass away, and what is to be feared does not pass away.

**3.** Surely just now in the Gospel the Lord was addressing the Jews, and saying to them: *Woe to you, scribes and Pharisees, because you build the tombs of the prophets, and say: If we had been in the time of our fathers, we would not have consented with them in the killing of the prophets. Surely you bear witness that you are the sons of those who killed the prophets. And you will fill up the measure of your fathers*[^3]. For when they had said: *If we had been in the time of our fathers, we would not have consented with them in the killing of the prophets*, they confirmed that they were the sons of those men. But we, if we hold the right road, do not say that our fathers were those who killed the prophets, but we say that our fathers were those who were killed by their fathers. For just as one degenerates by his ways, so he becomes a son by his ways. For surely, brethren, we are called the sons of Abraham, though we have neither known Abraham's face, nor draw the stock of our flesh from his race. How then are we his sons? Not in flesh, but in faith. *For Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness*[^4]. If, then, Abraham was just because he believed, all after Abraham imitating Abraham's faith have been made sons of Abraham. The Jews born of his flesh degenerated; we sprung from foreigners have by imitating attained what they by degenerating lost. Far be it, then, that Abraham be their father, though they came from Abraham's flesh. Their fathers were those of whom they themselves confessed. *If we had been*, they say, *in the time of our fathers, we would not have consented with them in the killing of the prophets*. How do you say you would not have consented with those whom you call your fathers? If they were fathers, you are a son; if you are a son, you were going to consent. But if you were not going to consent, you are not a son; if you are not a son, they are not fathers. So the Lord from this convicts them of being about to do what those did, because they named those their fathers. *Surely*, he says, *you yourselves bear witness of yourselves, that you are the sons of those who killed the prophets, because you said they were your fathers. And you will fill up the measure of your fathers*.

**4.** And now let us consider who are the sons of those killed, and who are the sons of those who killed. And you see many run to the memorial shrines of the martyrs, bless their cups at the shrines of the martyrs, return full from the shrines of the martyrs; and yet sift them, and you will find them among the persecutors of the martyrs. For through these very men there are tumults, seditions, dancings, all the wanton excesses that God hates; and now, because they cannot stone those already crowned, they persecute them with cups. Who were they, and whose sons were they, whose dancings have recently, almost yesterday in memory, been forbidden at the place of the holy martyr Cyprian? Surely they danced there, and rejoiced there; and they awaited that very feast with great longing, as if they rejoiced, and always desired to come to that day. Among whom are they to be numbered? among the persecutors of the martyrs, or among the sons of the martyrs? They showed themselves, when, being forbidden, they swelled into sedition. The sons praise, the persecutors dance; the sons say hymns, those prolong their banquets. So it does not matter how they may seem to honor; for such they are, when they honor, as those were who said: *If we had been in those times, we would not have consented with our fathers in the killing* of the martyrs, or in the killing *of the prophets*. Consent now to the faith of the martyrs, and we believe that you would not have consented with the killers of the martyrs. Whence were the martyrs crowned? I believe, by walking the way of God, by enduring, by loving even their enemies, by interceding for them. This is the crown of the martyrs, this the merit of the martyrs. Do you love, imitate, praise? You are a son of the martyr. Do you live a contrary life? then you will lay on it the contrary hand.

**5.** Accordingly, dearly beloved, since persecution, as I said, is never lacking, and the devil either lies in wait or rages, we ought always to be prepared, with our heart fixed on the Lord, and, as far as we can, amid these troubles, tribulations, temptations, to pray for strength from the Lord, since of ourselves we are small and nothing. What shall we say of ourselves? You heard the apostle Paul, when he was read: *As the sufferings of Christ abound in us*, he says, *so also through Christ does our consolation abound*[^5]. As it is said in the Psalm: *According to the multitude of my sorrows in my heart, your consolations, Lord, have gladdened my soul*[^6]. As this was said in the Psalm: *According to the multitude of my sorrows in my heart, so your consolations, Lord, gladden my soul*, so it was said by the apostle: *As the sufferings of Christ abound in us, so also through Christ does our consolation abound*. We would fail, when the persecutor was present, if the consoler were absent. And because the very strength of enduring, or a certain relaxation of living for a time on account of necessary ministry, was not of themselves, see what he said: *I make known to you, brethren, concerning our tribulation that befell us in Asia, that we were burdened beyond measure and beyond strength*[^7]. That tribulation surpassed human strength: did it also surpass divine help? *Beyond measure*, he says, *and beyond strength we were burdened*. How far beyond strength? See that he speaks of the strength of the soul: *So that we were weary even of living*[^8]. How was the apostle affected by the multitude of pressures, that, though love urged him to live, weariness held him back from life! How love compelled him to live, that love of which he says in another place: *But to remain in the flesh is necessary for your sake*[^9]. Behold, so great had the persecution grown, and so great the tribulation, that it wearied him even of living. Behold, fear and trembling came upon him, and darkness covered him, as you heard when it was said in the Psalm. For it is the voice of the body of Christ, the voice of the members of Christ. Do you wish to recognize your voice there? Be a member of Christ. *Fear*, he says, *and trembling fell upon me, and darkness covered me. And I said: Who will give me wings like a dove? And I will fly, and be at rest*[^10]. Does not the apostle seem to have said this, when he says: *So that we were weary even of living?* In a manner he suffered weariness from the birdlime of the flesh; he wished to fly to Christ; the abundance of tribulations beset the way, but did not block it. He was weary of living, but this not in that eternal life, of which he says: *To me to live is Christ, and to die gain*[^11]. But because out of love he was held here, what follows? *But if to live in the flesh, this for me is the fruit of labor, and what I should choose I know not. I am pressed from the two, having a desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ*[^12]. *Who will give me wings like a dove?* *But to remain in the flesh is necessary for your sake*[^13]. He had yielded to his whispering chicks; he covered them with outspread wings, he cherished his chicks, as he himself says: *I became a little one in your midst, like a nurse cherishing her children*[^14].

**6.** And see, brethren, just now it was read in the Gospel: *How often have I wished to gather your sons, as a hen gathers her chicks, and you would not!*[^15] Attend to the hen, attend also to the other birds, which nest before our eyes; they brood over their eggs, they nourish their chicks: you will see none made weak with their offspring. See the hen's condition, when she nurtures her chicks, how her voice is changed, and breaks into a kind of hoarseness. Her very feathers are not sleek and lively, but bristling and languid; so that, if you see another bird, whose nest you do not know, you cannot tell whether she has eggs or chicks; but when you see a hen, even if you do not see her eggs, nor see her chicks, from her very voice and the condition of her body you understand her to be a mother. What then did our mother, Wisdom, do? She was made weak in the flesh, that she might gather her chicks, that she might bring them forth, that she might cherish them. But the weak thing of God is stronger than men[^16]; under these wings of the weakness of his flesh, but under the hidden power of his divinity, he wished to gather the sons of Jerusalem. This he had taught his apostle, because this he himself was doing in him; for the apostle himself says: *Do you wish to receive a proof of him who speaks in me, Christ?*[^17] And he says that the sufferings of Christ abounded in him: not his own sufferings, but the sufferings of Christ. For he was in the body of Christ, and was a member of Christ; and whatever was done in the apostle, in his member, for the cherishing of the chicks, the head was doing. So this apostle, attending to the weakness of his chicks, in affection and desire longed to fly like a dove, but out of love of his sons remained like a hen. *We ourselves*, he says, *had within ourselves the answer of death, that we should not trust in ourselves, but in God who raises the dead, who rescued us from such great dangers, and will rescue us, in whom we hope, because he will yet rescue us*[^18]. *He rescued* and *will rescue*, what does he say? He keeps this life of ours for you. For he rescued from many deaths, lest he be overwhelmed by the persecutors, lest he be crowned sooner than the chicks had need, according to that which he says: *But to remain in the flesh is necessary for your sake. And being confident of this I know that I shall remain, and continue with you all for your progress and the joy of faith*[^19]. Desire snatched him to one thing, necessity held him back in another. *To be dissolved*, he says, *and to be with Christ is much the best*[^20]. He did not say: necessary, but: best. For what is best is sought for its own sake; what is necessary is taken up out of necessity: thence it is called necessary.

**7.** Necessity gave its name to a necessary thing; accordingly now this food we use is necessary for us, necessary for sustaining the temporal life; but that best food of virtue and wisdom is the living bread, always refreshing, never failing. That is best, this is necessary. Accordingly, when this necessity of hunger and of sustaining the mortal body passes away, this food will no longer be necessary. For what does the apostle say? *Food for the belly, and the belly for food; but God will destroy both this and these*[^21]. But when will he destroy it? When this animal body, rising again, becomes spiritual; for there will be no want there, and there will be no works of necessity there. For all these things, brethren, both the good things that are here called works, and those very works that we are warned to do daily, are works of necessity. What is so good, what so excellent, what so praiseworthy for a Christian, as to break bread for the hungry, to bring the needy without shelter into the house, to see the naked and clothe him, to see the dead and bury him, to see the quarrelsome and reconcile them, to see the sick and visit or heal him? All these are praiseworthy works. Attend and see that necessity bore them. For you break bread, because you see one hungering; if no one hungered, to whom would you break bread? Take away the need of another's misery, and your mercy will have no work. But yet through these works, which necessity bore, we come to that life where there will be no necessity, as through a ship to our homeland. In the homeland where we shall always remain, never to journey again, there will be no need of a ship; but that ship, which there will be no need of, leads to that place. When we have arrived, these things will not be; but, unless they are fulfilled here, one cannot arrive there. So be eager in the good works of necessity, that you may be blessed in the full enjoyment of that eternity, where necessity now dies, because the very mother of all necessities, death itself, will die. *For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal put on immortality*[^22]. When it shall be said to death: *Where is, O death, your victory? where is, O death, your sting?*[^23] it will be said to death consumed and conquered, because *the last enemy, death, will be destroyed*[^24].

**8.** But now, with all the works of necessity, there is a fight with death: for every failing draws toward death, and every sustaining calls back from death; and the body is so changeable that, in a manner, some deaths are driven off by other deaths. Whatever is taken up, where it cannot long be persevered in, is in a manner the beginning of death. Now look at this life: if whatever is taken up, where it cannot be remained in longer—where, if you remain longer, you die—is the beginning of death; and yet, unless it be taken up, another death is not driven off. For example, a man does not eat: if he eats and digests, he is refreshed. When he does not eat, he takes up fasting, that he may drive off from himself the death which surfeit was going to cause; unless he takes up abstinence and fasting, he will not drive it off from himself. Again, in this fasting which he took up, that he might drive off the death of surfeit, if he should wish to persevere in it, he will fear another death, of hunger. So as he took up fasting, that he might avoid the death of surfeit, so he must take up food, that he may avoid the death of fasting. For whatever of these you take up, if you persevere in it, you will fail. By walking you were wearied: if you remain in walking, by the very weariness you will fail, and die. So that you may not fail by walking, you rest by sitting; persist in sitting, and from that you will die. Heavy sleep had pressed you down; you must wake, lest you die. By waking you will die, unless again you sleep. Give me something you may take up for help, to drive off the evil that weighed you down, where you are so secure that you would wish to persevere in it; whatever you take up, this very thing will have to be feared. So in all the mobility and changeableness of failings and helps there is a fight with death. *But when this corruptible shall have put on incorruption, and this mortal shall have put on immortality*[^25], it shall be said to death: *Where is, O death, your striving? Where is, O death, your sting?*[^26] We shall see, we shall praise, we shall remain. There will be no want there, no help will be required. You do not find a beggar, for whom you may break bread, or a stranger, whom you may receive into your house. You do not find one thirsting, to whom you may hold out a cup; nor one naked, whom you may cover; nor one sick, whom you may visit; nor one quarreling, whom you may reconcile; nor one dead, whom you may bury. All are fattened with the food of justice and the drink of wisdom, all are clothed with immortality, all live in their own eternal homeland; the very health of all is eternity, eternal health, eternal concord. No one quarrels, no one seeks a judge, no one an arbiter of settlement, no one a sentence of vengeance; no sickness, no death.

**9.** These things we have been able to say, which will not be there; but the things that will be there, who can say? *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man*[^27]. So rightly the apostle: *The sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come that will be revealed in us*[^28]. Whatever you suffer, O Christian, know it to be nothing in comparison with what you are to receive. Surely we hold this by faith: let it not depart from your heart. One cannot grasp and see what you will be; what then will it be, which by him who is to grasp it cannot be grasped? Surely we will be what we will be, and we cannot grasp what we will be. It surpasses all our weakness, it surpasses all our thought, it surpasses all our understanding: and yet we will be it. *Dearly beloved*, says John, *we are sons of God*[^29]; surely now by adoption, by faith, by pledge. We have received a pledge, brethren, the Holy Spirit. When does he deceive, who has given such a pledge? *We are sons of God*, he says, *and it has not yet appeared what we will be. We know*, he says, *that, when he appears, we will be like him, because we will see him as he is*[^30]. He said: *It has not yet appeared*, and that which *has not yet appeared* he did not say. *It has not yet appeared what we will be*. He would say: We will be that, and we will be such. Whatever he would say, to whom would he say it? I do not dare to say: Who would say it? but surely: To whom would he say it? And perhaps indeed there was one who would say it, because he it is who reclined on Christ's breast, and from his breast at that supper drank wisdom; with which wisdom fattened he burst forth: *In the beginning was the Word*[^31]. So this he said: *We know that, when there appears what we will be, we will be like him, because we will see him as he is*. Like whom? Surely like him whose sons we are. *Dearly beloved*, he says, *we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we will be. We know that, when he appears, we will be like him*, whose sons we are, *because we will see him as he is*. So now, if you wish to be like him to whom you will be like, if you wish to know to whom you will be like, attend to him, if you can. You cannot yet. So you do not know to whom you will be like, and therefore you do not know how like you will be. Not knowing, then, as yet what he is, you do not know what you too will be.

**10.** Meditating on these things, then, dearly beloved, let us always await our everlasting joy, and let us always pray for strength in temporal labors and temptations, whether we for you, or you for us. For do not think, brethren, that our prayers are necessary for you, and your prayers are not necessary for us; mutually our prayers for one another are necessary, because these very prayers for one another are kindled by love, and this sacrifice, as from the altar of piety, fragrantly rises most sweetly to the Lord. For if the apostles asked that prayer be made for them, how much more we, far inferior, yet in some way desiring to follow their footsteps, and able neither to know how far we follow, nor daring to say? So those men, such as they were, wished to be prayed for by the Church, and said: Because *we are your glory, as you also are ours, in the day of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^32]. Mutually they prayed for one another before the day of our Lord Jesus Christ, glory in the day, weakness before the day. Let prayer be made in weakness, that there may be rejoicing in glory. For at different times, yet there at one time, we shall all arrive; the times of departing hence are different, the time of receiving there is one. For once and together we shall be gathered, that we may receive what at different times we have both believed and desired; just as those laborers in the vineyard, some were led at the first hour, some at the third, some at the sixth, some at the ninth, some at the tenth[^33]. At different times they were called, but at one time the wage is paid to all. Turning to the Lord, and so forth.
