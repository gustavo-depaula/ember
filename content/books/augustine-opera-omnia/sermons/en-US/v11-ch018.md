# Sermon 306/E: (Dolbeau 18)

**1.** The foretold day of your love's solemnity of the crown of Quadratus has dawned today: let us celebrate it both by assembly and by sermon. Of the glory of the martyrs before God and men, then, we speak as often as we celebrate their solemnities. Let assiduous celebration be frequent exhortation, that there may be exultation. We sang a psalm fitting indeed for the feast of martyrs: *I was pushed as a heap of sand, that I might fall, and the Lord took me up*[^1]. For this the whole choir of martyrs says, this the body of Christ in the world, living like Lot in Sodom amid temptations, seeing many things it does not wish, but by no means consenting to the deeds of the wicked. For in diverse ways the human mind is driven to sin, either by the enticement of pleasure or by the bite of tribulation, and whoever, lest he sin through enticement, has conquered pleasure, has made much progress, yet has not yet achieved perfection. For the love of justice ought to be so great that pleasure yields to it, and that it itself does not yield to pain. Whoever has arrived at this has achieved perfection. Therefore the martyrs are believed to have gone out from the world perfect, because they conquered by contending not only against the pleasures of the world but also against tribulations. Finally, as the faithful know, among the Christian sacraments we do not pray for the martyrs. And not only do we not pray for them, but we commend ourselves to their prayers.

**2.** This perfection the epistle of Paul to the Hebrews demands by rebuking and exacts, where it is said: *For you have not yet resisted unto blood, striving against sin*[^2]. This perfection is demanded; he to whom it is said "You have not yet done it" is admonished to do it. *For you have not yet resisted unto blood, striving against sin.* But the martyrs strove unto blood. And they strove not against a persecuting man, but against the plotting devil, and—if you gather the truth—against their very own weakness. For in the man himself a great contest is waged, where the theater is the conscience; but the supreme spectator is the inspector of the conscience. But if he were only the inspector of the conscience and not also the helper, every striver would fail. Accordingly, in the very words we sang you have what I say: *As a heap of sand,* he says, *I was pushed, that I might fall.* Why, then, did you not fall? *And the Lord took me up.* As if confessing he said this of his weakness. As far as concerns me, he says, *I would fall,* but *the Lord took me up.* Be, then, in the Lord, remain in the Lord and, when temptation pushes you, you do not fall, because you do not yield. If by resisting you do not yield, by not consenting you do not fall.

**3.** Take a likeness from the very name of him whose solemnity we celebrate: he was called Quadratus. A squared stone, when you push it, yields and does not fall. It yields by not resisting, but does not fall by remaining on every side in uprightness. For the squared one is straight on every side: it is pushed on one side, received on another, it can never fall. Rightly too the Lord God commanded the ancient just man Noah to build the ark of squared timbers. He willed timbers to be applied to the structure both incorruptible and squared. Incorruptible on account of eternity, squared on account of avoiding temptation. Let us too be squared, indeed let the whole body of Christ be the Mass of the Squared one. For this has the cornerstone, as it was sung: *The stone,* he says, *which the builders rejected, this has been made the head of the corner*[^3]. How, then, was the stone which the builders rejected made the head of the corner? He wishes the Pharisees and scribes and doctors of the law to be understood. They rejected him as if he were not the one; they said: "He is not the one. We indeed await the Christ, but we do not acknowledge this one." By confessing that they awaited the Christ, they were as it were builders; but by saying that they did not acknowledge the very one who was the true one, they rejected the cornerstone, because they themselves were not on the rock but on the sand. Therefore those builders, by boasting rather than by doing it, rejected the cornerstone. But lest you wonder how a stone rejected by the builders was made the head of the corner, Scripture subjoined: *This was done by the Lord*[^4]. Rejected by those builders, but made the head of the corner by the Lord. From opposite sides walls ran into this corner: circumcision and uncircumcision. If you attend to whence they come, what is so diverse? If you attend to where they cohere, what is so one? For the walls coming from opposite sides make the corner, but cohering with one another in one place in the bond of peace. It was done: they came from the circumcision, they came from the uncircumcision, they were joined to one another in the cornerstone. It was fulfilled what the apostle says: *He himself is our peace, who made both one*[^5].

**4.** Accordingly, dearly beloved, striving toward the cornerstone, come squared, that the builder may not reject you. Yet hope wholly in God; he himself squares who joins together. *You,* says the apostle Peter, *like living stones, are built up into a holy temple of God*[^6]. We ought so to think of the temple of God that we likewise know that God does not need a temple, but the temple needs God, lest we suppose that we bestow a benefit on God, that he may have where to dwell. That God dwell in you profits you. By so dwelling in you he makes you blessed, he is not made blessed by the dwelling. For he is so the true lord, who does not need a servant, and yet has a servant whom he does not need, but for whom he provides. For human weakness needs a servant, that the servant may do some work which the master cannot, and in doing what he can may help the master who cannot. For God is omnipotent, he does not need you, who made you. For you do not merit anything because you carry the Lord, you who merited to be made by the Lord. Do not think you bestow on God, because you believe in God. For if you do not believe, you are harmful not to him, but to yourself. This voice is expressed in the psalm where it is said: *I said to the Lord: You are my God, for you have no need of my goods*[^7]. Therefore my Lord, the true God, the Lord, who has no need of my goods, but I of yours.

**5.** Let no one think himself fit to square himself, just as he was fit to deprave himself, because no one ought to think himself fit to heal himself, even if he was fit to wound himself. In the power of the eye is darkness, for it is closed and is in it. When you close it, you are in darkness. Therefore it was in your power to be in darkness by closing your eyes; but is it in your power to see, when you have opened your eyes, unless there be light in which you can see? The closing of your eyes had no need of help for not seeing, but the opening of the eyes has need of help for seeing. For unless light be present, of the sun or a lamp or the moon or any kind of light, unless it be present to the opening, your eyes, though open, see nothing. It is ours, then, to receive, it is ours to have. But *what do you have that you have not received?*[^8]

**6.** Therefore the pious voice *as a heap of sand,* not a heap of sand, but *as a heap of sand;* for the body of Christ, when it was made solid and squared, was thought by the blind to be a heap of sand, and because it was thought a heap of sand, therefore it did not fall. Nor yet did it attribute even this to its own strength, since it subjoined: *The Lord took me up.* In all these temptations of the world, then—for now Sodom has not yet burned, another indeed great Sodom, for that one burned for an example, the other is kept for judgment—in these temptations of the world, then, let us meditate to strive daily *against sin unto blood,* which by another saying is said: *Strive for the truth unto death*[^9]. What was there said *against sin,* this is said here *for the truth.* And what was there said *unto blood,* this is said here *unto death.* The very disposition of a martyr one ought to have, for God is not delighted by blood poured out: he has many martyrs in secret. *Peace on earth to men of good will*[^10].

**7.** Nor is that persecution to be wished for which our forebears suffered from earthly powers, that they might become martyrs. The world does not cease, the abundance of temptations does not cease. Sometimes a fever, and you strive. To pass over the various threats of adversaries, the various temptations of each individual, what I had begun to say: a fever, and you strive. You are in bed, and you are an athlete. You are sick, and you fight, and you conquer. For what if, to one with a fever and set in peril of death, there comes someone who promises by certain incantations to drive off your fever, and those incantations are unlawful, devilish, to be detested and anathematized, then by the one who urges this, and of those who were so healed, many examples are set before you, and it is said to you: "When that man had this, he did it for him, he chanted over him, he purified him, he was present to him, and he was made well. Ask him, question him, hear him." And let those too say: "Truly it was done, we were nearly dead, and so we were freed, and let it be a proof to you that, if you admit that incantation, you will at once be freed from that plague." If you do not consent, will you not be a martyr, you who chose rather to die and did not consent to the sacrilegious one? For what the unjust judge said to the martyr set in chains or on the rack: "Consent to sacrifice, and I release you from this tribulation," this the devil says in secret to the one with a fever: "Consent to this sacrilege, and I release you from this fever."

**8.** If, then, you do not consent, if you conquer not a man but the devil, not any peer of yours, a sinner, but the very prince of sinners—but *because your struggle is not against flesh and blood,* that is, against men from whom you suffer troubles whether of persuasions or of tribulations, *but against the principalities and powers and rulers of the world,* not of heaven and earth, but *of this darkness*[^11], that is, of the unclean and the wicked, whence to the freed the apostle says: *You were once darkness*[^12]—if, then, you do not consent, do not think yourself not a martyr. Your solemnity indeed is not celebrated, but your crown is prepared. For it is the custom that the solemnity of those is celebrated who strove publicly. How many martyrs have gone forth from their bed, and from that infirmity passed as victors to things above. Know, then, that you are tempted, if you suffer anything of the kind, if anything of the kind is urged upon you; there it is needful that you have the disposition of a martyr, since he who made you watches you, and he who called you helps you. There it is that you may say that voice of the saints which I found: "*God is able to free* me from this deadly fever, *but even if not*[^13]." Let the enchanter be your contest, because the one to whom you speak is not a deceiver. So after these words, what shall please your Lord about you will be done. Either you are restored alive, or you are joined to the angels. He will do what he chooses about you: be you prepared for both, if you wish to be squared.

**9.** For do you think those words of the psalm which you just now heard, *Chastening, the Lord has chastened me, and has not handed me over to death*[^14], are only the voice of Daniel, who closed the mouths of the lions? Do you think this voice is only that of the three boys, who by faith tested the flames among which they walked? Do you think this voice is only theirs: *Chastening, the Lord has chastened me, and has not handed me over to death,* because the lions did not devour him, nor the fires burn them? Far from it! Do not think this, this voice is not only theirs: it is also the Maccabees'. For the God of these is not another, of those another, or present to these, absent from these, or to these a helper and to those angry. He himself is the same God, able to free in both ways, that he may show that he has all in his power. By closing the mouths of the lions, he chastened Daniel in humiliation, but did not hand him over to death. By cooling the fires, he chastened the three boys in humiliation, but did not hand them over to death. By making the Maccabees victors in the fire, who would not yield to torments, but would strive unto blood against sin, chastening he chastened them, and did not hand them over to death. For they all live. To what death did he not hand them, I say? The devil wished to hand them over to the second death, the eternal death, not the transitory one. Those, then, he openly freed, those he secretly crowned, neither these nor those did he hand over to death.

**10.** Prepared, then, we ought always to be for both, I have said. For God is able to free from all tribulation. But above all we ought to pray that he free us from tribulation, that we be victors over tribulation, that we not yield to tribulation so as to sin. Nor think yourself a martyr only if it be said to you: "Deny Christ," and you do not deny. When you think to do something against justice, nothing else is said to you than: "Deny Christ." Surely the great martyr John the Baptist was beheaded, and yet it was not said to him: "Deny Christ." He was speaking the truth to a bad king: the angry king ordered him shut up, delighted he ordered him killed. For there was dancing that he might be killed: she danced, he fell. For he rather fell, by killing the just one. For not the one killed fell, but the one who ordered the killing. And yet it was not said to him: "Deny Christ," but because he died for the truth, he died for Christ, who said: *I am the way, the truth, and the life*[^15].

**11.** And he who says to you: "Bear false witness for me," says nothing else to you than: "Deny Christ." For if you confess Christ with your tongue and bear false witness, there comes about in you what Paul says: *For they confess that they know God, but by their deeds they deny him*[^16]. You confessed by word, you denied by deed. God judges more the one who denies by deed than the one who confesses by word. For deeds are more than words. To do and to speak is good, but not to speak and not to do. Therefore you think yourself a martyr, if you strive. "Bear false witness for me." "I do not." "Just take this and say it." "I do not take it." You have conquered greed. If you also conquer fear, you have striven unto blood against sin. He who did not entice you by a reward will try to break you by threats, setting forth that he will display enmities, will harm more grievously and, when he has power, will kill—and perhaps he already has it—what do you do? He does not say: "Deny Christ," yet he says: "Do against Christ," that is, against the one who said: *You shall not bear false witness*[^17]. How many such temptations daily, not against all, as it were against the whole Church, as if a heap of sand were pushed, which is thought a heap of sand, but very often the enemy fights against single grains, sometimes tempts one by one. What does it matter whether he tempts one by one, or compels universally? The tempter is not lacking, but let the wrestler not be lacking either, because the crowner will not be lacking.
