# Sermon 94/A: On the Martyrdom of John the Baptist and on the Persecution That Must Be Borne by Christians Even in a Time of Peace

**1.** Concerning the present chapter, dearest brothers, of the holy Gospel, which the Lord willed to teach us, the Church of Christ surely does not doubt that John is a martyr. And before the Lord's passion he earned martyrdom; born before, suffering before, yet not the author of salvation, but the forerunner of the judge. For he went before the Lord, taking on himself a humble service, giving height to the heavenly Master. Whence do we say that John is a martyr? Was he seized by persecutors of Christians, led forth, questioned, and, having confessed Christ, did he suffer? For in this way the other martyrs after Christ's passion can be so called. Whence, then, is this man a martyr? Because he was beheaded? For it is not the punishment that makes a martyr, but the cause. Because he offended a powerful woman? Whence did he offend? on what matter did he offend? By telling the truth to the king, made her husband, that it was not lawful for him to have his brother's wife[^1]. The truth earned hatred, and by earning hatred came to suffering and a crown. These are the fruits of the age to come. In a word, self-indulgence dances, and innocence is condemned; but it is condemned by men, crowned by almighty God.

**2.** Let no one, then, say: I cannot be a martyr, because there is no persecution against Christians. You hear that John bore martyrdom; and, if you consider the truth, he died for Christ. How, you say, for Christ, who was not questioned about Christ, nor forced to deny Christ? Hear Christ himself saying: *I am the way, and the truth, and the life*[^2]. If Christ is the truth, whoever is condemned for the truth suffers for Christ, and is lawfully crowned. Let no one, then, excuse himself: all times lie open to martyrs. Nor let anyone say that persecution does not happen to Christians. The sentence of Paul the apostle cannot be made void, because it is true; Christ spoke through him, he did not lie. For he says: *All who want to live devoutly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution*[^3]. *All,* he says: he excluded no one, he separated no one. If you want to prove true what he said, begin to live devoutly in Christ, and you will see that what he says is true. Is it that, because persecution by earthly kings has died down, therefore the devil does not rage? That ancient enemy always watches against us: let us not sleep. He suggests enticements, ambushes, sends in evil thoughts; that he may provoke us to a worse fall, he sets out gains, threatens losses. It comes to the crucial point, and with labor the evil of his suggestion is rejected, so that present death is willingly undergone. Understand, brothers. If someone forces you, for example some nobleman, who has your blood in his power, forces you to bear false witness, and does not say to you: Deny Christ—what do you think, to choose falsehood, or to die for the truth? And yet the persecutor says nothing else to you than: Deny Christ. For if Christ is the truth, as we have already said, surely he denies Christ who denies the truth. But everyone denies the truth who speaks a lie. He himself who bears false witness, whence does he do so? Because he fears, surely. Is there not persecution for all Christians, when they contend for the truth? Now individuals are tested, and in his own case each one is tempted.

**3.** But what was your enemy going to do to you—threatening slaughter, thirsting for blood, puffed up with power, lifted up like smoke—what was he going to do to you, he who was driving you to perjury and false witness, what was he going to do? Weakness answers: He would kill me, it says. He would not kill you. Indeed I know, he would kill me. If it is so, I too will answer: You, brother, do the killing, who bear false witness. But he would kill—your body: what would he do to your soul? The house would be thrown down, the inhabitant crowned. Behold what your enemy was going to do to you, if you had stood in the truth and not borne false witness: he would surely kill, but the body, not the soul. Hear your Lord giving security with care: *Do not,* he says, *fear those who kill the body, and after that have nothing they can do; but fear him who has power to kill body and soul, and to send into hell. Yes, I say to you, fear him*[^4]. Him John feared: he refused to keep silent about the truth, and bore the iniquity of evil men; he earned the king's hatred through an unchaste woman, and came to martyrdom.

**4.** For all who want to live devoutly in Christ are shaken by such persecution. For they suffer persecution for the sake of worldly gain, or for fear of loss; either for the present life, or for the threat of death; because this world is not without persecution. But it must be discerned who suffers for what; and they will be true martyrs, if they contend for the truth, which is Christ, and they are lawfully crowned. For he who suffers persecution for the sake of this world, which lies in evil, can sustain a temporal punishment.

**5.** Therefore, brothers, since we have learned this in the present reading, to fight for the truth even unto death, and not to bear false witness, not to perjure ourselves, to hold to justice in dangers—for it is no great thing to hold to justice in security or in delights—let us always reckon that the devil, our tempter and persecutor, is on the watch against us; and in the name and with the help of the Lord our God let us watch more fervently against him, lest in anything he overcome us through cupidity, by which he is wont to tempt: because the one whom cupidity and fear do not conquer—which are the weapons of the enemy, by which men who have their hope in this world are entangled in various snares, so that they cannot prevail to hold the truth—the devil, in a word, has two doors through which he knocks to enter; first through cupidity, then through fear. If he finds both doors shut in the faithful, he passes by. And what is cupidity, you say, what is fear? Hear what it is, so that you may not covet what passes away, and not fear what fails and perishes in time; and then at last he will not find a nest where the enemy may dwell; because a contest is set before us unto the end—not only for us, who stand or sit in this higher place and speak to you, but in all the members of Christ a contest is set.

**6.** For this reason, to this day in Numidia it is the custom to adjure the servants of God thus: May you conquer. You see that not without reason such a swearing is said to belong to one who fights. For here too, where we speak, at Carthage, and in all the proconsular province and Byzacena, but also in Tripoli, this is the customary saying by which the servants of God adjure one another: By your crown. Which crown no one will receive, unless he conquers. And I too adjure you by your crown, that you fight against the devil with your whole heart; and, if we conquer together, together we are also crowned. Why do you say to us: By your crown, and yet act badly, live badly? Live well, act well, both outside and within have a good manner of life, and you will be our crown. The people of God, which you are, the Apostle was instructing, when he says: *My joy and my crown, stand in the Lord*[^5]. But if the prosperity of the times smiles, stand in the Lord; but if the adversity of the times roars, be steadfast in the Lord. Do not fall from him who always stands, and standing awaits the one fighting, and helps you, that by standing and fighting you may conquer, and at last come to him to be crowned.
