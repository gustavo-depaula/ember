# Sermon 128

**1.** We have heard the words of the holy Gospel: and this can stir someone, that the Lord Jesus says: *If I bear witness about myself, my witness is not true* [^1]. How, then, is the witness of the truth not true? Is he not the one who said: *I am the way, and the truth, and the life* [^2]? Whom, then, must one believe, if the truth must not be believed? For surely he does not want to believe except falsehood, who does not choose to believe the truth. This, then, was said according to them, so that you may understand it thus, and from these words conceive this sense: *If I bear witness about myself, my witness is not true*, that is, as you suppose. For he knew that his witness about himself was true; but on account of the weak, on account of the unbelieving, on account of those not understanding, the sun sought lamps. For their bleariness could not bear the brightness of the sun.  
Therefore John was sought, to bear witness to the truth; and you have heard what he says: *You went to John. He was a lamp burning and shining, and you wanted to exult for an hour in his light* [^3]. That lamp was prepared for their confounding, because of this it was said so long a time before in the Psalms: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ*. Why a lamp for the sun? *His enemies I will clothe with confusion: but upon him will flourish my sanctification* [^4]. In short, here they were confounded in a certain place through that very John, when the Jews said to the Lord: *By what power do you do these things?* Tell us. To whom he answered: *Tell me, you too: The baptism of John, is it from heaven, or from men?* They heard, and were silent. For they quickly thought within themselves: *If we say: From men, the people will stone us; because they hold John a prophet. If we say: From heaven, he will say to us: Why then did you not believe him?* Because John bore witness to Christ. Cramped in their hearts by their own questions, and ensnared in their own snares, they answered: *We do not know*. What other voice could be that of darkness? It is right indeed for a man, when he does not know, to say: I do not know. But when he knows, and says: I do not know, he is a witness against himself. Surely they knew the excellence of John, and that his baptism was from heaven; but they did not want to yield to him to whom John bore witness. But when they said: *We do not know*, Jesus answered them: *Neither will I tell you by what power I do these things* [^5]. And they were confounded, and it was fulfilled: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ; his enemies I will clothe with confusion*.

**2.** Are not the martyrs witnesses of Christ and do they not bear witness to the truth? But if we think more carefully, when those martyrs bear witness, he himself bears witness to himself. For he himself dwells in the martyrs, that they may bear witness to the truth. Hear one of the martyrs, the apostle Paul himself: *Or do you want to receive proof of him who speaks in me, Christ* [^6]? So while John bears witness, Christ bears witness to himself, who dwells in John. Let Peter bear witness, let Paul bear witness, let the other Apostles bear witness, let Stephen bear witness; he himself bears witness to himself, who dwells in all. For he without them is God, but they without him, what are they?  
Of him it was said: *He ascended on high, he led captivity captive, he gave gifts to men* [^7]. What is: *he led captivity captive?* He conquered death. What is: *he led captivity captive?* The devil procured death, and the devil himself was led captive by the death of Christ. *He ascended on high*. What do we know higher than heaven? Plainly and before the eyes of his disciples he ascended into heaven [^8]. This we know, this we believe, this we confess. *He gave gifts to men*. What gifts? The Holy Spirit. He who gives such a gift, what kind is he himself? For great is the mercy of God; he gives a gift equal to himself, because his gift is the Holy Spirit, and the whole Trinity is one God: the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. What did the Holy Spirit bestow on us? Hear the Apostle: *The love of God*, he says, *has been poured out in our hearts*. Whence, O beggar, has *the love of God been poured out* in your heart? By what, or in what, has the love of God been poured out in the human heart? *We have*, he says, *this treasure in earthen vessels*. Why *in earthen vessels?* *That the eminence of the power may be God's* [^9]. In short, when he had said: *The love of God has been poured out in our hearts*; lest anyone think that it was from himself that he loves God, he at once added: *through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us* [^10]. Therefore, that you may love God, let God dwell in you, and love himself from you; that is, let him move you to his love, kindle you, enlighten you, rouse you.

**3.** For there is a struggle in this body: as long as we live, we fight; as long as we fight, we are in peril; but in all these things we overcome, through him who loved us [^11]. Our battle you have heard, just now when the Apostle was read: *The whole*, he says, *Law is fulfilled in one saying, in that which is: You shall love your neighbor as yourself* [^12]. This love is from the Holy Spirit. *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*. First see whether you yet know how to love yourself; and I entrust to you a neighbor, whom you may love as yourself. But if you do not yet know how to love yourself; I fear lest you deceive your neighbor as yourself. For if you love iniquity, you do not love yourself. The Psalm is witness: *But he who loves iniquity hates his own soul* [^13]. But if you hate your soul, what does it profit you that you love your flesh? If you hate your soul, and love your flesh, your flesh will rise; but that your soul may be tortured. Therefore the soul must first be loved, which must be subjected to God, that this servitude may keep its order, the soul to God, the flesh to the soul. Do you want your flesh to serve your soul? Let your soul serve God. You must be ruled, that you may be able to rule. For this struggle is so perilous, that if the ruler lets go, ruin follows.

**4.** What struggle? *But if you bite and devour one another, see that you be not consumed by one another. But I say: Walk in the Spirit*. I speak the Apostle's words, which were just now recited from his Letter: *But I say, Walk in the Spirit and do not fulfill the desires of the flesh. But I say: Walk in the Spirit, and the desires of the flesh*; he did not say: Do not have them; nor did he say this: Do not do them; but: *do not fulfill them* [^14]. But what this is, with the Lord helping, I will say as I can; be present, that you may understand, if you walk in the Spirit. *But I say: Walk in the Spirit, and do not fulfill the desires of the flesh*. Let what follows come: in case something, so that what here is obscure may more easily be understood in his following words. For I said, not in vain did the Apostle refuse to say: Do not have the desires of the flesh; nor even did he want to say: Do not do the desires of the flesh; but he said: *Do not fulfill the desires of the flesh*. He set before us the very struggle. In this battle we are engaged, if we serve God as soldiers. What, then, follows? *For the flesh desires against the spirit, but the spirit against the flesh. For these things are contrary to one another, that you may not do those things that you want* [^15]. If this is not understood, it is most perilously heard. Therefore anxious lest men perish by ill understanding, I have undertaken to expound these words of the Apostle, with the Lord helping, to Your Charity. We have leisure, we began in the morning, the hour of dinner does not press; on this day, that is, the Sabbath, especially those are wont to assemble who hunger for the Word of God. Hear and attend; I will say it as diligently as I can.

**5.** What is this, then, that I said: It is heard perilously, if it is not understood? Many, conquered by carnal and damnable desires, commit all kinds of misdeeds and shameful acts, and wallow in such most wicked uncleannesses, which it is shameful even to say; and they say to themselves these words of the Apostle. See what the Apostle said: *That you may not do those things that you want*. I do not want to do it, I am forced, compelled, conquered, I do what I do not want [^16], as the Apostle says: *Because the flesh desires against the spirit, but the spirit against the flesh, that you may not do those things that you want* [^17]. You see how perilously it is heard, if it is not understood. You see how it pertains to the office of the shepherd to open covered fountains, and to minister pure, harmless water to the thirsting sheep.  
Do not, then, be conquered, when you fight. See what kind of war he set forth, what kind of battle, what kind of quarrel, within, within yourself. *The flesh desires against the spirit*. If the spirit too does not desire against the flesh, commit adultery. But if the spirit desires against the flesh, I see a struggle, I do not see one conquered, there is a battle. *The flesh desires against the spirit*: adultery delights. I confess that it delights. But *the spirit desires against the flesh*: chastity too delights. Therefore let the spirit conquer the flesh: or at least let it not be conquered by the flesh. Adultery seeks darkness, chastity desires light. As you want to be known, so live; as you want to be known to men, even apart from the eyes of men so live; since he who made you sees you even in the darkness. Why is chastity praised publicly by men? Why do not even adulterers praise adultery? He who seeks the truth, then, comes to the light [^18].

**6.** But adultery delights. Let it be contradicted, resisted, fought against. For you do not lack that by which you may fight. Your God is in you, the good Spirit has been given to you. And yet the flesh itself is allowed to desire against the spirit, by depraved suggestions and inborn delights. Let what the Apostle says come to pass: *Let not sin reign in your mortal body* [^19]. He did not say: Let it not be. It is already there. And it is for this reason called sin, because it befell by the desert of sin. For in paradise the flesh did not desire against the spirit, nor was that battle there, where peace alone was; but transgression having been made, after man refused to serve God, and was given over to himself; nor so given over to himself that he could even possess himself; but possessed by him by whom he was deceived; the flesh began to desire against the spirit. And it desires against the spirit in the good: for in the evil it has nothing against which to desire. For it desires against the spirit there, where the spirit is.  
For when he says: *The flesh desires against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh*, do not think this was given only to the spirit of man. It is the Spirit of God who fights in you against you, against that which is in you against you. For you refused to stand before the Lord; you fell, you were broken; just as a vessel when it falls from the hand of a man to the ground, you were broken. And because you were broken, therefore you are adverse to yourself, therefore you are against yourself. Let there be nothing in you against you, and you will stand whole.

**7.** For that you may know that this office pertains to the Holy Spirit, in another place the Apostle says: *For if you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you will live* [^20]. In these words man already was lifting himself up, as though by his own spirit he could mortify the deeds of the flesh. *If you live according to the flesh, you will die; but if by the spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you will live*. Expound to us, Apostle, by what spirit. For man too has a spirit pertaining to his proper nature, by which he is man. For man consists of body and spirit. And of that very spirit of man it was said: *No one knows the things of man, except the spirit of man which is in him* [^21]. I see, then, that man himself too has his spirit pertaining to his nature, and I hear you saying: *But if by the spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you will live*. I ask by what spirit: mine, or God's? For I hear your words, and am still moved by ambiguity. For when spirit is said, sometimes it is of man, and the spirit of a beast is also called spirit; as it is written, that through the flood all flesh died, which had in it the spirit of life [^22]. And by this both the spirit of a beast is called spirit, and the spirit of man is called spirit. Sometimes even the wind is called spirit; as it is in the Psalm: *Fire, hail, snow, ice, the spirit of the storm* [^23]. Since, then, spirit is said in many ways, by what spirit did you say, O Apostle, that the deeds of the flesh are to be mortified? mine, or God's? Hear what follows, and understand. The question is removed by the following words. For when he had said: *But if by the spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you will live*; he at once added: *For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God* [^24]. You act, if you are led; and you act well, if you are led by the Good. Therefore what he said to you: *If by the spirit you mortify the deeds of the flesh, you will live*; and it was ambiguous to you of what spirit he had spoken, in the following words understand the teacher, recognize the Redeemer. For that Redeemer gave you the Spirit, by which you may mortify the deeds of the flesh. *For as many as are led by the Spirit of God, these are sons of God*. They are not sons of God, if they are not led by the Spirit of God. But if they are led by the Spirit of God, they fight; because they have a great helper. For God does not so watch us fighting, as the people watch hunters. The people can favor the hunter, they cannot help him in peril.

**8.** So then here too: *The flesh desires against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh*. And what is: *That you may not do those things that you want* [^25]? For here is the peril of the ill understander. Let now there be the office of whatever sort of expounder. *That you may not do those things that you want*. Attend, all you holy ones who fight. I speak to those doing battle. Those who fight understand: he who does not fight does not understand me. For he who fights, I do not say understands me, but anticipates me. What does a chaste man want? That no desire at all opposed to chastity rise in his members. He wants peace, but does not yet have it. For when it comes to that, where no desire at all to be opposed rises, there will be no enemy with whom we may struggle; nor is victory awaited there, because over an enemy already conquered there is triumph. Hear that very victory, with the Apostle himself saying: *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal put on immortality. But when this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality; then will come to pass the saying that is written: Death is swallowed up in victory*. Hear the voices of those triumphing: *Where is, O death, your contention? Where is, O death, your sting* [^26]? You struck, you wounded, you cast down; but he who made me was wounded for me. O death, O death! He who made me was wounded for me, and by his death conquered you. And then those triumphing will say: *Where is, O death, your contention? Where is, O death, your sting?*

**9.** But now, when the flesh desires against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh, there is contention with death: we do not do what we want. Why? Because we want there to be no desires, but we cannot. Willy-nilly, we have them; willy-nilly, they tickle, flatter, prick, infest, want to rise. They are pressed down, not yet extinguished; as long as *the flesh desires against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh*. Is this so even when a man is dead? Far be it. You lay down the flesh, how do you drag the desires of the flesh with you? But if you have fought well, you are received unto rest. From which rest you are to be crowned, not condemned; that afterward you may be led to the kingdom. Therefore, as long as one lives here, brothers, it is so; so we too who have grown old in this warfare, have indeed lesser enemies: but yet we have them. Our enemies are in a way wearied now even by age; but yet, wearied, they do not cease to infest the quiet of old age with whatever sort of stirrings. The battle of the young is sharper: we know it, we passed through it. *The flesh*, then, *desires against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh; that you may not do those things that you want* [^27]. For what do you want, O holy ones, O good warriors, O strong soldiers of Christ? what do you want? That there be no evil desires at all. But you cannot. Wage the war, hope for the triumph. For now meanwhile there is fighting. *The flesh desires against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh; that you may not do those things that you want*; that is, that there be no desires of the flesh at all.

**10.** But do what you can; what the Apostle himself says in another place, which I had already begun to recall: *Let not sin reign in your mortal body, to obey its desires*. Behold what I do not want; evil desires rise, but do not obey. Arm yourself, take the instruments of wars. The precepts of God are your arms. If you hear me well, even from what I say you are armed. *Let not*, he says, *sin reign in your mortal body*. For as long as you bear a mortal body, sin fights against you; but *let it not reign*. What is: *Let it not reign?* That is, *to obey its desires*. If you begin to obey, it reigns. And what is to obey, except that *you offer your members as weapons of iniquity to sin* [^28]? Nothing is more glorious than this teacher. What do you want me now to expound to you? Do what you have heard. Do not offer your members as weapons of iniquity to sin. God gave you power through his Spirit, to hold your members. Lust rises, hold your members; what is the one which has risen going to do? Hold your members: do not offer your members as weapons of iniquity to sin; do not arm your adversary against you. Hold your feet, lest they go to unlawful things. Lust has risen, hold your members; hold your hands from all crime; hold your eyes, lest they attend badly; hold your ears, lest they gladly hear words of lust; hold the whole body, hold the sides, hold the upper parts, hold the lower. What does lust do? It knows how to rise, it does not know how to conquer. By rising constantly without cause, it learns even not to rise.

**11.** Let us return, then, to the words which I had set forth as obscure from the Apostle, and we shall now see them to be plain. For I had set forth this, that the Apostle did not say: Walk in the Spirit, and do not have the desires of the flesh; because it is necessary that we have them. Why then did he not say: Do not do the desires of the flesh? Because we do them; for we desire. The very desiring is doing. But the Apostle says: *Now it is no longer I that work it, but the sin that dwells in me* [^29]. Therefore what must you beware of? This without doubt, that you not fulfill it. A damnable lust rose, it rose, it suggested: let it not be heard. It burns, it does not restrain itself, and you would wish that it not burn. And where is: *That you may not do those things that you want?* Do not give your members. Let it burn without cause, and it consumes itself. In you, then, those very desires come to be. It must be confessed, they come to be. Therefore he said: *Do not fulfill them*. But let them not be fulfilled. You decided to do it, you fulfilled it. For you have fulfilled it, if you decide that adultery is to be committed, and therefore do not do it because a place was not found, because opportunity is not given, because perhaps she is chaste about whom you seem to be stirred; behold, now she is chaste, and you are an adulterer. Why? Because you fulfilled the desires. What is: you fulfilled them? In your mind you decided that adultery was to be committed. Now, far be it, if your members too were employed, you have rolled down into death.

**12.** Christ raised up the dead daughter of the ruler of the synagogue in the house [^30]. She was in the house, she had not yet been carried out. So is a man who has decided on a shameful deed in the heart; he is dead, but lies within. But if he comes even to the perpetration by the members, he is carried out. But the Lord raised up also the young man, the widow's son, when, dead, he was being carried out beyond the gate of the city [^31]. So then I dare to say something: You decided in your heart, if you call yourself back from your act, you will be healed before you perpetrate it. For if you do penance in your heart, because you had decided on an evil and wicked and shameful and damnable thing; there where you lay dead within, so within you have risen. But if you have fulfilled it, now you are carried out, but you have one to say to you: *Young man, I say to you, arise* [^32]! Even if you have perpetrated it, repent of it, return soon; do not come into the tomb. But here too I have a third dead man, who was even led to the tomb. Now he has above him the weight of habit, an earthly mass presses him much. For he has been much exercised in shameful deeds, he is weighed down by his excessive habit. Christ too cries out: *Lazarus, come forth* [^33]! For the man of most wicked habit now stinks. Rightly there Christ cried out; nor only cried out, but *cried out with a loud voice* [^34]. For at the cry of Christ even such men, though dead, though buried, though stinking, yet they too will rise; they will rise. For of no one lying low must one despair under such a raiser.
