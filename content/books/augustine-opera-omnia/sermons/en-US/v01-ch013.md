# Sermon 12: A Treatise Against the Manichees

On What Is Written in Job:

"And Behold, the Angels Came Into the Sight of God

And the Devil in the Midst of Them" and So on

And on What Is in the Gospel:

"Blessed Are the Pure in Heart, for They Shall See God"

**1.** That the Manichees, with most fraudulent deceit, lie in wait against the divine and holy old Books, we trust, dearly beloved brothers, has already been sufficiently proved to your prudence. Yet we still set their deceits before the eyes of your heart to be inspected, so that you may not only avoid them as far as concerns yourselves, but also teach others, the weak and the unlearned in the divine readings, as each of you can, to avoid and despise them. "In Job it is written," they say, "*Behold, the angels came into the sight of God, and the devil in the midst of them. And God said to the devil: Whence do you come? And he, answering, said: I have come from going around the whole world*[^1]." "Here," they say, "it is shown that the devil not only saw God, but even spoke with him. But in the Gospel he says, *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^2]. And again he says, *I am the door; no one can come to the Father except through me*[^3]." Then they add a reasoning, saying, "If, then, those alone who are pure in heart see God, how could the devil, with a most filthy and most unclean heart, see God? Or how does he enter through the door, that is, through Christ? Again," they say, "the Apostle attests and confirms, saying, that neither Principalities nor Powers nor Virtues knew God."

**2.** Their slander indeed is set forth up to this point in altogether these words, and it is truly a question to be discussed by a Christian. But the mind of those proposing that slander aims, in like manner, to twist the unlearned away from the most salutary authority of the Scriptures into believing them. But first I would wish to ask these people where Adimantus read in the Apostle—for he is the writer of such slanders—I would wish, then, that he say where he read the Apostle attesting and confirming, as he says, that neither Principalities nor Powers nor Virtues knew God, when the Lord says that even the angels of the men who believe in him daily see the face of the Father. Unless perhaps that which the apostle Paul says, *We speak wisdom among the perfect, but a wisdom not of this world, nor of the princes of this world, who are brought to nothing. But we speak the wisdom of God in a mystery, which is hidden, which God predetermined before the ages for our glory, which none of the princes of this world knew. For if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*[^4]? If he was thinking of writing down this passage, why did he add Powers and Virtues, which is not said there, and take away *of this world*, which is said? But would that he had done this through error rather than malice. Nevertheless, even if the Apostle had spoken in this way, could the devil not on that account hear the voice of God? For it is written that he came into the sight of God; it is not written that he himself beheld God. For the princes of this world are understood either as proud men, lifted up by the vain pomp of boasting, or as the devil himself and his angels. For the Lord most plainly calls him the prince or magistrate of this world, because by the name of this world sinners are understood, whose hope is nothing except in this world. For just as a house is called bad when its inhabitants are meant, so we call this world bad when we mean those who inhabit this world in their heart, that is, whose conversation is not in heaven. *For our conversation,* says the Apostle, *is in heaven*[^5]. But all sins serve the devil, who by free choice wished to be the prince of sin: therefore he is called the prince of this world. This rule of understanding I urge you to fix in your hearts. By it the Lord will help you to discuss and resolve many things of the Scriptures, with which they bait the snares of their error.

**3.** Since, then, it is not written that the devil saw God, but only that he came with the angels into the sight of the Lord and heard his voice, why do these wretches strive to slander the Scriptures and pervert the unlearned about the vision of God? Wherefore this proposition of theirs is overcome by a very brief reply. For however much, with whatever loquacity, they inquire how the devil saw God, we answer: The devil did not see God. They will say, "How, then, did he speak with him?" Here indeed the blindness of their heart is to be convicted not by us but by blind men. For those who are blind in the eyes of the flesh can daily speak with those whom they cannot see. "How, then," they say, "did he come into his sight?" As a blind man into the sight of one who sees, whom he himself does not behold. And these likenesses indeed, dearly beloved brothers, are said for this, that the wickedness of fleshly men may be refuted, so that, if it can be, repelled in this way, they may turn their hearts to the gentleness of learning. For is God contained in a place, who is present to every angelic and human conscience, not only of the good but also of the wicked? But there is this difference, that he is present to good consciences as a Father, to wicked ones as a judge, since it is written, *The Lord questions the just and the ungodly*[^6]. Likewise it is written, *In the thoughts of the ungodly there shall be questioning*[^7]. Nor does God question more vehemently in the ears of the body than in the secret of thought, where he alone hears, he alone is heard. Do not even wicked men, when sometimes they speak the truth and are not believed, swear and say, "God is witness," and say it most truly? Where, I ask, is he witness? On the tongue, or in the heart? In the sound of the voice, or in the silence of conscience? But why do they generally fret because they are not believed, when they know they speak the truth, except because they cannot open their heart to us, where God is witness?

**4.** Now there are many ways by which God speaks with us. He speaks sometimes through some instrument, as through the codex of the divine Scriptures. He speaks through some element of the world, as he spoke to the Magi through a star. For what is speech, except the signification of a will? He speaks through a lot, as he spoke concerning Matthias to be ordained in the place of Judas. He speaks through a human soul, as through a prophet. He speaks through an angel, as we receive that he spoke to certain of the Patriarchs and Prophets and Apostles. He speaks through some vocal and sounding creature, as we read and hold that voices were made from heaven, when no one was seen with the eyes. Finally, to the man himself, not outwardly through his ears or eyes, but inwardly in the mind, God speaks not in one way, but either in dreams, as it was shown to Laban the Syrian that he should not harm Jacob his servant in anything, and to Pharaoh about the seven plentiful years and as many barren ones; or with the spirit of the man taken up, which the Greeks call "ecstasy," as to Peter praying a vessel full of likenesses of the gentiles who would believe seemed to be let down from heaven; or in the mind itself, when anyone understands the majesty or the will, as Peter himself, from that very vision, by thinking within himself, came to know what the Lord wished him to do. For no one can do this, except by recognizing it within himself, with a certain silent cry of truth sounding. God speaks also in the conscience of the good and the wicked. For no one can rightly either approve what he does well or disapprove what he sins, except by the voice of truth either praising or crying out to those same things in the silence of the heart. But truth is God: who, since he speaks to men both good and wicked in so many ways—although not all to whom he speaks in so many ways can behold his substance and nature—what man can, by conjecturing or thinking, gather how many and by what ways the same Truth speaks to the angels, whether to the good who enjoy his ineffable form and beauty by contemplating it through wondrous love, or to the wicked who, depraved through their pride and ranked by Truth itself in the lower regions, can hear his voice by certain hidden ways, although they are not worthy to see his face?

**5.** Wherefore, dearly beloved brothers, faithful of God and most genuine sons of our Catholic mother, let no one deceive you with poisoned foods, even if you are still to be nursed with milk. Walk perseveringly now through faith in the truth, that at the certain and opportune time you may be able to come to the sight of that same Truth. For as the Apostle says, *While we remain here in the body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord; for we walk by faith, not by sight*[^8]. But to the sight of the vision of the Father the Christian faith leads. Whence the Lord says, *No one comes to the Father except through me*[^9]. In vain, then, do these people ask how the devil could come to God through Christ. For the devil cannot arrive at that blessedness of contemplation, to which the Christian faith leads *those who are pure in heart*[^10]. Nor yet on that account could the devil not hear the voice of God speaking, as many men too who did not believe in Christ were able to hear the voice from heaven of God saying, *I have both glorified it and will glorify it*[^11], when the Lord had said, *Father, glorify your Son*[^12].

**6.** But that it is written that the devil came into the sight of God, it is not written for this, that anyone can ever flee the sight of God, to whose eyes all things are subjected, and to whom the depth of any heart lies open, but because in the secret of the creature were done the things which Scripture narrated, therefore it is written, *And behold, the angels came into the sight of God*[^13], although they never depart from the sight of God. For wherever they are sent, there too the sight of God is present. But that is properly called the sight of God which human sight cannot penetrate, as are the secrets of conscience. Therefore, when we refute a liar, we do not say that he spoke in the sight of God, because he did not speak that which God beholds in his mind, to which a man cannot direct his sight. Because, then, these things were done so hiddenly that they could not be indicated to men through the holy Scriptures except by the revealing of the Holy Spirit, it is narrated that one came into the sight of God, and that there these things were done.

**7.** But that the devil was in the midst of the angels, if you understand good angels, so understand the devil in the midst of them as a defendant stands in the midst of the officers to be heard by the judge. For Scripture does not declare what kind of angels those were. But if in the midst of evil angels, what wonder is it that the prince and leader is surrounded by a throng of his ministers? But if you take what was said, "into the sight of God," so that those come into the sight of God who are not only beheld by him but also behold him, then it is to be understood that the devil was in the midst of them in such a way that he nevertheless did not see the God whom they themselves saw, so that God even spoke to the devil through some one of the holy angels. Nor yet is it written in the book except, *God said*. As also in public affairs, although the judge speaks most things through a herald, yet the judge's name, when the proceedings are written, is inserted, not also the herald's. But as some man unworthy of prophetic vision can nevertheless stand in the midst of the Prophets, so as only to hear what the Lord says through them, nor yet see what they see, so the devil too could be in the midst of the holy angels who see God, through whom he might hear the voice of God, whom he himself could not see.

**8.** And the contrivances of the Manichees, as far as pertains to this question, you perceive to have been resolved in many ways, so that you no longer think, dearest brothers, that the devil truly so spoke with God that he could even see the face of truth which pure hearts behold, or that he came to that contemplation of blessedness, to which no one is permitted to come except through the Lord Jesus Christ. But yet I greatly marvel at the shamelessness of these people, who wish to slander us about the vision of the divine substance, and lie that what is not written in our Scriptures, that the devil saw God, and try hence to establish such great ill-will, that whoever shudders and judges it unworthy that the devil should have seen God may be utterly turned away from the authority of the divine Scriptures by a suspicious ignorance not understanding what is written, when they themselves do not deny that our Lord Jesus Christ is God, and yet pretend that he appeared to men without the assumption of a human body.

**9.** When, then, the devil dared to tempt the Lord, since he saw him, what did he see? If he saw his body, then the Lord had a body, which the lost ones are unwilling to confess. But if he did not have a body, the divine substance itself lay open to the eyes of the devil. Which, if none see it except those who are pure in heart, as they themselves remind us from the Gospel, O the importunate blindness of the heretics! Why do you mendaciously charge our Scriptures, that the devil saw God, and, by denying the body of Christ, are convicted of wishing to publish his divine substance to the eyes of the devil? Or perhaps, as they are accustomed to say, did he so not have a human body, that he nevertheless showed himself as though having one? Who, then, thinks more truly and more rightly, you madmen—he who believes that God spoke with the devil, or he who believes that God not only spoke with the devil, but even lied to the devil? For Scripture recalls that certain angels appeared to human eyes. But certainly the Lord so subjected the corporeal creature to their power that he fitted it to them according to their will. Whence they too, although not born of a woman, nevertheless had a true body, which they might change from any one form into any other form according to the reason of their ministry and office, yet from a true into a true. For neither can we say of the Lord himself, when he turned water into wine, that either the water was false or the wine was false.

**10.** And so every body, whose nature and order is changeable, into whatever forms it has been changed at the nod of the most omnipotent Founder, nevertheless does not depart from the truth in its own kind, since by whatever variety it is changed, it does not cease to be both a body and a true body. But since these people pretend that all corporeal nature is not from God the omnipotent founder, but from some race of darkness—I know not what—we ask of them whence our Lord Jesus Christ assumed a body. For if they say that he assumed no body, what was that which appeared to human and corporeal eyes? For either it was the lie of a phantasm, which it is execrable to believe; or, if they contend that he showed his very divine substance to human eyes with no assumption of a body, and the devil too saw this, where is what they proclaim in this question with slanderous voice, *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^14]? But if perhaps they say that the divine and proper substance of the Lord is not such with the Father as he wished to show himself on earth with no body assumed, what else have the wretches believed but that he is changeable in places and times? For they are unwilling to read, or cannot easily understand, what is said through the Prophet, *You shall change them and they shall be changed; but you are the same, and your years shall not fail*[^15]. And what is written in the divine letters of Wisdom about Wisdom itself, *Remaining in herself she makes all things new*[^16].

**11.** But according to their thinking, if someone says to them, "Why, then, do you wonder if God too changed the form of his divinity, so that the devil who is most filthy in heart could behold him, as it seems to you concerning Christ God?"—what they will answer I do not know. For they have never dared to say that the Father and the Son are anything but of one substance. And if they said that the Son is of another substance, it could be answered them, "Whence, then, do you know whether that old Scripture recalls that the devil spoke with the Father or with the Son?" Then we ask, "Does the devil see this sun, or does he not see it? If he sees it, how then is the sun God, which the devil sees? If he does not see it, yet wicked men see it; how then is it God which those see who are not pure in heart? Or if it too was changed in order to be seen, and is not what it is seen to be, what then if you too show one thing and are another, so that you may be able even to imitate the sun, not only to adore it?" And yet, if you ask them whether the divine substance is changeable or unchangeable, they cannot but say unchangeable, taught not by reason but confounded by shame. It remains, then, that they be forced to confess that our Lord Jesus Christ assumed a body from elsewhere, that he might appear to human eyes. And if they confess this, I ask whence he assumed it. If they say from this world, I ask whence is the body of the world itself? Immediately they answer me, "From the race of darkness." O wondrous madness! Why, then, wretches, do you fear the womb of the Virgin in the body of the Savior, and do not fear the race of demons?

**12.** We indeed profess that the whole nature of body is from God the omnipotent founder. And therefore from wherever our Lord assumed a body, he would assume it from his own creature. But he preferred it from a woman. For he had come to free the human creature, which fell through a woman. Whence, wishing to bring both sexes into the hope of renewal and restoration, he chose the male in which he would be born, the female through whom he would be born. But you who shudder at the chaste inward parts of the virgin, choose, I beg, whence the Lord should assume a body. You say that all body is the substance of the race of darkness. Choose, then, as I said, whence the Son of God ought to assume a body. Or have you lost the light of answering, because darkness meets you wherever you turn your eyes? "But mortal flesh," they say, "seems more unclean." Recite to them the Apostle: *All things are clean to the clean*[^17]. And recite against them the Apostle: *But to the unclean and unbelieving nothing is clean, but both their mind and their conscience are polluted*[^18]. But if they say not "more unclean" but "weaker," we agree plainly. And therefore Christ is our strength, because our weakness did not change him. Here I recognize that voice of the Prophet, *You shall change them and they shall be changed; but you are the same, and your years shall not fail*[^19]. For not only did the weakness of the flesh not change him for the worse, but by him it itself was changed for the better. This corporeal sun, which they think is not a body—so far do they not understand even what a body is, who deceitfully boast of spiritual disputations—this corporeal sun, then, only because it is a heavenly body, illuminates the earth and is not itself darkened by it; dries water and is not moistened by it; melts ice and is not chilled by it; hardens mud and is not softened by it. And our Lord Jesus Christ, the Word of the Father, through whom all things were made, the Power and Wisdom of God, everywhere present, everywhere hidden, everywhere whole, nowhere enclosed, reaching mightily from end to end and disposing all things sweetly—do the unhappy ones fear that he could not so assume a man as to give life to mortal things and not be put to death by them, sanctify flesh and not be polluted by it, dissolve death and not be bound by it, change man into himself and not be changed into man? We have been compelled to dispute one thing from another, on account of the wavering and dangerous weakness of faith of certain people. But as far as concerns the proposed question, although that Scripture, by which they preferred to lie in wait rather than to be enlightened, does not prove that God was seen by the devil, yet let them themselves see how the race of darkness could see the divine substance, when before the battle in which they say good and evil were mixed, the divine substance had as yet assumed no body, so as to be able to be seen by its enemy. From which let them recognize that in vain they wish to overthrow the foundations of the Catholic faith, since they cannot prop up their ruinous fables with any supports of answers whatever.
