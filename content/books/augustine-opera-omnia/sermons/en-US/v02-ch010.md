# Sermon 25/A

**1** We have sung to the Lord: *Blessed is the man whom you instruct, O Lord, and teach out of your law: that you may give him rest from the evil days, until the pit is dug for the sinner*[^1]. The man, then, instructed by the Lord out of the law of God—not instructed in the law of God, but by the Lord out of the law of God—is given rest from the evil days, until the pit is dug for the sinner. For in this world the happiness of the evil is the pit of sinners. Now men, and often religious men, are usually disturbed by this, and, not daring to reproach the Lord, yet they wonder within themselves why the evil are so often happy. And especially are those disturbed who, knowing that they live better, are driven by miseries and calamities. For they see the evil flourish in all goods—earthly and temporal, to be sure, yet goods—with every happiness. And they sigh in their miseries, and scarcely restrain their hearts from reproach of God. Let them, then, hear the psalm, and be blessed. Let the Lord instruct them out of his law. Let him say to them from another psalm: *Do not be zealous against evildoers, nor envy those who do iniquity*[^2]. Now you, who were zealous and envious, said nothing but: "Why do they flourish?" Hear out of the law of God: *Because like grass they will quickly wither*[^3]. The grass flourishes: why do you praise the greenness? Ask summer; *they will quickly wither. All flesh is grass: the grass withered, the flower fell*[^4]. See where you have your root: *The word of the Lord abides forever*[^5]. Those impious men, then, are flourishing like grass, green in winter, dry in summer. But you, with your root fixed in the word of God, which abides forever, be a living tree in hiddenness. *For you have died*, the Apostle says, *and your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^6]. There is your root, there you live. For there you have placed your hope, if you have not believed falsely. Let not the winter season, then, sadden you, the season in which many fertile and fruitful trees, without the adornment of leaves in the winter season and without the adornment of fruits, are like dry trees, but are not dry. When the grass flourishes, those trees do not even have leaves. But what I said of the grass, this I say also of the tree living in hiddenness. What did I say of the grass? If you marvel at the greenness, ask summer. Between the grass that is green in winter and the fruitful tree that withers in winter, summer is the judge. So between the devout laboring in this world and the impious flourishing in this world, as in a winter season, the sun of justice is the judge. See, the judgment comes. The tree says to the flourishing grass: "Why do you exalt yourself? Summer will come, which proves us." That trees of this kind may be given rest from the evil days, the Apostle addresses them and consoles them, foretelling summer, promising greenness. *You have died*, he says, *and your life*—where your root is—*is hidden with Christ in God*. When summer comes, *when Christ, your life, appears*—where the greenness is—*then you too will appear with him in glory*[^7]. Finally, learn the sentence of that very sun of justice, what he is going to do in summer: *For the Son of man will come*, he says, *in his majesty, and all the angels with him, and before him will be gathered all the nations*[^8]. Now see the trees and the grass: *He will divide them, as a shepherd divides the sheep from the goats; and he will set the sheep at his right hand, but the goats at his left*[^9]. See the judgment of summer. In summer there is heat. What is the heat? Even in fruitful trees there is heat, but greenness follows that heat. What, then, is the heat? *When the just king has sat upon his throne, who will boast that he has a chaste heart?*[^10] Who will boast that he is clean from sin? Grievous heat. But the judge himself, who was feared, gives consolation. Quickly he gives refreshment to the heat or to those scorched by it. For they lived in the root, they were not dry trees. See, then, their greenness. *Come, blessed of my Father*[^11]. Come. Receive what you awaited, see what you believed, have what you hoped, hold what you loved. *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*[^12]. This is the greenness in summer of the trees that as it were withered through winter, but were green in hiddenness. Now attend to the dryness of the grass. For what is the dry grass destined? *Go into the eternal fire*[^13]. Therefore in the good and fruitful trees, which have been given rest from the evil days, the refreshment of rewards consoles the heat, the refreshment of the kingdom of heaven consoles it. There one lives always. What is named on this earth by a certain herb, "houseleek" [literally "ever-living"], there has its true place: there one lives always, there alone is life, no corruption, no want, an eternal greenness is held, no dryness is feared. *Go into the eternal fire*, it is said to the impious. That is the pit of the sinner: it is being dug, this is done in this present time. The happiness of the perverse man is the pit of the sinner. For he thinks, when he does evil and is happy, that God does not care for human affairs. That is the pit of the sinner.

**2** I address, then, your Charity. Do not love the happiness of this world, and you who perhaps have it, do not hope in it. It is lying, it deceives, it is not held. And if it is held, let it not be loved, let it not be hoped in, and it will not be a pit. *Charge*, the Apostle says, *the rich of this world*—charge that they be rich. But the rich of this world are Christians, are faithful. Charge them. What? *Not to be proud in mind, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches*[^14]. As the psalm too says: *If riches rain down*, as it were from a fountain, from which you may draw as much as you wish without labor, and what you draw is about to perish at once—*if they flow, set not your heart* where it flows[^15]. If it flows, it makes a river; you set your heart, and it sweeps it away. If, then, it falls to you even to become rich, do not gape with greed: receive what is offered, do not seize what is another's; receive what is offered, but do not sell justice. For if you sell justice, do not think you receive what is offered. You receive what is offered when he gives who wishes, not when even one who does not wish gives. "I sell justice," he says. "What evil do I receive from a good thing?" Then justice is for sale to you on this earth. You do not have justice in order to receive a heavenly reward for it, but to sell it on earth. *Amen I say to you, they have received their reward*[^16]. What is given freely by your friend, left as an inheritance, and where innocence is not injured—therefore Scripture did not condemn the rich, where it says: *Having food and covering, with these we are content*[^17]. *For those who wish*, it says, *to become rich*—it did not say, those who are rich, but: *those who wish to become rich*—through their very desires *fall into temptations and many harmful desires, which plunge men into ruin and perdition*[^18]. Do you want to know what it accused? *The root of all evils is avarice*[^19]. Do not have such a root: for it is the root of withering grass.

**3** Therefore, my brothers, who are poor, do not despise yourselves. Nothing is so rich as faith. Empty is your outer cellar, full is your inner chest. The chest full, the conscience good. All that he possessed outwardly the devil took from the holy man. He did not penetrate to that chest. In that one he was rich who said: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away. The Lord gave*—let not the devil boast—*the Lord has taken away*[^20]: for the evil one took away what the good one permitted, who wished to prove him by tempting, that he might set him forth for imitation. *As it pleased the Lord, so has it been done: blessed be the name of the Lord*[^21]. If he was poor, whence did he pour out such precious things? The inner chest was full, whose gains grew by spending; in short, he poured out, and did not lose. Whoever, then, you who are poor, give more effort to having inner riches, to having hearts full of virtues, justice, devotion, love, faith, hope. These are the true riches, which you cannot lose even by shipwreck. And lest the happiness of the evil afflict the good with sadness, may God give you rest from the evil days. The evil days pass, in which this seeming perversity, as it were, is seen; and the days will come in which no evil man is happy, no good man unhappy.

**4** But you, whoever you are who are rich in this world, do what the Apostle advises: *Let them be*, he says, *rich in good works: let them give easily*[^22]. For why give with difficulty, when there is the means to give easily? For I think that this is why means [facultates] are so called, as if they were called easements [facilitates]; for want too is usually called difficulty. *Let them give easily*, then, *let them share*[^23]. Let them too have, but give to the needy; let them both help the companion and unburden themselves. O rich man, the poor man has been made your companion in this life. You see him labor by not having, you by having. He, by not having, has nothing to be propped up by; you, by having much, have something to be weighed down by. Help his want and lessen your burden. This is why it is said: *Let them share*. For the same Apostle says in another place: *Not that there should be relief for others, and distress for you*[^24]. Therefore *let them share*. Let them have, but what they have over let them share. They both keep what is enough, and do not lose what they have given. They will possess more than what they keep here, which they will either leave behind here or consume in their own uses. But what they have given, hear what becomes of it. It follows: *Let them treasure up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*[^25]. What great thing, then, is it that you have given, if you have arranged to migrate from this place where all things perish? What you have given to the poor, you have made them your porters. If, then, you are such, you will not flourish like grass in the passage of this world, but, like the olive that is green even through winter, it will be said in your voice: *But I, like a fruitful olive in the house of God*[^26]. But, as a fruitful olive in the house of God, see what follows: *I have hoped in the mercy of God*, not *in the uncertainty of riches*[^27].
