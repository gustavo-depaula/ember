# Sermon 346/A: On the Word of God as the Leader of the Pilgrimage of Christians

And That It Is Necessary That the Times Be Harsh, Lest Earthly Happiness Be Loved

**1.** Brothers, we are Christians, and we all wish to make a journey, and even if we do not wish to, we make a journey. No one is permitted to remain here: the changeableness of the times compels all who come into this life to pass on. Let there be no place for sloth: walk, lest you be dragged. As we make our journey, a certain man meets us at the crossroads: not a man, but God for the sake of men made man; and he said to us: Do not go to the left: that passage seems indeed easy and gentle and delightful, worn by many, and wide; but the end of this way has destruction. But there is another way, which has very many labors, difficulties, straits, hardships; where not only are delights not found, but scarcely the fitting kindness is offered; along which you will walk with difficulty, but with the difficulty quickly ended you will come to a great height of joys, so that you may escape those snares which no one will escape.

**2.** Let us recall past times and the sacred Scriptures. Is not the Word of God himself a man? Was not that same Word afterward *made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^1]? Before it became flesh and dwelt among us, did not that same Word speak through the prophets? God surely spoke by his Word to Abraham, that his offspring would be on pilgrimage, when he himself to whom it was said was old, and Sarah aged and barren: it was believed, and it was done[^2]. That this same offspring, that is, the people born from it according to the flesh, would serve in Egypt for four hundred years: it was done. That it would be freed from that captivity: it was freed. That it would receive the land of promise: it received it. And things far in the future and things near were spoken, and accomplished, and are now being accomplished. The word of the Lord spoke through the prophets that that nation would sin, would be handed over into the hands of its enemies, because they offended their God: all things were done. That it would come into the Babylonian captivity; and this was done. That from there would come a king, Christ: Christ came, Christ was born, because the Word himself proclaimed that he himself would come. It was said that the Jews would crucify him: they crucified him. It was foretold that he would rise again and be glorified: it was done, he rose again, he ascended into heaven. It was foretold that in his name the whole earth would believe; it was foretold that kings would persecute his Church: these things were done. It was foretold that kings would believe in him: we now hold the faith of kings, and do we doubt about the faith of Christ? The cuttings-off of heresies were foretold: do we not see these too, and groan among them, clamoring around on every side? It was foretold that the idols themselves would be destroyed through the Church and the name of Christ: and this too we see fulfilled. Scandals were foretold in the Church itself, weeds were foretold too, chaff was foretold too: all these things we both behold with our eyes, and, with whatever fortitude is imparted by the Lord, endure. In what has this one deceived you, who said to you: Go this way? Say securely, if you are faithful, taking such great proofs of this one who speaks to you: In these matters I prove him, because he deigned thus to be proved by fact; if he tells me the truth altogether, he does not deceive me; all these things which he says, you hold all his words true, in nothing has he lied; such I have known him to be. He is the Word of God; he spoke through the mouths of his servants, and did not deceive: through his own mouth, can what he says deceive? But let that one to whom he is not yet known, who doubts about Christ, say himself too: I will go this way, lest perhaps he speak the truth, in whom now the whole world has believed.

**3.** My brothers, many not believing, nor hearing the voice of the holy fathers, are to be found such as that multitude was found in the day of Noah: none escaped except those who were in the ark. For if they had reflected and changed their ways from impiety, and turned to our Lord, made satisfaction to him for their offenses and, groaning, turned to his mercy, without doubt they would not have perished. For neither was God unmerciful to Nineveh, which in three days merited to be saved. What is so short as three days? Yet they, in so short a stretch of time, did not despair of God's mercy so as to bend his clemency. If, then, so great a city had a space of three days to bend God to mercy, how great could the space have been through a hundred years, and two hundred, and three hundred, in which the ark was being built? From the time Christ began to cut imperishable wood from the forest of the nations, that is, of the Church, if those men had changed their ways and habits, if they had offered to propitiate God the sacrifice of a contrite heart, without any doubt they would have escaped unharmed. Let men fear, then, lest they be found thus on that last day. But let us, brothers, so act as to change our ways from impiety, and amend our habits, while we have time, that that day may find us prepared; because he never lies who says it will come. Beware lest you doubt, for it is true. For in the days of Noah it was thus: *They were eating, drinking, marrying, taking wives, buying, selling, until Noah entered the ark, the flood came, and destroyed all*[^3] who placed their hope in this world, and desired to live secure, but not in the region of security, and none escaped except those who were in the ark.

**4.** But many say to themselves: We are bidden to await that day, and not to be found such as those were found outside the ark, who perished in that flood. Surely the trumpet of the Gospel terrifies us, the word of God terrifies us: what shall we do? Then no wife is to be taken? The young man says this, the youth says this. Is there to be no eating, no drinking? Must there always be fasting? Many say these things; and those who perhaps wished to buy something will say to themselves: Is nothing now to be bought, lest we be found in the number of those who perished in the flood? What, then, shall we do, brothers? If it is so, then we must weep, just as the Apostles were saddened over the human race when they had heard the Lord saying: *If you wish to be perfect, sell all that you have, and give to the poor; and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me*[^4]. That man to whom it was said was saddened, departed; and although he called him good teacher, of whom he was asking counsel about eternal life, he was seen to be a good teacher only so long as he said justly what he was asked. The Lord spoke, and the rich man was saddened. But as that man departed sadly, the Lord said: *How hardly does a rich man enter the kingdom of heaven!* as though the kingdom of heaven were closed against the rich. What will happen? It is closed. But he says: *Knock, and it will be opened to you*[^5]. And would that as few went into the fire as there are rich men! But now, both of the number of the rich many are going to go into the kingdom of heaven, and of the number of the poor many are going to go into the eternal fire—not one who is rich in means, but one who burns with greed.

**5.** But the disciples were saddened. The Lord said: *What is difficult for men is easy for God*[^6]. You were moved by the difficulty, because he named a camel: if he wills, that huge beast which is called a camel enters here through the eye of a needle. What is this? Let us see whether it becomes clear. For not without cause did John the Baptist too, the forerunner of the Lord himself, have a garment of camel's hair, who, of that judge coming after him, to whom he was bearing witness, had as it were a cloak from him. Let us recognize, when the camel is named, the figure of our Lord Jesus Christ: let us recognize the great one, and yet with humble neck, whom no one can load with persuasions, unless he lay himself down on the ground. So Christ too humbled himself unto death[^7], *that he might destroy him who had the power of death, that is, the devil*[^8]. Therefore let us see the eye of the needle too, through which that so great one entered: for in the prick of the needle is the suffering which he willingly endured, but in the eye the straits. Now, then, the camel has entered through the eye of the needle: let the rich not despair, secure they enter the kingdom of heaven.

**6.** But let us recognize what sort of rich men. Behold, I know not who, from across, clothed in rags, exulted and laughed, when it was said that a rich man does not enter the kingdom of heaven; and he said: I will enter, these rags will procure this for me; those will not enter who do us injuries, who oppress us: plainly such will not enter. But, poor man, see whether you enter. What if you are poor, and are greedy? What if you are pressed by want, and burn with avarice? If, then, you are such, whoever you are, poor—not because you did not wish to be rich, but because you could not be. God therefore does not look at your means, but attends to your will. If, then, you are such, of an evil life, of evil habits, a blasphemer, an adulterer, a drunkard, proud, withdraw from the number of the poor of God; you will not be among those of whom it was said: *Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^9]. Behold, I find such a rich man—by comparison with whom you boasted, and dared to look toward the kingdom of heaven—I find one poor in spirit, that is, humble, pious, innocent, not blaspheming, following the will of God; and if perhaps he loses some of the things of this world in losses, he at once says: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away: as it has pleased the Lord, so has it been done: blessed be the name of the Lord forever*[^10]. Behold a rich man meek, humble, not resisting, not murmuring, following the will of God, and rejoicing over that land of the living: *For blessed are the meek, for they shall possess the land as an inheritance*[^11]. But you perhaps are poor, and proud. I praise the humble rich man: do I not praise the humble poor man? The poor man has nothing by which to be puffed up, but the rich man has that with which to wrestle. This one rather, this rich man will enter, and against you the kingdom of heaven will be closed; because it will be closed against the impious, against the proud, against the blasphemer, against the adulterer, against the drunkard, it will be closed against the greedy. He held a faithful debtor, who believed in the promiser. But the rich man, humble, kindly, faithful, answered these things, saying: God knows that I am not proud in mind; and if perhaps I cry out, and say something hard, God knows my conscience, that I say these things by the necessity of governing: I will never bear myself above others for this. Within God sees the works that follow. For the rich who are in good works give easily, share with the one who has not. For there humility appears, if you are rich and humble. You declare yourself to be good and pious: let what you have be common to you and to him who has not; that you may lay up for yourself a good foundation for the future, that you may obtain the true and blessed life[^12]. If such men exist, let them be secure: when that last day comes, they are found in the ark, they will be in the structure, they will not belong to the flood; let them not be terrified, that they are rich. And if one is young, and cannot contain himself, he is permitted to take a wife. But, because *the time is short, let those who have wives be as though not having them, and those who buy as though not buying, and those who weep as though not weeping, and those who rejoice as though not rejoicing, and those who use this world as though not using it*: *for the figure of this world passes away*[^13].

**7.** My brothers, someone murmurs against God, and says: Bad times, hard times, troublesome times; and yet gifts are lavished, and he calls the time hard. How much harder are you, who are not corrected by a hard time! So great a madness of pomp still thrives, men gape after so many superfluous things; and greed has no end. How many diseases proceed from this! How much luxury has overflowed on account of theaters, and organs, and flutes, and pantomimes! You wish to use badly what you desire; therefore you will not receive it. Hear the apostolic voice: *You covet, and do not have; you kill, and envy, and cannot obtain; you quarrel, you ask, and do not receive, because you ask badly, that you may be consumed in your concupiscences*[^14]. Let us heal ourselves, brothers, let us correct ourselves. The Judge is going to come, and, because he is coming, he is still mocked: he is going to come, and there will be no leisure to mock. My dearest brothers, let us correct ourselves, because better times are going to come upon us, but not for those living badly. Now the age inclines itself, and has declined into old age. Are we going to come into youth? What do we hope for here? Let us now seek something else. Do not hope for any times except such as are read of in the Gospel: they are not bad because Christ came; but because they were bad and hard, he came who would console.

**8.** Hear, my brothers: for it was necessary that the times be troublesome and hard. What would we do, if so great a consoler were not present? The human race was gravely sick, from Adam to the end. From the time we were born here, from the time we were sent out of paradise, it is surely sickness: but at the end it was going to be greater, and to some perhaps near to health, to some near to death. When, then, the human race was sick, that great physician, taking up the sick man in a certain great bed, that is, in the whole world—but as a most skilled physician attends to the times of the sick man, and sees, and foresees what is to come, and in the gentler times of the sick man's illness first sends his servants; so our physician too first sent the prophets to look at us. They spoke, they preached; through them he cured some, and healed them. They foretold that a certain relapse into the extreme of sickness was coming, and a certain great tossing of this sick man, for which the physician himself needed to be presented, and to come to him. So it came about, because he said: The one believing I will refresh, I will save; *I will strike, I will heal*[^15]. He came, was made man, a partaker of our mortality, that we might be made partakers of his immortality. Still the sick man is tossed: and when he pants in fevers, and boils greatly, he says to himself: From the time this physician came, I suffer sharper fevers, I am more grievously tossed, I bear enormous burnings. From where did he come to me? I think he did not enter my house well. So say all who are still sick with vanity. Why are they sick with vanity? Because they do not wish to receive from him the potion of sobriety. God, seeing the wretched tossed by their cares, and occupied by the various preoccupations of this world, which strangle their souls, came as a physician. And they do not fear to say: From the time Christ came, we suffer these times; from the time there are Christians, the world has failed in all things. O foolish sick man! Not because the physician came has your sickness become more grievous: the good, pious, just, merciful physician foresaw it; for he did not cause it. For he came to console you, that you might be truly healthy. For what does he take from you, except the superfluous? For you gaped after harmful things, and were held fast in them: the things you gaped after were not useful for your fever. Is the physician harsh, because he snatches harmful fruit from the hand of the sick? What has he taken from you, except the bad security you were going to seize? Be rid of the destruction of your inward parts: and this, that you groan and murmur, pertains to his medicine. Unwilling to be cured, lest you be tormented against your will. It is necessary that the times be harsh. Why? Lest earthly happiness be loved. For it is fitting, and it is medicinal, that this life be disturbed, and that another life be loved. Behold, if with such great sloth men are still delighted with earthly things and the amphitheater, what if their works were not struck? Behold, so many bitter things are mixed in, and still the world is sweet!
