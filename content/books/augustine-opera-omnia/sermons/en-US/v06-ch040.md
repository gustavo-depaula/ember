# Sermon 217: On the Words of the Lord in the Gospel of John:

"This Is Eternal Life, That They Know You, the Only True God, and Him Whom You Sent, Jesus Christ"

And on the Trinity

**1.** The Lord Jesus Christ, who hears us with the Father, deigned to pray to the Father for us. What is more certain than our happiness, when he prays for us who gives what he prays for? For Christ is man and God: he prays as man, he gives what he prays for as God. But, which you ought to hold, he assigns all to the Father for this reason, that the Father is not from him, but he himself is from the Father. To the fountain, from which he is derived, he gives all. But he himself too, the fountain born of the Father, is the fountain of life. Therefore the Father, the fountain, begot the fountain. The fountain indeed begot the fountain; but the begetting fountain and the begotten fountain are one fountain: just as the begetting God and the begotten God are one God, the Son, namely, born of the Father. The Father is not the Son, the Son is not the Father; the Father is not from the Son, the Son is from the Father; but yet the Father and the Son are one on account of the one substance, and there is one God on account of the inseparable divinity. Accordingly, what you have heard him say: *This is eternal life, that they know you, the only true God, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ*[^1], see that you do not so take it as to think that the Father alone is the true God, but that the Son is not the true God. For we have a divine testimony on this matter, John saying manifestly in his epistle: *That we may be in his true Son Jesus Christ: for he is the true God, and eternal life*[^2]. Hold that Christ is the true God, and eternal life. Therefore, when you hear: *That they know you, the only true God, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ*, you ought to supply: *the only true God*; that is, that they may know you, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ, as the only true God.

**2.** From this question we have come out most fully. But what do we do about the Holy Spirit? For if of the Father and Christ what was said: *That they know you, the only true God, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ*, that is, that they know you and Jesus Christ as the only true God; we labor to show how the Holy Spirit too is true God. The Holy Spirit was not passed over because he is unmentioned. He is not only the Spirit of the Father, or only of the Son: but he is the Spirit of the Father and the Son. When, therefore, he is unmentioned, the two being named, he is understood in them, who is of both. I will tell you a certain manner of speaking from Scripture, whence you may understand what we say. The Apostle says: *No one knows the things of a man, except the spirit of the man which is in him*[^3]. For what are the things of a man? What a man thinks as a man: for there a man is properly, in his thoughts. Does your spirit know my thoughts, or does my spirit know your thoughts? *No one knows the things of a man, except the spirit of the man which is in him.* The Apostle said it; and he added: *So the things of God no one knows but the Spirit of God*[^4]. How do we take this? The sentence is definite. If, then, the things of God no one knows but the Spirit of God, then does the Son of God not know the things of God? Far be from us the devilish thought, let it depart from us. Then the Word of God does not know the things of God? then the Only-begotten of God does not know the things of God? does he not know the things of God, through whom all things were made[^5]? He knows them: and who so knows them, except the Spirit of God? As, then, where you hear: No one knows the things of God but the Spirit of God, you do not separate the Son from there; so, when you hear: *That they know you, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ, as the only true God*, do not separate the Holy Spirit.

**3.** If he is the only true God, says the heresy, I know not what you know; because there is no other true God but the Father, to whom Christ said: *That they know you, the only true God.* Add: *and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ.* I do not wish, he says, to add. But he himself added it. But I, he says, do not wish to add. Nor do I wish to hear you. And yet, you who define the Father alone to be the true God, what will you say of the epistle of John, where it is read of Christ: *He is the true God, and eternal life*[^6]? Finally, to whom was it said, or of whom was it said: *who alone does wonders*[^7]? Of the Father, or of the Son, or of both? If of the Father, then the Son does not do wonders. And where is what he himself says: *As the Father raises the dead and gives life, so the Son too gives life to whom he wills*[^8]? It is of the same power, of the same divinity. If, then, the Son too does wonders, how does the Father do them alone? But if it was said of both, then the Father and the Son are one God. Add the Holy Spirit: add, do not separate, lest you be separated.

**4.** Let us adore God, whose temples we are. For, brothers, if we were to make a temple of wood and stones, we could make it only to God. If we were pagans, we would make temples to gods: for we would make temples to false gods, as did the unbelieving nations estranged from God. But Solomon, because he was a prophet of God, made a temple of wood and stones, but yet to God: to God, not to an idol: to God, not to an angel: to God, not to the sun, not to the moon: to God, who made heaven and earth: to the living God, who made heaven and earth, and remains in the heavens, he made a temple of earth; and God did not disdain it, rather he commanded that it be made. Why did he command that a temple be made for him? Had he not where to remain? Hear holy Stephen, when he suffered: *Solomon*, he says, *built him a house, but the Most High does not dwell in temples made by hands*[^9]. Why, then, did he wish to make a temple, or a temple to be made? That it might be a figure of the body of Christ. That temple was a shadow: the light came, and put the shadow to flight. Seek now the temple which Solomon made, and you find a ruin. Why is that temple a ruin? Because what it signified has now been done. And that temple, the body of the Lord, fell, but rose again; and so rose that it can now no longer fall at all. Finally, when the Jews had said to him: What sign do you do, that we may believe you? he said to them: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.* But he was speaking to them in the temple built by Solomon, and was saying: *Destroy this temple*; but what *this* was they did not hear, what *this* was they did not understand, they thought he was speaking of that temple. Finally they answered him: *Forty-six years was this temple built, and will you raise it up in three days?* Then the Evangelist at once: *But he was saying this of the temple of his body*[^10]. The temple of God, then, is the body of Christ. What are our bodies? members of Christ. Hear the Apostle himself: *Do you not know that your bodies are members of Christ?*[^11] He who said: *Your bodies are members of Christ*, what did he show, except that our bodies and our Head, which is Christ, together are one temple of God? Let us trust that the body of Christ and our bodies are the temple of God, and we shall be: for if we do not believe it, neither shall we be. Therefore, since our bodies are members of Christ, hear something else the Apostle said: *Do you not know that your body is the temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God?*[^12] Behold, he has a temple: and is he not God? If he had one of wood and stones, he would be God: if he had a temple made by hands, he would be God: and is he not God, who has a temple of the members of God? Add, then, the Holy Spirit. The Holy Spirit is God. There is one God, Father and Son and Holy Spirit. The Father is not the Son, the Son is not the Father, the Spirit of both is neither Father nor Son: but the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit is one God. Be built up in unity, lest you fall in separation.

**5.** You heard what he asked for us, rather what he said he wills: *Father, those whom you have given me, I will.* I will, Father: what you will I do, do what I will. *I will.* What? *That where I am, they too may be with me*[^13]. O blessed house! O secure homeland! It has no enemy, it has no plague; there we live secure, we do not seek to migrate, we find no safer place. Whatever you choose here on earth, you choose for fear, not for security. Choose for yourself a place, while you are in an evil place, that is, in this age, in this life full of temptations, in this mortality full of groanings and fears; while you are in an evil place, choose for yourself a place to which you may migrate from the evil place. You will not be able to migrate to a good place from an evil place, unless you have done well in the evil place. What kind of place is that? where no one hungers. Therefore if you wish to dwell in a good place, where no one hungers, in this age break your bread for the hungry[^14]. Because in that blessed place no one is a pilgrim, all live in their own homeland; if, then, you wish to be in a good place, where you find a pilgrim in an evil place having no place to enter, receive him into your house; offer hospitality in the evil place, that you may come to the place where you cannot be a guest. In the good place no one needs clothing: there is no cold there, there are no heats there: why a roof? why a garment? Where there will be no roof, but protection, behold, even there we find a roof: *Under the shadow of your wings I will hope*[^15]. Therefore in this evil place offer a roof to one who has none, that you may be in the good place, where you may have such a roof that you need not make repairs to the roof: because there the rain does not drip, where there is the perpetual fountain of truth. But that rain gladdens, it does not soak; that rain is itself the fountain of life. What is: *Lord, with you is the fountain of life*[^16]? *And the Word was with God*[^17].

**6.** Therefore, brothers, do well in the evil place, that you may come to the good place, of which he says, who prepares it for us: *I will, that where I am, they too may be with me*[^18]. The Lord Christ ascended to prepare, that we may come secure to what is prepared. He himself prepares himself: remain in him. Is Christ a small house for you? Now you do not even fear his passion: he rose from the dead, and dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him[^19]. An evil place, evil days, this age; but let us do well in the evil place, and let us live well in the evil days. The evil place passes, and the evil days, and the good place, eternal, will come, the eternal good days; and those good days will be one day. For here why are they evil days? Because one passes, that another may come: today passes, that tomorrow may come: yesterday passed, that today might come. Where nothing passes, it is one day: and that day is Christ. And the Father is day: but the Father day of no day, the Son day from day. *Sing to the Lord a new song, sing to the Lord, all the earth. Sing to the Lord, and bless his name; proclaim well his salvation, day from day*[^20]. Christ is day. If you do not recognize it, hear a wise old man: if you are not yet wise, whoever you are, young man, hear the gray hairs of truth. That old man Simeon beheld the infant Lord Christ who was carried by his mother, and contained heaven. He beheld the little one, recognized the great one, and received him into his hands; because he had received an answer from God that he would not see death unless he had seen the Christ of the Lord. He received him into his hands, and said: *Now you dismiss, Lord, your servant according to your word in peace, for my eyes have seen your salvation*[^21]. Therefore, *proclaim well his salvation, day from day*[^22]. For so Simeon said: He has come whom I awaited; what do I do here? He received him, to be received by him: he carried the man Christ, he was carried by the God Christ.

**7.** Therefore hold this: I give you a rule, that you may not be alarmed, when the Son says something where he seems to signify the Father greater: either he says it from the person of the man, because God is greater than man; or he says it from the person of the begotten, in honor of him by whom he was begotten. Seek no more: for God begot God, the great one begot the equal. If God did not beget true God, and the great one did not beget the equal, he begot a monster, not a true son. But because he begot a true son, this is what he himself is whom he begot.
