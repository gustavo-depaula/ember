# Sermon 372: On the Nativity of the Lord

**1. 1.** Your faith knows, dearest ones, which has gathered this whole multitude, that a Savior is born for us today. He was born of the Father always, of the mother once: of the Father without sex, of the mother without intercourse. For with the Father there was no womb of one conceiving: with the mother there was no embrace of one sowing. The first nativity from the Father kept the nature: the second nativity from the mother sowed grace. The one held the majesty of the divine substance: the other took on the fellowship of human mortality. But in this he deigned to come for this reason, that he might be made obedient unto death[^1], and by dying conquer death. Both nativities ineffable, both wonderful. For what human heart could grasp, or what tongue could explain, how Christ was born always from God, or how lately from the womb? Who could understand the Father coeternal with the Son, who could express the virgin mother? him begetting without beginning, without end; her conceiving without lust, bringing forth without corruption? Both nativities are wonderful, because both are divine. Whether, then, the human mind considers the one, or the other, most rightly it says: *His generation who shall declare?*[^2] And we, what shall we do, brothers? Since we are not able to speak worthily, ought we to keep silent? Far be it from us, far be it that the tongue of the servant be silent, when it is the Birthday of the Lord. Let us say, then, what we can, what we read.

**1. 2.** Blessed David, speaking of Christ, says in the Psalms: *In the sun he has set his tabernacle, and he himself like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber, has exulted like a giant*[^3]. For he came forth today from the sacred chamber, that is, from the hidden and uncorrupted secret of the blessed virginal womb. From there came forth the son of the virgin, the bridegroom of the virgin: namely the son of Mary, the bridegroom of the Church. For the Apostle was speaking to the whole Church, when he said: *I have fitted you to one husband, to present a chaste virgin to Christ*[^4].

**2. 2.** To these wedding-feasts of Christ, the Father of the bridegroom first invited the people of the Jews. But what does the Gospel say? *Those who were invited were not worthy*[^5]. Afterward the multitude of all the nations was invited, this filled the Church, this received from the Lord's table not cheap feasts, or ignoble drinks, but tasted beforehand the very flesh and blood of the shepherd, of the slain Christ himself. The innocent lamb himself was slain at his own wedding, was slain at the wedding; and whomever he invited, he fed from his own flesh. Slain, he prepared the feasts: rising, he celebrated the wedding. Slain, he bore a voluntary passion: rising, he led home the wife he had appointed. In the womb of the virgin he received human flesh as a pledge: on the cross he poured out his most precious blood as a dowry: in his resurrection and ascension he confirmed the covenants of an eternal marriage. *For he ascended on high, he led captivity captive, he gave gifts to men*[^6]. What gifts? The Holy Spirit, by whom, with love poured out in human minds, the Church clung inseparably to Christ as to her husband. He came forth, then, today like a bridegroom from his sacred chamber, and, as the Psalm pursues: *He exulted like a giant to run the way*[^7]. He came forth like a bridegroom, he exulted like a giant. Beautiful and strong: beautiful, as a bridegroom; strong, as a giant. Beautiful, that he might be loved; strong, that he might be feared: beautiful, that he might please; strong, that he might conquer. Where in the sacred Scriptures is the beauty of this bridegroom found? *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men: grace is poured out on your lips*[^8]. Where is the strength of the giant found? *The Lord strong and mighty, the Lord mighty in battle*[^9]. But both, that is, both the beauty and the strength in him Isaiah had seen and understood, when he said: *Who is this who comes from Edom? the red of his garments from Bozrah, so beautiful in the robe of his garment with strength?*[^10] This prophet, then, who called him both beautiful and strong, knew the bridegroom and the giant.

**3. 3.** Therefore, dearest ones, *He exulted like a giant to run the way.* What way, except the mortal way, which he deigned to have in common with us? It is that way, through which the human race passes. For they pass through it both coming, being born, and departing, dying. And this river of the human race flows continually from the beginning to the end from the hidden veins of nature. From this swift and turbulent river Christ deigned to drink. Just now you heard in the psalm: *From the torrent on the way he drank*[^11]. This torrent brought us forth to birth, led us to death. As it were from the hidden spring, Christ received the abyss of the sea. Both for our sake, he was both born and died. And because men, set in the midst of this stream, for the most part are delighted by the coaxings and enticements of the world, which are wrapped in the mud of this torrent, and plunge into the depth of hell those who, while passing, greedily drink in the waves, and so are killed, wishing to stand in the headlong torrent, and seeking in the commonwealth fixed footprints: therefore the Lord *from the torrent on the way drank.* What is, *he drank on the way*? Passing, he drank. For he drank and passed on, he did not remain: nor did he stand in the way of sinners[^12]. Likewise men fear death; because it is necessary that all be cast headlong by the impulse of this torrent: but Christ could not fear the death which he had taken up of his own accord; therefore it is said: *And he exulted like a giant to run the way.* For he descended, and ran: he ascended, and sat. You know, because so you are wont to confess: after he rose again, he ascended into heaven, he sits at the right hand of the Father. This running-course of our giant the blessed Ambrose sang most briefly and most beautifully in the hymn, which you sang a little before. For speaking of the Lord Christ, he says thus: *His going forth from the Father, his returning to the Father: his course even to the lower regions, his recourse to the seat of God*. All these things, dearest ones, if we ask why they were done, we shall find done for our sake. For he descended, that we might ascend; he died, that we might live; he rose again, that we might rise again; he ascended into heaven, that we might learn to despise earthly things, and to lift the heart on high. Finally, that he might lift up our hope after him, he first lifted up his own flesh: and that we might hope this would follow in us, that went before which he received from us.
