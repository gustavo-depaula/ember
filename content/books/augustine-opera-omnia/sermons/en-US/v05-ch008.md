# Sermon 154/A

**1.** When the apostle Paul was being read, we heard what goes on in a human being; and each one, considering himself, found that the Apostle spoke truly. For he said that he does not do what he wills, but what he hates, that he does. And he said, *If I do what I do not will, I agree with the law, that it is good*[^1]; because what I do not will, the law too does not will; what the law forbids me to do, this I do not will to do: but what I do not will, this . . . there is in me something else that fights against my will. And he said, *For I delight in the law of God according to the inner man: but I see another law fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members*[^2]. Behold what goes on in a human being. But see what he said in what follows: *Unhappy man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^3].

**2.** Let each person, then, attend to himself; for in his Epistle Paul has set up a mirror in which every person may see himself. What the law commands delights our mind; and what the law forbids delights our flesh; and our mind and our flesh contend; the mind contends for the law, the flesh against the law; and one man walks about with the quarrel that goes on within him. In one man there is a quarrel: the tongue is silent, and within there is an uproar. I set before you something where you may see this better. A man sees another's wife, and covets her. He attends to the law, which says, You shall not commit fornication; and what does the man's mind say within? The law has spoken well, the law has said a great thing, it loves chastity; and the flesh is delighted by iniquity. A quarrel has arisen in the man. Let the two conquer the one; let the two—the law and the mind—conquer the contradicting flesh. But see what the mind itself said: *I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind, and leading me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members*[^4]. So the mind is conquered by the flesh. Let it call on the help of the Savior, and it will escape the snare of the deceiver. For see what the Apostle said. What sort of man was the Apostle, how great and strong an athlete of God! Yet he would be led captive, if the Crucified did not come to his aid. So, when he was in peril, what did he say? *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death*, that I may not do iniquity, which delights my flesh? *The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^5].

**3.** Christ, then, watches you as you fight. Your conscience is the theater, where two contend, the mind and the flesh. The mind consents to the law, the flesh contradicts the law; and it wishes to rein in the flesh. A great struggle: but the One who watches you fighting can help you in your peril. For if you were in a wrestling match with some man, you would strike him and he would strike you; and a man would watch you, to see which of you would win. Whoever was going to win would receive the prize. This is what the man would have to say to you: Whichever of you wins will receive this. He was ready to crown the winner; but could he help the one in difficulty? But now the One who watches you is Christ; cry out to him, when you labor, that you may conquer; for your flesh too contends, to its own harm, that it may conquer. For if two men contend and make a wrestling match, whichever of you wins would be crowned. But in this struggle, if the mind wins, it frees both soul and flesh; if the flesh wins, both are sent into hell. So the flesh contends to its own harm, and wishes to conquer to its own harm. It is granted to the flesh to be conquered, lest it be punished in eternal fire.

**4.** The Psalm we sang agrees with this too, as we said: *Out of the depths I have cried to you, O Lord; Lord, hear my voice*[^6]. This is the depth where the flesh contends against the mind. For if the mind wins, both are lifted up from it on high; even the conquered flesh is lifted up there, because it is conquered to its own good. Just as in a house, when an evil wife contends with a good husband, she wins to her own harm and is conquered to her own good. For if the evil wife wins, the house becomes perverse, when a bearded man serves an evil woman. But if the evil wife is conquered, she begins to serve a good husband; the woman herself becomes good, because she serves a good husband. So it is too with our flesh, like an evil wife conquered, because it is conquered to its own good. That he may be called on for help who joins the two and crowns both, therefore out of this depth we cry to our Lord, as we said in the Psalm. For if we conquer, and do not consent to the evil desires of our flesh, afterward the flesh will rise again; and there you do not find evil desires with which to contend. For now you find evil desires against which to fight: and it is said to you: Conquer, and you are crowned. And what is it for you now to conquer? Not to consent to evil desires. For can you not have those evil desires? But to conquer is . . . not to consent. But when we have received back the flesh on the day of resurrection, the flesh itself is changed, becomes immortal; and you no longer find a desire with which to wrestle . . . in which you may be crowned. And when you are on high with your flesh, do you cry out of the depth, where you were quarreling against your flesh?

**5.** And that woman who cried out after the Lord, the Canaanite—how she cried out! Her daughter was suffering from a demon: because the flesh does not consent to the mind, it was possessed by the devil. If she cried out so much for her daughter, how much ought we to cry out for our flesh and our soul? For you see what she obtained by crying out. For at first she was despised; for she was a Canaanite, of an evil race, where idols were worshiped. But the Lord Jesus Christ was walking in Judea, from where the patriarchs were, from where the Virgin Mary was, who bore Christ; and that nation alone worshiped the true God, did not worship idols. So when some Canaanite woman appealed to him, he was unwilling to hear her. He despised her for this reason, because he knew what he was keeping in store; not that he himself denied the benefit, but that she, by persevering, might obtain it. So his disciples said to him: Lord, now *send her away*, give her an answer: you see *that she cries out after us*, and is wearisome to us. And he said to his disciples, *I was sent only to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel*[^7]. I was sent to the Jewish nation, where I was to seek the sheep that had been lost. There were other sheep among other nations, but Christ had not come to them; because they did not believe in the presence of Christ, but believed the gospel of Christ. So he said, *I was sent only to the sheep*; so in his presence he chose the apostles. And of these sheep was Nathanael, of whom he said, *Behold an Israelite in whom there is no guile*[^8]. Of these sheep was that great crowd which carried branches before the Lord's donkey and said, *Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord*[^9]. Those sheep of the house of Israel had been lost, and had recognized the shepherd present, and had believed in Christ present. So when she was in distress, she was being put off along with the gentile sheep. When she had heard this, which the Lord said to his disciples, she persevered in crying out, and did not give up. And the Lord to her: *It is not good to take the children's bread and throw it to the dogs*. He made her a dog: why? Because she was of the gentiles, who worshiped idols: for dogs lick stones. *It is not good to take the children's bread and give it to the dogs*. And she did not say: Lord, do not make me a dog, for I am not a dog; but rather said: You speak truly, Lord, I am a dog. From where she recognized the reproach, from there she deserved the benefit; for where iniquity was put to distress, there humility was crowned. *Yes, Lord*, you speak truly: *but even the dogs eat of the crumbs that fall from the table of their masters*. And then the Lord: *O woman, great is your faith; let it be done to you as you wish*[^10]. A while ago a dog, now a woman, changed by her barking. She was longing for the crumbs falling from the table: suddenly she found herself reclining at the table. For when he says to her, *Great is your faith*, he now counted her among those whose bread he was unwilling to throw to the dogs.

**6.** What do all these things teach us, but that, when what we ask of God is good, we should persevere in praying until we receive it through the longing of one who sighs for it? For God puts off those who ask in order to exercise those who long. But we ought to ask for eternal life with great groanings—here a good life, and afterward eternal life; because you ought to ask of God even the good life, that he may help your will. If he does not help, you remain conquered; you will begin to be led captive, if that does not come to your aid which was said: *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^11]. Two things, then, let us securely ask: in this age, a good life; in the age to come, eternal life. As for other things, we do not know whether they are useful to us. A man asks of God that he may take a wife; how does he know whether he takes her to his own good? A man asks of God that he may acquire riches; how does he know whether the poor man slept secure, and, made rich, dreams of robbers? He does not know, then, what among all the things this world has is useful to him. But he securely asks for a good life and for eternal life; here, that he may merit God; there, that he may be crowned by God. But what is a good life? To love God with the whole heart, with the whole soul, with the whole mind, and to love one's brother as oneself. Let us, then, love our God, let us love one another in his unity, let us have peace in him, and love among ourselves; so that, when our Lord Christ himself comes . . . we may be able to say: Lord, with your help we have done what you commanded; in your mercy may we receive what you promised.
