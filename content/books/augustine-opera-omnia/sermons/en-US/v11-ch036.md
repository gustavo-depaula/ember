# (Erfurt 3 = Sermon 350/E) (Erfurt 3)

**1.** The Apostle says, *Let him who is taught the word share in all good things with him who teaches him*[^1], admonishing of course that which in another place too he confirms with the law as witness, that the mouth of the ox treading out the grain is not to be muzzled. For he says this: *Let the elders be honored with double honor, especially those who labor in word and teaching. For Scripture says, "You shall not muzzle the ox that treads out the grain."*[^2] This to Timothy; but to the Corinthians, when he was dealing with this matter and declaring that it was lawfully owed to him, yet that he did not demand it, he says, *Who ever serves as a soldier at his own expense? Who plants a vineyard and does not eat of its fruit? Who tends a flock and does not partake of the milk of the flock? Do I say these things on human authority? Does not the law say this too? For in the law of Moses it is written, "You shall not muzzle the ox that treads out the grain." Is it for oxen that God is concerned? Or does he say it altogether for our sake? For it was written for our sake, because the one who plows ought to plow in hope, and the one who threshes in hope of partaking. If we have sown spiritual goods for you, is it a great thing if we reap your carnal goods? If others share in this right over you, why not rather we? Yet we have not used this right.*[^3]

**2.** Since, then, these things are so, most beloved brethren, since no one at all of the faithful and good children of the Church doubts that carnal goods are to be rendered with due service, for necessary uses, to the servants and ministers of God, the dispensers of spiritual things, it may seem remarkable how these very carnal goods that are provided are sown not in the flesh but in the spirit. For when, writing to the Galatians, he had said, as I mentioned a little before, *Let him who is taught the word share in all good things with him who teaches him*[^4], then following on he added, *Do not be deceived: God is not mocked. For whatever a man sows, that he will also reap; for the one who sows in his flesh will from the flesh reap corruption, but the one who sows in the spirit will from the spirit reap eternal life. And let us not grow weary in doing good; for in due time we shall reap. So then, while we have time, let us tirelessly do good to all, but especially to those of the household of faith*[^5]. If, therefore, the one who sows in the flesh will from the flesh reap corruption, it is plain that those carnal goods which are ministered for sustenance to the dispensers of God are not sown by each person in his own flesh, since he will not from there reap corruption but eternal life, the Lord saying, *For I was hungry and you gave me to eat*[^6], and answering those who asked, *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^7], and at the last commending the very reward, *So the wicked will go into eternal burning, but the just into eternal life*[^8]. But those who give food to the hungry Christ and drink to the thirsty Christ in his least ones minister carnal goods, according to the Apostle who says, *Is it a great thing if we reap your carnal goods?*[^9], and yet from the spirit they reap eternal life, sowing not, of course, in their own flesh but in the spirit, although they sow carnal goods. So much depends not on what is given but on the mind with which it is given: for what is given is earthly food, which, as the Lord notes and the thing itself proclaims, goes into the belly and is sent into the privy; but the giver's intention is added, sowing the carnal thing in the spirit, and a tiny portion of earthly fruits becomes the price of the kingdom of heaven.

**3.** What are these but the mighty and marvelous works of God? Spiritual things are sown and carnal things are reaped: for what else does this mean, *If we have sown spiritual goods for you, is it a great thing if we reap your carnal goods?*[^10] Likewise carnal things are sown and spiritual things are reaped: for what else does this mean, *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world, for I was hungry and you gave me to eat*[^11]? O the venerable exchanges of the saints! Great things are given and small ones are received, and again for those very small things great ones are repaid—if indeed those are to be called great and these small, as though the sun were great and the moon small, when both are heavenly lights; yet this is not even so to be compared, as if a small star were compared to the great sun, for both of these too are from heaven. Far be it from us so to speak of the great teaching of wisdom, which Christ's apostles preached, as though, as of the same kind, the kindness of the table that believers ministered to the preachers were small, when the one is the illumination of the mind, the other the sustenance of the belly; when it belongs to the one to surpass even the least thing and the sun, while the other could not endure under the sun. But now if the spiritual things which the holy teachers sow incomparably surpass these carnal things that they reap from the devout faithful, what is to be said of that eternal life which in the end is repaid, most precious, for these most worthless things? For God is then to be seen, to him we are to cling, in him we are to delight without end. *For this is eternal life,* he says, *that they may know you the one true God and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*[^12]. For this he openly promised when he said, *The one who has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me; and the one who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him and will show myself to him*[^13]. For these earthly, scant, and temporal things, then, for the fact that in his least ones food is given to the hungry Christ, he is going to repay not something of his own but his very self. How great will be the victor in that judgment whose reward will be the judge himself!

**4.** The Apostle, knowing this—what reward awaited these works—did not rejoice for his own advantage when these carnal goods were provided for his temporal needs. Therefore, when he spoke of this, he says, *Not that I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit*[^14]. For a laborer in the Lord's field could have provided for his sustenance from elsewhere, but it was an evil for the tree to remain barren. For the Lord fed Elijah by the ministry of ravens, and afterward gave him to a widow to be fed—not because there was no other source from which God's servant might be nourished, but so that she might have something by which to earn his Lord's favor. The dispensers of God, then, rejoice as they sow spiritual things and reap carnal things; but they rejoice not from greed for what is given but from love for those who give. Otherwise far happier would be those who, by providing earthly things, are in the end to receive heavenly things, than those who sow spiritual things and reap carnal things. But since, again, those very carnal things that they reap they refer to the advancement of those from whom they reap them, love embraces all in one end, so that at the same time all may be ruled by all without end. For this is the reason that God wills to teach his little ones through the dispensing of men, and to sustain his dispensers through the ministry of men, so that, while they supply each other's need, they may conceive goodwill and give birth to beneficence. Otherwise, just as he fed the hungry, with no one giving anything to anyone, when he willed and from what source he willed—as he fed Elijah through the birds of the air, the people of Israel through the manna from heaven—so too he can teach even the unlearned with no words of men, as he did Moses, as he did the other prophets, as he did the Apostle who says, *Not from men nor through man*[^15], as he did the other Apostle to whom it is said, *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, for flesh and blood has not revealed it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^16]. The fact, then, that he teaches some through whom he may teach others, and enriches some through whom he may feed others, when he could teach or feed all without the ministry of man, shows that he wills the works of mercy to be exercised by men, so that one, out of another's temporal want, may gain the merit of eternal abundance: for carnal supplies Elijah is sent to the widow whom the birds had been supplying with food; the people are commanded to provide tithes to the Levites, all of whom together the manna had satisfied; finally the Lord himself, made man for us, is received now by some Zacchaeus, now by some Martha, and, though he fed so many thousands with very few loaves, is yet fed by them as if he were poor; and likewise in the dispensing of spiritual things Ananias receives Saul, called from heaven, to be instructed; the angel sent to Cornelius sends him to Peter; the Holy Spirit comes upon all, and yet appoints some as teachers of others. Thus weakness makes those who are temporally poor necessary to one another, so that love may make all rich and eternity receive all when time is past.

**5.** Buy the field with the treasure, then, while there is time; buy the pearl that is incorruptible in its perpetual brightness: it is the kingdom of heaven that I am speaking of, preached in the Gospel by the Lord's own mouth. Our fathers bought it and left it still to be bought by us. It is kept whole by the seller for the one who will buy; it is bought in turn by all, possessed whole by each. And let no care for the price torment you; for it has not been set at a level the richer can easily reach and to which the poorer cannot stretch: as much as each one has, for so much is it worth to him. It accepted half the goods of the very wealthy Zacchaeus; it did not shut out the fisherman's nets; it admitted even the two coins of a certain needy widow, and, weighing them on the scale of love, found them heavier than the many gifts of many rich men; nor would you separate from this exchange a cup of cold water, where the buyer's love is fervent. And even if perhaps that be lacking, let him not despair: let him love, and he has bought. There are certain great resources of the poor: pious wills. For what is more precious than peace, or toward what do we strive through all our labors except toward eternal peace? Hear, then, the testimony of the angels on this matter at the birth of the Savior, *praising God and saying, Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will*[^17]. This is brought forth not from a purse, not from storehouses: it is kept in the heart, dispensed from the heart, and is neither corrupted when kept nor lost when dispensed.
