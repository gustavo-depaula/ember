# Sermon 25

**1** When we sang to God, we said to him: *Blessed is the man whom you instruct, O Lord, and teach out of your law*[^1]. Therefore the Gospel of God sounded, Zacchaeus gave alms. Learn. For what better law of God is there than the holy Gospel? For it is the law of the new Testament, of which you heard the prophet when it was read: *Behold, the days are coming, says the Lord, and I will accomplish over the house of Jacob a new Testament, not according to the Testament I established with their fathers when I led them out of the land of Egypt*[^2]. There the Testament is promised, here rendered; promised through the prophet, rendered through the Lord of the prophets. That Testament which is called old—read and see. The law of God was given even then; read, or hear when it is read, and see what was promised there. There the land of the earth was promised, a land flowing with milk and honey, but still earth. Yet if we understand spiritually, since that earth did not flow with milk and honey, there is another land that will flow with milk and honey, that land of which it is said: *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^3]. For this is the land of the dying. Do you seek milk and honey? *Taste and see how sweet the Lord is*[^4]. His grace is signified by the name of milk and honey. It is sweet and nourishing. But this grace, prefigured in the old Testament, is revealed in the new.

**2** Finally that law, on account of those who are carnally wise, and who seek such rewards from God, and want to worship God for the sake of the things promised there, deserved to hear from the apostle Paul that it begets unto slavery. Why? Because it is understood carnally by the Jews. For understood spiritually, it is the Gospel. Therefore it begets unto slavery. Whom? Those who serve God for the sake of earthly goods. When these are present to them, they give thanks; when they are absent, they blaspheme. For those who serve God for the sake of these things cannot serve with a true heart. For they attend to those who do not serve our God. For they see them have the very thing for whose sake they themselves serve God, and they say in their heart: "What does it profit me that I serve God? Do I have as much as that man who blasphemes daily? He prays, and is hungry; the other blasphemes, and belches." He who attends to these things is a man, a man of the old Testament. He, then, who in the new Testament worships God ought to hope for a new inheritance, not an old one. If you hope for a new inheritance, pass over the earth, tread upon the peaks of the mountains—that is, despise the loftiness of the proud. But when you have despised and trodden it, be humble, lest you fall from the height. Hear: *Hearts up high*; but *to the Lord*, not against the Lord. All the proud have their heart up high, but against the Lord. But if you want truly to have your heart up high, have it to the Lord. For if you have your heart up high to the Lord, he himself holds your heart, lest it fall to the earth.

**3** Blessed, then, is the man: *Blessed is the man whom you instruct, O Lord*[^5]. See, I speak, see, I cry out, see, I expound. Who hears me? I know who hears me: *Blessed is the man whom you instruct, O Lord*, the one to whom God speaks in the heart. And when I am silent, he is the *blessed one whom you instruct, O Lord, and teach out of your law*[^6]. What follows? Thus far we have sung: *And teach out of your law. That you may give him rest from the evil days, until the pit is dug for the sinner*[^7]. He is the man who is instructed by the Lord, he is the one who learns from the Lord out of the law of God, who is given rest from the evil days, until the pit is dug for the sinner. Hear what this is. There are evil days. Since we were cast out of paradise, do we not spend evil days here? Both our forebears bewailed their days, and their grandfathers bewailed their days. The days they spent in living have pleased no men. But the days of their forebears please those who come after; and to those forebears in turn those very days pleased, the days they themselves did not feel, and therefore they pleased. For what is present has a keen feeling. I do not say it is brought nearer, but it touches the heart daily. Almost every year we usually say, when we feel cold: "It was never so cold. It was never so hot." It is always the same one who "makes" it who makes it. But *blessed is the man whom you instruct, O Lord, that you may give him rest from the evil days, until the pit is dug for the sinner*[^8].

**4** The evil days. Are these days evil, which the circuit of the sun drives round? Evil men make the days evil, and almost the whole world is so. Amid the crowds of the evil the few grains groan. Let us turn it back to the just themselves. Those are evil, and they make the days evil. What of the just themselves? Are they not in evil days? Both among others—besides that they endure evil men among whom they groan—and within themselves, when they are, let them attend to themselves, let them descend into themselves, let them consider themselves well. They find evil days within themselves. They do not want war, they want peace. And who does not? And since all do not want war and all want peace, even he who lives justly turns his eyes upon himself, and finds war within himself. Ask me what war. *Blessed is the man whom you instruct, O Lord, and teach out of your law*[^9]. See, a man asks me what war the just man suffers within himself. Teach him out of your law. Let the Apostle speak: *The flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh*[^10]. And where do I throw off the flesh, if war sounds, if—which God forbid—the enemy rushes in? The man flees and drags his war with him wherever he goes. I do not say, if he is evil. Even if he is good, if he lives justly, he finds in himself what the Apostle says: *The flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh*[^11]. In this war, how are the days good?

**5** Therefore the days are evil. But let us be given rest. What is "let us be given rest"? Let us not be angry at the divine judgment. Let us say to him: *It is good for me that you have humbled me, that I may learn your statutes*[^12]. You dashed me from paradise, you cast me from blessedness. I am in hardship, I am in groaning. *My groaning is not hidden from you*[^13]. But *it is good for me that you have humbled me, that I may learn your statutes*[^14]. In the evil days I learn to seek the good days. What are the good days? Do not seek them now. Believe me—rather, believe with me—you will not find them. The evil days will pass, and good ones will come. But the good will come to the good, worse ones to the evil.

**6** For indeed I too ask you: *Who is the man who wants life?*[^15] I know, the hearts of all answer me: "For who is the man who does not want life?" I add: *And loves to see good days?*[^16] You all answer: "Who is there who does not love to see good days?" You have answered well. You want life, you want good days. Surely when I was saying, *Who is the man who wants life?* every man answers me: "I." *Who is the man who wants to see good days?* Does not each of you say in silence, "I"? Hear what follows: *Restrain your tongue from evil*[^17]. Now say "I." You seek pardon; now I find you. Past things have passed. Suppose your tongue was wicked, you were a whisperer, you were an accuser, you were a slanderer, you were a reviler—suppose you were all these things—let those things pass with the evil days; do not you pass with the evil days. For there is something to hold to so that you do not pass. The human thing runs like a river; the evil days run like a river. Hold to the wood, lest you be dragged off. See, the river runs: *All flesh is grass, and all the honor of the flesh is like the flower of grass*[^18]. It is cast headlong, it passes, the grass withers, the flower falls. To what do I hold? *The word of the Lord abides forever*[^19].

**7** *Restrain, then, your tongue from evil, and your lips that they speak no deceit*[^20]. You who wanted life, or want life and good days, *turn away from evil and do good. Seek peace*[^21], which we all long for even in this mortality of the flesh, and in this frailty of the flesh, and in this most lying vanity. All of you, seek peace. *Seek peace, and pursue it*[^22]. Where is it? Where do I pursue it? By what way did it pass? By what way did it pass, that I may pursue it? It passed through you, but it did not remain in you. To whom do I say this? To the human race, not to each one of you, but to the human race. Through the human race peace itself passed. As it was passing, the blind man cried out in yesterday's reading. And where did it go? First see what peace is, and see where it went, and pursue it. What is peace? Hear the Apostle. He was speaking of Christ: *He himself is our peace, who made both one*[^23]. Christ, then, is peace. Where did it go? *He was crucified, and buried, he rose from the dead, he ascended into heaven*[^24]. See where peace went. How do I pursue it? *Hearts up high*. Hear how you may pursue it. Daily indeed you hear it briefly, when it is said to you: *Hearts up high*. Think more loftily from there, and you will pursue it. Yet hear it also more fully, that you may pursue true peace, your peace, the peace that endured war for you; peace that, while it bore war for you, prayed for the enemies of peace, and said as it hung: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^25]. There was war, and peace flowed from the wood. It flowed, but afterward, what? *He ascended into heaven*. Seek peace. And how do you pursue it? Hear the Apostle: *If you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; savor the things that are above, not the things that are on earth. For you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God. When Christ appears, your life, then you too will appear with him in glory*[^26]. See the good days; let us long for them, for this let us live, for this let us pray, for this let us give alms.

**8** See now, by God's favor, it is winter. Think of the poor, how Christ, naked, may be clothed. When the Gospel was read, did we not all call Zacchaeus blessed, when Christ looked up at him, intent in the tree to see the one passing by? For when would he have hoped for him dwelling in his house? When he said to him, *Come down, Zacchaeus, today I must remain in your house*[^27], I heard the groans of your congratulation. As though you had all been in Zacchaeus and had received Christ. So the heart of every one of you said: "O blessed Zacchaeus! The Lord entered into his house. O blessed man! Can it happen to us?" Now Christ is in heaven. Recite to me, O Christ, the new Testament. Make me blessed out of your law. Recite it, that you may know you are not cheated of the presence of Christ. Hear the one who will judge: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^28]. Each one of you waits to receive Christ seated in heaven. Attend to him lying under the portico, attend to him hungering, attend to him suffering cold, attend to him needy, attend to him a stranger. Do, you who are accustomed; do, you who are not accustomed. The teaching grows; let the good works grow. You praise the sowing; show forth the harvest. Amen.
