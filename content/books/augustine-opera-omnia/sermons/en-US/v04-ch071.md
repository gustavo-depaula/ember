# Sermon 145a

**1.** You have heard that those who do not see and yet believe are more praised by the Lord than those who see and believe, and were even able to touch. For the apostle Thomas, when the Lord had shown himself to his disciples, was absent; and when he heard from them that Christ had risen, he said, *Unless I put my hand into his side, I will not believe*[^1]. What then, if the Lord had risen without his scars? For could he not have resuscitated his flesh so that no traces of his wounds remained in it? He could have done this; but unless he had kept the scars in his body, he would not have healed the wounds in our heart. He was touched, and he was recognized. It was too little to see with one's eyes; he wished to believe by his fingers. *Come*, he says, *put your fingers here*; I have not taken everything away, I have kept something for you to believe; *and see my side, and do not be unbelieving, but believing*. But when he revealed to himself what had remained doubtful to him, he cried out, *My Lord and my God!*[^2] He was touching the flesh, he was crying out the divinity. What did he touch? The body of Christ. Was the body of Christ the divinity of Christ? The divinity of Christ was the Word; the humanity of Christ was soul and flesh. He could not touch that soul, but he could understand it, because the body that had been dead was moved alive. But that Word is neither changed nor touched, neither fails nor advances; because *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*. This Thomas cried out; he was touching the flesh, he was calling upon the Word, because *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^3].

[^1]: See John 20:25
[^2]: John 20:27-28
[^3]: John 1:1,14
