# Sermon 163

**1. 1.** If we consider, brothers, what we were before the grace of the Lord, and what we began to be through the grace of the Lord; surely we find that just as men are changed for the better, so too the places of the lands which before were against the grace of God are now dedicated to the grace of God. *For we*, as the Apostle says, *are the temple of the living God; concerning which God says, I will dwell in them, and walk about*[^1]. But the images that were here knew how to be fixed, they did not know how to walk. But the presence of the majesty walks about in us, if it finds the breadth of love. Exhorting us to this the Apostle says, *Be enlarged, do not be drawing the yoke with unbelievers*[^2]. If we are enlarged, God walks about in us; but that we may be enlarged, let God himself work it. For if love makes the breadth, which knows no straits; see that God makes for himself breadth in us, the Apostle himself saying, *The love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^3]. On account of this breadth, I say, God walks about in us.

**2. 2.** Just now, when the Apostle's Epistle was recited, we heard: *Walk by the Spirit, and do not accomplish the desires of the flesh. For the flesh covets against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh. For these are opposed to one another, that you may not do what you wish*[^4]. He was speaking to the baptized: but he was still building the temple of God, he was not yet dedicating it. See, my brothers, how, when those earthly places themselves are turned to the better, some are torn down and broken, others changed to better uses; so we too are. The works of the flesh were in us. You heard them when they were mentioned: *But the works of the flesh are manifest*, he says, *which are fornications, uncleannesses, the service of idols, sorceries*—not kindly deeds, that is, not so named from good things, but from poisons—*contentions, enmities, heresies, envies, drunkennesses, and the like*; these are to be cast down, not changed: *which I foretell*, he says, *you, as I have foretold, that those who do such things will not possess the kingdom of God*[^5]. These things in us are to be broken like idols. But the very members of our body are to be turned to better uses, so that what served the uncleanness of desire may serve the grace of love.

**3. 3.** But see what he said, and attend diligently. We are workers of God, the temple of God is still being built. In its head it is already dedicated; since the Lord rose from the dead, death conquered, mortality consumed, he ascended into heaven; because the Psalm of the dedication of the house was written about him. Therefore after the passion he says, *You have turned my mourning into joy for me, you have torn my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness; that my glory may sing to you, and I be not pricked*[^6]. That dedication, then, was made after the passion in the resurrection. So our building too is now being made through faith, that the dedication itself too may be made through the final resurrection. Finally, after that Psalm of the dedication of the house, where the raising up of our head is shown, there is another Psalm after it, not before it, whose title reads thus: *When the house was being built after the captivity*[^7]. Recall the captivity, where we were before, when the devil possessed the whole world as a lump of unbelievers. On account of this captivity the Redeemer came; he poured out his blood, our price: his blood poured out, he blotted out the instruments of our captivity. *The law*, says the Apostle, *is spiritual; but I am carnal, sold under sin*[^8]. Before, sold under sin, but afterward freed by grace. After that captivity, the house is now being built; and that it may be built, the gospel is preached. For so the Psalm itself begins: *Sing to the Lord a new song*. And lest you think this house is built in one corner, as the schismatics or heretics build, attend to what follows: *Sing to the Lord, all the earth*[^9].

**4. 4.** *Sing to the Lord a new song*: against the old song, the New Testament, because first the Old Testament; the new man, that the old man be put off. *Put off*, he says, *the old man with his acts; and put on the new, who was created according to God in righteousness and the holiness of truth*[^10]. Therefore: *Sing to the Lord a new song; sing to the Lord, all the earth*. Sing, and build; sing, and sing well. *Announce day from day his salvation*; *announce day from day* his Christ. For what is, *his salvation*, but "his Christ"? For this salvation we prayed in the Psalm: *Show us, O Lord, your mercy, and give us your salvation*[^11]. The ancient righteous longed for this salvation, of whom the Lord said to the disciples, *Many wished to see what you see, and could not*[^12]. *And give us your salvation*. The ancient righteous said this: *Give us your salvation*; may we see your Christ while we live in this flesh. May we see in the flesh him who frees us from the flesh; may the flesh come that cleanses the flesh; may the flesh suffer, and redeem the soul and the flesh. *And give us your salvation, O Lord*. In this longing was that holy old man Simeon; in this longing, I say, was that holy old man, well-deserving of God, Simeon; without doubt he too said, *Show us, O Lord, your mercy, and give us your salvation*. In this longing, in such prayers, he received an answer that he would not taste death unless he had seen the Christ of the Lord. Christ was born, that one was coming, this one was going; but until that one came, this one was unwilling to go. Now mature old age was shutting him out, but sincere devotion was holding him back. But when he came, when he was born, when he saw him carried in his mother's hands, and his devout old age recognized the divine infancy; he took him in his hands and said, *Now, Lord, you dismiss your servant in peace; for my eyes have seen your salvation*[^13]. Behold whence he said, *Show us, O Lord, your mercy, and give us your salvation*. The longing of the old man was fulfilled, the old age of the world itself declining. He himself came to an old man, who found the world old. Therefore, if he found the world old, let the world hear: *Sing to the Lord a new song; sing to the Lord, all the earth*. Let the oldness be destroyed, let newness rise.

**5. 5.** *Sing to the Lord a new song, sing to the Lord*. See the contest of the builders. *Sing to the Lord, bless his name. Announce well*—which in Greek is "evangelize." What? *Day from day*. What day from day? *His salvation*. What day from day? Light from light, the Son from the Father, *his salvation. Announce among the nations his glory, among all peoples his wonders*[^14]. Behold how the house is built after the captivity. He is terrible above all gods. Above what gods? *For all the gods of the nations are demons; but the Lord made the heavens*[^15]. He made the holy ones, he made the apostles. For *the heavens declare the glory of God. There are no speeches nor words whose voices are not heard. Their sound went out into all the earth*[^16]: because all the earth sings the new song.

**5. 6.** Let us hear, then, the Apostle too, the architect of the Master: *As a wise architect*, he says, *I laid a foundation*[^17]. Let us hear, then, this architect, constructing some new things, casting down some old. *Walk by the Spirit*, he says—this is the new construction; *and do not accomplish the desires of the flesh*—this is the destruction of the old. *For the flesh*, he says, *covets against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh. For these are opposed to one another, that you may not do what you wish*[^18]. For you are still being built, you are not yet being dedicated. *That you may not do what you wish*.

**6. 6.** For what do you wish? That there be no desires at all of evil and unlawful delights. What holy person would not wish this? But he does not bring it about: as long as one lives here, this is not fulfilled. *For the flesh covets against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh. For these are opposed to one another, that what you wish to do*—that there be in you no desires at all of unlawful things—*you cannot*. What, then, remains? *Walk by the Spirit; and*, because you cannot bring it about that you consume the desires of the flesh, *do not accomplish the desires of the flesh*. You ought indeed to wish to consume them, to end them, and to extirpate them utterly in every way; but as long as they are in you, and there is another law in your members fighting against the law of your mind, *do not accomplish the desires of the flesh*. For what do you wish? That there be no desires of the flesh at all. They do not permit you to fulfill what you wish; do not you permit them to fulfill what they wish. What do you wish? That they not exist at all. But they exist. *The flesh covets against the spirit*: let the spirit covet against the flesh. *That you may not do what you wish*—that is, that there be not in you the very desires of the flesh; nor let them do what they wish, so that you accomplish their work. If it is not wholly yielded to you, do not you yield either. Let the battle first be equal, that at some time there may be victory.

**7. 7.** For indeed, without doubt, my brothers, it will be; let us believe, let us hope, let us love—there will at some time be victory, in the dedication of the house which is now being built after the captivity. For the last enemy, death, will be destroyed, when this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality. Premeditate the words of those who triumph: *Where, O death, is your struggle*[^19]? This is the voice of those who triumph, not of those who fight. But the voice of those who fight is: *Have mercy on me, O Lord, for I am weak; heal me, O Lord, for my bones are troubled, and my soul is troubled exceedingly; and you, O Lord, how long*[^20]? See the one laboring in the contest. *And you, O Lord, how long*? What is, *How long*? Until you prove that I am helping. For if I helped quickly, you would not feel the struggle; if you did not feel the struggle, you would as it were be proud of your own strength; and through this pride you would never reach victory. It was indeed said, *While you are still speaking I will say: Behold, here I am*[^21]; but God both is present when he delays, and what he delays is present, and by delaying is present; lest, by fulfilling a too-hasty will, he not fulfill perfect health.

**8. 8.** For he was not absent, my brothers, from the apostle Paul, who, when he was contending, feared lest he be exalted. *In the greatness*, he says, *of my revelations, lest I be exalted*. See him contending in the conflict, not yet triumphing in security. *In the greatness of my revelations, lest I be exalted*. Who says, *Lest I be exalted*? O terror, O trembling! Who says, *Lest I be exalted*? When his words are so blunting of elation, so suppressing of swelling, and he says, *Lest I be exalted*? It is too little that he says, *Lest I be exalted*; see the medicine that he says was applied to him. *Lest I be exalted*, he says, *there was given to me a sting of my flesh, an angel of Satan*. O poison, which is not cured but by poison! *There was given to me a sting of my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me*[^22]. The head was beaten, lest the head be exalted. O antidote, which is as it were made from the serpent, and therefore called "theriac"! For that serpent persuaded pride. Taste, and you will be like gods[^23]; this is the persuasion of pride. Whence he fell, thence he cast down. Deservedly, then, the venom of the serpent is healed by the serpent. What does the Apostle say? *For which I asked the Lord three times that he take it from me*. Where is, *While you are still speaking I will say: Behold, here I am*? *For which*, not once, but again and a third time, *I asked the Lord*. Did he not then too say, *And you, O Lord, how long*? But surely, because he delayed, was he therefore not present, and was it false, *While you are still speaking I will say: Behold, here I am*? For what? When the physician gives what you desire, is he present; when he cuts, is he not present? Do you not under the physician's instrument cry out for him to spare; and because he is more present, he cuts more? Finally, that you may know he was present, see what he answered to the one asking three times. *He said*, he says, *to me: My grace is sufficient for you; for power is perfected in infirmity*[^24]. I, he says, know; the best physician. I, he says, know into what swelling that goes which I wish to heal. Be still, let me apply what I know. *My grace is sufficient for you*: your will is not sufficient for you. These were surely the words of one contending, and in the contest in peril, and asking for divine help.

**9. 9.** But what will be the words of one triumphing? The words of one contending, while the house is being built: the words of one triumphing, when the house is at last dedicated. *Where, O death, is your struggle? Where, O death, is your sting? But the sting of death is sin*[^25]. Thus the Apostle said these things, as though he were already there. Finally, after these words, which it is agreed are about the future reception, not the present conflict; since he says, *Then shall come to pass*; it is not happening now, but *then shall come to pass*. What *shall then come to pass*? The saying *that is written: Death is swallowed up in victory. Where, O death, is your struggle? Where, O death, is your sting*? Then it shall come to pass that the sting of death be nowhere, that sin can nowhere be found. Why do you hasten? *Then it shall come to pass, then it shall come to pass*. Let humility deserve it in you, that it may then come to pass in you; lest pride not permit that it even then come to pass in you. *Then it shall come to pass*. Now meanwhile, while you fight, while you labor, while you are in peril, say, say, *Forgive us our debts*[^26]. Say it utterly while you fight, say it, say it truly, say it from the soul: *If we say that we have no sin, we lead ourselves astray*. You will be your own devil. *We lead ourselves astray, and the truth is not in us*[^27]. For we do not say a true thing, by saying that we have no sin; since here we are not without sin. Let us, then, say the truth, that at some time we may find security. Let there be truth in the fight, that security may be gained in victory. *Then it shall come to pass: Where, O death, is your sting? For the sting of death is sin*.

**10. 10.** But you presume on the law, because the law was given to you, and a precept was given to you. It is good for you that the Spirit give you life, lest the letter kill. I wish that you may will; but it is not enough that you will. You must be helped, that you may will fully, and fulfill what you will. For do you wish to see what the commanding letter avails without the Spirit helping? There he said it. When it was said, *Where, O death, is your sting? But the sting of death is sin*; he at once added, *But the power of sin is the law*[^28]. What is, *The power of sin is the law*? Not by commanding evils, or by forbidding goods: rather indeed by forbidding evils and commanding goods. *But the power of sin is the law*: because, *the law*, he says, *crept in, that the offense might abound*. What is, *that the offense might abound*? Because where there was no grace, the prohibition increased the desire; and when one presumes as it were on one's own power, a great vice was made. But what did grace do? *Where sin abounded, grace abounded the more*[^29]. The Lord came: all that you drew from Adam, all that you added by your own depraved morals, he forgave all, blotted out all; he taught prayer, promised grace; he declared the contest, came to the aid of the laborer, crowned the victor. *And so*, says the Apostle, *the law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good. Did that which is good, then, become death to me? Far from it! But sin, that it might appear as sin*[^30]. For when you were not forbidden, it was there; but it did not appear. *For I would not have known coveting*, he says, *unless the law said, You shall not covet. Sin, then, taking occasion, deceived me through the commandment, and through it killed me*[^31]. Behold what is, *The letter kills*[^32].

**11. 11.** If, then, you wish to escape the threatening law, flee to the helping Spirit. For what the law commands, faith hopes for. Cry to your God, that he help you. Do not remain guilty under the letter, but let God help you by his Spirit: lest the proud Jew be like you. For when the sting of death was sin, and the power of sin the law; what would human infirmity do, in which the will was wearied? *To will*, he says, *lies near at hand for me; but to accomplish the good I do not find*[^33]. What, then, would it do? Behold, *the sting of death is sin*, behold, *the power of sin is the law. But the law crept in, that the offense might abound. For if the law could give life, righteousness would surely be from the law. But Scripture has shut up all things under sin*[^34]. How did it *shut up*? Lest you wander, lest you be cast headlong, lest you be drowned; the law made bars for you, that, not finding by what way to go out, you might fly together to grace. *But Scripture has shut up all things under sin, that the promise*. He who promises, promises what he does, not what you do. If you were going to do it, God would be a foreteller, not a promiser. *But Scripture*, he says, *has shut up all things under sin, that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe*[^35]. Hear: *might be given*. Why are you proud? Hear: *might be given*. *For what do you have that you have not received*[^36]? Therefore, because the sting of death is sin, but the power of sin the law; and this from the good providence of God, that men might be shut up under sin, and seek a helper, seek grace, seek God, not presume on their own power; therefore here too, when he had said, *But the sting of death is sin, but the power of sin is the law*; why do you fear? Why do you labor? Why do you sweat? Hear what follows: *But thanks be to God, who gave us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ*[^37]. Surely you do not give yourself the victory? *Thanks be to God, who gave us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ*.

**12. 12.** Therefore, when you have begun to labor, fighting against the desires of the flesh, walk by the Spirit, call upon the Spirit, seek the gift of God. And if the law in the members fights against the law of your mind from the lower part—that is, from the flesh—and holds you captive under the law of sin; this too will be amended, this too will pass into the rights of victory. Only cry out, only call. *One ought always to pray, and not to fail*[^38]. Call utterly, call for help. *While you are still speaking*, he says, *behold, here I am*[^39]. Understand afterward, and you hear him saying to your soul, *I am your salvation*[^40]. When, then, the law of the flesh begins to fight against the law of the mind, and to lead you captive in the law of sin which is in your members; say in praying, say in confessing, *Wretched man that I am*. For what else is man? *What is man, that you are mindful of him*[^41]? Say, *Wretched man that I am*; because unless the Son of man had come, man would have perished. Cry out in your straits, *Who will free me from the body of this death*? where the law in my members fights against the law of my mind. *For I delight in the law of God according to the inner man*[^42]. *Who will free me from the body of this death*? If you say this faithfully, if humbly; most truly it is answered, *The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^43]. Let us turn to the Lord . . .
