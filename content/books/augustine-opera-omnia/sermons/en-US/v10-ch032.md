# Sermon 357: In Praise of Peace

**1.** It is time to exhort your love, according to the strength the Lord gives, to love peace, and to beseech the Lord for peace. Let peace, then, be our beloved and friend, with whom our heart may be a chaste bed; with whom there may be faithful rest and no bitter consortium, with whom there may be a sweet embrace and an inseparable friendship. To praise peace is more difficult than to have it. For if we wish to praise it, we strive for strength, we seek thoughts, we weigh words: but if we wish to have it, we have it and possess it without any labor. Those who love peace are to be praised: but those who hate it are better, meanwhile, to be pacified by teaching and by keeping silent, than to be provoked by reproaching. The true lover of peace is a lover of its enemies. For just as, if you loved this light, you would not be angry at the blind, but would grieve over them; for you would know what a good you took in, and so, seeing of how great a good they were cheated, they would seem to you worthy of mercy; and if you had resources, if skill, if medicine, you would run rather to heal them than to condemn them: so if you are a lover of peace, whoever you are, have mercy on him who does not love what you love, who does not have what you have. For such is the thing you love, that you do not envy your fellow-possessor. He has peace with you, and does not narrow your possession for you. Whatever earthly thing you love, it is difficult not to envy the one who has it. Then, if perhaps it comes into your mind to share the earth which you possess with a friend, that your benevolence may be praised, that even in these temporal things love may be proclaimed: if, then, you wish to share your earthly possession, like an estate, like a house, or whatever of this kind, with a friend; you share with one, and admit him to fellowship, and rejoice with him. You think to admit perhaps a third, and a fourth: now you attend to how many it holds, how many it bears, whether the house for dwelling, or the field for feeding; and you say: Now it does not hold a fifth, a sixth cannot dwell with us, when does so small a possession feed a seventh? It is the narrowness, then, not you, that excludes the rest. Love peace, have peace, possess peace, take to yourself as many as you can to possess peace: it will be the wider, the more it is possessed by more. An earthly house does not hold many fellow-dwellers: the possession of peace grows with many inhabitants.

**2.** How good it is to love! For this is to have. But who would not wish what he loves to grow? If you wish few to be with you in peace, peace will be small for you. If you wish this possession to grow, add a possessor. For that which I said, brothers: It is good to love peace, and to love it is itself to have it, at how great a price does it stand? With what voice should it be praised, with what heart should it be considered: This is to have, which is to love? Consider the other things, with desire for which men burn. See one love estates, silver, gold, numerous children, precious and adorned houses, most pleasant and most precious estates. Does he love this? He loves it. But does he who loves now have it? It can happen that the lover of all these is empty. When he does not have it, he loves, he burns with desire to have it; but when he has begun to have it, he is tortured with fear lest he lose it. He loves honor, he loves power. How many men, deprived, sigh for offices to be received? And very often the last day overtakes them before they reach what they love. At how great a price, then, does it stand, that when you have loved, you will have it? You do not seek at a price what you love; you do not walk to a patron, through whom you may reach it. Behold, where you stand, love peace, and what you love is with you. This thing is of the heart; nor do you share peace with friends as you share bread. For if you wish to share bread, the more there are for whom it is broken, the less there becomes from which to give. But peace is like that bread which, by breaking and giving, grew in the hands of the disciples of the Lord[^1].

**3.** Have peace, then, brothers. If you wish to draw the rest to it, first have it yourselves, first hold it. Let what you have be fervent in you, that it may kindle others. The heretic hates peace, and the bleary-eyed light. Is the light therefore bad, because the bleary-eyed cannot bear it? The bleary-eyed hates the light; but yet for the sake of light the eye was created. Effort, then, is given by those who love peace, and wish what they love to be possessed with them, that, with a possessor added, the possession may grow. Let them give effort, then, to cure the eyes of the bleary, with whatever resource, with whatever endeavor. Unwilling he is cured, he does not wish it while he is being cured; but when he has seen the light, he will be delighted. Suppose he is angry; do not be wearied by persisting. Lover of peace, attend, and be yourself first delighted by the beauty of your beloved, and burn to draw another. Let him see what you see, love what you love, hold what you hold. Your beloved, whom you love, addresses you; she speaks to you: Love me, and at once you have me. Bring with you whom you can to love me: I shall be chaste, and remain whole. Bring whom you can, let them find, hold, fully enjoy. If many seeing do not corrupt this light; do many loving corrupt me? But they will not come, because they have nothing whereby they can see me. They will not come, because the splendor of peace strikes back the bleariness of dissension. See the pitiable voice of the bleary. It is announced to them: It has been decided that Christians should have peace. On receiving such news, they say among themselves: Woe to us. Why? Unity is coming. What is it? What a voice: Woe to us, unity is coming? How much more justly might you say: Woe to us, dissension is coming? Far be it that dissension should come: this is the darkness of those who see. For unity is coming, there should be rejoicing, brothers. Why were you frightened? Unity is coming, it was said. Was it said: A wild beast is coming, fire is coming? Unity is coming, light is coming. If he wishes to answer truthfully, he will say to you: I was not frightened because a wild beast is coming; for I am not timid; but I was frightened because light is coming; for I am bleary-eyed. Effort, then, must be given to healing. We must share with them what is not made narrow by sharing, according to our strength, as much as we can, as much as God grants.

**4.** Accordingly, dearest ones, I exhort your love to show them Christian and catholic gentleness. Now they are being urged toward healing. The eyes of the saints are inflamed, they must be cautiously healed and gently handled. Let no one take up a quarrel with anyone, let no one now wish even to defend his own faith by altercation, lest from the quarrel a spark be born, lest an occasion be afforded to those seeking an occasion. Plainly, when you hear an insult, bear it, ignore it, pass it by. Remember he must be healed. See how coaxing physicians are to those whom they heal even with biting remedies. They hear an insult, they apply the medicine, and do not return insult for insult. Let word answer word; so that one is to be healed, the other heals: not two quarreling. Bear it, I beg, my brothers. But I cannot bear it, he says, because he blasphemes the Church. This the Church asks of you, that you bear it, because the Church is blasphemed. He detracts, he says, from my bishop; he speaks a charge against my bishop, and shall I keep silent? Let him speak the charge, and keep silent; not by acknowledging it, but by bearing it. This you render to your bishop, if for that time you are not mixed up in it. Understand the time, take counsel. How many blaspheme your God? You hear it, and does he not hear it? You know it, and does he not know it? And yet *he makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^2]. He shows patience, he defers power. So you too recognize the time, do not provoke the swollen eyes to disturb themselves. Are you a lover of peace? Let it be well in your heart with your beloved. And what shall I do? You have something to do. Take away quarrels, turn to prayers. Do not repel the insulter with insults, but pray for him. Do you wish to speak to him against him: speak to God for him. I do not tell you to keep silent; but choose rather where you may speak, with whom, silent, you may speak, with lips closed, with heart crying out. Where he does not see you, there be good for him. But to him who does not love peace, and wishes to quarrel, answer peaceably: Whatever you wish to say, however much you hate, however it pleases you to detest, you are my brother. What do you do, that you may not be my brother? Plainly, good, bad, willing, unwilling, you are my brother. And he: How am I your brother, foe, enemy? Just by saying these things, you are my brother. It seems strange: he hates, detests, and is a brother? Do you wish me to believe him not knowing what he says? Whose health I desire, that he may see the light, and recognize his brother. Do you, then, wish me to believe him, that I am not his brother, because he detests it, because he hates it, and not rather the light itself? Let us hear what the light itself says. Read the prophet: *Hear, you who fear, the word of the Lord*. The Holy Spirit speaks through the prophet Isaiah: *Hear, you who fear, the word of the Lord. Say: You are our brothers, to those who hate you, and who detest you*. What is this? The light has shone, it has shown the brotherhood: and still the bleary-eyed says: Close the window. Offer your eyes to the light: recognize the brother not set in darkness, you set in darkness: and say, say securely, you say the words of God and not mine. *Say*, says God, *You are our brothers*: to whom? *To those who hate you*. For what wonder if you say it to those who love you? *To those who hate you, and who detest you*[^3]. Why this? Hear, and look at the fruit of the cause. As though you had asked the Lord your God, and said: Lord, how shall I say: You are my brother, to one who hates, who detests? Say why. *That the name of the Lord may be honored. Let him appear at least in gladness: but let them be ashamed*[^4]. See, I beg, the fruit of patience, of such great gentleness. *Say: You are our brothers*. Why? *That the name of the Lord may be honored*. But why does he not recognize you as a brother? Because the name of man has honored men. So say: My brother: though you hate, though you detest, you are my brother. Recognize in yourself the sign of my Father. The word of our Father. Bad brother, quarrelsome brother, you are my brother. For you say, as I too: *Our Father who are in heaven*[^5]. We say one thing: why are we not in one? I ask you, brother, recognize what you say with me, and condemn what you do against me. Attend to the words coming forth from your mouth. Hear, not me, but yourself. See to whom we say: *Our Father who are in heaven*. Not a friend, not a neighbor; but he himself to whom we say it commands us to be in concord. Together we have one voice toward the Father: why do we not together have one peace?

**5.** Say such things ardently, say them gently. Say them burning with the fervor of love, not with the swelling of dissension, and beseech the Lord with us by solemn fasts. What we now render to God, let us render also for the cause. For now we fast solemnly after Pentecost: and we would surely fast, even if this cause did not exist. What, then, do we owe to our brothers, whom in the name of the Lord our God, our physician, we take up to be healed and cured, offering them to him that they may be healed, not presuming for ourselves the physician's hands? But what do we do? Let us beseech the physician himself, fasting with humble heart, with pious confession, with brotherly fear. Let us show to the Lord piety, to the brothers love. For let our alms grow, by which our prayers may be more easily heard. Pursue hospitality. It is the time: the servants of God are coming. It is the time, it is the occasion, why does it perish? Attend to what you have in the upper room of your house. Attend also to what you may store above, what you may keep for yourself, about which treasure alone you are secure. Place it above, entrust it not to your servant, but to your Lord. Do you there fear lest a thief creep in, lest a burglar break in, lest a turbulent enemy snatch it away? Bring it about that you have something to be repaid you. Nor is this repaid you, what you have laid up. The Lord wishes you to be a lender, but his own, not your neighbor's.
