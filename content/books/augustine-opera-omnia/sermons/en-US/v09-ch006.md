# Sermon 306/E: A Sermon of Saint Augustine the Bishop on the Feast of the Holy Martyr Quadratus

**1.** The day of the solemn feast of the crown of Quadratus, foretold to Your Charity, has dawned today: let us celebrate it both by gathering and by preaching. We speak of the glory of the martyrs before God and men as often as we celebrate their feasts. Let frequent celebration be frequent exhortation, that there may be exultation. We have sung a psalm well suited to the feast of a martyr: *Like a heap of sand I was thrust, that I might fall, and the Lord upheld me*[^1]. For the whole choir of martyrs says this, the body of Christ in the world, living amid trials like Lot in Sodom, seeing many things it does not want, but by no means consenting to the deeds of the wicked. For the human mind is driven to sin in various ways, either by the enticement of pleasure or by the bite of tribulation; and whoever, so as not to sin through enticement, has conquered pleasure, has made great progress, yet has not reached perfection. For the love of righteousness ought to be so great that pleasure yields to it, and that it does not itself yield to pain. Whoever has come to this, has reached perfection. Therefore the martyrs are believed to have gone out of the world perfect, because they conquered by striving not only against the pleasures of the world, but also against tribulations. Finally, as the faithful know, among the Christian sacraments we do not pray for the martyrs. And not only do we not pray for them, but we commend ourselves to their prayers.

**2.** This perfection Paul's letter to the Hebrews demands, by rebuking and requiring it, where it is said: *For you have not yet resisted to the point of blood in your struggle against sin*[^2]. That perfection is demanded; he to whom it is said, "You have not yet done it," is admonished to do it. *For you have not yet resisted to the point of blood in your struggle against sin.* But the martyrs resisted to the point of blood. Yet they did not contend against the persecuting man, but against the plotting devil, and—if you gather the truth—against their own weakness. For in the man himself a great contest is waged, where the theater is the conscience; but the supreme spectator is the inspector of the conscience. But if he were only the inspector of the conscience and not the helper, every contender would fail. Accordingly, in the very words we sang you have what I say: *Like a heap of sand,* it says, *I was thrust, that I might fall.* Why, then, did you not fall? *And the Lord upheld me*[^3]. As though confessing, he said this about his weakness. As far as concerns me, he says, *I would have fallen,* but *the Lord upheld me.* Be, then, in the Lord, abide in the Lord, and when trial thrusts you, you will not fall, because you do not yield. If by not resisting you do not yield, by not consenting you do not fall.

**3.** Take a likeness from the very name of the one whose feast we celebrate: he was called Quadratus (the Square). When you thrust a squared stone, it yields, and does not fall. It yields by not resisting, but it does not fall by remaining upright on every side. For a square is upright on every side: it is thrust on one side, received on another, it can never fall. Deservedly the Lord God too commanded the ancient righteous man Noah to build the ark of squared timbers. He wanted timbers used for the building that were both incorruptible and squared: incorruptible for the sake of eternity, squared for the sake of avoiding the trial. Let us too be squared, indeed let the whole body of Christ be the Massa of the Square. For it has a cornerstone, as was sung: *The stone,* it says, *which the builders rejected has become the head of the corner*[^4]. How, then, has it become the head of the corner, which the builders rejected? He means the Pharisees and scribes and teachers of the law. They rejected this one as though he were not the one; they said: "He is not the one. We do indeed await the Christ, but we do not recognize this one." By confessing that they awaited the Christ, they were, as it were, builders; but by saying that they did not recognize the very one who was true, they rejected the cornerstone, because they themselves were not on the rock but on sand. So those builders, by boasting rather than by doing the work, rejected the cornerstone. But lest you wonder how a stone rejected by the builders became the head of the corner, Scripture added: *This was the Lord's doing*[^5]. By those builders indeed rejected, but made the head of the corner by the Lord. From opposite sides two walls ran into this corner: the circumcision and the uncircumcision. If you consider where they come from, what is so different? If you consider where they cohere, what is so much one? For walls coming from opposite directions make a corner, but cohering with one another in one place in the bond of peace. It came to pass: they came from the circumcision, they came from the uncircumcision, they were joined to one another in the cornerstone. What the Apostle says was fulfilled: *He is our peace, who made both one*[^6].

**4.** Accordingly, dearly beloved, striving toward the cornerstone, come as squares, that the builder may not reject you. Yet hope wholly in God: he himself squares who binds together. *You,* says the apostle Peter, *like living stones, are built together into a holy temple of God*[^7]. We ought so to think of the temple of God that we likewise know that God does not need a temple, but the temple needs God; lest we suppose that we do God a favor, in giving him somewhere to dwell. That God dwells in you profits you. By indwelling, he so makes you blessed; he is not made blessed by the dwelling. For he is so the true Lord, who does not need a servant, and yet has a servant whom he does not need, but for whom he provides. For human weakness needs a servant, that the servant may do some work which the master cannot, and in doing what he can, may help the master who cannot. But God is almighty; he who made you does not need you. For you do not merit anything because you carry the Lord, you who merited to be made by the Lord. Do not reckon that you do God a favor because you believe in God. For if you do not believe, you are harmful not to him, but to yourself. This voice is expressed in the psalm where it is said: *I said to the Lord: You are my God, for you have no need of my goods*[^8]. Therefore my Lord is the true God the Lord, who has no need of my goods, but I of yours.

**5.** Let no one think himself capable of squaring himself, just as he was capable of corrupting himself, because no one ought to think himself capable of healing himself, even if he was capable of wounding himself. Darkness is in the eye's power, for it is closed and darkness is in it. When you have closed it, you are in darkness. So it was in your power to be in darkness by closing your eyes; but is it in your power to see, when you have opened your eyes, unless light is present in which you can see? The closing of your eyes had no need of help in order not to see, but the opening of the eyes has need of help in order to see. For unless light is present—whether of the sun or a lamp or the moon or any kind of light—unless it is present to the opening, your eyes, even open, see nothing. Ours, then, is to receive, ours is to have. But *what do you have that you did not receive?*[^9]

**6.** Therefore the godly voice says *like a heap of sand*[^10]—not a heap of sand, but *like a heap of sand;* for the body of Christ, though it was made solid and squared, was thought a heap of sand by the blind, and because it was thought a heap of sand, therefore it did not fall. Yet it did not even attribute this to its own strength, since it added: *The Lord upheld me*[^11]. In all these trials of the world, then—for Sodom has not yet burned, indeed another great Sodom, for that one burned as an example, another is kept for judgment—in these trials of the world, then, let us daily meditate on contending *against sin to the point of blood*[^12], which is said in another saying: *Strive for the truth unto death*[^13]. What was said there, *against sin,* this was said here, *for the truth.* And what was said there, *to the point of blood,* this was said here, *unto death.* The mind itself ought to be that of a martyr, for God is not delighted by shed blood: he has many martyrs in secret. *Peace on earth to men of good will*[^14].

**7.** Nor is that persecution to be wished for, which our forefathers suffered from earthly powers, that they might become martyrs. The world does not cease, the abundance of trials does not cease. Sometimes there is a fever, and you contend. To pass over the various threats of adversaries, the various trials of each individual, what I had begun to say: there is a fever, and you contend. You are in bed, and you are an athlete. You are sick, and you fight, and you conquer. For what if, to one who is feverish and set in danger of death, someone comes who promises to drive away your fever by certain incantations—and those incantations are unlawful, diabolical, to be detested and anathematized—and then by the one who urges this on you, and of those who have been so healed, many examples are set before you, and it is said to you: "When that man had this, he did this for him, he chanted over him, he purified him, he was present to him, and he was made well. Inquire of him, ask him, hear him." And let them too say: "It truly happened; we were almost dead, and so we were delivered, and let it be a matter of faith to you that if you admit that incantation, you will at once be delivered from that plague." If you do not consent, will you not be a martyr, who chose rather to die, and did not consent to the sacrilege? For what the wicked judge said to the martyr set on the chain or the rack: "Consent to the sacrifice, and I release you from this tribulation," this the devil says in secret to the feverish man: "Consent to this sacrilege, and I release you from this fever."

**8.** If, then, you do not consent, if you conquer not a man but the devil, not just any fellow sinner of yours, but the very prince of sinners—but *because your struggle is not against flesh and blood,* that is, against the men from whom you suffer the troubles of either persuasions or tribulations, *but against the principalities and powers and rulers of the world,* not of heaven and earth, but *of this darkness*[^15], that is, of the unclean and the wicked, from whom, when delivered, the Apostle says: *You were once darkness*[^16]—if, then, you do not consent, do not think yourself no martyr. Your feast is indeed not celebrated, but your crown is prepared. For it is the custom for the feast of those to be celebrated who contended publicly. How many martyrs have gone forth from a bed, and from that sickness passed as victors to the things above. Know, then, that you are being tried, if you suffer anything of this kind, if anything of this kind is urged upon you; there you have need to have the mind of a martyr, since he who made you watches you, and he who called you helps you. There it is for you to say that word of the saints which I found: *God is able to deliver* me from this deadly fever, *but even if not*[^17]. Let the enchanter be a contest for you, because the one to whom you speak is not a transgressor. So after these words, whatever pleases your Lord concerning you will be done. Either you are restored alive, or you are joined to the angels. He will do what he has chosen concerning you: be you prepared for either, if you want to be square. Turned to the Lord . . .

**9.** Do you think that those words of the psalm which you have just heard: *The Lord chastening has chastened me, and has not handed me over to death*[^18], are only the voice of Daniel, who closed the mouths of the lions? Do you think this is only the voice of the three young men, who by faith made trial of the flames among which they walked about? Do you think this voice is theirs alone: *The Lord chastening has chastened me, and has not handed me over to death,* because the lions did not devour the one, nor the fires burn the others? Far be it! Do not suppose this; this voice is not theirs alone: it is also that of the Maccabees. For the God of these was not another, and of those another; nor was he present to these and absent from those, nor a helper to these and angry with those. He is the same God, able to deliver in either way, that he may show that he holds all things in his power. By closing the mouths of the lions, he chastened Daniel in his humiliation, but did not hand him over to death. By cooling the fires, he chastened the three young men in their humiliation, but did not hand them over to death. By making the Maccabees victors in the fire, who would not yield to torments, but would contend against sin to the point of blood, chastening he chastened them, and did not hand them over to death. For all those live. To what death did he not hand them over, I say? The devil wanted to hand them over to the second death, the eternal death, not the passing one. So those he openly delivered, those he secretly crowned; neither these nor those did he hand over to death.

**10.** We ought, then, always to be prepared for either, as I said. For God is able to deliver from every tribulation. But above all we ought to pray that he deliver us from tribulation, that we may be victors over tribulation, that we may not yield to tribulation unto sinning. And do not think yourself a martyr only if it is said to you: "Deny Christ," and you do not deny. When you think of doing something against righteousness, nothing else is said to you than: "Deny Christ." Surely the great martyr John the Baptist was beheaded, and yet it was not said to him: "Deny Christ." He spoke the truth to a bad king: the angry king ordered him shut up, the delighted king ordered him killed. For there was dancing so that he might be killed: she danced, he fell. For he fell rather, by killing a righteous man. For the one killed did not fall, but he who ordered the killing. And yet it was not said to him: "Deny Christ," but because he died for the truth, he died for Christ, who said: *I am the way, the truth, and the life*[^19].

**11.** And he who says to you: "Bear false witness for me," says nothing else to you than: "Deny Christ." For if you confess Christ with the tongue and bear false witness, there comes to be in you what Paul says: *For they confess that they know God, but by their deeds they deny him*[^20]. You confessed by word, you denied by deed. God judges the denier by deed more than the confessor by word. For deeds are more than words. To do and to say is good; but to say and not to do is not. So you think yourself a martyr, if you contend. "Bear false witness for me." "I will not." "Just take this and say it." "I will not take it." You have conquered greed. If you conquer fear too, you have contended against sin to the point of blood. He who did not entice you with a reward will try to break you with threats, proposing that he will pursue enmities, will harm you more gravely, and, when he has the power, will kill you—and perhaps he already has it—what do you do? He does not say: "Deny Christ," yet he says: "Act against Christ," that &lt;is&gt; against him who said: *You shall not bear false witness*[^21]. How many such trials there are daily, not against all, as against the whole Church, as if a heap of sand is being thrust which is thought a heap of sand, but for the most part the enemy fights against the single grains, sometimes tempting one by one. What does it matter whether he tempts one by one, or compels all together? The tempter is not lacking, but let the wrestler not be lacking either, because the one who crowns will not be lacking. Turned to the Lord . . .
