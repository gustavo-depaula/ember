# Sermon 59: Again on the Lord's Prayer in Matthew 6:9-13, to the Candidates for Baptism

**1. 1.** You have recited what you believe, you have heard what you should pray. Since you could not call upon him in whom you had not believed, as the Apostle says: *How will they call upon him in whom they have not believed?*[^1] Therefore you first learned the Creed, where is the rule of your faith, brief and grand; brief in the number of words, weighty in the weight of meanings. But the Prayer which today you have received, to hold and to recite eight days from now, as you heard when the Gospel was read, was handed down by the Lord himself to his disciples, and from them it came to us, *since their sound went out into all the earth*[^2].

**1. 2.** So do not cling to earthly things, you who have found a Father in heaven. For you are going to say: *Our Father, who are in heaven*[^3]. You have begun to belong to a great family. Under this Father, the rich man and the poor man are brothers, under this Father, the master and the servant are brothers, under this Father, the emperor and the soldier are brothers. All the faithful Christians have different fathers on earth, some noble, some lowly; but they call upon one Father, who is in heaven. If our Father is there, there an inheritance is prepared for us. And such a Father is this, that we may possess with him what he gives. For he gives an inheritance, but he does not leave it to us by dying; for he does not depart that we may succeed him, but he abides that we may come to him. Because, then, we have heard of whom we should ask, let us know also what we should ask, lest perhaps we offend such a Father by asking evil things.

**2. 3.** What, then, did the Lord Jesus teach us to ask of the Father who is in heaven? *Hallowed be your name*[^4]. What kind of benefit is it that we ask of God, that his name be hallowed, since it cannot be that his name not be holy? The name of God is always holy; why, then, do we ask that it be hallowed, except that we may be hallowed through it? What is always holy, then, we pray to be hallowed in us. The name of God will be hallowed in you when you are baptized. Why will you pray this even after you have been baptized, except that what you have received may persevere in you?

**2. 4.** There follows another petition: *Your kingdom come*[^5]. Whether we ask or do not ask, the kingdom of God is going to come; why, then, do we ask, except that there may come to us too what is going to come to all the saints, that God may have us too in the number of his saints, to whom his kingdom is to come?

**2. 5.** We say in the third petition: *Your will be done, as in heaven and on earth*[^6]. What is this? That as the angels serve you in heaven, so we too may serve you on earth. But his holy angels obey him, do not offend him; they do his commands by loving him. This, then, we pray, that we too may do the precept of God by love. Again these words are understood in another way: *Your will be done, as in heaven and on earth.* Heaven in us is the soul, earth in us is the body. What is, then: *Your will be done, as in heaven and on earth?* As we have heard your precepts, so may our flesh consent to us, lest, while flesh and spirit contend, we be able to fulfill the precepts of God less. Yet, dearly beloved, when the flesh lusts against the spirit[^7], as earth against heaven, let the spirit too lust against it, lest earth cast down heaven. And if we cannot take away this contention, let us deny consent. These words are understood also thus: *Your will be done, as in heaven and on earth.* Heaven are the faithful, who have put on the image of the heavenly man, that is, Christ. But the unbelievers, since they bear the image of the earthly man[^8], are called earth. When, then, we say: *Your will be done, as in heaven and on earth*, this we say to our good Father: "As the faithful have believed in you, may the unbelievers believe too." And thus we learn to pray for our enemies.

**3. 6.** There follows in the prayer: *Give us this day our daily bread*[^9]. Whether we ask of the Father the provision necessary for the body, signifying by bread whatever is necessary for us, or whether we understand that daily bread which you are going to receive from the altar, we ask well that he give it us this day, that is, in this time. For in this time it is necessary, when we hunger; but when we shall be in another life, hunger will perish: shall we then have need to ask for bread? Or whether we receive, as I said, that bread from the altar, we pray well that he give it us. For what is it that we pray, except that we not commit any evil whence we may be separated from such bread? And the word of God which is daily preached is bread. For it is not, because it is not the bread of the belly, therefore not the bread of the mind. But when this life has passed, we shall neither seek that bread which hunger seeks, nor have to receive the sacrament of the altar, because there we shall be with Christ whose body we receive; nor will these words which we say to you have to be said to us, nor a book to be read, when we shall see him who is the Word of God, through whom all things were made, by whom the angels are fed, by whom the angels are illumined, by whom the angels are made wise—not seeking the words of winding speech, but drinking the one Word, and filled from there, they belch forth praises and do not fail in praises. *For blessed*, says the psalm, *are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they shall praise you*[^10].

**4. 7.** So in this life too we ask what follows: *Forgive us our debts*[^11]. In baptism all your debts, that is, your sins, absolutely all, will be forgiven you. But because no one can live here without sin—and if not with a great crime whence he is separated from that bread, yet no one can be without sins on this earth—and we cannot receive baptism except once; but in prayer we receive whence we may be washed daily, that our sins may be daily forgiven us, but if we do what follows: *as we too forgive our debtors*[^12]. And so, my brothers, I admonish you: you will be sons of God, not of any great man whatever. Does your count deign to adopt any of you? Divine grace has made all sons. So because you are going to say it daily even after baptism, and more after baptism (for you will not pray this prayer except after baptism, for eight days from now you are to recite it, not pray it; after baptism you will pray it—for how does one say *Our Father* who is not yet born?)—because, then, you are going to say this prayer daily, I admonish you, my sons in the grace of God and my brothers under that Father, I admonish you that when anyone offends you and sins against you and comes and confesses and asks you to forgive him, you at once forgive from the heart, lest you yourselves hinder the pardon coming to you from God. For if you do not forgive, neither will he forgive you. And this God says to you: You justly ask pardon of me, who cannot sin; and, although no sin can be found in me, yet I forgive, and you are unwilling to forgive. Behold, I grant it, do not forgive. Act, then, so that I may not find in you what I am compelled to avenge. It is lawful for you to be avenged on a man who has offended you. But he asks pardon. He was an enemy, but by asking pardon he breaks the enmities. But I am unwilling, you say, I wish to be avenged. Beware lest it be avenged on you. You, a man who have sin, wish sin to be avenged. See lest it be avenged on you, in whom sin can be found. So this too we ask in this life: because here they can be forgiven, where sins can be committed. But in that life they are not forgiven, because they are not even had.

**5. 8.** But from there we ask, saying: *Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil*[^13]. He is led into temptation who consents to the tempter. For in this life to be tempted is useful, but to be led into temptation is not expedient. So when one tempts you who wishes to corrupt you with money, that you may do something evil for the money received, you are tempted but proven. If you do not consent, you will be found pure. And I give you counsel: despise avarice, and let money not corrupt you; against temptation close the door and set the bolt, the love of God. And who can do this, unless he helps who is asked by us? And men are tempted in various ways, tempted by rewards, tempted by threats, that one who cannot seduce by corrupting may seduce by terrifying. But the man who is fixed in God, and whom God hears saying: *Lead us not into temptation*, conquers evil loves, conquers empty terrors. So this too is necessary for us to ask in this life, that we not be led into temptation (because here are temptations), that we be delivered from evil (because here is evil). And through this all these petitions are seven: three pertain to eternal life, four pertain to the present life. *Hallowed be your name:* it will always be. *Your kingdom come:* this kingdom will always be. *Your will be done, as in heaven and on earth:* it will always be. *Give us this day our daily bread:* it will not always be. *Forgive us our debts:* it will not always be. *Lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil:* it will not always be, but where there is temptation and where there is evil, there it is necessary for us to ask. The prayer strengthens you, so that you not only learn to ask of God, the Father who is in heaven, what you desire, but also learn what you ought to desire. Amen.
