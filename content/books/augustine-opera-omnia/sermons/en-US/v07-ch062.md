# Sermon 272/B: (Augmented). a Sermon of Saint Augustine on Pentecost

**1.** Today I believe that your Charity knows that the Church celebrates the coming of the Holy Spirit of the Lord: for the Lord promised that he would send the Holy Spirit to his apostles, and according to his most faithful promise he surely fulfilled what he promised. For as the resurrection of the Lord confirmed in the faith of men his divinity, who for our sake condescended to become man, so much more his ascent into heaven, and more fully and more perfectly by the gift of the Holy Spirit which he sent and filled his disciples, now made new wineskins, that they might be able to receive the new wine—therefore when they spoke in tongues, they were said to be *drunk and full of new wine.* The voice of those who heard was a testimony of the Lord's Scripture: *for no one puts new wine into old wineskins*, the Lord had said. He was preparing, then, new wine for new wineskins. For they were old wineskins, as long as they thought carnally about Christ. For to the old wineskin belonged that saying of the apostle Peter, when the Lord said to him, fearing lest Christ should die and as it were perish as other men too: *Get behind me, Satan; you are a stumbling block to me*[^1]. This faltering of Peter belonged to the old wineskin. But when the Lord rose, and showed himself to them, and they handled what they had bewailed hanging on the cross; they saw living members, which they bewailed as dead and buried; they were confirmed in faith and believed him. He ascended into heaven, and commands that they gather themselves into one place and there await until he himself should send his promise. Gathered, then, into one place, praying and desiring the promise, they put off the oldness and put on newness. Made now capable, then, they received the Holy Spirit on the day of Pentecost. And not without cause do we celebrate this day as a great and most evident sacred sign. But let your Holiness attend, how the old and new Scriptures harmonize with each other: for there grace was promised, here given; there figured, here perfected. As, if some craftsman of things to be formed from some metal, bronze or silver, first composes from wax the forms which he is going to cast, and the first sketch makes the way for the future solidity—for he makes the very forms which he is going to fill—so too the Lord, for the old people, sketched and outlined all things in figures, but for the new people fulfilled them by a most perfect outpouring. What, then, that forming is, and what this fulfillment on the day of Pentecost, let your Holiness attend somewhat more intently: it is worth the effort of attention; with great fruit is it learned, when what is spoken is heard more intently. Be you too surely new wineskins, that through our ministry you may be able to receive the wine.

**2.** It is often asked of us: "If we celebrate the day of Pentecost because of the coming of the Holy Spirit, why do the Jews celebrate Pentecost?" For they too have a Pentecost. You heard this morning, you who were attentive, when the reading of Tobit was read at the memorial of blessed Theogenes, that on the day of Pentecost he made himself a dinner, about to invite some of his own who were worthy to share the table with him, in that there was in them the fear of the Lord: *On the day of Pentecost*, he says, *which is holy, of the weeks*[^2]. For seven times seven make forty-nine: to this one is added because of unity, that we may return to the head, because unity confirms all multitude, and a multitude, unless it is bound by unity, is quarrelsome and contentious; but a multitude that shares makes one soul, as even of those very ones who received the Holy Spirit, as Scripture says, *there was one soul and one heart toward God*[^3]. They become, then, fifty, which is the sacred sign of Pentecost. Why, then, do the Jews celebrate it, except because there it was a figure? Attend: you know—and there is no Christian at all who is ignorant of what I am going to say—that among the Jews a lamb is slain, and Easter is celebrated in figure of the future Lord's passion. For this too was commanded them, that they should seek a lamb from goats and sheep. From where can a lamb be found from goats and sheep? But what was there commanded as impossible announced the future possibility of the Lord: for a lamb from goats and sheep was found, because our Lord Jesus Christ, born according to the flesh of the seed of David, has his origin from sinners and the just. You find in the origin of the Lord, according to the generations which the Evangelists expound, both many sinners and the just: therefore he also called such, that is, sinners, because he came through such too. For he gathers his Church from the just and sinners, about to send the just into the kingdom of heaven, and to separate the sinners who persevere in sins and wickedness; yet he so came to bear our sins, that he did not disdain to take up the origin of sinners. And there are many sacred meanings there, in the generations themselves, which God will grant that there be time to expound to your Holiness. Now, however, let us return to what we had begun.

**3.** We were speaking about the day of Pentecost, why the Jews celebrate that day. They slay a lamb, it is the slaying of the paschal lamb: so we too celebrate Easter, where the lamb was slain, immaculate, without fault, truly a lamb, to whom John bore witness, saying: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sins of the world*[^4]; by his passion we celebrate Easter. The law was given to the Jews in fear; the Holy Spirit was given to Christians in grace. They through fear could not fulfill the law, and through the very law were made guilty. The law has five books; five porticoes surrounded the pool of Solomon, but they bore the sick, they could heal none of them. The five porticoes bore the sick, where they lay, as in the books too no one was healed. Why no one? Because of pride. For when they think they can by their own strength fulfill what is commanded, they did not fulfill what was commanded. And the law was against them, in which they are found guilty, until they cry out, what we said also this morning to your Holiness: *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^5]. The law, then, shows the guilty, grace frees from guilt; the law threatens, grace coaxes; the law intends punishment, grace promises pardon. Yet they are the same, the things commanded in the law, which are also in grace; and therefore that law is said to be written by the finger of God. So we have it written.

**4.** But what the finger of God is, let us seek in the Gospel, and we will find. What does the finger of God signify? For God does not truly have such a form of body as we have. And does he see from one side, and not see from one side? or is he defined by the shape of members, who is wholly everywhere and present to all? What, then, is the finger of God? The Holy Spirit. Attend: from where do we prove this? From the Gospel. For sometimes what one Evangelist says figuratively, another says the same place more openly. There is in a certain place of the Gospel where the Jews said of the Lord, that in the name of Beelzebub he cast out demons. But the Lord, answering: *If I cast out demons by the finger of God, surely the kingdom of God has come upon you*[^6]. Another Evangelist expounds the same place, saying: *If I by the Holy Spirit, therefore the kingdom of God has come upon you*[^7]. When, then, one Evangelist says the finger of God, the other expounds it, to show us that the Holy Spirit is the finger of God. Let us not seek fingers of flesh in God, but understand why the Holy Spirit is called a finger. Because through the Holy Spirit the apostles received divisions of gifts; but in the fingers the division of the hand appears: there is reckoning and distribution. Why, then, do the Jews celebrate Pentecost? A great sacred sign, brothers, and altogether marvelous: if you notice, on the day of Pentecost they received the law written by the finger of God, and on the day of Pentecost the Holy Spirit came.

**5.** But this must be proved from the giving of the law, which the Jews received on tablets of stone—but the hardness of their heart was signified—yet written by the finger of God, because all the things that are written there, these very things are commanded to Christians too, but now, as the Apostle says, *not on tablets of stone, but on the fleshly tablets of the heart*[^8]. This, then, is the difference, that it was written on their hard hearts and was not fulfilled; it was given onto the already believing and easy and everlasting hearts of Christians. Therefore that was stone; but the hearts of Christians were fruitful earth, which could bring forth fruit. Hence the Lord too in the Gospel, when that woman was brought to him who had been caught in adultery—and they wanted to stone her according to the law, but the Lord wanted her thereafter not to sin, ready to forgive her sin—said to those who wanted to stone her, when they themselves were of stone: *If anyone among you is without sin, let him first cast a stone at her.* But when he had said this, he bowed his head and began to write with his finger on the earth; but they, considering their consciences, departed one after another from the greater to the lesser, and that woman was left alone. But the Lord lifted his head and said to her: *What is it, woman? has no one condemned you?* And she said: *No one, Lord.* And the Lord: *Neither will I condemn you. Go, thereafter sin no more*[^9]. What did this pardon signify? Grace. What did that hardness signify? The law given on stones. Hence the Lord was writing with his finger, but now on the earth, from which fruit could be received. But in stone whatever is sown does not come up, because it cannot send out a root. The finger of God, and the finger of God: by the finger of God the law was written; the finger of God, the Holy Spirit.

**6.** The law was given on the day of Pentecost, and the Holy Spirit came on the day of Pentecost. But we had said that we would prove that the Jews received the law on the fiftieth day from the Easter that we celebrate. You have that it is commanded them, on the fourteenth day of the first month to slay the lamb and celebrate Easter. There remain of the month seventeen days, counting that very fourteenth day from which Easter begins. They came to the desert, where the law was given, and so the Scripture says: *But in the third month, from when the people was led out of Egypt*[^10], the Lord spoke to Moses, that those who were going to receive the law should purify themselves for the third day, on which the law was to be given. At the beginning, then, of the third month purification is commanded for the third day, and Easter begins . . . Attend, lest the numbers as it were trick you and bring a fog of understanding upon you. As far as we can, we open it, the Lord granting. If your attention helps us, you will quickly see what is said; but if not, whatever I say will be obscure, even if it is said most plainly. Therefore Easter is appointed on the fourteenth day of the month; and purification is commanded, that the law might be given on the mountain, written by the finger of God—but the finger of God is the Holy Spirit: remember, for we proved this from the Gospel. Purification is appointed for the third day of the third month. From the first month, then, subtract thirteen, and there remain seventeen, that you may begin from the fourteenth; add the whole second month: they make forty-seven days; from that very day of purification to the third day they make fifty days. Nothing is more manifest, nothing more evident, than that the Jews received the law on the day of Pentecost.

**7.** But for the hard it was a burden, for the hard it was a weight. But the Lord came with grace and cries out: *Come to me, you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you. Take my yoke upon you and learn from me, because I am gentle and humble of heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light*[^11]. How is his yoke easy? The law threatens, he coaxes. The law says: "If you do not do it, I will punish"; Christ says: "Whatever you do, I forgive; thereafter see that you do not sin." Therefore his yoke is easy, and his burden light. But let us be made new wineskins, let us await his grace intently: we will be greatly filled with the Holy Spirit, and through the Holy Spirit there will be in us love, now when we have been made fervent with new wine and inebriated with his inebriating and glorious cup, so that we forget those worldly things that held us, as the martyrs forgot, when they went to their passion—and forgot both sons and wives, and parents casting dust on their heads, and mothers offering their breasts, with the reproach of milk separating from food—they forgot all things, nor did they recognize their own. Why do you marvel, if he does not recognize his own? The man is drunk. But from where drunk? From love. But from where love? From the finger of God, from the Holy Spirit, from him who came at Pentecost.

**8.** From where do we prove that love is from the Holy Spirit and fulfills the law? Since the Apostle says: *The fullness of the law is love*[^12], and in another place: *Love of neighbor works no evil. For "you shall not commit adultery, you shall not steal, you shall not kill, you shall not covet," and if there is any other commandment, is summed up in this saying: you shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^13], because love fulfills the law. But from where do we prove that love is from the Holy Spirit? Hear the Apostle saying that *we glory in tribulations*[^14]. Through tribulations the Jews were compelled to fulfill the law, and could not; Christians by tribulations were not separated from the law, but rather ran to the law. See what I say, brothers. A punishment was set for the Jews, that whoever sacrificed to idols should be stoned or crucified, yet because fear pressed them, love did not hold them: they did not fear because they were conquered by desire, and went after idols, where the cross, where the threat of death and stoning was imminent, and they were not recalled by these punishments. Afterward, because love came, and fear, love succeeded. The Gospel was preached to the nations; that they should sacrifice to idols, they began to threaten them with fire, crosses, beasts, and they endured all those things which the kings threatened and inflicted on them, nor yet did their heart turn aside to idols. By what punishments those could not be recalled from idols, by those punishments Christians could not be compelled to idols, because now love was from the Holy Spirit. *But we even glory*, says the Apostle, *in tribulations, knowing that tribulation works patience, patience trial*—for we wish to prove that the love which fulfills the law of God is from the Holy Spirit—*for tribulation works patience, patience trial, trial hope; but hope does not confound, because the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^15].

**9.** And so, brothers, we celebrate an anniversary day of the coming of the Holy Spirit, but daily the Holy Spirit ought to be in our heart. Nor let us think that this festival ought to be only on this day, but on the rest of the days ought not to be: let us not celebrate it on one day, but for the whole time, that we may be found not reprobate, but approved on the day of the Lord when he comes, that those to whom he gave the pledge, these he may lead also into the everlasting possession. For Christ betrothed his Church to himself, he sent her the Holy Spirit. This Spirit was given as it were a ring. He who gave the ring, is going to give immortality in rest. Let us love him, let us hope in him, let us believe in him.  
Come a little earlier in the afternoon hours because of the hymns of God. Others are inebriated with the wine of the earthly vine, in which there is self-indulgence, and let us be inebriated with the songs of God; praising the Lord with songs of salvation, let us for a while forget the earth, that from earth we may merit to be lifted into heaven, our Lord Jesus Christ granting it, who lives and reigns with God the Father.
