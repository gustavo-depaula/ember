# Sermon 306/C: A Sermon of Saint Augustine the Bishop, at the Table of Cyprian, on Saint Quadratus

**1.** We have heard how the evangelical trumpet sounded, exhorting the martyrs to the contest by which they would conquer the world: *Whoever wants to save his soul will lose it; but whoever loses it for my sake will find it*[^1]. By saving he will lose it, by losing he will find it. What is this, but that there is a certain salvation of the soul according to this world, and there is another salvation of the soul according to God? At the moment of trial, then, by which the martyrs were tested, so that one or the other was set before them—either the denial of Christ, or the trial of death—without doubt those who wanted to save their souls according to the world denied Christ, and lost them; but those who according to the world lost their souls confessed Christ, and found them. For to whom is the soul more safely entrusted than to its Creator? Who can preserve what was, except he who could make what was not? Learning and teaching this, that Quadratus—for he was a bishop—whose feast we celebrate today, together with all his people, clergy and laity, confessed Christ. He sent ahead the flock that he was shepherding: four days later Quadratus followed. For the Massa Candida, whose feast was celebrated four days ago, was the people of God, entrusted to his governance. What a great wall, mortared with tile-dust, this Quadratus ruled! And those so many souls, and out of so many souls, did not yet see the Church which we see; they did not yet see it, and out of themselves they were building it, and into its framework, by dying, the living stones were running. And behold, we see the Church spread throughout the whole world, then great in a few, now wide and spread abroad among many; and we behold fulfilled the predictions of the prophets, which preceded all the future things that we see. But, in order that this might come about, when there was a small number, the courage was great. By passing along the narrow road they widened it, and by treading on rough things and passing along them, they went before us. Whoever in those times bore various tribulations for the name of Christ followed these. O blessed ones, in losing their souls! O happy losses! So do those cast away, so do those lose, who sow. For who would doubt that, in all sowing, those things that are sown are cast away, are buried? But how great is the hope of the harvest, which the sower's hope precedes! And when it is sown, no one sees the harvest; the whole is entrusted to God, committed to the earth. The earth keeps it, the earth makes it fruitful, the earth multiplies it; but with him working by whom heaven and earth were made.

**2.** The name of this martyr prompts us to say something about holy squareness. Holy Noah was commanded to build the ark of squared timbers. What was chosen in the squareness, what was prefigured, except that no one can throw down a square as it stands? For wherever you set it, it will stand. Wear yourself out by rolling it, labor at throwing it down; and in throwing it down you will find the one you have thrown down just as he stood before he was thrown down. Everywhere a standing place is set for him. Losses were set before him, the goods of those who confess Christ were taken away: Quadratus stood. Exiles were set before him, and the loss of his earthly homeland; with the spiritual homeland in mind, Quadratus stood. Torments and savage tortures were dealt out; with the great sufferings of Gehenna in mind, Quadratus stood. Vast worldly rewards were promised, that Christ might be denied; with the heavenly crown in mind, Quadratus stood.

**3.** Such ought the Christian to be: but, in order to be such, Christ must be called upon. Let him make us square, let him rule us, let him not permit the one who is falling to fall. For it was not without reason said: *The righteous man falls seven times, and rises again*[^2]. We should not refer what was said to the falls of some grave sin. For he falls gravely who was innocent and becomes a murderer; he falls who was chaste and becomes an adulterer. It was not of such falls that it was said: *The righteous man falls seven times and rises again;* but this was said of the falls of tribulations. For to fall is to be humbled. However much the righteous man has been humbled, he rises again. He is square who is turned and stands. And in saying *Seven times,* he put it for the whole, signifying by the perfect number, however often he falls. *Seven times,* however often; just as it is, *Seven times a day I will praise you*[^3]. *His praise is always in my mouth*[^4]. And that you may know this to be the sense in these words, *the righteous man falls seven times and rises again,* let us look at the surrounding context, what holy Scripture said before, and what it added after these words: there the sense will appear plainly. What, then, does Scripture say, where this sentence is placed? *Do not liken the wicked to the pastures of the righteous, and do not be led astray by the fullness of the belly*[^5]. And there follows: *For the righteous man falls seven times, and will rise again; but the wicked will be weakened in evils*[^6]. Now from the words that follow this sentence, this meaning begins to shine forth: *The righteous man will fall seven times and will rise again, but the wicked will be weakened in evils.* So he who falls seven times and will rise again is not weakened in evils; and this is the very thing—to fall and rise again, to be humbled and not be weakened.

**4.** What is it, then, that was said above: *Do not liken the wicked to the pastures of the righteous, and do not be led astray by the fullness of the belly*[^7]? As the Lord deigns to grant, I will say what I can; let one who knocks harder find something better. Behold, dearly beloved, we preach to you the word of God; you all hear it, you are all delighted, you all rejoice: you do not all do it. These are the very pastures where the soul of the righteous is fed; but there too the soul of the unrighteous is fed. The righteous and the wicked hear together; they hear together, and together, when the sermon is finished, they say: We are fattened. But you, distinguish the one who is more healthfully fed from the one who is himself also fed, and has fullness of belly, but does not have stability of mind. Distinguish, then, and *do not liken the wicked to the pastures of the righteous,* that is, do not compare him to the feeding righteous man. Do not liken, that is, do not compare. For the one is fed in one way, the other in another. Do not think the two alike: do not liken the righteous man to the other. But you hear that this man too holds to the Scriptures: do not be led astray by the fullness of the belly. *Do not liken the wicked to the pastures of the righteous,* because he too is fed and rejoices in these things; *and do not be led astray by the fullness of the belly,* because he holds it all. How each has been fed, let trial inquire, let trial test. Behold, I show the pastures of the righteous: *he will fall seven times and rise again.* Behold, I show the pastures of the wicked: *but the wicked is weakened in evils.* The righteous man is fed: like a squared stone, whichever way you turn it, it will stand. The wicked man is fed: like a round stone, it will stand nowhere.

**5.** Such, then, ought we to be: but God must be called upon, that we may be able to be such. *I will hope in him, for he will deliver me from the snare of the hunters and from the harsh word*[^8]. The snare of the hunters: the plots of the persecutors; the harsh word: the bitter reproach. How much these heard, who by losing their souls found them! What snares were set before them in their trials! And one soul of all said: *My eyes are ever toward the Lord, for he will pluck my feet out of the net*[^9]. *I will hope in him, for he will deliver me from the snare of the hunters.* How many slanders they heard! and they knew them to be false. Does not grief burn, when a false charge is heard? But there was in them true patience, because a white conscience shone. And from where was this to them, except that he himself delivers them from the snare of the hunters and from the harsh word, who had said to them: *You will be blessed when they speak every evil against you*[^10]? This is the harsh word. They did not say: What is it to me to suffer this reproach? Why am I a Christian, that I should always hear these false charges? They did not say it, because they stood square; and they were rescued, heeding the one who said: *You will be blessed, for your reward is great in heaven*[^11]. Was it that, because those trials existed then, they alone could contend? The evils of the world abound; let us too contend. The snare of greed is set before you, that you may betray someone. Be square: scorn what is promised, despise what is offered, look up to what is deferred. What is deferred is more certain than what is offered. What is offered either deserts you while you live, or is deserted by you when you die; what is deferred, when it has come, is not ended. It will neither desert you, nor be deserted by you. There you will be, you will not move on. *For blessed,* it says, *are those who dwell in your house, forever and ever they will praise you*[^12]. They will praise you, because they will love you. And how will they love you, when they see you, if they so loved you when, not seeing, they believed in you? The snare of fear is set before you, that, when you are afraid, you may commit some evil, and do to yourself what the one who frightened you could not do. For the one who frightened you could kill your flesh; you, by doing evil, kill your soul. These things have never been lacking in human affairs: the contests of trials have never been lacking. Let our eyes be ever toward the Lord.

**6.** In this world, my brothers, we do not know what is to our advantage. Riches have profited many who have used them well; though it was not the riches themselves that profited, but the mind of the one using them; who, even if he did not have riches, would hold the divine sentence, *peace on earth to men of good will*[^13]. Yet how many riches have harmed, who counts them? How many riches have ruined, when are they reckoned? They are seen every day, and those who see are not corrected. So riches have profited some, let us grant it: yet they have also harmed very many. Worldly powers have profited some, let us accept it: yet how many they have harmed! Still, we ought not to be blind. Can it be said that the kingdom of heaven has harmed anyone? Can it be said that living without end with Christ has harmed anyone? But those goods are not here, because they are not from here: they are not of the world. *Do not,* then, *love the world, nor the things that are in the world. Whoever loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him: for all that is in the world is the desire of the flesh, and the desire of the eyes, and the ambition of the age, which is not from the Father, but is from the world. And the world passes away, and its desire; but whoever does the will of God abides forever*[^14].

**7.** Behold the structure of the saints, behold the building of the squared. So then the things that we do not know whether they profit us or harm us, let us leave to God, who both does not hear some when he is favorable, and hears them when he is angry; and again, hears them when he is favorable, and does not hear them when he is angry. To him, I say, let us commit such things, who both does not hear some when he is favorable and hears them when he is angry; and again hears them when he is favorable and does not hear them when he is angry. So when through inexperience we ask for what is not to our advantage, let him be favorable, and not hear us. Therefore he who has been heard, and whose desire has been fulfilled, ought not to boast. The desire of the Israelites was fulfilled when they spurned the manna and craved meat. And read what is written: *While the food was still in their mouths*[^15]. What shall I say of men? The devil was heard, that he was believed worthy to be allowed to tempt God's servant. The demons were heard, that they should go into the swine. What great thing, then, is it to be heard? It matters very much in what matter you are heard. If you ask for what is to your advantage, and are heard, God is favorable. Or do we perhaps know what we ask, and the apostle Paul did not know? Behold, I have shown you the ungrateful people heard, but to their own harm; the devil heard, the demons heard. Hear the Apostle not heard, with God favorable. Surely you say in your hearts: If the devil was heard, how much more ought the Apostle to be heard? And yet what does the same Apostle say? *Lest I be exalted by the greatness of my revelations*[^16]. After he was not heard, then he found the reason why he was not heard. So he did not keep silent about it, but set it forth first. For so great a man was about to say a marvelous thing and contrary to opinion—so holy a chosen vessel, who labored more than all of them; yet not he, but the grace of God with him. When he read the promise of God, *While you are still speaking I will say, Here I am*[^17], he asks once, and is not heard: he asks again, and is not so heard: he asks a third time, and is altogether not heard. *Lest,* he says, *I be exalted by the greatness of my revelations, there was given me a thorn in my flesh, an angel of Satan to buffet me*[^18]. Many suspect many things about this passage: but it is better believed, what is said by the better authorities, that the apostle Paul suffered a certain most acute pain of the head; therefore he said he was buffeted, because he suffered in the head, lest he be exalted. He was struck there where, in pride, one ought not to be raised up. And what does he say, what did he add? *Therefore I asked the Lord three times that it might depart from me; and he said to me, My grace is sufficient for you, for power is made perfect in weakness*[^19]. O not to be heard! this is rather to be helped. A harsh medicine, but healthful, so great a physician applied to the sick man. The sick man, under the bite of the medicine, asked the physician to take away what he had applied; the physician did not hear him according to his will, and therein the more heard him according to his health.

**8.** Do we not see these things every day in human affairs, as a kind of harsh and inexorable mercy? How many contrary things the sick ask of their physicians, and with how much mercy the physicians refuse! They refuse, and they spare; they would be cruel if they granted. The physician knows this: and does God not know it? He who was created along with you knows how to deal with you in this way; and he who created you both does not know how to deal with you? Surely, dearly beloved, in all tribulations, in all fears, in all joys, ask God that, concerning temporal things, he grant what he himself knows to be to your advantage. But as for eternal things—such as *hallowed be your name, your kingdom come, your will be done on earth as it is in heaven*[^20], and the like—ask for these securely, they cannot harm you. Choose, love, gather them; for he opens his hand and fills every soul with blessing. *And when you have given,* it says, *they will gather*[^21]. Let no one doubt about the goods on high: and if they are deferred, they will be given; the reward is not denied, but the desire is exercised. Let us long for them long: because great is what we are to receive. Let us thirst long: because we shall drink from the fountain of life. Yet, dearly beloved, what we ask not shamelessly—because the Apostle taught it—let us ask: that we may lead a quiet and tranquil life, and with all godliness and love.
