# Sermon 360/A: (Dolbeau 24)

**1.** The promises of God made faith for our fathers, the gifts of God fulfilled faith for us. In them it was promised to us, in us it was repaid both to us and to them. There are two senses of the body through which faith enters: hearing and sight. They heard, we see. But both in us they see, and we in them have heard. Therefore God addresses the Church herself too: *Hear, O daughter, and see. Hear,* he says, *and see, and forget your people and your father's house, for the king has desired your beauty.* Who is the *king?* It follows and says: *For he is your God*[^1]. What, then, and how great a dignity of a woman, to whom the king is God and to whom God is husband? *Hear,* he says, *and see, and forget your people and your father's house.* He fixes something in the memory, that he may take away something from the memory. For she was under another father and in another people. To the still unbelieving, and still in darkness, the Lord said: *You are of your father the devil*[^2]. But the people of the impious is the son and crowd of the devil, pertaining as members to a lost head. The house of this father and this people she is bidden to forget by hearing and seeing, that she too may answer in her faithful from another psalm: *As we have heard, so also we have seen*[^3].

**2.** It was heard by our fathers: *In your seed all the nations shall be blessed*[^4]. This is seen by us, because it is fulfilled in us. It was heard: *There shall be a root of Jesse, and one who shall rise to rule the nations; in him the nations shall hope*[^5]. From Jesse, David; from David, the offspring of Christ. The root of Jesse, Christ, rules among the nations; in him the nations hope. They hope in their king, the provincials of the king and the servants of the ruler and the coheirs of the brother. This, as it is woven into the variety of the queen's garment, so is it clear to our eyes and faith. For it was said of this queen: *The queen stood at your right hand in gilded clothing, surrounded with variety*[^6]. What variety of garment? The multitude of tongues. The Latins speak in one way, the Greeks in another, the Carthaginians in another, the Hebrews in another, the Syrians in another, the Indians in another, the Cappadocians in another, the Egyptians in another. Variety in color, unity in the weave. For many colors, enclosed in the unity of the weave, paint, do not tear. A variety of tongues, but not a variety of teachings. A variety of speech, but a unity of love. And how does the Apostle say: *This is our glory, the testimony of our conscience*[^7], where the eyes of God are, where is the sight of him whom we have pleased, except because, when we were sinners, by forgiving us that wherein we displeased, he granted us that wherein we may please? Because, then, this was brought about, that we could, by his gift, not by our merit, say with the Apostle and from the Apostle: *This is our glory, the testimony of our conscience,* therefore of that queen too it is said: *All the beauty of the king's daughter is within*[^8]. By her garment she is adorned outwardly toward the face, by faith she is formed inwardly toward salvation. Whence, but by hearing and seeing, she to whom it was said: *Hear, O daughter, and see,* that she might answer, as we said, and sing together and in a manner resound through all her members: *As we have heard, so also we have seen?*

**3.** The world was bare of Christians and dry with the want of grace. One people worshiping God, born of Abraham by the stock of flesh and the series of generation; in which people were many holy ones, prophets, just men, patriarchs, our fathers; there was grace in worshiping God, and the merit of deserving and the hope of receiving the reward. And this was the one nation among all the nations, while the whole world was empty of this grace. It came to our times: that one nation remained dry of this grace, and the world was watered. How this was prefigured among our fathers, hear. After the solution I set forth what is wont to be set forth and solved. And so now, after this preface, when I have said it, pardon me. In the book of Judges it is written that Gideon, about to go to battle and to fight for his fatherland against foreigners, asked for a sign. He seemed to ask a sign for the present business, but that sign did not fit the present business. He asked for a present thing, he foretold a future one. For what does he ask? Hear. He asks of God that he set a fleece of wool on the threshing floor, in which fleece he wished to find rain, so that the fleece was moist, the whole floor dry. He asks another sign and says to God: "*Lord, let not your fury be angry with me. I will ask yet another and will test yet again in the fleece*[^9]. This I ask, that the whole floor be moist, the fleece dry." He received it; he found on another day the fleece dry, the floor watered. The floor is the world, in the fleece is the people of the Jews. The whole world was empty of that grace; the floor, then, was dry. This grace was among the Jewish people, but *in the fleece.* What is *in the fleece?* Not in the manifestation of faith, but it was in the cloud of secrecy, but it was not in the open. It was not to be shown that it might appear, but to be wrung out that it might flow. *The fleece was wrung out, and it filled a basin with water*[^10]. Not in vain a *basin* (pelvis). For the basin is named from washing the feet (a pedibus luendis), that is, from washing the feet, as it were a basin (pelvis), because it washes the feet (pedes luat). Therefore the very wringing of the fleece poured out Christ. For Christ, commending humility, washed the feet of the disciples in a basin. Now, then, the fleece is dry, Christ has been wrung out from it. Truly he was wrung out, because he was cast out by pressures. *For the tenants said: This is the heir, come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours. And they killed him,* he says, *and cast him out of the city*[^11]. The fleece wrung out, they sent the water outside. But that humility might be stretched out from those wrung out, he took away thence whom he wished, and went to the nations, that he might fulfill the other sign: the fleece dry, he watered the floor. And this faith is not in the cloud of the fleece, but in open manifestation: it is preached to all, it is known by all. It has its sacrament in secret, its word in public. As these things were said, so they are; as promised, so repaid. *As we have heard, so also we have seen,* because to us in unity it was said: *Hear, O daughter, and see.*

**4.** But the heretics, separated from the framework of this world, neither wish to hear what they read nor to see what they know. We have been invited to a wedding, we have praised the bride and commended her from the divine testimonies. We have commended ourselves to ourselves, indeed the Lord commends us to all. For he himself promised, he himself displayed. Let them open their eyes and see what they have heard. You hear with me, see with me. Indeed, what is worse, you both hear with me and see with me and are not with me. What wonder, then, brethren, if the pagans are unwilling to hold what they spurn, when the Donatists are unwilling to believe what they read? The pagan wished to throw my book into the flame, he detested the Scripture itself, he persecuted it itself. What wonder, if he grieves to see what he wished to burn? But you, heretic, say that you saved this book from the flame. The one you were unwilling to have burned, recognize when it is brought forth, hear when it is read. Surely in this book is: *In your seed all the nations shall be blessed.* Surely you were unwilling for these letters to burn. Why did you burn in them with the desire of dissension? Why, I beseech you, unless because you lie, unless because you say you saved what you handed over? Let us not seek the ancient things that were done: let the book itself be brought forth, let the one saved from the flame itself judge between us. Let us see who pertains to the eternal flame, who handed over the book to the temporal flame. And he who dared both to burn and to hand over willed it to perish, but it did not perish. It was saved, it is safe: it is brought forth. You ask by whom it was handed over, you ask by whom it was saved? Let it be brought forth, let it be read. He who believed it did not hand it over. Do you wish to see how dear that Scripture is to me? It is read, and I follow. Do you wish to see how hateful it is to you? It is read, and you fight back, you resist, you turn away your hearing. Since you do not have ears in your heart, you have closed your heart; the Scripture which you say you saved knocks, and you do not open. If you truly saved this book, your chest is better than your heart. But far be it that I believe you sought to save what I see you refused when it was read. From the saved book you wish to fashion my crime, from the read book I show your crime. The pagan, if he does not believe, hates what I read; you, who do not wish to seem led to impiety through a pagan, have conquered the pagan in impiety. He hates and throws it away, you hold it and deny it. You say I burned the testament, you who see me hold the inheritance; you say you saved the testament, when you have made yourself disinherited. I bring forth the testament, I read the testament: you do not wish me to bring it forth, you bring it forth. Let it be brought forth by you, which is read for me. You bring it forth, you hold it, you open it, see, read. The hands of one holding, the eyes of one seeing, the tongue of one sounding fight for me. From you, against you, I plead my cause. He who reads out the book in your congregation is your reader, my witness. But these hear and see, those neither hear nor see. Let us pass from these and leave them, if it can be done without the zeal of animosity, to think about their own impiety.

**5.** But the remaining pagans, still remaining for this reason, that our faith may still have rejecters, that it may have God as approver. Let him address them, and let them know that what is done was written before it was done, and was foretold because it was going to be; and let them not be terrified that what they themselves do not wish is done, but let them believe that what God promised would come to pass is done. They would wish their own will to be done, but God preferred to fulfill his promise. Let them not be angry. He is better, I think, he is more powerful. However proud they are, God is greater. A few things, then, which we could on account of the shortness of time, we have excerpted from the holy Scriptures, how the prophets foretold that it would come to pass at certain times that the idols would be taken away from the midst, and this has been fulfilled, just as they foretold that it would come to pass that the Church would be spread throughout the whole world, and so it has been fulfilled. Let us not, then, be much angry with the pagans, because they are unwilling to believe about the idols, when the heretics are unwilling to believe about the Church. Both see, both do not believe, but some neglect, some read. The crime of the pagan is not so great, not to believe what he neglects, as that of the heretic, not to believe what he reads. But yet the pagans too, by the very fact that the things foretold are fulfilled, ought to acknowledge the foreteller and fulfiller. The things, then, that were said about this matter, hear.

**6.** In the book of Wisdom it is written thus: *For this reason there shall be a regard even upon the idols of the nations*[^12]. For it was as if God did not regard the long-adored idols, the long-sacrificed-to idols. He who always sees, at some time regarded. He saw unto patience, he regarded unto vengeance. *Upon the idols,* he says, *of the nations there shall be a regard, because the creatures of God were made an object of hatred*[^13]. For they provoke God by the creatures of God. How do they provoke God by his creatures? For the smith made a god, but God the wood. The goldsmith made an idol, but God the gold. What do you do with his thing that he hates, you who from a creature of God fashion badly what he hates? You, fashion well what he loves. You wish to set your image in wood; receive the image of God in yourself. Why do you blot out within and carve without? *The creatures,* he says, *of God were made an object of hatred, and a temptation to the souls of men, and a snare to the feet of the foolish. For the beginning of fornication is the seeking out of idols*[^14]. If a woman having many husbands is praised, let a soul worshiping many gods be praised too. If a woman is an adulteress through true husbands, how much more a soul through false gods? *For the beginning of fornication is the seeking out of idols, and the invention of them is the corruption of life*[^15]. See how he charged it; let us see what he foretold: *For neither were they from the beginning, nor will they be forever*[^16].

**7.** The holy prophet Zechariah too says: *In that day.* See what he says, and recognize the day. For he sets "day" for "time," as the Apostle: *Behold, now is the acceptable time, behold, now is the day of salvation*[^17]. What, then, does Zechariah say? *In that day there shall be an open place for the house of David*[^18]. This is what I was saying, that it was closed in the fleece, open on the floor. *There shall be an open place,* the Church shall be manifest. What is *there shall be an open place?* *A city set on a mountain cannot be hidden*[^19]. *There shall be an open place for the house of David. And it shall be in that day, the Lord will exterminate the names of the images from the earth, and there shall be no mention of them at all*[^20]. Let the pagans hear these things. Let them see this happen which was foretold. If they love divination, let them recognize the divinity. Why do they go, as for a great thing, to one raving astrologer, consulted about one man—and let him say more false things than true by chance, who in false things meets his downfall? Why, then, does he wish to hear an astrologer about to speak about one man? Let him hear God foretelling about the human race. From the earth the names of the idols are blotted out, in heaven the names of the Christians are written.

**8.** Let Isaiah too say: *The injury of men shall be humbled and shall fall, and the Lord alone shall be exalted in that day, and they shall hide all things made by hand, bringing them into caves and into the caverns of the rocks*[^21]. See whether it is not so, see whether it does not happen, see whether the Scripture lies. As though we seek the idols and do not wish to make the worshipers of idols worshipers of God, they hide them in the earth, they hide them in a cave, they hide them in the cavern of a rock. Wherever they hide them, it is dug out thence, when in the heart it is overthrown. How many have hidden their false gods, perverse, and, corrected, have given them up. For we see altogether two kinds of men in the Church, indeed one kind there in the Church, the other now in the neighborhood of the Church: both of those who hide and of those who repel and bring forth. Both kinds God foretold. You have heard about the kind that hides; hear about the kind that gives up and throws away, in the same passage, in one testimony of Isaiah: *The Lord,* he says, *alone shall be exalted in that day, and they shall hide all things made by hand, in caves and in the caverns of the rocks and in the clefts of the earth, from the face of the fear of the Lord, from the brightness of his strength, when he shall arise to shatter the earth*[^22]. What is *to shatter the earth?* To terrify the earthly. Therefore every bold man, if he has feared, we say is shattered by fear. No wonder that the earth fears, for the heaven thunders. In the prophetic Scriptures there are thunders. Therefore those will hide. What of the others? They too out of fear, but a better one. Those out of fear of the laws hide, these out of fear of the divine law bring forth and throw away. In both there is fear, but by fear some make progress, some fail. We have heard of those hiding; let us hear of the other kind. *For in that day a man shall cast away his abominations of silver and of gold which they made*[^23]. It is a wonder that gold and silver are cast away: piety casts away what impiety loves. They cast away *their abominations:* do they not also cast away the wooden ones, do they not cast away the earthen ones, do they not cast away the stone ones? Yet for the commendation of a more devout mind, they cast away, he says, *the gold and silver ones.* What does he love, who now does not love gold? What losses does he fear, who does not fear the losses of gold and silver? They cast away *the abominations of silver and gold which they made to adore.* The *vain and harmful* things some hide, others cast away: yet the abomination is taken away. Let it be taken away, and would that, as from the places of the lands, so from the hearts of the earthly. Through the hands of his servants let God take these things away from places, through his own hand from hearts.

**9.** Let us hear Jeremiah too. Although I recalled this yesterday too, it is too sweet, and let it be repeated today as well—for we cannot grow weary of it: *Lord, my strength and my help and my refuge in the day of evils*[^24]. No longer is stone and wood *my refuge,* no longer is *gold and silver, the works of men's hands, my refuge.* I do not, he says, flee to those who cannot flee: *For they have feet and do not walk*[^25]. *Lord, my refuge in the day of evils,* in the day of temptations, in the day of this labor, in the day of weak life, in the day of sighs from desire of the heavenly Jerusalem. For as long as we are not where we long to be, it is the day of evils. In hope we rejoice, not yet in the reality. Hence it is also in another psalm: *You led me because you have become my hope*[^26]. Our hope is on the way, while he leads. And how a refuge? *A tower of strength from the face of the enemy*[^27]. What then of the idols, what was going to be? *To you,* he says, *the nations shall come from the ends of the earth and shall say: How did our fathers possess lies?*[^28] The very *how* is the wonder of the sons at the error of their fathers, as if they said: "How were our elders prudent, how did they worship these things, how did they bend their uprightness before wood and stones, how, forsaking him by whom they were made, did they possess the vain things they made, *how did our fathers possess lies?*" What are those *lies?* *Images, and there is no usefulness in them*[^29]. For lies sometimes seem to certain greedy men to be useful; and the merchant swears that he may sell dearer, in gain he swears that he may acquire, in gain he lies that he may acquire, in an idol he lies that he may perish. Nor in those lies is there *usefulness,* if we consider the true and healthful usefulness. But there is at least that which seems to men, which is thought by the unskilled. Here what, finally, do you spend that you may hire a craftsman, you spend that you may feed it, you spend that you may adorn it, you spend that you may set it up, you spend that you may sacrifice? You spend the whole that you may acquire nothing, you lose many things that you may perish.

**10.** Likewise Jeremiah: *Do not walk according to the ways of the nations.* Come now, my brethren, let none of you defend the idols. Every defender of an idol is near to a worshiper. *Do not walk according to the ways of the nations, and do not fear the signs of heaven, since they fear them, the sights of them*[^30]. Since it is the custom of the nations to fear *the signs of heaven,* that is, to choose days when you set out, when a wife is taken, when a web is set up and when another is cut down, *since their customs are vain*[^31]. And now hear about the idols: *It is a wood cut from the forest, the work of the smith and a casting of silver*[^32]. He seems to say a known thing, but too deaf is the one to whom he says it. He rouses him as from sleep, that he may see also with the heart what he sees with the eyes. *The work of the smith and a casting; they have adorned them with silver and gold, they have fastened them with anvils and nails, and they shall not be moved.* For by themselves *they shall not be moved:* men, movable in body, carry the immovable one. They would not, movable in body, carry the immovable one, if they were immovable in heart. *They have fastened them with anvils and nails, and they shall not be moved. Carved silver.* And it is added: *They shall not walk. From Tarshish shall come the gold of Ophaz*—precious and great gold—*and the hand of the artificers, every work of the artificers; with hyacinth and purple they shall clothe them. Lifted up, they are carried, because they do not walk. Do not fear them, because they will not harm, and there is no good in them. Thus you shall say to them.* What shall we say to them? *The gods who did not make heaven and earth, let them perish from the earth and from under heaven*[^33]. What is *thus you shall say to them?* You are Christians, you hear the prophet: thus say to them, do not be silent. *The gods who did not make heaven and earth, let them perish from the earth and from under heaven.* Surely from heaven, where they never were? Let those worshipers too attend to these foretold things, let them attend and believe. *Let* these *perish from under heaven,* let those be written in heaven. Surely thus you say to them; do not be seduced by those who say: "Neither worship nor mock them." Receive rather, Christian, the prophet; hear God rather through the prophet: *You shall say to them: The gods who did not make heaven and earth, let them perish from the earth and from under this heaven.* And as if he said: "Whom shall I worship, when those have perished?", *God made heaven and earth in his strength*[^34]. Why do you wish to worship the earth, when you are earth and on the earth and made from earth? You have God founding the earth. He has not withdrawn from you in his work, he is not far also from your heart. He made what you tread, he made what you look up to, he is the one in whom you believe. *God made the earth in his strength, founded the world in his wisdom*[^35]. Did he perhaps not speak of Christ who said: *God made the earth in his strength, founded the world in his wisdom?* Hear the apostle Paul: *Christ the strength of God and the wisdom of God*[^36]. *He stretched out the heaven and the multitude of waters in heaven, and brought clouds from the end of the earth, made lightnings into rain and brought forth light from his treasuries. Every man has been made foolish by his own knowledge, and every artificer has been confounded over his graven things, because they cast lies, there is no spirit in them. They are vain, works of mockery; in the time of their visitation they shall perish*[^37]. The false gods perish, but you have not remained without the true God, if you are the younger son Jacob, whom the elder serves. Let the false gods perish, hold you the true one. For hear what follows: *Not of this kind is the portion of Jacob*[^38]. Those vain things, those false gods will perish, but the *portion of Jacob* does not perish. *The portion of Jacob:* the inheritance of Jacob. The inheritance of Jacob, which God gives, or God himself? I dare to say that it is God himself. I take a psalm, the sentence is evident: *Lord, the portion of my inheritance*[^39]. *Not of this kind is the portion of Jacob, for he who fashioned all things, he is his inheritance, the Lord is his name*[^40].

**11.** We have heard the foretold things, what is going to be for the idols; let us hear the precepts, what is to be done to the idols. Let no one say: "Surely the images were said to be going to perish: let him destroy them. Why do you break them?" But God willed to destroy them through those whom he did not will to perish. *And he will bring you,* he says, *to the Amorite and the Hittite and the Perizzite and the Canaanite and the Girgashite and the Hivite and the Jebusite, and I will wipe them out. You shall not adore their gods nor serve them. You shall not do according to their works, but by casting down you shall cast down and by breaking you shall break their images*[^41]. And again: *Behold, I will cast out before your face the Amorite, the Canaanite and the Hittite and the Perizzite and the Hivite and the Girgashite and the Jebusite. Make no covenant with those who dwell in the land into which you will enter to them, lest it be an offense among you. Their altars you shall cast down and their pillars you shall break and their groves you shall cut down. For you shall not adore foreign gods. For the Lord God is jealous, his name is the Jealous God*[^42]. Hear, brethren, you shudder because it is said: *The Lord is jealous.* O soul of the Church, O that spouse, you fear a jealous husband: keep your chastity.
