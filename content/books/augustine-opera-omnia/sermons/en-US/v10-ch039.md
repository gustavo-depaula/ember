# Sermon 360/A: A Treatise of Saint Augustine the Bishop

On the Testimonies of the Scriptures

Against the Donatists and Against the Pagans

**1.** The promises of God gave faith to our fathers, the gifts of God fulfilled faith for us. In them it was promised to us, in us it was rendered to them too. There are two senses of the body through which faith enters: hearing and sight. They heard, we see. But in us they too see, and in them we have heard. Therefore God addresses the Church herself too: *Hear, daughter, and see.* *Hear*, he says, *and see, and forget your people and the house of your father, for the king has desired your beauty.* What king? It follows and says: *For he himself is your God*[^1]. What and how great, then, is this dignity of the woman, whose king is God and whose husband is God? *Hear*, he says, *and see, and forget your people and the house of your father.* He fixes something in the memory, that he may take away another from the memory. For she was under another father and in another people. For to the still unbelieving, and still in darkness, the Lord said: *You are of your father the devil*[^2]. But the people of the impious is the son and crowd of the devil, belonging as members to a lost head. The house of this father and this people she is bidden to forget by hearing and seeing, so that she herself too may answer from another psalm in her faithful: *As we have heard, so also we have seen*[^3].

**2.** It was heard from our fathers: *In your seed all the nations shall be blessed*[^4]. This is seen by us, because it is fulfilled in us. It was heard: *There shall be a root of Jesse, and one who shall arise to reign over the nations, in him the nations shall hope*[^5]. From Jesse David, from David the offspring of Christ. The root of Jesse, Christ, reigns over the nations, in him the nations hope. The provincials of the king hope in their king, and the servants in the one having lordship, and the co-heirs in the brother[^6]. This, just as it is woven in the variety of the queen's garment, so is it clear to our eyes and faith. For it was said of this queen: *The queen stood at your right hand in gilded clothing, clothed about with variety*[^7]. What is the variety of the garment? The multitude of tongues. The Latins speak one way, the Greeks another, the Punic another, the Hebrews another, the Syrians another, the Indians another, the Cappadocians another, the Egyptians another. Variety in color, unity in weave. For the many colors, enclosed in the unity of the weave, depict, they do not tear apart. Variety of tongues, but not variety of teachings. Variety of speech, but unity of love. And how does the Apostle say: *Our glory is this, the testimony of our conscience*[^8], where the eyes of God are, where the sight of him whom we have pleased, except because, since we were sinners, by remitting to us that whereby we displeased, he gave us that whereby we may please? Because, then, this came about that we could by his gift, not by our merit, say with the Apostle and from the Apostle: *Our glory is this, the testimony of our conscience*, therefore of that queen too it is said: *All the beauty of the king's daughter is within*[^9]. By the garment she is adorned outwardly for the face, by faith she is formed inwardly for salvation. Whence, except by hearing and seeing, she to whom it was said: *Hear, daughter, and see*[^10], so that she might answer, as we said, and sing together and in a certain way echo with all her members: *As we have heard, so also we have seen*[^11]?

**3.** The world was bare of Christians and was dry in the poverty of grace. One people worshiping God, born of Abraham by the stock of the flesh and the series of generation; in which people there were many saints, prophets, just men, patriarchs, our fathers; there grace in worshiping God, and the merit of deserving and the hope of receiving the reward. And this one nation was among all the nations, while the whole world was empty of this grace. We come to our times: that one nation remained dry of this grace, and the world was watered. How this was prefigured among our fathers, hear. After the solution I set forth what is wont to be set forth to be solved. So now after this preface, when I shall have said it, pardon me. In the book of Judges it is written that Gideon, going to battle and about to fight for his country against foreigners, asked for a sign[^12]. He seemed to ask for a sign for the present business, but that sign did not fit that present business. He asked a present thing, he foretold a future one. For what does he ask? Hear. He asks of God that he place a fleece of wool on the threshing-floor, in which fleece he wished to find rain, so that that fleece would be wet, the whole threshing-floor dry. He asks another sign and says to God: "*Lord, let not your fury be angry with me. I will ask yet another and will test yet again with the fleece.* This I ask, that the whole threshing-floor be wet, the fleece dry." He received it; he found on another day the fleece dry, the threshing-floor watered. The threshing-floor is the world, in the fleece the people of the Jews. The whole world was empty of this grace, the threshing-floor therefore was dry. This grace was among the Jewish people, but *in the fleece.* What is *in the fleece*? Not in the manifestation of faith, but in the cloud of secrecy it was, but in the open it was not. It was not to be shown that it might appear, but to be pressed out that it might flow. *The fleece was pressed out, and filled a basin with water.* And not in vain a *basin.* For a basin (pelvis) is named from washing feet (pedes luendi), that is, from washing the feet, as it were a basin, because it washes the feet. Therefore even that pressing-out of the fleece poured forth Christ. For Christ, commending humility, washed the disciples' feet in a basin[^13]. Now, then, the fleece is dry, Christ has been pressed out from it. He was truly pressed out, because thrust out through afflictions. *For the tenants said: This is the heir, come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours. And they killed him*, it says, *and cast him out of the city*[^14]. The fleece being pressed out, they sent the water outside. From which, yet so that humility might be stretched out, the pressed-out one took away whom he wished[^15], and went to the nations, that he might fulfill the other sign: with the fleece dry, he watered the threshing-floor. And this faith is not in the cloud of the fleece, but in open manifestation: it is preached to all, known by all. It has a mystery in secret, the word in public. These things, as they were said, so are they; as they were promised, so were they rendered. *As we have heard, so also we have seen*[^16], because to us in unity it was said: *Hear, daughter, and see*[^17].

**4.** But the heretics, separated from the framework of this world, neither wish to hear what they read nor to see what they know. We have been invited to the wedding, we have praised the bride and commended her from the divine testimonies. We have commended ourselves to ourselves, rather the Lord commends us to all. For he himself promised, he himself displayed. Let them open their eyes and see what they have heard. You hear with me, see with me. Rather, what is worse, you both hear with me and see with me and are not with me. What wonder is it, then, brothers, if the pagans are unwilling to hold what they spurn, when the Donatists are unwilling to believe what they read? The pagan wished to throw my book into the flame, he detested the Scripture itself, he persecuted it. What wonder, if he grieves to see what he wished to burn? But you, heretic, say that you saved this book from the flame. The one you would not have burned, recognize when it is brought forth, hear when it is read. Surely in this book is: *In your seed all the nations shall be blessed*[^18]. Surely you would not have these letters burned. Why did you burn in them with desire of dissension? Why, I beseech, except because you lie, except because you say you saved what you handed over? Let us not seek the old things that were done: let the book itself be brought forth, let it, saved from the flame, judge between us. Let us see who belongs to the eternal flame, who handed over the book to the temporal flame. And both he who dared to burn and he who dared to hand it over wished it to perish, but it did not perish. It was saved, it is safe: it is brought forth. Do you ask by whom it was handed over, do you ask by whom it was saved? Let it be brought forth, let it be read. He who believed it, did not hand it over. Do you wish to see how dear that Scripture is to me? It is read, and I follow. Do you wish to see how hateful it is to you? It is read, and you fight back, you resist, you alienate your hearing. Since you do not have ears in your heart, you have closed your heart; the Scripture which you say you saved knocks, and you do not open. If you truly saved this book, your strongbox is better than your heart. But far be it that I believe you sought to save what I see you refuse when it is read. From the saved book you wish to contrive a charge against me, from the read book I show a charge against you. The pagan, if he does not believe, hates what I read; you, who do not wish to seem led to impiety through the pagan, have overcome the pagan in impiety. He hates and throws it away, you hold it and deny it. You say I burned the testament, you who see me hold the inheritance; you say you saved the testament, when you have made yourself disinherited. I bring forth the testament, I read the testament: you do not want me to bring it forth, you bring it forth. Let it be brought forth by you, which is read for me. Do you bring it forth, do you hold it, do you open it, see, read. The hand of the one holding, the eyes of the one seeing, the tongue of the one sounding fight for me. From you, against you, I plead my cause. He who recites the book in your congregation is your reader, my witness. But these people hear and see, those people neither hear nor see. Let us pass from these and leave them, if it can be done without the zeal of animosity, to think about their own impiety.

**5.** But the remaining pagans, still remaining for this reason, that our faith may still have those who reject it, that it may have God as its approver. Let it address them, and let them know that what is done was written before it was done, and was foretold because it was going to be; and let them not be terrified because what they do not wish is done, but let them believe that what God promised would be is done. They would wish their own will to be done, but God preferred to fulfill his own promise. Let them not be angry. He is better, I think, he is more powerful. However proud they are, God is greater. A few things, then, which for the constraint of time we could, we have excerpted from the holy Scriptures: how the prophets foretold that at certain times the idols would be taken away from the midst, and this has been fulfilled; how they foretold that the Church would be spread through the whole world, and so it has been fulfilled. Let us not, then, be much angry with the pagans, because they are unwilling to believe about the idols, when the heretics are unwilling to believe about the Church. Both see, both do not believe, but some neglect, others read. The crime of the pagan in not believing what he neglects is not so great as that of the heretic in not believing what he reads. But yet the pagans too, by the very fact that the things which were foretold are fulfilled, ought to recognize the one who foretold and fulfilled them. The things, then, which were said about this matter, hear.

**6.** In the book of Wisdom it is written thus: *For this reason too there shall be a visitation upon the idols of the nations*[^19]. For as though God was not looking upon the idols long adored, the long-sacrificed-to idols. He who always sees, at some time looked upon. He saw unto patience, he looked unto vengeance. *Upon the idols*, he says, *of the nations there shall be a visitation, because the creatures of God have been made into a hatred*[^20]. For they provoke God from the creatures of God. How do they provoke God from the creatures? For the craftsman made the god, but God the wood. The goldsmith made the idol, but God the gold. Why do you make of his thing what he hates, from the creature of God do you form badly what he hates? Form rather well what he loves. You wish to place your image on wood, receive the image of God in yourself. Why do you erase within and carve without? *The creatures*, he says, *of God have been made into a hatred, and into temptations for the souls of men, and into a snare for the feet of the foolish. For the beginning of fornication is the seeking out of idols*[^21]. If a woman having many husbands is praised, let the soul too be praised that worships many gods. If a woman is an adulteress through true husbands, how much more the soul through false gods? *For the beginning of fornication is the seeking out of idols, and their invention is the corruption of life*[^22]. Behold how he condemned them, let us see what he foretold: *For neither were they from the beginning nor shall they be forever*[^23].

**7.** Zechariah too the holy prophet says: *In that day.* See what he says, and recognize the day. For he puts "day" for "time," as the Apostle: *Behold, now is the acceptable time, behold, now is the day of salvation*[^24]. What, then, says Zechariah? *In that day there shall be a place open to the house of David*[^25]. This is what I was saying, that it was closed in the fleece, open on the threshing-floor. *There shall be a place open*, the Church will be manifest. What is *there shall be a place open*? *A city set upon a mountain cannot be hidden*[^26]. *There shall be a place open to the house of David.* *And it shall be in that day, the Lord will exterminate the names of the idols from the earth, and there shall be no remembrance of them at all*[^27]. Let the pagans hear these things. Let them see this done which was foretold. If they love divination, let them recognize divinity. Why for a great matter do they go to one delirious astrologer to be consulted about one man—and let him say more false things than true by chance, who in false things makes his setting? Why, then, does he wish to hear what the astrologer is going to say about one man? Let him hear God foretelling about the human race. From the earth the names of the idols are blotted out, in heaven the names of Christians are written[^28].

**8.** Isaiah too says: *The insolence of men shall be humbled and shall fall, and the Lord alone shall be exalted in that day, and they shall hide all things made by hand, bringing them into caves and into the caverns of the rocks*[^29]. See whether it is not so, see whether it is not done, see whether the Scripture lies. As though we sought the idols and did not wish to make the worshipers of idols worshipers of God, they hide them in the earth, they hide them in a cave, they hide them in the cavern of the rock. Wherever they hide them, from there it is dug out, when it is overthrown in the heart. How many have hidden their false gods when perverse, and betrayed them when corrected. For altogether we see these two kinds of men to be in the Church, rather one kind from there in the Church, the other now in the neighborhood of the Church, both of those who hide and of those who repel and bring forth. Both kinds God foretold. You have heard of the kind that hides, hear of the kind that betrays and throws away, in the same passage, in one testimony of Isaiah: *The Lord alone*, he says, *shall be exalted in that day, and they shall hide all things made by hand, in caves and in the caverns of the rocks and in the clefts of the earth, from the face of the fear of the Lord, from the brightness of his might, when he shall arise to break the earth*[^30]. What is *to break the earth*? To terrify the earthly. Therefore we say that every bold man, if he has feared, is broken with fear. No wonder that the earth fears, for heaven thunders. In the prophetic Scriptures are thunders. Therefore those will hide them. What of the others? They too out of fear, but a better one. Those out of fear of the laws hide them, these out of fear of the divine law bring them forth and throw them away. In both there is fear, but by fear some advance, others fail. We have heard of those hiding, let us hear of the other kind. *For in that day a man shall cast away his abominations of silver and of gold which they made*[^31]. It is wonderful that gold and silver are cast away: piety casts away what impiety loves. They cast away *their abominations*, do they not also cast away the wooden ones, cast away the earthen ones too, cast away the stone ones too? Yet for the commendation of a more devoted mind, they cast away, he says, *the gold and silver ones.* What does he love, who now does not love gold? What losses does he fear, who does not fear the losses of gold and silver? They cast away *the abominations of silver and gold which they made to adore.* *The vain and harmful things* some hide, others cast away: the abomination yet is taken away. Let it be taken away, and would that, as from the places of the earth, so from the hearts of the earthly. Through the hands of his servants let God take away these things from places, by his own hand from hearts.

**9.** Let us hear Jeremiah too. Although I mentioned this yesterday too, it is too sweet, and let it be repeated today too—for we cannot grow tired of it: *Lord, my strength and my help and my refuge in the day of evils*[^32]. No longer is stone and wood *my refuge*, no longer is gold and silver, the works of the hands of men[^33], *my refuge.* I do not, he says, flee to those who cannot flee: *For they have feet and do not walk*[^34]. *Lord, my refuge in the day of evils*, in the day of temptations, in the day of this labor, in the day of weak life, in the day of sighs from the desire of the heavenly Jerusalem. For as long as we are not where we desire to be, it is a day of evils. In hope we rejoice, not yet in the reality. Hence too in another psalm: *You have led me because you have become my hope*[^35]. Our hope on the way, when it leads. And how a refuge? *A tower of strength from the face of the enemy*[^36]. What, then, of the idols, what was going to be? *To you*, he says, *the nations shall come from the end of the earth and shall say: How did our fathers possess lies?* The very *how* is the wonder of the sons at the error of their fathers, as though they say: "How were our elders prudent, how did they worship these things, how did they bend their uprightness before wood and stones, how, leaving him by whom they were made, did they possess the vain things which they made, *how did our fathers possess lies?*" What are those *lies*? *Idols, and there is no use in them*[^37]. For lies are wont to seem sometimes useful to certain greedy men; and the trader swears that he may sell dearer, he swears for gain that he may acquire, he lies for gain that he may acquire, he lies in the idol that he may perish. Nor in those lies *is there use*, if we consider true and wholesome use. But there is at least that which seems to men, which is thought by the unskilled. Here, in the end, what do you pay out to hire a craftsman, you pay out to feed it, you pay out to adorn it, you pay out to set it up, you pay out to sacrifice to it? You pay out the whole that you may acquire nothing, you lose many things that you may perish.

**10.** Likewise Jeremiah: *Do not walk according to the ways of the nations*[^38]. Come now, my brothers, let none of you defend the idols. Every defender of an idol is near to a worshiper. *Do not walk according to the ways of the nations, and do not be afraid of the signs of heaven, because the nations fear them, the sight of them*[^39]. Because it is the custom of the nations to fear *the signs of heaven*, that is, to choose days when you set out, when a wife is married, when a warp is set up and when another is cut down, *because the customs of them are vain*[^40]. And now hear about the idols: *It is wood cut out of the forest, the work of the craftsman and a casting of silver*[^41]. He seems to say a known thing, but the one to whom he says it is too deaf. He rouses him as it were from sleep, that he may see with his heart too what he sees with his eyes. *The work of the craftsman and a casting, they are adorned with silver and gold, on anvils and with nails they fastened them, and they will not be moved.* For of themselves *they will not be moved*: men movable in body carry the immovable thing. They would not carry the immovable thing, movable in body, if they were immovable in heart. *On anvils and with nails they fastened them, and they will not be moved. Engraved silver.* And it is added: *They will not walk.* *From Tarshish will come gold of Uphaz*—precious and great gold—*and the hand of the artisans, the work of all the artisans, they will clothe them with hyacinth and purple.* *Lifted up they are carried, because they do not enter in. Do not fear them, because they will not harm, and there is no good in them. Thus you shall say to them.* What shall we say to them? *Gods who did not make heaven and earth, let them perish from the earth and from under heaven*[^42]. What is *thus you shall say to them*? You are Christians, you hear the prophet: thus say to them, do not keep silent. *Gods who did not make heaven and earth, let them perish from the earth and from under heaven.* Surely from heaven, where they never were? Let those worshipers too take note of these foretold things, let them take note and believe. *Let* these *perish from under heaven*, let those be written in heaven[^43]. Surely thus you say to them; do not be seduced by those who say: "Neither worship nor mock." Receive rather, Christian, the prophet; hear God rather through the prophet: *You shall say to them: Gods who did not make heaven and earth, let them perish from the earth and from under this heaven.* And as though he said: "Whom shall I worship, when those have perished?", *God made heaven and earth in his strength*[^44]. Why do you wish to worship the earth, when you are earth and on the earth and you yourself made of earth? You have God founding the earth. He has not withdrawn from you in his work, nor is he a stranger even to the heart. He himself made what you tread, he himself made what you look up to, he himself is the one in whom you believe. *God made the earth in his strength, he founded the world in his wisdom*[^45]. Did he perhaps not speak of Christ who said: *God made the earth in his strength, he founded the world in his wisdom*? Hear the apostle Paul: *Christ the strength of God and the wisdom of God*[^46]. *He stretched out heaven and the multitude of waters in heaven and led the clouds from the end of the earth, he made lightnings into rains and led out light from his storehouses. Every man is made foolish from his own knowledge, and every craftsman is confounded over his graven images, because they cast lies, there is no spirit in them. They are vain, works of mockery, in the time of their visitation they shall perish*[^47]. The false gods perish, but you have not remained without the true God, if you are the younger son Jacob, whom the elder serves[^48]. Let the false gods perish, do you hold the true one. For hear what follows: *Not of this kind is the portion of Jacob*[^49]. These vain things, these false gods will perish, but the *portion of Jacob* does not perish. *The portion of Jacob*: the inheritance of Jacob. The inheritance of Jacob which God gives, or God himself? I dare to say that it is God himself. I take up the psalm, the sentence is evident: *Lord, the portion of my inheritance*[^50]. *Not of this kind is the portion of Jacob, for he who formed all things, he is his inheritance, the Lord is his name*[^51].

**11.** We have heard the things foretold, what will happen to the idols; let us hear the precepts, what must be done to the idols. Let no one say: "Surely the images were foretold as going to perish: let him destroy them. Why do you break them?" But God wished to destroy them through those whom he did not wish to perish. *And he will bring you*, he says, *to the Amorite and the Hittite and the Perizzite and the Canaanite and the Girgashite and the Hivite and the Jebusite, and I will blot them out.* *You shall not adore their gods nor serve them. You shall not do according to their works, but by tearing down you shall tear down and by breaking you shall break their images*[^52]. And again: *Behold, I will cast out before your face the Amorite, the Canaanite and the Hittite and the Perizzite and the Hivite and the Girgashite and the Jebusite.* *Make no covenant with those dwelling in the land into which you will enter to them, lest it be an offense among you. Their altars you shall tear down and their pillars you shall break and their groves you shall cut down. For you shall not adore alien gods. For the Lord God is jealous, his name is God the jealous one*[^53]. Hear, brothers, you shudder because it was said: *The Lord is jealous.* O soul of the Church, O that spouse, you fear a jealous husband: keep chastity.
