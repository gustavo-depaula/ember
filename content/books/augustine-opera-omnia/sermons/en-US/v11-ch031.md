# Sermon 272/B: Augm. (Dolbeau 31)

**1.** On this day I believe that your love knows that the Church celebrates the coming of the Holy Spirit of the Lord: for the Lord promised that he would send the Holy Spirit to his apostles, and according to his most faithful promise he surely fulfilled what he promised. For as the resurrection of the Lord confirmed in the faith of men his divinity, who for our sake deigned to become man, so much more his ascension into heaven, and more fully and more perfectly by the gift of the Holy Spirit which he sent and filled his disciples, now made new wineskins, so that they could receive new wine—therefore, when they spoke with tongues, they were called *drunk and full of new wine*[^1]. The voice of those hearing was a testimony of the Lord's Scripture: for *no one puts new wine into old wineskins*[^2], the Lord had said. He was preparing, then, new wine for new wineskins. For they were old wineskins, as long as they thought carnally of Christ. For to an old wineskin pertained that saying of the apostle Peter, when the Lord said to him, fearing lest Christ die and as it were so perish as the rest of men too: *Get behind me, Satan; you are a scandal to me*[^3]. That wavering of Peter pertained to an old wineskin. But when the Lord rose, and showed himself to them, and they handled what they had lamented hanging on the cross; they saw the living members, which dead and buried they were lamenting; they were confirmed in faith and believed him. He ascended into heaven, and charged that they gather themselves in one place and there await until he should send his promise. Gathered, then, in one place, by praying and desiring the promise, they put off oldness and were clothed with newness. Made now capable, then, they received the Holy Spirit on the day of Pentecost. And not without cause do we celebrate this day, a great and most evident sacrament indeed. But let your holiness attend, how the old and new Scriptures sound in harmony with one another: for there grace was promised, here given; there figured, here perfected. Just as, if some artificer of things to be formed from some metal, bronze or silver, first composes of wax the forms which he is going to cast, and the first sketch makes the way for the future solidity—for he makes those very forms which he is going to fill—so too the Lord delineated and shaped all things for the old people by figures, but for the new people he filled them by a most perfect outpouring. What, then, that formation is and what this fulfilling on the day of Pentecost, let your holiness mark somewhat more attentively: it is worth the attention; with great fruit it is learned, when what is said is more attentively heard. Be surely you too new wineskins, that through our ministry you may be able to receive the wine.

**2.** It is often asked of us: "If we celebrate the day of Pentecost on account of the coming of the Holy Spirit, why do the Jews celebrate Pentecost?" For they too have a Pentecost. You heard this morning, you who were attentive, when the reading of Tobit was read at the memorial of the blessed Theogenes, that on the day of Pentecost he made himself a dinner, intending to invite some of his own who were worthy to share the table with him, from the fact that there was in them the fear of the Lord: *On the day of Pentecost,* he says, *which is holy of the weeks*[^4]. For seven times seven make forty-nine: to this is added one for the sake of unity, that we may return to the head, because unity confirms all multitude, and multitude, unless it be bound by unity, is quarrelsome and contentious; but a multitude in concord makes one soul, as it was even for those very ones who received the Holy Spirit, as the Scripture says, *there was one soul and one heart toward God*[^5]. They make, then, fifty, which is the sacrament of Pentecost. Why, then, do the Jews celebrate it, except because there was there a figure? Attend: you know—and there is no Christian at all who is ignorant of what I am about to say—that among the Jews a lamb is slain, and Passover is celebrated in figure of the future passion of the Lord. For this too was commanded them, that they seek a lamb from the goats and the sheep. Whence can a lamb be found from goats and sheep? But what was there commanded as impossible announced the future possibility of the Lord: for a lamb was found from goats and sheep, because our Lord Jesus Christ, born according to the flesh of the seed of David, has his origin from sinners and just men. You find in the origin of the Lord, according to the generations which the evangelists set forth, both many sinners and just men: therefore he also called such, that is, sinners, because he came through such too. For he gathers his Church from just men and sinners, intending to send the just into the kingdom of heaven, and to set apart the sinners who persevere in sins and wickedness; yet he came so to bear our sins that he did not disdain to take on the origin of sinners. And there are many sacraments there, in those very generations, which God will grant that there be time to set forth to your holiness. But now let us return to what we had begun.

**3.** We were speaking of the day of Pentecost, why the Jews celebrate that very day. They slay a lamb, the slaying is of the paschal lamb: so we too celebrate the Pasch, where the lamb is slain, immaculate, without fault, truly a lamb, to whom John bore witness, saying: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sins of the world*[^6]; by his passion we celebrate the Pasch. The law was given to the Jews in fear; the Holy Spirit was given to Christians in grace. They could not fulfill the law through fear, and through the law itself they were made guilty. The law has five books; five porticoes surrounded the pool of Solomon, but they bore the sick, they could heal none of them. The five porticoes bore the sick, where they lay, just as in the books no one was healed. Why no one? On account of pride. For when they think they can fulfill by their own strength what is commanded, they did not fulfill what was commanded. And the law was against them, in which they are found guilty, until they cry out, what we said this morning too to your holiness: *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^7]. The law, then, shows men guilty, grace frees from guilt; the law threatens, grace caresses; the law intends punishment, grace promises indulgence. Yet these very things which are commanded in the law are also commanded in grace; and therefore that law is said to be written by the finger of God. So we have it written.

**4.** But what the finger of God is, let us seek in the Gospel, and we shall find it. What does the finger of God signify? For God does not truly have such a form of body as we have. Does he both see from one part and not see from another? or is the figure of his members bounded, who is everywhere whole and present to all? What, then, is the finger of God? The Holy Spirit. Attend: whence do we prove this? From the Gospel. For sometimes what one evangelist says figuratively, another says the same place more openly. There is in a certain place of the Gospel, where the Jews said of the Lord that in the name of Beelzebub he casts out demons. But the Lord answering: *If I cast out demons by the finger of God, surely the kingdom of God has come upon you*[^8]. Another evangelist so expounds that same place, saying: *If I by the Holy Spirit, then the kingdom of God has come upon you*[^9]. When, then, one evangelist says the finger of God, another expounds it, that he may show us that the Holy Spirit is the finger of God. Let us not seek fingers of flesh in God, but let us understand why the Holy Spirit is called a finger. Because through the Holy Spirit the apostles received divisions of gifts; but in the fingers the division of the hand appears: there is computation and distribution. Why, then, do the Jews celebrate Pentecost? A great sacrament, brethren, and altogether wonderful: if you observe, on the day of Pentecost they received the law written by the finger of God, and on the day of Pentecost the Holy Spirit came.

**5.** But this must be proved concerning the giving of the law, which the Jews received on tablets of stone—but the hardness of their heart was signified—yet written by the finger of God, because all the things written there are themselves commanded also to Christians, but now, as the Apostle says, *not on tablets of stone, but on the fleshy tablets of the heart*[^10]. This, then, is the difference, that the same law was written on their hard hearts and was not fulfilled; the same was given to the already believing hearts, and is easy and everlasting for Christians. Therefore that was a stone; but the hearts of Christians were fruitful earth, which could bring forth fruit. Therefore the Lord too in the Gospel, when that woman was offered to him who had been found in adultery—and they wished to stone her according to the law, but the Lord wished that she should not sin thereafter, ready to forgive her sin—said to those who wished to stone her, since they themselves were stony: *If any among you is without sin, let him first cast a stone at her*[^11]. And when he said this, he bent his head and began to write with his finger on the ground; but they, considering their consciences, withdrew one after another from the greatest to the least, and that woman was left alone. But the Lord lifted his head and said to her: *What is it, woman? Has no one condemned you?* And she said: *No one, Lord.* And the Lord: *Neither will I condemn you. Go, sin no more hereafter*[^12]. What did this indulgence signify? Grace. What did that hardness signify? The law given on stones. Whence the Lord was writing with his finger, but now on the earth, whence the fruit could come. But on stone whatever is sown does not come up, because it cannot send down a root. The finger of God, and the finger of God: by the finger of God the law was written; the finger of God is the Holy Spirit.

**6.** The law was given on the day of Pentecost, and the Holy Spirit came on the day of Pentecost. But we had said we would prove that on the fiftieth day the Jews received the law, from the Pasch which we celebrate. You have that it is commanded them, that on the fourteenth day of the first month they slay the lamb and celebrate the Pasch. There remain of the month seventeen days, if you count the fourteenth day itself, from which the Pasch begins. They came to the desert, where the law was given, and so the Scripture says: *But in the third month from when the people was led out of Egypt*[^13], the Lord spoke to Moses, that those who were to receive the law should purify themselves unto the third day, on which the law was to be given. At the beginning of the third month, then, purification is commanded unto the third day, and the Pasch begins . . . Attend, lest the numbers as it were deceive you and bring a mist of understanding upon you. As much as we can, we open it, the Lord granting. If your attention aids us, you will quickly see what is said; but if it does not, whatever I say will be obscure, even if it is said most plainly. Therefore the Pasch is announced on the fourteenth day of the month; and purification is commanded, that the law might be given on the mountain, written by the finger of God—but the finger of God is the Holy Spirit: remember, for we have proved this from the Gospel. Purification is announced unto the third day of the third month. From the first month, then, subtract thirteen, and there remain seventeen, that you may begin from the fourteenth; add the whole second month: they make forty-seven days; from that day of purification unto the third day they make fifty days. Nothing more manifest, nothing more evident, that on the day of Pentecost the Jews received the law.

**7.** But to the hard it was a burden, to the hard it was a weight. But the Lord came with grace and cries out: *Come to me, you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will refresh you. Take my yoke upon you and learn of me, for I am meek and humble of heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light*[^14]. How is his yoke easy? The law threatens, he caresses. The law says: "If you do it not, I will punish"; Christ says: "Whatever you have done, I forgive; hereafter see that you sin not." Therefore his yoke is easy, and his burden light. But let us become new wineskins, let us await his grace attentively: greatly shall we be filled with the Holy Spirit, and through the Holy Spirit there will be in us love, now when we have been made fervent with new wine and inebriated with his inebriating and glorious cup, so that we forget those worldly things which held us, just as the martyrs forgot, when they went to the passion—and they forgot both sons and wives, and parents casting dust on their heads, and mothers offering their breasts, with the reproach of milk separating them from food, they forgot all things and did not recognize their own. Why do you wonder, if he does not recognize his own? The man is drunk. But whence drunk? From love. But whence love? From the finger of God, from the Holy Spirit, from him who came through Pentecost.

**8.** Whence do we prove that love is from the Holy Spirit and fulfills the law? By the Apostle saying: *The fullness of the law is love*[^15], and in another place: *Love of the neighbor works no evil. For "you shall not commit adultery, you shall not steal, you shall not kill, you shall not covet," and if there is any other commandment, it is summed up in this saying: you shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^16], because love fulfills the law. But whence do we prove that love is from the Holy Spirit? Hear the Apostle saying that *we glory in tribulations*[^17]. Through tribulations the Jews were compelled to fulfill the law, and could not; Christians were not separated from the law by tribulations, but rather ran to the law. See what I say, brethren. A penalty was set for the Jews, that whoever sacrificed to idols should be stoned or crucified, yet in such a way that fear pressed them, love did not hold them: they did not fear, because they were conquered by desire, and they went after idols, where the cross, where the threat of death and stoning hung over them, and they were not called back by these penalties. Afterward, because love and fear came, love succeeded. The Gospel was preached to the nations; that they should sacrifice to idols, they began to threaten them with fire, crosses, beasts, and they suffered all those things which were threatened and inflicted on them by kings, nor yet did their heart turn aside to idols. By what penalties those could not be called back from idols, by those penalties Christians could not be compelled to idols, because now there was love from the Holy Spirit. *But we also glory,* says the Apostle, *in tribulations, knowing that tribulation works patience, patience proof*—for we wish to prove that the love which fulfills the law of God is from the Holy Spirit—*for tribulation works patience, patience proof, proof hope; but hope does not confound, because the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^18].

**9.** And so, brethren, we celebrate the anniversary day of the coming of the Holy Spirit, but daily the Holy Spirit ought to be in our heart. Nor let us think that only on this day ought this festivity to be, but on the other days it ought not to be: let us not celebrate it on one day, but at all times, that we may be found not reprobate, but proved on the day of the Lord when he comes, that those to whom he gave the pledge, these he may lead also into everlasting possession. For Christ betrothed his Church, he sent her the Holy Spirit. This Spirit was given as it were a ring. He who gave the ring will give in rest immortality. Let us love him, hope in him, believe in him.  
Come at the afternoon hours a little earlier on account of the hymns of God. Others are inebriated with the wine of the earthly vine in which is luxury, and let us be inebriated with the songs of God; with the songs of salvation praising the Lord, let us at some time forget the earth, that from the earth we may merit to be lifted to heaven, by the bestowing of our Lord Jesus Christ who lives and reigns with God the Father.
