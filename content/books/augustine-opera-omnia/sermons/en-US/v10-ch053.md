# Sermon 371: On the Nativity of the Lord

**1.** A Savior is born for us today; and therefore today the true sun has risen for the whole world. God was made man, that man might become God: and that the servant might be turned into the Lord, the Lord received the form of a servant. The dweller of the heavens dwelt on earth, that man the dweller of the earth might dwell in the heavens. A Savior, then, is born for us; he suffered the day of birth, conceived of the repudiated Law: but born of the Father always, of the mother once. For we have received two nativities of our Lord Jesus Christ: first the divine, then the human; but plainly both wonderful: in the one, so that the offices of a mother, in the other, so that the offices of a father should cease: the one eternal, that he might create temporal beings; the other temporal, that he might furnish eternal ones. He, then, in the form of God equal to the Father, he in the form of a servant subject to the Father. He the creator of the times, was born in time: and was made so small, that he was brought forth by a woman; but surely he remained so great, that he was not separated from the Father. These nativities of the Lord the two beginnings of the evangelists too attest. For one of the divine nativity says thus: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: the same was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was nothing made*[^1]. But of this human nativity, thus another evangelist reports: *The book of the generation of Jesus Christ son of David*[^2]. This, then, is the birthday of the Lord, this is the day of the second nativity: let us exult and rejoice in it. And not undeservedly, with the day now increased from this point, today the light receives an increment; since to the human race on this very day the true light has come: that the day rightly increases the path of the sun, on which it brought to us him, through whom we might be loosed from the darkness of death. Him, then, the prophets announcing as going to come, like lamps, preceded the day of his rising, and the wonders which he was going to do in the flesh they published with the clearest proclamations. For it was right that he be foretold as going to come, lest, when he had come, there be doubt. This our God, then, lived as man among men. For he appeared to those who saw, man; to those who understood, God: offering himself as man to those gazing, keeping himself as God to those believing. Therefore the sight of his weakness saved the weak: but the contemplation of his divinity seeks the strong.

**2.** I beseech you, dearest ones, behold how great is this mystery of truth. To save men he had given the Law, had sent the prophets: and because these remedies preceded for the healing of languors, God wished to offer himself to men for their salvation. But men could not see God himself in his substance: yet men ought not to place their hope in man alone. What, then, should be done here? They ought not to follow man, man who could be seen was not to be followed: God who could not be seen was to be followed. That, then, there might be displayed to man both one who could be seen by man and one whom man might follow, God was made man. Finally, when he was now living among men, and stood in the midst of three apostles brought aside in secret, suddenly he shone forth to them in that brightness of divine glory: which the apostles who were present could scarcely behold for the weakness of human feeling[^3]. He willed to be born in human fashion, that we might be born in him, and to his future worshipers to sanction the sacraments of a repeated nativity: that we who from that nativity were held bound and liable, accompanying the footsteps of our Savior, might obtain the safeguard of a second nativity by a sure protection; by which, born in God and from God, we might break the chains of the ancient death, when we received as the pledge of salvation the Holy Spirit. Therefore, when God wished to appear to men, and even present wished to teach them the things which he had before commanded, he tempered his divine power by the assumed manhood, and set darkness as his hiding-place around him[^4], when he covered himself with the tabernacle of the flesh. By such and so ineffable a sacrament, then, Christ our God is held both man and God; through the mother man, through the Father God. And so it comes about that both of those are true which he said: *The Father is greater than I*[^5]; and: *I and the Father are one*[^6]. For by the divinity he is equal to the Father, by the incarnation he is subject to the Father.

**3.** Yet certain ones are wont to ask, how man and God could be mingled. They seek the reason of this mystery, which was done once: when they themselves can by no means render the reason of that which is done always, that is, how the soul is mingled with the body, that a man may be made. As, then, a corporeal thing and an incorporeal thing are joined, that a man may be effected: so man was joined to God, and Christ was made. And yet, that Christ might be made, those two incorporeal things, that is, soul and God, could more easily be joined and mingled, than one incorporeal and another corporeal are mingled, that is, soul and body, that the person of a man may exist. But if God the creator of heaven and earth, since he was God, was made man, and humbled himself unto death, even death on a cross[^7], how much ought not earth and ashes to be proud[^8]? See, brothers, how greatly God humbled himself for the sake of men. How greatly ought a servant to cast himself down, when even the Lord descended to so great humility? Which humility, dearest ones, if it is most fully possessed by men, will even advance unto charity. For while one esteems another superior, love makes equality. Whence let not man despise himself, for whose sake surely God deigned to undergo these things.

**4.** And I indeed, brothers, for whom I desire to be spent and over-spent[^9], although I have always made you great in my conscience; yet this very matter makes you somehow greater to me, when I attend to how great is the regard of my Lord for man. You surely are the price of the Lord's incarnation, you the price of the Lord's blood, you the members of Christ, for you Christ is the head: for you he did not defer to be born, for you to suffer all things; the cross itself too he bore for this, that he might fit you to himself into his household. You are called the brothers of Christ, you are called the heirs of Christ. Accordingly, dearest ones, let each one hold himself worthy, before whom he ought not to sin; let him esteem himself worthy, before whom, if he should think of an offense, he should blush. *For you were bought with a great price: glorify and bear God in your body*[^10]. Here he was born for you, here he was offered for you; here too, if you act worthily, he dwells in you. Let us act, then, meditating on the law of the Lord day and night, that we may merit to grasp him, to see him. Let us act, that since God deigned to descend for the sake of men, man may be able to ascend to God.
