# Sermon 200: On the Epiphany of the Lord

**1. 1.** To adore the childbearing of the Virgin, the Magi came from the East. This day we celebrate today, to it we pay the solemnity and sermon that are due. To them this day first shone forth, to us it has returned in a yearly festival. They were the firstfruits of the Gentiles, we the people of the Gentiles. To us the tongue of the apostles announced this, to them the star as it were the tongue of the heavens: and to us the same apostles, as it were the heavens, declared the glory of God[^1]. For why should we not recognize as heavens those who were made the seats of God? as it is written: *The soul of the righteous is the seat of wisdom*[^2]. For through these heavens that maker and dweller of the heavens thundered, by which thunder the world trembled, and behold, now it believes. A great sacrament. He lay then in a manger, and led the Magi from the East. He was hidden in a stable, and recognized in heaven; that, recognized in heaven, he might be made manifest in the stable, and this day be called "Epiphany," which in Latin can be called "manifestation": commending at once his loftiness and his humility, that he who was shown in the open heaven by sidereal signs, sought in a narrow lodging, might be found; weak in infant limbs, wrapped in infant swaddling clothes, might be adored by the Magi, feared by the wicked.

**1. 2.** For King Herod feared him, when those same Magi announced it to him, while they were still seeking the little child whom they knew, with heaven as witness, to be already born. What will the tribunal of the one who judges be, when the cradle of an infant terrified proud kings? when now more wisely kings seek not, as Herod, to kill him, but rather, as the Magi, delight to adore him, especially now that he, even the death itself which the enemy desired to inflict, endured, even for those very enemies, from his enemies, and, being slain, slew it in his own body. Let kings now piously fear him as he sits at the right hand of the Father, whom that impious king feared while he still suckled at his mother's breasts. Let them hear what is written: *And now, you kings, understand; be instructed, you who judge the earth: serve the Lord in fear, and exult in him with trembling*[^3]. For that king, the avenger of impious kings and the ruler of the pious, was not born as kings are born in the world; for he too was born whose kingdom is not of this world[^4]. There was nobility in his being born, in the virginity of her who bore him; and nobility in her who bore him, in the divinity of him who was born. In short, when so many kings of the Jews had already been born and had died, the Magi never sought to adore any of them: because they learned of none of them by the speech of heaven.

**2. 3.** Yet, which must not be passed over, this enlightenment of the Magi stood as a great testimony to the blindness of the Jews. In their land these men sought the one whom those did not recognize in their own. Among them these men found the infant whom those denied among themselves as he taught. In these lands these pilgrims from afar adored the child Christ not yet bringing forth words, where those, his fellow citizens, crucified the young man working miracles. These men recognized God in small limbs; those did not spare him even as a man in great deeds: as though it were more to see a new star gleaming at his nativity than his sun mourning at his death. But now, that the same star which led the Magi to the place where God the infant was with his virgin mother — which surely could have led them even to the city itself — yet withdrew itself, and did not appear to them at all, until the Jews themselves were questioned about the city in which Christ would be born, so that they themselves should name it according to the testimony of the divine Scripture, they themselves should say: *In Bethlehem of Judah. For so it is written: And you, Bethlehem, land of Judah, are not the least among the princes of Judah: for out of you will come forth a leader who will rule my people Israel*[^5] — what else did divine providence signify here, except that among the Jews there would remain only the divine writings, by which the Gentiles would be instructed, they themselves blinded; which they would carry not to the aid of their own salvation, but to the testimony of ours? For today, when we bring forth the prophecies foretold about Christ, now made clear by the light of accomplished facts, if perhaps the pagans, whom we wish to win, say that these things were not foretold so long before, but composed by us after the event, so that what has been done might be thought prophesied: we recite the books of the Jews, that the doubt of the pagans may be removed: who were already figured in those Magi, whom the Jews instructed, from the divine oracles, about the city in which Christ was born, and yet themselves neither sought him nor recognized him.

**3. 4.** Now therefore, dearest ones, sons and heirs of grace, *see your calling*[^6], and, Christ being made manifest to Jews and Gentiles as a cornerstone, cleave to him with most persevering love. For he was made manifest in the very cradle of his infancy to those who were near and to those who were far off[^7]; to the Jews in the nearness of the shepherds, to the Gentiles in the distance of the Magi. The former are believed to have come to him on the very day on which he was born, the latter today. He was made manifest, then, neither to the learned nor to the righteous. For unskillfulness prevails in the rusticity of the shepherds, and impiety in the sacrileges of the Magi. Both that cornerstone joined to himself: for he came to choose the foolish things of the world, that he might confound the wise[^8]; and to call not the righteous, but sinners[^9]; that no great one might be proud, no lowly one despair. Whence the scribes and Pharisees, while they seem to themselves too learned and too righteous, of whose being born they pointed out the city by reciting the prophetic oracles, rejected him, building. But because *he was made the head of the corner*[^10], and what he showed in being born he fulfilled in suffering: to him let us cleave, with the other wall, having the remnants of Israel, which by the election of grace have been saved[^11]. For those shepherds prefigured them to be joined from close by, that we too, whose calling from afar the coming of the Magi signified, may now no longer be pilgrims and strangers, but remain citizens of the saints and members of the household of God, built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, Christ Jesus himself being the chief cornerstone[^12]: who made the two one, that in one we may love unity, and may have an untiring love for gathering the branches which, grafted even from the wild olive, by pride broken off, became heretics, since God is able to graft them in again[^13].
