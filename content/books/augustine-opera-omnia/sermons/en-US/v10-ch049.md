# Sermon 367: On the Words of the Gospel, Luke 16:19-31, on Dives and Lazarus

**1.** Your holiness noticed, dearest brothers, and with me, as I think, with the whole intention of the mind perceived the opulence of the rich man, and the want of the beggar; the one boiling with foods, and the other failing with hunger. Both indeed men, both carnal, both mortal: but not both equal. One nature, but not one life. None of them immune from the condition of death: and yet the one feasts splendidly, and the other grows foul in rags and in misery. He rejoiced in delicate foods by the contrivance of cooks: the other, if crumbs fell from his table, waited for them. Let the rich now hear, who are unwilling to be merciful; let them hear that we are all born by one law, live by one light, breathe one air, are extinguished too by one death: which, if it did not intervene, the poor man himself would not endure. This ulcerous and naked Lazarus, lying, is carried by the hands of the angels to Abraham's bosom. Behold, the rich man, refreshed and splendid, is shut in the tartarean prison. Where is that garment of fine linen? where the life abounding and overflowing with many resources? Do not all things pass away in death like a shadow? *We brought nothing into this world*, says the Apostle, *but neither can we take anything away*[^1]. We take or snatch nothing with us. What if we took anything, would we not devour living men? What is this greed of concupiscence, when even the beasts have a limit? For they seize when they hunger: but they spare the prey, when they have felt satiety. Inexplicable alone is the greed of the rich. Always it seizes, and is never satisfied: it neither fears God, nor reveres man: it neither spares the father, nor recognizes the mother: it neither obeys the brother, nor keeps faith with the friend: it oppresses the widow, invades the property of the orphan: it recalls the freed to slavery, brings forth a false testament. The goods of the dead are seized; as though those too who do this did not die. What, then, is this madness of souls, to lose life, to seek death? to acquire gold, and lose heaven? But because no one thinks of God, therefore judgment remains in death.

**2.** Deservedly it was said to the rich man: *Because you received good things in your life, and Lazarus likewise evil things; but now he is consoled here, but you are tormented*[^2]. Let the rich hear these things, who are unwilling to be merciful. Let them hear that punishments are inflicted on those who are unwilling to dispense aids. Let them hear the poor man being refreshed, let them hear the rich man boiling with graver punishments. *Father Abraham*, he says, *send Lazarus, that he may dip the tip of his finger in water, and cool my tongue; for I am tormented in this flame.* But he: *Remember*, he says, *son, that you received good things in your life, and Lazarus likewise evil things*[^3]. Punishments are weighed for riches, refreshment for poverty, flame for purple, refreshment for nakedness, that the fairness of the balance may be safe, and the measure of that measuring may not lie: *In what measure*, he says, *you have measured, in that it will be measured back to you*[^4]. Therefore mercy in punishments is denied to the rich man, because he himself, while he lived, was unwilling to be merciful. Therefore the rich man asking is not heard in torments; because he did not hear the poor man asking on earth.

**3.** The rich man and the poor man are two contraries to each other: but again two necessary to each other. No one would be in need, if they supported one another; and no one would labor, if they both helped each other. The rich man was made on account of the poor man, and the poor man was made on account of the rich man. It is the poor man's to pray, and the rich man's to dispense; it is God's to weigh great things for small. From his small mercy, great abundance is born. The field of the poor is fruitful, it quickly returns the fruit to those who have lordship. The poor man is the way of heaven, by which one comes to the Father. Begin, then, to dispense, if you do not wish to stray. The fetter of your patrimony, by which you are bound, loose in this life; that you may freely be able to approach heaven: cast away from yourself the burdens of riches, cast away the voluntary chains, cast away the anxieties and weariness, which disquiet you in your many years. Give to the one asking, that you yourself may receive: bestow on the poor man, if you do not wish to be burned by the flames. Give on earth to Christ, what he may render you in heaven. Forget what you are, and attend to what you will be. The present life is fragile, and prone to death. No one can remain: but we are all compelled to pass on. Unwilling we come, against our will we go out, because we are evil. But if we sent something ahead of us, we would not come to an empty lodging. For what we give to the poor man, we send ahead of us: but what we snatch, we leave the whole of here.

[^1]: 1 Tim 6:7
[^2]: Luke 16:25
[^3]: See Luke 16:24-25
[^4]: See Matt 7:2
