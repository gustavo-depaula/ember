# Sermon 23

**1** What we have sung to the Lord, let us believe to be set before us to speak of. From this let our sermon to you arise. And he to whom we said, *You have held my right hand, and in your will you have led me, and with glory you have taken me up*[^1], may he himself take up your hearts to a clearer understanding, and may his mercy and grace help us—me as I speak, you as you judge. For although, for the convenience of delivering the voice, we seem to stand in a higher place, yet in that very higher place you judge and we are judged. We are called teachers, but in many things we seek a teacher, and we do not want to be held masters. For it is perilous, and forbidden by the Lord himself, who says: *Do not wish to be called masters; one is your master, Christ*[^2]. The office of master is perilous, then; discipleship is secure. Therefore the psalm says: *To my hearing*, it says, *you will give joy and exultation*[^3]. For the hearer of the word is more secure than the utterer of the word. Therefore he, secure, *stands and hears him, and rejoices with joy because of the voice of the bridegroom*[^4].

**2** And because the Apostle had taken on the person of a teacher by the necessity of his stewardship, see what he says: *I was among you in much fear and trembling*[^5]. It is safer, then, that both we who speak and you who hear should know ourselves to be fellow disciples under one master. It is altogether safer, and this is expedient, that you should hear us not as masters but as your fellow disciples. For see that anxiety is laid upon us where it is said: *Brothers, do not become many masters, for in many things we all offend*[^6]. Who would not tremble when the Apostle says, *all*? And it follows: *Whoever does not offend in word, this man is a perfect man*[^7]. But who would dare to say that he is perfect? He, then, who stands and hears does not offend in word. But he who speaks, even if—which is difficult—he does not offend, what does he suffer when he fears lest he offend? You ought, therefore, to be not only hearers of those who speak, but also compassionate toward those who fear, so that in what we say truly—since all truth is from the Truth—you may praise not us, but him; but where we offend as men, you may pray to that same one for us.

**3** The holy Scriptures are truthful, they are blameless. *All Scripture inspired by God is useful for teaching, for reproof, for exhortation, for instruction*[^8]. There is no reason, then, why we should accuse Scripture, if perhaps we, not understanding it, go astray in some matter. When we understand it, we are upright. But when, not understanding it, we are crooked, we are leaving it upright. For we who are corrupted do not corrupt it; rather it stands upright, that we may return to it to be corrected. Yet, that this same Scripture may exercise us, in many places it speaks, as it were, carnally, though the law is always spiritual. *For the law*, as the Apostle says, *is spiritual, but I am carnal*[^9]. Since, then, it is spiritual, yet often it walks with the carnal, as it were, carnally. But it does not want them to remain carnal. For a mother too loves to nurse her little one, but she does not love for him to remain little. She holds him in her bosom, warms him with her hands, consoles him with caresses, nourishes him with milk. She does everything for the little one, but she longs for him to grow, that she may not always do such things. See the Apostle. For we look better at him, who did not disdain even to call himself a mother, where he says: *I became a little one in your midst, like a nurse cherishing her children*[^10]. For there are nurses who cherish, but not their own children. Likewise there are mothers who give their children to nurses, not cherishing their own children. But the Apostle, with the genuine and rich affection of love, took on both the person of a nurse by saying *cherishing*, and of a mother by adding *her children*. The same nourisher and cherisher, then, says in another place, which I recalled a little before: *I was among you in much fear and trembling*[^11].

**4** But you say: "What sort of people were they, that he was among them with much fear and trembling?" *As to little ones*, he says, *in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not solid food. For you were not able to take it, but neither indeed are you yet able; for you are still carnal*[^12]. Those whom he calls carnal he nevertheless calls little ones in Christ. He so rebukes them as not to disown them. They are both carnal and little ones in Christ. Yet he does not want them to be carnal, those whom he calls little ones in Christ. He longs for them to be spiritual, judging all things, and judged by no one. For *the unspiritual man*, as he himself says, *does not receive the things that are of the Spirit of God: for it is folly to him, and he cannot know it, because it is judged spiritually. The spiritual man judges all things, but he himself is judged by no one*[^13]. The same man says: *We speak wisdom among the perfect*[^14]. Why, then, do you speak, if it is among the perfect? For what need is there of your speaking to a perfect man? But ask in what he is perfect. For perhaps I do not find a perfect knower, yet I do find a perfect hearer. There is, then, a perfect hearer too, now capable in mind, in whom solid food causes no disturbance, brings on no indigestion. *Who is this, and we will praise him?*[^15] Yet I do not doubt that there are some spiritual, who hear well and judge well. With these I have no trouble. For either I am found carnal, and he deals mercifully with me; or he grasps what I say, and rejoices with me.

**5** See, now I have taken up the words of the psalm we just sang: *You have held my right hand*[^16]. Suppose a carnal hearer—what will he think, except that God appeared in human form, took hold of his right hand, not his left, and led him into his own will, took him up where he wished? If he understands this, or rather if he thinks this, does he understand at all? For he who understands, understands what is true. But he who thinks what is false does not understand. Therefore if this carnal man thinks that God's nature and substance is distinguished by members, defined by a shape, circumscribed by quantity, abiding in a place, what do I do with him? If I say, "God is not so," he does not grasp it. If I say, "He is so," he as it were grasps it, but I deceive him. I cannot say "He is so," lest I lie; and not about anything whatever, but about my God, about my Savior and Redeemer, about my hope, about him toward whom I stretch out my longing. It is no light matter to lie about such a one. To be deceived about such a one is grievous and perilous. But to lie about such a one is destructive and ruinous. Yet not everyone who lies is deceived. For if he himself knows the truth, and speaks what is false, he lies, he is not deceived. But if he himself thinks true what is not true, he is deceived; and if he says what he thinks to be true, he does not lie, but is nonetheless deceived. May God grant him not to be deceived, who did not wish to lie.

**6** If, then, as I said, that little one of ours believes God to be such, having members arranged in fixed places of his body, circumscribed by a figure, bounded by a shape, abiding in a place, moving himself from place to place, according to what is said: *Where shall I go from your spirit, and where shall I flee from your face? If I ascend into heaven, you are there; if I descend into hell, you are present*[^17]. If he himself is in heaven, if he is on earth, if he is present in hell, what does that little one do now? If he hears, let him not seek, with the Samaritan woman, mountains and temples where he wishes to go to God, from Jerusalem to the mountains of Samaria. Let him not hasten to a visible temple, let him not seek some temple where he may come to God. Let him himself be the temple, and to him God will come. He does not despise, does not shrink away, does not disdain—rather he deigns, if you do not provoke his indignation. Hear him promise, hear him for the time deigning by promising, not provoked to indignation by threatening: *We will come*, he says, *to him, I and the Father*[^18]. To the one whom he called above his lover, the one who obeys his commands, the keeper of his commandment, the lover of God, the lover of his neighbor: *We will come*, he says, *to him, and make our dwelling with him*[^19].

**7** The heart of the faithful man is not narrow, for whom the temple of Solomon was narrow. For he too, when he built it, said: *If the heaven of heavens does not suffice you*[^20]. And yet *the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^21]. For we, he says in another place, are the temple of the living God. "And from where do you prove this?"—as if it were said to him—*As it is written*, he says, *I will dwell among them*[^22]. If some great patron said to you, "I will dwell with you," what would you do? Since your house is narrow, no doubt you would be troubled, you would be utterly terrified, you would wish it would not happen. For you would not want to be in narrow straits, receiving a great man, for whose coming your poor little house would not suffice. Do not fear the coming of your God, do not fear the affection of your God. He does not narrow you when he comes; rather, by coming he will widen you. For that you may know he will widen you, he promised not only his coming, *I will dwell among them*, but also that very breadth, by adding: *And I will walk about*[^23]. This breadth, if you love, you see. *Fear has torment*[^24], therefore it has narrowness; and thus love has breadth. See the breadth of love: *Because the love of God has been poured out*, he says, *in our hearts*[^25].

**8** But why were you asking him to widen the place? Let the indweller himself widen it: *For the love of Christ has been poured out in our hearts*, yet not from ourselves, but *through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^26]. If love has been poured out in our hearts, and *God is love*[^27], see, already from however small a pledge God walks about in us. For we have received a pledge. What is that, of which such is the pledge? Although the codices are better that have "earnest" than those that have "pledge." For the translators meant to say the same thing. Yet there is some difference in the usage of speech between earnest and pledge. For a pledge, when it is given, once that has been given for which the pledge was given, the pledge will be taken back. I do not doubt that many of you have now understood. For I see—but from the conversation in which you speak to one another, I perceive that those who have understood want to explain to those who have not yet understood. So I will say it somewhat more plainly, that it may reach all. You receive, for example, a codex from your friend; that he may give it to you, you give some pledge. When you have returned what you received, for which you set down the pledge, he will have what you returned, you will receive the pledge back. For he will not have both things.

**9** What then, brothers? If God has now given love as a pledge, as it were, through his Spirit, when he has given the reality itself, by promising which he gave the pledge, is the pledge to be taken from us? Far be it. Rather, what he gave he will fulfill. This is why "earnest" is better than "pledge." For sometimes you prepare, for example, to give the price for a thing you hold under a contract of good faith; of that very price you give something. And it will be an earnest, not a pledge—something to be fulfilled, not to be taken back. Now, then, understand. If I find a lover, he has the earnest, and from the earnest he longs for the fullness. Let him consider the earnest itself. For that will be fulfilled from which the earnest was given. Let him think of it, let him examine it within himself, let him inspect it, let him question it about that fullness which he does not see, lest perhaps he should long for something different in the fullness than is in what he received as earnest. Perhaps God will give gold, will complete a fullness of gold, and gave an earnest of gold. It is to be feared lest you long for lead instead of gold. Look, then, at the earnest. If I can persuade you where to look: *God is love*[^28].

**10** From there we have something, from there we have been sprinkled, from there bedewed. Of what is the dew such as the fountain? Sprinkled with this dew, but burning toward the fountain, say to your God: *For with you is the fountain of life*[^29]. In this dew the longing is born, at the fountain you will be satisfied. There is what suffices us. *But the sons of men will hope under the covering of your wings*[^30]. Do we long, as for something great, for those benefits of God that he gives even to the beasts? They are indeed his benefits, who doubts it? From whom is the safety even of the least living thing, except from him of whom it was said: *Salvation is the Lord's*[^31]?

**11** But the same psalm says: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord, as your mercy has been multiplied, O God*[^32]. Although merciful God, you have so manifold a mercy that it reaches not only to men but even to beasts. So great is your mercy that you make your sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rain upon the just and the unjust. Do your holy ones receive nothing special from you, does the devout man receive nothing proper, which the impious does not receive? Plainly he does receive it. Hear what follows. For he had already said: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord, as your mercy has been multiplied, O God*, and he added and said: *But the sons of men*[^33]. What then? A little before, those whom you called men, were they not sons of men? *Men*, he says, *and beasts you will save, O Lord; but the sons of men*. What then? *The sons of men will hope under the covering of your wings*[^34]. This not with the beasts. Why, then, are both these and those men? Or are not the sons of men also men? Surely the sons of men are also men. Whence, then, this distinction, except that there is a man who was not a son of man? A man not a son of man, Adam; a man son of man, Christ. *As in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive*[^35]. They seek salvation with the beasts who die, and who die not to live again. They seek salvation with the sons of men who die, that they may never die. That distinction is refined out. Those belong to men, as men; these, the sons of men, belong to the Son of man.

**12** What, then, follows? *But the sons of men will hope under the covering of your wings*[^36]. See, I hope, see, hope. But *hope that is seen is not hope*[^37]. The things to come, then, that are promised, will inebriate. *They will be inebriated by the abundance of your house*[^38]. I fear lest perhaps, just as a little before he was seeking members of the body in God, so in his inebriation he may think not of the satiety of ineffable goods, but of the drunkenness of carnal banquets. Let us say it nonetheless. Let him think what he can, if he cannot think greater things. Let him not withdraw from the bosom while he grows. Let us follow, and we who can, as much as we can, let us be spiritually delighted. *They will be inebriated*, he says, *by the abundance of your house, and you will give them to drink from the torrent of your delight*[^39]. With what wine, what new wine, what water, what honey, what nectar? You ask with what? *For with you is the fountain of life*[^40]. Drink life, if you can. Prepare your conscience, not your gullet, your mind, not your belly. If you have heard, if you have understood, if you have loved, as much as you could, you have already drunk from there.

**13** Attend to what you have drunk. You have drunk love. If you know it: *God is love*[^41]. If, then, you have drunk love, tell me in what place you drank it. If you know it, if you have seen it, if you love—from what do you love? For whatever you love well, you love by love. But how do you love something by love, you who do not love by love? Therefore if you love, from what do you love? It came to you, and you know it and you see it. And it is not seen in a place, nor sought with bodily eyes, that it may be loved the more ardently. Nor is it heard by address, and when it came to you, it was not perceived by its step. Did you ever feel the soles of love walking about in your heart? What is it, then? Whose is this, which is already in you, and is not grasped by you? Thus learn to love God.

**14** But he walked about in paradise, but he was seen at the oak of Mamre, but he spoke with Moses on Mount Sinai face to face. And what of it? See, one who is seen in a place, yet is not perceived by his step. Do you want to hear Moses himself, lest the restless infant make me weary, though I desire to nurse him? Do you want, then, to hear Moses himself? Surely he was speaking with God face to face. To whom, then, was he speaking, except to the very one with whom he was speaking: *If I have found grace before you, show me yourself*[^42]? He speaks with him face to face, *as one speaks to his friend*[^43], and he says to him: *If I have found grace before you, show me yourself plainly*[^44]. What did he see, and what did he thirst for? If it was not he himself, how is it said to him, *Show me yourself*? We cannot say that it was not he himself. If it had not been he himself, Moses would have said to him, "Show me God." When, therefore, he says, *Show me yourself*, he makes plain that it was he himself whom he wanted shown to him. *And he spoke with him face to face, as one speaks to his friend*[^45]. Do you want, then, to hear, if you grasp God? He appeared to Moses while remaining hidden. For if he did not appear, there would not be one with whom, speaking face to face, Moses might say, *Show me yourself*. But if he were not hidden, Moses would not still seek to see that same one. If, then, you grasp it, if you understand it, God can at the same time both appear and lie hidden—appear in form, lie hidden in nature.

**15** If you have understood this as you could, see that it does not steal upon you that God, in order to appear in whatever form he wishes, changes his own nature. God is unchangeable, God is unconvertible—not only the Father, but the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^46]. That very Word, God, is unchangeable, as is God with whom the Word is. Think of no loss in any person, no change. For God is the Father of lights, *with whom there is no change, nor shadow of a moment*[^47]. "If, then, he is unchangeable," you say, "what is that form in which, as he wished, to whom he wished, he appeared—by walking, by sounding, or even by presenting himself to those very carnal eyes?" You ask me what it is from which God makes his own presence, as though I could already explain from what he made the world, from what he made heaven, from what he made earth, from what he made you. "I see now," you answer, "from mud." See, you from mud. From what the mud? You answer, "From earth." But I suppose not from that earth which another made, but from that earth which *he made who made heaven and earth*[^48]. From where, then, the earth itself? From where heaven and earth? *He spoke and they were made*[^49]. Good, you answer most excellently. You acknowledge: *He spoke and they were made*. I ask nothing more. But just as, when you say, *He spoke and they were made*, I ask nothing more, so do not you ask more, when I say: He willed, and he appeared. He appeared as he judged fitting; he lay hidden, as he was.

**16** Let our true affection, our love, our delight burn from that pledge toward that for which Moses too burned, who said to the one he saw: *Show me yourself*[^50]. If, then, we seek this, we are his sons. *For we are sons of God, and it does not yet appear what we shall be. We know that when he appears we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^51]. Not as he was seen at the oak of Mamre, not as he was seen by Moses, that we should still say to him, "Show us yourself," but *we shall see him as he is*[^52]. By what merit? Because we are sons of God. And this not from our merits, but by the grace of his mercy. *For you set apart a free rain, O God, for your inheritance. And it was made weak*[^53], not by presuming of itself to see what it does not see, but by believing what it longs to see. *But you perfected it*[^54]. His inheritance, therefore, made perfect, his sons, *we shall see him as he is*[^55]. But what did the Lord say of the sons? *Blessed are the peacemakers, for they shall be called sons of God*[^56].

**17** Therefore, concerning such hidden and most difficult questions, if we understand something too little, let us inquire peaceably. *Let not one be puffed up* for one *against another*[^57]. *For if you have bitter zeal and contentions among you, this is not the wisdom that comes down from above, but earthly, unspiritual, devilish*[^58]. We are, then, sons of God, and let us be recognized as his sons; and we are not recognized unless we are peacemakers. For we shall have no means by which to see God, if by contending we put out that very eye within us.

**18** Attend to what he says, why it is in fear and trembling that I speak. *Pursue peace with all, and sanctification, without which no one will be able to see God*[^59]. How did he terrify the lovers? He terrified none but lovers. Did he say, *Pursue peace with all, and sanctification*, that whoever does not have it will be cast into the fire, will be tormented in eternal fire, will be given over to tireless torturers? These things are true, yet he did not say them. He wanted you to be a lover of the good, not one who dreads evil, and from the very thing you longed for he terrified you. You will see God. Is this why you despise it, this why you stir up quarrels, this why you raise disturbances? *Pursue peace with all, and sanctification, without which no one will be able to see God*[^60]. How foolish would two men be who, wishing to see the sun rise, contended between themselves in what part it would rise and how it could be seen, and a controversy arising between them, quarreled, and quarreling struck each other, and striking put out their own eyes, so that they could not see that rising? Therefore, that we may be able to see God, let us cleanse our hearts by faith, heal them by love, strengthen them by peace, because this very thing by which we love one another is already from him whom we long to see.
