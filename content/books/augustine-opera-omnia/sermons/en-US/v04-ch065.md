# Sermon 141

**1.** Among other things, when the holy Gospel was read, you heard what the Lord Jesus says: *I am the way, and the truth, and the life* [^1]. The truth and the life every man desires; but the way not every man finds. That God is a certain eternal, unchangeable, intelligible, understanding, wise life, making men wise, even some philosophers of this world saw. The fixed, stable, undeviating truth, where are all the reasons of all created things, they saw indeed, but from afar; they saw, but set in error; and therefore by what way one might come to that so great and ineffable and beatifying possession, they did not find. For that they too saw (as far as it can be seen by a man) the creator through the creature, the maker through what is made, the fabricator of the world through the world, the apostle Paul is witness, whom surely Christians ought to believe. For he says, when he was speaking of such men: *The wrath of God is revealed from heaven upon all impiety*. These, as you recognize, are the words of the apostle Paul. *The wrath of God is revealed from heaven upon all impiety and injustice of men, who hold back the truth in iniquity* [^2]. Did he say they do not hold the truth? But: *they hold back the truth in iniquity*. Good is what they hold; but evil is where they hold it. *They hold back the truth in iniquity*.

**2.** But it occurred, that it might be said to him: Whence do those impious men hold the truth? Did God speak to any of them? Did they receive the law, as the people of the Israelites through Moses? Whence, then, do they hold the truth, or in that very iniquity? Hear what follows, and he shows it. *Because what is known of God*, he says, *is manifest in them; for God manifested it to them*. *He manifested it to them* to whom he did not give the law? Hear how *he manifested it. For his invisible things, through the things that are made, are understood and beheld*. Question the world, the adornment of heaven, the brightness and arrangement of the stars, the sun sufficient for the day, the moon the solace of the night; question the earth fruitful with herbs and trees, full of animals, adorned with men; question the sea, with how great and what kind of swimming things it is full; question the air, with how great flying things it teems; question all things, and see whether they do not, as it were, answer you by their own perception: God made us. These things the noble philosophers too sought, and from the art knew the artificer. What then? why is the wrath of God revealed upon that very impiety? Because *they hold back the truth in iniquity?* Let him come, let him show how. For he has already said how they knew. *His invisible things*, that is, of God, *through the things that are made, are understood and beheld; his everlasting power too and divinity; that they may be inexcusable. Because, when they had known God, they did not glorify him as God, or give thanks; but they became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened*. They are the Apostle's words, not mine: *And their foolish heart was darkened; for saying that they were wise, they became fools* [^3]. What they found by curiosity, they lost by pride. *Saying that they were wise*, that is, attributing the gift of God to themselves, *they became fools*. They are, I say, the Apostle's words: *Saying that they were wise, they became fools*.

**3.** Show, prove their foolishness. Show us, Apostle, and just as you have shown us whence they could arrive at the knowledge of God, since *his invisible nature is clearly perceived in the things that have been made*[^4], so now show how, *claiming to be wise, they became fools*[^5]. Listen: For they *exchanged*, he says, *the glory of the incorruptible God for the likeness of an image of corruptible man and of birds and of four-footed animals and of serpents*[^6]. For the pagans made gods for themselves out of the shapes of these animals. You found God, and you worship an idol. You found the truth, and you hold that very truth in unrighteousness. And what you came to know through God's works, you lose through the works of man. You considered everything; you gathered up the order of the heavens, the earth, the sea, and of all the elements; yet you refuse to attend to this, that the world is the work of God, the idol the work of a craftsman. If, just as the craftsman gave the idol its shape, he could also give it a heart, the craftsman would be worshiped by that very idol. For just as your craftsman, O man, is God, so man is the craftsman of the idol. Who is your God? The one who made you. Who is the craftsman's God? The one who made him. Who is the idol's god? The one who made it. So if the idol had a heart, would it not worship the craftsman who made it? See in what iniquity they held the truth, and did not find the way that leads to that possession which they had seen.

**4.** But Christ, because he himself with the Father is truth and life, the Word of God, of whom it was said, *the life was the light of men*[^7]—because, then, he himself with the Father is truth and life, and we had no way to go to the truth, the Son of God, who in the Father is always truth and life, by taking up a human being became the way. Walk by way of the man, and you arrive at God. You go through him, you go to him. Do not seek any way of coming to him apart from him. For if he had been unwilling to be the way, we would forever go astray. So he became the way by which you might come. I do not say to you, Seek the way. The way itself has come to you: rise and walk. Walk by your conduct, not by your feet. For many walk well with their feet and walk badly in their conduct. For sometimes those who walk well run off the way. Indeed, you will find people who live well and are not Christians. They run well, but they do not run on the way. The more they run, the more they go astray, because they depart from the way. But if such people arrive at the way and hold to the way, oh what great security there is, since they both walk well and do not go astray! But if they do not hold to the way, however well they walk, alas how lamentable it is! For it is better to limp on the way than to walk strongly off the way. Let this be enough for your Charity.
