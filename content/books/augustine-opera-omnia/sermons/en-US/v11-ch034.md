# (Erfurt 1 = Sermon 282 augm.) (Erfurt 1)

**1.** The holy martyrs whose feast day we celebrate today not only stood out for the surpassing virtues of their suffering, but also, in return for so great a labor of devotion, marked out their own reward and that of their companions by their very names. For Perpetua and Felicity are indeed the names of two women, but the reward belongs to all. For not all the martyrs labored bravely for a time in the contest of confession and suffering except in order to rejoice in perpetual felicity. By the governance, therefore, of divine providence these women had to be not only martyrs but also most closely joined companions, as in fact they were, so that they might mark out one day for their glory and hand down a common solemnity to be celebrated by those who came after. For just as by the example of their most glorious contest they exhort us to imitate them, so by their names they bear witness that we are to receive a gift that cannot be divided. Let the two hold to each other, let them bind each other together; we do not hope for the one without the other. For Perpetua is of no profit if Felicity is not there, and Felicity abandons us if Perpetua is not there. On the names of the martyrs, by which this day is consecrated to us, let this little, suited to the occasion, suffice.

**2.** But as for the women themselves, to whom these names belong: as we have heard when their suffering was read, and as we know to have been handed down to memory, these women of such great virtues and merits were not only females but also women, one of whom was also a mother, so that to the weakness of her sex was added a more impatient affection. Take your example of faith, fortitude, patience, and devotion from a mother if you are celibate, from women if you are young men, from wives if you are virgins! To this was added that, because of the refined station of their birth, they had been reared after the manner and with the love of their parents, so that in everything the enemy, attacking them as though they were unable to bear the hard and cruel burdens of persecution, believed they would at once yield to him and soon become his own. But by the most cautious and most steadfast strength of the inner man they blunted all his snares and broke his assaults.

**3.** In the warfare of Christ the King, so as to yield to no adversities in the most ready array of battle, not held back by their weaker sex, not unnerved by womanly thoughts, not softened by the world's flattery, not terrified by its threats, they fought as women ardently, as wives manfully, the delicately reared with hardness, the weak with strength, conquering the flesh by the spirit, fear by hope, the devil by faith, the world by love. With these arms our King's army is unconquered; girded with these arms, the soldiers of Christ triumphed not by preserving but by having the members of their body cut to pieces, and not by killing but by dying. For they preferred the rule of the eternal King to the rule of a temporal king, and they handed over their bodies, so as neither to serve nor to worship any god but their own God, not fearing those who kill the body but him who has the power to kill both body and soul in the gehenna of fire. And so they took thought not only for their spirits, which they fixed unmoved in their victorious resolve, but even for the very bodies that they seemed to despise, so that the iniquity of persecution might sow them in dishonor and the truth of the judgment raise them again in glory.

**4.** In this struggle Perpetua, as had been revealed to her in a vision, turned into a man and conquered the devil, stripped of the world and clothed with Christ, advancing into the unity of the faith and the knowledge of the Son of God, into a perfect man, and made a chief member in his body, for which she had cast off the whole body, not one member.

**5.** In this struggle Felicity was not hindered from the confession of martyrdom even by the burden of her womb. For she was pregnant both in body and in heart: the one she had conceived by a divine fellowship that made her fruitful, the other by a human marriage; the one birth the law of nature was delaying, the other the force of persecution was pressing on; the one lacked the ripeness of time, the other had at hand the opportunity of interrogation; the one would be lost in miscarriage if it were hurried, the other would be slain if it were denied. This most devout woman, then, remembered that she would give birth to the penalty of a woman and confess the offspring of a virgin: to the one she had made room in her bodily womb, to the other in her spiritual breast; by the one she was burdened in human fashion and languished, by the other she was honored in divine fashion and rejoiced. And so, having heard well and firmly believed Christ himself when he said, *Whoever does the will of my Father, he is my brother and sister and mother*[^1], she who had become Christ's mother before Adam's brought forth the heavenly man by confessing him before the judge, and kept the earthly man by groaning in prison. There too, what was her answer, when she gave the cries of Eve in the pains of childbirth and it was said to her that, if she was so unable to bear the distress of giving birth, what would she do under the beasts! *Here,* she said, *it is I who suffer what I suffer; but there another will suffer for me, for whose faith I am about to suffer.* Rightly did it come about that, when she met that savage cow renowned for cruelty, she so failed to feel it that she did not even retain it in memory. It was shown to her what she herself was in the burden of her womb, and what was granted to her in the suffering of martyrdom.

**6.** (3) In this company of distinguished glory there were also men who were martyrs; on that very same day men too conquered by a most brave suffering; yet they did not commend that day by their own names. This came about not because the women were preferred to the men in dignity, but because womanly weakness overcame the fiercest enemy by a greater miracle, and manly virtue contended for perpetual felicity.

[^1]: See Matt 12:50
