# Sermon 229/V: From the Sermons of the Octave of Easter

**1.** On the sixth day God said: *Let the earth bring forth the living soul*; not the creeping things of living souls, but the living soul. And the earth brought forth all the cattle and beasts and serpents and whatever creeps upon the earth. *And God saw that it was good. And God said,* on the same sixth day, when the earth brought forth the living soul: *Let us make man to our image and likeness*[^1], and the rest that follows. We have said that God made light, that is, all the faithful; he made the firmament in the world, the heaven between the waters and the waters; he made the firmament in the Church, the authority of the Scriptures between the peoples of the angels, to whom this is not necessary, and the peoples of men, who would seek God there; God made in the world the separation of the sea from the land, God made in the Church the separation of the nations from his servants; the dry land thirsts for rain, men in the Church thirst for the heavenly rain; the earth brought forth the herb of fodder and fruit-bearing trees, men in the Church too bring forth the works of mercy; luminaries are made in heaven; the greater luminary, the word of wisdom; the lesser luminary, the word of knowledge; the stars, the gifts of healings, of prophecy, of faith, and the rest; all these in the firmament of heaven. When, then, they had begun to walk and journey through the whole world, the waters generated, that is, out of the nations there arose the creeping things of living souls, the holy sacraments; and men consecrated, having the form; for the form of the sacrament is in man, and sometimes the power of the sacrament follows at once, but now in the earth that has been separated; therefore the earth brings forth the living soul. And indeed, brothers, it is needful to be a living soul. The brothers have received baptism; let the power follow the form. Let baptism be not unto judgment, but unto salvation. It was unto judgment for Simon Magus, unto salvation for Peter. Let us see, then, what the living soul is, which the earth brought forth. Attend to man: he has a body, he has a soul. In the soul itself he has many motions like those of the beasts; and he has something else, which the beasts do not have. What motions has he like the beasts? To eat and drink, to sleep and wake, and to beget. Are these not common to us with the beasts? Whoever casts these motions into self-indulgence has a dead soul, not a living soul. How do we prove it? Hear the Apostle: *She who lives in pleasures is dead while living*[^2]. What is it to live in pleasures, but to loosen those motions of the soul that we have in common with the cattle into immoderate pleasures and desires, into voracity, into drunkenness, into fornication, into drowsiness? They live in pleasures who live thus; but living, they are dead. But if all these affections are constrained, and these motions are held to chastity, to continence, the earth brings forth a soul; which, because it was dead in pleasures, it remains that, restrained from pleasures, it begin to live and to have the power of piety. But what is the power of piety? Love toward God and neighbor. But he who has love restrains by faith all lusts, all desires, all the motions of his soul which he has in common with the cattle; he will be a living soul. And it follows that man is now formed to the image and likeness of God. But he is formed when all those things that have been spoken of are brought together in that man. Because God made light, it was said of man in the Church; and he made the firmament, the authority of the Scriptures for the sake of man; and he separated the waters from the land, the nations from believers; and the earth brought forth the herb of fodder, the works of mercy; and the lights in heaven, the holy Evangelists in man; and the earth brought forth the creeping thing of living souls, the sacraments; and this in men. Afterward it brought forth the living soul, the restraints of all lusts; and this in man. Join all these things for understanding, and man was made to the image and likeness of God. Attend to how we may prove this. Therefore of each single thing he said: *God saw that it was good*[^3]. But when he made the better thing, he did not see that it was made better; but in man himself he concludes all the things that were made, and it was said: *God saw all the things he made, and behold, they were very good*[^4]. Long before he had said of each single thing that they were good; with man made, he named them all. It is understood that those single things are all found in man. Do you seek light in man? You find faith. Do you seek the firmament? You find the authority of the Scriptures. Do you seek the separation of the waters? You find the believer separated from the nations. Do you seek the sprouting of herb and fruit-bearing trees? You find good works, the works of mercy. Do you seek the luminaries of heaven? You find the Evangelists. Do you seek the creeping things of living souls? You find the sacraments. Do you seek the living soul? You find continence. Do you seek man in man? You find the image and likeness of God.

**2.** And now attend: man was set in his punishment, and how much the remnants of the image of God avail, which remained in him. He rubbed away that very image through sin, and that same image, which was worn dim through lust, is reformed through grace. For just as a coin, if it is rubbed by the earth, loses the image of the emperor, so the mind of man, if it is rubbed by earthly lusts, loses the image of God. But there comes the moneyer, Christ, who restrikes the coins. And how does he restrike the coins? By forgiving sins through grace: and he will show you that God seeks his own image. For when it was said to him about the tribute of Caesar: *Is it lawful to give tribute to Caesar?*[^5]—for they were tempting him, so that if he said, "Let it not be given," they might bring a false charge against him through the collectors of tribute: "Behold, one who teaches that we ought not pay tribute"; but if he said, "Let it be given": "Behold, one who has cursed Jerusalem, who has made it tributary"—what, then, did he himself say, and what did he warn? *Why do you tempt me, hypocrites? Bring me a coin. And they offered it to him. Whose image and inscription does it have? They answered: Caesar's. Render, then, the things that are Caesar's to Caesar, and the things that are God's to God*[^6]; that is: if Caesar seeks his image on a coin, does not God seek his image in man?

**3.** The earth brought forth the herb of fodder and fruit-bearing trees; men too in the Church bring forth the works of mercy, after those very works which were shown even to the Lord, when he was in the flesh, not only by men, as it was by Zacchaeus[^7], but also by women, who ministered to him of their own substance[^8].

[^2]: 1 Tim 5:6
[^3]: Gen 1:10, etc.
[^4]: Gen 1:31
[^5]: Matt 22:17
[^6]: Matt 22:18-21

[^8]: See Luke 8:3
