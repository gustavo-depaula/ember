# Sermon 197: On the Kalends of January, Against the Pagans

**1.** *For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness*[^1]. Of whom, except both Jews and Gentiles? But lest it be said: Why against the ungodliness of the Gentiles? For the Gentiles never received the Law and became transgressors. Rightly is the wrath of God revealed against the Jews, to whom the Law was given and who refused to observe it; but to the Gentiles it was not given. Behold, brothers, and understand how he shows all to be guilty, and all to be in need of salvation and of the mercy of God. *For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness of those men who hold the truth in iniquity*[^2]. See how he did not say, They do not have the truth, but, *They hold the truth*, he says, *in iniquity*. And as though you should ask, saying: How can those who did not receive the Law have the truth? he goes on: *For what is known of God*, he says, *is manifest in them*[^3]. And how could what is known of God be manifest in those who did not receive the Law? He goes on, and says: *For his invisible things, from the creation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood through the things that are made; his eternal power also and divinity*[^4]. Surely we are to supply, are understood and seen. For why should anyone attend to the works, and not seek the maker? You attend to the earth bearing fruit, you attend to the sea full of its animals, you attend to the air full of birds, you attend to the heaven gleaming with stars, and the rest, and you do not seek the maker of so great a work? But you say to me: These things I see, him I do not see. To see these things he gave the eyes of the body, to see himself he gave the mind. For neither do you see the soul of a man. As, then, from the movements and management of the body you understand the soul, which you do not see, so from the management of the whole world, and from the governing of the souls themselves, understand the Creator. But it is too little to understand. For they understood; and see what the Apostle says: *Because, knowing God, they did not glorify him as God, or give thanks; but they became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened*[^5]. By what desert, except of pride? For see what follows: *For claiming*, he says, *to be wise, they became fools*[^6]. For they ought not to have claimed for themselves what he had given, nor to have boasted of what they had not from themselves, but from him. That ought surely to have been rendered back to him, so that, to hold to what they could see, they might be healed by him who gave them to see. For if they had done this, they would have kept humility, and could have been purged, and would have clung to that most blessed contemplation. But because there was pride in them, the false and deceitful and proud one interposed himself, who would promise them that their souls would be purged by some stages of pride or other, and he made them worshipers of demons. Hence all the rites that are celebrated by the pagans, which they say avail for the purgation of their souls. And hear the Apostle saying these things in consequence, that they received these as the reward of pride; because they did not so honor God as God is to be honored. *And they changed the glory of the incorruptible God into the likeness of an image of corruptible man*[^7]. Now they are idols. And these indeed of all the Greeks and other nations, which have the likeness of men. But because there is no greater and more superstitious idolatry than that of the Egyptians; for Egypt flooded the world with such figments as the Apostle next describes. When he had said, *into the likeness of an image of corruptible man*, he added: *and of birds, and of four-footed beasts, and of serpents*[^8]. For have you, brothers, in other temples seen an image with a dog's head or a bull's, and the figments of the other irrational animals? For these are the idols of the Egyptians. Embracing both kinds, the Apostle says: *into the likeness of an image of corruptible man, and of birds, and of four-footed beasts, and of serpents. Therefore God handed them over to the desires of their heart, to uncleanness; that they might dishonor their bodies among themselves*[^9]. These evils of theirs are from the ungodliness of pride. But these sins, because they are from pride, are not only sins, but also punishments. For when he says, *God handed them over*, it is now of the vengeance of some sin, that they should do these things. *Who changed the truth of God into a lie*[^10]. What is, *They changed the truth of God into a lie? Into the likeness*, namely, *of an image of corruptible man, and of birds, and of four-footed beasts, and of serpents*. And lest any of them should say: I do not worship the image, but what the images signify; he at once subjoined: *And they worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator*[^11]. Understand prudently. For they worship either the image or the creature. He who worships the image turns the truth of God into a lie. For the sea is truth; but Neptune is a lie made by man, the truth of God being turned into a lie: for God made the sea; but man, the image of Neptune. So God made the sun; but man, by making an image of the sun, turned the truth of God into a lie. But lest they say: I do not worship the image, but I worship the sun; therefore he said: *They worshiped the creature rather than the Creator*.

**2.** But perhaps someone will say: Even if he himself was born in humility, he wished to boast in the nobility of his disciples. He did not choose kings, or senators, or philosophers, or orators: rather, he chose common folk, the poor, the unlearned, fishermen. Peter a fisherman, Cyprian an orator. Unless the fisherman had gone faithfully before, the orator would not have followed humbly. Let no one cast down despair of himself: let him hold to Christ, and his hope will not be false.

**3.** What did Simon want, but to be praised in miracles, to be lifted up in pride? For that very thing compelled him to think the gift of the Holy Spirit something to be bought with money[^12]. Contrary to this pride, the Apostle, by remaining in humility, *fervent in spirit*[^13] at noonday, shining with prudence, says: *Neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters; but God who gives the growth*[^14]. For he had said: *I planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the growth*[^15]. And again: *Was Paul crucified for you? or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^16] See how he refuses to be worshiped in place of Christ, and is unwilling to display himself as a bridegroom to a fornicating soul. Does it not seem great to plant and to water? But *neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters*. How did he fear? He says he is not anything toward the salvation of those whom he desired to build up in Christ.

**4.** Nor did the Apostle himself wish hope to be placed in himself, but in the truth which he announced. What was said through him was better than he himself, through whom it was announced. *Even if we*, he says. It is too little; hear what follows: *or an angel*, he says, *from heaven should announce to you anything beyond what you have received, let him be accursed*[^17]. He saw that a false mediator could transform himself into an angel of light[^18], and announce something false. As, then, proud men wish to be adored in place of God, to claim for themselves whatever they can, to be named, and, if it can be done, to surpass Christ himself in glory: so do the devil and his angels. The Donatists hold Donatus in place of Christ. If they hear someone, a pagan, disparaging Christ, they will perhaps bear it patiently, more than if they hear someone disparaging Donatus.

**5.** Because Christ himself speaks in his saints, as the Apostle says: *Do you seek proof of Christ who speaks in me?*[^19] And although he says, *Neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters; but God who gives the growth*[^20] — not because he wished himself, but because he wished him to be loved in himself — yet he bears witness to certain ones, saying: *Because you received me as an angel of God, as Christ Jesus*[^21]. In all his saints, then, he himself is to be loved, who says: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^22]. For he does not say: You gave them; but: *You gave me*. So great is the love of the Head toward his body!

**6.** What is Juno? Juno, they say, is the air. A while ago he was inviting us to worship the sea in Neptune, to worship the earth in the image of Tellus: now he invites us to worship the air. These are the elements of which this world consists. So, setting this forth, the apostle Paul in his Epistle says: *Beware lest anyone deceive you through philosophy and empty deceit, according to the elements of this world*[^23]. For he was touching upon those very ones who expound idols as if more prudently. Therefore, when he said *philosophy*, in the same place he says, *according to the elements of this world*: admonishing that they are to be guarded against not as common worshipers of images, but as more learned interpreters of signs.
