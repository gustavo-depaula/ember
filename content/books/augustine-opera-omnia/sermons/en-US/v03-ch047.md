# Sermon 81: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 18:7-9, Where We Are Admonished to Beware the Scandals of the World

**1.** The divine readings, which we just now heard when they were read, admonish us to receive, against the scandals which were foretold to come, the strength of virtues, to fortify the Christian breast, and this from the mercy of the Lord. *For what is* man, he says, *that you are mindful of him?*[^1] *Woe to the world from scandals*[^2], the Lord says: the Truth says it: it terrifies and admonishes, it does not wish us to be incautious; for it surely did not make us desperate. Against this *Woe*, that is, against this evil to be feared, dreaded, guarded against, Scripture consoles us, and exhorts, and instructs in that place where it is said: *Much peace to those who love your law, and there is no scandal for them*[^3]. It showed the enemy to be guarded against, but it did not cease to show the fortified wall. You were thinking, hearing: *Woe to the world from scandals*, where you might go outside the world, that you not suffer scandals. So, in order to guard against scandals, where will you go outside the world, except to flee to him who made the world? But how can we flee to him who made the world, except by hearing his law, which is everywhere preached? It is too little that we hear, unless we love. For the divine Scripture, making you secure against scandals, does not say: Much peace to those who hear your law. For not the hearers of the law are just before God. But because the doers of the law will be justified[^4], and faith works through love[^5]: *Much peace*, it says, *to those who love your law, and there is no scandal for them.* To this sentence there agrees too what, by hearing and answering, we sang: *But the meek will possess the earth by inheritance, and will delight in the multitude of peace*[^6]. Because: *Much peace to those who love your law.* For they themselves are the meek, who love the law of God. For *blessed is the man whom you instruct, O Lord, and teach out of your law; that you may soften him from the evil days, until a pit be dug for the sinner*[^7]. How diverse the voices of the Scriptures seem, and yet they so flow together and run into one sentence, that whatever you can hear from that most abundant fountain, you too agree, a friend in accord with the truth, full of peace, fervent with love, fortified against scandals.

**2.** It is set before us, then, to see, or seek, or learn, how we ought to be meek: and from this which I just now mentioned from the Scriptures, we are admonished to find what we seek. Let your love be a little attentive; a great matter is at hand, that we be meek: a necessary thing in adversities. For the adverse things of this world are not called scandals: notice what scandals are. Someone, for example, set in some need, is pressed by affliction. This is not a scandal, that he is pressed by affliction. By affliction the martyrs too were pressed, but not oppressed. Beware the scandal, not so much the affliction. The affliction presses you, the scandal oppresses you. What, then, is the difference between affliction and scandal? In affliction you were preparing to keep patience, to hold constancy, not to abandon faith, not to consent to sin. If you keep this, or if you have kept it, the affliction will not be a ruin to you: but that affliction will avail for that for which it avails in the press, not that the olive be oppressed, but that the oil be pressed out. Finally, if in that affliction you say praises to God, how useful is the press, whence the liquid flows from you? The Apostles sat in affliction, chained, and in that affliction they sang a hymn to God[^8]. What was being pressed? what was being pressed out? Job sat under great affliction on the dunghill, destitute, without help, without substance, without children; full, but of worms, which surely pertains to the outer man. But because within too he was full of God, he praised God, and that affliction was not for him a scandal. Where, then, the scandal? When his wife approached him, and said: *Say something against God, and die*[^9]. For all things being taken away by the devil, Eve, his exerciser, was kept, not for the consolation, but for the temptation of the man. Behold where the scandal. She exaggerated his miseries, her own miseries too along with his, and began to persuade blasphemy. But to him, who was meek, because God had taught him out of his law, and had softened him from the evil days, there was much peace in his heart, loving the law of God, and there was no scandal for him. She was a scandal, but to him she was not. Finally see the meek man, see the one trained in the law of God, the eternal law of God I say. For that law given on the tablets to the Jews was not yet in the times of Job, but the eternal law still remained in the hearts of the godly, from which that was copied which was given to the people. Because, then, he had been softened by the law of God from the evil days, and there was much peace to him loving the law of God, see how meek he is, what he answers. Here learn what I set forth, who are the meek. *You have spoken*, he says, *like one of the foolish women. If we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, shall we not bear evil things?*[^10]

**3.** We have heard by example who the meek are: let us define them in words, if we can. The meek are those to whom, in all good deeds, in all the things they do well, nothing pleases except God; in all the evils they suffer, God does not displease them. Come, brothers, attend to this rule, to this norm; let us extend ourselves to it, let us seek increase, that we may fulfill it. For what does it profit, that we plant and water, unless God gives the increase? *Neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters; but God who gives the increase*[^11]. Hear, you who wish to be meek, who wish to be softened from the evil days, who love the law of God; that there be no scandal in you, and there be much peace for you, that you may possess the earth, and delight in the multitude of peace: hear, you who wish to be meek. Whatever good you do, do not please yourself. *For God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble*[^12]. So whatever good you do, let nothing please you except God: whatever evil you suffer, let not God displease you. What more? *Do this, and you will live*[^13]. The evil days will not swallow you up; you will escape what was said: *Woe to the world from scandals.* For to what world is the *Woe from scandals*, except that of which it was said: *And the world did not know him*[^14]? Not to that world of which it was said: *God was in Christ reconciling the world to himself*[^15]. The bad world, the good world: the bad world, all the bad in the world; and the good world, all the good in the world. As we generally attend to a field. This field is full: of what fruit? Of wheat. And likewise we say, and we say truly, This field is full of chaff. There is a tree, it is full of fruit. Another says: It is full of leaves. And he who says: It is full of fruit, says the truth: and he who says: It is full of leaves, says the truth. Neither did the fullness of leaves take away the place for fruit, nor did the fullness of fruits drive out the crowd of leaves. It is full of both: but the wind seeks one thing, the cultivator gathers another. So then, when you hear: *Woe to the world from scandals*, do not be terrified; love the law of God, there will be no scandal for you.

**4.** But a wife approaches, persuading some evil. You love her, as a wife ought to be loved: she is your member. But *if your eye scandalizes you, if your hand scandalizes you, if your foot scandalizes you*, you just now heard the Gospel, *cut it off, cast it away from you*[^16]. Whoever is dear to you, whoever is held great by you, let him be great so long, let him be your beloved member so long, as long as he does not begin to scandalize you, that is, to persuade something evil. Hear that this is the scandal. We set up the example of Job and his wife: but there the scandal was not named. Hear the Gospel: when the Lord foretold his passion, Peter began to persuade him not to suffer. *Go back behind, Satan, you are a scandal to me*[^17]. The Lord, who furnished you an example of living, plainly taught you both what the scandal is, and how the scandal is to be guarded against. To him, to whom he had said a little before: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah*[^18], he had shown that he was his member. But when he began to be a scandal, he cut off the member: he restored the member, he replaced the member ... A scandal, then, that one will be to you, who begins to persuade you something evil. And let your love attend: this happens very often, not by malevolence, but by perverse benevolence. For your friend, who loves you, and in turn is loved by you, your father, your brother, your son, your spouse, sees you in evil, and wishes to make you evil. What is, sees you in evil? Sees you in some affliction. That affliction perhaps you suffer for justice's sake: you suffer the affliction for this reason, because you are unwilling to bear false witness. By way of example I have said it. Examples abound, because *Woe to the world from scandals.* Behold, for example, some powerful man seeks of you, for his plunder, for his robbery, the service of false testimony. You refuse: you refuse the false, lest you deny the true. Not to delay over many things, he grows angry, he is powerful, he presses. A friend approaches, who does not wish you to be in affliction, does not wish you to be in evil: I beg you, do what is said to you; what great thing is it? Now perhaps as Satan too to the Lord: *It is written of you, that he has charged his angels concerning you, lest you dash your foot against a stone*[^19]. Perhaps this friend of yours too, because he sees you a Christian, wishes to persuade you from the law, what he thinks you ought to do. Do what he says. What? This which he wishes. But it is a lie, it is false. Have you not read: *Every man is a liar*[^20]? Now this is a scandal. He is a friend, what will you do? He is an eye, he is a hand: *Cut it off, and cast it away from you.* What is: *Cut it off, and cast it away from you?* Do not consent. This is what it signifies: *Cut it off, and cast it away from you*, do not consent. For our members in our body make unity by consent, by consent they live, by consent they are joined to one another. Where there is dissension, there is a disease or a wound. So he is your member: love him. But he scandalizes you; *Cut him off, and cast him away from you.* Do not consent; turn him away from your ears, perhaps, corrected, he will return.

**5.** How will you do this which I say, cutting off, and casting away, and perhaps by it correcting him? How will you do it, answer. From the Law he wished to persuade a lie. For he says: Speak. And perhaps he did not dare to say, Speak a lie: but thus, Say what he wishes. You say, But it is a lie. And he, to excuse it: *Every man is a liar*[^21]. And you against it, brother: *The mouth that lies kills the soul*[^22]. Attend, it is not a light thing you heard: *The mouth that lies kills the soul.* What does that powerful enemy do to me, who presses me, because you pity me, and have mercy on my condition, and do not wish me to be in evil; while you wish me to be evil? What does that powerful man do to me? What does he press? The flesh. The body, you say, he presses: I say, he destroys. How much more gently he acts with me, than I if I lie? He kills my flesh: I kill my soul. The angry powerful man kills the body: *The mouth that lies kills the soul.* He kills the body; it was going to die, even if it were not killed: but the soul, which iniquity does not kill, truth receives forever. Keep, then, what you can keep: let that perish which at some time is going to perish. You have answered; and yet you have not resolved *Every man is a liar.* Answer him on this too, lest he seem to himself to have said something to persuade a lie, bringing forth testimony from the Law, pressing you from the Law against the Law. For in the Law it is written: *You shall not bear false witness*[^23]: and in the Law it is written: *Every man is a liar.* Look back to that which I admonished a little before, when I defined the meek man in such words as I could. He is meek, to whom in all the things he does well, nothing pleases except God; in all the things he suffers ill, God does not displease him. This, then, answer him who says: Lie, because it is written: *Every man is a liar:* I do not lie, because it is written: *The mouth that lies kills the soul.* I do not lie, because it is written: *You will destroy all who speak a lie*[^24]. I do not lie, because it is written: *You shall not bear false witness.* Let him press with afflictions my flesh, to whom I am displeasing in the truth: I hear my Lord: *Do not fear those who kill the body*[^25].

**6.** How, then, is every man a liar? Or perhaps you are not a man? Answer quickly, and truly, Let me not be a man, lest I be a liar. For see: *God looked down from heaven upon the sons of men, to see if there is one understanding and seeking God. All have turned aside, together they have become useless: there is none who does good, there is not even one*[^26]. Why? Because they wished to be sons of men. But that he might take them from these iniquities, redeem, cure, heal, change the sons of men, he gave them the power to become sons of God[^27]. What wonder, then? You were men, if you were sons of men: you were all men, you were liars; for every man is a liar. The grace of God approaches you, it gave you the power to become sons of God. Hear the voice of my Father saying: *I said: You are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High*[^28]. Since men, the sons of men, if not the sons of the Most High, are liars; because every man is a liar. If sons of God, if redeemed by the grace of the Savior, if bought by precious blood, if reborn of water and the Spirit, if predestined to the inheritance of heaven, surely sons of God. Therefore now gods. What does a lie do to you? For Adam is a mere man, Christ a man-God, the creator of every creature, God. Adam a man, Christ a man, the mediator of God, the only Son of the Father, God-man. Behold, you a man far from God, and God on high far from man: in the middle God set himself a man. Acknowledge Christ, and through the man ascend to God.

**7.** Now, then, corrected, and if we have done anything, meek, let us hold an unswerving confession[^29]. Let us love the law of God, that we may escape what was said: *Woe to the world from scandals*[^30]. Let us speak something about the scandals, of which the world is full, and how the scandals multiply, the afflictions abound. The world is laid waste, the press is trodden. Come, Christian, heavenly offshoot, pilgrims on earth, who seek a city in heaven, who desire to be associated with the holy angels, understand that you have so come as to depart. You pass through the world, striving toward him who created the world. Let not the lovers of the world disturb you, who wish to remain in the world, and, whether they will or not, are forced to migrate: let them not deceive you, not seduce you. These afflictions are not scandals. Be just, and they will be exercises. Tribulation comes: it will be what you wish, either an exercise, or a condemnation. Such as it finds you, such it will be. Tribulation is fire: does it find you gold? it takes away the dross: does it find you chaff? it turns you into ash. So the afflictions which abound are not scandals. But what are scandals? Those sayings, those words, by which it is said to us: Behold what the Christian times do, behold what scandals there are. For to this end it is said to you, that you, if you love the world, may blaspheme Christ. And this your friend says to you, your counselor: therefore your eye. This your minister says to you, your fellow worker: therefore your hand. This perhaps he says to you who sustains you, who lifts you from earthly lowliness: therefore your foot. *Cast it away, cut it off, throw it away from you*, do not consent. Answer such ones, as that man answered, to whom false testimony was being persuaded. Answer, you too: say to the man saying to you, Behold, in Christian times there are such great afflictions, the world is laid waste: answer you, This Christ foretold to me before it happened.

**8.** For why are you disturbed? Your heart is disturbed by the afflictions of the world, like that boat, where Christ was sleeping[^31]. Behold what the cause is, O man of heart, that your heart is disturbed: behold what the cause is. That boat, in which Christ sleeps, is the heart where faith sleeps. For what new thing is said to you, Christian? what new thing is said to you? In Christian times the world is laid waste, the world fails. Did your Lord not tell you, The world will be laid waste? Did your Lord not tell you, The world will fail? Why did you believe when it was promised, and are disturbed when it is fulfilled? So the tempest rages against your heart: beware shipwreck, rouse Christ. *That Christ dwell*, says the Apostle, *through faith in your hearts*[^32]. Through faith Christ dwells in you. Faith present, Christ is present: faith awake, Christ is awake: faith forgotten, Christ is asleep. Be roused, be moved, say: *Lord, we perish!* Behold what the pagans say to us: what, what is more grievous, the bad Christians say to us. Arise, *Lord, we perish.* Let your faith wake, Christ begins to speak to you. Why are you disturbed? All these things I foretold to you. Therefore I foretold them, that when the evils came, you might hope for good things, that you might not faint in evils. Do you wonder that the world fails? wonder rather that the world has grown old. A man is born, grows, grows old. There are many complaints in old age: cough, phlegm, blearedness, anxiety, weariness are present. So a man has grown old; he is full of complaints: the world has grown old; it is full of afflictions. Has God done little for you, that in the old age of the world he sent you Christ, that he might refresh you then, when all things fail? Do you not know that he signified this in the seed of Abraham? For the seed of Abraham, the Apostle says, *which is Christ. He does not say: And to seeds, as in many; but as in one, And to your seed, which is Christ*[^33]. Therefore to the old Abraham a son was born, because Christ was surely going to come in the old age of the world itself. He came when all things were growing old, and made you new. A thing made, a thing founded, a thing about to perish was now declining toward its setting. It was necessary that it abound with labors: he came both to console you amid the labors, and to promise you rest forever. Do not wish to cling to the old world, and to be unwilling to grow young in Christ, who says to you: The world perishes, the world grows old, the world fails, it labors with the panting of old age. Do not fear, *your youth will be renewed like the eagle's*[^34].

**9.** Behold, he says, in Christian times Rome perishes. Perhaps Rome does not perish: perhaps it is scourged, not slain: perhaps it is chastised, not destroyed. Perhaps Rome does not perish, if the Romans do not perish. For they will not perish, if they praise God: they will perish, if they blaspheme. For what is Rome, except the Romans? For it is not about stones and timbers, about lofty buildings and most ample walls. This was so made, that it might at some time fall. When a man built it, he set stone upon stone; and when a man destroyed it, he drove out stone from stone. A man made it, a man destroyed it. Is an injury done to Rome, because it is said, It falls? Not to Rome, but perhaps to its builder. Do we do an injury to its founder, because we say, Rome falls, which Romulus founded? The world is going to fall, which God founded. But neither does what a man made fall, except when God wills; nor does what God made fall, except when God wills. For if the work of a man does not fall without the will of God, when can the work of God fall by the will of a man? Yet God both made for you a world about to fall; and therefore he founded you about to die. Man himself, the ornament of the city, man himself the inhabitant, ruler, governor of the city, so comes that he go, so is born that he die, so enters that he pass on. *Heaven and earth will pass away*[^35]: what wonder, then, if at some time there is an end to a city? And perhaps not now is there an end to the city: yet at some time there will be an end to the city. But why amid the sacrifices of the Christians does Rome perish? Why amid the sacrifices of the pagans did its mother Troy burn? The gods, in whom the Romans placed their hope, the wholly Roman gods, in whom the pagan Romans placed their hope, migrated from burning Troy to found Rome. The Roman gods themselves were first the Trojan gods. Troy burned, Aeneas carried off the fugitive gods: rather, fleeing, he carried off the stolid gods. For they could be carried by one fleeing: they themselves could not flee. And coming into Italy with those gods, with false gods he founded Rome. It is long to pursue the rest: yet let me briefly mention what their own writings have. Their author, known to all, speaks thus: *The city of Rome, as I have received it, the Trojans founded and held at the beginning, who, with Aeneas as their leader, fugitives, were wandering with no fixed seats*[^36]. So they had their gods with them, they founded Rome in Latium, they set there to be worshiped the gods who were worshiped in Troy. By their own poet Juno is brought in, angry at Aeneas and the fleeing Trojans, and says:  
*A people hostile to me sails the Tyrrhenian sea,  
carrying Troy into Italy and its conquered household gods*[^37];  
that is, carrying their conquered gods with them into Italy. When conquered gods were being carried into Italy, was that divine power or a bad omen? Love God's law, then, and let there be no stumbling block for you. We ask you, beseech you, exhort you: be gentle, have compassion on those who suffer, support the weak[^38]. And on this occasion, with so many travelers, needy people, and sufferers, let your hospitality abound; let your good works abound. Let Christians do what Christ commands, and let the pagans blaspheme only to their own harm.
