# Sermon 113b

**1.** If the holy reading terrifies us healthfully in this life, after this life no one terrifies us; for the fruit of fear is correction: for I did not say only, if the divine reading terrifies us, but if it healthfully terrifies us; for many know how to fear, but do not know how to be changed. But what is more barren than unfruitful fear? For how the hearts of us all trembled and shook, when we heard that that proud rich man, the despiser of the poor man lying at his gate, was so tormented in hell that not even suppliant prayers could profit him anything; and that it was answered him, not cruelly but justly, that he could not be helped! For at that time, when the mercy of God would have helped him had he been converted, he neglected impunity, and deserved torment. He was spared when he was proud, and rejoiced in the boastings of his riches, not thinking of the torments to come, which in that pride he knew neither how to believe nor to fear. Yet at some time he came to them. And what is: at some time? For how great was the delay of his dignity and pride? How great is the delay of the flower of grass, as you have just now heard, when the letter of the apostle Peter was read, the testimony commended from prophecy: *All flesh is grass, and the brightness of man like the flower of grass: the grass withered, and the flower fell; but the Word of the Lord abides forever* [^1].

**2.** However much, then, this flesh is clothed in purple and fine linen, what is it more than flesh and blood, and grass that withers? And however much dignity and honor men offer this flesh, it is indeed a flower, but a flower of grass; for the flower of grass cannot remain while the grass withers, but, as the grass withers, so the flower falls. We have, then, that by which we may hold ourselves, that we may not fall, because *the Word of the Lord abides forever*. Did the Word of God despise us, brothers? did he look down on this fragility and mortality of ours, and say: It is flesh, it is grass; let the grass wither, and the flower of grass fall, let it not be helped? Rather, he took on our grass, that he might make us gold: for the Word of the Lord, which abides forever, did not disdain to be for a time grass, not that the Word itself should be changed, but that it might bestow a better change on the grass. For *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us* [^2]; and the Lord suffered for us and was buried, and rose again, and ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father, now not grass, but incorrupt and incorruptible gold. A change, then, is promised us, dearly beloved brothers; yet until we come to that change, this grass must pass, that is, all the dignity of the flesh passes away with the age, all this fragility grows old. In that rich man the grass had passed, the flower of grass had passed too; but if in the time of his grass, and in the time of the flower of his grass, he had understood the Word of the Lord which abides forever, and, all the cushions of pride laid down and leveled, had spread himself out before God, and, if he refused to throw away his riches, yet had given something of them to those very poor lying there, he would have been refreshed after the time of this grass; he would not ask mercy without cause, who, when he could, did not show mercy.

**3.** So, my brothers, when it was read, and we heard the voice in the Gospel: *Father Abraham, send Lazarus, let him dip his finger in water, and drip it on my tongue, for I am tormented in this flame* [^3], how we were all struck in heart, lest something of the sort befall us after this life, and we entreat in vain! For there is no place of correction, when this life has passed. This life is like a stadium; either we conquer here, or we are conquered. The one conquered in the stadium, does he seek to wrestle outside the stadium, that he may recover the crown he lost? What then? if we have feared, if we have shuddered, if our inner parts have trembled, let us change ourselves, while there is time; that is the fruitful fear. For no one, brothers, can be changed without fear, without tribulation, without trembling. We beat our breasts, when the conscience of sins pricks us; what we beat is something within, some evil conception; let it break out into confession, and there will perhaps be nothing to prick; let all sins break out into confession. For that rich man too, swollen in fine linen, had within what, would that it had broken out while he lived; perhaps the perpetual flame would not have been applied to him; but because then he was proud, that moisture had made a swelling, not an eruption. But the poor man Lazarus *lay at the gate full of sores* [^4]. Let no one, then, be ashamed, brothers, to confess sins; for to lie low pertains to humility. Yet see how they exchange turns. When the tribulation of confessions has passed, the refreshment of merits comes; for the angels will come, will take up this sore-covered man, and will set him in Abraham's bosom, that is, in eternal rest, in the secret place of the great Father; for the bosom signifies a secret place, where the wearied one may rest.

**4.** So this man lay at the gate full of sores, but the rich man despised him; he desired to be filled with the crumbs that fell from his table, the dogs themselves fed his sores, and by the rich man he was not fed. Attend, brothers, because the poor man is in need: *Blessed*, he says, *is he who understands concerning the needy and the poor* [^5]; attend, and do not despise him as a sore-covered man lying at the gate. Give to the poor, because he receives, who both wished to be in need on earth, and to enrich from heaven. For the Lord says: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink; I was a guest, and you received me*, and the rest. And they: *When did we see you hungry, or thirsty, or naked, or a guest?* And he: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me* [^6]. In his least ones laboring on earth he mercifully wished, in a way, his own person to be, helping from heaven all who labor. To Christ, then, you give, when you give to the needy; or do you fear, lest either such a keeper lose anything, or such a rich man not repay? God is almighty, Christ is almighty; you can lose nothing: entrust it to him, and you lose nothing. When do you entrust it? when you give to the poor. Such riches do not pass away, when the flesh passes like grass, and the brightness of man like the flower of grass. And so, brothers, if we have been terrified, lest after this life we suffer such penalties and torments of burning flame, as the proud and unmerciful rich man endured, now, while there is time, let us be corrected; for then there is no place of helping, because there is no place of correcting; for then each one is helped, when he is corrected. This life is for correction, this life is for help and aid. Turned toward the Lord.  
[Here ends the treatise on the rich man and the poor man Lazarus.]
