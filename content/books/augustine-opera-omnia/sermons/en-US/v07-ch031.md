# Sermon 256: In the Days of Easter, on the Alleluia

**1.** Since it has pleased the Lord our God that, established here by bodily presence, we should sing *Alleluia* to him together with your Charity, which is interpreted in Latin: "Praise the Lord"; let us praise the Lord, brothers, with life and tongue, with heart and mouth, with voices and conduct. For God wishes *Alleluia* to be said to him in such a way that there be no discord in the one praising. Let the tongue, then, first agree in us ourselves with the life, the mouth with the conscience. Let the voices, I say, agree with the conduct, lest perhaps good voices bear witness against bad conduct. O happy *Alleluia* in heaven, where the angels are the temple of God! For there is the highest concord of those praising, where there is secure exultation of those singing: where no law in the members fights against the law of the mind; where there is no brawl of desire, in which the victory of love is endangered. Here, then, let us sing *Alleluia* still anxious, that there we may be able someday to sing it secure. Why anxious here? Do you not want me to be anxious, when I read: *Is not human life on earth a temptation?*[^1] Do you not want me to be anxious, when it is still said to me: *Watch and pray, that you enter not into temptation*[^2]? Do you not want me to be anxious, where temptation so abounds that prayer itself prescribes it to us, when we say: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^3]? Daily petitioners, daily debtors. Do you want me to be secure, where daily I ask pardon for sins, help for dangers? For when I have said, because of past sins: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*; at once, because of future dangers, I add and join: *Lead us not into temptation.* But how is the people in a good state, when it cries out with me: *Deliver us from evil*[^4]? And yet, brothers, in this still present evil let us sing: *Alleluia* to the good God, who delivers us from evil. Why do you look around to see from what he delivers you, when he delivers you from evil? Do not go far, do not stretch the gaze of your mind everywhere around. Return to yourself, look at yourself. You are still bad. When, then, God delivers you from yourself, then he delivers you from evil. Hear the Apostle; and there understand from what evil you are to be delivered. *I delight*, he says, *in the law of God according to the inner man; but I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind, and taking me captive in the law of sin, which is.* Where? *Taking me captive*, he says, *in the law of sin, which is in my members.* I thought it had taken you captive under some unknown barbarians; I thought it had taken you captive under some foreign nations, or under some men as masters. *Which is*, he says, *in my members.* Cry out, then, with him: *Wretched man that I am, who will deliver me?* From where will someone deliver? Say from where. One says, from the levy, another from prison, another from the captivity of barbarians, another from fever and sickness; say you, Apostle, not to where we are sent, or to where we are led; but what we carry with us, what we ourselves are, say it: *From the body of this death*[^5]. From the body of this death? *From the body*, he says, *of this death.*

**2.** Another says: The body of this death does not pertain to me; my prison is for a time, my chain is for a time: I am in the body of death; I am not the body of death. You argue, therefore you are not delivered. For I, he says, am a spirit; I am not flesh, but I am in the flesh; when I have been delivered from the flesh, what will I then have to do with the flesh? To this argument do you want, brothers, that I answer, or the Apostle? If I answer, perhaps the greatness of the word will be despised because of the cheapness of the minister. I keep silent rather. Hear with me the Teacher of the gentiles, hear with me the Vessel of election; that the controversy of dissension may be taken away from you. Hear, but say first what you were saying. Surely this you were saying: I am not flesh, but I am a spirit. In my prison I groan; when this chain and this workhouse has been dissolved, I depart free. Earth is rendered to earth, the spirit is received into heaven; I go, I leave behind what I am not. So you were saying this? This, he says. I do not answer you: answer, Apostle, answer, I beseech you. You preached, that you might be heard; you wrote, that you might be read; everything was done, that you might be believed. Say: *Who will deliver me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^6]. From what does he deliver you? *From the body of this death.* But are you not yourself the body of this death? He answers: *So then I myself with the mind serve the law of God, but with the flesh the law of sin*[^7]. But *I myself*; how through different things yourself? *With the mind*, he says, because I love; *with the flesh*, because I desire; a victor indeed, if I do not consent; yet still a wrestler, with the adversary pressing. And how, when you have been delivered, O Apostle, from this flesh, will you then be nothing but a spirit? The Apostle answers, death now threatening, the debt that no one escapes: I do not lay down the flesh forever, but for a time set it aside. So you are going to return to the body of this death? And what? Let us rather hear his own words. How do you return to the body, from which with so devout a voice you cried out that you were to be delivered? He answers: I return indeed to the body, but no longer of this death. Hear, unskilled one, deaf against the daily voices of the readings; hear, how he returns indeed to the body, but not of this death. Not because he takes another body, but because *this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality.* My brothers, when the Apostle said *this corruptible, this mortal*, in a way he was touching the flesh with his own voice. Therefore not another. I do not, he says, lay down the earthly body, and receive an airy body, or receive an ethereal body. The very one I receive, but no longer of this death. Because *this corruptible*, not another, but *this must put on incorruption; and this mortal*, not another, but *this must put on immortality. Then will come to pass the saying that is written: Death is swallowed up in victory.* Let *Alleluia* be sung. *Then will come to pass the saying that is written*, the saying now not of those fighting, but of those triumphing: *Death is swallowed up in victory.* Let *Alleluia* be sung. *Where, O death, is your sting?* Let *Alleluia* be sung. *But the sting of death is sin*[^8]. But *you will seek its place and will not find it*[^9].

**3.** But even here, amid dangers, amid temptations, both by others and by us let *Alleluia* be sung. *For God is faithful, who will not allow*, he says, you *to be tempted beyond what you are able.* Therefore here too let us sing *Alleluia.* Man is still guilty, but God is faithful. He did not say: He will not allow you to be tempted, but: *He will not allow you to be tempted beyond what you are able; but will with the temptation also make a way out, that you may be able to endure*[^10]. You have entered into temptation: but God will also make a way out, lest you perish in the temptation; so that, like the potter's vessel, you may be formed by preaching, baked by tribulation. But when you enter, think of the way out; because God is faithful, *the Lord will guard your going in, and your going out*[^11]. Moreover, when this body has been made immortal and incorruptible, when the whole temptation has perished; because *the body indeed is dead;* why *is it dead? Because of sin. But the spirit is life*, they are the Apostle's words; why? *Because of righteousness.* Do we then send away the dead body? No, but hear: *But if the Spirit of him who raised Christ from the dead dwells in you; he who raised Christ from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies*[^12]. For now the body is animate, then spiritual. *For the first man was made into a living soul, the last man into a life-giving spirit*[^13]. Therefore *he will give life to your mortal bodies also because of his Spirit dwelling in you.* O happy *Alleluia* there! O secure! O without an adversary! Where no one will be an enemy, no one perishes as a friend. There praises to God, and here praises to God; but here from the anxious, there from the secure; here from those about to die, there from those always to live; here in hope, there in reality; here on the road, there in the homeland. Now then, my brothers, let us sing, not for the delight of rest, but for the solace of labor. As travelers are wont to sing; sing, but walk; console your labor by singing, do not love laziness; sing and walk. What is "walk"? Make progress, make progress in good. For there are, according to the Apostle, some making progress for the worse. If you make progress, you walk, but make progress in good, make progress in right faith, make progress in good conduct: sing and walk. Do not stray, do not turn back, do not remain behind. Turning to the Lord.  
*And after the Sermon*:  
Tomorrow the festival of the holy martyrs Marian and James dawns; but, since because of so great a gathering of the holy council we are still somewhat occupied, on the third day after that birthday of theirs, with the Lord's help, we will render you the sermon owed.
