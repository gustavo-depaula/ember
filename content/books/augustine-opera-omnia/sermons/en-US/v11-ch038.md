# (Erfurt 5 = Sermon 362/A) (Erfurt 5)

**1.** If faith is present, the consolations of Christians are not empty. But that faith may be present, the examples of the saints have gone before and the testimonies of the books cry out. For why should we not believe the things narrated to us as done in past times, when we see being done the things that were foretold as to come? For those who foretold to us the future things which we now behold are the same who narrated to us the past. These things which we now see on the earth—that all men forsake the demons they were serving, that the images they were worshiping are broken, that everywhere the temples are overthrown and the whole human race is turned to one name—were foretold in the holy books, and when they were being foretold they seemed quite incredible. But because we see them fulfilled, they have become cheap, because they are ready to hand. So then let us set before our eyes the lands and all the nations as they were before, where there was no Christian at all, where sacrifice was offered to demons, stone altars were set up, sacrifices were immolated to stones, priests were appointed, and whatever is owed to the true God was rendered to false gods, that is, to demons. Let us set before our eyes such lands, and suppose that someone—I know not who—had suddenly arisen who said that all those things could quickly be changed, and that men, leaving those vain things behind, would be turned to the one God: who would not have laughed at him? Who would not have thought him raving? Who would have deigned to hear him? Who would have hesitated to drive him away? And yet these things have come to pass. But where there were written these things which we see and which, when they were spoken, were not seen, there too were written the things which are not yet seen. What are the things which are not yet seen? That the Lord himself will come in the body in which he deigned to appear here and to die and to rise again—but now immortal and incorruptible, as he remains in the heavens and sits at the right hand of the Father; that there will be a judgment; that all the dead will rise from their tombs; that all bodies will return from every consuming, and not only the monuments but the world itself will give back from its elements what it received. When these things have come to pass, they likewise will not be wonders, just as those things are not wonders which have already come to pass. But now they are not believed, because they are not yet shown. Here faith is required. Believe what you do not yet see: what great thing will you do if you are going to believe only when you see? A reward is given to belief because it believed what it did not yet see, so that it may rejoice over it when it sees; and again, punishment is repaid to unbelief, which refused to believe what it did not yet see, so that it may wail when it sees.

**2.** But this alone is wont, as it were, to disturb men: how consumed bodies will rise again—bodies which are buried for the reason that they offend the eyes when they are consumed; because they were dear to us together with the souls they had, but, the souls having departed from there, the things we loved lie there, and we do not want them to be corrupted before our sight, and so we bury them. When, therefore, it is said that they are to rise again, you take note, human heart, of what is now done, and you do not believe what is to be? For if you weigh it carefully and judge rightly, you give too little consideration to the hidden and daily miracles of nature. For whence do there come forth in the standing crops things that are not buried in the seed? Whence do the leaves grow green again on the trees? Whence are the woods, bare in winter, clothed in summer? For this reason all these things are restored, that we may believe that bodies too can in their own time be restored. It is more incredible that what did not exist should be established than that what existed should be restored. The man who did not exist was established: and you do not believe that what existed can be restored? These things that are buried are both flesh and bones: but before you were born, what were you? You were brought forth from the hidden places of nature, that in this form you might appear, visible to the eyes: and do you not believe that, when you have departed into the hidden places of nature, he can bring you forth again from there, who was able to create you before you existed?

**3.** Finally, let us believe this because he said it who cannot deceive. He who promised us the resurrection of bodies, when he was here in the body, raised the dead. He who promised us the resurrection of bodies, when he was here in the body, himself rose on the third day. Let us not believe even these things to have been done, if there did not follow upon them the things that were foretold as to follow. When the Lord appeared after the resurrection to his disciples, because they too did not believe that he had risen, *they thought they were seeing a spirit. He said to them: Why are you troubled, and why do thoughts rise up in your hearts? See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself. Touch and see, for a spirit does not have flesh and bones as you see me to have*[^1]. He offered himself not only to be seen by their eyes but also to be handled by their hands. *He spent forty days with them, going in and out, eating and drinking*[^2], but by power, not by necessity. He convinced them of the grace of the resurrection by the evidence of a true body, and before their eyes ascended into heaven. But when they were seeing him after the resurrection, *he said to them: These are the words that I spoke to you while I was still with you, that everything must be fulfilled which is written in the law and the prophets and the psalms concerning me. Then he opened their understanding*—this the Gospel says—*that they might understand the Scriptures, and he said to them that it is thus written and thus Christ had to suffer and to rise from the dead on the third day, and that repentance and forgiveness of sins be preached in his name to all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^3]. This the Gospel has. See when it was written down. For it was being written then, when what was foretold there did not yet exist. For Christ was passing by, and his disciples were looking at him in the body in which they had known him. Surely then, when they were looking at him in that body in which they had known him, did that which follows exist? And what? *And that repentance and forgiveness of sins be preached in his name to all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^4]. That existed, this did not exist. The disciples saw that which existed, they believed that which did not yet exist. We already see it fulfilled: in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ repentance and forgiveness of sins is preached to all the nations. Is it not being done? Did it already exist when those things were being shown and when they were being written? As, then, the apostles saw Christ and believed the future Church, so we see the Church; let us believe concerning Christ. For of these two things they saw the one, they believed the other. But what they saw, we do not see. If we see what they believed, let us believe what they saw.

**4.** This our Lord Jesus Christ, then, the word of the Father, the only-begotten Son, through whom all things were made, who at the opportune time deigned to take flesh from the Virgin Mary and to appear to human eyes, to do worthy things, to suffer unworthy things, to work divine things, to bear human things, to die, to rise again, to ascend into the heavens—this our Lord God, then, hear what he has said concerning the future resurrection, and, setting disputations aside, believe, for he who said it cannot deceive: *The hour will come,* he says, *when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and will come forth, those who have done good things into the resurrection of life, but those who have done evil things into the resurrection of judgment*[^5]. Thanks be to God, therefore, because they will rise even to judgment who have done evil things. All things, brethren, have been forgiven us in baptism. Believe, most dear brethren, that he whom he willed in a moment of time to free from all sins, he grants to reign with him without end.

[^1]: Luke 24:37-39
[^2]: See Acts 1:3-4
[^3]: Luke 24:44-47
[^4]: See Luke 24:47
[^5]: John 5:28-29
