# Sermon 364

**1.** In this reading, which was recited to us, dearest brothers, many and too obscure divine mysteries are contained; which, because they cannot be briefly explained, for this sole reason we wished the morning psalms to be finished earlier, lest a longer sermon weary you. And because at the customary hour you are going to leave the church, hear the things which must be said, according to your custom, with silence and quiet, with attentive mind.

**2.** But Samson, dearest brothers, what strength he had, was from the grace of God, not from nature: for if he were strong by nature, when his hair was removed, his strength would not be taken away. And where was that most mighty strength, except in that which Scripture says: *The Spirit of the Lord walked with him*[^1]? To the Spirit of the Lord, then, that strength belonged. In Samson there was a vessel, in the Spirit was the fullness. A vessel can be filled and emptied. But every vessel has its filling from elsewhere; therefore in Paul that grace was commended, when he was called a vessel of election[^2]. Let us see, then, what parable Samson set forth to the Philistines. *From the eater*, he says, *came food, and from the strong went forth sweetness*[^3]. This parable was betrayed, carried to the friends, solved: Samson was conquered. Here if he is just, it lies deeply hidden, and in the depth is this man's justice: for both that he is read to have been conquered by womanly coaxings, and that he went in to a harlot, his merit seems to waver to those who understand less the secrets of truth. For a prophet too is commanded by the precept of the Lord to take a harlot as wife[^4]. Perhaps we will be able to say that these things in the Old Testament were not criminal or damnable, since indeed this, which he either said or did, was prophecy. Let us seek, then, what he signified by being conquered, what he signified by being conqueror, what he signified by yielding to womanly coaxings, what he signified by betraying the secret of the parable, what he signified by going in to a harlot, what he signified by catching foxes, and by the tails of the foxes, to which he tied fire, burning the crops of the enemies. Which crops indeed he could have burned by a shortcut, if he had not pondered a mystery in the foxes. Could the dry stubble not burn, unless the foxes drew fire through it[^5]? Let us understand, then, that great mysteries lie hidden here.

**3.** What was Samson? If I say he signified Christ, I seem to myself to say something true; but at once it occurs to those who think: Is Christ too conquered by womanly coaxings? and how is it understood that Christ could go in to a harlot? then, is Christ too, when his head is bared, shaved of hair, despoiled of strength, bound, blinded, mocked? Wake up, faith, attend to what Christ is: not only what he did, but also what Christ suffered. What did he do? He worked as the strong one, he suffered as the weak one. In one I understand both: I see the strength of the Son of God, I see the weakness of the Son of man. Another thing is added, that the whole Christ, as Scripture commends him, is both head and body: as Christ is the head of the Church, so the body of Christ is the Church; and that it may not be alone, with its head the whole Christ is. The Church, then, has in herself the strong, has the weak; has those who feed on solid bread, has those who must still be nourished with milk[^6]. I add another thing, which it is necessary to confess: in that very fellowship of the sacraments, in the communication of Baptism, in the participation of the altar, she has the just, has the unjust. For now the body of Christ, which you know, is a threshing-floor, afterward it will be a granary; yet, while it is a threshing-floor, it does not refuse to bear the chaff: when the time for storing comes, it will separate the wheat from the chaff[^7]. Certain things, then, Samson did from the person of the head, certain from the person of the body, yet the whole from the person of Christ. For in that Samson worked virtues and wonders, he figured Christ the head of the Church: but in that he acted prudently, he bore the image of those who in the Church live justly: where perhaps he was caught off guard and acted incautiously, he figured those who in the Church are sinners. The harlot, whom Samson takes in marriage, is the Church, which before the knowledge of the one God fornicated with idols, whom Christ afterward joined to himself; but after she received faith from him, illumined, she also merited this, that through him she might know the sacraments of salvation, and that by him the mysteries of the heavenly secrets might be revealed to her. For the question itself which holds: *From the eater came food, and from the strong went forth sweetness*[^8], what else does it signify, than Christ rising from the dead? For surely from the eater, that is, from death, which devours and consumes all things, came forth that food, which said: *I am the living bread, which came down from heaven*[^9]. Whom human iniquity exasperated, and to whom it offered the bitterness of vinegar and gall, from him the people of the nations, converted, received the sweetness of life; and so from the mouth of the dead lion, that is, from the death of Christ, who reclining slept like a lion, there came forth a swarm of bees, that is, of Christians. But that he says: *You would not have found my parable, unless you had plowed with my heifer*[^10], this heifer is the Church, which, with the secrets of faith revealed to her by her husband, spread abroad to the ends of the earth, by the teaching and preaching of the apostles and saints, the mysteries of the Trinity, of the resurrection, of the judgment and likewise of the kingdom, and promised the rewards of eternal life to those who understand and know.

**4.** But it follows: *Samson was angry, because his companion took his wife*[^11]. This companion bore the figure of all heretics. A great secret, my brothers. For the heretics, who divided the Church, wished to take and lead away the wife of their Lord; for from the Church and from the Gospels went out those who by the adultery of impiety try to invade the Church, that is, the body of Christ, into their own party. Whence that faithful servant and friend of the Lord's bride speaks[^12]: *For I betrothed you to one husband, to present a chaste virgin to Christ.* And with zeal of faith and rebuke he touches the person of the corrupted companion: *And I fear*, he says, *lest, as the serpent seduced Eve, so also your senses be corrupted away from the truth which is in Christ Jesus*[^13]. But who are the companions, that is, the deserting heretics, who wished to invade the Lord's bride, except Donatus, Arius, Manichaeus, and the other vessels of error and perdition? Of such the Apostle says: *I hear that there are divisions among you*, he says. *For one says: I indeed am of Paul; but another: I of Apollos; but another: I of Cephas*[^14]. Let us see, then, what this mystical Samson did, offended in his wife by the stranger. For he caught foxes, that is, the adulterating companions, of whom it is said in the Song of Songs: *Catch us the little foxes, that destroy the vineyards*[^15]. What is, catch? that is, seize, convict, refute, lest the ecclesiastical vineyards be destroyed. What else is it to catch foxes, except to convict heretics by the authority of the divine law, and to bind and constrain them by the testimonies of the holy Scriptures as by certain chains? He catches foxes, he sets fire to their tied tails. What do the tied tails of the foxes mean? What are the tails of the foxes, except the hinder parts of the heretics (who have alluring and deceptive first parts) tied, that is, condemned and drawing fire at the end; that they may consume the fruits and works of those who acquiesce in their seductions? He says to a man now: Do not hear the heretics, do not acquiesce to the heretics, do not be seduced by the heretics. He answers: Why? did not this man and that man hear the heretics? did not some other Christian commit such great evils, do such great adulteries, exercise such great robberies? and what evil befell him? The first parts of the foxes are those which the seduced attend to; and behind is the fire. Nothing, he says, has befallen him now. Because nothing precedes, does it draw nothing behind it? He will come to the fire that comes after. But do you think that, because the heretics draw fire, by which the fruits of the enemies may burn, they themselves will not burn? For without doubt the foxes, where they set fire to the harvests, themselves surely burned too. The judgment will come, then, to the heretics from behind: which now they do not see, they have behind them; with coaxings they soothe, they show their first parts free: in the judgment of God they are tied by the tails, that is, in their hinder parts they draw fire, because wickedness goes before its own punishments.

**5.** But that he went in to a harlot, if he did it without cause, whoever did it, he is unclean; but if a prophet did it, it is a mystery. If he did not go in so as to lie with her, perhaps he went in for the sake of a mystery. But we do not read that he lay with her. It follows: *But the enemies were waiting for him at the gates of the city, that they might seize him, when he had come out from the harlot to whom he had gone in; but he was sleeping*. See how it is not written that he was joined to the harlot, but it is written that he was sleeping. *When he arose*, it says, *at midnight he went out, and took away the gates of the city itself with the bars, and lifted those same gates onto the top of the mountain*[^16], and he could not be held by the Philistines. The gates of the city, by which he went in to the harlot, he took away, and lifted onto the mountain. What is this? Hell and the love of a woman, Scripture joins both[^17]: the house of the harlot held the image of hell. Rightly is it put for hell: because it repels no one, it draws to itself everyone who enters. We recognize in this place the works of our Redeemer: after the synagogue, to which he had come, separated itself from him through the devil, after it trod him down, that is, crucified him in the place of Calvary, he descended to hell; and the enemies guarded the place of the sleeper, that is, the tomb, and wished to seize him whom they could not see. *But he was sleeping*: there for this reason it said this, because it was not true death. What was said, *At midnight he arose*, this signified that he arose in secret: for he openly suffered, but only to the disciples and certain sure ones, when he had risen, he was manifested[^18]. Therefore, that he went in, all saw; that he arose, few knew, held, and handled. Yet he takes away the gates of the city, that is, takes away the gates of hell. What is it to take away the gates of hell, except to remove the empire of death? For it received, and did not let go. But what did the Lord Jesus Christ do, with the gates of death taken away? He ascended to the top of the mountain: for we know that he both rose again, and ascended into heaven.

**6.** But what is it, that he had strength in his hair? And this too, brothers, attend to diligently: he had strength not in his hand, not in his foot, not in his breast, not in his head itself, but in his hair, in his locks. What is hair? what is the lock? We see it, and the Apostle, when asked, answers us: *Hair is a covering*[^19]; and in the covering Christ had strength, when the shadows of the old law covered him. The hair of Samson, then, was in a covering: because in Christ one thing was seen, and another understood. But what does it mean, that the secret was betrayed, and Samson shaved? The law was despised, and Christ suffered: for they would not kill Christ, if they had not despised the law; for they themselves knew that it was not lawful for them to kill Christ. They said to the judge: *It is not lawful for us to kill anyone*[^20]. Samson was shaved, the dense things were revealed[^21], the covering was removed; and Christ, who lay hidden, appeared. But the hair, growing again, clothed the head again, because the Jews refused to recognize Christ even rising again. Indeed he was at the mill, was blinded, was in the prison house: the prison house, or the mill, is the labor of this age. But the blinding of Samson indicates those who, blinded by unbelief, recognized Christ neither working virtues nor ascending to the heavenly places; the blindness, then, which they brought on signified the blindness of the Jews. Christ was both seized and slain by the Jews; but rather he slew those slaying him. *The enemies, then, brought him to mock him.* Now here attend to the image of the cross: for he stretched out his outspread hands to the two columns as to two woods of the cross; but slain he crushed his adversaries, and his passion became the slaying of his persecutors. And therefore Scripture concludes thus: *Dying he slew more than he had slain living*[^22]. Which mystery was evidently fulfilled in our Lord Jesus Christ: for our redemption, which living he had by no means celebrated, dying he consummated. Who lives and reigns unto ages of ages. Amen.
