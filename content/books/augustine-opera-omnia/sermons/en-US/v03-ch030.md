# Sermon 71

On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 12:32: "Whoever Says a Word Against the Holy Spirit, It Will Not Be Forgiven Him, Neither in This Age nor in the Age to Come," or on Blasphemy Against the Holy Spirit

**1. 1.** A great question is set before us from the recent Gospel reading; for whose resolving, as far as concerns us, we are unequal: but our sufficiency is from God[^1], as far as we can either receive or grasp his help. So first notice the magnitude of the question: that, when you see its mass laid on our shoulders, you may pray for our labors, and in the help that is furnished us may find edification for your minds. When there was offered to the Lord a blind and mute man having a demon, and he had healed him, so that he spoke and saw, and all the crowds were amazed, saying: *Is not this the son of David?* the Pharisees, hearing, said: *This man does not cast out demons, except by Beelzebub the prince of demons. But Jesus, knowing their thoughts, said to them: Every kingdom divided against itself will be brought to desolation, and every city or house divided against itself will not stand; and if Satan casts out Satan, he is divided against himself: how then will his kingdom stand?*[^2] Saying this, he wished to be understood, from their own confession, that by not believing in him they had chosen to be in the kingdom of the devil, which surely, divided against itself, could not stand. So let the Pharisees choose what they wish. If Satan cannot cast out Satan, they could find nothing to say against the Lord; but if he can, much more let them look out for themselves, and withdraw from his kingdom, which, divided against itself, cannot stand.

**1. 2.** But by what the Lord Christ casts out demons, lest they think him the prince of demons, let them attend to what follows: *And if I*, he says, *cast out demons by Beelzebub, by whom do your sons cast them out? Therefore they will be your judges*[^3]. He said this surely of his disciples, the sons of that people, who certainly, the disciples of the Lord Jesus Christ, were well aware in their own conscience that they had learned no evil arts from the good Master, so as to cast out demons by the prince of demons. *Therefore*, he says, *they will be your judges.* "They, he says, the lowly and contemptible things of this world[^4], in whom not artful malignity but holy simplicity of my power appears, they, my witnesses, will be your judges." Then he adds: *But if I cast out demons by the Spirit of God, then the kingdom of God has come upon you*[^5]. What is this? "*If I*, he says, *cast out demons by the Spirit of God*, your sons too could not otherwise cast them out, to whom I gave not a malignant doctrine but simple faith. Without doubt the kingdom of God has come upon you, by which the kingdom of the devil is subverted; with which you too are subverted, if you are not changed."

**1. 3.** And since he had said: *By whom do your sons cast them out?*, that he might show his grace in them, not their merit: *Or how can anyone*, he says, *enter the house of the strong man and plunder his vessels, unless he first binds the strong man, and so plunders his house?*[^6] "Your sons, he says, who either have believed in me, or are still going to believe, and to cast out demons not by the prince of demons but in simple holiness; who surely either were, or are this which you too are, that is, sinners and impious, and therefore in the house of the devil and vessels of the devil; how could they be rescued from him, whom, by prevailing iniquity, he strongly held, unless he were bound by the bonds of my justice, and I rescued his vessels which had been vessels of wrath, and made them my vessels of mercy[^7]?" This is what the blessed Apostle too says, rebuking the proud and those boasting, as it were, of their own merits: *For who distinguishes you?*[^8] This is: "Who distinguishes you from the mass of perdition from Adam and from the vessels of wrath?" And lest anyone say "my own justice," *For what do you have*, he says, *that you have not received?*[^9] Whence too he says of himself: *For we too were once by nature children of wrath, like the rest*[^10]. So he too was a vessel in the house of that evil strong man, when he was a persecutor of the Church, a blasphemer, insolent, acting in malice and envy, as he confesses[^11]; but he who bound the strong man rescued from him a vessel of perdition and made it a vessel of election[^12].

**2. 4.** Then, lest the unbelieving and impious, opposing the Christian name on account of the diverse heresies and schisms of those who, under the Christian name, gather flocks of the lost, should think that the kingdom of Christ too is divided against itself, he consequently adds: *He who is not with me is against me; and he who does not gather with me scatters*[^13]. He did not say: "He who is not under the sound of my name," or: "under the appearance of my sacrament," but: *He who is not with me is against me.* Nor did he say: "He who does not gather under the sound of my name," but: *He who does not gather with me scatters.* So the kingdom of Christ is not divided against itself, even if men try to divide what was bought with the price of Christ's blood[^14]. *For the Lord knows who are his. And let everyone who names the name of the Lord*, he says, *depart from iniquity*[^15]. For if he does not depart from iniquity, he does not belong to the kingdom of Christ, even naming the name of Christ. So, that I may mention some things, for example: the spirit of avarice and the spirit of luxury, because the one contracts, the other pours out, are divided against each other; and both belong to the kingdom of the devil. Among the worshipers of idols, the spirit of Juno and the spirit of Hercules are divided against each other; and both belong to the kingdom of the devil. The pagan, the enemy of Christ, and the Jew, the enemy of Christ, are divided against each other; and both belong to the kingdom of the devil. The Arian and the Photinian, both heretics, and both divided against each other; the Donatist and the Maximianist, both heretics, and both divided against each other; all the vices and errors of mortals, contrary to one another, are divided against each other; and all belong to the kingdom of the devil. Therefore his kingdom will not stand. But the just man and the impious, the believer and the unbeliever, the Catholic and the heretic, are indeed divided against one another; but they do not both belong to Christ's kingdom. *The Lord knows those who are his*[^16]. Let no one flatter himself over a name. If he wants the Lord's name to benefit him, *let everyone who invokes the Lord's name depart from iniquity.*

**3. 5.** But these Gospel words, even if they had something of obscurity, which I think has been explained with the Lord's help, were not yet of such great difficulty as that which follows appears to be. *Therefore I say to you: Every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven men; but the blasphemy of the Spirit will not be forgiven. And whoever says a word against the Son of man, it will be forgiven him; but he who says it against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven him, neither in this age nor in the age to come*[^17]. What, then, will become of those whom the Church desires to win? Is a false hope held out, in the remission of all sins, to those corrected and coming to her from any error whatever? For who is not convicted of having said a word against the Holy Spirit, before he became a Christian or a Catholic? First, those who are called pagans, the worshipers of many false gods and adorers of idols, when they say that the Lord Christ did miracles by magic arts; are they not like those who said he cast out demons by the prince of demons? Then, when they daily blaspheme our sanctification, what else do they blaspheme but the Holy Spirit? What of the Jews, who said of the Lord that from which this discourse arose? Do they not even to this day speak a word against the Holy Spirit, so denying that he is in the Christians, as those denied that he was in Christ? For neither did those revile the Holy Spirit, either by saying he does not exist; or that he exists indeed, but is not God, but a creature; or that he avails nothing for casting out demons. They spoke nothing of these unworthy things, nothing of the sort about the Holy Spirit. For the Sadducees denied the Holy Spirit; but the Pharisees defended that he exists, against the heresy of the Sadducees[^18], but denied that he was in the Lord Jesus Christ, whom they thought to cast out demons by the prince of demons, while he cast them out by the Holy Spirit. And through this both the Jews and any heretics confess the Holy Spirit, but when they deny that he is in the body of Christ, which is his only Church, which is none other than the one Catholic Church; without doubt they are like the Pharisees, who then, even if they confessed the Holy Spirit to exist, yet denied that he was in Christ, whose works in casting out demons they attributed to the prince of demons. I pass over the fact that certain heretics contend that the Holy Spirit himself is altogether not a creator but a creature, as the Arians and Eunomians and Macedonians; or deny him so utterly that they deny that God himself is a Trinity, but assert that there is only God the Father, and that he himself is sometimes called the Son, sometimes called the Holy Spirit, as the Sabellians, whom some call "Patripassians," because they hold that the Father suffered; and since they deny that he has any Son, without doubt they deny that there is a Holy Spirit. The Photinians too, saying that the Father alone is God, but the Son is nothing but a man, deny altogether that there is a third Person, the Holy Spirit.

**3. 6.** It is manifest, therefore, that the Holy Spirit is blasphemed both by pagans and by Jews and by heretics. Are they then to be abandoned and reckoned without any hope, since the sentence is fixed: *He who says a word against the Holy Spirit, it is not forgiven him, neither in this age nor in the age to come*[^19]; and are those alone to be thought freed from the guilt of this most grave sin, who are Catholics from infancy? For whoever believed the word of God, that they might become Catholics, surely came into the grace and peace of Christ either from pagans, or from Jews, or from heretics; and if it has not been forgiven them that they spoke a word against the Holy Spirit, it is vainly promised and preached to men, that they may be converted to God and receive, whether in baptism or in the peace of the Church, the remission of sins. For it was not said: "It will not be forgiven him, except in baptism," but: *It will not be forgiven*, he says, *neither in this age nor in the age to come.*

**4. 7.** To some it seems that those alone sin against the Holy Spirit who, washed in the laver of regeneration in the Church and having received the Holy Spirit, then, as it were ungrateful for so great a gift of the Savior afterward, have immersed themselves in some deadly sin; such as adulteries, or murders, or that very departure either from the Christian name altogether or from the Catholic Church. But whence this view can be proved, I do not know, since both a place for penance of any crimes is not denied in the Church, and the Apostle says that the heretics themselves are surely to be corrected for this: *Lest perhaps God give them penance to know the truth, and they come to their senses from the snares of the devil, by whom they are held captive according to his will*[^20]. For what is the fruit of correction without any hope of remission? Finally, the Lord did not say: "He who, a faithful Catholic, says a word against the Holy Spirit," but: *He who says it*, that is: "anyone who says it, whoever says it," *it will not be forgiven him, neither in this age nor in the age to come.* Whether, then, he be a pagan, or a Jew, or a Christian, or a heretic from among the Jews or Christians, or have any other name of error, it was not said: "this one," or "that one," but: *He who says a word against the Holy Spirit*, that is: "blasphemes the Holy Spirit," *it will not be forgiven him, neither in this age nor in the age to come.* But further, if every error contrary to the truth, and an enemy to Catholic peace, as we showed above, says a word against the Holy Spirit, and yet the Church does not cease to correct and gather from every error those who may receive the remission of sins and that very Holy Spirit whom they had blasphemed, I think that we have shown the great secret of this so great a question. Let us seek, then, the light of the explanation from the Lord.

**5. 8.** So lift up, brothers, lift up to me your ears, to the Lord your minds. I say to your love: perhaps in all the holy Scriptures no greater question, none more difficult, is found. Whence—that I may confess to you something about myself—always in the sermons which I held to the people, I avoided the difficulty and trouble of this question: not because I had nothing that I might somehow think about it—for in so great a matter I would not neglect to ask, seek, knock[^21]—but because I did not think I could suffice, with words occurring on the spur of the moment, for the very understanding which was being opened to me somewhat. But today, hearing the readings about which a sermon was to be rendered to you, when the Gospel was read, my heart was so struck that I believed God wished you to hear something from this through my ministry.

**5. 9.** So first, that you may notice and understand, I admonish that the Lord did not say: "Every blasphemy of the Spirit will not be forgiven," nor say: "Whoever says any word at all against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven him," but: *He who says a word.* For if he had said that, nothing at all would remain to us whence we could dispute; since, if every blasphemy and every word that is said against the Holy Spirit will not be forgiven men, the Church would win no one from any kind of impiety of those who contradict the gift of Christ and the sanctification of the Church, whether of pagans, or of Jews, or of any heretics, or even of some untaught ones in the Catholic Church itself. But far be it that the Lord said this, far be it, I say, that the Truth said that every blasphemy or every word that was said against the Holy Spirit has no remission, neither in this age nor in the age to come.

**6. 10.** For he wished to exercise us by the difficulty of the question, not to deceive us by the falsity of the sentence. Wherefore it is not necessary that anyone think that every blasphemy or every word that is said against the Holy Spirit has no remission; but it is plainly necessary that there be some blasphemy and some word, which, if it is said against the Holy Spirit, never merits any pardon and remission. For if we take it as every one, who then could be saved? But if again we think it none, we contradict the Savior. There is, then, without doubt some blasphemy and some word, which, if it is said against the Holy Spirit, will not be forgiven. But what this word is, the Lord wished to be sought by us; therefore he did not express it. He wished it sought, I say, not denied. For the Scriptures are wont to speak in such a way that, when something is so said that it is bounded as said neither wholly nor in part, it is not necessary that it can be done wholly, that it not be understood even in part. So this sentence would be pronounced wholly, that is, universally, if it were said: "Every blasphemy of the Spirit will not be forgiven," or: "He who says any word whatever against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven him, neither in this age nor in the age to come." But it would be pronounced in part, that is, particularly, if it were said: "A certain blasphemy of the Spirit will not be forgiven." Because, then, the sentence is enunciated neither universally nor particularly (for it was not said: "Every blasphemy of the Spirit," or: "A certain blasphemy," but only indefinitely was it said: *The blasphemy of the Spirit will not be forgiven*; nor was it said: "He who says any word whatever," or: "He who says a certain word," but indefinitely: *He who says a word*), it is not necessary that we understand every blasphemy or every word, but it is plainly necessary that the Lord wished a certain blasphemy and a certain word to be understood: although he was unwilling to express it, that, by asking, seeking, knocking[^22], if we have received any right understanding, we may not hold it cheaply.

**7. 11.** That you may see this more manifestly, attend to what the same one says about the Jews: *If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not have sin*[^23]. For it was not said in such a way that he wished the Jews to be understood as going to be without any sin at all, if he had not come and spoken to them. For he found them full of and burdened with sins. On account of which he says: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened*[^24]. Whence, except by the loads of sins and the transgressions of the Law? Since *the Law entered in, that the offense might abound*[^25]. Since, then, he himself elsewhere says: *I did not come to call the just, but sinners*[^26]; how, if he had not come, would they not have sin, except because this sentence, pronounced neither universally nor particularly, but indefinitely, does not compel us to understand every sin? But plainly, unless we understand some sin, which they would not have unless Christ had come and spoken to them, we would say the sentence was false, which God forbid. So he did not say: "If I had not come and spoken to them, they would have no sin," lest the Truth lie. Nor again did he say definitely: "If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not have a certain sin," lest godly zeal be too little exercised. For in all the abundance of the holy Scriptures we are fed by the open things, exercised by the obscure; there hunger is driven away, here disdain. Because, then, it was not said: "They would have no sin," let us not be disturbed, when we recognize the Jews as sinners, even if the Lord had not come. But yet, because it was said: *If I had not come, they would not have sin*, thence it is necessary that, even if not every, yet some sin which they did not have they contracted from the coming of the Lord. That very thing surely it is, that they did not believe in him present and speaking to them, and, reckoning him an enemy because he spoke true things, moreover killed him. This sin, so great and horrendous, if he had not come and spoken to them, they surely would not have. As, then, there, when we hear: *They would not have sin*, we understand not every but some sin; so in today's reading, when we hear: *The blasphemy of the Spirit will not be forgiven*, not every blasphemy but a certain one; and when we heard: *He who says a word against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven him*, not every word but a certain one we ought to understand.

**7. 12.** For this very thing too which he says: *But the blasphemy of the Spirit will not be forgiven*, surely we must understand the blasphemy not of every spirit, but of the Holy Spirit. And even if he did not say it more plainly elsewhere, who would be so senseless as to understand otherwise? According to this rule of speech that too is taken: *Unless one is born again of water and the Spirit*[^27]. For there too he does not say "of the Holy Spirit," and yet this is understood. Nor, because he said: *Of water and the Spirit*, is anyone compelled to understand every spirit. Wherefore when you hear: *But the blasphemy of the Spirit will not be forgiven*, as you ought not to take every spirit, so you ought not to take the blasphemy of every spirit.

**8. 13.** I see you now wish to hear—since it is not every one—what that blasphemy of the Spirit is which will not be forgiven, and what is the word—since it is not every one—which, if it is said against the Holy Spirit, will not be forgiven, neither in this age nor in the age to come. Now I too would wish to say what you most intently await to hear; but bear with some delays of greater diligence, until, with the Lord's help, I unfold all that occurs. For the other two Evangelists, Mark and Luke, when they spoke of this matter, did not say "blasphemy," or "word," that we might understand not every blasphemy, but a certain one, nor every word, but a certain one. What, then, did they say? In Mark it is written thus: *Amen I say to you, that all sins will be forgiven the sons of men, and the blasphemies with which they shall have blasphemed. But he who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit has no forgiveness forever, but will be guilty of an eternal offense*[^28]. In Luke thus: *Everyone who says a word against the Son of man, it will be forgiven him. But to him who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven*[^29]. Surely, on account of some diversity of words, do we depart from the truth of the same sentence? For what other cause is there why the Evangelists say the same things not in the same way, except that from this we may learn to prefer realities to words, not words to realities, and to seek nothing else in a speaker than the will for whose conveying the words are brought forth? For what does it matter to the matter, whether it is said: *The blasphemy of the Spirit will not be forgiven*, or it is said: *He who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven him*, except perhaps that the same matter is said more openly in this way than in that, and one Evangelist does not destroy another but explains him? *But the blasphemy of the Spirit* was said obscurely, because it was not expressed of what spirit. For not every spirit is the Holy Spirit. Likewise it can be called *the blasphemy of the spirit*, when one blasphemes with the spirit; just as it can be called "the prayer of the spirit," when one prays with the spirit; whence the Apostle says: *I will pray with the spirit, I will pray also with the mind*[^30]. But when it was said: *He who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit*, those ambiguities are resolved. Likewise, what is written: *He has no forgiveness forever, but will be guilty of an eternal offense*, what else is it than what is read according to Matthew: *It will not be forgiven him, neither in this age nor in the age to come?* For with other words and in another manner of speaking the very same sentence is expressed. And what is in Matthew: *He who says a word against the Holy Spirit*, lest we understand anything other than blasphemy, the others said more plainly: *He who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit.* Yet the same matter was said by all, nor did any of them depart from the will of the speaker, for whose understanding the words are said, written, read, heard.

**9. 14.** But someone says: Behold, I have received and understood, that when it is said: *Blasphemy*, and neither "every" nor "a certain" is expressed, "every" can indeed be understood, but it is not necessary; but unless "a certain" is understood, what is said is false. So too "word": if neither "every" nor "a certain" is said, it is not necessary that "every" be understood; but unless at least "a certain" has been understood, in no way can what is said be true. But where it is read: *He who blasphemes*, how do I understand "a certain blasphemy," where "blasphemy" is not read, or "a certain word" where "word" is not read, but it seems to be said as it were generally: *He who blasphemes?* To this objection we answer thus: Because here too, if it were said: "He who blasphemes any blasphemy whatever against the Holy Spirit," there would be no reason why we should think some blasphemy must be sought, when we ought to understand "every"; but because "every" cannot be understood, lest hope of remission, if they correct themselves, be taken away from pagans, Jews, heretics, and every kind of men who by their diverse errors and contradictions blaspheme against the Holy Spirit; it remains surely that in what is written: *He who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit, has no forgiveness forever*, he is understood who has blasphemed not in any way whatever, but in that way that he can never be pardoned.

**10. 15.** For just as in what was said: God tempts no one[^31], God must be understood to tempt no one not in every way, but in a certain way of temptation, lest that be false which is written: *The Lord your God tempts you*[^32], and lest we deny that Christ is God, or call the Gospel false, where we read that he questioned a disciple *tempting him, but he himself knew what he was going to do*[^33]. For there is a temptation that leads to sin, by which God tempts no one; and there is a temptation that proves faith, by which God too deigns to tempt. So, when we hear: He who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit, we ought not to take every kind of blaspheming, just as not there every kind of tempting.

**10. 16.** Likewise, when we hear: *He who believes and is baptized will be saved*[^34], we surely do not understand one believing in that way in which the demons too believe and tremble[^35], nor among that number of baptized in which Simon the magician could be baptized[^36], but could not be saved. As, then, when he said: *He who believes and is baptized will be saved*, he regarded not all who believe and are baptized, but certain ones; namely those set in that faith which, as the Apostle distinguishes, *works through love*[^37]; so, when he said: *He who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit has no forgiveness forever*, he had in view not every, but a certain guilt of one blaspheming the Holy Spirit, by which whoever is bound will never be loosed by any remission.

**11. 17.** That too which he says: *He who eats my flesh and drinks my blood, abides in me and I in him*[^38], how are we to understand it? Can we here too take in those of whom the Apostle says, that they eat and drink judgment to themselves[^39], when they eat the very flesh and drink the very blood? Did Judas too, the seller of his master and impious betrayer, although he ate and drank that very sacrament of his flesh and blood, first confected by his hands, with the other disciples, as the evangelist Luke more openly declares, abide in Christ or Christ in him[^40]? Finally, many, who either with feigned heart eat that flesh and drink that blood, or, when they have eaten and drunk, become apostates, do they abide in Christ, or Christ in them? But surely there is a certain way of eating that flesh and drinking that blood, in which way he who has eaten and drunk abides in Christ and Christ in him. So not in any way whatever that anyone eats the flesh of Christ and drinks the blood of Christ does he abide in Christ and Christ in him, but in a certain way; which way surely he himself saw, when he said these things. So therefore in what he says: *He who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit has no forgiveness forever*, not in any way whatever that he blasphemes is he guilty of this irremissible offense, but in a certain way, which he wished us to seek and understand, who brought forth this sentence, true and terrible.

**12. 18.** But what this way of blaspheming is, or rather this immoderation, what this blasphemy is, and what is the word against the Holy Spirit, now, as I judge, the very order demands that we say, and that we no longer defer your expectation, held back so long but necessarily. You know, dearly beloved, in that invisible and incorruptible Trinity, which the true faith and the Catholic Church holds and preaches, that God the Father is not the Father of the Holy Spirit, but of the Son; and God the Son is not the Son of the Holy Spirit, but of the Father; but God the Holy Spirit is the Spirit not of the Father alone or of the Son alone, but of the Father and the Son; and that this Trinity, although the property and substance of each of the Persons is preserved, yet, on account of that individual and inseparable essence or nature of eternity, truth, goodness, is not three gods, but one God. And through this, according to our capacity, as far as these things are granted to be seen through a mirror and in an enigma[^41], especially to such as we still are, there is conveyed to us in the Father authority, in the Son nativity, in the Holy Spirit the community of the Father and the Son, in the three equality. What, then, is common to the Father and the Son, through this they wished us to have communion both among ourselves and with them, and through that gift to gather us into one, which both have as one, that is, through the Holy Spirit, God and the gift of God. For in this we are reconciled to divinity, and delighted by it. For what would it profit us, whatever good we knew, unless we also loved it? But as by truth we learn, so by love we love, that we may both more fully know, and, blessed, fully enjoy the known. *Moreover, love is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^42]. And because by sins we were estranged from the possession of true goods, love covered the multitude of sins[^43]. So the Father is to the Son, the truth, the truthful origin, and the Son is the truth sprung from the truthful Father, and the Holy Spirit is the goodness poured forth from the good Father and the good Son; but the divinity of all is not unequal, nor the unity separable.

**12. 19.** First, then, into us, for receiving eternal life, which will be given at the last, there comes from the goodness of God, from the beginning of faith, the gift of the remission of sins. For while those remain, there remain in a way the enmities against God, and the estrangement from him, which is from our evil, since Scripture does not lie when it says: *Your sins separate between you and God*[^44]. So he does not bring his good things upon us, unless he takes away our evil things. And those increase as much as these are diminished; nor will those be perfected, unless these are ended. Now indeed, that the Lord Christ so forgives sins in the Holy Spirit as in the Holy Spirit he casts out demons, can be understood from this, that, after he rose from the dead, when he had said to his disciples: *Receive the Holy Spirit*, he at once added: *If you forgive anyone's sins, they will be forgiven him; if you retain anyone's, they will be retained*[^45]. For that regeneration too, where the remission of all past sins takes place, takes place in the Holy Spirit, the Lord saying: *Unless one is born again of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God*[^46]. But it is one thing to be born of the Spirit, another to be fed by the Spirit; just as it is one thing to be born of the flesh, which happens when the mother gives birth, another to be fed by the flesh, which happens when she suckles the infant, turned to this, that with pleasure it might drink whence it was born to live; that from there it might receive the nourishment of living, whence it received the beginning of being born. So the first benefit of believers is, by the kindness of God, in the Holy Spirit, the remission of sins. Whence the preaching of John the Baptist too, who was sent as the forerunner of the Lord, so began. For it is written thus: *But in those days came John the Baptist, preaching in the desert of Judea, and saying: Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near*[^47]. Hence too the Lord's own, which is read thus: *From then Jesus began to preach and say: Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near*[^48]. But John said, among the other things he spoke to those who came to be baptized by him: *I indeed baptize you with water unto penance; but he who is to come after me is stronger than I, whose sandals I am not worthy to carry; he will baptize you in the Holy Spirit and fire*[^49]. The Lord too said: *John indeed baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit, whom you are also going to receive not many days hence*[^50], up to Pentecost. But what John said: *And fire*, although it can be understood also of the tribulation which believers were going to suffer for the name of Christ; yet it is not beside the point that the same Holy Spirit seems to be signified also by the name of fire. On account of which it was said at his coming too: *There appeared to them divided tongues, as of fire, which also settled upon each one of them*[^51]. Hence the Lord himself too says: *I came to cast fire upon the world*[^52]. Hence the Apostle too says: *Fervent in spirit*[^53], since hence love is fervent. For it is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us[^54]. To which fervor that is contrary which the Lord says: *The love of many will grow cold*[^55]. But perfect love is the perfect gift of the Holy Spirit. But that is first which pertains to the remission of sins; through which benefit we are rescued from the power of darkness[^56], and the prince of this world is cast outside by our faith[^57], who works in the children of unbelief[^58] by no other force than by the fellowship and bond of sin. For in the Holy Spirit, by whom the people of God is gathered into one, is cast out the unclean spirit, who is divided against himself.

**12. 20.** Against this free gift, against this grace of God, the impenitent heart speaks. So impenitence itself is the blasphemy of the Spirit, which will not be forgiven, neither in this age nor in the age to come. For against the Holy Spirit, by whom those are baptized whose sins are all forgiven, and whom the Church has received, that to whom she forgives sins they may be forgiven him, he speaks a very evil and exceedingly impious word, whether by thought or even by his tongue, who, while the patience of God leads him to penance, himself, according to the hardness of his heart and an impenitent heart, treasures up for himself wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works[^59]. This impenitence, then (for thus by some one name we can somehow call both the blasphemy and the word against the Holy Spirit which has no remission forever); this, I say, impenitence, against which both the herald and the judge cried out, saying: *Do penance, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near*[^60]; against which the Lord opened the mouth of evangelical preaching, and against which he foretold that the Gospel itself was to be preached in the whole world; where, after he rose from the dead, he said to the disciples: *It behooved Christ to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day, and that penance and the remission of sins be preached in his name through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^61]; this impenitence altogether has no remission, neither in this age nor in the age to come; because penance obtains the remission in this age, which avails in the age to come.

**13. 21.** But this impenitence, or impenitent heart, as long as anyone lives in this flesh, cannot be judged. For of no one must we despair, as long as the patience of God leads to penance; nor does he snatch the impious from this life, who does not wish the death of the impious so much as that he turn back and live[^62]. He is a pagan today: how do you know whether he will be a Christian tomorrow? He is an unbelieving Jew today: what if tomorrow he believes in Christ? He is a heretic today: what if tomorrow he follows the Catholic truth? He is a schismatic today: what if tomorrow he embraces Catholic peace? What if those whom you mark in any kind of error, and condemn as most desperate, before they end this life, do penance and find the true life? Accordingly, brothers, let this too admonish you, what the Apostle says: *Do not judge anything before the time*[^63]. For this blasphemy of the Spirit, for which there is never any remission (which we understand not as every blasphemy but a certain one, and that the persevering hardness of the impenitent heart we have either said or found or even, as far as we judge, shown), cannot, as we said, be detected in anyone, while he is still in this life.

**13. 22.** Let this not seem absurd, that, although a man persevering in hard impenitence even to the end of this life speaks long and much against this grace of the Holy Spirit, yet the Gospel called so long a contradiction of the impenitent heart, as if something brief, a *word*, saying: *Whoever says a word against the Son of man, it will be forgiven him; but he who says it against the Holy Spirit; it will not be forgiven him, either in this age or in the age to come*[^64]. For although this blasphemy is lengthy, woven together and extended through very many words, Scripture nevertheless commonly calls even many words a *word*. No prophet spoke just one word, yet we read: *The word that came* to this or that prophet. And the Apostle says: *Let the elders be honored with double honor, especially those who labor in the word and in teaching*[^65]. He did not say, “in words,” but, *in the word*. And Saint James says: *Be doers of the word, and not hearers only*[^66]. He too did not say “of words,” but *of the word*, although so many words from the holy Scriptures are read, spoken, and heard in the Church's customary solemn assemblies. Just as, however long any of us has labored in preaching the Gospel, he is called a preacher of the word, rather than of words, and however long any of you has diligently and persistently heard our preaching, he is called most eager to hear the word, rather than words, so, according to Scripture's manner of speaking, familiar to the Church's usage, anyone who throughout his whole life in this flesh, however long it extends, speaks any words, whether aloud or only in his thoughts, with an impenitent heart against the forgiveness of sins that takes place in the Church, speaks a word against the Holy Spirit.

**13. 23.** But for this reason not only the word that is said against the Son of man, but absolutely every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven men, because where this sin of the impenitent heart against the Holy Spirit—by whom in the Church sins are loosed—is not present, all the others are forgiven. But how will this be forgiven, which even hinders the remission of others? So all things are forgiven those in whom this is not present which is never forgiven; but in those in whom it is present, since this is never forgiven, neither are the others forgiven; because the remission of all is hindered by the bond of this. So not for this reason will it be forgiven whoever says a word against the Son of man, but it will not be forgiven him who says it against the Holy Spirit, because in the Trinity the Holy Spirit is greater than the Son—which no one, not even a heretic, has ever said; but because whoever has resisted the truth and blasphemed the truth, which is Christ, even after so great a preaching of himself among men, that the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us[^67], which is the Son of man, the same Christ himself, if he has not spoken that word of the impenitent heart against the Holy Spirit, of whom it was said: *He who is not born again of water and the Spirit*[^68], and of whom likewise it was said: *Receive the Holy Spirit; if you forgive anyone's sins, they are forgiven him*[^69], that is, "if it has repented him, he will receive through this gift the remission of all sins," together also of this, that he spoke a word against the Son of man; because to the sin of ignorance or of obstinacy or of any blasphemy whatever, he did not add the sin of impenitence against the gift of God and the grace of regeneration or reconciliation, which takes place in the Church by the Holy Spirit.

**14. 24.** Accordingly, neither must that be held which some think, that for this reason the word that is said against the Son of man is forgiven, but the word that is said against the Holy Spirit is not forgiven; because, on account of the assumed flesh, Christ became the Son of man, than which flesh surely the Holy Spirit is greater, who in his own proper substance is equal to the Father and the only-begotten Son according to his divinity, according to which the only-begotten Son himself too is equal to the Father and the Holy Spirit. For if it had been said for this reason, surely there would be silence about every other blasphemy, and this alone would seem remissible, which is said against the Son of man, as when he is thought to be a man only. But since it was set before: *Every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven men* (which in another Evangelist too is so placed: *All sins will be forgiven the sons of men, and the blasphemies with which they shall have blasphemed*[^70]), without doubt that blasphemy too which is said against the Father is included in this generality; and yet this alone is defined as irremissible, which is said against the Holy Spirit. Did the Father too take on the form of a servant, than which the Holy Spirit is greater? Surely not. But for this reason, after the universal mention of all sins and all blasphemy, he wished more eminently to express the blasphemy which is said against the Son of man; because, even if men have been bound in that sin which he mentioned, where he says: *If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not have sin*[^71] (which in the Gospel according to John too he shows to be a very grave sin, where he says of the Holy Spirit himself, when he promised that he would send him: *He will convict the world of sin, and of justice, and of judgment; of sin indeed, because they did not believe in me*[^72]); yet, if the hardness of the impenitent heart has not spoken a word against the Holy Spirit, even this which was said against the Son of man will be forgiven.

**15. 25.** Here perhaps someone may ask whether the Holy Spirit alone forgives sins, or the Father and the Son too. We answer, that both the Father and the Son do. For the Son himself says of the Father: *If you forgive men their sins, your Father too will forgive you your sins*[^73]. To whom we too say in the Lord's Prayer: *Our Father, who are in heaven*[^74], and among the rest this too we ask, saying: *Forgive us our debts*[^75]. But of himself he says: *That you may know that the Son of man has power on earth to forgive sins*[^76]. "If, then, you say, both the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit forgive sins, why is that impenitence which is never forgiven said to pertain only to the blasphemy of the Spirit; as though he who is bound by this sin of impenitence seems to resist the gift of the Holy Spirit, because by that gift the remission of sins takes place?" Here I too ask, whether Christ cast out demons, or the Father and the Holy Spirit too. For if Christ alone, what is it that he himself says: *But the Father abiding in me, he does the works?*[^77] For it is so said: *He does the works*, as though the Son does not do them, but the Father abiding in the Son. Why, then, does he say in another place: *My Father works until now, and I work?*[^78] And a little after: *For whatever things he does, these the Son too does likewise?*[^79] But what he says in another place: *If I had not done among them works that no one else did*[^80], he says as though he alone did them. But if these things are so said that yet the works of the Father and the Son are inseparable, what must be believed of the Holy Spirit, except that he too equally works? For in that very place from which arose this question that we are examining, when the Son was casting out demons, he himself yet says: *If I cast out demons by the Holy Spirit, then the kingdom of God has come upon you*[^81].

**16. 26.** Here perhaps someone may say, that the Holy Spirit is given by the Father or the Son rather than works anything by his own will; and that to this pertains what was said: *By the Holy Spirit I cast out demons*, that not the Spirit himself but Christ did it by the Spirit: so that what was said: *By the Holy Spirit I cast out*, may be understood as if it were said: "I cast out with the Holy Spirit." For the Scriptures are wont to speak thus: *They killed in the sword*, that is, "with the sword." *They burned in fire*, that is, "with fire"; and *Jesus took stone knives, in which he might circumcise the sons of Israel*[^82], that is: "with which he might circumcise the sons of Israel." But those who for this reason take away from the Holy Spirit his own proper power, let them attend to what is read as said by the Lord: *The Spirit breathes where he wishes*[^83]. And that which the Apostle says: *But all these things one and the same Spirit works*[^84], it is to be feared lest anyone think that the Father and the Son do not work them, since in those works he mentioned both the gifts of healings and the workings of powers[^85], where surely is the expulsion of demons too. But when he adds and says: *Dividing to each his own, as he wishes*[^86], does he not manifest the power of the Holy Spirit too, but plainly inseparable from the Father and the Son? If, then, these things are so said that yet the working of the Trinity is understood inseparable; so that, when the working of the Father is spoken of, it is not understood to work it without the Son and the Holy Spirit; and when the working of the Son, not without the Father and the Holy Spirit; and when the working of the Holy Spirit, not without the Father and the Son; it is well enough known to those believing rightly, or even to those understanding as they can, both that this too was said of the Father: *He does the works*, because from him is the origin even of the works, from whom is the existence of the cooperating Persons (because both the Son is born of him, and the Holy Spirit principally proceeds from him from whom the Son is born and with whom the same Spirit is common to him), and that what the Lord said: *If I had not done among them works that no one else did*, he referred not to the Father or the Spirit, as though they did not cooperate with him in those works, but to men, by whom many miracles are read to have been done, and yet by none the things that the Son did; and that what the Apostle says of the Holy Spirit: *But all these things one and the same Spirit works*, was not said for this reason, because the Father and the Son do not cooperate with him, but because in these works there are not many but one Spirit, and in his diverse workings he is not diverse from himself.

**16. 27.** Yet it is said, not vainly but reasonably and truly, that the Father said, not the Son or the Holy Spirit: *You are my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased*[^87]; but we do not deny that the Son and the Holy Spirit cooperated in this miracle of the sounding word from heaven, although it pertains only to the person of the Father. For not because the Son, then bearing flesh, conversed with men on earth, was he therefore not also in the bosom of the Father as the only-begotten Word, when that voice was made from the cloud; nor can it wisely and spiritually be believed that God the Father separated the working of his sounding and passing words from the cooperation of his Wisdom and his Spirit. In the same way, although we most rightly say that not the Father, nor the Holy Spirit, but the Son walked upon the sea[^88], whose alone was that flesh and the soles resting on the waves; yet who would deny that the Father and the Holy Spirit cooperated in that work of so great a miracle? For so too we most truly say that the Son alone took on that very flesh, not the Father or the Holy Spirit; and yet whoever denies that the Father or the Holy Spirit cooperated in this incarnation pertaining to the Son alone, does not savor rightly. Likewise we say that neither the Father, nor the Son, but the Holy Spirit alone appeared both in the form of a dove and in tongues as it were of fire, and gave to those upon whom he had come to proclaim, in many and various tongues, the great works of God[^89]; yet from this miracle pertaining to the Holy Spirit alone we cannot separate the cooperation of the Father and the only-begotten Word. So the Trinity works also the works of the individual ones in the Trinity, with the two cooperating with each one working, the concord of acting agreeing in the three, the efficacy of accomplishing not failing in any one. Since these things are so, hence it is that the Lord Jesus casts out demons by the Holy Spirit. For neither could he not also fulfill this alone, and take up that help as not sufficient for this work; but the spirit divided against itself fittingly was expelled by that Spirit whom the Father and the Son, not divided against themselves, have in common.

**17. 28.** So sins too, because they are not forgiven outside the Church, had to be forgiven in that Spirit by whom the Church is gathered into one. Finally, if anyone outside the Church repents of his sins, and has an impenitent heart concerning this so great a sin, that he is estranged from the Church of God, what does that penance profit him; since by this alone he speaks a word against the Holy Spirit, that he is a stranger to the Church which has received this gift, that in her, in the Holy Spirit, the remission of sins takes place? Which remission, though the Trinity makes it, yet is properly understood to pertain to the Holy Spirit. For he is the Spirit of the adoption of sons, in whom we cry: *Abba, Father*[^90], that we may be able to say to him: *Forgive us our debts*[^91]. And: *In this we know*, as the apostle John says, *that Christ abides in us, by the Spirit whom he gave us*[^92]. *The Spirit himself bears witness to our spirit, that we are sons of God*[^93]. For to him pertains the fellowship by which we are made into one body of the only Son of God. Whence it is written: *If, then, there is any exhortation in Christ, if any comfort of love, if any fellowship of the Spirit*[^94]. On account of this fellowship, those upon whom he first came spoke in the tongues of all nations[^95]. Because by tongues the association of the human race subsists; and so it was fitting that by the tongues of all nations this fellowship of the sons of God and members of Christ, to be in all nations, be signified; so that, as then he appeared to have received the Holy Spirit who spoke in the tongues of all nations, so now let him acknowledge that he has received the Holy Spirit who is held by the bond of peace of the Church, which is spread among all nations. Whence the Apostle says: *Being eager to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace*[^96].

**18. 29.** But that he is the Spirit of the Father, the Son himself says: *He proceeds from the Father*[^97], and in another place: *For it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you*[^98]. But that he is also the Spirit of the Son, the Apostle says: *God sent the Spirit of his Son into our hearts, crying: Abba, Father*[^99], that is, "making us cry." For we cry, but in him, that is, with him himself pouring out love in our hearts, without which whoever cries, cries in vain. Whence likewise he says: *But whoever does not have the Spirit of Christ, this one is not his*[^100]. To whom, then, in the Trinity would the communion of this fellowship properly pertain, except to that Spirit who is common to the Father and the Son?

**18. 30.** This Spirit, that they do not have who are separated from the Church, the apostle Jude most openly declared, saying: *Those who separate themselves, animal, not having the Spirit*[^101]. Whence in the Church itself too, Paul the Apostle, rebuking those who, through the names of men, although established in her unity, were contriving certain schisms, says among the rest: *But the animal man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God. For it is foolishness to him, and he cannot know, because it is spiritually discerned*[^102]. He showed what he said: *does not perceive*, that is, "does not grasp the word of knowledge." These, established in the Church, he calls little ones, not yet spiritual, but still carnal, and to be fed with milk, not solid food. *As to little ones*, he says, *in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not food; for you were not yet able, but neither indeed are you yet able*[^103]. Where it is said: *Not yet*, or: *Not indeed yet*, surely it is not despaired of, if it tends to this, that there may at some time be what is not yet. *For you are still*, he says, *carnal*[^104]. And showing whence they are carnal: *For while there is*, he says, *among you rivalry and contention, are you not carnal and walking according to man?*[^105] And opening this same thing more plainly: *For while one says: I indeed*, he says, *am of Paul, but another: I of Apollos, are you not men? What then is Apollos? And what is Paul? Ministers, through whom you believed*[^106]. These, then, that is, Paul and Apollos, were in concord in the unity of the Spirit and the bond of peace[^107]: and yet, because these men had begun to divide them among themselves and to be inflated for one against another[^108], they are called both men, and carnal, and animal, unable to perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God; and yet, because they are not separated from the Church, they are called little ones in Christ; whom surely he wished to be either angels or gods, those whom he rebuked because they were men, that is, in these contentions savored not the things that are of God but the things that are of men. But of those who are separated from the Church, it was not said: "Not perceiving the things that are of the Spirit," lest they be referred to the perception of knowledge; but it was said: *Not having the Spirit*[^109]. But it is not consequent that he who has it also perceives by knowing what he has.

**18. 31.** So the little ones in Christ, established in the Church, still animal and carnal, have this Spirit, unable to perceive—that is, to understand and know—what they have. For how would they be little ones in Christ, unless born again of the Holy Spirit? Nor ought it to seem a wonder, that someone has something and is ignorant of what he has. For—that I may be silent about the divinity of the Almighty and the unity of the unchangeable Trinity—who would easily perceive by knowledge what the soul is? And who does not have a soul? Finally, that we may know most certainly that the little ones in Christ, not perceiving the things that are of the Spirit of God, yet have the Spirit of God; let us look a little after at how, rebuking these same ones, he says: *Do you not know that you are the temple of God, and the Spirit of God dwells in you?*[^110] This surely he would in no way say to those separated from the Church, who are said not to have the Spirit.

**19. 32.** But neither must he be said to be in the Church and to belong to this fellowship of the Spirit, who is mingled with the sheep of Christ only by a bodily mixing, with a feigned heart. For the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee the feigned[^111]. Wherefore, whoever are baptized in schismatic or heretical congregations, or rather segregations, although they are not born again of the Spirit—like Ishmael, who was born to Abraham according to the flesh, not like Isaac, who according to the spirit, because by promise[^112]—yet, when they come to the Catholic Church and are joined to the fellowship of the Spirit, whom outside they without doubt did not have, the laver of the flesh is not repeated for them. For that form of godliness was not lacking even to those placed outside, but there is added to them what cannot be given except within, the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace[^113]. For such they were, before they were Catholics, as those of whom the Apostle says: *Having a form of godliness, but denying its power*[^114]. For there can be a visible form of a branch, even apart from the vine; but the invisible life cannot have roots except in the vine. Accordingly, the bodily sacraments, which even those segregated from the unity of the body of Christ carry and celebrate, can present a form of godliness; but the power of godliness, invisible and spiritual, can no more be in them than sensation follows a member of a man when it is cut off from the body.

**20. 33.** Since these things are so, the remission of sins, because it is given only in the Holy Spirit, can be given in that Church alone which has the Holy Spirit. For this is done by the remission of sins, that the prince of sin, the spirit who is divided against himself, may not reign in us; that, rescued from the power of the unclean spirit, we may thereafter be made the temple of the Holy Spirit; and that we may receive him from whom we are cleansed by receiving pardon, as the indweller for doing, increasing, and perfecting justice. For even at his first coming, when those who had received him spoke in the tongues of all nations[^115], and the apostle Peter addressed those who were present, amazed, they were pricked in heart and said to Peter and to the Apostles: *What then shall we do, brothers? Show us*. *And Peter said to them: Do penance, and let each of you be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ unto the remission of sins, and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit*[^116]. Surely in the Church both things were done, that is, both the remission of sins and the receiving of this gift, in which Church was the Holy Spirit. But for this reason in the name of Jesus Christ, because, when he promised the same Holy Spirit: *Whom the Father sends*, he says, *in my name*[^117]. For neither does the Holy Spirit dwell in anyone without the Father and the Son, just as neither the Son without the Father and the Holy Spirit, nor the Father without them. For inseparable is the indwelling of those whose working is inseparable; but they are mostly shown singly through the significations of a creature, not through their own substance; just as their own spaces of times, the syllables occupying them, are pronounced separately by the voice, and yet are not separated from one another by any intervals and moments of times. For they can never be said at once, since they cannot be except always at once. But, as we have now said more than once, for this reason the remission of sins, by which the kingdom of the spirit divided against itself is overthrown and expelled, for this reason the fellowship of the unity of the Church of God, outside which that very remission of sins does not take place, is as it were the proper work of the Holy Spirit, with the Father indeed and the Son cooperating, because the Holy Spirit himself is in a way the fellowship of the Father and the Son. For the Father is not held in common as Father by the Son and the Holy Spirit, because he is not the Father of both; and the Son is not held in common as Son by the Father and the Holy Spirit, because he is not the Son of both; but the Holy Spirit is held in common by the Father and the Son, because he is the one Spirit of both.

**21. 34.** Whoever, therefore, is guilty of impenitence against the Spirit, in whom the unity and fellowship of the communion of the Church is gathered, it will never be forgiven him; because he has closed to himself that whereby it is forgiven; and deservedly he will be condemned with the spirit who is divided against himself, himself too divided against the Holy Spirit who is not divided against himself. The very Gospel testimonies admonish us of this too, if we scrutinize them more attentively. For according to Luke the Lord does not say this in the place where he answered those who said that he cast out demons by the prince of demons, that it is not forgiven him who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit. Whence it appears that this was not said once by the Lord. But there too it must not be passed over negligently in what place it was said. For he was speaking of those who confessed or denied him before men, where he says: *But I say to you: Whoever confesses me before men, the Son of man too will confess him before the angels of God; but he who denies me before men will be denied before the angels of God*[^118]. And lest from this the salvation of the apostle Peter, who denied him three times before men, be despaired of, he at once added: *And everyone who says a word against the Son of man, it will be forgiven him; but to him who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven*[^119]; namely that blasphemy of the impenitent heart, by which the remission of sins, which takes place in the Church through the Holy Spirit, is resisted. Which blasphemy Peter did not have, who soon repented when he wept bitterly[^120], and, the spirit divided against himself being conquered—who had demanded him to be harassed, against whom the Lord asked for him that his faith not fail[^121]—he received also that very Holy Spirit whom he had not resisted, so that not only the sin was forgiven him, but through him the remission of sins was preached and given.

**21. 35.** But in the other two Evangelists, what is narrated, that this sentence about the blasphemy of the Spirit was said, the cause arose from the mention of the unclean spirit who is divided against himself. For it had been said of the Lord that he cast out demons by the prince of demons; there the Lord says that he casts out demons by the Holy Spirit, that the Spirit who is not divided against himself may conquer and cast out him who is divided against himself; but that man may remain in his perdition, who declines to pass over by penance into the peace of him who is not divided against himself. For Mark narrates this thus: *Amen I say to you, that all sins will be forgiven men, and the blasphemies with which they shall have blasphemed; but he who blasphemes against the Holy Spirit has no forgiveness forever, but will be guilty of an eternal offense*[^122]. When he had said these words of the Lord, he then joined his own, saying: *Because they said: He has an unclean spirit*[^123], to show that from this the cause arose for him to say this, in that they had said that he drove out demons by Beelzebub the prince of demons. Not because it was a blasphemy that is not forgiven, since this too is forgiven, if right penance follows; but because from this, as I said, the cause arose for the Lord to bring forth that sentence, mention having been made of the unclean spirit, whom the Lord showed to be divided against himself, on account of the Holy Spirit, who not only is not divided against himself, but even makes those whom he gathers undivided, by forgiving the sins that are divided against themselves and by dwelling in them cleansed; that there may be, as it is written in the Acts of the Apostles, one heart and one soul of the multitude of believers[^124]. To which gift of remission no one resists, except one who has the hardness of an impenitent heart. For in another place too the Jews said of the Lord that he had a demon[^125]; nor yet did he say anything there about the blasphemy of the Holy Spirit: because they did not so object an unclean spirit that it could be shown, from their own mouth, to be divided against itself, as Beelzebub by whom they said demons could be cast out.

**22. 36.** But in this reading according to Matthew the Lord opened much more manifestly what he wished to be understood here; that is, that he speaks a word against the Holy Spirit who, with an impenitent heart, resists the unity of the Church, where in the Holy Spirit the remission of sins takes place. For this Spirit they do not have, as has been said, who, even carrying and handling the sacraments of Christ, are sundered from his congregation. For where he spoke of the division of Satan against Satan, and that he himself cast out demons by the Holy Spirit, surely by the Spirit who is not divided against himself, as that one is; there he at once, lest through those who, under the name of Christ, gather their conventicles outside his fold, anyone should think that the kingdom of Christ too is divided against itself: *He who is not with me*, he says, *is against me; and he who does not gather with me scatters*[^126]; to show that those do not belong to him, who, by gathering outside, are unwilling to gather but to scatter. Then he added: *Therefore I say to you: Every sin and blasphemy will be forgiven men; but the blasphemy of the Spirit will not be forgiven*[^127]. What is this? Is the blasphemy of the Spirit alone for this reason not forgiven, because he who is not with Christ is against him; and he who does not gather with him scatters? For this reason surely. For he who does not gather with him, however he gathers under his name, does not have the Holy Spirit.

**23. 37.** Here altogether, here it has compelled us to understand that the remission of every sin and every blasphemy cannot take place anywhere else than in the congregation of Christ, which does not scatter. For it is gathered in the Holy Spirit, who is not divided against himself, as that unclean spirit is. And therefore all congregations, or rather dispersions, which call themselves churches of Christ, and are divided among themselves, and contrary, and enemies to the congregation of unity which is his true Church, do not belong to his congregation merely because they seem to have his name. But they would belong, if the Holy Spirit, in whom this congregation is associated, were divided against himself. But because this is not so (for he who is not with Christ is against him; and he who does not gather with him scatters), therefore every sin and every blasphemy will be forgiven men in this congregation, which Christ gathers in the Holy Spirit, who is not divided against himself; but that blasphemy of the Spirit himself, by which it comes about that with an impenitent heart this so great gift of God is resisted even to the end of this life, will not be forgiven. For even if anyone is so contrary to the truth that he struggles against God speaking not in the Prophets but in his only Son (when, for our sake, that he might speak to us in him, he willed him to be the Son of man), it will be forgiven him, when by repenting he has been converted to the kindness of God; who, since he did not wish the death of the impious so much as that he turn back and live[^128], gave to his Church the Holy Spirit, that to whomever in him she forgave sins, they might be forgiven him. But he who has been an enemy to this gift, so as not to ask it through penance, but to contradict it through impenitence, makes irremissible not any sin whatever, but the very remission of sins, despised or even opposed. And so a word is said against the Holy Spirit, when one never comes from dispersion to the congregation which received the Holy Spirit for forgiving sins. To which congregation, even if through a bad cleric, but yet a Catholic minister, reprobate and feigned, one approaches with a heart not feigned, in that very Holy Spirit he receives the remission of sins. Which Spirit in the holy Church, even at this time when, as it were, the threshing floor with chaff is threshed, so works, that he spurns no one's true confession, is deceived by no one's pretense, and so flees the reprobate that even through their ministry he gathers the approved. So there is one refuge, that the blasphemy be not irremissible, that the impenitent heart be guarded against; nor let penance be believed to profit otherwise than that the Church be held, where the remission of sins is given and the fellowship of the Spirit is kept in the bond of peace[^129].

**24. 38.** As I could, the most difficult question, if indeed I could in some measure, I have treated, with the Lord having mercy and helping. Yet whatever I was unable to grasp in its difficulty, let it not be imputed to the truth itself, which wholesomely exercises the godly even when it is hidden, but to my weakness, who was unable either to behold the things to be understood or to explain the things understood. But for those things which perhaps we have been able both to track down by thinking and to unfold by speaking, thanks must be given to him from whom we sought, from whom we asked, at whom we knocked[^130], that we might have whence both we ourselves might be nourished by meditating, and might minister to you by speaking.
