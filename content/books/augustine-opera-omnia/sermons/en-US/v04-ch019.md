# Sermon 109

**1.** We have heard the Gospel, and in it the Lord rebuking those who know how to interpret the face of heaven, and do not know how to find the time of the faith of the kingdom of heaven drawing near. He said this to the Jews: but the word reaches us too. The Lord Jesus Christ himself thus began the preaching of his Gospel: *Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near* [^1]. Likewise John the Baptist, his forerunner, thus began: *Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near* [^2]. And now the Lord rebukes those who refuse to repent, as the kingdom of heaven draws near. *The kingdom of heaven*, as he himself says, *will not come with observation* [^3]. And again he himself says: *The kingdom of heaven is within you* [^4]. Therefore let each one prudently receive the warnings of the master, that he may not lose the time of the Savior's mercy, which is now bestowed, as long as the human race is spared. For man is spared for this, that he may be converted, and there may be no one to be condemned. Let God see when the end of the age comes: yet now is the time of faith. Whether the end of the age will find any of us here, I do not know: and perhaps it will not find us. The time for each of us is near, because we are mortal. We walk among mishaps. If we were of glass, we would fear mishaps less. What more fragile than a vessel of glass? And yet it is preserved, and lasts through ages. For although mishaps are feared for a glass vessel, old age and fever are not feared for it. We, then, are more fragile and weaker: because both all the mishaps which do not cease in human affairs we daily dread, surely by our fragility; and if the mishaps themselves do not befall, time walks on: a man avoids a blow, does he avoid his exit? he avoids the things that befall from without, is what is born within driven off? In short, now the inner parts breed worms, now some sickness suddenly seizes: in the end, however much a man is spared, at last when old age comes, there is no way to defer it.

**2.** Accordingly let us hear the Lord, let us do in ourselves what he commanded. Let us see who is that adversary, of whom he frightened us, saying: *If you go with your adversary to the ruler, make an effort on the way to be freed from him; lest perhaps he hand you over to the ruler, and the ruler to the officer, and you be cast into prison, from which you will not come out until you pay the last farthing* [^5]. Who is this adversary? If the devil; we are already freed from him. What price was given for us, that we might be redeemed from him? Of which the Apostle says, speaking of our very redemption: *Who rescued us from the power of darkness, and transferred us into the kingdom of the Son of his love* [^6]. We have been redeemed, we renounced the devil: how shall we make an effort to be freed from him, lest he again make us captive sinners? But he is not the adversary of whom the Lord warns us. For in another place another evangelist set it down in such a way that, if we join both sayings, and compare both sayings of the two Gospels with each other, we may quickly understand this adversary. For behold, what did he say here? *When you go to the ruler with your adversary, make an effort on the way to be freed from him* [^7]. But another evangelist set this very thing down thus: *Be agreeing with your adversary quickly while you are with him on the way* [^8]. The rest are similar: *Lest perhaps the adversary hand you over to the judge, the judge to the officer, the officer into prison* [^9]. Both evangelists explained this similarly. One said: *Make an effort on the way to be freed from him*: the other said: *Agree with him*. For you cannot be freed from him, unless you agree with him. Do you want to be freed from him? *Agree with him*. Is it the devil, with whom a Christian ought to agree?

**3.** Let us seek, then, that adversary with whom we ought to agree, lest he hand us over to the judge, and the judge to the officer: let us seek him, and let us agree with him. If you sin, your adversary is the word of God. For example, perhaps it delights you to get drunk: it says to you: Do not. It delights you to gawk and to trifle: it says to you, Do not. It delights you to commit adultery: the word of God says to you, Do not. In whatever sins you may want to do your own will, it says to you, Do not. It is the adversary of your will, until it becomes the author of your salvation. O how good an adversary, how useful an adversary! It does not seek our will, but our profit. It is our adversary, as long as we too are our own. As long as you are an enemy to yourself, you have the word of God as an enemy: be a friend to yourself, and you are in concord with it. *You shall not commit murder* [^10]: hear it, and you are in concord. *You shall not commit theft* [^11]: hear it, and you are in concord. *You shall not commit adultery* [^12]: hear it, and you are in concord. *You shall not give false witness* [^13]: hear it, and you are in concord. *You shall not covet your neighbor's wife* [^14]: hear it, and you are in concord. *You shall not covet your neighbor's property* [^15]: hear it, and you are in concord. In all these you have been in concord with this adversary of yours: and what have you lost for yourself? Not only have you lost nothing: but you have even found yourself, who had perished. The way is this life: if we have agreed with him, if we have consented to him; the way ended, we shall not fear the judge, the officer, the prison.

**4.** When is the way ended? It is not ended for all at one hour. Each one has his hour, when he ends the way. The way is called this life: you have ended this life, you have ended the way. We walk; and the very living is going forward. Unless perhaps you think that time goes forward, and we stand still? It cannot be. As time goes forward, we too go forward: and the years do not come to us, but rather depart. Men greatly err, when they say: This boy is still of little sense, the years come to him, and he will be prudent. Attend to what you say. They come, you said: I show that they depart, when you say, They come. And hear how easily I prove it. Suppose we know his years from the time he was born: for example, that we may wish him well, he will live eighty years, he will reach old age. Write eighty years. He has lived one year: how many do you have in the sum? how many did you hold? Eighty. Subtract one. He has lived ten: seventy remain. He has lived twenty: sixty remain. Surely they came: what is this? They come, that our years may go away: they come, I say, that they may go. For they do not come to stand with us: but as they pass through us, they wear us away, and make us be worth less and less. Such is the way on which we have come. What shall we do with that adversary, that is, with the word of God? Agree with him. For you do not know when the way will be ended. When the way has been ended, the judge remains, and the officer, and the prison. But if you keep good will toward your adversary, and agree with him; instead of a judge you will find a father, instead of a cruel officer an angel taking you up into Abraham's bosom, instead of a prison paradise. How quickly on the way you have changed everything, because you agreed with your adversary!
