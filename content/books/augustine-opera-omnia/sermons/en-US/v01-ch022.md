# Sermon 17: A Sermon on Psalm 49

**1.** We have sung, *God shall come manifest, our God, and he shall not keep silence*[^1]. This Scripture foretold that God Christ would come to the judgment of the living and the dead. For when he first came to be judged, he was hidden; when he shall come to judge, he will be manifest. How hidden he then was, understand from this, where the Apostle says, *For if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*[^2]. But then, being questioned, he kept silence, as the Gospel relates, that the prophecy of Isaiah might be fulfilled in him, saying, *As a sheep he was led to the slaughter, and as a lamb before the shearer was without voice, so he opened not his mouth*[^3]. *He shall come, then, manifest, and he shall not keep silence.* For this it was said, *He shall not keep silence* when about to judge, because he kept silence when judged. For as far as concerns his utterances necessary to us, when did he keep silence? He did not keep silence through the Patriarchs, he did not keep silence through the Prophets, he did not keep silence through the mouth of his flesh. And now, if he were silent, would he not speak through the Scriptures? The reader ascends, but he himself does not keep silence. The expounder speaks. If he speaks true things, Christ speaks. If Christ kept silence, I too would not say these things to you. Nor did he keep silence through your mouth. For when you were singing, he himself was speaking. He does not keep silence. There is need that we hear, but with the ear of the heart, for it is easy to hear with that of the flesh. With those ears we ought to hear which the master himself sought, when he said, *Whoever has ears to hear, let him hear*[^4]. For who, before him, when he said these things, stood without ears of the flesh? All had ears, and few had them. Not all had ears to hear, that is, to obey.

**2.** How terribly he spoke through the prophet Ezekiel, I believe you applied your ear. I believe you heard how he said, *To the house of Israel I send you, not to a people of another tongue do I send you. But the people are unwilling to hear you, because they are unwilling to hear me*[^5]. What does he show, except that God himself was speaking through the Prophet? But because in those very prophetic words we especially have been terrified—that is, the prelates whom he set to speak to his people—we first see our own face in those words. For there was shown to us, the reader sounding, as it were a mirror in which we might look at ourselves, and we have looked at ourselves. Look at yourselves. Behold, I am doing what I heard there. *If you do not distinguish,* he says, *the just from the unjust, if you do not say to the sinner: You shall die the death, and show him so that he may withdraw from his iniquities, he indeed shall die in his sins, but his blood I will require from your hand. But if you say it, and he despises it and does not obey, he shall die in his crimes, but you shall free your soul*[^6]. I tell you, I free my soul. For I am placed in great—not danger, but ruin—if I keep silence. But when I have spoken and fulfilled my office, do you now attend to your own danger. But what do I want? What do I desire? What do I crave? Why do I speak? Why do I sit here? Why do I live? except with this intent, that we may live together with Christ. This is my desire, this is my honor, this is my possession, this is my joy, this is my glory. But if you do not hear me, and yet I do not keep silence, I shall free my soul. But I do not want to be saved without you.

**3.** Do not, then, despise, my brothers, the sins in which perhaps you have now made a habit. For every sin grows cheap by habit, and becomes to a man as though it were nothing. Hardness has now lost its pain. What is very rotten does not even hurt; what does not hurt is not to be held as sound, but to be counted as dead. Attend to what Scripture says, and there see how you ought to live. Who does not despise the sin of drunkenness? Such a sin abounds, and is despised. Now the heart of drunkards has lost feeling, has no pain, because no health either. When something is pricked and hurts, either it is sound or there is in it some hope of soundness. But when it is touched, pricked, or bound and does not hurt, it is to be held as dead, or to be cut off from the body. But sometimes we spare and know only how to speak. We are slothful to excommunicate, to cast out of the Church. For sometimes we fear lest, by that very scourge, he who is struck become worse. But will he spare, will he keep silence, whom we ought to fear? You have heard in that very psalm, my brothers, when he enumerated sins to sinners, he said, *These things you did and I kept silence*[^7]. Against this it was said, *He shall come and shall not keep silence*[^8]. By his presence he shall not keep silence. For, apart from the fact that the Lord Christ is signified as having kept silence in judgment, that there might be fulfilled in him that prophecy too which I recalled a little before—apart from this, now God himself, the Lord Christ, through himself keeps silence. For he ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father, whence he is to come to judge the living and the dead. As long as he is there, until he comes, he keeps silence. His utterances we hear in the books, from his own mouth we do not hear. But you have heard his utterances from the holy Scriptures in this place; you have heard them when you remind yourselves, and perhaps converse about these matters among yourselves.

**4.** Do you not hear him when you commit adultery, and think you are hidden because a man does not see you? He sees you, but keeps silence. When you commit theft, you watch the eyes of the one from whom you steal, and if it is hidden from him you do it. If you see it cannot be hidden, you do not do it. If you do not do it for this reason, that you fear to be seen, you have done it within, you have done it in the heart. You are held a thief, and you took nothing. But also, if opportunity is given for fulfilling your evil deed, you steal and rejoice. Why? Because he keeps silence? Hear, then, the psalm. It admonished you, you, whoever you are, who perhaps stand here today and by night did something evil, it admonished you, it said to you, *These things you did and I kept silence. You suspected iniquity, that I would be like you*[^9]. O you people, who have these words I am about to say neither in mouth nor in heart, you are happy! Do not people daily, doing evil, or those who repent of having done well and by a perverse repentance pour out what they had milked, do they not daily say and gnaw these murmurings among themselves: "Truly, if these things displeased God, would he permit them to be done? Or would those who do them be happy on earth? We see robbers, we see oppressors of the weak, we see expellers of neighbors, we see violent invaders of boundaries, we see slanderers, and yet the powerful, the rich, happy in this earth. If God truly saw these things, if he cared about these things, would he spare them?" They add this too, which is worse: "Only the wicked please God." But should it happen that one does something good and perhaps some temptation follows, at once he has it at hand: "It is not expedient to do good. Better that one do well by doing evil." Is it little that you wish to do evil, and you also curse those doing well? *These things you did,* he says, *and I kept silence. You suspected iniquity, that I would be like you.* What is, *that I would be like you?* That evil pleases me as it does you—this you suspected. This you said in your heart, you did not say it to your equal, but I heard it, when you said it in your heart. What is worse, they go forth into these words, so that they do not even fear to be heard.

**5.** Therefore *you suspected iniquity, that I would be like you. I will reprove you*[^10]. When you do not think it, and when you do not expect it, *I will reprove you.* I am silent when I spare, but I will not be silent when I judge. *I will reprove you.* And what will I do to you when I reprove you? *I will set you before your own face*[^11]. For now when you do evil, you think you are good, because you do not wish to see yourself. You reprove others, you do not look at yourself; you accuse others, you do not think about yourself; you set others before your eyes, you put yourself behind your back. I, when I reprove you, do the contrary. I take you from your back, and set you before your eyes. You will see yourself, and you will lament yourself. There will then be no way for you to correct yourself. You despise, then, the time of mercy; the time of judgment will come. For you have sung to me in church, *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord*[^12]. From our mouth it goes forth, everywhere the churches of Christ resound, *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord.* It is the time of mercy, that we be corrected; the time of judgment has not yet come. There is room, there is space; we have sinned, let us be corrected. The way is not yet finished, the day is not yet closed, the last breath is not yet drawn. Let there not be despair, which is worse, since on account of those very human and tolerable sins, the more frequent the smaller they are, God established in the Church times of mercy to be dispensed, a daily medicine, that we may say, *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^13]; that with these words, our face washed, we may approach the altar; that with these words, our face washed, we may communicate in the body and blood of Christ.

**6.** What is graver, people so despise this very medicine that they not only do not grant pardon when they are sinned against, but are unwilling even to ask it when they themselves sin. Temptation has entered, anger has crept in. Wrath has lorded it as much as it could, so that not only was the heart in tumult, but the tongue also vomited insults and accusations. Do you not see whither it has driven you? Do you not see whither it casts you down? See, correct it. Say, "I did wrong," say, "I have sinned." For you will not die when you have said it, rather you will die if you do not say it. Believe—not me, but God. What am I? I am a man, I am your equal, I bear flesh, I am weak: let us all believe God. Attend to yourselves. The Lord Christ himself says—attend to yourselves—*If your brother sins, reprove him between you and him alone. If he hears you, you have gained your brother. If he does not hear you, take with you two or three. For in the mouth of two or three witnesses every word shall stand. If he does not hear them, refer it to the Church. If he does not hear even the Church, let him be to you as a heathen and a publican*[^14]. A heathen is a gentile. The gentile is he who does not believe in Christ. If he does not hear even the Church, count him dead. But behold he lives, behold he enters, behold he signs himself, behold he bends the knee, behold he prays, behold he approaches the altar. Let him be to you as a heathen and a publican. Do not attend in him to these false signs of one living. He is dead. Whence does one live, how does one live, if even this medicine is despised? If I say to someone in your sight, "You did this," he will afterward answer, "How much was it? Let him admonish me within, let him say to me within that I did wrong, let me see my sin within. Why does he reprove me before the people?" What if you did it and did not correct it? What if you did it and still persevere? What if you did it, and still seem to yourself in your heart to have done well? Because he keeps silence, are you just? Because he does not now take vengeance, have you done no evil? Do you not fear, *I will reprove you?* Do you not fear, *I will set you before your own face?*[^15] Do you not fear?

**7.** "But the judgment is far off," you say. First, who told you that the day of judgment is far off? Is it that, if the day of judgment is far off, your own day is far off? Whence do you know when it is? Have not many healthy ones fallen asleep and grown stiff in death? Do we not carry our falls with us in this flesh? Are we not more fragile than if we were of glass? For glass, though it is fragile, yet, kept, lasts a long time, and you find cups from grandfathers and great-grandfathers, in which grandsons and great-grandsons drink. So great a fragility, kept, has become aged. But we men walk fragile under so many daily falls. And if those sudden falls do not happen, yet we are not able to live long. Human life is wholly brief. From infancy to decrepit old age it is wholly brief. If Adam were still living and were to die today, what would the length of life have profited him? Add to this, that the very day, which as it were naturally grows hot with disease taken in, is uncertain. Daily people die. And those who live carry them out, celebrate the funerals, and promise themselves life. No one says, "I will correct myself, lest tomorrow I be what this man is whom I have carried out." Words please you, I seek deeds. Do not sadden me by your depraved ways, because my delight in this life is nothing but your good life.
