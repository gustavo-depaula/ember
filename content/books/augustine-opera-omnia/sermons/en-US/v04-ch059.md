# Sermon 137

**1.** Your faith is not ignorant, dearly beloved, and we know that you have so learned, with the Master from heaven teaching, in whom you have set your hope, that our Lord Jesus Christ, who has already suffered for us and risen, is the head of the Church, and the Church is his body, and in his body the unity of the members and the joining of love exists, as it were, as health. But whoever grows cold in love is sick in the body of Christ. But he who has already exalted our head is able to heal even the weak members: provided only that they not be cut off by excessive impiety, but cling to the body until they are healed. For whatever still clings to the body is not of desperate health; but what has been cut off can neither be cured nor healed. Since, then, he is the head of the Church, and the Church is his body, the whole Christ is both head and body. He has already risen. We have, then, a head in heaven. Our head intercedes for us. Our head, without sin and without death, now propitiates God for our sins; that we too, rising at the end, and changed into heavenly glory, may follow our head. For where the head is, there too the other members. But while we are here, we are members; let us not despair, because we are going to follow our head.

**2.** For see, brothers, the love of our head himself. He is now in heaven, and here he labors, as long as the Church labors here. Here Christ hungers, here he thirsts, is naked, is a guest, is sick, is in prison. For whatever his body suffers here, he said that he himself suffers: and at the end, separating his body to the right hand, and the rest by whom he is now trampled separating to the left, he will say to those who are at the right hand: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the origin of the world*. By what merits? *For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*; and he so continues, as though he himself had received it: so much so that those who do not understand answer and say: *Lord, when did we see you hungry, a guest, and in prison?* And he says to them: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*. So too in our body the head is above, the feet are on the earth: yet in some crowding and pressing of men, when someone treads on your foot, does not the head say: You tread on me? No one trod on your head, nor on your tongue: it is above, it is in safety, nothing evil happened to it; and yet because through the connection of love there is unity from head to foot, the tongue did not separate itself from there, but said: You tread on me; when no one touched it. As, then, the tongue, which no one touched, says: You tread on me; so Christ the head, whom no one treads on, said: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*. And to those who did not do this, he said: *I was hungry, and you did not give me to eat*. And how did he conclude? Thus: *Those will go into the eternal burning, but the just into eternal life* [^1].

**3.** Since, then, the Lord just now spoke, he said that he is the shepherd, he said that he is also the door. You have both there; both: *I am the door*; and: *I am the shepherd* [^2]. The door is in the head, the shepherd in the body. For he says to Peter, in whom alone he forms the Church: *Peter, do you love me?* He answered: *Lord, I love you. Feed my sheep*. And a third time: *Peter, do you love me?* Peter was saddened, because he asked him a third time [^3]: as though he who saw the conscience of the denier did not see the faith of the confessor. He always knew him, knew him even when Peter himself did not know himself. For then he did not know himself, when he said: *I will be with you even to death*; and he did not know how weak he was. Just as very often indeed it happens even with the sick, that the sick man does not know what is going on in him, but the physician knows; while the one suffers the sickness itself, the physician does not suffer it. The physician says better what is going on in another, than the one who is sick says what is going on in himself. Peter, then, was then weak, but the Lord was the physician. The one said he had strength, who did not have it; but the other, touching the vein of his heart, said that he would deny him three times. And so it came to pass as the physician foretold, not as the sick man presumed [^4]. Therefore after his resurrection the Lord questioned him, not ignorant of the mind in which he confessed the love of Christ, but that by the threefold confession of love, he might blot out the threefold denial of fear.

**4.** Therefore the Lord demands this of Peter: *Peter, do you love me?* as though: What will you give me, what will you bestow on me, because you love me? What was Peter going to bestow on the Lord rising and going to heaven, and sitting at the right hand of the Father? As though he said: This you will give me, this you will bestow on me, if you love me, that you feed my sheep; that you enter through the door, not ascend by another part. You heard, when the Gospel was read: *He who enters through the door is the shepherd; but he who ascends by another part is a thief and a robber; and seeks to scatter, and to disperse, and to take away* [^5]. Who is it who enters through the door? He who enters through Christ. Who is this? He who imitates the passion of Christ, who knows the humility of Christ; and since God was made man for us, let man, knowing himself, acknowledge himself to be not God, but man. For he who wants to seem God, when he is man, does not imitate him who, when he was God, was made man. But it is not said to you: Be something less than you are; but: Know what you are. Know yourself weak, know yourself man, know yourself a sinner; know that he justifies, know that you are stained. Let the stain of your heart appear in your confession, and you will belong to the flock of Christ. Because the confession of sins invites the physician who will heal; as in sickness one who says: I am well, does not seek the physician. Had not that Pharisee and the Publican gone up to the temple? The one gloried in his health, the other showed his wounds to the physician. For the one said: *God, I give you thanks, that I am not like this Publican*. He gloried over the other. Therefore if that Publican were well, the Pharisee would envy him; because he would have no one over whom to lift himself up. How then had he come, who so envied? He was surely not well: and while he said he was well, he did not go down cured. But the other, casting his eyes to the ground, and not daring to lift them to heaven, beat his breast, saying: *God, be merciful to me a sinner*. And what does the Lord say? *Amen I say to you, that the Publican went down justified from the temple, more than the Pharisee: because everyone who exalts himself will be humbled; and he who humbles himself will be exalted* [^6]. Those, then, who exalt themselves, want to ascend to the sheepfold by another part: but those who humble themselves, enter through the door to the sheepfold. Therefore of the one he said: *enters*; of the other: *ascends*. He who ascends, you see, who seeks the heights, does not enter, but falls. But he who submits himself, that he may enter through the door, does not fall, but is a shepherd.

**5.** But the Lord named three persons, and we ought to investigate them in the Gospel: of the shepherd, the hireling, and the thief. When it was read, I think you noticed, he designated the shepherd, designated the hireling, designated the thief. He said the shepherd lays down his soul for the sheep and enters through the door. He said the thief and robber ascends by another part. He said the hireling, if he sees a wolf or even a thief, flees; because he has no care for the sheep: for he is a hireling, not a shepherd. The one enters through the door, because he is a shepherd; the other ascends by another part, because he is a thief; the other, seeing those who want to take the sheep, fears and flees, because he is a hireling, because he has no care for the sheep; for he is a hireling. If we find these three persons, Your Holiness has found both whom you should love, and whom you should tolerate, and whom you should beware. The shepherd is to be loved, the hireling is to be tolerated, the robber is to be bewared. There are men in the Church, of whom the Apostle says, who evangelize from occasion, seeking from men their own advantages [^7], whether of money, or of honors or human praise. In whatever way wanting to receive gifts, they evangelize, and seek not so much the salvation of him to whom they announce, as their own advantage. But he who hears salvation from one who does not have salvation, if he believes him whom that one announces, and does not set his hope in the one through whom salvation is announced to him: the one who announces will have loss; the one to whom it is announced will have gain.

**6.** You have the Lord saying of the Pharisees: *They sit on the chair of Moses* [^8]. The Lord did not signify them alone: as though indeed he would send those who believed in Christ to the school of the Jews, that there they might learn how the way is to the kingdom of heaven. Did not the Lord come for this, to establish the Church, and to separate the very Jews who believed well and hoped well and loved well, as grains from chaff, and make one wall of the circumcision, to which would be joined another wall from the uncircumcision of the Gentiles, of which two walls coming from different sides he himself would be the cornerstone? Did not the same Lord, then, say of these two peoples to be one: *I have other sheep too, which are not of this fold?* But he was speaking to the Jews: *I must*, he says, *bring them too, that there may be one flock, and one shepherd* [^9]. Therefore there were two boats, from which he had called the disciples. They signified those two peoples, when they cast their nets, and raised so great a force and so great a number of fishes, that the nets were almost broken: *And they filled*, he says, *two boats* [^10]. The two boats signified one Church, but made of two peoples, joined in Christ, although coming from different sides. This too the two wives signify, having one husband Jacob, Leah and Rachel [^11]. These two also the two blind men signify, who sat beside the way, whom the Lord enlightened [^12]. And if you attend to the Scriptures, in many places you will find two Churches signified, which are not two, but one. For to this the cornerstone avails, that from two it may make one. To this that shepherd avails, that from two flocks he may make one. Therefore the Lord, going to teach the Church, and to have his school apart from the Jews, as we now see, was he going to send those believing in him to the Jews, that they might learn? But by the name of Pharisees and Scribes he signified certain men who would be in his Church, who would say, and not do: but he had figured himself in the person of Moses. For Moses bore his person, and for that reason set a veil before himself, when he spoke to the people; because, as long as they in the Law were given over to carnal joys and pleasures, and sought an earthly kingdom, a veil was set against their face, lest they see Christ in the Scriptures. For the veil being taken away, after the Lord suffered, the secrets of the temple were seen. Therefore when he hung on the cross, the veil of the temple was torn from top to bottom [^13]; and the apostle Paul openly says: *But when you pass over to Christ, the veil will be taken away*. But he who does not pass over to Christ, although he reads Moses, *a veil is set over his heart* [^14], as the Apostle says. Since, then, the Lord prefigured that there would be certain such men in his Church, what did he say? *The Scribes and Pharisees sit on the chair of Moses; what they say, do; but what they do, do not do* [^15].

**7.** When evil clergy hear that which is said against them, they want to pervert it. For I have heard some wanting to pervert this saying. Would they not, if it were permitted them, blot it out of the Gospel? But because they cannot blot it out, they seek to pervert it. But the grace and mercy of the Lord is present, nor does it allow them to do so; because he has fenced round all his sayings with his truth, and balanced them; so that whoever wants to cut off anything from them, or to bring in error by reading or interpreting badly, he who has a heart may join what was cut off from the Scripture to the Scripture, and read what is above or below, and find the sense which that one wanted to interpret badly. What, then, do you suppose those say of whom it is said: *What they say, do?* Because indeed it is said to the laity. For a layman who wants to live well, when he attends to an evil cleric, what does he say to himself? The Lord said: *What they say, do; what they do, do not do*. Let me walk the way of the Lord, let me not follow the conduct of that man. Let me hear from him, not his words, but God's. Let me follow God, let him follow his own desire. Because if I want to defend myself before God thus, so as to say: Lord, I saw that cleric of yours living badly, and therefore I lived badly; will he not say to me: Wicked servant; had you not heard from me: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do?* But the evil layman, the unbeliever, not belonging to the flock of Christ, not belonging to the wheat of Christ, who is tolerated as chaff on the threshing floor, what does he say to himself, when the word of God begins to reprove him? Get out: why do you speak to me? The bishops themselves, the clergy themselves do not do it, and do you force me to do it? He seeks for himself not a patron for an evil cause, but a companion to punishment. For never will that one defend him on the day of judgment, whatever evil man he wants to imitate. For as the devil seduces all whom he seduces, not those with whom he may reign, but those with whom he may be condemned: so all who follow the evil seek companions for themselves to hell, not patronages to the kingdom of heaven.

**8.** How then do these pervert this saying, when it is said to them living badly: Rightly was it said by the Lord: *What they say, do; what they do, do not do?* It was rightly said, they say. For it was said to you, that what we say, you should do; but what we do, you should not do. For we offer sacrifice, it is not lawful for you. See the cunning of men: what shall I say? of hirelings. For if they were shepherds, they would not say these things. Therefore the Lord, to shut their mouth, went on, and said: *They sit on the chair of Moses: what they say, do; but what they do, do not do; for they say, and do not do*. What is it, then, brothers? If he were speaking of offering sacrifice; would he say: *For they say, and do not do?* For they do offer sacrifice, they offer to God. What is it that they say, and do not do? Hear what follows: *For they bind heavy and unbearable burdens, and lay them upon the necks of men, which they themselves refuse to touch with one finger* [^16]. Openly he reproached, described and showed. But they, when they want so to pervert the saying, show that they seek nothing in the Church, except their own advantages; nor have they read the Gospel: for if they knew that very page, and had read the whole, they would never dare to say this.  
But attend more openly, that the Church has such men. Lest anyone say to us: He said it utterly of the Pharisees, said it of the Scribes, said it of the Jews; for the Church does not have such men. Who, then, are those, of whom all the Lord says: *Not everyone who says to me: Lord, Lord, will enter the kingdom of heaven?* And he added: *Many will say to me in that day: Lord, Lord, did we not prophesy in your name, and in your name do many mighty works, and in your name eat and drink?* Do the Jews do these things in the name of Christ? Surely it is manifest that he speaks of those who have the name of Christ. But what follows? *Then I will say to them: I never knew you. Depart from me, all you who work iniquity* [^17]. Hear the Apostle groaning about such men. He says that some announce the Gospel through love, others from occasion; of whom he says: *They announce the Gospel not rightly* [^18]. A right thing, but they themselves not right. What they announce is right; but those who announce are not right. Why is he not right? Because he seeks something else in the Church, he does not seek God. If he sought God, he would be chaste; because the soul has God as its lawful husband. Whoever seeks from God anything besides God, does not seek God chastely. See, brothers; if a wife loves her husband because he is rich, she is not chaste. For she does not love the husband, but the husband's gold. But if she loves the husband, she loves him even naked, loves him poor. For if she loves him for this reason, because he is rich; what, if (as are human mishaps) he is proscribed and suddenly remains needy? She renounces him perhaps; because what she loved was not the husband, but his property. But if she truly loves the husband, she even loves him more poor; because she loves him with mercy.

**9.** And yet, brothers, our God can never be poor. He is rich, he himself made all things, heaven and earth, sea and angels. Whatever we see, whatever we do not see in heaven, he himself made. But yet we ought not to love riches, but him who made them. For he promised you nothing except himself. Find something more precious, and this he will give you. The earth is beautiful, heaven and the angels, but more beautiful is he who made these things. Those, then, who announce God, loving God; who announce God on account of God, feed the sheep, and are not hirelings. This very chastity our Lord Jesus Christ demanded of the soul, when he said to Peter: *Peter, do you love me?* What is: *Do you love me?* Are you chaste? Is your heart not adulterous? You seek not your own things in the Church, but mine? If, then, you are such, and love me, *feed my sheep* [^19]. For you will not be a hireling, but you will be a shepherd.  
But those announced not chastely, over whom the Apostle groans. But what does he say? *For what then? whether in every way, by occasion, or in truth, Christ is announced*. He allowed, then, hirelings to be. The shepherd in truth announces Christ, the hireling announces Christ from occasion, seeking something else. Yet both the one announces Christ, and the other Christ. Hear the voice of the shepherd Paul: *Whether by occasion, or in truth, Christ is announced* [^20]. The shepherd himself was willing to have a hireling. For they do where they can, they are useful as much as they can. But when the Apostle sought for other uses, one whose ways the weak might imitate: *I have sent you*, he says, *Timothy, that he may remind you of my ways*. And what does he say? I have sent a shepherd to remind you of my ways; that is, who walks as I walk. And sending a shepherd, what does he say? *For I have no one so likeminded, who with sincere affection is anxious for you*. Were there not many with him? But what follows? *For all seek their own, not the things of Jesus Christ* [^21]; that is: I wanted to send you a shepherd; for many are hirelings, but it was not fitting that a hireling be sent. For other matters and business to be carried out a hireling is sent: but for those things which Paul then wanted, a shepherd was necessary. And he scarcely found one shepherd among many hirelings: because few are shepherds, many are hirelings. But what is said of hirelings? *Amen I say to you, they have received their reward* [^22]. But of the shepherd what does the Apostle say? *But whoever cleanses himself from such things, will be a vessel sanctified unto honor, and useful to the Lord, ready for every good work always* [^23]. Not ready for some things, and not ready for some; but *ready for every good work*. These things I have said about shepherds.

**10.** But now we will speak of hirelings. *The hireling, when he sees the wolf lying in wait for the sheep, flees* [^24]. This the Lord said. Why? *Because he has no care for the sheep*. So long, then, is the hireling useful, as long as he does not see the wolf, as long as he does not see the thief and robber; but when he has seen them, he flees. And who of the hirelings is there who does not flee from the Church, when he sees the wolf and the robber? Wolves abound, robbers abound. They are those who ascend by another part. Who are these who ascend? Those of the party of Donatus who want to plunder the sheep of Christ, they ascend by another part. They do not enter through Christ: because they are not humble. Because they are proud, they ascend. What is: they ascend? They are lifted up. Whence do they ascend? By another part: whence they want to be called "of the party." Those who are not in unity are of another part, and from that very part they ascend, that is, are lifted up, and want to take the sheep. How they ascend, see. We, they say, sanctify, we justify, we make men just. Behold whither they have ascended. But he who exalts himself will be humbled [^25]. The Lord our God is able to humble them. But the wolf is the devil: he lies in wait to deceive, and those who follow him; for it was said, that clothed indeed in the skins of sheep, but within they are ravening wolves [^26]. If the hireling sees someone speak depraved things, or feel things according to the ruin of his soul, or do something wicked and obscene, and yet because he seems to have a person of some weight in the Church, from which if he hopes for advantages, he is a hireling; and when he sees a man perishing in sin, sees him following the wolf, sees his throat dragged by a bite to punishment; he does not say to him: You sin; he does not rebuke him, lest he lose his advantages. This, then, is: *When he sees the wolf, he flees*; he does not say to him: You act wickedly. This flight is not of the body, but of the soul. Whom you see standing in body, he flees in soul, when he sees the sinner, and does not say to him: You sin; when he even takes counsel with him.

**11.** My brothers, does not sometimes either a presbyter or a bishop ascend, and say nothing else from the higher place, except that others' goods not be seized, that frauds not be done, that crimes not be committed? They cannot say otherwise, who sit on the chair of Moses, and it speaks of them, not they. What is it, then: *Do they gather grapes from thorns, or figs from thistles?* and: *Every tree is known by its fruit* [^27]? Can a Pharisee speak good things? A Pharisee is a thorn: how do I gather a grape from a thorn? Because you, Lord, said: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do*. You bid me pluck a grape from thorns, when you said: *Do they gather a grape from thorns?* The Lord answers you: I did not bid you gather a grape from thorns; but see, attend well, lest perhaps, as is wont to happen, when a vine wanders round over the ground, it is entangled in thorns. For sometimes we find this, my brothers, a vine set over a sedge, because there it has a thorny hedge, it stretches out its tendrils, and inserts them into the thorny hedge, and the cluster hangs among the thorns; and he who sees the cluster plucks it, yet not from the thorns, but from the vine, which is entwined among the thorns. So then those are thorny; but by sitting on the chair of Moses, the vine entwines them, and clusters hang on them, that is, good words, good precepts. You pluck the grape, the thorn does not prick you, when you read: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do*. But the thorn pricks you, if you do what they do. So that you may pluck the grape, and not cling to the thorns: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do*. Their deeds are thorns, their words are the grape, but from the vine, that is, from the chair of Moses.  
These men, then, flee, when they see the wolf, when they see the robber. But I had begun to say this, that from the higher place these men can say nothing, except: Do well, do not perjure, do not defraud, do not circumvent anyone. But sometimes they so live, that about taking another's farm, counsel is taken with the bishop, and such counsel is asked of him. Sometimes it has happened to us, we speak from experience: for we would not believe it. Many ask of us evil counsels, counsels of lying, of circumventing; thinking that they please us. But in the name of Christ, if it please the Lord what we say, no such person has tempted us, and found in us what he wanted. Because if he wills, he who called us, we are shepherds, not hirelings. But what does the Apostle say? *To me it is a very small thing to be judged by you, or by a human day; but neither do I judge myself. For I am conscious of nothing in myself; but in this I am not justified. But he who judges me is the Lord* [^28]. Not for this reason is my conscience good, because you praise it. For what do you praise, which you do not see? Let him praise who sees; let him also correct, if he sees anything there which offends his eyes. Because we too do not say that we are perfectly well; but we beat our breast, and say to God: Be merciful, lest I sin. Yet I think—for before him I speak, seeking nothing else from you except your salvation—and we groan very often over the sins of our brothers, and we suffer violence, and are tortured in mind, and sometimes we rebuke them; rather we never fail to rebuke them. All who recall what I say are witnesses, how often sinning brothers have been rebuked by us, and vehemently rebuked.

**12.** Now I treat our counsel with Your Holiness. In the name of Christ you are the people of God, you are the Catholic people, you are the members of Christ: you are not divided from unity. You are in communion with the members of the Apostles, you are in communion with the memorials of the holy martyrs, spread through the whole world, and you belong to our care, that we may render a good account of you. But what our whole account is, you know. Lord, you know that I said it, you know that I did not keep silent, you know with what mind I said it, you know that I wept before you, when I said it, and was not heard. This, I think, is our whole account. For the Holy Spirit made us secure through the prophet Ezekiel. You know that reading about the watchman: *Son*, he says, *of man, I have set you a watchman for the house of Israel; if, when I say to the impious man: Impious one, you will die the death, you do not say it*; that is, for this I tell you, that you should say it; if you do not announce it: *And the sword comes, and takes him*, that is, what I threatened to the sinner; *he indeed, the impious one, will die in his impiety, but his blood I will require from the hand of the watchman*. Why? Because he did not say it: *But if the watchman sees the sword coming, and sounds the trumpet, that he may flee; and he does not take heed to himself*; that is, does not correct himself, so that the punishment which God threatens not find him; *and the sword comes, and takes someone; he indeed, the impious one, died in his iniquity: but you*, he says, *have freed your soul* [^29]. And in that place of the Gospel what else does he say to the servant? When he said: *Lord, I knew you to be a troublesome or hard man, that you reap where you did not sow, and gather where you did not scatter; and fearing I went away, and hid your talent in the earth: behold you have what is yours*. And he: *Servant*, he says, *wicked and slothful*, rather because *you knew me to be troublesome and hard, to reap where I did not sow, and gather where I did not scatter*, that very greed of mine ought to have taught you that I seek gain from my money. *You ought, then, to have given my money to the moneychangers, and I coming would demand with interest what is mine* [^30]. Did he say: You should give and demand? We, then, brothers, give; he will come who demands. Pray, that he may find us ready.
