# Sermon 60

A Treatise on Psalm 38 (39), Where It Says: "Although Man Walks in the Image, Yet He Is Troubled in Vain; He Stores Up, and Does Not Know for Whom He Gathers It," and from the Gospel of Matthew on Storing Up Treasure in Heaven

**1.** Every man set in some tribulation, and failing in his own cause, seeks some prudent person from whom he may receive counsel, and may know what to do. Let us suppose, then, that the whole world is, as it were, one man. He seeks to escape evils, but is loath to do good things: and through this, as his tribulations multiply, and he fails in his own cause, whom can he find more prudent for receiving counsel than Christ? Let him find a better, and do what he wishes. But if he has no one where he may find a better, let him come to this one, whom wherever he finds, let him consult, let him receive true counsel, let him keep the good commandment, let him escape a great evil. For present temporal evils, which men greatly shudder at and grieve over, under which they murmur most, and by murmuring offend the one who amends them, lest they find the one who saves them: present evils, then, are without doubt going to pass. Either they pass through us, or we pass through them; either they pass while we live, or they are left behind when we die. There is nothing great in a tribulation that is short in time. Whoever you are who think of the morrow, you do not call back yesterday. When the day after tomorrow comes, it will be yesterday and tomorrow. But if men burn with such cares to escape temporal and passing—or rather, swiftly flying—tribulations, what must be devised that a man may escape the abiding ones that endure without end?

**2.** Hard is the cause of mortals' life. What else is it to be born here, except to enter upon a laborious life? Of the greater labor to come, the very weeping of the infant is witness. From this troublesome banquet no one excuses himself. We must drink what Adam gave us to drink. We were made by the hands of truth, but on account of sin cast out into the days of vanity[^1]. We were made to the image of God[^2], but we wore it away by the transgression of sin. Therefore the psalm admonishes us how we were made, and to what we have come. For it says: *Although man walks in the image*; behold what he was made. To what has he come? Hear what follows: *Yet he is troubled in vain*[^3]. He walks in the image of truth, and is troubled by the counsel of vanity. Finally, see his being troubled; see, and as in a mirror, be displeased with yourself. *Although*, it says, *man walks in the image*; and therefore man is a great thing, *yet he is troubled in vain.* And as if we asked: Whence, I ask you, whence is he troubled in vain? *He stores up, and does not know for whom he gathers it*[^4]. Behold, that man is the whole human race, as it were one man who has failed in his own cause, has lost counsel, has strayed from the way of the sound mind. *He stores up, and does not know for whom he gathers it.* What more demented? What more unhappy? Surely for himself? No. Why not for himself? Because he is going to die, because man's life is short, because the treasure lasts, and the one who gathers it quickly passes away. Therefore, having pity on man walking in the image, confessing true things, pursuing vain things: *In vain*, it says, *he is troubled.* I grieve for him: *He stores up, and does not know for whom he gathers it.* For himself, then? No, because man dies while the treasure lasts. For whom, then? Have you counsel? Give it to me too. You do not have counsel to give me? Then neither do you hold it. Accordingly, if both of us do not have it, let us both seek it, let us both receive it.

**3.** So let us treat of it. You are troubled, you store up, you think, you labor, you keep watch. By day you are harassed with labors, by night you are agitated with fears. That your little purse may be filled with coins, your soul is feverish with cares. I see, I grieve: you are troubled, and as he says who cannot be deceived, in vain you are troubled. For you store up. That whatever you do may turn out well, that we may be silent about your losses, about such great perils and the single deaths for each single gain (deaths, I say, not of bodies, but of evil thoughts; that gold may come, faith perishes; that you be clothed without, you are stripped within)—so that we may pass over these, that we may be silent about others, that we may pass by the adverse things, that we may think only of the prosperous: behold, you store up, behold, gains flow in from every side, and like fountains the coins run; everywhere want is on fire, on every side abundance flows. Have you not heard: *If riches flow in, do not set your heart on them?*[^5] Behold, you acquire, you are not fruitlessly troubled, yet in vain you are troubled. Why, you say, am I troubled in vain? Behold, I fill my sacks, my walls scarcely hold what I acquire: why am I troubled in vain? Because you store up, and do not know for whom you gather it. Or if you know for whom, I beg you, tell me too. Let me hear you. For whom? If you are not troubled in vain, tell for whom you store up. For myself, you say. Do you dare to say this, who are going to die? For my sons, you say. Do you dare to say this about those who are going to die? Great piety: a father stores up for his sons; rather, great vanity: one who is going to die stores up for those who are going to die. If for this reason it is not for yourself, because, being going to die, you leave behind whatever you gather, this is the case with your sons too; they are going to succeed, not to abide. I forbear to say what kind of sons; lest perhaps what avarice gathered, luxury squander. Another loses by flowing it away, what you gathered by laboring. But I forbear this. Perhaps your sons will be good, will not be luxurious; they will keep what you left, they will increase what you kept, they will not lose what you gathered. Your sons are equally vain, if they do this, if they imitate you, their father, in this. To them I say what I was saying to you, to your son for whom you keep it I say, to him I say: You store up, and do not know for whom you gather it. For as you did not know, so neither does he know. If vanity has abided in him, has truth then failed him?

**4.** I forbear to say that perhaps, while you live, you store up for a thief. He comes in one night, and finds gathered what was gathered in so many days and nights. You store up perhaps for a robber, perhaps for a brigand. I do not wish to say more, lest I recall and rub raw the griefs of those who have suffered such things. How many things that empty vanity gathered, hostile cruelty has found ready! It is not for me to wish it, but for all to fear it. May God avert this. Let his scourges suffice. Let us all pray, may God avert this. May he spare us, whom we ask. But if he says, To whom? what do we answer? You, then, O man, O every man, you who store up in vain, what do you answer me, who treat with you and seek with you counsel in our common cause? For you said and answered: I store up for my son, for my sons, for my posterity. I have said how much is to be feared even in those sons. But suppose your sons will live otherwise than the enemy thinks: let them live as the father wishes. How many have failed in these causes, I have said, I have recalled: you shuddered, and did not correct yourself. For what will you answer me, except that you say: Perhaps not? And I have so spoken: Perhaps, I say, for a thief, perhaps for a robber, perhaps for a brigand. I did not say "certainly," but "perhaps." Between "perhaps it will happen" and "perhaps it will not happen": you do not know, then, what will happen, you are troubled in vain. You see how truly the Truth has spoken, how vainly vanity is troubled. You have heard, at last at some point you have grown wise, because by this very thing, when you say: Perhaps for my sons, you do not dare to say: I am certain it is for my sons. You do not know, then, for whom you gather it. So, as I see, what I said before, you have failed in your cause; you do not find what to answer me: but neither I what to answer you.

**5.** Let us both seek, let us both ask counsel. We have an abundance, not of a wise man, but of Wisdom itself. Let us both hear *Christ, to the Jews a scandal, but to the Gentiles foolishness; but to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the Power of God and the Wisdom of God*[^6]. Why do you prepare defenses for your riches? Hear the Power of God: nothing is stronger. Why do you prepare arguments for your riches? Hear the Wisdom of God: nothing is more prudent. Perhaps when I have spoken, you will be scandalized, you will be a Jew, because Christ is to the Jews a scandal. Perhaps when I have spoken, it will seem foolish to you, you will be a Gentile, because Christ is to the Gentiles foolishness. You are a Christian, you are called; but to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ is the Power of God and the Wisdom of God. Do not be saddened when I have spoken; do not be scandalized, do not, as it were, insult my foolishness with a twisted mouth. Let us hear. For what I am going to say, Christ has said. Do you despise the herald? Fear the judge. What, then, shall I say? Now the reader of the Gospel a little while ago has almost freed me from this care. I do not read, but recall what was read. You were seeking counsel, failing in your cause: see what the fountain of right counsel says, the fountain whence, whatever you draw, you do not fear poison: *Do not store up for yourselves treasures on earth, where moth and rust destroy, and where thieves dig through and steal: but store up for yourselves treasure in heaven, where no thief comes near, nor moth corrupts. Where your treasure will be, there will your heart be also*[^7]. What more do you await? The matter is open. The counsel is plain, but the desire is hidden; rather, it is not hidden, but even—what is worse—it too is open. For robbery does not cease to rage, avarice does not cease to defraud, malice does not cease to swear false oaths. All of it, for what? That treasure may be stored up. And where put? On earth. Rightly indeed, from earth to earth. For to the man—whom, I told you, the labor was given to drink—it was said as he sinned: *You are earth, and into earth you shall go*[^8]. Deservedly the treasure on earth, because the heart on earth. Where, then, is what we *have unto the Lord?*[^9] Grieve, you who have understood; be corrected, if you have grieved. How long to praise, and not to do? It is true, nothing is truer. Let what is true be done. To the one God we give praise, and are not changed, so that even in this we are troubled in vain.

**6.** Therefore: *Do not store up for yourselves treasure on earth*, whether you have experienced how what is stored on earth perishes, or have not experienced it but fear to experience it from others. Whom words do not correct, let experiences correct. One does not rise, one does not go forth, except that it is said by all with one voice: Woe to us, the world is collapsing. If it is collapsing, why do you not migrate? If an architect told you that your house was going to collapse, would you not migrate before you murmured? The Builder of the world tells you that the world is going to collapse. There is no one whom you may contradict. Hear the voice of the one foretelling, hear the counsel of the one warning. The voice of the one foretelling is: *Heaven and earth will pass away*[^10]. The voice of the one warning is: *Do not store up for yourselves treasure on earth*[^11]. So if you believe the one foretelling, if you do not despise the one warning, let what he says be done. For he who gave such counsel did not wish you to lose what you have, but rather warned you not to lose it. Why is it not heard, that we should migrate to heaven? For we shall not migrate into that heaven of which it was said: *Heaven and earth will pass away.* Otherwise, who would hear the counsel of one warning about ruin, from ruin to ruin? There are *heavens of heavens*[^12], as there is *Holy of holies*[^13], as there is *ages of ages*. *Store up for yourselves treasure in heaven*[^14]. *The heavens declare the glory of God*[^15]. Perhaps when you give to a just man, you give to heaven. But if you give to an unjust man—because *if your enemy hungers, give him food*[^16]—neither there do you go astray: for indeed you obey him who made heaven and earth. So migrate diligently. Have you much that you have gathered? Migrate the more. I do not wish piety to lose what vanity gathered. Migrate. You have whence the poor of Christ may abound. The calamity of the world has made many porters for you. We have spoken, and you have heard; rather, he has spoken, and we have heard together. May he who gave the counsel for correcting grant the help for doing. Turned to the Lord. Amen.
