# Sermon 305: On the Feast of the Martyr Lawrence

[Delivered at the Table of Saint Cyprian]

**1.** Your faith recognizes the grain that fell into the earth, and, having died, was multiplied. It recognizes, I say, this grain, your faith, because it itself dwells in your mind. For what Christ said of himself, no Christian doubts. But surely, with that grain dead and multiplied, many grains were scattered in the earth: of which is also blessed Lawrence, whose sowing we celebrate today. And from those grains scattered throughout the whole world, how great a crop has sprung up, we see, we rejoice, we are: if, however, we too by his grace belong to the storehouse. For not everything that is in the crop belongs to the storehouse. For the same rain, useful and nourishing, feeds both the wheat and the chaff. Far be it that both should be stored together in the storehouse; though both are nourished together in the field, and both are threshed together on the threshing floor. Now is the time of choosing. Before the winnowing comes, let there be the separation of ways: just as on the threshing floor the grain is still distinguished by cleansing, not yet separated by the final winnowing fan.

**2.** Hear me, holy grains, which I do not doubt are here; for if I doubt, I myself shall not be a grain: hear, I say, me; rather, hear first the grain through me. Do not love your souls in this age: do not love them, if you love them; that by not loving you may keep them: because by not loving you love them more. *He who loves his soul in this age will lose it*[^1]. A grain speaks, the grain that fell into the earth and was put to death that it might be multiplied speaks: let it be heard, because it does not lie. What it admonished, it itself did: it instructed by precept, it went before by example. Christ did not love his soul in this age; therefore he came, that he might lose it here, lay it down for us, and take it up again when he willed. But because he was so man that he was also God—for Christ is Word, soul, and flesh, true God and true man; but man without sin, who would take away the sin of the world—he was surely of greater power, so that he could truly say: *I have power to lay down my soul, and power to take it up again: no one takes it from me; but I lay it down of myself, and I take it up again*[^2]. Since, then, he was of so great power, why did he say: *Now my soul is troubled?*[^3] A God-man of so great power, why is he troubled, unless because in him is the image of our weakness? *I have power to lay down my soul, and power to take it up again*. When you hear this from Christ, he is in himself; when, I say, you hear this from Christ, he is in himself: when his soul, with death drawing near, is troubled, he is in you. For his body, the Church, would not be, unless he were also in us.

**3.** Attend then to Christ: *I have power to lay down my soul, and power to take it up again: no one takes it from me*[^4]. *I slept*: for he says this in the Psalm: *I slept*. As if to say: Why do they rage? why do they exult? why are the Jews tossed with joy, as if they themselves had done something? *I slept. I*, he says, I who have power to lay down my soul, by laying it down *slept, and took my rest*[^5]. And since he had power to take it up again, he added: *And I rose up*. But giving glory to the Father: *Because the Lord*, he says, *upheld me*[^6]. These words, where he says: *Because the Lord upheld me*, let them not so meet your minds as if Christ himself did not raise his own body. The Father raised him, and he himself also raised himself. Whence shall we teach that he raised himself too? Recall what he said to the Jews: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up*[^7]. So understand Christ to have been born of a virgin by power, not by condition, but by power: dead by power, dead thus by power. He used to good purpose those who knew not, and turned the raging mad people to the use of his own power for our blessedness, and in those by whom he was being killed he saw his own who would live with him: and seeing them in the mad people still mad: *Father*, he said, *forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^8]. I, he says, I the physician touch the vein, from the wood I examine the sick; I hang, and I touch; I die, and I give life; I pour out blood, and from it I make for my enemies a medicine of salvation. They rage and pour it out: they will believe and drink.

**4.** So Christ himself, our Lord and Savior, the head of the Church, born of the Father without a mother; he himself, I say, our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, as far as concerns himself, laid down his soul by power, took it up again by power. To this power it does not properly belong: *My soul is troubled*. He transfigured us into himself; he saw us, he looked upon us, he took up and cherished us in our weariness; lest perhaps, when the last day came to some member of his, on which this life was to be ended, that member should be troubled through weakness, and despair of salvation, and say that it did not belong to Christ, because it was not so prepared for death that no disturbance arose in it, no sadness clouded its most devoted mind. So, since his members would be imperiled by despair, when, with death drawing near, someone was troubled, unwilling to end a wretched life, sluggish to begin one never to be ended: lest then they be broken by despair, he turned his attention to his own weak ones, he gathered his own last and not very strong members into his bosom, he covered his not very strong ones like a hen her chicks; and he as it were addresses them: *Now my soul is troubled*[^9]. Recognize yourselves in me, so that when perhaps you are troubled, you may not despair, but call back your gaze to your head, and say to yourselves: When the Lord said: *My soul is troubled*, we were in him, we were signified. We are troubled, but we do not perish. *Why are you sad, my soul? and why do you trouble me?*[^10] Do you not wish a wretched life to be ended? It is the more wretched, the more even a wretched thing is loved, and you do not wish it ended: it would be less wretched, if it were not loved. What is the blessed life like, when a wretched life is so loved, only because it is called life? *Why* then *are you sad, my soul? and why do you trouble me?* You have what to do. Have you failed in yourself? *Hope in the Lord*[^11]. Are you troubled in yourself? *Hope in the Lord*, who chose you before the foundation of the world, who predestined you, who called you, who justified you the impious, who promised you everlasting glorification, who for you bore a death not owed, who for you poured out his blood, who transfigured you into himself, when he said: *My soul is troubled*. Do you belong to him, and do you fear? And will the world do you any harm, for which he died, through whom the world was made? Do you belong to him, and do you fear? *If God is for us, who is against us? He who did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all; how has he not also with him given us all things?*[^12] Resist disturbances, do not consent to the love of the age. It tickles, it flatters, it lies in wait: let it not be believed, let Christ be held.
