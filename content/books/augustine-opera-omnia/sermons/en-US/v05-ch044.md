# Sermon 179/A

**1.** A terrible reading sounded in our ears before the Psalm, since: *Whoever keeps the whole law, but offends in one point, has become guilty of all*[^1]. In so great a peril, then, who would not cry out, *Have mercy on us, Lord, have mercy on us*[^2]? For without his mercy subtracted from us, we will be able to mention the peril itself more vehemently, and more vehemently to commend it. *Do not enter into judgment with your servant*, says a certain man, holy indeed, but yet a man; *Do not enter into judgment with your servant, for no living thing will be justified in your sight*[^3]. He did not wish, then, that one enter into judgment with him. *But judgment without mercy to the one who does not do mercy*. By the contrary let us understand the other sentence, and let us see where we ought to place hope. For if *judgment without mercy to the one who does not do mercy*, without doubt judgment with mercy to the one who does mercy. This sentence the apostle James brought forth, from whose Epistle the same terrible reading was recited, that *judgment is without mercy to the one who does not do mercy*[^4]. But the contrary one, which we have said—judgment with mercy to the one who does mercy—the Lord expressed, where he says, *Blessed are the merciful, for they will obtain mercy*[^5]. There is hope, then, in the mercy of God, if our misery is not dry of the work of mercy. What do you seek from the Lord? Mercy. *Give, and it will be given to you*. What do you seek from the Lord? Pardon. *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you*[^6].

**2.** Yet it is wont to be asked how this is to be taken: *Whoever keeps the whole law, but offends in one point, has become guilty of all*[^7]. For it must be feared and guarded against, lest we suppose a thief is to be compared with a murderer or an adulterer; for this error is dangerous and troublesome; there is much difference between theft and murder. But it is objected to us: Surely, then: *He who keeps the whole law, but offends in one point, has become guilty of all*. He keeps himself from murder, keeps from adultery, keeps from false testimony, keeps from idolatry, keeps from any condemnable sacrilege. He has stolen; will he now be held both murderer and adulterer and false witness and idolater and sacrilegious, because he committed theft? Surely not, then, the one who offends in one point has become guilty of all. But expounding this very thing, the same apostle whose epistle it is, added and said, *For he who said: You shall not commit adultery, said also: You shall not kill. But if you do not kill, but you commit adultery, you have become a transgressor of the law*[^8]. When he had set this peril before our eyes, and all had trembled, as if to console he added, *So speak and so act, as those who are going to be judged by the law of liberty. For judgment is without mercy to the one who does not do mercy*[^9]. Whether, then, we can unfold the knot of this question, or whether our effort perhaps succumbs to its difficulty, and either I cannot worthily think what I should say, or perhaps cannot say what I have been able to think—be fervent in works of mercy. This is plain, this is open, this does not need an expositor, but a hearer and a doer. Yet I will try somehow to open that too; let your prayer help us before God. It is indeed the time of hearing, not of praying; but if you await from God what I am going to say, you pray.

**3.** The law contains many precepts; and indeed that law which is called the *Decalogue* has ten precepts. But the ten precepts are as it were general, to which all the rest, innumerable, are referred. Yet how the other precepts, which seem innumerable, are reduced to this fewness of the number ten, it is endless to discuss. But in this way, as far as the Lord helps, we shall be able to show that, just as all the other precepts are referred to these—which I said is most laborious to discuss—so these ten are referred to two. The ten are these. About worshiping the one God, not worshiping another, there is one precept: *You shall not make for yourself an idol, nor the likeness of anything, neither what is in heaven above, nor what is on earth below*. *The Lord your God is one Lord*. The second precept is: *You shall not take in vain the name of the Lord your God*. The third precept is: *Observe the day of the sabbaths*. These precepts pertain to God. About the observance of the sabbath it is as it were superfluous to teach a Christian anything. Rather, not only is it not superfluous, but this is solid; for that is a shadow. For the people are forbidden from servile works on the sabbath. Are we not forbidden from servile works? Hear the Lord: *Everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin*[^10]. Yet this very thing—to hope from God that we not commit sin—this is to keep sabbath; for it is therefore written, *God rested on the seventh day from all his works*[^11]. God rested: God makes you rest. For how would God rest, where he labored, who created all things by a word? Therefore these three pertain to God. The other seven pertain to man: *Honor father and mother; you shall not commit adultery; you shall not kill; you shall not bear false witness; you shall not steal; you shall not covet your neighbor's wife; you shall not covet your neighbor's property*[^12]. If you love God, you neither worship another, nor take his name in vain, and you keep sabbath for him, that he may rest in you, when he makes you rest. But if you love your neighbor, and honor your parents, and do not commit adultery, and do not kill, and harm no one by false testimony, and steal from no one, and covet no one's wife, and covet no one's property. And through this: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind*; and: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*. *On these two precepts the whole Law and the Prophets are fulfilled*[^13]. Hear the Apostle too: *The fullness*, he says, *of the law is love*[^14]. He did not send you into many things, nor into ten, nor into two: one love fulfills all. But love itself is twofold: toward God and toward neighbor. Toward God, how much? Wholly. With what *whole*? For not with the ear, and the nose, and the hand, and the foot. Whence can he be loved wholly? *With the whole heart, with the whole soul, with the whole mind*; with the whole that lives in you, you shall love the fountain of life. If, then, with the whole that lives in me I ought to love God, what do I leave for myself with which to love my neighbor? For about loving the neighbor, when it was commanded you, it was not said to you: With the whole heart, and with the whole soul, and with the whole mind; but *as yourself*. God with the whole of you, because he is better than you; your neighbor as yourself, because this is what you are.

**4.** There are, then, two precepts; yet three are to be loved. Two precepts were given: love God and love your neighbor; yet three I see to be loved. For it would not be said, *and your neighbor as yourself*, unless you loved yourself too. Therefore if three are to be loved, why are there two precepts? Why? Hear. God did not judge that you must be admonished to love yourself; for there is no one who does not love himself. But because many, by loving themselves badly, lose themselves, by saying to you that you love your God with the whole of you, there was given you a rule how to love yourself. Do you wish to love yourself? Love God with the whole of you; for there you will find yourself, lest in yourself you lose yourself. If you love yourself in yourself, you are going to fall both away from yourself, and you will seek many things outside yourself. Whence the Apostle began from that head all evils, where he says, *For men will be lovers of themselves*. Behold, you have chosen to love yourself; let us see whether you remain even in yourself. It is false, you do not remain there; fallen from God, you fall away from yourself too. For there is the firmament; there you ought to have cleaved, to make that for yourself a fortified place and a house of refuge. But now you have loosed, or rather broken, the bond of your love from him to yourself; you do not remain even in yourself. Hear finally the Apostle himself. When he had said, *For men will be lovers of themselves*, he at once added: *lovers of money*[^15]. Did I not say that you would not remain even in yourself? Surely you are not money? Behold, you have perished even from yourself, you who withdrew from God. What remains, but that you pour out the whole patrimony of your mind, living prodigally with harlots—that is, with lusts and diverse cupidities—and from want be compelled to feed swine—that is, because unclean greed has possessed you, let unclean demons be fed from you? But that son, having suffered want, and worn down by hunger, *returning to himself, said*. Returning to himself, because he had perished from himself, and in himself found himself in want; everywhere he sought happiness, and nowhere found it. Returning to himself, what did he say? *I will rise and go*. Where shall I go? *To the father*[^16]. Now returned to himself, but still lying down, *I will rise and go*; I will not lie down, I will not remain. So a rule has been established for you, how to love yourself; love one better than you, and you have loved yourself. But I say better by nature, not by will. For many men are found better than you by will, but God alone by nature; creator, founder, maker, made by none. There fix yourself. Understand at last, and say: *But for me*. What, but for you? *To cleave to God is good*[^17]. Why this? Attend to what he said above: *You have destroyed everyone who fornicates away from you*. Because, then, he destroyed everyone who fornicates away from him, you found yourself. *But for me to cleave to God is good*—that is, not to fornicate away from God, not to take myself away from God. But do you wish to see in this matter what is promised you? *But he who cleaves to the Lord is one spirit*[^18]. This, then, is your love, or the love of yourself—that is, the love by which you love yourself, that you may love God. Now I commit to you the neighbor too, whom you should love as yourself; for I see that you have begun to love yourself. The one, then, whom you love as yourself, lead where you have led yourself. For if you loved gold and had gold, and loved your neighbor as yourself, by loving you would divide what you had, and make him a partaker of your gold. But by dividing you would both have less. Why, then, do you not possess that One, where you will suffer no straits with a co-heir? However many you can persuade, however many you can invite, call, compel to love God, he is both wholly for all, and wholly for each.

**5.** Love God, then, and love your neighbor as yourself. For I see that you love yourself, because you love God. Love is the root of all good works. For just as *the root of all evils is cupidity*, so the root of all goods is love. *The fullness, then, of the law is love*[^19]. So I quickly say: he who offends against love has become guilty of all. For he who offends against the root itself, what of the tree does he leave unoffended? What, then, do we do? He who offends against love has become guilty of all; it is wholly true. But the thief offends against love in one way, the adulterer in another, the murderer in another, the sacrilegious in another, the blasphemer in another. All offend against the same love, because, where there is full and perfect love, there can be no sin. But it is love that grows in us, that it may at some time be perfected; and so be perfected that there is nothing further to be added. When it has been so perfected that there is no further whither it may grow, no further whither it may be heaped up, there will be no sins. But where will this be, except when death is swallowed up in victory? For there it will be said, because there will be no sin at all: *Where, O death, is your struggle? Where, O death, is your sting*? Where is it? Behold, it is not; behold, now you do not prick, you do not lay low. *Where, O death, is your sting*? And what is: Where is your sting? Hear him expound: *But the sting of death is sin*[^20]. Therefore, brothers, he who commits lesser sins is guilty of all; and he who commits some greater and criminal sin is guilty of all. But the latter is more guilty, the former less guilty; yet both guilty of all, so that in the diversity of sins nothing is sought but the greater and the less. He committed theft: if he had love, he would not commit theft; *for, you shall not commit adultery*, says the Apostle, *you shall not commit murder, you shall not steal, you shall not covet, and if there is any other commandment, it is summed up in this saying; you shall love your neighbor as yourself. The love of neighbor works no evil. But the fullness of the law is love*[^21]. Therefore both he who commits theft is guilty of all, because he wounded love; and he who commits adultery is guilty of all, because he wounded love. For how did he not violate all things, who wounded the root? But the latter wounded less, the former wounded more.

**6.** Yet let even the one who wounded less not be slothful: *For judgment is without mercy to the one who does not do mercy*[^22]. All sins are forgiven to the baptized faithful; absolutely none is left which that sanctification has not first removed and extinguished. It is true: who doubts it? But if one then migrated from the body, at once innocence, as it had begun, would remain; but because one lives here, the iniquity is blotted out, but the infirmity remains. Through certain very thin little cracks of this infirmity and as it were of human frailty there enters from the sea what flows together into the bilge. He did not commit murder: as a shipwrecked man he escaped the wave. But do you not know that a neglected bilge drowns? On account of the greater waves, then, and on account of any creeping moisture, the sacrament of baptism washes all away; but on account of the frailty of this ship, which is borne over a long sea, and is tossed by temptations as by storms, because it is necessary that certain, though lesser, sins creep in, another remedy has been given, because another sacrament of baptism could not be given. That is one; this is daily, which I said a little before: *Judgment without mercy to the one who does not do mercy. Give, and it will be given to you; forgive, and it will be forgiven you*[^23]. And in a way in your prayers you are daily cleansed from daily, light, and lesser sins, if you say from the soul, if you say truly, if you say faithfully, *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^24].

**7.** If you do not forgive your adversary, you are an adversary to yourself. Do you wish to see how much difference there is? For example, he wronged you by taking away money; you defraud yourself by not earning pardon. Finally you will say: He rages much, he seeks my blood. He, the blood of your flesh; you, the death of your soul. I do not forgive, he says; he wronged me much, he was much my adversary. You are worse to yourself. I do not forgive. I beg you, forgive, let it go. But he does not ask me. Do you ask for him. Plainly I do not forgive. You wish to litigate, and you do not know with whom you ought to. You love to litigate; return to yourself, be angry, but do not sin[^25]. Be angry with yourself, that you may not sin; rage against yourself, chastise yourself. You have within what to tame, and you sleep. You attend with whom you litigate outside—your neighbor, your consort, your companion, your fellow-possessor. You do not attend to her, you do not see, the other law in your members, fighting against the law of your mind, and taking you captive in the law of sin which is in your members. But he despoiled me. You are led captive, and you are angry with the despoiler! You have recognized yourself, you have seen where you are. You have known the captor, show yourself a fighter, seek the redeemer. Just as that very man, when he had said, *Taking me captive in the law of sin which is in my members*. *Unhappy man that I am*, he says, *who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^26]. But who calls upon grace, who does not see his own punishment? Understand, then, see where you are dragged. But righteousness delights me. I know that it delights you: for you delight in the law of God according to the inner man, but you see another law in your members; you delight in the law of God, there is another law in your members. By this you live, by this you die. Half-alive the robbers left you on the road, but now, lying down, you have been found by the passing and merciful Samaritan; wine and oil have been poured on you, you have received the sacrament of the Only-begotten; you have been lifted onto his beast, you have believed in Christ incarnate; you have been brought to the inn, you are cared for in the Church[^27].

**8.** Hence it is that I speak: this too I do, this we all do; we perform the office of innkeepers. To him it was said, *If you spend anything more, on returning I will repay you*[^28]. Would that we at least spend out what we have received! But however much we spend, brothers, it is the Lord's money. We are your fellow-servants: we live from where we feed you. Let no one impute the benefit to us; we will be bad servants if we do not do it; but if we do it, let there be no arrogance of ours, because we do it not from our own. Let us all love him, let us love him, on account of him let us love one another. We all have one king; let us all come to one kingdom.
