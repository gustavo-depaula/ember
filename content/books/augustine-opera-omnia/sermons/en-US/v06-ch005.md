# Sermon 188: On the Birthday of the Lord

**1. 1.** If we should attempt to praise the Son of God — as he is with the Father, equal and coeternal to him, in whom *all things were created in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible*[^1], the Word of God and God, *the life and the light of men*[^2] — it is no wonder that no human thought, no speech, suffices. For how could our tongue worthily praise him whom our heart is not yet able to see, where he set the eye by which he might be seen, if iniquity be purged, if weakness be healed, and they be made *blessed who are pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^3]? It is no wonder, I say, that we do not find words by which to speak of the one Word, in whom it was spoken that we should be[^4], we who might say something of him. For these words, thought and uttered, our mind forms, but by that Word our mind itself is formed. Nor does man make words in the same way that he himself was made through the Word: for neither did the Father beget the one Word in the same way that he made all things through the Word. For God begot God; but at once the one begetting and the one begotten are one God. But God made the world; the world passes away, and God remains. And just as the things that were made surely did not make themselves, so he was made by no one through whom all things could be made. It is no wonder, then, if man, made among all things, does not unfold in words the Word through whom all things were made.

**2. 2.** So let us turn our ears and minds here a little, to see whether we may be able to say something fitting and worthy, not from this, that *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^5], but from this, that *the Word was made flesh*: to see whether we may say by what means *he dwelt among us*[^6]; whether there he can be utterable where he willed to be visible. For on this account we celebrate this day, on which he deigned to be born of a virgin: a generation of his which he caused to be in some manner recounted by men. But in that eternity in which God was born of God, *his generation, who shall declare?*[^7] There no such day exists, to be solemnly celebrated. For it does not pass away, to return in the yearly cycle, but remains without setting, because it did not begin by rising. There is, then, that one Word of God, that life, that *light of men*[^8], the eternal day indeed: but this day, on which, joined to human flesh, he was made *like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber*[^9], is now today, and tomorrow becomes yesterday. Yet today commends the eternal one born of a virgin, because the eternal one born of a virgin consecrated today. What praises, then, shall we speak to the love of God, what thanks shall we give? He who so loved us that for our sake he came to be in time, he through whom times were made; and was younger in age than many of his servants in the world, more ancient than the world itself in eternity; that he who made man might become man, that he might be created of a mother whom he created, carried by the hands he formed, that he might suck the breasts he filled, that the Word might wail with speechless infancy in a manger — the Word, without whom human eloquence is speechless.

**3. 3.** See, O man, what God became for you: recognize the teaching of such great humility, even in a teacher who does not yet speak. You once were so eloquent in paradise that you gave names to every living soul[^10]: but for your sake your Creator lay an infant, and did not even call his mother by her name. You lost yourself in the most spacious estate of fruitful groves, by neglecting obedience: he, obedient, came into the narrowest lodging of mortality, that by dying he might seek the dead. You, when you were man, willed to be God, that you might perish[^11]; he, when he was God, willed to be man, that he might find what had perished. So far did human pride press you down that nothing but divine humility could lift you up.

**3. 4.** Let us then celebrate with joy the day on which Mary bore the Savior, a married woman the creator of marriage, a virgin the prince of virgins; both given to a husband and a mother not by a husband; a virgin before marriage, a virgin in marriage; a virgin with child, a virgin nursing. For the almighty Son, in being born, in no way took away the virginity of his holy Mother, which, about to be born, he had chosen. For fruitfulness in marriage is good: but integrity in holiness is better. Therefore Christ the man, who as God could grant both (for the same is man and the same is God), would never so give to his Mother the good that married people love as to take away the better good for the sake of which virgins disdain to be mothers. And so holy Mother Church celebrates today the Virgin's childbearing. For to her the Apostle says: *I have betrothed you to one husband, to present you as a chaste virgin to Christ*[^12]. Whence is there *a chaste virgin* among so many peoples of both sexes, among so many not only boys and virgins, but also married fathers and mothers? Whence, I say, *a chaste virgin*, except in the integrity of faith, hope, and charity? Christ, therefore, about to make virginity in the heart of the Church, first preserved it in the body of Mary. For in human marriage a woman is handed over to a bridegroom, so that she may be a virgin no longer: but the Church could not be a virgin, had she not found, as a bridegroom to whom she might be handed over, the son of a virgin.
