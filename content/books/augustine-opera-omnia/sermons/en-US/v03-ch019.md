# Sermon 63/B: On the Daughter of the Synagogue Ruler and on the Woman Suffering a Flow of Blood

**1.** The deeds of the past, while they are recounted, both enlighten the mind and add hope of things to come. Jesus was going to raise up the daughter of the synagogue ruler, who had already been announced as dead; and as he proceeded, a woman, as it were from across, intervened, afflicted with disease, full of faith, flowing with blood, to be redeemed by blood. And she said in her heart: *If I only touch the fringe of his garment, I shall be saved*[^1]. When she said it, she touched: Christ is touched by faith. She approached and touched, and what she believed was done. But the Lord asked, saying: *Who touched me?*[^2] He wishes to know, from whom nothing is hidden; he asks by whom was done what, before it was done, he knew. So it is a mystery: let us gaze, and understand as much as he grants.

**2.** The daughter of the synagogue ruler signifies the Jewish people; but this woman signifies the Church of the Gentiles. The Lord Christ, born of the Jews in flesh, was presented to those same Jews in flesh; he sent to the Gentiles, he did not go himself. His bodily and visible converse took place in Judea. Whence the Apostle says: *For I say that Christ was a minister of the circumcision for the truth of God, to confirm the promises of the fathers* (for to Abraham it was said: *In your seed all nations will be blessed*): *but the Gentiles glorify God for his mercy*[^3]. So Christ was sent to the Jews. He was going to raise up the daughter of the synagogue ruler. The woman intervenes, and is healed; first she is healed by faith, and as it were is unknown to the Savior. For whence was it said: *Who touched me?* Ignorance of God, confidence of mystery; he wishes to signify something, when he is ignorant who cannot be ignorant. What, then, does he signify? The healed Church of the Gentiles, which Christ did not see in bodily presence, whose voice is in the psalm: *A people whom I did not know served me, by the hearing of the ear it obeyed me*[^4]. The world heard, and believed; the Jewish people saw, and first crucified him, but afterward came to him too. The Jews believe too, but at the end of the age.

**3.** Meanwhile let this woman be saved, let her touch the fringe of the garment. Understand the garment, the chorus of the Apostles. There was there a certain last and least one, a kind of fringe, the apostle Paul. He it is who was sent to the Gentiles, who says: *For I am the least of the Apostles, who am not fit to be called an apostle*[^5]. Again he says: *I am the last of the Apostles*[^6]. This last and least fringe is the one necessary for the woman, not whole, to be made whole. What we have heard was done, what we have heard is being done: daily this woman touches the fringe, daily she is made whole. For the flow of the flesh, that is the flow of blood. When the Apostle is heard, when that last and least fringe is heard, and says: *Mortify your members, which are upon the earth*[^7], the flow of blood is restrained, fornication is restrained, drunkenness is restrained, worldly delights are restrained, all the works of the flesh are restrained. Do not wonder: the fringe is touched. When the Lord said: *Who touched me?*, knowing he did not know: he signified and designated the Church, which he did not see in body, but redeemed by his blood.

[^1]: Matt 9:21
[^2]: Luke 8:45
[^3]: Rom 15:8-9; Gen 22:18
[^4]: Ps 18:43-44 (17:44-45)
[^5]: 1 Cor 15:9
[^6]: 1 Cor 15:8
[^7]: Col 3:5
