# Sermon 306/D: A Sermon on the Feast of Saint Quadratus

**1.** The blessed Apostle brought forward a testimony from the Scriptures in which he commended to us the glory of the martyrs. *Because it is written,* he says: *I believed, therefore I have spoken, we too believe, therefore we also speak*[^1]. For if they had only believed and did not speak, they would not have suffered. So by believing they laid hold of life, and by speaking they found death—but a death in which the corruptible body was sown and incorruption was reaped. This sense, that is, that we believe and therefore also speak, the same Apostle expounded thus in another place: *With the heart one believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth confession is made unto salvation*[^2].

**2.** Here perhaps someone may ask: "How is confession made unto salvation, when the martyrs, at the very time they were confessing, were being killed? Surely, then, confession is &lt;not&gt; made unto salvation." You have forgotten: *Give us help from trouble, for vain is the salvation of man*[^3]? Plainly they confessed with the mouth unto salvation—the salvation proper to the faithful, proper to Christians, not the salvation common to men and beasts. For even that salvation which is common to men and cattle, from whom is it, except from the Creator of all? For from him by whom we were made so as to exist, we have received that we can be saved. And it is known to the faithful that God is the Creator not only of man but also of beast. Hence: *You will save men and beasts, Lord, according to the multiplication of your mercy, O God*[^4]. For because you are God and Creator of men, the multiplication of your mercy reaches to the salvation even of beasts. What, then, do men have more, except what follows: *But the sons of men will hope under the cover of your wings*[^5]? They will hope for what, except another salvation, not of this time, but eternal? For one salvation is held, another is hoped for; and therefore the martyrs were most strong in faith, because they despised what they held, in order to receive what they hoped for. Salvation and salvation: one they knew, another they believed; one they saw, another they did not see. What faith, then, dearly beloved, what faith! Despise what you see, and faith will arrive.

**3.** Hence a certain distinction between "men" and "the Son of man" grows clear to us. But all sons of men are men. Why are not all men sons of men? Because Adam and Eve were men, not sons of men. Which of these two did the Lord Christ prefer to be? Because it was fitting that he who had made man should become man for the sake of man, and should become what he had made, lest what he had made should perish. He could certainly form his flesh from wherever he willed, a true man with true flesh. For, in order to form the first man, he did not seek a father or a mother. He did not seek the seed of man, in order that the first man might come to be. It came also to this, that he himself who had made man should become man. He did not want only to be man, which he could be if he willed, but he preferred to be the Son of man. And how vehemently, how constantly he commends that he is the Son of man! Notice and consider, when the holy Gospels are read aloud, how incomparably more abundantly he calls himself the Son of man than the Son of God! For the majesty worthy of God had to be commended briefly, but the humility of the Most High had to be impressed more often. Among other places, then, where the Lord Jesus constantly testifies that he is the Son of man, let us recall especially that where he says to his disciples: *Who do men say that I, the Son of man, am?*[^6] Whom do they say you are, except the Son of man? But many are sons of men, the same in kind, unequal in virtues or conditions. So he says: *Who do men say that I am?* The answer was that some said he was Elijah, others Jeremiah, others John the Baptist or one of the prophets, as though the Son of man were one of the great men. But these were men with whom even beasts share salvation. *But you,* he says, *who do you say that I am?*[^7]—as though they were not men. He says: This and that men say I am, men pertaining to "man." But you, surely sons of men pertaining to the Son of man, who do you say that I am? Then Peter answered, one for all, the unity in all, and said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^8]. Christ commends his humility. Peter confesses Christ's majesty. So it was fitting, so it was right. Hear, Peter, what Christ is for you, and you, say who for you is the Son of man. *For whom do men say that I, the Son of man, am?* Who is this who was made the Son of man for you? *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.* I, he says, commend my humility, you, acknowledge my divinity. I say what I have been made for you, you, say from where I made you.

**4.** So that temporal salvation, common to men and beasts, was despised by the martyrs—the salvation they had with Adam the man, not with the Son of man. But the sons of men, pertaining to the Son of man, in order to despise the salvation common to men and beasts, *will hope under the cover of your wings*[^9]. What then, dearly beloved—for the martyrs to despise that salvation, is that salvation not some good? If it were not a good, who would boast that he despises what is no good? Let him despise a good in order to come to a better. We are speaking of that salvation which no one who seeks it is to be blamed. We see men do nothing else, as far as concerns the support of the present life, except that this salvation be either restored if it has failed, or obtained if it is lacking. But how long—there will be an end of this, like it or not—how long will you be able to hold this salvation, you who cannot recall the last day? A certain boundary of the final day is fixed for each one. To that boundary all run, and they run not willingly, but are driven. To this boundary we are nearer today than yesterday, tomorrow than today; and what has always been unknown—when—will someday come. So let us live carefully, because we do not know when we shall die. We do not know when, and we do not know how near is this thing we have said, "someday." And so the most blessed martyrs, when they had come to the moment of trial, so that either temporal life had to be retained by admitting an uncertain "when," or that life had to be seized which is to be retained without end—Why do you marvel, my brothers?—because they had faith, weighed and chose, conquered because they chose the better, chose because they loved, and in order to love—from where, except that they had it because they were loved? O blessed! O glorious! O strong! O great! O happy! O dedication to God! O on every side square! It is a good, but a temporal one, that you despised. It is an eternal and incorruptible good that you chose. Deservedly you stood firm in confession: wherever the persecutor wanted to turn you, you did not topple.

**5.** This is to be truly blessed: he in a marvelous way whitened you from blood. The divine word says in the *Apocalypse*: *These are they who have come out of great and much tribulation, and have washed their robes and made them white*[^10]. Where did they make them white? *In the blood*[^11], not of just anyone, but *of the Lamb*[^12]. All blood stains; the blood of the Lamb whitens. How does it whiten? *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sins of the world*[^13]. For that is no marvel, that they washed their robes in blood. For something can be washed in blood, whence a certain poet says: *They wash their necks and shoulders with abundant blood*[^14]. They wash; but do they whiten? Therefore the divine word was watchful, and when it had said: *They washed their robes,* it added what you would marvel at: *and made them white in the blood of the Lamb.* For how would whatever was washed not be whitened by that blood, of which it was said: *This is my blood, which will be poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins*[^15]?
