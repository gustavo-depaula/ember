# Sermon 186: On the Birthday of the Lord

**1.** Let us rejoice, brothers: let the nations be glad and exult. This day was consecrated for us not by this visible sun, but by its invisible Creator; when the Virgin Mother poured forth, from fruitful womb and unbroken purity, the one who for our sake was made visible, by whom, invisible, she herself was also created. A virgin conceiving, a virgin bearing, a virgin with child, a virgin pregnant, a virgin forever. Why do you marvel at this, O man? God had to be born thus, when he deigned to be man. Such did he make her who was made from her. For before he was made, he was: and because he was almighty, he could be made while remaining what he was. He made for himself a mother, while he was with the Father: and when he was made of a mother, he remained in the Father. How would he cease to be God, when he began to be man, who granted to his own mother that she should not cease to be a virgin when she gave birth? Therefore, that *the Word was made flesh*[^1] does not mean that the Word, by perishing, yielded into flesh; but that the flesh, that it might not perish, came over to the Word: so that, just as a man is soul and flesh, so Christ might be God and man. The same is God who is man, and he who is God the same is man: not by a confusion of nature, but by a unity of person. In short, he who as Son of God is coeternal with the one who begets him, always from the Father, the same began to be the son of man from the Virgin. And thus humanity was added to the divinity of the Son; and yet there was not made a quaternity of persons, but the Trinity remains.

**2.** Therefore let not the opinion of certain people creep upon you who are too little attentive to the rule of faith and to the oracles of the divine Scriptures. For they say: he who is the son of man was made the Son of God; but he who is the Son of God was not made the son of man. In saying this, they attended to what is true, but they were not able to express the truth. For what did they attend to, except that human nature could be changed for the better, but the divine could not be changed for the worse? This is true: but even so, that is, with the divinity in no way changed for the worse, the Word was nevertheless made flesh. For the Gospel does not say, "the flesh was made Word," but it says, *the Word was made flesh*. But the Word is God; for *God was the Word*[^2]. And what is flesh, but man? For in Christ the flesh of a man is not without a soul. Hence he says, *My soul is sorrowful even unto death*[^3]. If, then, the Word is God, and man is flesh, what else is *the Word was made flesh*, but, He who was God was made man? And by this, he who was the Son of God was made the son of man, by the taking up of the lesser, not by the conversion of the greater; by receiving what he was not, not by losing what he was. For how would we confess in the rule of faith that we believe in the Son of God who was born of the Virgin Mary, if not the Son of God, but the son of a man, was born of the Virgin Mary? For what Christian would deny that the son of a man was born of that woman? But still, that God was made man, and thus man was made God. For *God was the Word, and the Word was made flesh*. It must therefore be confessed that he who was the Son of God, in order to be born of the Virgin Mary, was made the son of a man by taking up the form of a servant, remaining what he was and taking up what he was not: beginning to be that in which *he is less than the Father*[^4], and always remaining in that in which *he and the Father are one*[^5].

**3.** For if he who is always the Son of God was not himself made the son of a man, how does the Apostle say of him: *Though he was in the form of God, he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man*[^6]? For it is not another, but he himself, equal to the Father in the form of God, who is surely the only-begotten Son of God, who emptied himself, being made in the likeness of men. Nor is it another, but the very same one, equal to the Father in the form of God, who humbled not another but himself, *being made obedient even unto death, even the death of the cross*[^7]. All of which the Son of God did only in that form in which he is the son of a man. Again, if he who is always the Son of God was not himself made the son of a man, how does the Apostle say to the Romans: *set apart for the gospel of God, which he had promised beforehand through his prophets in the holy Scriptures, concerning his Son, who was made for him of the seed of David according to the flesh*[^8]? Behold, the Son of God, which he always was, was made of the seed of David according to the flesh, which he was not. Again, if he who is the Son of God was not himself made the son of a man, how did *God send his Son, made of a woman*[^9]? By which name, according to the Hebrew tongue, virginal dignity is not denied, but the female sex is shown. For who was sent by the Father, except the only-begotten Son of God? How, then, made of a woman, except that the very one who was the Son of God with the Father was sent and made the son of a man? Born of the Father with no day of time, born of the mother on this day. For this day, which he created, he chose to be created on, just as he was made of a mother whom he made. For the very day from which the day henceforth receives an increase of light signifies the work of Christ, by whom *our inner man is renewed from day to day*[^10]. For to the eternal Creator, in created time, that day had to be the birthday on which a temporal creation would be fitting.
