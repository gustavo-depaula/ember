# Sermon 198: Augm. (Dolbeau 26)

**1.** {We admonish your love, since we see that you have come together as it were solemnly and are gathered at this hour and at this place more frequently than usual, that again and again you remember what you were just now singing, lest the tongue be resounding while the heart is mute, but that what you sounded with your voice into one another's ears you cry with affection into the ears of God. But this you sang: *Save us, Lord our God, and gather us from the nations, that we may confess to your holy name*[^1]. And now, if the solemnity of the nations which takes place on this day in worldly and carnal joy, in the noise of most vain and most base songs, in the celebration of that false festivity, if the things which the nations do today do not delight you, you are gathered from the nations.

**2.** You surely sang, and still the sound of the divine song is fresh in your ears: *Save us, Lord our God, and gather us from the nations.* Who can be gathered from the nations, except when he is made safe? Those, then, who are mingled with the nations are not safe. But those are saved who are gathered from the nations, saved by the salvation of faith, by spiritual salvation, by the salvation of God's promises, by the salvation of good hope, by the salvation of most sincere love. He, then, who believes and hopes and loves is not at once now to be called saved. For it matters what he believes, what he hopes, what he loves. For no one lives in any life whatever without these three affections of the soul: of believing, of hoping, of loving. If you do not believe what the nations believe, do not hope what the nations hope, do not love what the nations love, you are gathered from the nations. And let not the bodily mingling in so great a separation of mind terrify you. For what is so separated as that they, the demons, believe in gods, you believe in God who is the true God? that they hope for the empty things of the world, you hope for eternal life in Christ? that they love the world, you love the maker of the world? He, then, who believes one thing, hopes another, loves another, let him prove it by his life, show it by his deeds. You are going to perform today the celebration of new-year gifts with a pagan, you are going to play dice with a pagan, you are going to make yourself drunk with a pagan: how do you believe one thing, hope another, love another? how with unblushing brow do you sing: *Save us, Lord our God, and gather us from the nations?* For you are separated from the nations, mingled in body with the nations, by an unlike life. And see how great this separation is, if only you do it, if only you prove it. For now the Lord our God Jesus Christ, the Son of God, who for our sake became man, has given a price for us. And so, if he has now given a price, he gave it to this end, that he might redeem us, that he might gather us from the nations. But if you are mingled with the nations, you do not wish to follow him who redeemed. But you are mingled in life, in deeds, in heart, by hoping such things, by believing such things, by loving such things. You are ungrateful to your redeemer and do not acknowledge your price, the blood of the immaculate Lamb. That you may, then, follow your redeemer, who redeemed you by his blood, do not be mingled with the nations by likeness of conduct and deeds. They give new-year gifts, you *give alms*[^2].} For we do not say to you, brethren: "They give, do you not give"; rather, give more than they give, but as those who believe one thing, hope another, love another, for we do not say to you: "They believe, do you not believe; they hope, do you not hope; they love, do you not love"; but we say to you: "They believe that, you believe this; they hope that, you hope this; they love that, you love this; they give that or to those, you give this or to these." They give, then, new-year gifts, you *give alms.* {They commit themselves to the chances of earthly things, commit yourselves to the words of the divine Scriptures. They run to the theater, you to the church. They get drunk, you fast. If you do these things, you have truly sung: *Save us, Lord our God, and gather us from the nations.* Now those who have gladly heard what I said certainly stand with those who have not gladly heard,} and yet those are now gathered from the nations, those are mingled with the nations.

**3.** {I now speak to Christians:} if you believe what the nations believe, if you hope what the nations hope, if you love what the nations love, live as the nations live; {but if you believe one thing, hope another, love another, live otherwise, and prove your differing faith and hope and love by differing conduct.} What is it that the nations believe? As I have already said, they call those gods whom the apostle Paul shows us otherwise: {*For what,* he says, *the nations sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons and not to God. I do not wish you to become partners of demons*[^3]. Therefore the conduct of those men delights their gods. But he who said: *I do not wish you to become partners of demons,* willed that we be separated in life and conduct from those who serve demons. For those demons are delighted by the songs of vanity, are delighted by frivolous noise and the various basenesses of the theaters, the madness of the circus, the cruelty of the amphitheater, the spirited contests of those who, for the sake of pestilent men, undertake both quarrels and contentions even to enmities, for a mime, for an actor, for a charioteer, for a hunter. Those doing these things as it were set incense to the demons from their hearts. For the seducing spirits rejoice in the seduced, and are fed by the evil conduct and the base and infamous life of those whom they have seduced and deceived.} But now if you say to a man: "You love a charioteer," he answers: "Surely I love him," because, even if he denies it, he is caught favoring him, shouting for him, quarreling for him. And if you add: "You love him greatly," he will answer: "Greatly." If you say to him: "May you be such, and your sons," at once, if he appears honorable, in vexation he will answer: "Why have you done me an injury?" "Do I do you an injury, when I say: May you be such, or do you, who so love such a one as you dread to be?" You, on the contrary, love the martyrs. Therefore, just as you say to a pagan: "You love that charioteer," and he says: "I love him," and does not blush, so say to a Christian: "You love Cyprian," he answers: "I love him," say to him: "May you be such," he answers: "Would that God might bestow this!" How sincere is this love, how chaste, how secure, especially of one already crowned! For of one still struggling love is anxious, but yet all are loved in him who has already conquered, already sits at the right hand of the Father, already from above, as it were the umpire of that contest which Paul recalls, not only watches those struggling, but also helps those enduring.

**4.** {And so many will struggle today in their heart with the word they have heard. For we said: Do not give new-year gifts, give to the poor. It is too little to give only as much, give even more. Do you not wish to give more? at least give as much. But you say to me: "When I give new-year gifts, I too receive." What then? When you give to a poor man, will you receive nothing? Surely you do not believe what the nations believe, surely you do not hope what the nations hope, surely you do not love what the nations love. See, if you say that you receive nothing when you have given to a poor man, you have become equal to the gentiles, in vain you have sung: *Save us, Lord our God, and gather us from the nations.* You have forgotten what will be said to those who have given: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom,* and what will be said to those who have not believed: *Go into the eternal fire,*} *which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^4]. If he so gave the kingdom to these as to give nothing to those but only forsake them, you ought to love what he gives and to be unwilling to be cheated of so vast and ineffable a good. But it is not only this, that he sends these into the kingdom, but those he does not, but he even says: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels.* Let fear and love compel you. If you love less what he promises, fear what he threatens. For you begin from fear, you are perfected in love. By fearing hell you do what is said, but as long as you do it by fearing, you do it as a servant; but when you do it loving, you now do it free. Be a good servant, that you may merit to be free. Begin to fear the one you may love, whom, when you have loved, you will not fear. For it is written: *Fear is not in love, but perfect love casts out fear*[^5]. The apostle John says this. But if *perfect love casts out fear,* let fear first possess the breast: love will be born there, and so, as much as love grows, fear will decrease; as much as that is increased, this will be diminished; when that is perfected, this is excluded. *For fear is not in love, but perfect love casts out fear.* You do it if you love; if you do not yet love, do it if you fear; but if you neither fear nor love, in vain will you sing: *Save us, Lord our God, and gather us from the nations.* For you are still nations, drawing the yoke with unbelievers. What your Lord did for you, do you for the pagans, because, when the Lord did it for you, you were not yet a Christian. Let those who believe one thing, hope another, love another, hear, like it or not: let us say what we know; let them do what they will, only let them know that they do not do for nothing whatever they do. For there is a reward not only for good deeds, but also for evil ones. The reward of evil deeds is called punishment, of good deeds a crown.

**5.** What, then, did your Lord do for you, before you were a Christian? He suffered for you. And what does he say in the psalms? *But I, when they were troublesome to me, clothed myself with sackcloth and humbled my soul in fasting*[^6]. If you understand it figuratively, the Lord as it were fasted, by not receiving the impious into his body. And by that very fasting he hungered, when, finding no fruit on that tree, he cursed it, and it withered. What is it that he says: *I clothed myself with sackcloth?* As it were with his power hidden within the weakness of the flesh, I set that same mortal flesh before the eyes of the persecutors. For in that flesh, which he had taken on from the oldness of our mortality without any sin of his own, he bore our sins. And to sins surely the sackcloth pertains, on account of the haircloth of the hair of goats which will be set at the left, unless they first pass over into the lamb. In that form of a servant, then, sinning in nothing, and therefore owing nothing to death, he yet rendered for us what he did not owe, that he might free us from the debt. In that sackcloth he bore our price. For from sackcloth (saccus) the little bag (saccellus) too is named by diminution. But he bore our great price in the sackcloth with which he clothed himself, setting it before the persecutors, from whose impiety he humbled his soul in fasting. Finally, when he hung on the cross, the sackcloth was torn open by the lance, and the price of the world flowed out. Therefore your Lord suffered for you, before you were a Christian, that he might buy you and you might become a Christian.

**6.** But what does Scripture say? *As he laid down his life for us, so we ought to lay down our life for the brethren*[^7]. If we cannot yet suffer for the nations, we can at least fast for the nations. How far you are from the imitation of your Lord! and when you have fasted, how far still you are from it! And it is good that you come near. But how do you shudder at that very perfection, whose step you fear, a step so near, so humble! This step is near, on the ground—I do not know whether it is even to be called a step—which you are unwilling to ascend. For what great thing is it to fast at this time, at so small a day to dine late? It is not great, it is not at all laborious. The care of one piece of business often compels you to do what you are unwilling to display to God out of devotion. Because the Church of God wishes it, you are unwilling to fast. If you played dice, you would fast, and you would fast that you might not rise conquered. That you may conquer, fearing lest you be conquered in money by a man, you would fast, and you do not fast, not fearing lest you be conquered in heart by the devil. For nothing is easier in such small days than fasting. But on the first of January you are unwilling to fast. Prove yourselves that you may rejoice over yourselves, that we may rejoice over you. How small a temptation! Yet it proves the Christian breast.

**7.** You hear the sermon now: you are held in suspense, you are quickened by the word of God, and the very exhortation grows warm in the heart. We kindle something in your minds: we see, we recognize it, but let that flame seize firewood. For from this place a little after, the sermon ended, you are going to go out to the storms and to the cold of the world. You know that this world seems to seethe in a perverse solemnity: the winds are cold, where it is to be feared lest Christian hearts grow frozen. And so, brethren, as I said, let the divine word seize the firewood of your heart. But if it seizes it, those storms which you are going to suffer a little after, outside, will rather kindle a greater flame—not extinguish it—if it is not a small little fire that can be extinguished. If your heart now burns as tow, when you cry out, when you love, when you rejoice in the word of God, you are going to go out a little after, and by the breath of one mouth it will be extinguished, when it is said to a man: "Are you really going to fast today?" It will be extinguished at once, because it had begun to burn like tow. But if it burns like coals of fire, however much by perverse persuasion contrary blasts arise, they will increase the fire and kindle it more vehemently. But those burn like coals who have revived. For coals, when they burn, as it were revive from the dead. Many have this word too in their custom; when they send to kindle, they say: "Bring living ones," wishing them to be understood as burning. Whence surely they signify the extinguished ones to be as it were dead. If, then, you have revived by the fervor of the love of Christ, let the desire of him so burn in your hearts that no blast of those dissuading may extinguish it. For I know what you are going to suffer when you go out from here, and thanks be to God that you have come together. For that you come together more frequently in these days does not displease us, rather it even pleases us. For you have found a way, because those whose conduct is not like yours hasten to other things and occupy themselves in various vain pleasures, and so they furnish you leisure and vacancy, that there may be brought about in you what was said: *Save us, Lord our God, and gather us from the nations.* Therefore you are gathered now; even if you go out and are mingled with them by bodily intercourse, yet do not consent to them in evil and frivolous things, you will remain gathered from the nations, wherever you are in body. And would that you suffer the wicked dissuaders only in the streets, and not perhaps even in your houses! The father wishes to fast, the son does not, or the son wishes, the father does not; or the husband wishes, the wife does not, or she wishes and he does not. He, then, who does not wish, and therefore does not wish because he thinks this a festal day, is a contrary blast: but let that other so burn that not only can he himself not be extinguished, but the other can be kindled by him.

**8.** My brethren, if you understand well what you hear, I do not doubt that you grieve over those who are still held by that madness. You grieve over them, because you too were perhaps once held by such madness; and now your heart is healthy, which you have compared to their madness, and pitying you grieve, but do not grieve with despair. For if it could be brought about in you that what you loved yesterday you do not love today, it can be brought about in him too. But if it can be brought about in him too, as long as he is such and you grieve over him, pray for him. But that your prayer may be heard, fast for him and give alms, and in this spend the day for him whom you love—while he, not loving himself, spends it in contrary things: for *he who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^8]—that at some time he too may hate iniquity, loving his own soul, and within with you grieve over others and with you pray and fast for them. For, brethren, although the other days too hold them in trifles, yet especially on their solemnities a greater license of trifles rouses them to a greater love of the world and the ruin of pleasures. For when one does it in his own house, with cooler neighborhoods he goes less mad; but when all do it, they kindle one another. But I have already said to you: their ardor is your cold. Every love is an ardor. But it matters what is loved. When the heart burns for the world, it is cold to God. Therefore let it grow cold to the world, let it burn to God. When, therefore, they do these things more fervently and more frequently, just as they rouse themselves to wickedness against themselves, so let them rouse you to mercy for them. They are indeed always to be grieved over, as long as they are pagans, as long as they pursue vanities, as long as they are given over to demons. As long as they wish to adore what they made, forgetting by whom they were made, they are always to be lamented. But through their solemnities they rouse a new grief. When you see them flowing apart through various trifles, through the pleasures of luxury, through immoderate drunkenness, through dice and manifold madnesses, let their new movement make for you a new grief, if you are a Christian, if you have mercy, because, when you were not what you are, the Church had mercy on you, which was then in a few. But now grown up and spread copiously and widely in the name of Christ, are not those more to be grieved over who are still even now heavy of heart and love vanity and seek a lie? To those surely who, now separated from the human race by I know not what most hardened obstinacy, have remained few, a greater grief is owed, because a greater disease makes them vanish and not be healed by the authority of so great a medicine.

**9.** And would that we lamented only the pagans! We would lament almost no one. Let not Christians go to the theaters: the pagans flee thence, even if not from love of the truth, at least from shame at their fewness. A greater sacrifice of prayers must surely be offered by us in a sharper grief of heart, that those who are called Christians may merit to be corrected. It is not, then, shameless, brethren, rather it is a most opportune fast, when it is done for those who labor because they will, who, if they were unwilling, would not labor. For they are pressed by their own sins and rejoice together over their most filthy pleasures, and do not hear the voice of him who says: *Come to me, all you who labor and are heavy laden, and I will refresh you*[^9]. They are unwilling to be refreshed in the sweetness of Christ and think themselves refreshed in the satiety of lust. That is not refreshment, but ruin. Nor should you celebrate the festal days of the Christians as the drunken do, although fasts ought to be relaxed on account of the sacrament of joy. Yet it is one thing to signify joy by the relaxation of a fast, another to repel justice by a burden of the heart. The Lord says, the truth says: *Let not your hearts be weighed down in surfeiting and drunkenness*[^10]. It is foolish and irreligious to wish to please the martyrs by that which they, if they had not despised it, would never have come to the glory of a martyr.

**10.** But perhaps you will not suffer pagans of this kind. For these, given over to the wickedness of pleasures and drunkenness, certain of them themselves despise and say: "Just as you have bad Christians, so we have bad pagans. For the good pagans, see what they are." Then they name as it were the wise and the philosophers of the world, as though endowed with excellent teaching. But let them not terrify you. One thing is greatness, another is swelling. For what swells too appears as it were great, but it is not healthy. Hear the Apostle saying: *Beware lest anyone seduce you through philosophy and empty seduction according to the tradition of men, according to the elements of this world and not according to Christ, because in him dwells all the fullness of divinity bodily, and you are filled in him, who is the head of all principality and power*[^11]. For that they may learnedly and as it were prudently interpret their idols, they flee to *the elements of the world.* You reproach someone because he worships idols, and it is manifest that he adores an idol: he is convicted by the thing itself, and there he has his affection—namely in that idol—there he places his hearing-of-prayers; but as it were a more skilled and more learned pagan, what does he say to you? "The unskilled pagans do this, that they adore an idol as an idol, just as yours too do who adore columns in the church."

**11.** Therefore I say, brethren: do not do anything whence the pagans may insult us; so enter the church that you do not give occasion to the pagans why they should be unwilling to enter the church. For on account of the brotherly assembly the church is entered. For the temple of your prayer is your heart. Cleanse the place where you pray, and you will be heard. For if you cleanse the visible place where you make your prayer, not for the sake of God's eyes—for he sees all things, just as he created all things—but lest your eyes be offended and turn away your attention, how much more ought you to cleanse where you call upon and to which you call upon God? Your inner chamber, into which you enter and shut the door, as the Lord himself says, that is, the sense of your body, have barred against the corporeal enticements; but the brotherly assembly makes ardor in praying and praising God. As, if some weight were being moved, and that which is called the work-chant were sung, would not the very multitude of those singing together and working together, harmonious in the same voice and most concordant in the same effort, rouse even your feeble strength, so that you too would wish to hold the rope and rejoice in the partnership of that work? The assemblies of the brethren, then, sharpen love. And every man prays within, when he prays well, as the Lord answered the Samaritan woman: *The hour will come,* he says, *and now is, when you will adore the Father neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem*[^12]. And a little after: *The hour will come,* he says, *and now is, when the true adorers will adore the Father in spirit and in truth. For the Father too seeks such to adore him. God is a spirit, and those who adore him must adore in spirit and in truth*[^13]. If, then, God is to be sought *in spirit,* let there be a dwelling: the dweller will be present. *We will come to him,* he says, I and my Father, *and we will make our dwelling with him*[^14]. Come, then, to the church, that the fervor of praying may be roused, that the devotion of the peaceful assembly may make the merit of being heard before God, not because God dwells in an earthly place where he may hear you. Do not think that within walls of this kind alone does God hear: in prison he heard the martyrs. *What house,* he says, *will you build me, or what is the place of my rest? Did not my hand make these things?*[^15] And yet he said that he has a place in the pious heart of man. For he says: *Upon whom does my Spirit rest? Upon the humble and quiet one who trembles at my words*[^16].

**12.** Why did we say this? Lest, when we insult the pagans, we do something whence the pagans may insult us. Come to the places of the martyrs in such a way that from the places there may be a pious commemoration in your hearts, and from the honor of the martyrs there may arise affection toward God, who did not abandon the martyrs in tribulation, who helped them as they fought, who crowned them as they conquered. So you make yourselves worthy, for whom the martyrs may pray. Otherwise a good servant is greatly indignant, if, his Lord despised, he himself is adored, a good servant who has even been made a son from a servant. For one thing is one who is still a servant, who is going to be also a son from a servant, and another a son already. One thing a servant by fear, another a son by love. A great house has all: hired men and servants and sons. The hired men are those who seek worldly advantages in the Church, of whom the Apostle says that they announce the Gospel not chastely, and yet he permits it, saying: *Whether by occasion or in truth, let Christ be announced*[^17]. But the servants are those who by fear do what is commanded by the Lord. They are indeed of the house, and more inward in the great house than the hired men; from those servants are made sons, when they begin to serve out of love. Therefore, as I had begun to say, the great house has all. What do we think of the martyrs, my brethren? Far be it that we reckon them among the hired men, but neither among those who are not yet sons! For they loved Christ and, before the love of him, despised not only all the pleasures of the world, but even all torments, inebriated with the cup of his Spirit, of which it is said: *Your inebriating cup, how glorious it is!*[^18] Therefore the good servant, as I said, who is now also called a son, does not wish himself to be adored, but his Lord. Attend, brethren, and remember what you daily frequent, what the truth teaches in the Church. The faithful know in what order the martyrs are commemorated in the sacraments, when our vows and prayers are directed to God; the faithful know, and let the catechumens hasten to know. For who repels them? To whom who wills does what is closed to one deferring not lie open?

**13.** But let us attend, brethren, to what the good servants did while still set in this body, when they were honored by men and began to be worshiped as the gods of the nations: Paul and Barnabas, because they worked miracles in Christ, because they had exceeded the human measure, the pagans according to their custom called Barnabas Jupiter, but Paul Mercury, because he was readier in speaking, and they had begun to offer victims to them; which honor they so shuddered at that they tore their garments and taught them, as far as they could, who was the one to be adored, in whose power they did these things. Attend to these things, brethren, that you may be strong and fortified against the pagans even of the more elegant sort. Why do I say "more elegant," who are rather more in peril? For the more learned they seem to themselves, the more unteachable they become. For they blush to learn, lest they confess that they are ignorant, and there is not in them the pious humility which the humble God came to teach alone. Or will someone perhaps say: "Therefore Paul and Barnabas rejected the honor of sacrifices which was offered them, because they made the one Jupiter, the other Mercury, false gods whom the apostles by Christian religion detested, and therefore they were indignant at being compared to demons or idols, not because they wished God to be adored rather than themselves"? They would, then, say this to those wishing to sacrifice: "You do us an injury, you compare us to demons than whom we are better"; rather they tore their garments grieving and humbly admonished that these things ought to be done neither to themselves nor to anyone, but that from these vanities one ought to turn to the one God, saying: *Men, what are you doing? We are men like you, announcing to you that from these vain things you turn to him who made heaven and earth and sea and all that is in them*[^19]. Saying these and such things, they scarcely persuaded them not to sacrifice to them. But that you may more fully show the impious that they did not reject these sacrifices because they thought an injury was being done them, but that God alone might be honored, to whose worship, that all might be turned, they especially labored, attend to Peter: when men marveled at his deed—rather not his, but God's—when that lame man of forty years, who sat at the beautiful gate, by the word of God rose and walked, he was unwilling for glory to be given to himself, but to Christ, and he says: *Men of Israel, why do you marvel at this, or why do you gaze at us, as though by our own power or authority we had made this man walk? The God of Abraham and the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, the God of our fathers, has glorified his Son, whom you handed over to judgment*[^20].

**14.** But if this is too little, what men did, that they wished glory to be given not to themselves but to God, behold the angels. In the Apocalypse a holy angel—for there are also perverse angels, for whom and for the devil their prince the eternal fire is prepared—an angel, then, not fallen by pride, but remaining in holy humility under God, not raising himself by elation against God nor saying: *I will set my seat in the north and I will be like the Most High*[^21], but remaining in the south, where Christ feeds and where he lies down . . . For it is written: *Where you feed, where you lie down at noon*[^22]. For to the southern part the northern is contrary: therefore it signifies the cold and dark. But noon signifies the illumined and fervent. Those, then, who are good, as it were at noon, are fervent and shine; but those who are evil, as it were in the north, cold, are darkened by a dark mist. With them at noon God feeds and lies down, whence it was said: *Where you feed, where you lie down at noon,* not with those of whose part the devil says: *I will set my seat in the north and I will be like the Most High.* For he could not be adored except by such, that they might think him god. That angel, then, remaining at noon, fervent with holiness and bright with wisdom, was demonstrating certain things in the Apocalypse to the apostle John. But he, disturbed, fell at the feet of the angel.

**15.** See, brethren, above all, to what angels the humble men are like and to what the proud are like. For those who make schisms and heresies wish their own name to be named and the name of Christ to be obscured, and they themselves choose a seat in the north. For men would not follow them, the Church forsaken, as it were following Christ, unless darkened in understanding and estranged from the fervor of love. But to what are those like who, holding humility, choose *to be cast away in the house of the Lord, rather than to dwell in the tents of sinners*[^23]? {What did Simon wish, but to be praised in miracles, to be exalted in pride? For this very thing compelled him to think the gift of the Holy Spirit was to be bought with money. To which pride contrary, the Apostle, in humility, remaining at noon, fervent in spirit, shining in prudence, says: *Neither is he who plants anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase*[^24]. And again: *Was Paul crucified for you, or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^25] How he refused to be worshiped in place of Christ, how he is zealous for the bridegroom and is unwilling to display himself to a fornicating soul in place of the bridegroom?} Such, then, those saints were according to their measure—for they will be perfect like the angels after the resurrection of the body—but yet according to their measure, just as they themselves, they rejected the honor which men as it were properly offered them, while they wished him to be worshiped and all hearts turned to him, in whom they themselves too gloried, truly good and faithful servants, sons, such are also the holy angels. For even though still men set in the flesh and placed in such great temptations, they rejected the honor that was conferred on them unto pride, that the one might be honored in all who alone has been honored without peril to the one honoring. For that he may be honored, where do they set themselves? how do they cast themselves down? {Does it not seem a great thing to plant and to water? But *neither is he who plants anything, nor he who waters.* He says that he is not anything for the salvation of those whom he desired to build up in Christ.} If, then, men still living in the flesh do these things, how much more the martyrs and the angels! For the more holily they live, the more they love the glory of God and have their hope in him alone. For we too shall be as the angels, but after the resurrection. So the Lord said: *They will be equal to the angels of God*[^26].

**16.** Return, then, with me to that which I had begun to narrate, and be healthfully instructed why you have leisure for God. That angel, then, was showing in the Apocalypse wonderful and mystical visions to the apostle John, servant of Christ, son of mother Church and made among the sons of God. But he, disturbed in a certain vision, fell, as I had begun to say, at the feet of the angel. But the angel did not accept the honor offered him by a man, which was owed to God, and said to him: "Rise, what are you doing? *Adore God. For I too am your fellow servant, and of your brethren*[^27]." Why did we say this? Behold the sum. We had begun to deal with the pagans as the more learned—for those are more unlearned, from whom these are unwilling to be prescribed to—since they say to us: "You too have adorers of columns and sometimes even of pictures." And would that we did not, and may the Lord grant that we do not! But yet the Church does not teach you this. For which of their priests ever ascended, and from a higher place commanded the people not to adore idols, as we in Christ publicly preach, that columns or stones of buildings in holy places or even pictures be not adored? Rather, their very priests, turned to the idols, offered victims for the peoples, and still desire to offer them.

**17.** "We," they say, "do not worship images, but what is signified through the image." I ask what the images signify, I ask what the image of the sun signifies: surely nothing but the sun? For the interpretations of other images perhaps have hidden meanings. For now let us leave these things and set them aside a little to be taken up again. The image of the sun surely signifies nothing but the sun, and that of the moon the moon, and that of Tellus the earth. If, then, they do not adore what they see in the image, but that which the image signifies, why, having before their eyes those very most well-known things which are signified by those images, do they adore the images of them in their place? For if what is signified were not seen, rightly would the sign be adored in place of the thing which is signified. But since they see the sun which is signified by the image of the sun, why do they set their back to the one who is signified, and their face to the sign by which he is signified? For if they did not make images for those things which are seen and made them only of those which are not seen, they could deceive the less prudent and say: "See, we worship the sun. But because we see it, we have set up no image of it. Likewise too the moon and the stars, as we see them, so we worship them; we have made or have no images of them. For it is foolish to set a visible sign in a closed temple of that thing which can be seen and adored in the open sky. But when we worship the mind or talent or soul or virtue or justice—these now are invisible—we set up for them visible images, that, seeing and adoring them, we may venerably think of those invisible things." But now they are most openly convicted of being given over to the images themselves, not to those things which are signified through the images, when they have made images even of visible things set before the eyes of all. The sun has an image, which if they adored in the sky, they would justly be reproached by piety and most true religion. Besides, they have come to such madness that they set their back to the sun and their face to its image. How will so perverse a one hear you, when you forsake him and turn to his false and most deceitful image made by the hand of a man? As if you should come to the house of the head of a household to ask something, and he stood in his courtyard, but you turned your back to him and your face to the panel where he is painted, and as if you poured out the prayers of your desire not only not to the man but to the picture, indeed even with the man himself present, whose image that picture is, would he not order you, mocked or reckoned as it were mad, to be driven from his house? Nothing would be like this folly which these men do, even if the sun were to be adored. But because these things are not to be adored, we will speak in the name of the Lord, if he gives space and means. But now how these men are caught in their works, this we say. You made a temple of the moon, you placed in it an image; the moon itself rises daily, scarcely for two or three days in a month can it not be seen: adore it, if it is to be adored, if you have now come to this disgrace, that you wish to adore a visible creature as God, than which surely the invisible creature is better, which yet is not itself, but only the Creator, to be adored as God. But behold, before your eyes are the very things you worship, whether the sun or the moon or the stars or the earth, why do you seek their images, when they are present for you to adore, except because men are given over to the things which they made with their own hands, forgetting him by whose hands they were made?

**18.** But now let us see how they interpret the images. "I do not," he says, "call the statue of Neptune Neptune, but Neptune is something else which is signified through this image." What is Neptune? "The sea," he says. This is the whole understanding, that Neptune is the sea. But the Christian neither worships that statue which you call Neptune, nor the sea which you interpret as Neptune. And what does he worship? Namely him *who made heaven and earth and sea and all that is in them.* But you, what do you say? "That statue signifies the sea." And this is said as it were in part, in secret, in the ear, that it may be thought a great mystery, that that statue signifies the sea, so that, namely, if anyone adores that statue, he may seem to himself to adore the sea. Only inlanders, then, who do not see the sea itself, ought to have images of Neptune, that those who do not have the thing might have a sign in place of the thing. But now they have made the shrine of Neptune beside the sea itself, and setting their back to the sea they adore Neptune. Nor do the very waves resounding behind them turn the adorer to themselves, and—what is more foolish—he who prays wishes the sea to be silent and the statue to hear, because, namely, the sea has hearing not in its salt waves, but in the alien airy ears.

**19.** So too the image of Tellus. "It is not," he says, "this which you see that is worshiped, but this signifies the earth." This, even if he did not say it, would appear. In vain does he wish to bring forth as it were from a mystery what he cries out in the name. For Tellus is by another word the earth. Yet they call the same one mother earth, for whom they made a temple and an image. See, I understand, as of the sun, as of the moon: there is no need that he expound it; for these images are called the same as the very things of which they are images, which surely we see and which appear most well-known. Or is it perhaps that they add "mother," that this is to be learned? "She is," they say, "the mother of all things that are begotten." And let her be the mother of all things that are begotten: shall I therefore adore the earth, and not him who said: *Let the earth bring forth* whatever it brought forth[^28]? For without his word it would not only be barren, but would not exist at all. What, then, does this man, as it were a narrator of mysteries, wishing to turn me from the Creator and turn me to the creature, what does he expound to me as a great thing, that I should worship the earth? Clearly this is rightly said to farmers, that, if they do not wish to suffer hunger, they should cultivate (colant) the earth, not by adoring it, but by plowing it.

**20.** So I ask about the image of Juno: now here we desire a mystical interpreter. For Juno does not, like mother earth, show by her own name what she signifies, or the image of the sun or moon. The secret of Juno, then, is great. What secret, I ask you? "This," he says, "few know." Perhaps from those few many have heard and told it. Perhaps even their own writings betray to those to whom they do not wish it betrayed what they worship, although our writings too betray to them what we worship, but we do not fear. Our books are carried about publicly for sale: the light does not blush. Let them buy, read, believe; or let them buy, read, mock. That Scripture knows how to hold guilty those who read and do not believe. The book is carried around for sale, but he who is preached in the book is not for sale. Or is he too for sale, because he set himself to be bought by the nations at such a price as each has? Zacchaeus bought him with half his patrimony, the widow bought him with two small coins, the poor host bought him by offering a cup of cold water; and he is possessed by all the buyers: to none is he too narrow, he enlarges all. Such is he who is preached in the book. Buy the book and read, we do not blush. You, hide the books of your rites in hidden caves; so great is the curiosity of men that they both search out and betray.

**21.** {What, then, is Juno? "Juno," they say, "is the air." Just now they invited us to worship the sea in Neptune, to worship the earth in the image of Tellus; now they invite us to worship the air. These are the elements of which this world consists. This, then, the apostle Paul forewarning in his letter: *Beware,* he says, *lest anyone deceive you through philosophy and empty seduction, according to the elements of this world*[^29]. For he was touching those very men, who as it were more prudently expound idols. Therefore when he said: *Through philosophy,* in the same place he says: *According to the elements of this world.* He admonished that not any sort whatever, as it were adorers of images, but as it were more learned interpreters of signs, were to be guarded against.} "Therefore," they say, "Juno is the air." Let them call by whatever name a thing known to all: I will not worship the air, because neither can it be worshiped, as the earth is worshiped by farmers. For in the earth the farmer works, as I said above, not by adoring, but by laboring, that he may find fruit. But this air can neither be so worked nor is it to be adored. Praise the work, adore the worker. "That you may know," they say, "that the air is Juno, the Greeks call her so: {she is called *hra* in Greek; but era often repeated sounds aer (air)." But we, who say that one must serve the one Creator in religion, do not adore Juno for this reason, that it is an image; we do not adore it for this reason, that it is a creature.

**22.** But this I ask of these men: if I worship God alone, will the air be angry; for if I worship the air, God is angry; although he is always tranquil and without any disturbance administers and rules all that he created, yet in a certain unspeakable manner God is angry. He who, since he wishes himself alone to be worshiped, alone wishes this not out of pride: for he alone is worthy to be worshiped, and he wishes himself to be worshiped not to this end, that he himself be more exalted, but that man be better. For he does not need worshipers, but you need to worship. *I said,* says the prophet, *to the Lord: You are my God, for you have no need of my goods*[^30]. If, then, he alone without pride requires to be worshiped, whoever else requires it and arrogates to himself privately, that he as it were properly be worshiped, for whom it does not suffice to be worshiped in him who founded him, requires it out of pride. And the pride of the devil is to be avoided, that we humble may come to the height of God. For the devil alone wishes himself to be worshiped in place of God, because he himself said: *I will set my seat in the north and I will be like the Most High*[^31].

**23.** For what are they going to say of the sign even, for example, of Vulcan, that we may recall all the elements—for they are said to be four: earth, water, air, fire; and of heaven we have already spoken, when we spoke of the sun and moon? "Vulcan," they say, "is fire," not the aethereal fire, where are the sun and moon and the other stars, but this fire which is on earth, accommodated to the uses of men. "For therefore," they even say, "Vulcan is painted lame, because the motion of this fire vibrates as if limping." What then? Since a man who kindles it and extinguishes it when he pleases has fire in his power, uses it as he pleases, is there so great a power of men over a god, and does he who blows on a lamp extinguish a god? I do not know whether it is worse to worship the image itself in itself, than such an interpretation of it. But what is it to us? For perhaps they interpret even invisible things: for these are visible things. Let us quickly come to the security of our faith, that we be not at all corrupted by them.

**24.** "When I worship Mercury," he says, "I worship talent; talent is not seen, it is something invisible." Clearly we too concede that talent is something invisible, and so invisible and such a thing that it is better than heaven, than earth, than sea and all these things that are seen; an invisible substance indeed, since it is a certain life, better than every visible substance, because everything visible is a body. And talent is a certain great thing: yet if you consider that very talent which they say they worship, what has it done? Do not many err with great talent? And perhaps especially those very men who think talent is to be worshiped in the image of Mercury. If, then, human talent can err without God as guide, when human talent is healthy, it does not wish to be adored in place of God, but wishes him to be adored by whom it wishes to be illumined. For unless human talent is illumined by God who illumines, it is dark in its own errors. But I ask you what you worship in Mercury? "Talent," you said. Talent is a certain middle thing, because talent either, turned away from the Creator, is darkened and made foolish, or, turned to the Creator, is illumined and made wise. But you said something middle, when you said you worship talent. "Yes," he says, "middle, for the very name of Mercury sounds as it were running in the middle." For Mercury is said to be named as it were running in the middle. If, then, he is worshiped for this reason, that he is a certain middle thing, why is not he rather worshiped who recalls to himself the middle thing which you call talent, that, suspended from lower things and turned away, he may make it cohere to himself, such as are the talents of the saints, the talents of the martyrs, the talents of the angels? For if talent be such as that of this angel whom I recalled, illumined by God, it repels from itself the adoration of a man and admonishes its adorer rather to adore God. "*Adore God,*" he says, "*for I am your fellow servant, and of your brethren.*" Should you, then, worship a middle talent, whence a better talent may reproach you? For if you worship a middle talent, an illumined talent will reproach you. Why? Because the illumined talent does not wish talent to be worshiped, but the illuminator of talents. For by that very illumination it is kindly and benevolent, and wishes all talents to be turned to him, by whom it knows itself too to have been illumined. Or does a foolish talent perhaps wish itself to be worshiped? Clearly this is true. Beware, then, lest, when you wish to worship Mercury as talent, you worship the devil, who has a foolish talent. For the devil too, when he said: *I will set my seat in the north and I will be like the Most High,* was unwilling to worship God and wished to be worshiped in place of God, rising up against God. But because he departed as it were from the south to the north, his talent was made dark, and because dark, also proud, and because proud, therefore, by you deceived, he wishes Mercury to be worshiped, that, while you worship Mercury, you may worship the devil.

**25.** Let your holiness attend, and from men see what we say. Attend your mind and understand. Every creature is either corporeal or spiritual. The Creator alone is spiritual; but the creature, as we said, is corporeal or spiritual. Attend, that you may have, according to our strength, as much as the Lord has helped, against the pagans, not any sort whatever, but those who think themselves more learned and interpreters of images, and are inflated and inflate others and, empty, mock the true name of truth. Every creature, as I said, is either corporeal or spiritual. Corporeal, as the earth and all that is begotten in it, as the sea and all that swims or creeps in it, as the air and all that flies in it, as heaven and all that shines in it. All this creature is corporeal. But the spiritual is better than the corporeal. What is the spiritual creature? That which is not perceived by the senses of the body, but by the understanding of the mind, such as are all souls, whether irrational as in beasts, or rational as in men, whether failing as in the impious, or making progress as in the converted, or perfect as in the angels and archangels, thrones, dominations, principalities, powers of the higher dwelling. Since, then, the creature is either corporeal or spiritual, nor can a third be found, the highest bodies, than which there are no better, are the heavenly ones. Now what is beyond—not by intervals of place, but by force of nature—is not body, but spirit: a certain life, but a changeable life, because a creature; the true unchangeable life is the Creator. The changeable life, then, that is, the spiritual creature, is also formable and illuminable, that is, which can be formed and illumined; but the unchangeable life, that is, the very substance of the Creator, is the creatrix and illuminatrix. The creature is formed and illumined, but the Creator forms and illumines.

**26.** Attend and understand. Every life, then, which can be illumined and arrive at wisdom, when it is of good will, loves God who illumines it, and turned to him makes progress and, clinging to him, is formed unto the integrity and perfection of wisdom, and in its own kind unto the fullness of beatitude: namely the rational and intellectual life, such as is that of the angels, such as is that of men. If, then, such a life, when it is praiseworthy, loves its illuminator, every such life, by a culpable will forsaking the life that illumines it, is darkened and made proud, as is that either of the unjust angel or of the unjust man: for it is unwilling to cling to God, but wishes by a certain private arrogance to be held as God. Into this certain proud and unjust life, fallen and turned, the archangel was made the devil and his angels and those demons, and they received a certain space of this dark air, where they rule over all unjust men. And therefore the Apostle: *According to the prince,* he says, *of the power of this air, who now works in the sons of disobedience*[^32]. And again, exhorting to endure the persecutions of this world: *Ours is not,* he says, *a struggle against flesh and blood,* that is, against men, *but against the principalities,* he says, *and powers and rulers of this world of darkness, against the spiritual things of wickedness in the heavenly places*[^33]. For because he said that the *prince of the power of this air* works *in the sons of disobedience,* lest they attend to men and think they struggle with those through whom they suffered persecutions, and not behold in mind that enemy whom in Christ they ought to conquer: *Yours is not,* he says, *a struggle against flesh and blood, but against the principalities and powers and rulers of this world of darkness, and against the spiritual things of wickedness in the heavenly places.* For lest, calling them *rulers of the world,* he be thought to say that they are rulers of this whole fabric which God founded, he showed what he called the world, by adding and saying: *of this darkness.* And what darkness is you can recognize. For that the unbelieving are signified by the name of darkness the Apostle himself says, when he was speaking to the faithful: *For you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^34]. So too what he said, *in the heavenly places,* it must be understood that he said it not in those high seats of the holy angels, where the luminaries and stars too are set, but because this air too is called heaven, whence the birds too are called birds of heaven.

**27.** Let your holiness, then, attend, that the mercy of the Lord may illumine you. For we sound with the ears; within he acts, as we believe, as we hope of the mercy of him to whom we sing praises. Therefore, as I had begun to say, the soul turned away from God as from the light of truth and as from the south comes to be in the north. But the kingdom of the north is the kingdom of the devil, who says: *I will set my seat in the north and I will be like the Most High;* there the hearts of men grow cold, and, made cold from that fire of divine wisdom, cannot be wise about spiritual things. And so they begin to think of bodies alone, so that they seek even the divinity itself in bodies, that is, in the sea, in the earth, in the air, and especially in the heavenly bodies, as in the moon or the sun or the stars. For because the chief among the senses of the body is the sense of the eyes, whatever shines to the eyes, even if it tower above the place of the world, is thought great. But if anyone says to them that there is something great *which neither eye has seen nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man,* they say that it is not a thing which cannot be seen. Such hearts, then, have grown cold. If they have grown cold, they are in the north; if they are in the north, he rules over them who said: *I will set my seat in the north and I will be like the Most High.* But those who in some way raised their hearts and as much as they could sharpened them by study, that they might see something which cannot be seen with the eyes of the body, transcending—which was easy—the earth which they trod and all things that are on the earth, transcending also the sea and whatever swims or creeps in it, transcending also the air and all the birds—for it too is a corporeal element—transcending all that aethereal heaven with all the luminaries that shine there or whatever others perhaps lie hidden, saw that there is something which is not seen, such as is the mind, such as talent, such as reason; they saw too that this too changes, and they sought something unchangeable, they passed by thought and the keenness of the mind even the spiritual creature, and understood the Creator spirit, and as it were beaten back by the unaccustomed brilliance of wisdom they returned, as it were about to rest in the darkness of their flesh, and certain of them perceived that there was need of a purgation of their soul, which, cleansed from all carnal concupiscences, might be made fit to obtain that whose ineffable light had struck it.

**28.** Attend, my brethren, and grievingly mark. They saw that there was need for them of a purgation, that that light which cannot be grasped by the weak gaze of the mind, the same gaze healed and formed and strengthened, might be able to be grasped. They saw that there was need of a medicine, and the devil at once interposed himself to those seeking that very medicine, because they sought proudly, boasting themselves as it were in their teachings, especially because they were able, if any were able, to attain, by the keenness of their talent and the sharpness of their mind, all creatures both corporeal and spiritual being transcended, to know that there is something which is both spiritual and unchangeable, and that from it are all these things which subsist whether spiritually or corporeally; but that that Creator is not moved by places, is not moved by times, since bodies are moved by places and times; but the substance of the soul, that is, the spiritual creature, is indeed not moved by places, but yet is moved by times through the changeableness of its various affections, and therefore it is a certain middle thing, because the highest God is moved neither by places nor by times, but the substance of bodies—that is, the lowest creature—is moved both by places and by times. Whence that is a middle thing, because it is not moved by places as neither is God, but is moved by times as bodies are. When, then, they saw and sought a purgation, that proud devil forestalled those seeking proudly and boasting themselves proudly, and set himself in place of a mediator, through whom it might seem to them that their souls could be purged. He, persuading them by certain signs of his pride that he was necessary, that it might seem to men that through magic arts the soul could be purged which wishes to come to God, instituted sacrilegious rites in temples, which promise purgation to the sacrilegious. For many images, as Scripture says, were instituted from the honors of men who were held great, whether absent or dead. All which things, now taken away in the name of Christ by public laws, have ceased to be done publicly, since some of them had been as it were public magic. But just as before there was private magic, so now these things are done secretly, after they were forbidden to be done publicly.

**29.** Since, then, that proud and unjust enemy of souls set himself in place of a mediator, hear the Apostle teaching why he set himself there, namely because their pride gave him place. Whence he admonishes, saying: *Neither give place to the devil*[^35]. But how we may find out their pride, let the same Apostle say. And first let us attend that even certain of them had come to the knowledge of God, whom yet he wished to be saved through Christ. For they had come to the knowledge of God, but had not come to salvation. For it is one thing to come to the knowledge of God, another to come to salvation, where that very knowledge too is made full, when the knower clings to the known. When the Apostle was speaking among the Athenians as to pagan men, because they themselves boasted of the summit of their teaching above the other nations, and among them was as it were the highest philosophy—for there indeed arose the learned and wise of this world—when, therefore, the Apostle was speaking there, he did not bring forth to them testimonies from the prophets, but he brought forth from their own and did not keep silent that they were theirs, not ours, because, even if something good is found there, many evil things are found there, not as our prophets, whatever they have in their Scriptures is good. Among the Athenians, then, the Apostle, when he spoke of God, says: *In him we live, are moved, and are,* *as certain even of your own have said*[^36].

**30.** But what kind those *certain* were and why they were disapproved, expounding in another place: {*The wrath of God is revealed from heaven over all impiety*[^37]. What do we understand by *over impiety,* but both of Jews and of nations? But lest it be said: "Why is the wrath of God revealed over the impiety of the nations, since you said over all? For did the nations receive the law and become transgressors? Rightly is the wrath of God revealed over the Jews, to whom the law was given and they were unwilling to keep it; but to the nations it was not given," consider, brethren, and understand how the same Apostle shows all to be guilty, and all to need salvation and the mercy of God. *For the wrath of God is revealed from heaven over all impiety and injustice of men, of those who hold the truth in iniquity*[^38]. Understand, brethren, see how he did not say: "They do not have the truth," but: *The truth,* he says, *they hold in iniquity.* And as if you should ask, saying: "How can they have the truth, who did not receive the law?", *because what is known of God,* he says, *is manifest in them*[^39]. How could *what is known of God be manifest in them* who did not receive the law? It follows and says: *For his invisible things, from the creation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood through the things that are made; his everlasting power too and divinity*[^40], surely we supply "is understood and clearly seen." Through what is it seen? *Through the things that are made.*

**31.** For why does a man attend to the works, and not seek the maker? You attend to the earth bearing fruit, you attend to the sea full of its animals, you attend to the air full of birds, you attend to heaven shining with stars, you recognize the changes of the seasons, you consider the fourfold year, that the leaves fall and return to the trees, that numbers are given to seeds, that there are for each thing its measures, its weights, that all things are administered in their orders, that the higher heaven has all its peace, that the lower earth has its own beauty proper to its kind of things yielding and succeeding: beholding all these things, you now behold all things quickened by a spiritual creature, and do you not seek the artificer of so great a work? But you say to me: "These things I see, him I do not see." Because he made for you one thing whereby you may see these, another whereby he himself may be seen—for seeing these things he gave the eyes of the body, for seeing himself he gave the mind—therefore you are not permitted to say in vain: "I do not see him"; look at these things according to the understanding, and you will see the worker. For neither do you see the soul of a man; for the soul of a man is not seen, but from the administration of the body, when we behold him walking, working, speaking, moving all the members according to the proper offices of each, we say: "He lives, there is in him that which we do not see," and we understand this through these things which we see. As, then, from the movements and administration of the body you understand the soul, which you do not see, so from the administration of the whole world and from the governing of souls understand the Creator.

**32.** But it is too little to understand; for they too understood, and see what the Apostle says, consider those very words which I had begun to say from the beginning. *The wrath of God,* he says, *is revealed from heaven over all impiety and injustice of men, who hold the truth in iniquity, because what is known of God is manifest in them: for God has manifested it to them*[^41]. And as if you should ask whence he manifested it, *the invisible things,* he says, *from the creation of the world are clearly seen, being understood through the things that are made; his everlasting power too and divinity*—that is, is understood and clearly seen—*that they may be,* he says, *inexcusable*[^42]. Why *inexcusable?* For because he had said: *The truth they hold in iniquity,* he shows how. *Because knowing God,* he says—he did not say: "Because they did not know God," but what does he say?—*because knowing God they did not honor him as God or give thanks, but became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened*[^43]. By what desert, but of pride? For see what follows: *For saying themselves to be wise,* he says, *they became fools*[^44]. For they ought not to arrogate to themselves what he had given, nor boast themselves of that which they had not from themselves, but from him: which surely had to be rendered to him, so that, for holding that which they could see, they might be healed by him who had given that they might see. For if they did this, they would keep humility, and could be purged and cohere to that most blessed contemplation. For to such the true and truthful physician would be revealed, the mediator, the stormer of pride, the exalter of humility. But you will say: "Was he not yet born humbly?" But he would be revealed through prophecy, just as he was revealed to Abraham—for the Lord himself said: *Abraham desired to see my day, and he saw it, and rejoiced*[^45]—just as he was revealed to the other fathers and prophets. For so they were saved by faith in the one to be born and to suffer, just as we, born and having suffered, are saved. But it is not to be wondered at that he revealed himself to their humility and veiled himself from their pride. But because pride was in them, the false and deceitful and proud one interposed himself, who promised them that through I-know-not-what arts of pride their souls would be purged, and made them worshipers of demons, that is, of evil angels. Hence all the sacraments which are celebrated by the pagans, which they say avail for the purgation of their souls.

**33.** And hear the Apostle saying this consequently, that as the wage of their pride they received these things, because they did not so honor God as God is to be honored. *Their foolish heart,* he says, *was darkened. Saying themselves to be wise, they became fools*[^46], now indeed through that false mediator, who rejoices in images, who rejoices when unclean cattle are fed—that is, the swine which that younger son fed, having gone far from the father: for as *the Lord is near to those who have crushed the heart*[^47], that is, the humble, so the Lord is far from those who have exalted the heart, that is, the proud. *Saying,* he says, *themselves to be wise, they became fools, and changed the glory of the incorruptible God into the likeness of an image of corruptible man*[^48]. Now they are images, and these indeed of all the Greeks and other nations, which have the likeness of men. But because there is no greater nor more superstitious idolatry than that of the Egyptians—for Egypt flooded the world with such figments, such as the Apostle next mentions—when he had said: *Into the likeness of an image of corruptible man,* he added: *And of birds and quadrupeds and serpents*[^49]. For have you, brethren, seen in other temples images with a dog's head or a bull's, or the figments of other irrational animals? Has anyone in any temple, except in the temple of Isis, seen these? For they are idols of the Egyptians. But comprehending both kinds, the Apostle says: *Into the likeness of an image of corruptible man and of birds and quadrupeds and serpents. Therefore God handed them over to the desires of their heart unto uncleanness, that they should affect their bodies with insults among themselves*[^50]. These evils of theirs come from impiety. For the head was pride. But these following sins are not only sins, but also punishments. For when he says: *God handed them over,* it is now of the vengeance of a certain sin, that these things should come about,} but these punishments are still sins too. Why? Because they can still return from them, if they will. But when it has come to punishment, it is no longer granted to return from it; that will be the punishment which will no longer be called sin. For in these middle things there are both punishments and sins. The first, which is pride, is sin alone, not yet punishment. Those which follow are both sins and punishments. He who has been unwilling to return from them will come to a punishment which is no longer sin, but the penalty of all sins. But because the first sin is pride, it is openly written elsewhere: *The beginning of all sin is pride*[^51]. And how is *avarice the root of all evils?*[^52] Because to wish more than God is avarice, to wish more than suffices is avarice. For God alone suffices the soul. Whence it is said by Philip: *Show us the Father, and it suffices us*[^53]. But what is prouder than he who, presuming on himself, forsakes God? What more avaricious than he for whom God does not suffice? It is, then, pride itself which is avarice in the origin of sins. Therefore the fornicating soul, the one true God forsaken as it were a lawful husband, prostitutes itself to many false gods, that is, demons, and is in no way satisfied.

**34.** How many gods the Romans worshiped! First as it were their own, the one true and unchangeable God dismissed, they worshiped as it were their proper gods. They began to fight with other nations, and believing them as it were defended by their own gods, they pressed on and by certain sacrifices to make the gods of those nations transgress, and they increased the number of their own gods, by taking up the sacrilegious rites of the nations whether subdued or to be subdued: so they invited in the dog-faced and bull-faced and serpent forms and bird shapes and all the monsters of the Egyptians, and so they appeased them as it were by propitiating them. For that the gods of the Egyptians too were even hostile to the gods of the Romans not long before the time of the Lord's incarnation is read among their own authors. For as the Apostle says of the Cretans: *A certain prophet of their own said: The Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy bellies*[^54], so we too can say: A certain prophet of their own said:  
monsters of gods of every kind and barking Anubis  
against Neptune and Venus and against Minerva  
hold their weapons.  
For it seemed unworthy to this poet that the dog-heads of the Egyptians fought against the human images of the Romans. But the demons fought against themselves, those of the Egyptians for the Egyptians, and those of the Romans for the Romans. But that they might possess together both nations, they composed it among themselves, and by the Romans all began to be worshiped. For so the Apostle says: *Not that an idol is anything, but what the nations sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons and not to God. I do not wish you to become partners of demons*[^55]. As these do, deceiving themselves and saying: &lt;...&gt; as if you despised the idol which the smith made, because it has no life: you would despise the idol, if you despised the demon which rejoices in the idol. In vain, then, do you interpret the image as the signification of a creature, because neither does the creature rejoice in such worship of venerating men, except the sinful creature which, growing proud, demands undue honor, deceitfully terrifying human frailty. *God handed them over,* he says, *to the desires of their heart unto uncleanness, that they should affect their bodies with insults among themselves,* {*who changed the truth of God into a lie, and worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed forever*[^56]. What is *they changed the truth of God into a lie?* *Into the likeness,* namely, *of an image of corruptible man and of birds and quadrupeds and serpents.*

**35.** And lest any of them say: "I do not worship images, but what the images signify," he at once subjoined: *And they worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator.* Understand prudently, dearly beloved. For they either worship an image, or a creature. He who worships an image changes *the truth of God into a lie.* For the sea is the truth, but Neptune a lie made by a man, the truth of God being changed into a lie, because God made the sea, but man the image of Neptune. So God made the sun too, but man, by making an image of the sun, changed *the truth of God into a lie.* But lest he say: "I do not worship the image, but I worship the sun," let him hear what follows: *They worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator*}*.* The Creator dismissed, then, they fell to the creature. And what is more shameful, they were not even content with that. For if, God dismissed, they worshiped what God made, they would be detestable. But how much more detestable are they, who, even the things God made dismissed, worship what smiths made? The creature dismissed, he goes to the image, and confounded about the image, when you try to defend yourself, you return to the creature. Now clearly at some time, that you may truly defend yourself, pass from the creature to the Creator. "But I cannot," he says, "come to him except through these things." Who says this? That learned one. Who says this? He who holds *the truth of God in a lie.* Who says this? He who, when he had known God, did not glorify him as God or give thanks: not he who did not know him, but he who knew him.

**36.** But of these proud men who *hold the truth of God in a lie* there are two kinds. Some committed themselves to their own virtue, sought no helper, thinking their souls could be purged through philosophy, as if needing no mediator. But of these there is no need to dispute at present. We are now dealing against the sacrilegious rites of the pagans. But those said that no sacrifices helped them. They say Pythagoras was such a one. Let that boasting too see what it promises itself of its own strength! He who presumes on himself presumes on a man. But the unhappy man, *who will free him from the body of this death, but the grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^57], through the one true mediator, God and man? Who, if he were only man, would not be a mediator; if he were only God, would not be a mediator. But if he were not a mediator, he would not reconcile man to God, who through iniquity had been far separated. And so these too hold *the truth of God in a lie,* because they presume on themselves, when *every man is a liar,* nor is anyone freed except through the confession of sins, with a propitiator interposed. And these the proud enemy of souls seduces, not indeed through sacrilegious rites, but in another way, making them like himself through the arrogance of human presumption, not seeking again in humility him from whom by pride they fell. Such were all to whom Christ was not revealed, even if they were unwilling to be purged by any false rites. For it is itself a great uncleanness for an unclean soul to think it can purge itself by itself. But of those who worshiped no idols nor bound themselves to any Chaldean or magic rites, nothing rash is to be said, lest perhaps it escape us that to them in some way that Savior was revealed, without whom no one can be saved.

**37.** But there are others who, when they had seen or believed that there is a God to whom they are to be reconciled and did not presume on their own strength, wished to be purged by rites, but they themselves too, swollen with vain curiosity and preferring themselves to the rest by the teachings of demons, through that very pride gave place to the devil and through the deceits of the airy powers, that is, of demons, and through vain mysteries thought they could be cleansed. Such the Apostle recalled, when he said: *They changed the glory of the incorruptible God into the likeness of an image of corruptible man and of birds and quadrupeds and serpents.* For certain of them stretched themselves and, all creation transcended, understood the Creator above all, but, remaining weak, they grew proud even in that. And now such men, or those who follow their authority, say to us: "We cannot be purged except through these mediators," powers, namely, of this kind. They seek, then, a mediator. Why? "Because the human mind, involved and oppressed by its desires, has need of purgation. And unless it is purged, what it sees unchangeable does not delight it, even if somewhat and in some way it can be seen." And that indeed they perceive well, that without a mediator no one can come to God.

**38.** But what the mediator is, must be sought. For there is a false mediator, there is a true one. The false mediator, as we have often said, is the devil. He interposes himself to those seeking badly and wishing to be proud, doing certain signs and miracles. For the magicians of Pharaoh too did similar things to Moses, although they could not do everything, because these do as much as the airy spirits through whom they do them are permitted. But God did as much as it pleased him. Through certain signs, then, as I said, the spirits interpose themselves to the proud and promise them purgation. But the true mediator is the one Lord Jesus Christ, whom both the ancient humble ones acknowledged through revelation, and through him wished to be purged. Before he was born of Mary, he was revealed to the worthy, that through faith in him who was to be born and to suffer they might be saved, just as we are saved through faith in him who was born and suffered. For therefore he came so humbly, that he might show that he does not purge and save any but the humble. For neither at that time too, before the Word became flesh, was he revealed only to the holy patriarchs and prophets among the Hebrew people, but examples are found also in other nations, since, whoever sought humbly, the humble mediator was not lacking to them, who alone reconciles to the Father and who alone most truly says: *No one comes to the Father but through me*[^58], accommodating to them his humility, that, persevering in humility, they might merit to be purged through him who is the humble mediator. For was Melchizedek of the people of Israel? And yet Scripture commends him a *priest of God Most High*[^59], in prefiguration of that very mediator. For by him Abraham too is blessed. Was Job, so great a man and so great a wrestler with the devil and a victor, who, sitting on a dunghill, full of worms, humble, conquered him by whom Adam, proud, with his body whole, was conquered in paradise—was this man of the people of Israel? And yet in his words that true mediator is understood to be preached and foretold. But as the mediator recalls certain ones, as much as was enough, so it is to be believed that the revelation of the mediator was present to all, through whom they might be purged, even before he had appeared in the flesh, who humbly sought the purging mediator, without whom no one is purged. But no one, except purged through the mediator, comes to that which, even if it can be looked at in some small part by a certain understanding of the soul, yet cannot be obtained except by the most purged. Therefore even if some sought in such a way, as the Apostle says, that they beheld the invisible things of God, understood through the things that are made, but in such a way that they held *the truth of God in a lie,* that is, that they said themselves wise and were inflated through pride, not worthily honoring him from whom they had received understanding, to such, as I said, that mediator opposes himself, proud to the proud, as humble to the humble, through certain congruences and through a certain ineffable and wonderful justice, remaining in the inner sanctuaries of God, which, even though we cannot see it, let us honor. The proud mediator, then, meets the proud, the humble the humble, but therefore the humble to the humble, that he may lead them to the height of God; therefore the proud to the proud, that he may shut off from the lofty in himself the height of God.

**39.** For see how the devil interposes himself in place of a mediator. He is not mortal in flesh, but a sinner. But the Lord willed to be mortal in flesh, but not a sinner. He shared death with men, not sins. For even if he were a sinner, he could not be a mediator. For if he were both mortal and a sinner, he would be what the rest of men are, and would no longer be a mediator, but would need a mediator. For any man whatever is a sinner and mortal. But God is just and immortal. The humble mediator is just, mortal, and not just in any way whatever, but just because God, mortal because man. But the proud mediator is unjust and in a certain manner immortal: for he is not dissolved in body, because he is not clothed with flesh—according to this I call him immortal; for true immortality is of God alone, according to what is said: *Who alone has immortality and dwells in inaccessible light*[^60], according to which the only Son too is immortal, because he and the Father are one, but he willed to be mortal, having taken on a man. The devil, then, unjust and in a certain manner immortal, interposed himself as a mediator to man, unjust, mortal. Two things at the bottom, two things at the top. But that the lowest may be reconciled to the highest, they need a mediator. What two things at the bottom? Unjust, mortal. What two things at the top? Just, immortal. That mediator, then, purging and leading back, if he were both unjust and mortal, would not be a mediator, who would not have one thing from the bottom and one from the top, but both from the bottom, both iniquity and mortality. Again, if he had immortality from the higher things and iniquity from the lower, he would have one from here and one from there and through this would seem a mediator, but not one leading back, because not purging. For having iniquity with man, he would be held worthy of punishment with him, and so he would be a mediator in such a way as to shut off the way to God. For sins know nothing but to separate from God; hear the Scripture saying: *Has God made the ear heavy, that it does not hear? But your sins separate between you and God*[^61].

**40.** Therefore sins shut off; but mortality does not shut off, because mortality is the penalty of sin, coming from the judgment of God. That shuts off which merited this penalty. We say this: not what God did to you shuts off your way to God, but what you yourself did to yourself. For the mortality of the body God made for you, but the sin you yourself made. And so that true and truthful mediator shared with you what God, avenging, did to you, but did not share with you what you, sinning, did. He shared with you mortality, but did not share with you iniquity. For he was made in mortal flesh, yet was not made through sin a debtor of death. For *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness of men and found in form as a man*[^62]. Which is so said that we do not think him changed, but that he willed to appear humbly and in servile form, remaining in secret Lord and God with God, the Son equal to the Father, through whom all things were made. And so, mortality assumed and the weakness of our penalty shared with us, he purges from sins and frees from mortality itself: therefore worthy by dying to slay death, because unworthy he suffered death. This is the true and truthful mediator, the humble and exalted mediator, the mediator leading back to that whence we fell. But that proud mediator, the false and deceitful mediator, has iniquity in common with sinful men; but he does not have the mortality of flesh, and therefore he does not loose their mortality, while he offers iniquity to be imitated, because, just as the iniquity of the first life made the desert of this death, so the iniquity of this life persevering makes the desert of eternal death. Which indeed the devil will suffer in common with those whom he seduces, for whom he does not now take on mortality, which is accomplished in the mortality of the flesh; and therefore he wishes to be a mediator, because he has one thing in common with them—that is, iniquity—but he does not have the other—that is, mortality.

**41.** Therefore the proud one rather seduces the proud, because mortality offends the proud more than iniquity, and therefore they rather abominate mortality in the humanity of Christ than iniquity in the pride of the devil. And so, inflating them with vain and false teachings, he leads them through I-know-not-what sacrilegious rites, promising purgation in temples, and through magic consecrations and detestable secrets draws them to astrologers, to soothsayers, to augurs, to diviners. He dares to boast himself stronger and better than Christ, because he was not born in flesh of a woman, not seized, not scourged, not spit upon, not crowned with thorns, not hung on a cross, not dead and buried: all which things the proud mock, which the humble mediator took on, not taking on iniquity with men and taking on humanity that he might heal them from the swelling of pride and make them victors over that false mediator, when they had learned to confess their sins, and through the justice of Christ be purged from their own injustice, and through the humble fellowship of his mortality come to the lofty summit of immortality.

**42.** Let us, then, reject, brethren, the malignant mediator, the false and deceitful mediator, the mediator not reconciling but more and more separating. Let no one promise you any purgation outside the Church, whether in temples or anywhere else through sacrilegious rites; let no one outside unity, even through the Christian sacraments, because, even if there is a sacrament outside unity—which we cannot deny nor dare to violate—yet the virtue and salvation of the sacrament, making one a coheir with Christ, is only in unity and in the bond of the peace of the Church. Let no one seduce you from God, no one from the Church, no one from God the Father, no one from the Church the mother. From God that false mediator wishes to separate, transfiguring himself into an angel of light, and his ministers, transfiguring themselves into ministers of justice; if he cannot lead to sacrilegious rites, he wishes to separate from the Church either to heresies or to schisms, that, by deserting the mother, the Father be offended. We had two parents who begot us mortally, we have two who beget us immortally: God and the Church. Those begot successors to themselves, these beget those who will remain with them. For to what else were we born of human parents, but that we might succeed the dead? But by the Father God and the Mother Church we are so begotten that we may live with our parents forever. Whoever goes to sacrilegious rites or to magic arts or to consult astrologers, augurs, diviners about his life or what pertains to this life, and the diabolical machinations of this world, is separated from the Father, although he does not withdraw from the Church. But if anyone is separated from the Church by the division of schism, although he seems to hold the Father for himself, he most ruinously deserts the mother. But he deserts both who forsakes both the Christian faith and the mother Church. Hold the Father, hold also the mother. You are a little one: cling to the mother. You are a little one: suck the milk of the mother, and nourished with milk she will lead you to the table of the Father.

**43.** Your Savior took on flesh, your mediator took on flesh, and by taking on flesh he took on the Church. He fore-tasted as it were from the head what he would offer to God, *a priest forever* and *the propitiation for our sins*[^63]. The Word took on human nature, and the two were made one, as it is written: *They shall be two in one flesh. This is a great sacrament,* he says, *but I speak in Christ and in the Church*[^64]. The bridal chamber of this marriage was the womb of the virgin. *And he, like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber, exulted like a giant to run the way*[^65]. *A giant* because strong, conquering weakness by weakness and slaying death by death. But he ran in the way: he did not stand in the way, lest he become a man who is signified to have stood in the way of sinners. For when the psalm says: *Blessed is he who has not gone in the counsel of the impious and in the way of sinners has not stood*[^66], it signifies a certain one who stood in the way of sinners. The Lord Jesus Christ, then, ran in the way of sinners, but Adam stood in the way of sinners. And because he stood, wounded by robbers, he fell and lay there. But he saw it, who so walked this way that he did not stand, but ran; he found the wounded one, set him on his beast, and handed him to the innkeeper, because he himself ran the way, that he might fulfill what was foretold of him: *He drank from the torrent in the way, therefore he will lift up his head*[^67]. For the torrent is this world. Torrent waters are those which flow from sudden rains, whether winter ones or floods, soon surely to pass away. So are all these temporal things: it is a passing torrent, soon to cease. On this day of the first of January, those who rejoice in the luxuries and vanities of the world do not see the rush of the torrent sweep them away. Let them call back, if they can, a day like that of the prior year; let them call back at least yesterday's day. They do not see even their joys passing like a torrent, that they may afterward say: *These are they whom we once had for a laughingstock and a likeness of reproach. We fools esteemed their life madness and their end without honor. How are they counted among the sons of God, and their lot is among the saints? Therefore we have erred from the way of truth, and the light of justice has not shone for us, and the sun has not risen for us*[^68]. What *sun?* Is it this visible one? Daily this one rises for them. For of this the Lord says: *Who makes his sun to rise upon the good and the evil*[^69]. There is another who made this sun, invisible and intelligible, that sun of justice, of which it is said in another place: *The sun of justice has risen for me*[^70]. He has not risen for these. And hear what they lament: *What did pride profit us, and what did the boasting of riches confer on us? All those things passed away like a shadow*[^71]. As if that torrent had now flowed away; but yet he who was born, suffered, was crucified, was buried, rose again: he drank of that torrent in the way, passing hence to the Father, with the faithful to follow for whom he drank of this torrent in the way; therefore he lifted up his head, that is, himself.

**44.** For he himself is the head of the Church, who has now ascended into heaven and sits at the right hand of the Father, showing us in his own whole-burnt offering what we too ought to hope for of our flesh. For as the Apostle says: *For by hope we are saved, and in ourselves we groan, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body*[^72]. For we are adopted, but he is the only one born: *in the beginning the Word* God *with God,* as the evangelist testifies, and equal to the Father, as the apostle Paul says, yet he was made humble, that he might be *the one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^73]. But such as is that evil and false mediator who shuts off the way to God, such all proud men strive to be: wherever they are, they wish to be like their mediator. And as you see in men, so also in angels. He who wished to be a mediator and reconciler to God, of whom we have said much, that is, the humble and exalted mediator, the Lord Jesus Christ, took on every creature—for in man is all of it: for we said above that the creature is either spiritual or corporeal—he took on, then, the whole man, taking the form of a servant; whatever is in the rational mind, whatever in the soul, whatever in the flesh, he took on the whole, except sin. Therefore in him are all things, lest anyone err by seeking, for his purgation, a mediatrix and as it were cleansing creature; therefore that mediator has the whole, therefore that mediator took on the whole, because a mediating creature of such a kind can cleanse from iniquities and free from mortality, which does not presume itself to be of any worth, but clings personally and is in an ineffable manner coupled and mingled to God the Word, so that it could be said: *The Word was made flesh.* Whose humility was despised to this end and exalted, lest men despair of themselves out of humility and presume out of pride. When, therefore, proud men wish to be worshiped and, if God be set before them, are angry, they are imitators of that deceitful mediator. On the contrary, the holy humble men, when they are so honored by erring men who wish to place their hope in man, not attending to the divine Scripture which says: *Cursed is everyone who places his hope in man*[^74], grieve over them in their heart and faithfully admonish them, as far as they can, to hope in God, not in man, since {not even the Apostle himself wished hope to be placed in himself, but in the truth which he announced. What was said through him was better than he himself through whom it was said, and he wished those who believed to place their hope in what was said through him, not in himself through whom it was said. *Although if we,* he says—it is too little to say *if we,* hear what follows—*or an angel,* he says, *from heaven announce to you anything besides what you have received, let him be anathema*[^75]. He saw that the false mediator could transfigure himself into an angel of light and announce something false. As, then, proud men wish to be worshiped in place of God, to arrogate to themselves whatever they can, to be named and, if it can be, to surpass Christ himself in glory, so the devil and his angels.

**45.** My brethren, we speak to catholics. The Donatists hold Donatus in place of Christ. If they hear some pagan detracting from Christ, they perhaps bear it more patiently than if they hear one detracting from Donatus.} You know what I say, and you are compelled to experience this daily. So perversely do they love Donatus that they set him before Christ. For not only do they have nothing to say, but they even know that they have nothing to say. For nothing holds them but the name of Donatus under the name of Christ; to the name of a man against Christ they have been seduced. Therefore they have contracted against us a most monstrous hatred, because we cry out to them: "Do not place your hope in man, lest you be cursed." They hate those who preach peace and, if they suffer anything for their so great crime—and not for Christ, but for Donatus—they think themselves martyrs; and because we say to them: "Do not blow away the baptism of Christ, love peace, give yourself back to the world: Christ redeemed the whole by his blood, do not draw the buyer of the whole back to a part," for this they hate us and, if the chance be given, they kill us by the hand of the Circumcellions. But because the Lord helped us, we escaped, giving thanks to the mercy of the Lord. Whence we admonish and ask you to pray for us, that the Lord may always inspire in us confidence to preach his peace, and that we may not fear them, but rather love them and rejoice that there is fulfilled in us what is written: *With those who hated peace I was peaceful; when I spoke to them, they fought against me without cause*[^76]. And if they cannot otherwise be healed, let them fight against us, strike us, kill us, and yet be healed.

**46.** But let us return, brethren, to that which I had begun to say; for grief snatched me from what I had intended. As there are proud men—they prefer that they be worshiped rather than God—so there are proud angels who prefer that they be worshiped rather than God. And as there are holy men—they prefer that God be worshiped rather than themselves—so all the holy angels, which without any doubt is also to be believed of the martyrs, prefer that God be worshiped rather than themselves. They wish him to be honored whom they honor; in him they choose to be loved; private honors from men they not only do not accept, but even reject. Whence we have given an example too, as much as the Lord gave, of that angel in the Apocalypse, who was unwilling to be adored by a man, but God. Do not, then, fear, brethren, lest perhaps, if you worship God alone with religious service, you offend some holy angel or some martyr. For if the prudence of the flesh persuades you of this, measuring from yourselves—if perhaps it delights you, by a certain private arrogance of pride, not in God nor for God's sake, but in yourselves for your own sake, to be honored, so that, if it is not done, you are indignant—and you so think that either the holy angels or the holy martyrs rejoice in such services of men and require for themselves the service that is owed to God, by a most easy seduction you will be deceived by the pagans and will withdraw altogether far from the Lord your God, of whom we have received such a command: *You shall adore the Lord your God and him alone shall you serve*[^77]. Do you wish securely to honor the holy angels and the holy martyrs? Worship him, in whom alone they wish themselves to be honored. For if they are holy, they are angry with you, if you worship them privately and not him alone from whom they too received the grace of their beatitude. Because, then, they are holy, the more do not wish to offend them by as it were wishing to worship them apart. For worshiping God, you worship everyone who by pious love and holy devotion cleaves to God. But if he is angry, because he himself is not worshiped by some proper sacraments, he is now that proud transgressor and false mediator, transfiguring himself into an angel of light. Now if he has bent you to himself, he has shut off the way: not only can you not come through him, but through him rather you are not permitted to come.

**47.** Let them not sell you their empty talk and say: "We worship the powers of God, that through them we may come to God, and we make those rites to Saturn, those to Jupiter, those to Pluto, those to Neptune, those to Mars, those to Ceres." For therefore not all rejoice in the same rites, because they arrogate to themselves private and proper worship; and through this they are not rites, but sacrileges. And in all these their prince exults and triumphs, delighted not so much in those rites as in the deception and error of men through proud malevolence. Let them not, then, seduce you, when they say to you: "If you worship the martyrs and think yourselves helped through them before God, how much more ought we to worship the powers of God, through which we are helped before God." For attend to the sacraments of the Church and see whether a sacrifice is offered to any martyr, so that we display one thing to that martyr, another to that one; but at all the memorials we offer one thing, nor to any of them, but to the Lord of us all, in which sacrifice we honor even the martyrs according to their rank, not in themselves, but in him through whom they conquered the devil. And the more dearly they are mindful of us, the less we offer them private rites, because, in whom alone they have their joy, in him alone they have their honor. If anyone says to you: "Invoke the angel Gabriel thus, invoke Michael thus, display this to that one, this to this one," be not deceived, do not consent; nor let it deceive you that the names of these angels are read in the holy Scriptures: attend rather to how they are read there, whether they exacted anything for themselves from men of private religion and did not rather always wish glory to be given to the one God, whom they obey.

**48.** But not only if a man, but if anyone as it were an angel, whether by some visitation or through a dream, should wish to tempt you and say: "Do this for me, celebrate this for me, because I am an angel," for example "Gabriel," do not believe it. Worship securely the one God, who is Father and Son and Holy Spirit. By such worship of yours he rejoices, if he is an angel; but if he is angry, because you did not give something apart, he is now to be understood as that one of whom the Apostle says that he *transfigures himself into an angel of light:* he wishes to shut off the way, he interposes himself badly; he is not a mediator reconciling, but rather separating. For that angel in the Apocalypse and the rest like him do not wish themselves to be adored, but God. They are messengers, announcing what they have received to announce; they are ministers, doing what they have been bidden to do, carrying our prayers to God, not themselves exacting them in place of God. The angel says to a man: *I offered your prayer in the sight of the brightness of God*[^78]. Yet he did not ask the angel himself, and that one was asking God: the minister offered his prayer. Did he say, as the perverse and corrupt ministers of certain powers do: "Give me something, if you wish me to announce it, if you wish me to admit you"? Our Lord does not have such a great house. His servants love him, his sons love him. If you should wish to corrupt one of them as it were privately, that you may be admitted to their Lord, you will be driven far from that great house. For if . . . they imitate the angelic life—because it is written: *There is one soul and one heart toward God, and no one has anything proper, but all things are common to them*[^79], as we read in the Acts of the Apostles that so many thousands of men from the number of the Jews were converted, to whom, possessing nothing proper, from the common store *it was distributed to each as each had need*[^80]—if, then, these imitate the angelic life, and if to any of them some friend of his, or brother or father or mother or any near one, should wish to offer something privately, he not only rejects it, but even admonishes that, if it is to be offered and received, it be offered to God, just as those did who placed the prices of their goods at the feet of the apostles, just as those too whom the Apostle exhorted to make some offering for the poor of the saints who were at Jerusalem—and he teaches this, that they may know they offer to God what they do—how much more those angels, surely more perfect, who in the heavenly arrangements and in the great house and the heavenly city Jerusalem, the mother of us all, serve God in most concordant love, wish no honor to be displayed to themselves privately and properly, but rejoice in that honor alone which is displayed in common to God as God, and to the city itself as his temple, that is, to the whole Church.

**49.** And therefore, on account of the purgation which comes about through a mediator, he willed there to be one mediator and that he himself become man, who is equal to the Father, that through a kindred substance—which is man—we might come to the highest substance—which is God. And therefore he descended, because we were in the lower things, and ascended, lest we remain in the lower things. He is the one true mediator, who deceives no one, who even, though he is equal to the Father, willed for our sake to be also less than he, not by losing what is equal, but by taking on what is less. He has already freed our flesh too in his flesh. *He now does not die, and death will no longer have dominion over him*[^81]. To him come our prayers, although in the sacraments of the Church they are directed to the Father. *He now does not die.* {For he himself, the high priest, offers them, who offered himself a whole-burnt offering for us. He himself is the one who leads us through, interposing himself, not to shut off, but to direct; not to separate, but to reconcile; not to hinder, but to break the hindrances. He himself is the one pontiff and the one priest, who was prefigured in the ancient priests of God. Therefore a priest without blemish of body was sought, because he alone lived without the blemish of sin, even in a mortal body. For what was prefigured in their body was signified in his life.} But we all bishops are called priests for this reason, that we are set over. Yet the whole Church is the body of that priest. To the priest pertains his own body. For {the apostle Peter says to the Church itself: *A holy people, a royal priesthood*[^82].

**50.** Then one priest was anointed, now all Christians are anointed. The king was anointed, the priest was anointed, the rest were not anointed. Both persons the Lord bore, not in figure, but now in truth, both of king and of priest. For therefore in that very king David, of whose seed he was made according to the flesh, both were figured. He was a king, which we know and which is manifest. There was prefigured in him also the person of the priest, when he ate of those loaves of proposition, which it was not lawful to eat except for the priests alone: which the Lord himself too recalled in the Gospel, that by those who can understand he might be recognized to have been prefigured in David. For Mary too drew her origin not only from a royal race, but also from a priestly one. And of the royal race it is manifest; on this account of the Lord himself the Apostle says: *Who was made to him of the seed of David according to the flesh*[^83]. For although Joseph is called his father by the effect of love, yet he was not born of his seed, as is declared in the Gospel. It remains, then, that Christ be called of the seed of David on account of Mary, because she was of the seed of David, that is, of the royal one. But how do we find also a priestly race in Mary? It is written in the Gospel that the priest Zechariah had Elizabeth as a wife from the daughters of Aaron. Therefore Elizabeth was of the priestly race: for Aaron the brother of Moses was a priest, and the whole tribe of Levi. But it is said in the Gospel to the Virgin Mary by the angel: *Your kinswoman, Elizabeth*[^84]. If, then, Elizabeth, one of the daughters of Aaron the priest, was a kinswoman of Mary, it is not doubted that the Virgin Mary pertained not only to the royal but also to the priestly blood. Wherefore in the Lord, according to the man he took on, both persons are present, the royal and the priestly: the royal, leading us to every imitation of spiritual warfare, *until he set his enemies under* his *feet, and the last enemy to be destroyed is death*[^85]—for by our enemy the king himself too was tempted, that the soldier might learn to fight—; but the priestly, by which to expiate and purge our sins he offered himself a whole-burnt offering. Therefore then in figure two were anointed: the king and the priest.}

**51.** Therefore there are also two fish with those five barley loaves. For fish pertain to the relish: but relishes are wont to be seasoned with oil. Two fish, then, with five loaves, that is, with the Old Testament, where the five books of Moses hold the primacy. But with the seven loaves, which are not called barley and now signify the New Testament, a few fish are said to have been, because Christians had now begun to be few, that is, the anointed, from whom the whole Church throughout the world might be filled. For the five barley loaves signify, covered as it were with little husks, those things in the law itself which are to be understood spiritually, which, when set forth, the Lord instructed the apostles. Whence there from the remnants of the fragments twelve baskets are filled. But the seven loaves signify the sevenfold working of the Spirit in the New Testament, as John too says in the Apocalypse. For the seven spirits are understood to be recalled for the seven spiritual workings, of which Isaiah earlier made mention: *of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and fortitude, of knowledge and piety, and the fear of the Lord*[^86]. And therefore from those remnants of fragments seven baskets were filled, by which number the whole Church is signified. Whence the same apostle John too writes to seven churches. And in the epistles of the apostle Paul that same sevenfold number is found, not of epistles, but of churches. And many things in those sacraments, sought more diligently, are found more sweetly. But now from this I have recalled something on account of the anointing of the fish, that I might show the royal and priestly person signified then by the two fish, because two were then anointed, the king and the priest. But from the chrism (chrisma) Christ is named, that is, from the anointing. But the body of Christ is the Church. And therefore all Christians are anointed, by a hidden sacrament to the rest, but known to the faithful. But the only priest is the mediator himself, the head of the Church without sin, through whom comes about the purgation of our sins.

**52.** Whence it comes to my mind with great grief to recall that Parmenian, once a bishop of the Donatists, dared in a certain letter of his to set down that the bishop is the mediator between the people and God. You see that they set themselves up in place of the bridegroom: with sacrilegious adultery they corrupt the souls of others. This is no ordinary audacity, and it would seem altogether incredible to me, unless I had read it. For if the bishop is a mediator between the people and God, since there are many bishops, it follows that many mediators are understood. That the letter of Parmenian may be read, then, let the letter of the apostle Paul be blotted out, who says: *For there is one God, and one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^87]. But between whom is there a mediator, except between God and his people? Therefore between God and his body, since his body is the Church. Monstrous, then, is that pride, which dares to set up the bishop as a mediator, claiming for himself the marriage of Christ by adulterous deceit. Let us see the friend of the bridegroom, zealous for the bridegroom, not setting himself up in place of the bridegroom. Does he say: "I betrothed you to myself"? He can say this who says that he is a mediator between the people and God, not this one who says: *Was Paul crucified for you, or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*; not this one who says: *For there is one God, and one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus;* not this one who says: *I betrothed you to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ*[^88]. And therefore that adulterer, who did not have the wedding garment, was cast out from the wedding feast: for he did not have a garment in which to honor the bridegroom, but through the garment he had, he sought his own honor in the bridegroom's feast.

**53.** Therefore we have one mediator, brethren, who is also our head. But we, in the name of Christ, even if we are not set over the churches with you, are yet members of the body of Christ with you: we have one head, not many; for a body that wishes to have many heads is now a monster. But we were speaking of the anointing, because then the priest alone was anointed and the king, but now all Christians. Hence see that you all pertain with us to the body of the priest, that is, because you are all faithful; yet especially are those called priests who are set over the Church, but not therefore is the rest of the body not the body of the priest. And therefore {in those ancient sacraments, because that one was prefigured, that is, our Lord the priest Jesus Christ, one priest entered into the holy of holies; but all the people stood outside. Now when the bishops stand at the altar, are you outside, and do you not see within and hear and bear witness and receive? Then one priest entered into the holy of holies once a year. The year signifies all time. Once, then, in all time he entered into the holy of holies, not the figured but the true, beyond the veils of the heavens, our one priest rising from the dead, the Lord Jesus Christ, offering himself for us. He entered, and there he is. But the people still stands outside with us: we have not yet risen to go to meet Christ and to remain always with him within, when he is to say to the good servant: *Enter into the joy of your Lord*[^89]. This, then, was figured then by the one priest alone entering into the holy of holies, and the people standing outside, which is now fulfilled through our Lord Jesus Christ alone entering above the secrets of the heavens, and the people still groaning outside, saved in hope, awaiting the redemption of its body, which is to be in the resurrection of the dead.} But yet we have a priest and pontiff who intercedes for us in the holy of holies, sitting at the right hand of the Father, that we may not fear in this our pilgrimage, if we do not stray from the way of truth, if we do not love another in place of him, but love one another in him, that in every brother of ours walking his ways we may behold him, honor him, receive him, *who was handed over for our offenses and rose again for our justification*[^90] {because he himself speaks in his saints, as the Apostle says: *Do you wish to receive proof of him who speaks in me, Christ?*[^91] And although he says: *Neither is he who plants anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase*[^92], not because he wished himself, but because he wished him to be loved in himself, he gives testimony to certain ones, saying: *As an angel of God you received me, as Christ Jesus*[^93]. In all his saints, then, he himself is to be loved, because he says: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^94]. For he did not say: "You gave to them," but: *You gave to me;* so great is the love of the head toward its body!}

**54.** And so, because the mediator and priest himself alone entered into the holy of holies, but the Church—which is his body—still, as we said, groans outside in tearful prayers and laborious works, we do not find in the ancient books that a priest commended himself to the people, that it might pray for him, because he was figuring our Lord Jesus Christ, for whom no one prays. For of whom was the priest a figure, for whom no one prayed, but of our Lord Jesus Christ *who intercedes for us*[^95] and does not need our prayers for himself? Which he deigned to figure even on this earth, when he prayed alone on the mountain, while the disciples in the little boat were troubled by storms. And we too, as it were in a ship, in the Church are tossed by the storms of this world; but because he, as then on the mountain, so now in the heights of the heavens *intercedes for us,* let us be secure.

**55.** The apostle John too says: *These things I write to you that you may not sin. And if anyone has sinned, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the just: he is the propitiation for our sins*[^96]. See who John is? He who reclined upon the breast of the Lord and drank from that breast in that banquet what he would belch forth to the peoples: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.* Did that John, then, say: "And if anyone has sinned, you have me with the Father: I pray for you"? See who says what. For not only did he not say this, but even if he said: "And if anyone has sinned, you have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the just: he is the propitiation for your sins," he would perhaps seem proud and arrogant. He did not say this. And if he did not say this, with how great pride would he say: "You have me as an advocate with the Father!", with how much more monstrous a sacrilege would he say: "You have me as a mediator with the Father!", so as to wish to interpose himself between sinners and God! And this these men neither fear nor blush to say, that the bishop is a mediator between God and men. Clearly he is a mediator, but in the party of Donatus, to shut off, not to lead through, as Donatus did: for he interposed his own name, to shut off the way to Christ. For thence they are unwilling to come to the Church, because Donatus shut it off, because . . . through a whitewashed wall: for it is earthly, not by Christian religion. He whitewashed it that he might deceive . . . and those coming as far as him could not pass over to Christ, that is, to the body of Christ which is the Church spread throughout the whole world. See how he made himself a mediator, as that proud angel too does, of whom we have already said much. But attend to that saying of John: *These things I write to you that you may not sin. And if anyone has sinned, we have an advocate with the Father.* He would not say *we have,* unless because he knew himself well and humbly commended himself. *And he himself is,* he says, *the propitiation for our sins.* He did not say "yours," as if making himself a stranger to sins. But if he said that, there would be recited to him his own saying in another place: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^97].

**56.** And so, brethren, give heed that you do not sin, strive strongly that you do not sin. But if anyone has sinned, he will expiate us who *is the propitiation for our sins.* Avoid all those evils through which the name of God is blasphemed, that in your good way of life, others being gained, there may be glory to the name of Christ. Avoid above all those things which can separate you from the altar of God. But those sins which through the habit of daily life creep in daily, and as it were from the waves of the sea of this world through a certain weakness of ours do not cease to enter in, bail out by good works, lest you suffer shipwreck. Let those daily wounds be healed by the daily medicines of alms, fasts, prayers. Be fervent in all these good works, and neither show to men what you do for the sake of your own glory nor avoid the eyes of those wishing to imitate, *that they may see your good deeds and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^98]. Whatever you do, *do all to the glory of God*[^99], that he, the humble and exalted, may make us from humble exalted. For so our alms will come to him who for our sake *became poor, though he was rich*[^100], so our fasts will come to him who for our sake fasted; so our prayers will come to him, and when in them we truly ask that *our debts be forgiven us, as we too forgive our debtors*[^101], just as he too forgave his debtors, hanging on the cross saying: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^102]. Let him be blessed, who gave you to be able to do what you do. For if you attribute to yourselves and arrogate to yourselves your good works, so that in those very sacrifices of humility pride inflates you, you will give place to that false mediator, that he may interpose himself and shut off the way to you. For he seeks all the entrances and through those very good works tries to creep in by serpentine slidings. And if he finds a man as it were working well and attributing to himself what he does and by a certain swelling exalting himself over the one who does not do it, he at once sets himself against him, that he may deceive him in place of a mediator.

**57.** But as I had begun to say, we do not find any priest in the ancient books commending himself to the people, that it might pray for him, because they were figuring him for whom no one was going to pray, that is, our Lord Jesus Christ, who is the one mediator and priest, not figured but true. But the apostle Paul, who knew himself to be of the body of the priest with the rest of the members, commends himself to the prayers of the Church, because *the members are mutually solicitous* for one another; *and if one member is glorified, all the members rejoice together, and if one member suffers, all the members suffer together*[^103]. The head intercedes for all the members; let the members intercede for one another under the head. What, then, does the Apostle say? *Praying,* he says, *at the same time for us too, that God may open to us a door for the word*[^104]. And the Church prayed for Peter too, when Peter was in chains, and was heard, just as Peter too prayed for the Church, because the members pray for one another. These men do not call themselves mediators and pray for those by whom they wish to be prayed for; and those call themselves mediators, who, if they took themselves out of the middle, both would become one which by criminal pride they divided. Far be it, then, from the hearts of Christians what is shown in the pride of the heretics! As we have said to your love, that first priesthood bore a certain figure: now is fulfilled what was then figured. We have a mediator and pontiff: he ascended into heaven, entered within the veil, into that true, not figured, holy of holies. The sacrament of this thing is celebrated in the Church: within you pray with us, you answer "Amen" to the words of the bishop. For so the people as it were subscribes, because all pertain to the body of the priest. Let no one, then, as is wont to be said, sell you smoke: we have one mediator, the Lord Jesus Christ; he is the prayer, *he is the propitiation for our sins;* let us securely hold to him.

**58.** For you see, brethren, that we may now return to that which I was saying against the pagans, how you are fortified, so that you not only not be conquered by them, but—as far as can be done through you—may, having convicted them, gain them to salvation and pray and fast for them, that they may know God and honor him as he is to be honored, not as the Apostle says of certain ones: *that, when they had known God, they did not glorify him as God or give thanks, but became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened*[^105], so that they sought a superstitious and sacrilegious defilement under the name of purgation. For wishing to cleave to that thing which always is, which is always of the same kind, which remains unchangeable, which in some way by sharp talent they were able to touch, but with a humble heart were unwilling to honor, they fell into that false mediator, who envies the human soul and tries in all ways that it not pass from the labors in which he has dominion to the rest where it would be loftier than he, to whom pertain all sacrilegious rites and the most deceitful machinations of the iniquity of astrologers, soothsayers, diviners, Chaldeans.

**59.** But you, my brethren, who perhaps do not see with the keenness of the mind what those men saw, and are not yet fit to transcend by the thought of the mind all creation, not only corporeal but also spiritual, and to see the unchangeable God from whom and through whom and in whom all things were made, do not tremble, do not despair, because he who willed to descend even to the lowest and weakest has made himself the way for you. For what does it profit them that, from afar, growing proud, they see the fatherland? They do not find the way, since to that height of the fatherland the way begins from humility. They see the fatherland as it were from the mountain of pride, they see it from the opposite mountain. But no one ascends to it unless he has first descended. They are unwilling to descend, that they may be able to ascend, that is, they are unwilling to be humble, that they may be able to be Christians. When they say to themselves: "Am I to be this too which my doorkeeper is, and not rather what Plato was, what Pythagoras?", blowing empty things with a reckless mouth, they are unwilling to descend, they cannot ascend. For our Lord descended to us from the height of his majesty, and these are unwilling to descend to him from the swelling of their pride. He descended, then, that he might choose the weak things of the world to confound the strong, and the foolish things of the world to confound the wise, *and he chose the lowborn things of the world and the contemptible things, and the things that are not, as though they were, that he might empty out the things that are*[^106].

**60.** For therefore he willed to become man, that, made God-man, he might teach humility, not by changing himself into man, but by taking on man. To this humility it would seem nothing could be added, and yet not even of human things did he choose those of which men are puffed up. He did not choose noble parents or those endowed with any dignity. For he willed to be born of a woman who was betrothed to a carpenter, lest anyone, against the justice of the poor and lowborn, boast of the nobility of his parents and be incurably inflated. He did not even choose a noble city in which to be born—Bethlehem is of few men—: thence indeed he himself was, of the tribe of David. Yet in that very tribe, as in the rest, there were many poor and lowborn. And David himself, of whose seed the Lord Jesus Christ was made according to the flesh, did he not feed sheep, before he was divinely chosen for the kingdom? For even in him he had chosen the lowborn things of the world, that the noble might be confounded, and in that very thing was prefigured what would be accomplished through his seed. He chose, then, a humble race from which to be born. {But perhaps someone would say: "Even if he himself was born humbly, in the nobility of his disciples he wished to boast himself." He did not choose kings, or senators, or philosophers, or orators. Rather he chose plebeians, the poor, the unlearned, fishermen. Peter a fisherman, Cyprian an orator. Unless the fisherman faithfully went before, the orator would not humbly approach. Let no one despair of himself as abject: only let him hold Christ, and his hope will not be false.}

**61.** For those who from afar, as I had begun to say, and from the opposite mountain of pride see the fatherland, spurn humility: therefore they do not hold the way. For our way is humility. This Christ showed in himself. If anyone deviates from this way, he will run into the perplexed and inextricable mountain that interposes itself, into that devil who interposes himself in place of a mediator, ruinously and deceitfully, through innumerable sacrilegious rites, through diviners, augurs, soothsayers, astrologers, magicians. For those given over to these do not descend to the way, but wander on a certain wooded mountain, from which some of them lift their eyes and see the fatherland, but cannot come to it, because they do not hold the way. But those who now hold the way, that is, the true and truthful mediator, the mediator leading through and not shutting off, the mediator purging and not entangling, perseveringly walk in what they hold. For some of them too see the fatherland, some of them do not see it. But let those who do not yet see the fatherland not withdraw from the way, and they will come there where those too who see arrive. For there is in some such keenness of eyes that they can see from afar. To these it profits nothing to see where they may go and not to know by what way they may proceed. But if they know the way, it does not so much profit them to see from afar whither they may arrive, as that they know whither they tend. But those who do not have such keenness, if they walk together, will arrive together. He, then, of you who can transcend all creation by the mind and see the ineffable light of wisdom, when he has seen it, will see that it cannot be told, and will see that all things said of it are unworthy of its greatness, although they are fit for the little ones, who are nourished to this end, that they may hear as it can be told, until they hear what cannot be told. But those who cannot transcend creation and see the ineffable truth, let them hold the mediator, whom, unless even those hold who now see something unchangeable, they surely see in vain. In him we have the corporeal creature, which he took on even in the flesh; in him also the spiritual creature, because there is the soul, there is the rational mind; in him the very Word through whom all things were made, because *the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us.* Holding this way, let us not deviate from it, lest we run into false mediators who promise us purgation and bring hindrances upon us.

**62.** For he alone is the mediator, *in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge,* *who is the head of all principality and power*[^107]; through that which we hold, he leads us through to that which we ought to hold. For nothing so holds a man as human affection. Therefore he, God and man, through that which we hold, conveys us to that which is to be held. For lest a man think himself far from human nature, that Word of God through whom all things were made . . . but to draw near to the heavenly or supercelestial virtues, and therefore to wish those very ones to become for him mediatrixes and cleansers toward that Word, and so through pride and vain curiosity to fall into the airy powers which deceive and beguile the senses of human weakness by various and manifold errors, envying the passage to God and desiring to dominate the captured, therefore the very Word through whom all things were made *was made flesh,* that is, coupled to himself even a fleshly nature from man, that so a man might learn that what he thought drew near only to the heavenly was not far from his own nature, and, purged through the recognized middle thing, might cleave to the unchangeable divinity. Therefore he deigned to do both earthly and heavenly miracles, that he might show that even those very things were subject to him, by which men could be terrified in lying signs and prodigies. He showed too that all spiritual power either fears Christ or loves Christ, so that what fears Christ may not be feared by the Christian, but what loves Christ may be loved in Christ.

**63.** Wherefore, dearly beloved brethren, let not men terrify you from this either, because they say that those powers of the elements, on account of the necessities of this life and on account of the temporal things which are necessary for our uses, are to be appeased and honored by their sacraments: neither do these have any power, except as much as they are permitted from above. Remember that holy Job, and that he could not be tested by such great temptations, unless the tempter received power from the Lord God. And attend to the Apostle saying: *God is faithful, who will not let you be tempted beyond what you can bear, but will make with the temptation also a way out, that you may be able to endure*[^108]. Attend too to those who presume on such things and bind to the sacrilegious sacraments of demons and to magic arts the souls of the light, how they lose their salvation for nothing. For neither do they not suffer the things that the rest of men suffer, or do they not suffer even worse things with the torment of a bad conscience: losses, diseases, condemnations, deaths, very often from the common scourge of the human race, very often even for their very deeds. See whether those who worship Neptune sail more happily than those who do not worship him; whether those who are bound to the temple of Tellus have more fertile fields than those who are held by no such superstition; whether the women who worship Juno give birth with less pain or peril than the Christian women who detest her; whether those who worship Mercury are keener than those who mock such figments, or, because he is also said to be the god of merchandise, whether those who sacrifice to him make more profits than those who defile themselves with no such sacrilege. And so through all things lead the prudent eye of the mind even over these temporal advantages: you will find all things governed under the most high power of God, serving whom alone the Christian, and holding the way, his mediator, takes no care for the deceitful enticements and terrors of vanity, giving honor to the most true God, whether by the prosperity of temporal things his temperance be proved, or by adversities his fortitude, because *God is faithful, who does not let* us *be tempted beyond what* we *can bear, but makes with the temptation also a way out, that* we *may be able to endure,* consoling us in all things and filling us with the joy of good hope, until he leads us where the way leads which he deigned to provide for our weakness. Turned to, etc.  
Here ends the treatise of Saint Augustine delivered on the first of January against the pagans, concerning the false mediator, the devil, and the true mediator, Christ.
