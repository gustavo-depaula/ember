# Sermon 286: On the Heavenly Birthday of the Martyrs Protasius and Gervasius.

**1. 1.** “Martyrs” is a Greek word, but common usage now treats it as Latin; in Latin it means “witnesses.” Thus there are true martyrs and false martyrs, because there are true witnesses and false witnesses. But Scripture says, *A false witness will not go unpunished*. If a false witness will not be without punishment, neither will a true witness be without a crown. It was indeed easy to bear witness to the Lord Jesus Christ and to the truth, since he is God; but to do so even to death was a great achievement. There were certain leaders of the Jews, whom the Gospel mentions, who had believed in the Lord Jesus but, it says, did not dare to confess him publicly because of the Jews. Immediately a mark of reproach was added to their name, for the Evangelist went on to say, *For they loved human glory more than God’s*. Thus there were some who were ashamed to confess Christ before others; but there were others, already better, who were not ashamed to confess Christ before others, yet could not confess him even to death. For these are God’s gifts, and sometimes they are nourished in the soul by degrees.

**2. 1.** First consider these three witnesses and compare them with one another: one who believes in Christ and scarcely dares to whisper Christ’s name; another who believes in Christ and confesses Christ publicly; a third who believes in Christ and is ready to die for Christ in making his confession. The first is so weak that shame overcomes him, not fear. The second already has a firm face, but not yet to the point of shedding blood. The third has everything, with nothing further left to achieve. For he fulfills what is written: *Fight for the truth even to death*.

**2. 2.** What do we say about Peter? He preached Christ; he was sent out and proclaimed the Gospel even before the Lord’s Passion. For we know that the apostles were sent to preach the Gospel: he was sent and he preached. How far he had surpassed those Jews who were afraid to confess publicly! Yet he was still not like Protasius and Gervasius. He was already an apostle, the first among them, clinging to the Lord. He had been told, *You are Peter*; but he was not yet a Protasius or a Gervasius, not yet a Stephen, not yet a Nemesianus the boy. Peter was not yet this; he was not yet what certain women were, what young girls were, what Crispina and Agnes were. Peter was not yet what these women were in their feminine weakness.

**3. 2.** I praise Peter, but first I blush for Peter. What a ready soul, yet unable to take its own measure! Surely, if it had not been ready, he would not have said to the Savior, “I will die for you. *Even if I must die with you, I will not deny you*.” But the physician who knew how to examine the pulse of his heart foretold the danger of the coming attack. *Will you,* he says, *lay down your life for me?* Recognize the order: I lay mine down first. *Will you lay down your life for me? Amen, I say to you, before the rooster crows, you will deny me three times*. The physician foretold what the sick man did not know. The sick man therefore discovered that his confidence had been mistaken when he was asked, *Are you one of them*? The servant girl who questioned him was the fever. See, the fever has come upon him; see, it grips him. What shall I say? See, Peter is in danger; see, he is dying. For what else is dying but denying life? He denied Christ, denied life, and died. But he who raises the dead—*the Lord looked upon him, and he wept bitterly*. By denying he perished; by weeping he rose again. And the Lord died for him first, as was fitting; afterward Peter died for the Lord, as the proper order required, and the martyrs followed. The road, once thorny, was laid down and trodden by the apostles’ feet, made smoother for those who would follow.

**4. 3.** The earth was filled with martyrs as with the seed of their blood, and from that seed the harvest of the Church arose. They bore stronger witness to Christ dead than alive. Today they bear witness; today they preach. Their tongues are silent, their deeds ring out. They were arrested, bound, imprisoned, brought forth, tortured, burned, stoned, struck down, and thrown to the beasts. In all their deaths they were mocked as worthless; but *precious in the Lord’s sight is the death of his saints*. Then it was precious only in the Lord’s sight; now it is precious in ours as well. For then, when being a Christian was a disgrace, the death of the saints was worthless in human eyes. They were detested and held accursed; their fate was flung at others as an insult: “May you die like that! May you be crucified like that! May you be burned like that!” What believer now does not long for those curses?

**5. 4.** Today, then, brothers and sisters, we celebrate the memorial established in this place of Saints Protasius and Gervasius, the martyrs of Milan. Today we celebrate not the day on which it was established here, but the day on which their death, precious in the Lord’s sight, was brought to light through Bishop Ambrose, a man of God. I too was then a witness of the martyrs’ great glory. I was there; I was in Milan. I know of the miracles that occurred as God bore witness to the precious deaths of his saints, so that through those miracles their death might be precious not only in the Lord’s sight but also in human eyes. A blind man, very well known to the whole city, received his sight. He hastened there, had himself led to the place, and returned without a guide. We have not yet heard that he has died; perhaps he is still alive. In the very basilica where their bodies lie, he vowed to spend his whole life in service. We rejoiced to see him seeing, and left him serving.

**5. 5.** God does not cease to bear witness, and he knows how to commend his own miracles. He knows how to make them esteemed; he knows how to keep them from becoming cheap. He does not give health to everyone through the martyrs, but he promises immortality to everyone who imitates the martyrs. Let the person to whom he does not give what he does not give to everyone refrain from demanding it, and not murmur against him because he does not give it, so that he may give what he has promised at the end. For even those who are healed now will die after a little while; those who rise at the end will live with Christ.

**6. 5.** The head has gone before and awaits the members who will follow. The whole body will be made complete: Christ and the Church. May he count us among those enrolled there, and in this life give us what is beneficial. For he knows what is beneficial to his children. *If you, then,* he says, *though you are evil, know how to give good gifts to your children, how much more will your Father in heaven give good things to those who ask him*? What good things? Temporal ones? He gives those too, but gives them also to unbelievers. He gives those too, but gives them also to the impious, even to those who blaspheme him. Let us seek good things that we will not share with the wicked. That Father knows how to give these good things to his children. Now his child asks him for bodily health, and he does not give it; he still scourges him. But when a father scourges, is he not doing good? He brings out the scourge, but consider what an inheritance he is preparing. *He scourges,* it says, *every son he receives. For the Lord corrects the one he loves*. I say these things, my brothers and sisters, so that you will not be saddened when you ask and do not receive, or think that God does not keep you before his eyes if for a time he does not grant your wishes. For a physician does not always listen to a sick person’s wishes, although he undoubtedly cares for and seeks that person’s health. He does not give what the patient asks for; rather, he provides what the patient does not ask for. The patient asks for cold water; he does not give it. Has the man who came to heal become cruel? This is medical skill, not cruelty. For the moment he does not give what delights: some things are denied while the patient is not yet healthy, so that when healthy he may enjoy everything.

**7. 6.** Consider God’s promises. What about these very martyrs? Do you think he gave them everything they asked for? No. Many wished to be released, and released through some miracle, as the three young men were released from the furnace. What did King Nebuchadnezzar say? *Because,* he said, *they trusted in him and changed the king’s word.* What testimony is borne by the man who was trying to kill them! He wanted them burned, and afterward believed through them. If they had died in the fire, they would have been crowned in secret and it would not have benefited him. Therefore they were preserved for a time so that an unbeliever might believe, so that the man who condemned them might praise God. The God of the three young men was the same God as the God of the Maccabees. He delivered the former from the fire and caused the latter to die in the fire. Had he changed? Did he love the former more than the latter? A greater crown was given to the Maccabees. Certainly, the others escaped the flames, but they were preserved for the dangers of this world; the Maccabees ended all their dangers in the flames. No further temptation remained, only their crowning. Thus the Maccabees received more.

**8. 6.** Rouse your faith; bring forward the eyes of your heart, not your human eyes. For you have other eyes within, made for you by the Lord, who opened the eyes of your heart when he gave you faith. Ask those eyes: who received more, the Maccabees or the three young men? I am asking faith. If I ask people who love this world, a weak soul tells me, “I would have wanted to be among the three young men.” Be ashamed before the mother of the Maccabees, who wanted her sons to die before her because she knew they were not dying.

**8. 7.** I sometimes recall the written accounts of the martyrs’ miracles that are read in your presence. A few days ago an account was read in which a sick woman, tormented by the sharpest pains, said, “I cannot bear it.” The martyr himself, who had come to heal her, said to her, “What if you were undergoing martyrdom?” Many, then, undergo martyrdom in bed—many indeed. There is a persecution by Satan more hidden and more cunning than the persecution of those days. A believer lies in bed, tormented by pain. He prays and is not heard—or rather, he is heard, but is being tested, trained, and scourged so that he may be received as a son. Thus, while he is tormented by pain, a temptation comes through someone’s tongue. Some woman, or a man—if he deserves to be called a man—comes to the bed and says to the sick person, “Use that charm and you will be well; have that incantation spoken and you will be well. This person and that person and that other person were cured by it—ask them.” He does not yield, does not comply, does not bend his heart; yet he struggles. He has no strength, and he defeats the devil. He becomes a martyr in bed, crowned by the one who hung on the wood for his sake.
