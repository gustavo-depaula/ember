# Sermon 155

**1. 1.** Yesterday's reading of the holy Apostle was concluded at the point where it was said, *So then it is I myself who serve the law of God with the mind, but the law of sin with the flesh*[^1]. In this conclusion the Apostle showed that he had said what he had said above—*It is no longer I that work it, but the sin that dwells in me*[^2]—for this reason: because he was not working it with the mind, by consenting, but with the flesh, by coveting. For he calls this by the name of sin, whence all sins arise, that is, carnal coveting. For whatever there is of sins in words, in deeds, in thoughts, they arise only from evil craving, they arise only from unlawful delight. If, then, we resist this unlawful delight, if we do not consent to it, if we do not furnish our members as arms; sin does not reign in our mortal body. For sin first loses its reign, and so it perishes. In this life, then, as far as concerns the saints, it loses its reign; in the other, it perishes. For here it loses its reign, when we do not go after our cravings; but there it perishes, when it will be said, *Where, O death, is your struggle*[^3]?

**2. 2.** So when the Apostle had said, *I serve the law of God with the mind, but the law of sin with the flesh*—not by giving his members for the committing of iniquities, but only by coveting, and yet not giving a hand to unlawful coveting: so when he had said, *I serve the law of God with the mind, but the law of sin with the flesh*, he went on and said, *There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus*[^4]. For those who are in the flesh there is condemnation; for those who are in Christ Jesus there is no condemnation. Lest you should think this would be only afterward, he therefore added *now*. Wait for that afterward—that there may not even be in you a coveting against which you must contend, with which you must struggle, to which you must not consent, which you must bridle and tame; wait for that afterward, because it too will not exist. For if that which contends with us from the mortal body will exist even afterward, *Where, O death, is your struggle*? will be false. Let us know, then, what is to be afterward. For *then shall come to pass what is written: Death is swallowed up in victory. Where, O death, is your struggle? Where, O death, is your sting? For the sting of death is sin; but the power of sin is the law*[^5]. Because by the prohibition the desire was increased, not extinguished. The law gave power to sin, only by commanding through the letter, not by aiding through the spirit. Therefore then this will not be; but what now? You ask what is now? What he said also a little before: *But now it is no longer I that work it*[^6]—there too *now*. What is, *it is not I that work it*? I do not consent, I do not assent, I do not decide, it always displeases me; I hold back my members. And this is a great thing: since coveting is from the flesh, and the members of the body pertain to the flesh, when sin—that is, the coveting of the flesh—does not reign, the mind has more right to hold back the members of the flesh, lest they be given as arms of iniquity, than the coveting of the flesh itself has to stir the members of the flesh. So there is the coveting of the flesh, and the members of the flesh; yet the mind, because it has dominion—provided it be helped from above, lest, when we give it too much against the grace of God, we make it not a king but a tyrant—the mind, then, has such power, so rules when it is ruled, that of the members of the flesh itself, against the coveting of the flesh itself, it can do what the Apostle says, *Let not sin, then, reign in your mortal body, to obey its desires; nor present your members as arms of iniquity to sin*[^7].

**3. 3.** *There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus*[^8]. Let them not be anxious if they are tickled by unlawful covetings; let them not be anxious that there still seems to be a law in the members fighting against the law of the mind. For *there is no condemnation*. But for whom? Even *now*, for whom? *For those who are in Christ Jesus*. And where is that saying of which he spoke a little before, *I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind, and taking me captive in the law of sin which is in my members*[^9]? But he said *me* from the flesh, not from the mind. Where, then, is that law, if *there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus? For the law of the spirit of life in Christ Jesus*. *For the law*—not that one on Mount Sinai through the letter; *for the law*—not that one in the oldness of the letter; but, *the law of the spirit of life in Christ Jesus, has freed you from the law of sin and death*. For that you delight in the law of God according to the inner man, whence would you have it, unless the law of the spirit of life in Christ Jesus freed you from the law of sin and death? Therefore, human mind, do not ascribe it to yourself, do not be too proud—rather, do not be proud at all, O human mind, that you do not consent to the desires of the flesh, that the law of sin does not cast you down from the citadel: *The law of the spirit of life in Christ Jesus has freed you from the law of sin and death*[^10]. That law did not free you of which it was said above, *That we serve in newness of spirit, and not in oldness of the letter*[^11]. Why did that law not free you? Was it not itself written by the finger of God? Is not the finger of God understood to be the Holy Spirit? Read the Gospel, and see that where one evangelist says, the Lord saying, *If I cast out demons by the Spirit of God*[^12], another says, *If I cast out demons by the finger of God*[^13]. If, then, that law too was written by the finger of God—that is, by the Spirit of God; by which Spirit the magicians of Pharaoh, defeated, said, *This is the finger of God*[^14]—if, then, it too, indeed since it too was written by the Spirit of God, that is, the finger of God, why is it not said of it, *For the law of the spirit of life in Christ Jesus*?

**4. 4.** The law that is called the law of death—the law of sin and death is not that law which was given on Mount Sinai. The law of sin and death is that of which he says, groaning, *I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind*[^15]. But that law is the very one that was called, *And so the law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good*. And he added, *Did that which is good, then, become death to me? Far from it! But sin, that it might appear as sin, worked death in me through what is good; that the sinner*, or sin, *might become beyond measure through the commandment*[^16]. What is, *beyond measure*? That transgression be added. That law was therefore given, that infirmity might be found out. It is too little, this—not only that it be found out, but also that it be increased, and that even so a physician might be sought. For if the disease were slight, it would be despised; if the disease were despised, the physician would not be sought; if the physician were not sought, the disease would not be ended. Therefore, where sin abounded, grace abounded the more[^17]; which blotted out all the sins it found, and supplied aid to our will as it strove not to sin; so that our will itself might be praised not in itself, but in God. For *in God we will be praised all the day*[^18]; and, *In the Lord my soul will be praised; let the meek hear, and be glad*[^19]. *Let the meek hear*: for the proud and the contentious do not hear. Why, then, is it not the law written by the finger of God which gives this help of grace of which we speak? Why? Because it was written on tablets of stone, not on the fleshly tablets of the heart[^20].

**5. 5.** In short, my brothers, in a great mystery see the concord, see the distance; the concord of the law, the distance of the people. The Passover is celebrated among the old people, as you know, by the slaying of a lamb with unleavened bread; where the slaying of the sheep signifies Christ, and the unleavened bread the new life, that is, without the oldness of leaven. Hence the Apostle says to us, *Cleanse out the old leaven, that you may be a new lump, as you are unleavened; for Christ our Passover has been sacrificed*[^21]. The Passover, then, was celebrated among that old people, not yet celebrated in the shining light, but in the signifying shadow; and fifty days after the celebration of the Passover, as one who wishes will find by counting, the law is given on Mount Sinai, written by the finger of God. The true Passover came, Christ is sacrificed; he makes a passing over from death to life. For "Passover" is interpreted in Hebrew as "passing over"; which the Evangelist expressed, saying, *But when the hour came for Jesus to pass over from this world to the Father*[^22]. The Passover, then, is celebrated, the Lord rises again, makes a passing over from death to life, which is the Passover; and fifty days are counted, and the Holy Spirit comes, the finger of God.

**6. 6.** But see how it was there, and how it was here. There the people stood far off, there was fear, there was no love. For they feared so much that they said to Moses, *You speak to us, and let not the Lord speak to us, lest we die*[^23]. So God came down, as it is written, on Sinai in fire; but terrifying the people who stood far off, and writing with his finger on stone[^24], not on the heart. But here, when the Holy Spirit came, the faithful were gathered together in one place; nor did he terrify on a mountain, but entered into the house. From heaven, indeed, a sound was suddenly made, as of a violent blast being borne; it sounded, but no one was terrified. You have heard the sound, see also the fire; for on the mountain too there were both, both fire and sound; but there also smoke, here a serene fire. *There appeared to them*, says Scripture, *divided tongues, as of fire*. Surely not terrifying from afar? Far from it! For *it sat upon each one of them, and they began to speak in tongues, as the Spirit gave them to utter*[^25]. Hear the tongue speaking, and understand the Spirit writing, not on stone, but on the heart. *The law*, then, *of the spirit of life*, written on the heart, not on stone; *in Christ Jesus*, in whom the truest Passover was celebrated, *has freed you from the law of sin and death*[^26]. For that you may know that this is the most evident distance between the Old and New Testaments—whence the Apostle also says, *Not on tablets of stone, but on the fleshly tablets of the heart*[^27]—the Lord says through the Prophet, *Behold, the days are coming, says the Lord, and I will complete over the house of Jacob a new testament, not according to the testament that I established with their fathers, on the day when I took their hand and led them out of the land of Egypt*. Then, showing the very difference plainly: *Giving*, he says, *my laws into their hearts; into their hearts*, he says, *I will inscribe them*[^28]. If, then, the law of God be written in your heart, that it may not terrify from without, but soothe within; then *the law of the spirit of life in Christ Jesus has freed you from the law of sin and death*[^29].

**7. 7.** *For what was impossible for the law*. For this follows in the reading of the Apostle: *What was impossible for the law*. And lest the law itself be blamed, what did he add? *In which it was weakened through the flesh*. For the law commanded, and did not fulfill; because the flesh, where there was no grace, resisted most unconquerably. And the law *was weakened through the flesh, because the law is spiritual; but I am carnal*[^30]. How, then, would the law aid me, commanding through the letter, and not giving grace? *It was weakened through the flesh*. What did God do, since this was impossible for the law, and it was weakened through the flesh? *God sent his Son*. Through what was the law weakened, and on account of what was this impossible for the law? *It was weakened through the flesh*. What, then, did God do? Against the flesh he sent flesh; rather, also for the flesh he sent flesh. For he slew the sin of the flesh, he freed the substance of the flesh. *God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh*[^31]. In true flesh indeed, but not in the flesh of sin. But what is, *in the likeness of sinful flesh*? That is, that it was flesh, true flesh. And whence the likeness of sinful flesh? Because from sin comes death: death is surely in all sinful flesh; of which the Apostle says, *That the body of sin might be destroyed*[^32]. Because, then, death is in all sinful flesh: but both are there, both death and sin, in the rest of flesh. In sinful flesh there are both death and sin; in the likeness of sinful flesh there was death, and there was not sin. For if it were sinful flesh, and were paying the penalty of death by the desert of sin, the Lord himself would not say, *Behold, the prince of the world comes, and in me he will find nothing*[^33]. Why, then, did he kill me? Because *what I did not seize, I was then paying back*[^34]. Plainly, what he did about the tribute, this he did about death. The tribute was demanded, the didrachma: *Why*, it was said, *do you and your disciples not pay the tribute*? He called Peter to him and said to him, *The kings of the world, from whom do they demand tribute? From their sons, or from strangers*? It was answered, *From strangers. Therefore*, he said, *the sons are free. Yet, lest we give them offense, go to the sea, cast a hook, and the first that comes up*—that is, the firstborn from the dead—*open*, he said, *its mouth, and you will find there a stater*—that is, two didrachmas, four drachmas; because a didrachma, that is, two drachmas, was demanded per head. *You will find there a stater*, that is, four drachmas; *give it to them for me and you*[^35]. What is, *for me and you*? Christ himself, Peter, the Church of Christ; the four Gospels of the Church. The mystery lay hidden: yet Christ paid a tribute not owed. So too he paid death: he did not owe it, and he paid it. Unless he had paid what was not owed, he would never have freed us from the debt.

**8. 8.** *What*, then, *was impossible for the law*, which made a transgressor, because the mind, not yet convicted, had not sought the Savior; *in which it was weakened through the flesh, God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and from sin condemned sin in the flesh*[^36]. How, then, did he not have sin, if from sin he condemned sin? We have already expounded this to you at one time, but let those who remember recognize it; let those who did not hear, hear it; let those who have forgotten, recall it. In the law a sacrifice for sin was called sin. The law constantly mentions this; not once, not twice, but very often the sacrifices for sins were called sins. Such a sin was Christ. For what shall we say? Did he have sin? Far from it! He did not have sin, and he was sin. He was sin, I said, according to that understanding, because a sacrifice for sin. Hear that he was sin in this way—hear the Apostle himself. Speaking of him, he says, *Him who knew no sin*. I was expounding this very saying to you when I said these things: *Him*, he says, *who knew no sin*—that is, our Lord Jesus Christ—God the Father *made him who knew no sin sin for us; him*, this same Christ *who knew no sin*, God the Father *made sin for us, that we might be the righteousness of God in him*[^37]. See two things: the righteousness of God, not ours; *in him*, not in us. Hence those great saints, of whom the Psalm says, *Your righteousness is like the mountains of God*. And as though it were said in that very Psalm, where it was said, *Your righteousness*—for not their righteousness, but, *Your righteousness is like the mountains of God. For I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, from where my help will come*; but not from the mountains: *For my help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth*[^38]; therefore when he had said, *Your righteousness is like the mountains of God*, as though it were asked, Why, then, are others born who do not belong to the righteousness of God? he added, *Your judgments are like a great deep*[^39]. What is, *like a great deep*? It is deep, impenetrable, inaccessible to human striving. For the riches of God are unsearchable; his judgments unsearchable, his ways untraceable[^40]. Therefore here too: *God sent his Son*, on account of the foreknown, and the predestined, to be called, to be justified, to be glorified: that the mountains of God may say, *If God is for us, who is against us*[^41]? *God sent his Son in the likeness of sinful flesh, and from sin condemned sin in the flesh, that the righteousness of the law might be fulfilled in us*[^42]. It was not fulfilled of itself; it was fulfilled through Christ. For he came not to abolish the law, but to fulfill it[^43].

**9. 9.** But how is *the righteousness of the law fulfilled in us*, or how is it fulfilled in us, or in which of us? Do you wish to hear in which of us? *Who walk not according to the flesh, but according to the spirit*[^44]. What is it to walk according to the flesh? To consent to carnal covetings. What is it to walk according to the spirit? To be aided by the spirit in the mind, and not to obey the covetings of the flesh. Thus, then, the law is fulfilled in us, the righteousness of God is fulfilled in us. For now, meanwhile, this is fulfilled: *Do not go after your desires*[^45]. *After your desires*—when you hear this, understand the unlawful ones. *Do not go after your desires* ought to be fulfilled out of our will, aided by the grace of God; it ought to be fulfilled, *Do not go after your desires*. For whatever that coveting of the flesh did in us of past sins, whether in deeds, or in words, or in thoughts, all was blotted out by holy Baptism; one act of forgiveness blotted out all the debts. There remains, then, the conflict with the flesh: because the iniquity has been blotted out, but the infirmity remains. The delight of unlawful coveting is within, it tickles; fight, resist, do not consent; and here is fulfilled, *Do not go after your desires*; because even if at some time they creep up and usurp for themselves the eye, the ear, the tongue, the flitting thought, even so let us not despair of our salvation. Therefore we say daily, *Forgive us our debts*[^46]. *The righteousness*, he says, *of the law might be fulfilled in us*.

**10. 10.** But in which of us? *Who walk not according to the flesh, but according to the spirit. For those who are according to the flesh savor the things of the flesh; but those who are according to the spirit, the things of the spirit. For the prudence of the flesh is death; but the prudence of the spirit is life and peace. For the prudence of the flesh is hostile to God. For it is not subject to the law of God: for neither can it be*[^47]. What is, *for neither can it be*? It is not that man cannot, not that the soul cannot, not, finally, that the flesh itself—because it is a creation of God—cannot; but the prudence of the flesh cannot; the vice cannot, not the nature. Just as if you said: Limping is not subject to walking straight; for neither can it be. The foot can, but limping cannot. Take away the limping, and you will see walking straight. But as long as the limping is there, it cannot; so as long as the prudence of the flesh is there, it cannot. Let there be no prudence of the flesh, and man can. *The prudence of the spirit is life and peace*. So, then, that which he says, *The prudence of the flesh is hostile to God*, do not take as though this hostile thing could harm God. It is hostile by resisting, not by killing. But it harms the one in whom the prudence of the flesh is; because the vice harms the nature in which the nature is. But medicine was found for this reason, that the vice might be driven out, and the nature healed. So the Savior came to the human race, found no one healthy, and therefore the great physician came.

**10. 11.** He said this for this reason: that the Manicheans, wishing to introduce another nature of evil against God, think their error somewhat aided by this testimony of the Apostle, and suppose it was said as it were of nature, because it was said, *It cannot, it is hostile to God: for it is not subject to the law of God, for neither can it be*[^48]; and they did not notice that it was not said of the flesh, *it cannot*; not said of man, *it cannot*; not said of the soul, *it cannot*; but of the prudence of the flesh. This prudence is a vice.

**11. 11.** Do you wish to know what is, *To savor according to the flesh? It is death*[^49]. But that one and the same man, the same nature established by the Lord, the true and good God, yesterday savored according to the flesh, today savors according to the spirit; the vice has been driven out, the nature healed. For as long as that prudence of the flesh existed, it could in no way be subject to the law of God. For as long as limping is present through the vice, there can in no way be walking straight. But the vice being healed, the nature is restored. *You were once darkness; but now you are light in the Lord*[^50].

**11. 12.** See, then, what follows: *But those who are in the flesh*—that is, those who trust in the flesh, who follow their covetings, who dwell in them, who delight in their pleasures, who place the happy and blessed life in their delights, these are in the flesh—*cannot please God*. For it was not said, *But those who are in the flesh cannot please God*, as though it were said: While men are in this life, they cannot please God. Did the holy patriarchs, then, not please? Did the holy prophets, then, not please? Did the holy apostles, then, not please? Did the holy martyrs not please, who, before they laid down the body by suffering, by confessing Christ, not only despised pleasure, but also bore even pains most patiently? They pleased, but they were not in the flesh. They carried the flesh, they were not carried by the flesh. For already it had been said to the paralytic, *Take up your bed*[^51]. *Those, then, who are in the flesh*, as I have said, as I have already expounded—not by living in this age, but by consenting to the covetings of the flesh—*cannot please God*.

**12. 13.** In short, hear him resolving the question without any doubt. He was surely speaking while living in this body; and yet he added, *But you are not in the flesh*. Do you suppose there is anyone here among us to whom it was said? Behold, he said it to the people of God, he said it to the Church; he was writing to the Romans, indeed, but he said it to the whole Church of Christ; but he said it to the wheat, not to the chaff; he said it to the hidden lump, not to the apparent stubble. Let each one recognize it in his own heart. We speak to the ears, we do not see consciences; yet, according to what we have said above, I reckon that in the name of Christ there are in the people of Christ those to whom it was said, *But you are not in the flesh, but in the Spirit: if indeed the Spirit of God dwells in you*[^52]. *You are not in the flesh*, because you do not do the works of the flesh by consenting to the covetings of the flesh; *but* you are *in the Spirit*, because according to the inner man you delight in the law of God; and this is, *if indeed the Spirit of God dwells in you*. For if you presume upon your own spirit, you are still in the flesh. If, then, you are not in the flesh, it is that you may be in the Spirit of God; for then you are not in the flesh. For if the Spirit of God withdraws, by its own weight the spirit of man rolls back into the flesh, returns to carnal deeds, returns to worldly covetings; and the last state of that man will be worse than the first[^53]. So, then, have free choice in such a way that you implore help. *You are not in the flesh*—and this from your own strength? Far from it! Whence, then?

**13. 13.** *If indeed the Spirit of God dwells in you. But if anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ, he is not his*[^54]. Let not the needy and vitiated nature, then, stretch itself out, boast, arrogate to itself its own virtue. O human nature! O Adam, when you were healthy, did you not stand, and did you rise by your own strength? *If anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ*: for it is the same Spirit of God that is the Spirit of Christ; for it is of both the Father and the Son: *If anyone does not have the Spirit of Christ*, let him not deceive himself, *he is not his*.

**13. 14.** Behold, with the help of his own mercy, we have the Spirit of Christ; from the very love of righteousness, by whole faith, catholic faith, we recognize that the Spirit of God is within us. But what of that mortal flesh? What of the law in our members fighting against the law of the mind? What of that groan, *Wretched man that I am*? Hear: *But if Christ is in you, the body indeed is dead because of sin, but the spirit is life because of righteousness*[^55]. Must we then despair of the body, dead because of sin? Is there no hope? Does it so sleep that it does not rise again[^56]? Far from it! *The body indeed is dead because of sin, but the spirit is life because of righteousness*. Sadness remains concerning our body. But no one ever hated his own flesh[^57]. We see how anxiously the burial of the dead is cared for. *The body indeed is dead because of sin, but the spirit is life because of righteousness*. Now you were saying, for consolation: I would indeed wish my body too to be in life; but since it cannot be, let at least my spirit be, let at least my soul be. Wait, do not be anxious.

**14. 15.** *For if the Spirit of him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you; he who raised Christ Jesus from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies*[^58]. What do you fear? Why are you anxious even for the flesh itself? *A hair of your head will not perish*[^59]. Adam by sinning condemned your bodies to death; but Jesus, *if his Spirit is in you, will also give life to your mortal bodies*; because he gave his own blood for your salvation. Do you doubt that the promise will be paid, you who hold such a pledge? Thus, then, O man, there will not be that struggle of death; thus will be fulfilled what was said, *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death*? Because Christ Jesus, *if his Spirit dwells in you, will also give life to your mortal bodies*. Thus you will be freed from the body of this death, not by not having a body, or by having another; but by no longer dying. For if he had not added, *of this death*, and had said, *Who will free me from the body*? perhaps error would be suggested to human thought, and it would be said: You see that God does not wish us to be with a body? *From the body*, he says, *of this death*. Take away death, and the body is good. Let death, the last enemy, be taken away, and my flesh will be my friend forever. For no one ever hated his own flesh. Even if the spirit covets against the flesh, and the flesh covets against the spirit[^60]; even if there is now a quarrel in this house, the quarreling husband seeks not the ruin, but the concord of his wife. Far from it! My brothers, far be it that the spirit, by coveting against the flesh, should hate the flesh. It hates the vices of the flesh, it hates the prudence of the flesh, it hates the struggle of death. Let this corruptible put on incorruption, and this mortal put on immortality; let the animal body be sown, let the spiritual body rise[^61]; and you will see full and perfect concord, you will see the creation praising the Creator. *If*, then, *the Spirit of him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you; he who raised Christ from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies, on account of his indwelling Spirit who dwells in you*[^62]; not on account of your merits, but on account of his gifts. Let us turn to the Lord, etc.
