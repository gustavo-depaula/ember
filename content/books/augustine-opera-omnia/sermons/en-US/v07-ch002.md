# Sermon 231: In the Days of Easter

**1.** The resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ is, as is customary, read during these days from all the books of the holy Gospel. In this reading we notice how the Lord Jesus rebuked his disciples, his first members, who were clinging to his side, because they did not believe that he whom they grieved as slain was alive. The fathers of the faith, not yet faithful, the teachers by whom the whole world was to believe what they were going to preach and for which they were going to die, did not yet believe. They did not believe that he had risen, whom they had seen raise the dead. Rightly, then, were they rebuked. They were shown to themselves, that they might come to know who they were of themselves and who they would be through him. So too Peter was shown to himself, when at the approach of the Lord's passion he presumed, and when the passion itself came he faltered. He saw himself in himself, he grieved over himself in himself, he wept over himself in himself; he turned to him who had made him. See, these men still did not yet believe; in this reading they did not yet believe, though they already saw. How great the condescension of him who granted us to believe what we do not yet see! We believe their words; they did not believe their own eyes.

**2.** Now the resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ is the new life of those who believe in Jesus. And this is the mystery of his passion and resurrection, which you ought thoroughly to know and to enact. For it was not without cause that life came to death, not without cause that the fount of life, from which one drinks in order to live, here drank a cup that was not owed to him. For dying was not owed to Christ. Where death came from, let us seek its origin thus: the father of death is sin. For if no one ever sinned, no one would die. The first man received the law of God, that is, the commandment of God, with a condition: that if he kept it, he would live; if he broke it, he would die. By not believing that he would die, he brought about the very thing from which he died, and he found that what he had said who had given the law was true. From there came death, from there the mortal condition, from there toil, from there misery, from there too, after the first death, a second death, that is, after temporal death, everlasting death. To this condition of death, then, bound by these laws of the underworld, every man is born; but not that man who was made man, lest man should perish. For he did not come bound by the laws of death, and therefore it is said in the Psalm: *Free among the dead*[^1]. Whom a Virgin conceived without desire, whom a Virgin bore and remained a Virgin, who lived without fault, who did not die because of fault, sharing the penalty with us, not sharing the fault with us—death being the penalty of fault—the Lord Jesus Christ came to die, he did not come to sin. By sharing the penalty with us without the fault, he loosed both the fault and the penalty. What penalty did he loose? The one owed to us after this life. Therefore he was crucified, so that by the cross he might show the setting of our old self, and he rose, so that in his own life he might show the newness of our life. For thus the apostolic teaching instructs us: *He was handed over*, it says, *for our sins, and rose again for our justification*[^2]. The sign of this matter had been given to the fathers in circumcision, that every male should be circumcised on the eighth day. Circumcision was done *with knives of stone*[^3], because *the rock was Christ*[^4]. In this circumcision was signified the stripping off of the carnal life on the eighth day through Christ's resurrection. For the seventh day of the week is completed with the Sabbath. On the Sabbath the Lord lay in the tomb, on the seventh day of the week; he rose on the eighth. His resurrection renews us. Therefore on the eighth day he circumcises us. In this hope we live.

**3.** Let us hear the Apostle saying: *If you have risen with Christ . . .*[^5]. When have we risen, we who have not yet died? What, then, did the Apostle mean to say: *If you have risen with Christ*? Would he have risen unless he had first died? He was speaking to the living, to those not yet dying and already rising. What does he mean? See what he says: *If you have risen with Christ, set your minds on the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; seek the things that are above, not the things that are on earth. For you have died*[^6]. He says it, not I, and yet he says what is true, and therefore I too say it. Why do I too say it? *I believed, therefore I have spoken*[^7]. If we live well, we have died and have risen; but whoever has not yet died and risen still lives badly; if he lives badly, he does not live; let him die, that he may not die. What does it mean, let him die, that he may not die? Let him be changed, that he may not be condemned. *If you have risen with Christ*—I repeat the Apostle's words—*set your minds on the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; seek the things that are above, not the things that are on earth. For you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God. When Christ who is your life appears, then you also will appear with him in glory*[^8]. These are the Apostle's words. To him who has not yet died, I say: let him die; to him who still lives badly, I say: let him be changed. For if he lived badly and no longer lives badly, he has died; if he lives well, he has risen.

**4.** But what is it to live well? *Set your minds on the things that are above, not the things that are on earth*[^9]. As long as you are earth and will go into the earth, as long as you lick the earth—by loving earth you do indeed lick earth—and become the enemy of him of whom the Psalm says: *And his enemies shall lick the earth*[^10]. What were you? Sons of men. What are you? Sons of God. *Sons of men, how long will you be heavy of heart? Why do you love vanity and seek a lie?*[^11] What lie do you seek? I will say it now. You want to be happy, I know. Give me a man who is a robber, a criminal, a fornicator, a sorcerer, a sacrilegious person, defiled with every vice, sunk in every disgraceful deed or crime, who does not truly want a happy life. I know you all want to live happily; but from what does a man live happily? This you do not want to seek. You seek gold because you think you will be made happy by gold; but gold does not make you happy. Why do you seek a lie? Why do you want to be exalted in this world? Because you think you will be made happy by men's honor and the pomp of the world, but the pomp of the world does not make you happy. Why do you seek a lie? And whatever else you seek here, when you seek it in a worldly way, when you seek it by loving the earth, when you seek it by licking the earth, you seek it in order to be happy; but no earthly thing will make you happy. Why do you not stop seeking a lie? From where, then, will you be happy? *Sons of men, how long will you be heavy of heart?* Do you not want to be heavy of heart, you who burden your heart with earth? How long were men heavy of heart? Before Christ came, before Christ rose, men were heavy of heart. *How long will you be heavy of heart? Why do you love vanity and seek a lie?* Wishing to be happy, you seek the very things that make you wretched. What you seek deceives you; it is a lie that you seek.

**5.** Do you want to be happy? I will show you, if you wish, where you are to find happiness. Follow on there: *How long will you be heavy of heart? Why do you love vanity and seek a lie? Know.* Know what? *That the Lord has magnified his holy one*[^12]. Christ came to our miseries: he hungered, he thirsted, he grew weary, he slept, he did wonders, he suffered evils, he was scourged, he was crowned with thorns, he was smeared with spittle, struck with blows, fastened to the wood, wounded with a lance, laid in the tomb—but rising on the third day, his toil finished, death dead. See, fix your eye there, on his resurrection. For the Lord *magnified his holy one*, raising him from the dead and giving him the honor of sitting in heaven at his right hand. He shows you what you ought to set your mind on, if you wish to be happy. For here you cannot be happy. In this life you cannot be happy. No one can. You seek a good thing, but this earth is not the region of the thing you seek. What do you seek? A happy life. But it is not here. If you were seeking gold in a place where it is not, one who knew that it is not there would say to you: Why are you digging? why are you disturbing the earth? You make a pit to go down into, not where you find anything. What will you answer the one who warns you? I am seeking gold. And he: I do not say to you, There is nothing you seek; you seek a good thing, but it is not where you seek it. So too you, when you say, I want to be happy, you seek a good thing, but it is not here. If Christ had it here, you too will have it. In the region of your death, what did he find? Pay attention: coming from another region, what did he find here, except what abounds here: toils, sorrows, death—see what you have here, what abounds here. He ate with you what abounded in the cell of your misery. Here he drank vinegar, here he had gall. See what he found in your cell. And he invited you to his own great table, the table of heaven, the table of the angels, where he himself is the bread. Coming down, then, and finding these evils in your cell, he did not disdain such a table of yours, and he promised his own. What does he say to us? Believe, believe that you will come to the good things of my table, since I did not disdain the evil things of your table. He bore your evil—will he not give his good? Surely he will give it. He promised us his life, but what he did is more unbelievable: he advanced us his death. As if he said: I invite you to my life, where no one dies, where the life is truly happy, where the food does not spoil, where it restores and does not fail. See where I invite you: to the region of the angels, to the friendship of the Father and the Holy Spirit, to the everlasting supper, to my brotherhood; finally, to myself. I invite you to my life. Do you not want to believe that I will give you my life? Hold my death as a pledge. Now, then, while we live in this corruptible flesh, let us die with Christ by a change of conduct, let us live with Christ by love of righteousness. We shall not receive the happy life until we have come to him who came to us, and have begun to be with him who died for us.
