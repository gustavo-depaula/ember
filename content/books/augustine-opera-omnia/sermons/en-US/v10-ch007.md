# Sermon 345: A Treatise of Saint Augustine on Those Who Despise Temporal Things:

Delivered on the Feast of the Martyrs of Thuburbo

**2.** Riches, then, are sought for the sake of life, not life for the sake of riches. How many have made a pact with their enemies, that they should take everything and leave them their life! However much they had, at that price they bought their life. At what price is eternal life to be purchased, if a perishing life is so precious? Give something to Christ, that you may live blessed, if you give everything to the enemy, that you may live a beggar. For your temporal life, which you ransom at so great a price, weigh how much the eternal one is worth, which you neglect, so that you may live a few days, even if you are to reach old age. For all the days of a man, from infancy to old age, are few days; and if Adam himself were to die today, he would have lived few days, because he would have finished them all. Do you then ransom a few days, and these laborious, in such great want, in such great temptation? At what price? You wish to have nothing, that you may have yourself. Do you wish to know how much eternal life is worth? Add yourself too. Behold, the enemy who had taken you captive said to you: If you wish to live, give me whatever you have; and that you might live, you gave everything; ransomed today, perhaps to die tomorrow; let go by this one, perhaps to be thrust away by that one. Let dangers instruct us, my brothers. What is so untaught, both amid the words of God and amid human experiences? Behold, you have given everything, and gone away rejoicing, because you live, even if poor, even if needy, even if naked, even if a beggar—yet you rejoice, because you live, and the light is sweet. Let Christ appear: let him too make a pact, not he who took you captive, but he who was taken captive for you; not he who seeks to kill you, but he who deigned to be killed for you. He who gave himself for you (what a price!), who made you, says to you: Make a pact with me. Do you wish to have yourself, and lose everything? If you wish to have yourself, you must have me; so that you may hate yourself, that you may love me, and by losing your life may find it, lest by keeping it you lose it. Concerning these riches of yours, which you love to possess, and which yet you are prepared to give for your present life, I have already given wholesome counsel. If you love them too, do not lose them either; but where you love them, they will perish with you. I give counsel about these too. Do you love them? Send them ahead, where you may follow them; lest, while you love them on earth, you either lose them while alive or leave them when dead. And here too, he says, I have given counsel. I did not say: Lose; but: Keep. Do you wish to lay up treasure? I do not say: Do not; but I say: Where. Receive a counselor, not a forbidder. Where, then, do I say lay up treasure? *Lay up for yourselves treasure in heaven, where the thief does not approach, nor moth, nor rust destroys*[^1].

**3.** But I do not see, you say, what I put in heaven. For you do see what you bury in the earth. Are you secure in burying it in the earth, and anxious in giving it to him who made heaven and earth? Keep it where you wish: if you find one better than Christ, entrust it to him to guard. I entrust it, you say, to my servant. How much better to your Lord? Perhaps the servant takes it away and flees; and amid so many evils which have already happened, this was to be wished, that the servant should take it away and flee, and not lead the enemy to his master. Many servants have suddenly become enemies of their masters, and have betrayed them, with what they had, to the enemy. To whom, then, do you entrust it? Meanwhile, you say, I entrust my gold to my servant. Your gold to a servant: to whom your soul? I entrust my soul to God, you say. How much better to him both your gold, to whom also your soul? Or is he perhaps faithful in guarding your soul, and unfaithful in guarding your money? Does he not keep for you, who keeps you? Have faith, then. The servant acts so as not to take it away: does he act so as not to lose it? His whole fidelity is that he not defraud you; you attend to his fidelity, and do not attend to his weakness? He put it away, it was not hidden; another came, took it away. Will anyone do this to Christ? Shake off your sloth, receive counsel; lay up treasure in heaven. And why did I say: Shake off your sloth? As if it were a labor to lay up treasure in heaven. Even if it were a labor, it would have to be done, and the labor would have to be undertaken, and the things we hold of great value would have to be placed in a fortified place, from which no one could take them away. Yet Christ does not say to you: Lay up treasure in heaven, seek ladders, fit wings; but: Give to me on earth, and I will keep it there. Give to me on earth, he says; for this I came to be poor here, that you might be rich there. Make a transfer. Do you fear a defrauder, lest you lose it? Do you seek one to carry it where you are migrating? Christ is present to you in both; he does not commit fraud, but he does the carrying.

**4.** But where, you will say, do I find Christ? Since my faith holds what I heard in the Church, this I learned, thus I believed, in this I was steeped by the sacrament: he was buried, he rose again on the third day, after forty days he ascended into heaven before the eyes of his disciples, he sits at the right hand of the Father, he will come at the end. When do I find him here? To whom shall I give? Do not fret, hear it all; or if you have heard it all, say it all. I know that you received this: Christ was hung on the cross, taken down from the wood, placed in the tomb, rose again, ascended into heaven. And you have read this too: that when Saul was persecuting his Church, being proud, cruel, breathing slaughter[^2] and thirsting for the blood of Christians, when he was raging, when he was persecuting, carrying letters to Damascus, that whatever men and women of this teaching he found, he might bring bound to punishment—you have heard what he cried out to him, whom you confess to be sitting in heaven? Recall, then, what he said, what you heard, what you read: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^3] For Paul neither saw nor touched him; and yet that one was saying: *Why do you persecute me?* He did not say: Why do you persecute my household, my servants, my saints—add the honor, my brothers? None of these, but what did he say? *Why do you persecute me?* That is, my members, for whom, trampled on the earth, the head was crying out from heaven; just as for your foot too, if it is trodden on the earth, your tongue cries out, not: You tread on my foot, but: You tread on me. Why, then, do you doubt to whom you may give? He who said: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?* he himself says to you: Feed me on earth. Saul was raging, and yet he was persecuting Christ; so you too, give out on earth, and you feed Christ. For this question, by which you are moved, the Lord himself foretold—by which those too will be moved who will be set at the right hand; and when he has said: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*, they will answer: *Lord, when did we see you hungry?* And they will at once hear: *When you did it for one of my least ones, you did it for me*[^4].

**5.** If, then, you do not wish to give, you have something for which to accuse yourself, you have nothing by which to excuse yourself. Concerning these riches, then, your Lord says to you: I have given most wholesome counsel. Do you love them? Move them; and when you have moved them, you will follow them. Follow them meanwhile in your heart: *For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also*[^5]. If you entrust your treasure to the earth, you bury your heart too in the earth; but when you have buried your heart in the earth, blush, when you answer, when you hear: *Hearts on high; we hold them toward the Lord*. I have given, he says, wholesome counsel about your riches. If you do it, if you heed it, if you are such a rich man as the Apostle describes—that you be not proud in mind, nor hope in the uncertainty of riches, that you give easily, share, lay up for yourself a good foundation for the future, that you may lay hold of true life[^6]—now ask me, says your Lord. Behold, I have moved to heaven what I have, either by giving it all out, or by so holding what remains as though not having it, using this world as though not using it[^7]. Is the kingdom of heaven worth so much? Behold, if I have done this, is it worth so much? It is dear. It is worth more dearly: for it is not really such that it should be worth so much. You will live forever. For you would give all those treasures even for a life of a few days; you will be truly rich there, because you will not be in want. But this you do, when you wish to be rich, that you may not be in want on earth; and therefore you heap up and pile up for yourself dense mud, by which you are weighed down, by which you are pressed, by which, when it has dried, you are more tightly enclosed. Therefore, that you may not be in want, you seek many animals for carrying you, abundant feasts for nourishment, the most precious garments for clothing. So that you, by having much, may not be rich while the angel is poor, who neither uses a horse, nor is carried in a carriage, nor fills a table with elaborate dishes, nor is covered with garments, because he is clothed with light. Learn true riches. You wish to have these, so that you may have something with which to soothe your throat and fill your belly; he makes you truly rich, who grants you not to hunger. This is to be in no want at all, not to hunger; for however much you have, when the hour comes at which you should dine, or before you come to the table, when you hunger you are needy. Finally, when the feasts are removed, you pant in your pride. This is not a filling of needs, but a smoke of cares. See what you think about increasing these riches of yours; see whether you sleep easily, when you think either of not losing where you have stored, or of increasing what you have stored. Therefore you have found riches, you who have found rest. When you are awake, you think of the increase of riches; when you sleep, you dream of robbers; by day anxious, by night fearful, always a beggar. He wishes to make you truly rich, who promises you the kingdom of heaven. And how much do you think you will buy those true riches for, that true life, and that an eternal one? What? Do you therefore think it true, because you are going to buy it for as much as you were prepared to buy those few laborious and wretched days? Something must be worth more, which is by far greater.

**6.** And what shall I do? you say. All that I had I gave to the poor, and what I have I share with the needy: what more can I do? You have more: yourself; you have yourself as more, you are among your possessions, you are to be added. Attend to the counsel of your Lord, which he gave to the rich man: *Go, sell all that you have, and give to the poor*. And did he let him go, after he had said this? And lest that man should think he lost what he loved, he first made him secure, because he did not lose it but stored it up: *You will have*, he said, *treasure in heaven*. Is this enough? No. *And come, follow me*[^8]. Have you loved? Do you wish to follow? He whom you wish to follow ran, flew. By what way? Through afflictions, through reproaches, through false accusations, through spittle in the face, through the blows of slaps and scourges, through a crown of thorns, through the cross, through death. Why are you slothful? You wished to follow; the way has been shown. And do you say: Who follows there? Blush, you bearded one; women followed, whose birthday we celebrate today. We celebrate today the solemnity of the women martyrs of Thuburbo. Your Lord, our Lord, their Lord, the Lord of all, the Redeemer of life, by going ahead made of the narrow way a paved road, a royal road, fortified and clean, on which it might delight even women to walk; and are you still slothful? Do you not wish to shed blood for so great a blood? This your Lord says to you: I suffered first for you; give what you received, render back what you drank. Cannot you? Boys and girls could; delicate men and women could; the rich could, and the very rich, who, when the temptation of suffering suddenly rushed in, were not held back by their abundance of riches, nor captured by the sweetness of this life, considering that rich man who ended his riches and found torments; and they did not send their riches ahead, but rather went ahead of them in martyrdom. With such great examples going before, are you slothful? And yet you celebrate the feasts of the martyrs. Today is the birthday of the martyrs: I will go forth, you say, and perhaps with a better tunic. See with what conscience: love what you do, imitate what you celebrate, do what you praise. But I cannot. *The Lord is at hand, be not anxious about anything*[^9]. I, you say, cannot. Do not you fear the fountain itself; from where those women were filled, you too can be filled, if you approach eagerly, if you do not swell like a hill, but humble yourself like a valley, that you may merit to be filled.

**7.** Let things not be hard for us, then, brothers, especially in these times when affliction abounds; the world was despised by the martyrs when it was flourishing; truly with great praise it was despised when flourishing, and it is loved when perishing. They despised its flowers, and you embrace its thorns. If you are slothful to migrate, let at least the ruinous house frighten you. But the pagan mocks you. From where does the pagan mock you? It is indeed time for the pagan to mock you, because the predictions of your Lord are being fulfilled; he would more rightly mock you if what he foretold were not being fulfilled. He denies the God whom you worship; you, from the things the world suffers, show him to be truthful, and, not saddened by what was foretold, rejoice in what was promised. For he came at that time when the world, now declining in age, made as it were so as to be ended, was going to abound in disasters and calamities and straits and troubles. He came for your consolation, who came then; lest you fail amid the afflictions of the perishing and passing life, he promised another life. Before the world labored under these afflictions and calamities, the prophets were sent; servants were sent to this great sick man, to the human race, like one languishing man stretched out and lying from East to West; the powerful physician sent his servants. It came about that such relapses should come upon this sick man, in which he would labor much. And the physician said: This sick man is going to labor much, I am necessary. Now let the foolish sick man say to the physician: Lord, I labor from the time you came. Fool, you do not labor because I came, but because you were going to labor, I came. In short, then, brothers, why do we say much? *The Lord, completing his word, has cut it short upon the earth*[^10]. Let us live well, and for our good life let us not hope for the passing goods of the earth. Earthly happiness is a cheap wage for a good life; what you live well here is not worth as much as what you crave here, although by craving such things you do not even live well. If you wish to change your life, change your craving. You keep faith with God, and this so that you may be happy on earth. Is this the whole reason why you keep faith with God? Is your faith worth so much? Do you value it at so much? Do you sell it off thus? If you have something for sale on earth, and you make a pact with a buyer, you name a dearer price, he a cheaper: It is worth so much, you say, since you raise up what you sell; and he: It is not worth so much, but so much; and he gives himself a lower price, wishing to buy more cheaply. The Lord Christ corrects you. You say to your Lord Christ: Lord, I keep faith with you, and you, give to me on earth. Fool, what you sell is not worth so much; you err, you do not know what you have. You keep faith, and you seek earth? Your faith is worth more than the earth; you do not know how to value it. I know how much it is worth, who gave it to you; it is worth as much as the whole earth is; add to the earth heaven too, it is worth more. From where is there more than earth and heaven? He who made both earth and heaven. Turned to the Lord . . .
