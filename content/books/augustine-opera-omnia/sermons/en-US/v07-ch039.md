# Sermon 260/D: A Discourse on the Lord's Day of the Octave of Holy Easter

**1.** Your Charity knows that on the first Lord's day, that is, before these eight days, we sang this to the Lord, which today: *This is the day which the Lord has made*[^1]. For what day is there that the Lord has not made? But by the name of day the Spirit of God wished to commend a certain chief work of God. But what is better among all the works of God than a faithful man? For as on that day too we recalled to you, in the first works of the world *God said: let there be light, and there was light. And God divided between the light and the darkness; and called the light day, and the darkness night*[^2]. If, then, he called the light day, without doubt those to whom the Apostle says: *You were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^3], are day. All the saints, all the faithful, and through this all the just—because *the just lives by faith*[^4]—all together in most concordant unity are day; and that very unity of all is one day. For how is it not one day, of which it is said in the Acts of the Apostles: *They had one soul and one heart in the Lord*[^5]? God plainly divided among men too between day and night, between light and darkness; about to give eternity to the light, condemnation to the darkness. It does not yet appear now, but God has already divided. A man lives badly, and perhaps in God's predestination is light: and another lives well, and perhaps in predestination is night. But God divided, and keeps the division itself hidden with himself, lest the day grow proud, and become night. For there are some who wish to ascribe to their own virtue that they are just, and with a hellish heart and sacrilegious mouth dare to say: God made us men, but we make ourselves just. If they make themselves just, this is not the day which the Lord made. Let the faithful one, then, made light from darkness, to whom the Apostle says: *You were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*; let him say with the same Apostle: *I am not worthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God*[^6]. See the night, see the darkness. When he persecuted the Church of God, *darkness was over the abyss.* The Lord called him from above: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^7] The darkness was wiped away, the light rose; he began to preach whom he persecuted, and said: *I am not worthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God.* From where, then, comes to you what you are? *By the grace of God I am what I am*[^8]. This is the day which the Lord has made.

**2.** And so I address you, one day, infants born badly of Adam, born well in Christ. See that you are day, see that the Lord made you. He drove from your hearts the darkness of sins, he renewed your life. You are to be mingled today with the number of the peoples: choose whom you will imitate, do not choose for yourselves the lost, with whom you will perish. Do not say: Why, is this man not faithful? and he gets drunk. Why, is this man not faithful? and he has a wife, and also has a concubine. Why, is this man not faithful? and he daily swears a lie for gain. Why, is this man not faithful? and he gives his money at interest. Why, is this man not faithful? and he questions a witch. Why, is this man not faithful? and when his head aches, he ties amulets to his neck; and he who does not want to die, ties them to his neck. If you say these things, you perish. I testify to you before God and his angels; what was ours, we have given what we received; nor did we give it, but it was given through us. The money is the Lord's: we are dispensers, not bestowers. We have a common Lord, we measure out rations to fellow servants: from that same barn we too live. We are not our own, but his who poured out his blood as a price for us. We have been redeemed together, we all have one price: our wheat is the holy Gospel. He who redeemed us made us brothers from servants: the Only-begotten made us coheirs. He was one, and condescended to have brothers: do not forget this condescension, beloved. You are called faithful, live faithfully: keep faith to your Lord in your heart and conduct. Do not mingle yourselves with bad conduct in the crowd of bad Christians. See what I say: be grains. The chaff abounds on the threshing floor, but the winnowing is to come, the chaff will be separated; neither does one straw enter the barn with you, nor does one grain go into the fire. He knows how to separate, who was able to gather; you err, if you think the Lord errs. He knows you, who created you, and re-created you; for if he had only created and not re-created, you would enter into the mass of perdition. Do I say to you, my brothers, that you will not be able to find Christians living well? Far be it that I should think this of the threshing floor of my Lord. If this is so, why do I labor? See the good, whom you may imitate: be such, and you will find them. But if you begin to be bad, you will believe all are bad; and it is a lie, you are deceived. You attend to the threshing floor from afar, therefore only the chaff meets you: approach, seek, fill your hand, apply the judgment of the mouth; from there is shaken out everything that flies, and what is heavy remains. You will find good Christians, believe me; good married men, keeping faith to their wives; good married women, keeping faith to their husbands. Seek, and you will find: be such, and it will not be hidden from you. Like meets like. Are you a grain? you join yourself to a grain. Are you chaff? you join yourself to chaff. You will find those who do not give their money at interest, you will find those who hate fraud more than loss: you will find these. Begin to be such, and you will see how many there are. They are few, but in comparison with the many. It is being winnowed, and it is a mass. He will winnow, *who bears the winnowing fan in his hand*[^9].

**3.** Now, beloved, I address also you who are faithful, baptized long ago, baptized in a former year, baptized in earlier years, you too I address; go by that same way, by which you will not perish, if these wish to follow. For it will pertain to your judgment, if with your bad conduct you both perish, and are lost.

[^2]: Gen 1:3-5
[^3]: Eph 5:8
