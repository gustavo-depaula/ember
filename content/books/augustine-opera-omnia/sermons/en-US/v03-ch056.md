# Sermon 90: On the Words of the Gospel, Matthew 22:1-14: on the Marriage of the King's Son, Against the Donatists; on Love

**1.** The marriage of the king's son and his banquet all the faithful know, and the preparation of the Lord's table is set before the will of all. But it matters how one approaches, since to approach is not forbidden. For the holy Scriptures teach us that there are two banquets of the Lord: one to which both good and bad come, the other to which the bad do not approach. Therefore the Lord's banquet, of which we just now heard as the Gospel was read, has at any rate both good and bad. All who excused themselves from this banquet are bad: but not all who entered are good. I address you, then, who recline at this banquet as good men, whoever attend to what was said: *He who eats and drinks unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself*[^1]. All of you who are such, I address you, that you do not seek the good outside, but tolerate the bad within.

**2.** I do not doubt that your Charity wants to hear who these are about whom I have been speaking, that they should not seek the good outside, but tolerate the bad within. If all are bad within, whom have I addressed? But if all are good within, whom have I admonished to tolerate the bad? First, then, with the Lord's help, let us get out of this question as we can. If you weigh good perfectly and clearly, no one is good but God alone. You have the Lord saying most plainly: *Why do you ask me about the good? No one is good but God alone*[^2]. How, then, does that marriage have good and bad, if no one is good but God alone? First, you must know that in a certain way we are all bad. Altogether in a certain way we are all bad: but in a certain way we are not all good. For can we compare ourselves to the Apostles? to whom the Lord himself says: *If, then, you, though you are bad, know how to give good gifts to your children*[^3]. If we consider the Scriptures, there was one bad man there among the twelve Apostles, on account of whom the Lord says in a certain place: *And you are clean, but not all*[^4]. Yet, addressing them all in common, he says: *If you, though you are bad.* Peter heard this, John heard it, Andrew heard it, all the other eleven Apostles heard it. What did they hear? *You, though you are bad, know how to give good gifts to your children: how much more will your Father who is in heaven give good things to those who ask him*[^5]? Hearing that they were bad, they despaired: hearing that God in heaven was their Father, they breathed again. *Though you are,* he says, *bad:* what then is owed to the bad, but punishment? *How much more,* he says, *your Father who is in heaven?* what is owed to sons, but a reward? In the name of the bad, the fear of punishments: in the name of sons, the hope of heirs.

**3.** In a certain respect, then, those same men were bad who in a certain respect were good. For to those to whom it was said: *Though you are bad, you know how to give good gifts to your children;* it was at once subjoined: *How much more your Father who is in heaven?* The Father, then, of the bad, but of those not to be abandoned; because he is the physician of those to be healed. In a certain way, then, they were bad. And yet those guests of the head of the household at the king's marriage, I think, were not of that number of whom it was said: *They invited the good and the bad*[^6]; so as to be reckoned in the number of the bad, those whom we heard were excluded in the one who was found not to have a wedding garment. In a certain respect, I say, they were bad who were good: in a certain respect they were good who were bad. Hear John, in what respect they were bad: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^7]. Behold, in what respect they were bad: because they had sin. In what respect were they good? *If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all iniquity*[^8]. If, then, we say, according to this exposition which you have heard me bring forward, I think, from the holy Scriptures, that the same men are both good in a certain way and bad in a certain way; if according to this sense we wish to take what was said: *They invited the good and the bad,* that is, those same men both good and bad; if we wish to take it thus, we are not permitted to, on account of the one who was found not having a wedding garment, and not merely cast out so as only to be deprived of that banquet, but condemned to the eternal punishment of darkness.

**4.** But someone says: What of one man? What is the wonder? What is the great thing, if one man crept in among the crowd, into the servants of the head of the household, not having a wedding garment? Could it be said on his account: *They invited the good and the bad*[^9]? Attend, then, and understand, my brothers. That one man was one kind; for they were many. Let a diligent hearer answer me here, and say: I do not want you to tell me your surmises; I want it proved to me that that one was many. The Lord will be present, I will prove it plainly, nor will I seek farther afield to be able to prove it. In his very own words God will help me, and what may be plain to you he will minister through me. Behold, *the head of the household came in, to inspect those reclining*[^10]. See, my brothers, that it did not pertain to the servants except to invite and bring in the good and the bad: see that it was not said, The servants considered those reclining, and there found a man not having a wedding garment, and said to him. This is not written. The head of the household inspected, the head of the household found, the head of the household distinguished, the head of the household separated. This indeed was not to be passed over. But we undertook to prove something else, how that one was many. *The head of the household,* then, *came in to inspect those reclining, and found a man not having a wedding garment: and he said to him; Friend, how did you come in here not having a wedding garment? But he was silent*[^11]. For such a one was questioning him, to whom he could feign nothing. For that garment was inspected in the heart, not in the flesh: which, if it had been put on outwardly, would not have been hidden even from the servants. Where the wedding garment is to be put on, hear, where it speaks: *Let your priests be clothed with righteousness*[^12]. Of that garment the Apostle says: *If indeed, clothed, we be not found naked*[^13]. So he was found by the Lord who lay hidden from the servants. Questioned, he is silent: he is bound, cast out, condemned, one out of many. Lord, I had said that you, who admonish all to admonish. Recall with me the words you have heard, and now you will find, now you will judge, that that one man was many. The Lord had certainly questioned one, had said to one: *Friend, how did you come in here?* One had been silent, and of that one it had been said: *Bind his hands and feet, and cast him into the outer darkness: there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^14]. Why this? *For many are called, but few chosen.* What could anyone say against this manifestation of the truth? *Cast him,* he says, *into the outer darkness.* Him, certainly, the one of whom the Lord says: *For many are called, but few chosen.* Therefore the few are not cast outside. Surely it was one who did not have a wedding garment. *Cast him out.* Why is he cast out? *For many are called, but few chosen*[^15]. Leave the few, cast out the many. Surely it was one. That one was altogether not only many, but in the multitude of the good they exceeded the number. For there are many good men too: but in comparison with the bad, the good are few. Much grain was born: compare it to the chaff, and the grains are few. Those same men, in themselves many, in comparison with the bad are few. How do we prove that in themselves they are many? *Many will come from east and west*[^16]. Where will they come? To that banquet into which the good and the bad enter. Speaking of the other banquet he added: *And they will recline with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, in the kingdom of heaven*[^17]. That is the banquet to which the bad are not going to approach. Let this banquet which is now be worthily received, that one may come to that. Those same, then, are the many who are the few: many in themselves, few in comparison with the bad. So what does the Lord say? He found one, and says: Let the many be cast out, let the few remain. For to say: *Many are called, but few chosen,* is nothing other than to show plainly who are held in this banquet as such that they are brought through to the other banquet, to which none of the bad will approach.

**5.** What, then, is it? All you who approach the Lord's table, which is here, I do not want you to be with the many to be separated, but with the few to be preserved. How can you do this? Receive the wedding garment. Expound to us, you will say, the wedding garment. Without doubt it is that garment which only the good have, who are to be left in the banquet, to be preserved for the banquet to which no bad man approaches, to be brought through by the Lord's grace: they have the wedding garment. Let us seek, then, my brothers, among the faithful, who they are who have something the bad do not have, and that will be the wedding garment. If we say the Sacraments, you see how they are common to the bad and the good. Is it baptism? Without baptism indeed no one comes to God: but not everyone who has baptism comes to God. I cannot, then, understand baptism to be the wedding garment, that is, the sacrament itself; for I see that garment in the good, I see it in the bad. Perhaps it is the altar, or what is received from the altar. We see that many eat, and eat and drink judgment to themselves. What, then, is it? Is it fasting? The bad fast too. Is it running to the Church? The bad run too. Finally, is it that miracles are done? Not only do the good and the bad do them, but sometimes the good do not do them. Behold, among the old people the magicians of Pharaoh did miracles, the Israelites did not do them: among the Israelites only Moses and Aaron did them[^18]; the rest did not do them, but saw, feared, believed. Were the magicians of Pharaoh, doing miracles, better than the people of Israel, who could not do miracles, and yet the people pertained to God? In the Church itself, hear the Apostle: *Are all prophets? do all have gifts of healings? do all speak with tongues*[^19]?

**6.** What, then, is that wedding garment? This is the wedding garment: *But the end of the precept,* the Apostle says, *is love from a pure heart, and a good conscience, and faith unfeigned*[^20]. This is the wedding garment. Not any sort of love: for often even men who share a bad conscience seem to love one another. Those who together commit robberies, who together do evil deeds, who together love actors, who together cheer for charioteers and hunters, often love one another: but in them there is not *love from a pure heart, and a good conscience, and faith unfeigned.* Such love is the wedding garment. *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become,* he says, *sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal*[^21]. Tongues alone arrived, and it is said to them, Why have you come in here not having a wedding garment? *If I have,* he says, *prophecy, and know all mysteries, and all knowledge, and have faith so as to move mountains, but have not love, I am nothing*[^22]. Behold, those are the miracles of men who often do not have a wedding garment. If I have all these, he says, and do not have Christ, I am nothing. *I am,* he says, *nothing.* Is prophecy, then, nothing? Is knowledge of the mysteries, then, nothing? Those things are not nothing: but I, if I have them and do not have love, I am nothing. How many goods profit nothing without one good? If I do not have love, if I lavish alms on the poor, if to confession of the name of Christ I go even to blood, even to the flames—these things can be done even out of love of glory; they are empty. Because, then, they can be done even out of love of glory, empty, not out of the richest love of devotion, he mentions even these, and hear these: *If I distribute all my goods for the use of the poor, and if I hand over my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^23]. This is the wedding garment. Question yourselves; if you have it, you are secure at the Lord's banquet. There are two in one man, love and cupidity. Let love be born in you, if it is not yet born; and if it is born, let it be nourished, fed, grow. But that cupidity, although in this life it cannot be entirely extinguished—*for if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^24]: but in so far as cupidity is in us, in so far we are not without sin—let love grow, let cupidity decrease; that at some point love may be perfected, and cupidity consumed. Clothe yourselves with the wedding garment: I address you who do not yet have it. You are already within, you already approach the banquet, and you do not yet have a garment in honor of the bridegroom: you still seek your own things, not the things of Jesus Christ. For the wedding garment is received in honor of the coupling, that is, of bridegroom and bride. You know the bridegroom: it is Christ. You know the bride: it is the Church. Bring a gift to her who is wedded, to him who weds. If you bring well to those who wed, you will be sons. So in this make progress. Love the Lord, and there learn to love yourselves: that when, by loving the Lord, you have loved yourselves, you may securely love your neighbors as yourselves. For when I do not find a man loving himself, how do I entrust to him a neighbor whom he should love as himself? And who is there, he says, who does not love himself? Behold who it is: *He who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^25]. Does he love himself who loves his flesh and hates his soul, to his own harm, to the harm of his soul and his flesh? But who loves his own soul? He who loves God with all his heart and all his mind. Now to such a one I entrust a neighbor. Love your neighbors as yourselves.

**7.** Who, he says, is my neighbor? Every man is your neighbor. Did we not all have two parents? The animals of each kind are neighbors to one another, dove to dove, leopard to leopard, asp to asp, beast to beast; and is not a man neighbor to a man? Recall the institution of creation. God spoke, the waters brought forth; swimming things, great whales, fish, winged creatures, and the like they brought forth. Are all birds from one bird? Are all vultures from one vulture? Are all doves from one dove? Are all snakes from one snake? Are all gilt-fish from one gilt-fish? Are all sheep from one sheep? Surely the earth brought forth all kinds at once. It came to man, and the earth did not bring forth man. One father was made for us: nor even two, father and mother: one father, I say, was made for us, nor even two, father and mother: but from one father one mother; one made from no one, but by God, and one from him[^26]. Attend to our race: we flowed from one fountain; and because that one was turned into bitterness, we have all become wild olives from the olive tree. Grace too has come. One man begot to sin and to death, yet one race, yet all neighbors to one another; yet not only alike, but also kinsmen. One came against one: against the one who scattered, the one who gathers. So against the one who killed, the one who gives life. *For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive*[^27]. But as everyone who is born from him dies, so in Christ everyone who believes is made alive. But if he has a wedding garment, if he is invited to be preserved, not to be separated.

**8.** Have love, therefore, my brothers. I have expounded to you the wedding garment, I have expounded to you the garment. Faith is praised, it is certain, it is praised: but what faith, the Apostle distinguishes. For the apostle James chides certain men glorying in faith and not having good morals, and says: *You believe that there is one God, and you do well. The demons also believe, and tremble*[^28]. For what Peter was praised, for what he was called blessed, recall with me. Because he said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^29]? He who pronounced him blessed attended not to the sound of the words, but to the affection of the heart. For do you want to know that Peter's blessedness was not in those words? The demons too said these things. *We know who you are; you are the Son of God*[^30]. Peter confessed the Son of God: the demons confessed the Son of God. Distinguish, lord, distinguish. I distinguish plainly. Peter said it in love, the demons from fear. Finally, he says: *I am with you even to death*[^31]. They say: *What have we to do with you*[^32]? Therefore you who have come to the banquet, do not glory in faith alone. Distinguish faith itself too, and then the wedding garment is recognized in you. Let the Apostle distinguish, let him teach us: *Neither circumcision,* he says, *avails anything, nor uncircumcision, but faith*[^33]. Tell which: do not even the demons believe and tremble? I tell you, he says, hear, I distinguish, now I distinguish: *but faith, which works through love*[^34]. What faith, then? what sort of faith? *That which works through love. If I have all knowledge,* he says, *and all faith, so as to move mountains, but have not love, I am nothing*[^35]. Have faith with love: for you cannot have love without faith. This I admonish, this I exhort, this in the name of the Lord I teach your Charity, that you have faith with love: because you can have faith without love. For I do not exhort you to have faith, but love. For you cannot have love without faith; for I mean the love of God and neighbor: how can this be without faith? How does one love God who does not believe in God? How does the fool love God who says in his heart: *There is no God*[^36]? It can happen that you believe Christ came, and do not love Christ. But it cannot happen that you love Christ, and do not say that Christ came.

**9.** Therefore have faith with love. This is the wedding garment. Love one another, you who love Christ: love friends, love enemies. Let it not be hard for you. For what do you lose, where you gain much? Why do you ask God, as for a great thing, that your enemy may die? This is not the wedding garment. Attend to the bridegroom himself hanging on the cross for you, and asking the Father for his enemies: *Father,* he says, *forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^37]. You have seen the bridegroom saying these things; see also the friend of the bridegroom invited with the wedding garment. Attend to blessed Stephen, how he rebukes the Jews, as though fierce, as though angry: *Stiff-necked, and uncircumcised in heart and ears, you have always resisted the Holy Spirit. Which of the Prophets did your fathers not kill*[^38]? You have heard how the tongue rages. Still you are ready to speak against anyone—and would that you spoke against him who has offended God, not him who has offended you. He offends God and you do not rebuke him; he offends you, you cry out: where is that wedding garment? You have heard, then, how Stephen raged: hear how he loved. He offended those whom he was rebuking, he was stoned by them. And when on every side he was pressed and beaten by the hands of the raging and the blows of stones, he first said: *Lord Jesus Christ, receive my spirit*[^39]. Then, after he had prayed standing for himself, for those who were stoning him he bent his knee, and said: *Lord, do not lay this sin against them*[^40]: let me die in the flesh, not they in the heart. And having said this, he fell asleep. After these words he added nothing: he spoke, and departed; his last prayer was for his enemies. Learn to have the wedding garment. So you too bend your knees, and strike your forehead on the ground, and, about to approach the table of the Lord, the banquet of the holy Scriptures, do not say, If only my enemy would die: Lord, if I have deserved anything from you, kill my enemy. But if perhaps you say this, do you not fear that he may answer you: If I wanted to kill your enemy, would I not first kill you? Or do you glory because you have now come invited? Consider, a little while ago what you were. Did you not blaspheme me? did you not mock me? did you not want to wipe my name from the earth? but you applaud yourself, because you have come invited. If I had killed you, my enemy, whom would I make my friend? Why, by praying badly, do you teach me what I did not do to you? Nay rather, says God to you, let me teach you to imitate me. Hanging on the cross I said: *Forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^41]. I taught this to my soldier. Be my recruit against the devil. Otherwise you will in no way fight unconquered, unless you pray for your enemies. Speak plainly, speak even this, speak so as to pursue your enemy: but speak knowingly; distinguish what you say. Behold, a man is your enemy: answer me, what in him is hostile to you; is it that he is a man that is hostile to you? No. But what? That he is bad. That he is a man, which I made, is not hostile to you. He says to you: I made man, not bad: he became bad through disobedience, who obeyed the devil rather than God. What he himself made, this is hostile to you: in that he is bad, he is your enemy; not in that he is a man. For I hear both man and bad: the one name is of nature, the other of fault: I heal the fault, and preserve the nature. This your God says to you: And behold, I avenge you, I kill your enemy; from him I take away what is bad, I preserve what is a man: if I make that man good, have I not killed your enemy, and made your friend? So ask what you ask, that not men may perish, but the enmities themselves may perish. But if you pray this, that the man may die; you, a bad man, pray against a bad man: and when you say, Kill the bad man; he answers you, Which of you?

**10.** Extend your love, then, not only to your spouses and your children. That love is found even in beasts and sparrows. You know how these sparrows and swallows love their mates, together warm their eggs, together nurse their young, with a certain pleasing and natural goodness, with no reward thought of. For the sparrow does not say: I will nurse my young, that when I have grown old, they may feed me. It thinks none of these things: it loves freely, it feeds freely; it shows the affection of a parent, it does not require a repayment. And we—I know, I know—you love your children thus. *For children ought not to lay up treasure for their parents, but parents for their children*[^42]. Hence too many of you excuse your greed, because you acquire for your children, and save for them. But extend your love, let that love grow: but to love children and spouses is not yet that wedding garment. Have faith toward God. First love God. Extend yourself to God; and whom you can, snatch to God. Is he an enemy: let him be snatched to God. Is he a son, a wife, a servant: let him be snatched to God. Is he a stranger: let him be snatched to God. Is he an enemy: let him be snatched to God. Snatch, snatch the enemy: by snatching him he will not be an enemy. So let progress be made, so let love be nourished, that, nourished, it may be perfected: so let the wedding garment be put on: so let the image of God, to which we were created, be re-engraved by progress. For by sinning it had been worn out, it had been rubbed away. Whence worn out? whence worn down? When it is rubbed against the earth. What is it, rubbed against the earth? It is worn by earthly desires. *For although man walks in an image, yet he is vainly troubled*[^43]. Truth is sought in the image of God, not vanity. For by loving truth that image, to which we were created, is re-engraved, and the proper coin is rendered to our Caesar. For so you heard from the Lord's answer, the Lord saying to the Jews tempting him: *Why do you tempt me, hypocrites? Show me the coin of the tribute*[^44], that is, the impression of the image and the superscription. Show me what you pay, what you make ready, what is exacted of you; show me. They showed him a denarius: and he asked whose image and superscription it had. They answered: *Caesar's*[^45]. This Caesar seeks his own image too. Caesar does not want to lose what he commanded, and God does not want to lose what he made. Caesar, my brothers, did not make the coin: the mint-workers make it; it is commanded to craftsmen, he commanded his ministers. The image was impressed on the coin: on the coin is the image of Caesar. And yet what others impressed is sought: he treasures it, he does not want it denied to himself. The coin of Christ is man. There is the image of Christ, there the name of Christ, the gift of Christ, and the offices of Christ.
