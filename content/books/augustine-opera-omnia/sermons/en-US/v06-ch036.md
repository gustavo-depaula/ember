# Sermon 214: On the Handing Over of the Creed

**1.** According to the measure of our age and of our beginnings, according to the inexperience of the office undertaken and the effect of our love toward you, we who now, as ministers, stand by the altar to which you are about to draw near, ought not to defraud you of the ministry of a sermon. The Apostle says: *For if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord, and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved. For with the heart one believes unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation*[^1]. This the Creed builds up in you, which you ought both to believe and to confess, that you may be able to be saved. And those things, indeed, which you are briefly to receive, to commit to memory and to bring forth with the mouth, are not new or unheard of to you. For in the holy Scriptures and in the ecclesiastical sermons you are wont to hear them set down in many ways. But, briefly collected and reduced into a certain order and bound together, they are to be handed over to you, that your faith may be built up, your confession prepared, and your memory not burdened. These are the things you are faithfully to retain, and to give back from memory.  
After this preliminary, the whole Creed is to be pronounced, without any disputation interposed: I believe in God the Father almighty, and the rest that follows in it. Which Creed you know is not wont to be written. This said, this disputation is to be added.

**2.** These things which you have briefly heard, you ought not only to believe, but also to commit to memory in those very words and to bring forth with the mouth. But since they must be fortified against those who think differently and are taken captive by the devil, who lie in wait for the faith when they oppose salvation, remember that you so believe God almighty, that there is no nature at all which he himself did not create. And therefore he punishes sin, which he himself did not make, because by it the nature is defiled which he made. All creatures, then, visible and invisible: whatever, with a rational mind, can be a partaker of unchangeable truth, such as angel and man; whatever lives and feels, although it lacks understanding, such as are all animals, on earth, in the waters, in the air, walking, creeping, swimming, flying; whatever, without understanding, without any feeling, is in any way said to live, such as are those things which are fixed by roots in the earth and, sprouting, burst forth and rise up into the air; whatever by bulk alone occupies a place, such as a stone, and whatever elements of the very worldly mass are perceived or even touched; all these the almighty made, joining the lowest and the highest by middle things, and disposing all that he created in fitting places and times. But he made them not from any matter which he himself did not make. For he did not form things foreign to him, but he himself established what he should form. For he who says that he could not make something from nothing, how does he believe that the Almighty made it? Without doubt he denies the almighty, who says that God could not make the world, if he had not whence to make it. For what omnipotence is there, where there is such great want, that, like a craftsman, he could not reach the effect of his work unless a matter which he himself did not establish helped him? From these opinions and errors, then, let him cleanse his mind who believes in God almighty. For that which is called the formless matter of things, capable of forms and subject to the work of the Creator, is convertible into all things which it shall please the Founder to make. God did not find it, as though coeternal with himself, whence to fashion the world: but he himself established it out of altogether nothing, together with the things which he made of it. Nor was there, before the very things which are seen to be made of it, that out of which they were made: and through this the Almighty first made all things out of nothing, together with which he made at the same time that out of which he made them. So the matter of heaven and earth, as these were created in the beginning, was created together with them at the same time; nor was there whence the things should be made which God made in the beginning: and yet they were made which the Almighty made, which, made, he composed, filled, adorned. For if the things which he made in the beginning he surely made out of nothing, even of these things which he made, whatever he wishes to make he is able, because he is almighty.

**3.** Nor let the unrighteous believe for this reason that God is not almighty, because they do many things against his will. For even when they do what he does not will, he himself will do of these what he wills. In no way, then, do they change or overcome the will of the Almighty: for whether a man be justly damned, or mercifully freed, the will of the Almighty is fulfilled. What, then, the Almighty does not will, this alone he cannot do. He uses the evil, then, not according to their depravity, but according to his own right will. For as the evil use ill their nature, which is good, that is, his good work, so he, good, uses well even their evil works, that the will of the almighty may not in any part be overcome. For if he had not what, being good, he might justly and well do of the evil, in no way would he allow them either to be born or to live; whom he himself did not make evil, because he made men; because he created not the sins which are against nature, but the natures themselves. Yet, foreknowing, he could not be ignorant that they would be evil; but, as he knew what evils they themselves would do, so he also knew what good he himself would do of them. Who could explain in words, who could equal with praises, how great a good the passion of the Savior conferred on us, whose blood was shed unto the forgiveness of sins? And yet so great a good was accomplished through the malice of the devil, through the malice of the Jews, through the malice of Judas the betrayer. Nor is the good justly repaid to them which God, not they, through them conferred on men; but punishment is justly repaid to them, since they wished to harm. But as we could find something which was manifest even to us, how God used well even the evil works of the devil, of the Jews, and of Judas the betrayer for our redemption and salvation; so, in the hidden and secret recesses of the whole creation, which we penetrate neither with the keenness of the eyes nor of the mind, he knows how God uses the evil well, that in all things which are born and administered in the world, the will of the Almighty may be fulfilled.

**4.** But since I have said that the Almighty cannot do this alone, what he does not will, lest anyone think that I rashly said that the Almighty cannot do something, this too the blessed Apostle said: *If we believe not, he remains faithful, he cannot deny himself*[^2]. But because he does not will it, he cannot, because he cannot even will it. For righteousness cannot will to do what is unjust, or wisdom will what is foolish, or truth will what is false. Whence we are admonished that God almighty cannot, not only this which the Apostle says, *He cannot deny himself*, but many things cannot. Behold, I say, and by his truth I dare to say what I dare not deny: God almighty cannot die, cannot be changed, cannot be deceived, cannot become wretched, cannot be conquered. These and such things, far be it that the Almighty could do. And through this not only does truth show him to be almighty, in that he cannot do these things; but truth even compels him not to be almighty who could do these things. For God is willingly whatever he is; eternal, then, and unchangeable, and truthful, and blessed, and insuperable he is willingly. If, then, he could be what he does not will, he is not almighty; but he is almighty, therefore whatever he wills he can do. And therefore what he does not will, cannot be; who is therefore called almighty, since whatever he wills he can do. Of whom the Psalm too says: *In heaven and on earth he has done all things whatsoever he willed*[^3].

**5.** God almighty, then, who has made all things whatsoever he willed, begot the only Word through whom all things were made: but this not out of nothing, but out of himself: therefore he did not make, but begot. For *in the beginning he made heaven and earth*[^4]: but he did not make the Word in the beginning; because: *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^5]. This is God of God. But the Father is God, but not of God. This is the only Son of God, because, coeternal of the substance of the Father, equal to the Father, there is no other son. The Word is God: not as a word, whose sound can both be thought in the heart and brought forth by the mouth; but, as it was said, which cannot be said more briefly and better: *The Word was God.* Remaining unchangeably with the Father, and himself unchangeable too with the Father, of whom the Apostle says: *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*[^6]. For to be equal to the Father is nature to him, not grasping. So we believe *in Jesus Christ*, the only *Son* of God the Father, *our Lord.*

**6.** But because he who, in the form of God, did not count it a thing to be grasped to be equal to God, through whom we were created, that he might seek and save what had perished, *emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man*[^7]: so we believe in him that he *was born of the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary.* For both his nativities are truly to be believed wonderful, both of divinity and of humanity. That one is of a father without a mother, this of a mother without a father; that one is without any time, this in an acceptable time; that one eternal, this opportune; that one without a body in the bosom of the Father, this with a body, by which the virginity of the mother was not violated; that one without any sex, this without any male embrace. But we therefore say *born of the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary*, because, when the holy Virgin had inquired of the angel: *How will this be?* he answered: *The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you.* Then he added: *Therefore what will be born of you holy will be called the Son of God*[^8]. He did not say: "What will be born of you will be called the Son of the Holy Spirit." For the whole man, that is, the rational soul and body, was taken up by the Word, so that the one Christ, the one Son of God, was not only the Word, but the Word and man, the whole of this is the Son of God the Father on account of the Word, and the son of man on account of the man. Through that which is the Word, he is equal to the Father: through that which is man, the Father is greater. And together with the man he is the Son of God, but on account of the Word, by which the man was taken up; and together with the Word he is the son of man, but on account of the man, who was taken up by the Word. On account of whose holy conception in the womb of the Virgin, made not by the burning desire of the flesh, but by the fervent love of faith, therefore he is said to be *born of the Holy Spirit and the virgin Mary*; so that one of these may pertain not to the begetter, but to the sanctifier, but the other to her who conceived and bore. *Therefore*, he says, *what will be born of you holy will be called the Son of God.* Because "holy," therefore "of the Holy Spirit"; because *born of you*, therefore "of the virgin Mary"; because *the Son of God*, therefore "the Word made flesh."

**7.** But it behooved that, in the man taken up, not only the invisible should be seen and the one coeternal with the Father should be born in time, but also the one who cannot be handled should be held, the insuperable should be hung on a tree, the inviolable should be pierced with nails, and Life and Immortality should die on the cross, should be buried in a tomb; all this the Son of God, our Lord Jesus Christ. Whence it follows that the same only-begotten Son of God, our Lord Jesus Christ, not only born a man from man, but also having suffered human things even to death and burial, we should both believe with the heart unto righteousness and confess with the mouth unto salvation[^9]. For since the whole only Son of God, our Lord Jesus Christ, is the Word and man, and, that I may say it more expressly, the Word, the soul, and the flesh; to the whole is referred that in the soul alone he was sorrowful unto death[^10], because the only Son of God, Jesus Christ, was sorrowful; to the whole is referred that in the man alone he was crucified, since the only Son of God, Jesus Christ, *was crucified*; to the whole is referred that in the flesh alone he *was buried*, since the only Son of God, our Lord Jesus Christ, was buried. For from the time we began to say that we believe in Jesus Christ his only Son our Lord, from that whatever else we say of him, none is understood but Jesus Christ the only Son of God our Lord. Nor wonder: for we so say that the only Son of God, Jesus Christ our Lord, was buried, when his flesh alone was buried, just as, for example, we say that the apostle Peter today lies in the tomb, when we also most truly say that he rejoices in rest with Christ. For we say it is the same Apostle: for there are not two apostles Peter, but one. The same one, then, we say, both lies in the body alone in the tomb, and in the spirit alone rejoices with Christ. But it is added: *Under Pontius Pilate*; whether that the truth of the time may be gathered, or that the humility of Christ may be the more commended, that he suffered so much under a man as judge, who is to come as judge of the living and the dead with so great power.

**8.** But *on the third day he rose* in true flesh, but in no way thereafter to die. This his disciples proved with their eyes and hands, nor would so great a goodness deceive their faith, nor would the truth beguile it. But on account of brevity it is at once subjoined that *he ascended into heaven.* For he was with his disciples forty days, lest so great a miracle of his resurrection, if it were quickly withdrawn from their eyes, should be thought a mockery. Where he now *sits at the right hand of the Father*; which we ought prudently to behold with the eyes of faith, lest we think him immovably fixed in some seat, so that it is not permitted him to stand or to walk. For not because holy Stephen said he saw him standing[^11], did either he see a falsehood, or did he disturb the words of this Creed. Far be it to think this, far be it to say it. But his dwelling in the high and ineffable blessedness was so signified, that he was said to sit there. Whence also dwellings are called "seats," as when we ask where someone is, and it is answered: "In his seats." And of the servants of God it is most and most often said: "So many years that one sat in that monastery"; that is, he rested, sojourned, dwelt. Nor is the holy Scripture ignorant of this kind of speech. For that Shimei, ordered by King Solomon to dwell in the city of Jerusalem, with a threat added, that if ever he dared to go out from there, he should pay the due penalties, is reported to have sat there for three years[^12], which is understood to have dwelt. But the right hand itself of the Father is not so spoken of according to the form of a human body, as though the Father himself were at the left to the Son, if the Son were placed at his right hand according to these bodily positions and postures of the members. But the right hand of God means the loftiness of honor and ineffable felicity, as is read said of wisdom: *His left hand is under my head, and his right hand embraces me*[^13]. For if earthly convenience lies beneath, then from above eternal felicity embraces.

**9.** From the lofty dwelling of the heavens, then, where now is even his already immortal body, our Lord Jesus Christ *is to come to judge the living and the dead*, according to the most evident angelic testimony, which is written in the Acts of the Apostles. For the disciples, gazing at him ascending into heaven, and following him with suspended gaze, heard the angels say: *Men of Galilee, why do you stand here? This Jesus, who has been taken up from you, will so come, as you have seen him going into heaven*[^14]. The much and varied human presumption is repressed. In that form Christ will judge, in which he was judged. For so the Apostles saw him ascend into heaven, when they heard that he would so come. That form will be visible to the living and the dead, to the good and the evil; whether we understand the good to be called by the name of the living, and the evil of the dead; or take as living those whom his coming will find not yet to have finished this life, but as dead those whom his presence will raise up, as he himself in the Gospel says: *The hour will come, when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice, and will come forth, those who have done good to the resurrection of life, those who have done evil to the resurrection of judgment*[^15]. And they will see him in the form of man, those who believed in him, and those who despised him. But the form of God, in which he is equal to the Father, the ungodly will not see. *For the ungodly will be taken away*, as the Prophet says, *that he may not see the glory of the Lord*[^16]. And: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God*[^17]. Let these things, said of Jesus Christ the only Son of God our Lord, be enough.

**10.** We believe also *in the Holy Spirit*, proceeding from the Father, and yet not the Son; remaining upon the Son, and yet not the father of the Son: receiving of the Son, and yet not the son of the Son: but the Spirit of the Father and the Son, the Holy Spirit and himself God. For he would not have such a temple, if he were not God. Whence the Apostle says: *Do you not know that your bodies are the temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God?*[^18] Not the temple of a creature, but of the Creator. For far be it from us that we should be the temple of a creature, since the Apostle says: *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^19]. In this Trinity nothing is greater or less than another, no separation of works, no unlikeness of substance. One Father God, one Son God, one Holy Spirit God. Nor yet are the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit three gods, but one God; so that the Father himself is not the same as the Son, nor is the Son the same as the Father, nor is the Holy Spirit either Father or Son; but the Father, the Father of the Son, and the Son, the Son of the Father, and the Holy Spirit, the Spirit of the Father and the Son: and each one singly God, and the Trinity itself one God. May this faith imbue your hearts, and direct your confession. By hearing this, believe, that you may understand; that what you believe, you may, by advancing, be able to understand.

**11.** The *holy* Church too, your mother, as the heavenly Jerusalem, the holy city of God, honor, love, proclaim. She it is who, in this faith which you have heard, bears fruit and grows in the whole world, the Church of the living God, the pillar and ground of truth[^20]: who tolerates in the communion of the sacraments the evil who are to be separated at the end, from whom in the meantime she departs by the unlikeness of manners. She, on account of her grains now groaning amid the chaff, of which the mass owed to the granaries will be declared in the last winnowing, receives the keys of the kingdom of heaven, that in her, through the blood of Christ, by the working of the Holy Spirit, may be made *the forgiveness of sins.* In this Church the soul will come back to life, which had been dead by sins, that it may be made alive together with Christ, by whose grace we have been saved.

**12.** But neither ought we to doubt of this mortal flesh, that it will rise at the end of the age. *For this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality. It is sown in corruption, it will rise in incorruption; it is sown in dishonor, it will rise in glory: an animal body is sown, a spiritual body will rise*[^21]. This is the Christian, this the catholic, this the apostolic faith. Believe Christ saying: *A hair of your head will not perish*[^22], and, unbelief being driven away, think rather of how much you are worth. For what of ours can be despised by our Redeemer, whose hair cannot be despised? Or how shall we doubt that he will give eternal life to our soul and flesh, who for our sake both took up soul and flesh in which to die, and laid them down when he died, and took them back lest death be feared? All things that are handed over in the Creed, according to the measure of my ministry, I have expounded to your Charity. Which is therefore called the Creed (Symbolum), because there the agreed faith of our partnership is contained, and by its confession, as by a given sign, the faithful Christian is recognized.
