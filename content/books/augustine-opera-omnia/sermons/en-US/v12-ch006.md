# Sermon 204B

**1.** Just as today's annual return reminds us that the magi worshiped our Lord Jesus Christ as the firstfruits of the nations, so we also owe this solemnity a sermon on so great a mystery. Therefore I remind Your Charity: recall the recent day on which the Lord was born, and join to it today, on which the Lord was worshiped by the magi. The star could not tell them whose it was or whose excellence it foretold; yet a new and unique heavenly light shone forth and startled with its unprecedented wonder even those accustomed to observing the stars carefully. For no one had ever been signified by such a star, because no one like him had ever been born. Yet although that star was silent about who he was, where he was, and whose king he was, it shone. But God, not disappointing hearts that seek and knock, undoubtedly revealed in another way to those who asked what the star could not indicate. Scripture does not say what that way was, but left it for the discerning to understand, showing in the narrative itself how we can understand it.[^1]

**2.** For when the Jews had named Bethlehem as the city where, according to the Scriptures, Christ was to be born, since the house where God's Word lay as an infant was unknown, the same star led the magi to the house. Under its roof lay the one who had made the world and whom heaven had announced; thus far the radiance of the star leading them was evident. But we read that they were warned in a dream not to return to Herod, king of the Jews, who had said he wished to worship the one he longed to kill. Thus God showed by another revelation what the star could not express. We should therefore understand that it was not through the star alone, though it shone before their eyes with an unfamiliar and unprecedented light, that the magi in the east could know who was worthy of being signified by so great a star, to whom that radiance bore witness, whose glory such brilliance announced, where he should be sought, and where they must go to find and worship him. Rather, what the star's light silently signified was divinely made known through another revelation. As they wondered greatly and asked what this new appearance in heaven meant and to what it referred, they were informed—not by the star itself speaking, but in another way—who had descended from heaven and, while ruling heaven in his divinity, lay beneath heaven in the flesh. We understand that whose star it was, which had already been seen for a long time, was disclosed when the one it signified was born.[^2]

**3.** For it was seen for almost two years by those who wondered what it was. But after Christ was born, it was revealed to the magi that they should no longer marvel at the star but worship the one the star had foretold, and that he was king of the Jews. They came from the east and on this day worshiped him whom they had learned was born a few days earlier, and whose star they had learned was the one that had appeared to them for two years. Here someone will say, “How is it shown that the star appeared for two years?” Let us recall the Gospel's words. Herod, king of the Jews, when he had been outwitted by the magi—who, warned in a dream, refused to return to him so as not to reveal where Christ had been found—*killed the infants two years old and under*. The Evangelist did not pass over the reason why he did this, that is, why he ordered the little children two years old and under to be killed: *According to the time*, he says, *he had ascertained from the magi*. This shows that the star did not first appear when Christ was born, a very few days earlier, but had shone in heavenly foreannouncement for two years. Whose star it was became known when they were sent to worship on earth the one they had already seen prefigured in heaven.[^3]

**4.** If anyone asks why the star shone for two years foretelling Christ, the two years may be seen to signify the two commandments, one of love of God and the other of love of neighbor, *on which two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets*. For what seemed endlessly dispersed through many commands was enclosed in this brevity, so that we may understand the prophet to have said of it: *For the Lord will make his word complete and brief upon the earth*. Thus the one whose coming the star foretold for two years completed his word in two commandments. In these two years we may also understand the two testaments, the old and the new. For the author of the law that frightens us is not different from the author of the grace that helps us, since he himself said: *If you believed Moses, you would believe me too, for he wrote of me*; and he himself is God's Wisdom, of whom it was said: *She carries law and mercy on her tongue*. But since two years may more naturally be understood to signify two times, we may also understand Christ's two comings, one in humility, the other in glory, and our two births, because he through whom all things were made is certainly the author of both: one by which we are born in the flesh, the other by which we are reborn in faith.[^4]

**5.** Let us, then, exult and rejoice in him whom swaddling clothes wrapped and angels announced, who lay in a manger and shone among the stars, whom a star in heaven, not a jewel in a crown, revealed as the everlasting king whom the nations should worship, who sucked at the breast and made nursing infants martyrs. Let us recognize the cornerstone in whom two walls coming from different directions, one from the circumcised Jews, the other from the uncircumcised nations, are joined with a faithful kiss: *He is our peace, who made both one. He came*, as you heard the Apostle say, *and proclaimed peace to those far away and peace to those near*. The shepherds were near; the magi were far away. Both recognized him, both came; and according to the prophet Isaiah, both, like the ox and the donkey, found their food in the manger. For Christ glorified took to himself these peoples from Jews and gentiles, whose firstfruits he received at his birth. Thus the rejoicing of this day belongs especially to us who have come from the gentiles.[^5]

**6.** When the magi worshiped Christ, they brought forth gold, incense, and myrrh: gold because they saw a king, incense because they recognized the Lord. They also recognized the flesh that would suffer, for they brought myrrh for burial too. They offered these things according to their custom. From us, however, he receives gold if we give alms, incense if we pour out prayers, myrrh if we die for him. Therefore, since the Greek word “epiphany” means manifestation—for the Lord's hidden infancy was revealed to the magi who desired to worship him, with the star pointing the way—let us follow the path of the shining Gospel and devoutly worship him to whom we are manifest. Then, when God comes openly and will no longer be an infant, for he will not be silent, may we deserve to appear at his right hand.[^6]
