# Sermon 370: On the Nativity of the Lord

**1.** This present day brought great joy to the human race for having the hope of eternal life. For the first man, by whose falling we all fell, by whose ruin is our mortality, was not born, but made; with no father, no mother, but God working. This is the first condition of man. Adam from the earth. The second condition of man is that by which the woman is created from the side of the man. The third condition of man is that by which man is born of man and woman. The fourth condition is of God and man, by which Christ was born without a man from a woman. Of these four conditions only one is familiar to us, the other three are not in the eyes of the flesh, but in the faith of the heart. That a man was made from the earth without father, without mother, we have not known; that a woman was made from the side of a man we have not known, but read and heard we have believed. The third is familiar to us, that very daily one: from the embrace of male and female men are daily born. Now, then, there was the one without man and woman, now there was the second from man without woman, now there was the third from man and woman, the fourth remained without a man from a woman. But this fourth freed the three. For the first and the second fell, they begot the third from the ruin, in the fourth they found salvation.

**2.** Let virgins, then, exult; a virgin bore Christ. Let them not think anything that they vowed exterminated in her: she remained a virgin after the birth. Let widows exult; the widow Anna recognized Christ as an infant. Let the married exult; the married Elizabeth prophesied of the Lord Jesus Christ who was to be born. No rank was passed over, of which the salvation of all might not have testimony. For do only virgins come to the kingdom of heaven? Widows come too. Of great merit was that holy widow Anna. For from her virginity she had lived seven years with her husband; and when he died she had come to an old age, and in holy old age she awaited the infancy of the Savior; that, aged, she might see the little one, that, an old woman, she might recognize the little one, that she might see the Savior entering the world, herself about to go forth[^1]. And in the male sex these same three kinds are commended. Christ himself was born a boy; let boys exult, who vow continence to the boy. He himself truly consecrated the integrity of chastity, who brought fruitfulness to his mother, and did not take away her virginity. That old man Simeon had lived long, whose age is compared to Anna's; and he had heard a response, that he would not see death unless he first saw the Christ of the Lord[^2].

**3.** Understand, brothers, how great a desire the ancient saints had of seeing Christ. They knew that he was going to come, and all who lived piously said: O if that nativity might find me here! O if what I believe in the Scriptures of God, I might see with my own eyes! And that you may know how great a desire the saints had, who knew from the holy Scriptures that a virgin would give birth, as you heard when Isaiah was read: *Behold, a virgin shall conceive in the womb, and shall bear a son, and his name shall be called Emmanuel*[^3]. What Emmanuel is, the Gospel opened to us, saying: *which is interpreted: God with us*[^4]. Let it not, then, be a wonder to you, whatever unbelieving soul, let it not seem impossible to you that a virgin should give birth, that a virgin giving birth should remain a virgin. Understand God born, and you will not wonder at a virgin's birth. That you may know, then, that the ancient saints and just men desired to see what was granted to this old man Simeon; our Lord Jesus Christ, speaking to the disciples, says: *Many just men and prophets wished to see the things which you see, and did not see them; and to hear the things which you hear, and did not hear them*[^5]. This old man was very late to hear, but ripe to see. He did not wait to hear Christ speaking, since he recognized the infant. And this was granted to him, now decrepit, as it were desiring and sighing and saying daily in his prayers: When will he come? when will he be born? when shall I see him? Do you think I will last? do you think this will find me here? do you think these my eyes will see him, through whom the eyes of the heart will be revealed? He said these things in his prayers, and for his desire received a response, that he would not taste death, unless he first saw the Christ of the Lord. Mary his mother was carrying him as an infant: he saw, and recognized him. Where had he known the one whom he recognized? Was the one revealed within, who was born without? He saw, and recognized him. Simeon recognized the infant keeping silent; and the Jews killed the young man working wonders. When, therefore, he had recognized him, he received him into his arms, that is, into his arms, he embraced him. He was carrying the one by whom he was carried. For he himself is Christ the wisdom of God, reaching from end to end mightily, and disposing all things sweetly[^6]. How great was he there, and how mighty, how small had he been made? Made small, he was seeking the small. What is, he was seeking the small? Not the proud, not the exalted; but the humble and meek he was gathering. He deigned to be laid in the manger, that he might be the fodder of the pious beasts. Simeon, then, received him in his arms, and said: *Now you dismiss, Lord, your servant in peace.* You dismiss in peace, because I see peace. Why, then, do you dismiss in peace? *Because my eyes have seen your salvation*[^7]. The salvation of God, the Lord Jesus Christ. *Announce day from day his salvation*[^8].

**4.** You have, then, boys, the boy Jesus; holy old men, the old man Simeon. But if you seek that some married man too should bear testimony to the Lord, consider Zechariah. Let no one, then, seek anything else, my brothers, faithful Christians, whether virgin, or widow, or married woman, or boy, or continent man, or married man: whatever more anyone wishes to be, he does not find how he may pertain to Christ. We do not find that adulterers, fornicators, the unclean bore testimony to Christ. And that testimony might be borne to him by such people, he himself granted it, he himself gave it. For no one is holy by his own strength. Let us exult, then, dearest ones. From this present day the days grow. Believe in Christ, and the day grows in you. Have you believed? The day is begun. Have you been baptized? Christ is born in your heart. But did Christ, born, so remain? He grew, came to youth: but did not decline into old age. Let your faith, then, grow too, find strength, know no oldness. So you will pertain to Christ the Son of God, in the beginning the Word with God, the Word God; but the Word made flesh, that he might dwell among us[^9]. Majesty lay hidden there, where weakness appeared. Simeon received weakness in his hands, but recognized majesty within[^10]. Let no one despise the one born, if he wishes to be reborn. It pertained to him to be born for our sake, let it pertain to us to be reborn in him: who lives and reigns unto ages of ages. Amen.
