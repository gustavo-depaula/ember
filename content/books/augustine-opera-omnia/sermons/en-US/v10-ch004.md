# Sermon 342: On the Evening Sacrifice

In Which the Beginning of the Gospel of John Is Explained

**1.** A sermon must be rendered on the evening sacrifice. For we have prayed by singing, and by praying we have sung: *Let my prayer be directed as incense in your sight; the lifting up of my hands as an evening sacrifice*[^1]. In the prayer we observe the man, in the stretching out of the hands we recognize the cross. This, then, is the sign that we bear on our forehead, the sign by which we are saved. A sign mocked, that it might be honored; despised, that it might be glorified. May God appear, that man may pray; and God lies hidden, that man may die. *For if they had known it, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*[^2]. This sacrifice, then, where the priest is the victim, redeemed us by the poured-out blood of the Creator. Yet he did not create us with blood, but redeemed us with blood. For he created us *in the beginning*, which *was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^3]. By him we were created. The connected discourse adds: *All things were made through him, and without him was nothing made*[^4]. This is that by which we were created. But by what we were redeemed, hear: *What was made*, he says, *in him was life, and the life was the light of men; and the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not comprehended it*[^5]. It is still God: still that is being spoken of which always remains unchangeable; still that is being spoken of for whose seeing hearts must be cleansed; but by what they may be cleansed, he does not yet say. *The light*, he says, *shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not comprehended it*. But so that there may not be darkness, and that they may be able to comprehend it—for the darkness are sinners, the darkness are unbelievers—so, then, that there may not be darkness, and that they may be able to comprehend: *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^6]. See the Word, see the Word flesh, the Word before the flesh. *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; all things were made through him*[^7]. Where here is blood? Behold, here already is your author, but not yet your price. From where, then, are you redeemed? Because *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^8].

**2.** Attend a little above. *The light*, he says, *shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not comprehended it*[^9]. Because, then, the darkness did not comprehend the light, men needed a human testimony. They could not see the day; perhaps they could bear a lamp. Because, then, they were less fit to see the day, they could yet somehow bear a lamp: *There was a man sent from God, whose name was John. He came to bear witness to the light*[^10]. Who is he of whom this man came to bear witness to the light? How was he not that light, if he was even a lamp? First see that he was a lamp. Do you wish to hear the lamp speak of the day, and the day of the lamp? *You*, he says, *sent to John, and you were willing for an hour to exult in his light; he was a burning and shining lamp*[^11]. This John, then, what did he see, who despised the lamp? *He was not that light, but came to bear witness to the light*[^12]. Of what light? *He was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^13]. If every man, then John also. He who did not yet wish to show himself the day, had kindled for himself his own lamp as a witness. But it was such a lamp as could be kindled from the day. Hear John himself confessing: *We*, he says, *have all received of his fullness*[^14]. He was thought to be Christ, he professed himself to be a man. He was thought to be the Lord, he confessed himself to be a servant. You rightly recognize, O lamp, your humility, lest the wind of pride extinguish you. *For he was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*; that is, every living being which is capable of enlightenment, that is, every man having mind and reason, by which he can be a partaker of the Word.

**3.** That, then, which was the true light, which enlightens every man having a mind, coming into this world—where was it? *It was in this world*. But the earth too was in this world, and the sun and moon were in this world. Hear of your day, O eye of the human mind! *It was in this world, and the world was made through it*[^15]. So it was here, as even before the world existed, not as though it had not where it might be. For God by inhabiting contains, is not contained. Therefore in a wonderful and ineffable way it was in this world. *And the world was made through it, and the world did not know it*[^16]. What is the world that was made through it? *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^17], because all things were made through it. What is the world that *did not know it*? World and world, as house and house: a house in its structure, a house in its inhabitants. A house in its structure, as in: He made a great house, he built a most beautiful house. A house in its inhabitants, as in: A good house, may the Lord bless it; a bad house, may God spare it. Therefore *the world was made through it*, both the dwelling and the dwellers; *and the world did not know it*—the dwellers.

**4.** *He came unto his own, and his own did not receive him*[^18]. Why, then, did he come, as though not foreknowing that his own would not receive him? Hear for what he came: *But as many as received him*[^19]. His own did not receive him, and his own received him; the world did not believe, and the whole world believed. As we say: The whole tree is full of leaves; has the place for fruits been taken away? Both can be said, both can be understood, that the tree is full of leaves and that the tree is full of fruits; one tree in both, but with leaves to be scattered, fruits to be gathered. Therefore, you faithful of his, his servants, his lovers, whose glory, whose hope, whose substance he is: when you hear, *His own did not receive him*, do not grieve; for by believing you are his. *His own did not receive him*. Who are these? Perhaps the Jews, once called out of Egypt, freed by a mighty hand, led across the Red Sea, escaping on dry ground, rid of their pursuing enemies, fed with manna, rescued from slavery, led to a kingdom, bought with so many benefits. Behold his own, who did not receive him; but by not receiving him they were made strangers. They were in the olive tree; by being proud they were broken off. The contemptible wild olive, despicable for the bitterness of its berries, was throughout the whole world; at that wild olive the whole world shuddered, yet through humility it there merited to be grafted in, from where the olive by pride deserved to be cut off[^20]. Hear the olive being proud and deserving to be broken off: *We were not born of slavery, we have Abraham as our father*. It is answered: *If you were the children of Abraham, you would do the deeds of Abraham*. Against what was said, *We were not born of slavery: If the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed*[^21]. You boast that you are free? *Everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin*[^22]. How much safer, then, for you, a man, to be the slave of a man, than of a perverse desire? Yet by being proud they did not receive the humble one. See the wild olive worthy to be grafted in, that centurion, not from the Israelites but from the Gentiles: *Lord, I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof*[^23]. And the Lord: *Amen, I say to you, I have not found such faith in Israel*[^24]. In the olive tree I have not found what I found in the wild olive. Therefore let the proud olive be cut off, let the humble wild olive be grafted in. See the one grafting, see the one cutting off: *Therefore I say to you that many shall come from the East and the West*[^25]; much wild olive shall come to be grafted into the olive; *and shall recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven*. You have heard how the humble wild olive is grafted in; hear how the proud olive is cut off: *But the sons of the kingdom shall go into the outer darkness; there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth*[^26]. Why? Because *his own did not receive him*. And why is the wild olive grafted in? Because *as many as received him, he gave them the power to become sons of God*[^27].

**5.** Lift up your heart, human race, breathe in the air of life and of most secure freedom. What do you hear? What is promised to you? *He gave them the power*. What power? Perhaps that by which men are puffed up, of judging concerning human lives, of passing sentences upon the innocent and the guilty? *Power*, it says, *he gave them to become sons of God*[^28]. For they were already not sons, and were becoming sons; because he through whom they become sons of God was already the Son of God, and was made the Son of man. They too, then, were already sons of men, and were made sons of God. He came down to what he was not, who was something else. He raised you up to what you were not, because you were something else. Lift up your hope, then. Great is what has been promised to you, but it has been promised by the Great One. It seems much and incredible, and it is reckoned as though it could not be, that the sons of men should become sons of God. But more has been done for them, because the Son of God was made the son of man. Lift up your hope, then, O man, drive unbelief from your heart. Something more incredible has already been done for you than what is promised to you. Do you marvel that a man should have eternal life? Do you marvel that a man should arrive at eternal life? Marvel rather that for your sake God arrived at death. Why do you doubt the promise, having received so great a pledge? See, then, how he confirms you, how he strengthens the promise of God: *As many*, it says, *as received him, he gave them the power to become sons of God*. By what begetting? Not by that usual one, not the old, not the passing or carnal one. *Not of flesh*, it says, *not of blood, nor of the will of man, but of God they were born*[^29]. Do you marvel, do you not believe? *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^30]. Behold from where the evening sacrifice came about. Let us cling to him: let him who was offered for us be offered with us. For so, by the evening sacrifice, the old life is put to death, and at dawn the new one rises.
