# Sermon 223/B: A Treatise on the Holy Night

**1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ, about to suffer in the fullness of time for our eternal salvation, admonished the disciples, saying: *Watch and pray, lest you enter into temptation*[^1]. Although the Christian ought assiduously to take care of this, lest sleep consume the single nights, by the example of the Apostle who says: *In watchings often*[^2], yet on this night, that the whole world may watch for Christ, a more sacred and holier vigil has been established; that what divine mercy did once, human devotion may celebrate by a yearly solemnity, lest forgetfulness permit it to be blotted out. *For Christ died once, the righteous for the unrighteous*[^3]. *But rising from the dead he dies no more, death will no longer have dominion over him. For in that he died to sin, he died once; but in that he lives, he lives to God. So let us too reckon ourselves to be dead to sin, but living to God in Christ Jesus*[^4]. That memory might always retain so great a sacrament, the day of the year on which it was done surely ought not to pass like the other nights; but by a most solemn celebrity it returns into the minds of the religious. But for this the Jews killed Christ, that they might destroy his memory from the earth. This is repaid them in the meantime, the eternal punishment excepted, that, his memory being glorified, the world may not forget their crime either; for whoever marvels at the light of this night throughout the whole world, at the same time detests the darkness of the Jews. But let us abominate their crime; yet so great a good, which the Lord granted us out of their evil, let us faithfully hold, and solemnly celebrate.

**2.** Because on this night, dearest brothers, on which we recall the buried Lord, we celebrate vigils, let us watch at that time at which he slept for us. For he, long before announcing his passion through the Prophet: *I slept*, he says, *and rose, because the Lord took me up*[^5]. He called the Lord Father. On the night, then, on which he slept, we watch, that by the death by which he suffered we may live; by his temporal sleeping we celebrate vigils, that, with him watching for us, having risen into the eternal vigil, we may remain untiring. But on this night he also rose, for whose resurrection our expectation watches. *He was handed over for our sins*, and slept: *He rose for our justification*[^6]. Whence on this very one night, on which he slept, we keep our vigil, that in his vigil, secure, we may at some time lack that same sleep; and we await by watching the hour of his waking, lest in that very justification of ours, on account of which he rose, by creeping negligence we sleep, not in body but in heart. Let us watch, then, dearest ones, and pray, lest we enter into temptation.

[^1]: Matt 26:41
[^2]: 2 Cor 11:27
[^3]: 1 Pet 3:18
[^4]: Rom 6:9-11
[^5]: Ps 3:5 (3:6)
[^6]: Rom 4:25
