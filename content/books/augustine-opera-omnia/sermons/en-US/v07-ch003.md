# Sermon 232: In the Days of Easter

**1.** The resurrection of our Lord Jesus Christ has been recited today as well, but from another book of the Gospel, the one according to Luke. For first it was read according to Matthew, yesterday according to Mark, today according to Luke, in the order of the Evangelists, just as his passion was written down by all the Evangelists. But these seven or eight days give the space for the resurrection of the Lord to be recited according to all the Evangelists; the passion, however, since it is read on one day, is not usually read except according to Matthew. I once wished that each year the passion too should be read according to all the Evangelists. It was done; people did not hear what they were used to, and they were disturbed. But whoever loves the letters of God and does not wish to remain forever unlearned, knows all and diligently inquires into all. But *as God has apportioned to each a measure of faith*[^1], so each one makes progress.

**2.** Now let us attend to what we have heard today as it was read; for what I also commended yesterday to your Charity, we heard more plainly today—the unbelief of the disciples—so that we may understand how great a benefit has been bestowed on us by his grace, that we should so believe what we have not seen. He called them, he instructed them, he lived with them on earth, he did such great wonders before their eyes—and he even raised the dead; yet his own flesh was not believed to rise. The women came to the tomb; they did not find the body in the tomb; they heard from the angels that Christ had risen; the women announced it to the men. And what is written? What did you hear? *These things seemed before their eyes like nonsense*[^2]. Great is the unhappiness of the human condition! When Eve spoke what the serpent had said, she was quickly heard. The lying woman was believed, so that we should die; the women telling the truth were not believed, so that we might live. If women were not to be believed, why did Adam believe Eve? If women are to be believed, why did the disciples not believe the holy women? And therefore in this event the kindly dispensation of our Lord is to be considered. For this is what the Lord Jesus Christ did, that the female sex should first announce that he had risen. Because through the female sex man fell, through the female sex man was restored; because a Virgin had borne Christ, a woman announced that he had risen. Through woman death, through woman life. But the disciples did not believe what the women said; they thought them raving, when nevertheless they were announcing the truth.

**3.** See, two others were walking on the road and talking with each other about the things that had happened in Jerusalem, about the wickedness of the Jews, about the death of Christ. They were walking along chatting, grieving as it were for a dead man, not knowing he had risen. He appeared and became a third traveler with them; he joined them in friendly conversation. Their eyes were held, so that they should not recognize him. For their heart needed to be the better instructed; recognition is deferred; he asks them what they were saying among themselves, so that they might confess to him what he himself already knew. And what did you hear? They began to wonder that they were being questioned, as though by one who did not know, about a matter so clear and so manifest. *Are you the only one staying in Jerusalem who does not know the things that have happened there?* But he said: *What things?* *Concerning Jesus of Nazareth, who was a prophet mighty in deeds and words*[^3]. Is this so, O disciples? Was Christ a prophet, the Lord of the prophets? You impose on your judge the name of a herald. They had come down to the words of strangers. What do I mean, the words of strangers? Recall when Jesus himself said to his disciples: *Who do men say that the Son of man is?* They reported the opinions of strangers. *Some say*, they said, *that you are Elijah, others John the Baptist, others Jeremiah, or one of the prophets*[^4]. These were the words of strangers, not of the disciples. See, the disciples had come to those very words. *But who do you say that I am?*[^5] You answered me with the opinions of strangers; I want to hear your faith. Then Peter said, one for all, because there is unity in all: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^6]. Not just anyone, one of the prophets, but *the Son of the living God*, the fulfiller of the prophets, the creator even of the angels. *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*. He heard what it was fitting for him to hear, from this voice and from such a voice: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, for flesh and blood has not revealed it to you, but my Father who is in heaven. And I tell you: You are Peter, and on this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it. To you I will give the keys of the kingdom of heaven; and whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven, and whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in heaven*[^7]. Faith merited to hear this, not the man. For the man himself, what was he, except what the Psalm says: *Every man is a liar*[^8]?

**4.** Finally, immediately after these words, he foretold to them his passion and death. Peter was terrified and said: *Far be it from you, Lord, this shall not happen*. Then the Lord: *Get behind me, Satan*[^9]. Peter Satan? Where are those words: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona*? Surely not blessed Satan? Blessed in what is of God. Satan in what is of man. Finally, the Lord himself explained why he called him Satan: *For you do not savor the things that are of God, but the things that are of man*[^10]. From what was he then blessed? *Because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^11]. From what afterward Satan? *You do not savor the things that are of God*; when you savored them, you were blessed: *but you savor the things that are of men*[^12]. See how the soul of the disciples alternated, as it were by rising and setting; now it stood, now it lay low; now it was lit up, now it was darkened; because from what was of God it was lit up, from what was its own it was darkened. From what was it lit up? *Draw near to him and be enlightened*[^13]. From what was it darkened? *He who speaks a lie speaks of his own*[^14]. The Son of God had spoken, life had spoken, and they were afraid that life might die, when life can in no way die, and the Son of God had come for this very purpose, that he might die; for if he had not come to die, from what would we live?

**5.** From where comes life for us, from where death for him? Attend to this: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^15]. Seek death there. Where? from what? How was the Word? The Word with God, the Word God. If you find flesh and blood there, you find death. So from where comes death for that Word? But for us human beings, set on earth, mortal, corruptible, sinners, from where comes life? There was nothing from which he could have death; we had nothing from which we could have life. He took death from what is ours, that he might give us life from what is his. How did he take death from what is ours? *The Word was made flesh and dwelt among us*[^16]. From here, from us, he took what he might offer for us. But from where comes life for us? *And the life was the light of men*[^17]. He himself is life for us, we are death for him. But what kind of death? By condescension, not by condition. Because he condescended, because he willed it, because he had mercy, he died by his own power. *I have power to lay down my life, and I have power to take it up again*[^18]. Peter did not know this when, hearing of the Lord's death, he was terrified. But see, the Lord had already said that he would die and rise on the third day. What he foretold came to pass, and those who had heard did not believe. *See, it is now the third day since these things were done, and we were hoping that he was the one to redeem Israel*[^19]. You were hoping; have you now despaired? You have fallen from hope. He who walks with you lifts you up. They were disciples, they had heard him, they had lived with him, they had known him as their master, they had been instructed by him, and they could not imitate and have the faith of the robber hanging on the cross.

**6.** But perhaps some of you do not know what I said about the robber, since you do not hear the passion read according to all the Evangelists. For this Evangelist Luke narrated what I am saying. That two robbers were crucified with Christ, Matthew too said this; but that one of those robbers insulted the Lord and that the other believed in Christ, Matthew did not say, but Luke said. Let us recall the faith of the robber, which Christ did not find in his disciples after the resurrection. Christ was hanging on the cross, the robber too was hanging. He in the middle, they at the sides. One insults, the other believes, the one in the middle judges. For the one who was insulting said this: *If you are the Son of God, free yourself*. And the other to him: *Do you not fear God? If we suffer deservedly for our deeds, what has he done?* And turning to him: *Remember me, Lord, when you come into your kingdom*[^20]. Great faith! What could be added to this faith, I do not know. Those who saw Christ raising the dead faltered; he believed in the one whom he saw hanging beside him on the wood. When they faltered, then he believed. What fruit Christ gathered from the dry wood! For what did the Lord say? Let us hear. *Truly I tell you, today you shall be with me in paradise*[^21]. You put yourself off, I acknowledge you. When would the robber have hoped, from robbery to the judge, from the judge to the cross, from the cross to paradise? Finally, considering his own deserts, he did not say: Remember me to free me today. But: *When you come into your kingdom*, then *remember me*. As if to say, even if torments are owed me until you come into your kingdom. And he: Not so; you have invaded the kingdom of heaven, you have used force, you have believed, you have seized it. *Today you shall be with me in paradise*. I do not put you off: to such great faith I today repay what I owe. The robber says: *Remember me when you come into your kingdom*. He believed not only that he would rise, but also that he would reign. To one hanging, crucified, bloody, fixed there, he said: *When you come into your kingdom*. And those others: *We were hoping*. Where the robber found hope, the disciple lost it.

**7.** Then now, beloved, we have come to know a great mystery. Hear. He was walking with them, he is received as a guest, he breaks bread and is recognized. And let us not say that we do not know Christ; we know him if we believe. It is too little to say, we know him if we believe; we have him if we believe. Those men had Christ at table, we have him within, in the soul. It is more to have Christ in the heart than in the house. For our heart is more inward to us than our house is. But now, where ought the faithful one to recognize him? He who is faithful recognizes him; but he who is a catechumen does not know him. But let no one shut the door against him, so that he may not enter.

**8.** Yesterday I warned and admonished your Charity that the resurrection of Christ is in us, if we live well; if our old, evil life dies and the new one daily makes progress. Penitents abound here; when hands are laid on them, a very long line is formed. Pray, penitents. And the penitents go to pray. I examine the penitents, and I find them living badly. How does one repent of what he keeps doing? If he repents, let him not do it. But if he does it, the name is wrong, the offense remains. Some have asked for the place of penance for themselves; some, excommunicated by us, have been put into the place of the penitents. And those who asked for it themselves wish to keep doing what they were doing, and those who, excommunicated by us, have been put into the place of the penitents do not wish to rise from there, as if the place of the penitents were a choice place. What ought to be a place of humility becomes a place of iniquity. To you I say, you who are called penitents and are not, to you I say. *What shall I say to you? Shall I praise you? In this I do not praise you*[^22], but I groan and lament. And what am I to do, having become a worthless song? Be changed, be changed, I beg. The end of life is uncertain. Every man walks along with his own downfall. You put off living well, while you think your life will be long. You think your life long, and do you not fear a sudden death? But suppose it is long: see that it be good. And I look for one penitent and do not find him. How much better a long and good life will be than a long and bad one! No one wants to have or to endure a long bad supper, yet almost everyone wants to have a long bad life. Surely, if it is a great thing that we live, let that great thing itself be good. For what do you want bad, tell me, in all your acts, thoughts, desires? On the land you do not want a bad crop; you do not want a bad one but a good one, a good tree, a good horse, a good slave, a good friend, a good son, a good wife. And why do I mention these great things, when you do not want to have even a bad garment, but a good one? Finally, you do not want even a boot unless it is good; or else give me something you want that is bad, and that you do not want something good. I think you do not want a bad farm, but a good one. Only your own soul do you want to be bad. How have you offended yourself? What have you deserved badly of yourself? Among your good things you do not want anything bad, except only yourself. I think I am saying what I usually say, and some perhaps are doing what they usually do. I did not say it for certain, but perhaps. Let no one slander me, because I have spoken by way of fear rather than of assertion. Before God *I shake out my garments*[^23]. I fear lest it be charged to me because I fear. What do you want? I fulfill my office, I seek your fruit. I want to have joy from your good works, not money. For it is not he who lives well that makes me rich. And yet let him live well, and he does make me rich. My riches are nothing but your hope in Christ. My joy, my consolation, and my relief amid my dangers in these trials is nothing, except your good life. I beseech you, if you have forgotten yourselves, have pity on me.
