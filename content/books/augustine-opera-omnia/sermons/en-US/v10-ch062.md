# Sermon 376: The Sunday in the Octave of Pasch

**1.** Your love heard, when the holy Gospel was read, that our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, after his resurrection, entered to his disciples through closed doors. A great miracle: but you cease to marvel if you think of God. For it would be a marvel if a mere man had done this. Refer it to omnipotence, not to imagination. He entered with the doors closed. I answer you, so that you may know it was true flesh: he showed his scars to be touched. But, you say, just as it is not of bodily nature to enter through a closed door, so it is not of bodily nature to walk upon the waves of the sea. He entered through the closed doors—answer me, give me the solidity of the flesh. He walked upon the waters of the sea—give me too the weight of the flesh. Do you wish to know that this belonged to omnipotence? He gave it to Peter too. He who gave what he willed kept for himself what was his own. For he entered, living, through the closed doors, who by being born did not violate the integrity of his mother. Therefore, brothers, having marveled let us believe, believing let us obey, obeying let us hope for the things promised, if we do the things commanded: because, that we may do the things commanded, he himself helps from whom we hope for the things promised.
