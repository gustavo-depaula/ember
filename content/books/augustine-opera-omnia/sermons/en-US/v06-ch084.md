# Sermon 229/O: A Tractate on the Saturday of the Octave of Holy Easter

**1.** You have heard the confession of the apostle Peter at the Lord's questioning, whose denial you had heard at a maidservant's frightening. For it had been said to the presumptuous one: *You will deny me*[^1]; it was said to the loving one: *Do you love me?*[^2] For, so that the apostle Peter might stumble, he presumed on the strength of his own soul. Now long before the psalm had said: *Those who trust in their own strength*[^3] . . . Peter, then, had become like the one of whom it is sung in the Psalms: *I said in my abundance, I shall not be moved forever*[^4]. In his abundance he had said to Christ: *With you, even unto death*[^5]; in his abundance he had said: *I shall not be moved forever.* Yet the Lord, like a skilled physician, knew better what was happening in the sick man than the sick man himself. Physicians do this in bodily illnesses, which the Lord can do also in the illnesses of souls. For what seems to you, I ask, that a sick man should wait to hear from the physician what is happening in him? For he himself can know the pains he suffers; but whether they are dangerous, and what causes they have, whether he can or cannot escape them, the physician inspects the pulse, and what is happening in the sick man he himself reports to the sick man. When, then, the Lord said to blessed Peter: *You will deny me three times*[^6], he was touching the pulse of his heart. Behold, what the physician foretold came to pass, and what the sick man presumed was found false. For there in the same psalm the Holy Spirit goes on: *I said in my abundance, I shall not be moved forever,* as one presuming on the strength of his own soul. He immediately added: *Lord, in your will you furnished strength to my beauty. You turned away your face, and I became troubled*[^7]. What did he say? What I had, I had from you, and I believed it was from myself. You turned away your face, you took away what you had given; and I became troubled. I found what I was, because you had withdrawn. The Lord, then, in order to make Peter wholesomely humble, abandoned him for a time: yet when he looked back at him, then he wept. For so you have it in the Gospel: after he had denied three times, and what the Lord had foretold was fulfilled, what is written? *The Lord looked back at him, and Peter remembered.* If the Lord had not looked back, Peter would have forgotten it all. *The Lord looked back at him, and Peter remembered, that Jesus had said to him: Before the cock crows, you will deny me three times. And going outside, he wept bitterly*[^8]. Peter, then, needed for the washing away of the sin of denial the baptism of tears, but whence would he have this, unless the Lord gave this too? Therefore the apostle Paul, when he admonished the people how they should deal with certain who held different views, said: *Correcting in gentleness those who hold different views, lest perhaps God give them penance*[^9]. Therefore penance too is the gift of God. Hard earth is the heart of the proud; it does not soften to penance, unless it is watered by the grace of God.

**2.** Now, then, after the Lord's resurrection Peter is questioned: the confession is drawn out, the passion is foretold; he is found in love, he is made firm in virtue. The Lord says to him, now after the resurrection: *Peter, do you love me more than these?*[^10] You who denied me, do you love me? Now enough has been done for you: you see alive the one whom you saw dying, when you feared to die. Behold, I am alive, behold, it is I: why did you fear to die? When you denied me, you certainly did not lose me. Do you love me, then? Because it is I. And he: *Lord, yes, you know that I love you*[^11]. Why do you ask me what you know? You knew it, when you foretold to me that I would deny you. What I did not know in myself, you knew: and what I know, do you not know? For I see in my heart that I love you, but you too see it; for you do not fail to see the present love, you who saw the future fear. And again the Lord knows, and asks; and again, as he asks this very thing, this very thing Peter answers. And the Lord asks a third time, that a threefold confession might blot out the threefold denial. Let us rejoice with the Apostle: *He was dead, and came to life again; he was lost, and is found*[^12].

**3.** He is armed for greater and higher things; it is said to him: *Feed my sheep*[^13]; wherein surely he was to be in peril in the flesh, to be glorified in the spirit. For in feeding the sheep of Christ, how much was he to suffer for the name of Christ! *Feed my sheep, feed my lambs*[^14]. For what are you to offer me, if you love me? The Prince of shepherds appointed a shepherd, that Peter might feed the sheep of Christ, not his own. For certain men, who wished to be the teachers of the Apostles, are called back by the Apostles themselves to a sound mind. For they were the sheep of Christ, and wished to be men's; and some said: *I am Paul's, but I am Apollos's, and I am Cephas's.* There were sheep there who acknowledged the Lord: *But I am Christ's*[^15]. But Paul, knowing that Christ had entrusted his own sheep to the Apostles, not his, repelled from himself such lordship; he confesses that he is not the Lord, that he may be with the Lord. *Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^16] You are his sheep, you do not know whose sheep you are; read the brand with which you are marked. *Feed my sheep.* Why? Because you love me, because you have love for me, I commit my sheep to you; feed them, but remember they are mine. But the leaders of heresies want to make their own the sheep that are Christ's; yet, whether they will or not, they are forced to impose the brand of Christ; to make their own private flock, and to inscribe the Lord's name. But what does the divine Scripture say to such men in the *Song of Songs*? For Christ the bridegroom addresses the bride the Church, and says: *Unless you know yourself, O fair one among women*[^17]. What is it: *Fair one among women*? The catholic one among heresies. Attend to how he threatens. Unless you know yourself, whose you are, what you believe, to whom you belong, how far and wide you are spread, by whose blood you have been redeemed; unless you know yourself, O fair one among women, unless you know yourself, I cast you out, *go forth.* What is it: *go forth*? What John says in his epistle: *They went forth from us, but they were not of us*[^18]. *Go forth in the footsteps of the flocks*; not in the footsteps of the shepherd, but in the footsteps of the flocks; following the footsteps of men, not of Christ. *And feed your kids*; not, like Peter, my sheep. And where are you to feed your kids? *In the tents of the shepherds*[^19]; divided in the tents of the shepherds, not in the tent of the shepherd. *I have other sheep, which are not of this fold; I must bring them too, and they will hear my voice; and there will be one flock, and one shepherd*[^20].

**4.** I announce, then, to your Charity, that yesterday we were not severe in vain; by the sharpness of discipline we have made a gain. One of those four today anathematized the heresy of the Arians and Eunomians, together with Arius and Eunomius themselves, and was made a catholic. And so, the one whom yesterday we showed was to be guarded against, today we show is to be loved. Therefore I commend him to you, whom you may now see rejoicing, not suspect. I commend also that you pray for the rest.
