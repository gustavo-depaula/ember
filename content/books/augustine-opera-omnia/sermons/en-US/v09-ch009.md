# Sermon 308/A: Delivered at Carthage in the Mappalia, That Is, in the Basilica of the Blessed Martyr Cyprian, During the Vigil of His Feast

**1.** We speak to Your Charity in the house of God what the present psalm has prompted. Who is it that says: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ. I will clothe his enemies with confusion; but upon him my sanctification will flourish*[^1]; and what that lamp is which he prepared for his Christ; and who the enemies of his Christ are, whom through that lamp he clothed with confusion; and what the sanctification of him who prepared a lamp for his Christ, which will flourish upon his Christ. For in all these words only that seems plain and open which he here says: *my Christ;* for nothing else is to be understood than Christ our Lord and Savior. Searching out, then, as far as God grants, the depth of meaning here, we find that God the Father says this. So God the Father, that is, the person of God the Father, speaks through the prophet: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ.* But that the Son of God is the same as the Christ of God need not be expounded to Christians. The speaker being found, then, let us see what the lamp is, prepared by God the Father for Christ the Son. The Lord himself says of John the Baptist: *He was a burning and shining lamp, and you wanted to exult for an hour in his light*[^2]. So he called John a lamp, kindled from the fountain of light. That he might bear witness to the truth. For in this blindness men lay, and in this weakness of the inner eye, so that the sun of righteousness was sought through a lamp. For if anyone had a clean eye of the heart, he would gaze upon it first, and would not seek a lamp for its testimony. For when he had said of that lamp: *You wanted to exult for an hour in his light, but I,* he says, *have a testimony greater than John*[^3]. So a lamp was kindled for the weak in the night. And how did he kindle it? The Father says to Christ his Son concerning John: *Behold, I send my messenger before you, who will prepare the way before your face*[^4]. He prepared a lamp for his Christ.

**2.** How did he clothe his enemies with confusion through that very lamp? And first see what we said, that the lamp was kindled from the fountain of light. John himself testifies: *But we,* he says, *have received of his fullness*[^5]. Now John was so preeminent in excellence that he was thought not sent before Christ, but to be Christ himself. But he, if he had been a lamp extinguished, and smoking with the reek of pride, when it was sent to him by the Jews, and it was said to him: *Who are you? Are you the Christ, or Elijah, or the Prophet?*[^6] would have said: *I am.* For he had found an occasion for his boasting, with the error of men freely conferring a false honor upon him. Did he labor to persuade them of this, which the earlier ones who questioned were saying? But he was sent, a humble man, to prepare the way for the exalted; hence the friend of the bridegroom, because the servant who knows the Lord. And he said: *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness; prepare the way of the Lord, make straight his paths*[^7]. I am not the Christ, nor Elijah, nor the Prophet. And they: *Who then are you?*[^8] And what did he say? *I am the voice of one crying in the desert: Prepare the way of the Lord.* For Isaiah had already foretold these things; and of whom he foretold them, is there understood. You have read this, he says, in Isaiah, and perhaps, having read it, you did not know of whom it was said: I am the one of whom it was said. But see how greatly he casts himself down, who was so exalted that he was thought to be Christ; see how greatly he casts himself down. *I indeed,* he says, *baptize you in water; but he who comes after me is greater than I*[^9]. And he could be said to be somewhat greater than I. He says he is greater than himself. Say, how much greater? Of whom *I am not,* he says, *worthy to loosen the strap of his sandal*[^10].

**3.** From this now understand the dispensation, why John was sent ahead before Christ. You see how much less he is, and how much greater he confesses him to be, that he says himself unworthy to loosen the strap of his sandal. How great is this one, who says himself unworthy to loosen the strap of this man's sandal? How great is he? where do we seek it? If we seek from John himself how great John is, we do not find it; for he humbles himself, and says nothing of himself, even truly, in a boastful way. How great, then, John is—he who is not worthy to loosen the strap of the sandal of one who was thought a man—from whom shall we hear it? Let us question the Lord himself, and say to him: Lord, John bore great witness of you, and although his great excellence appeared among men, so that he was thought to be Christ, and when it was asked of him who he was, he said he was not the Christ, he said one greater than himself would come, and so much greater that he is not worthy to loosen the strap of his sandal. Like a faithful lamp he spoke of your excellent light. This John says of you. Let us see who he is who says these things of you, how great is he who so humbled himself before you, and confessed you so much greater than himself. Who is he? He says these things of you: do you say something to us of him. Hear the Lord's voice concerning John: *Among those born of women none has arisen greater than John the Baptist*[^11]. But what does he say? *But he who is least in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he*[^12]. He means himself: for God is not arrogant, when he commends his own greatness. What is: *He who is least?* He who is later in age, greater in majesty. For the Lord Christ was born later than John, but in that which he was made for our sake, not in that through which we were made. He who was born after John hears from the Father: not before John, not before David, not before Abraham, but: *Before the morning star I begot you*[^13]. If, then, something was accommodated to our weakness, that the lamp should precede the day, and the lamp was believed concerning the day, how much more is the day to be believed concerning the lamp, because *among those born of women none has arisen greater than John the Baptist*[^14]? A man, then, than whom none greater had been born, when he says himself unworthy to loosen the strap of someone's sandal, what is he, whose sandal-strap a man than whom none was greater is not worthy to loosen? If now John is so great a man that there can be no greater man, whatever is greater than he is more than a man. If now John is so great a man that no man is greater than he, then he who is greater than he is more than a man. And he who is more than a man, yet for the sake of man a man, deservedly the sanctification of the Father flourishes upon him.

**4.** For upon him the Holy Spirit descended in the form of a dove: the flower of holiness in the form of a dove, in a simple and innocent shape, was then shown more fully to John himself, that it might be fulfilled: *And upon him my sanctification will flourish*[^15]. *I,* he says, *did not know him; but he who sent me to baptize in water, he said to me, upon whomever you see the Spirit descending like a dove, and remaining upon him, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit. And I,* he says, *have borne witness to what I saw, that he is the chosen one of God*[^16]. Of whom does he bear witness? Of the one upon whom he saw the sanctification of the Father flourish. From where did he see the Holy Spirit descending? For the Spirit never withdrew from the Son, nor the Son ever from the Spirit, nor the Son ever from the Father, nor the Father from the Son, nor the Spirit from the Son and the Father; but these things are understood in one way by a purified mind, and shown in another way to the eyes. At no time does the Father precede the Son, at no time does the Son follow the Father; at no time, because there there is no time. The Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, the creator of times, one God. There, then, it is not for you to say: The Father is prior, the Son is later; from whom the Father, from him the Son. Ask: From whom is the Father? You surpass in thought the earth, heaven, the angels, things visible, things invisible, the whole creation, and you ask: From whom did the Father begin? Eternal things are not so inquired into; do not ask "from whom," except of what begins. Do not ask "from whom"—from whom begins whatever has begun, and what begins from no one, because it does not begin. But just as the Father does not begin, neither does the Son begin, but the Son is the splendor of the Father. Just so is the splendor of fire, from which the fire; and the splendor of the Father, from which the Father. But from whom is the Father? From eternity to eternity. Therefore the splendor of the Father too is from eternity to eternity; and yet, because it is his splendor, it is his Son, and so it did not begin in time, in that it was begotten of the Father. Who can see this? File your heart, shake off the dust, wash away the stain; let whatever disturbs the inner gaze be cared for and healed, and there will appear what is said and believed before it is seen.

**5.** Yet for now we believe, brothers. What do we believe? That the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit precede one another at no time. Since, then, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit precede one another at no time, yet I could not name the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit unless these names occupied times, and were occupied by their times. The Father is not prior and the Son later, and yet I could not say it except as one prior and another later, and all the syllables occupied their times, and the second syllable in my words could not sound until the first had passed. Times were carried out in my syllables, when I said the things that have no times. So then, my brothers, when that Trinity was shown perceptibly to this flesh, the whole Trinity appeared at the river, where the Lord was baptized by John. For he was baptized, he came up from baptism, the dove descended, and a voice sounded from heaven: *This is my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased*[^17]. The Son in the man, the Spirit in the dove, the Father in the voice. The undivided reality was shown distinctly; if indeed it is to be called a reality, but rather the cause of all realities, if indeed a cause. For what do we say, when we speak of God? And yet we speak, and he allows himself to be spoken of, who is not as he is thought, and cannot be spoken in the way he is thought. But as far as concerns men, brothers, behold, he appeared through a dove, and it was fulfilled: *But upon him my sanctification will flourish*[^18]. For "will flourish" was said, will appear clearly; for nothing in a tree is clearer than the flower, nothing brighter. Come now, we have arrived at the last words of the antiphon, *but upon him my sanctification will flourish.* I recall that I passed over who the enemies are who were confounded by the lamp.

**6.** *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ*[^19]. The Father says of his Son. What lamp? John. Ask the Son himself. *He was a burning and shining lamp*[^20]. *I will clothe his enemies with confusion*[^21]. The open enemies of Christ, who are they, but the Jews? For Christ has hidden enemies. All who live unjustly and impiously are enemies of Christ, even if they are sealed with his name, even if they are called Christians. For to them he will say: *I do not know you;* and they say to him: *Lord, in your name we ate and drank, in your name we did many mighty works*[^22]. What, "we ate and drank in your name"? For they did not boast of their food as a great thing, and on that ground say that they belonged to Christ. There is a certain food which is eaten and drunk, and it is Christ; and by enemies Christ is eaten and drunk. The faithful know the immaculate Lamb on whom they feed; and would that they so feed that they be not debtors to punishment! For as the Apostle says: *Whoever eats and drinks unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself*[^23]. So they are enemies of Christ who prefer to live unjustly than to obey him, and, when it is said that he will come to judge the living and the dead, they fear lest he come. If it were allowed them, they would bring it about that he not come. Because they could not bring it about that he not come, they would bring it about that he not return. The Jews already wanted to do this, that he not return. For the son was sent to those bad tenants, to the bad lessees unwilling to render the rent, and stoning the servants sent to them. Then the householder, the lord of the vineyard, said: *I will send my son;* perhaps they will reverence him. But they thought, saying: *This is the heir, come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours*[^24]. They could not bring it about that he not come from the Father, and they tried to bring it about that he not return to the Father. But to what good? They indeed saw and despised him as mortal, but in him they could kill nothing but death. In the death of Christ, death died. He, rising, ascended to the Father, and is to come. What do you fear? Love, and you will be secure. Do we not pray: *Your kingdom come*[^25]? So, brothers, do we pray, and fear lest we be heard?

**7.** But these, as I had begun to say, are hidden enemies. Let us speak of those open ones, who openly envied, raged, seized, scourged, mocked, crucified, killed, and guarded him when buried. Let us see how they were clothed with confusion through that lamp. When these same enemies saw the miracles in the Lord: *Tell us,* they say, *by what authority you do these things?*[^26] They asked with a hostile mind, so that, if he should confess his authority, they might hold him as guilty of blasphemy, as it were. But just as he did with the coin, when they wanted to slander him—if he said: Let the tribute be rendered to Caesar, as though he had cursed the Jewish people, whom he had made subject and tributary; but if he said: Let it not be rendered, they would accuse him before Caesar's friends and ministers, that he forbade it to be rendered; but he: *Show me,* he said, *the coin: Whose image and inscription does it have? They answered: Caesar's. Render therefore to Caesar what is Caesar's, and what is God's to God*[^27]. This is to say: If Caesar seeks his image on the coin, does God not seek his image in man? So here too the slandering enemies *spoke evil with heart and heart*[^28]. For it would have said only *with heart,* unless those who spoke such things had a double heart, and, as was said a little before, a doubled heart, not a simple one. For see how much it matters. Of the many servants of God it was said that they had one heart: *There was,* it says, *to them one soul and one heart toward God*[^29]. Many simple ones have one heart, one deceitful one has two hearts. So, because they spoke with heart and heart: *Tell us by what authority you do these things?* that is, if you say we honor you, if you say we venerate you, if you say we entreat you. This seems to sound from one heart. But from another—for there was a double one there—if you say we slander you, if you say we will find what we may hold against you, if you say we will find what we may accuse you of. Such are the enemies: but let them be confounded by the lamp; now you will see them confounded. And rightly, because it is the time. We are keeping the lamp-lighting; let the enemies of Christ be confounded through the lamp which the Father prepared for his Christ. *For he was a burning lamp*[^30], the Lord himself says. What, then, did he answer these who said: *Tell us by what authority you do these things? I too will ask one word. Tell me, the baptism of John, from where is it? from heaven, or from men?* But they, troubled among themselves: *If we say, from heaven, he will say to us, why then did you not believe him?*[^31] that is: Why do you ask me by what authority I do these things, when of me that man bore witness whom you questioned? So, *if we say: From heaven; he will say to us: Why did you not believe him? If we say: From men, we fear the people. For all held John as a prophet*[^32]. On this side fearing the people, on that side fearing the truth; here timid, there envious; everywhere blind, they answered: *We do not know*[^33]. The lamp was brought forth, the darkness fled; for, although they were present in body, they fled in heart, by saying that they did not know what they knew. The sign of flight, the fear of the heart; they feared to be stoned by the people, if they said the baptism of John was from men; they feared to be convicted by Christ, if they said the baptism of John was from heaven. They fled confounded. So at the naming of John they feared, troubled they were silent. And he: *Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things*[^34].

**8.** So John the Baptist was prepared as a lamp for Christ our Lord; his slandering, questioning enemies withdrew confounded when the light of the lamp was brought forth; it was fulfilled: *I will clothe his enemies with confusion*[^35]. But we, brothers, both recognizing the Lord through John the Baptist the forerunner, indeed also through the testimony of the Lord himself, of whom he says: *I have a testimony greater than John*[^36], believing in Christ, let us be made the body of him who is the head, so that there may be one Christ, head and body; and when all of us are made one, it will be fulfilled: *But upon him my sanctification will flourish*[^37]. Turned to the Lord.
