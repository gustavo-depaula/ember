# Sermon 229/B: A Tractate on the Lord's Day of Holy Easter

**1.** The Lord has indeed made every day: and not only has he made it, but he also makes it; for he makes every day in this way, when he *makes his sun to rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^1]. This ordinary day, then, common to good and evil alike, must not be thought to be the one signified in the place where we heard: *This is the day that he has made*; for it proclaims a certain day more distinctly, and makes us intent upon a certain day by saying: *This is the day that the Lord has made.* What sort of day is this, where it is said: *Let us exult and rejoice in it*[^2]? What sort, if not good? What sort, if not desirable, lovable, longed-for, delightful, the kind of which holy Jeremiah said: *And the day of men I have not desired, you know*[^3]? Who, then, is this day that the Lord has made? Live well, and you yourselves will be it. For it was not of that day which the rising of the sun begins and its setting closes that the Apostle was speaking when he said: *Let us walk honorably as in the day*[^4]. Where he also says: *For those who are drunk are drunk at night*[^5]. No one sees men drunk at midday; but when it happens, it belongs to the night, not to the day that the Lord has made. For just as there is day in those who live devoutly, holily, and religiously, temperately, justly, soberly; so, on the contrary, in those who live impiously, in luxury, proudly, irreligiously, without doubt night will be the thief of such a night: *The day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night*[^6], for so it is written. But when the Apostle recalled this testimony, he turned to those to whom he had elsewhere said: *You were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord*[^7]; where the day that the Lord has made was made, turning to them, when he had said: *You know, brothers, that the day of the Lord will come like a thief in the night,* he says to them: *But you are not in darkness, that that day should overtake you like a thief. For you are all sons of light and sons of the day; we are not of the night, nor of the darkness*[^8]. This song of ours, then, is a reminder of a good life. When we all say with one voice, with joyful spirit, with concordant heart, *This is the day that the Lord has made,* let us be in harmony with our own sound, lest our tongue speak testimony against us. You who are about to make yourself drunk today say: *This is the day that the Lord has made*; are you not afraid he may answer you: That day the Lord did not make; and that very day is called a good day which luxury and worthlessness make into a most wicked day for themselves.

**2.** Behold the joy, my brothers, joy in your assembly, joy in psalms and hymns, joy in the memory of the passion and resurrection of Christ, joy in the hope of the life to come. If what we hope for produces such great joy, what will it be when we possess it? Behold, on these days, when we hear *Alleluia,* our spirit is in a way changed. Do we not taste, as it were, something of that city on high? If these days produce such great joy in us, what will that day be like, where it is said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*[^9]; when all the saints are gathered into one; when those see each other there who had not known each other; there they recognize each other who had known each other; there they will so be together that a friend will never be lost, an enemy never feared? For behold, we say *Alleluia*; it is good, it is joyful, it is full of joy, of pleasantness, of sweetness. Yet, if we should say it always, we grow weary of it; but when it comes round at a fixed time of the year, with what pleasantness it returns, with what longing it departs! Will joy be there in such a way, and weariness be there too? It will not. Perhaps someone says: And how can it come to pass that it is thus always, and never weariness? If I show you something in this life that cannot involve weariness, you will believe that there everything will be thus. There is weariness in food, weariness in drink, weariness in a spectacle, weariness in this thing and that; but there was never weariness of health. Just as here, then, in this mortality of the flesh, in this frailty, in this wearisome burden of the body, there could never be weariness of health, so there will never be weariness of love, of immortality, of eternity.
