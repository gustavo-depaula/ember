# Sermon 310: On the Feast of Cyprian the Martyr

**1.** May the Holy Spirit teach us in this hour what we ought to say: for we are about to say something concerning the praise of Cyprian the most glorious martyr, whose feast we celebrate today, as you know. This name the Church so frequents—that is, "feasts" (Natales, "birthdays")—that it calls the precious deaths of the martyrs "birthdays." So, I say, the Church frequents this name, that even those who are not in her say it with her. For who today—I will not say in this our city, but indeed throughout all Africa and the regions overseas—not a Christian only, but a pagan, or a Jew, or even a heretic, could be found, who would not say with us the birthday of the martyr Cyprian? What is this, brothers? When he was born, we do not know; and because he suffered today, we celebrate his birthday today. But we would not celebrate that day, even if we knew it. For on that day he contracted original sin: but on this day he conquered all sin. On that day he came forth from the tedious womb of his mother into this light, which entices the eyes of the flesh: but on this day he departed from the most hidden bosom of nature to that light which happily and blessedly illuminates the sight of the mind.

**2.** He governed the Church of Carthage while living, he honored it by dying. There he held the episcopate, there he consummated his martyrdom. For in that place where he laid down the spoils of his flesh, a savage multitude had then gathered, which through hatred of Christ would shed the blood of Cyprian: there today a venerating multitude runs together, which on account of the birthday of Cyprian drinks the blood of Christ. And so much the more sweetly in that place, on account of the birthday of Cyprian, is the blood of Christ drunk, as the more devoutly there, on account of the name of Christ, the blood of Cyprian was shed. Finally, as you know, whoever of you know Carthage, in the same place a table has been built to God; and yet it is called the table of Cyprian, not because Cyprian ever feasted there, but because he was immolated there, and because by his very immolation he prepared this table, not on which he might feed or be fed, but on which a sacrifice may be offered to God, to whom he too was offered. But that that table, which is God's, may also be called Cyprian's, this is the reason; that, as it is now surrounded by attendants, there Cyprian was surrounded by persecutors: where now it is honored by friends praying, there Cyprian was trampled by enemies raging: lastly, where it has been raised up, there he was laid low. *Sing to God, sing a psalm to his name: who has ascended over the setting*[^1]; he himself did these things over the slain.

**3.** But since Carthage had his chair, let Carthage have his memorial; from where would we celebrate his birthday feasts, unless *precious in the sight of the Lord were the death of his saints*[^2]? Into all the earth his sound went out, and to the ends of the world his words. He faithfully taught what he was to do, he bravely did what he had taught. By living justly he came to a precious death, and by dying unjustly he came to a glorious life; and he obtained the triumphal name of martyr, because he carried through, even to blood, the contest for the truth.

**4.** But because he not only said things that were heard, but also wrote things that were read, and came to some places through foreign tongues, but to others through his own letters, and became known to many regions, partly through the fame of his most brave passion, partly through the sweetness of his most pleasant reading: let us eagerly celebrate this day, and so all with one mind entreat, that in the greater Church we may merit to hear and see our common father; about to have both joy from his sermon, and profit from the glory of his passion, through our Lord Jesus Christ. Amen.

[^1]: Ps 68:4 (67:5)
[^2]: Ps 116:15 (115:15)
