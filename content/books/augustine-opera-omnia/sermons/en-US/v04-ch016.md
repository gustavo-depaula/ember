# Sermon 107

**1.** You who fear God, I do not doubt that you hear his word with fear, and do it with cheerfulness; that what he promised, you may now hope for, and afterward receive. We have just now heard the Lord commanding us, Christ Jesus the Son of God. The Truth has commanded us, which neither deceives nor is deceived: let us hear, let us fear, let us beware. What, then, did he command? *I say*, he says, *to you, abstain from all greed* [^1]. What is: *from all greed?* What is: *all?* Why did he add: *all?* For he could say thus: Beware of greed. It mattered to him to add: *all*; and to say: *Beware of all greed*.  
Why he said this—the very occasion, as it were, from which the sermon arose—appears to us in the holy Gospel. For a certain man appealed to him against his brother, who had taken away the whole patrimony, and was not giving his brother his share. So you observe what a good cause this petitioner had. For he was not seeking to seize others' goods, but was seeking his own, left to him by his parents: he was demanding them with the Lord appealed to and judging. He had an unjust brother: but he had found a just judge against his unjust brother. Should he, then, in so good a cause, lose this occasion? Or who would say to his brother, Give your brother his share, if Christ did not say it? Would that judge, whom perhaps the richer, plundering brother was corrupting with a bribe, say it? Wretched, then, and destitute of his father's wealth, having found so great a judge, he approaches, appeals, asks, sets forth his cause most briefly. For what need was there to plead the cause at length, when he was speaking to one who could even see the heart? *Lord*, he says, *tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me* [^2]. The Lord did not say to him, Let your brother come. But neither did he send for him to be present, nor, when he was present, say to the petitioner: Prove what you said. He was asking half the inheritance, asking half on earth: in heaven the Lord was offering the whole. The Lord was giving more than he was asking.

**2.** *Tell my brother to divide the inheritance with me*. A just cause, a brief cause. But let us hear him both judging and teaching. *Man*, he says: *Man*: for you who hold this inheritance for a great thing, what are you but a man? He wanted to make him something more than a man. What did he want to make him more, from whom he wanted to take away greed? What did he want to make him more? I tell you: *I have said, you are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High* [^3]. Behold what he wanted to make him, to number among the gods the one who has no greed. *Man, who set me a divider between you* [^4]? And the apostle Paul, his servant, when he said: *I beseech you, brothers, that you all say the same thing, and that there be no schisms among you* [^5], did not want to be a divider. Indeed he admonished those who ran to his name, and divided Christ: *Each of you says: I am of Paul, I of Apollos, I of Cephas, I of Christ. Is Christ divided? Was Paul crucified for you? or were you baptized in the name of Paul* [^6]? See, then, how wicked are the men who want to divide him who did not want to be a divider. *Who*, he says, *set me a divider between you?*

**3.** You asked a favor: hear counsel. *I say to you, beware of all greed* [^7]. Perhaps, he says, you would call him greedy and covetous, if he sought another's goods: but I say, do not covetously and greedily seek even your own. This is, *all. Beware*, he says, *of all greed*. A great weight. If perhaps this weight is laid upon the weak; let him be asked, who lays it on, to deign to give strength. For it is not to be held lightly, my brothers, when our Lord, our Redeemer, our Savior, who died for us, who gave his blood as a price to redeem us, our advocate and judge; it is no light thing, when he says: *Beware*. He knows how much evil it is: we do not know; let us believe him. *Beware*, he says. Of what? from what? *Of all greed*. I keep my own, I take not another's. *Beware of all greed*. Not only is he greedy who seizes another's goods; but he too is greedy who covetously keeps his own. But if he is so blamed who covetously keeps his own; how is he condemned who seizes another's? *Beware*, he says, *of all greed: for a man's life does not consist in abundance from the things which he has there* [^8]. He who stores up many things, how much does he take from them to live on? When he has taken from them, and in a way set apart in thought what suffices for him to live on, let him see for whom the rest remains: lest perhaps, while you keep what you may live on, you gather what you may die by. Behold Christ, behold truth, behold severity. *Beware*, says truth: *Beware*, says severity. If you do not love truth, fear severity. *A man's life does not consist in abundance from the things he has*. Believe him, he does not deceive you. Do you say the contrary? Rather, a man's life does consist in abundance from the things he has. He does not deceive you: you deceive yourself.

**4.** From this occasion, then, since that petitioner was seeking his own share, and did not desire to invade another's, arose this saying of the Lord, that he should not say: *Beware* of greed, but should add, *of all greed*. That was too little: he gives another example, about a certain rich man, whose region had yielded well. *There was*, he says, *a rich man, whose region had yielded well* [^9]. What is, *had yielded?* The region he possessed had brought forth great fruits. How great fruits? That he found nowhere to put them: he was suddenly made cramped through abundance, the old greedy man. For how many years had now passed, and yet those barns had sufficed? So much, then, was produced that the places that used to suffice did not suffice. And the wretched man was seeking counsel, not how to dole out what had been produced in excess, but how to keep it: and by thinking he found counsel. He seemed wise to himself, by finding counsel. He thought prudently, he saw wisely. What did he see wisely? *I will pull down*, he says, *my old barns, and make new, larger ones, and fill them: and I will say to my soul* [^10]. What do you say to your soul? *Soul, you have many goods laid up for very many years, rest, eat, drink, feast* [^11]. This the wise finder of counsel said to his soul.

**5.** *And God to him*: who does not disdain to speak even with fools. Perhaps one of you says: And how did God speak with a fool? O my brothers, with how many fools he speaks here, when the Gospel is recited! When it is read, are not those who hear and do not do it fools? What, then, does the Lord say? Because that man again seemed wise to himself in finding counsel: *Fool*, he says; *Fool*, who seem wise to yourself: *Fool*, who said to your soul: *You have many goods laid up for very many years: today your soul is required of you* [^12]. The one to whom you said: *You have many goods*; today it is required, and it has no good. Let it despise these goods, and be itself good; so that, when it is required, it may depart secure. For what is more unjust than a man, who wants to have many goods, and does not want himself to be good? You are unworthy to have, who do not want to be what you want to have. For do you want to have a bad farm? Surely not, but a good one. A bad wife? No, but a good one. In short, a bad little cottage? Or even a bad boot? Why a bad soul alone? He did not here say to this fool, thinking vain things, building barns, and not seeing the bellies of the poor; he did not say to him: Today your soul will be snatched to hell: he said none of these things; but, *it is required of you*. I do not tell you where your soul is to go: yet from here, where you keep so much for it, willy-nilly, it is to migrate. Behold, fool, you thought to fill new, larger barns, as though there were nothing to be done with it.

**6.** But perhaps that man was not yet a Christian. Let us hear, brothers, we to whom, as believers, the Gospel is recited, by whom the one who said those things is adored, whose sign is carried by us on the forehead, and held in the heart. For it matters very much where a man has the sign of Christ, whether on the forehead, or both on the forehead and in the heart. You have heard what the holy prophet Ezekiel was saying today, how, before God sent the exterminator of the wicked people, he first sent the marker, and said to him: *Go, and put a mark on the foreheads of those who groan and mourn over the sins of my people, which are done in the midst of them* [^13]. He did not say, *which are done* outside them; but, *in the midst of them*. Yet they groan and mourn: and therefore they are marked on the forehead; on the forehead of the inner man, not the outer. For there is a forehead in the face, there is a forehead in the conscience. Indeed, sometimes when the inner forehead is struck, the outer reddens: either it reddens with shame, or it pales with fear. There is, then, a forehead of the inner man. There those were marked, lest they be laid waste: because, although they did not correct the sins that were done in the midst of them; yet they grieved, and by that very grief they separated themselves; and, separated unto God, they were mingled to the eyes of men. They are marked secretly, they are not harmed openly. Afterward the destroyer is sent, and it is said to him: *Go, lay waste, spare not the lesser, the greater, males, females: but to those who have the mark on the forehead, do not draw near* [^14]. How great security has been given you, my brothers, who in this people groan and mourn the iniquities that are done in the midst of you, and do them not?

**7.** But that you may not do iniquities: *Beware of all greed* [^15]. I tell you more broadly, what is *all greed*. In lust he is greedy, for whom his own wife does not suffice. And idolatry itself has been called greed [^16]; because even in divinity itself he is greedy, for whom the one true God does not suffice. What soul makes for itself many gods, except a greedy soul? What soul makes for itself false martyrs, except a greedy soul? *Beware of all greed*. Behold, you love your own, and you boast, because you do not seek another's: see what evil you do by not hearing Christ saying: *Beware of all greed*. Behold, you love your own, you take not another's: from labor you have it, from justice you have it: you were left an heir, he bestowed it on you whom you deserved well of: you sailed, you were in peril, you committed no fraud, you swore no lie, you acquired what God willed: and you keep it covetously, as though with a good conscience, because you have nothing from evil, and seek not another's. If you do not hear him who said: *Beware of all greed*, hear how much evil you are going to do on account of your own. Behold, it happens, for example, that you become a judge. You are not corrupted, because you seek not another's: no one gives you a bribe, and says: Judge against my adversary. Far be it; a man who seek not another's, when can this be persuaded to you? See what evil you are going to do on account of your own. He who wants you to judge badly, and to pass sentence in his favor against his adversary, is perhaps a powerful man, and can bring a false charge against you, so that you lose your own. You attend to his power, you think on it, you think on your own goods which you keep, which you love: not those you possessed, but those to which you have wickedly clung. You attend to your birdlime, on account of which you have not the wings of virtue to free yourself: and you say within yourself, I offend this man, he can do much for the moment; he will lay evil charges about me, and I am proscribed, and I lose what I have. You are going to judge badly, not when you seek another's, but when you keep your own.

**8.** Give me a man who has heard Christ, give me a man who has heard with fear: *Beware of all greed*: and let him not say to me, I am a poor man, a commoner, of the middle sort, one of the herd; when do I hope to be a judge? I do not fear that temptation, whose peril you have set before my eyes. Behold, I tell the poor man too what he ought to fear. A rich and powerful man calls you to give false witness for him. What will you do now? Tell me. You have a good little store: you labored, you acquired, you kept. He demands: Give false witness for me, and I give you so much and so much. You who seek not another's: Far be it from me, you say: I seek not what God did not want to give me, I take it not; depart from me. You do not want to take what I give? what you have, I take. Behold, now prove yourself, now question yourself. Why do you attend to me? Attend to yourself within, see yourself within, examine yourself within; sit by yourself, and set yourself before yourself, and stretch yourself on the rack of God's precept, and torture yourself with fear, and do not caress yourself: answer yourself. Behold, if anyone should threaten this, what will you do? I take from you what you acquired with so great labor, unless you give false witness for me. Give that man: *Beware of all greed*. O my servant, he will say to you, whom I redeemed and made free, whom from a servant I adopted as a brother, whom in my body I set as a member, hear me: Let him take what you acquired, me he will not take from you. Lest you perish, do you keep your own? Did I not tell you: *Beware of all greed?*

**9.** Behold, you are troubled, behold, you waver: your heart is shaken, as a ship by storms. Christ sleeps: wake the sleeper, and you will not suffer the raging storm. Wake him who wanted to have nothing here, and you have everything; who came even to the cross for you, whose bones, as he hung naked, were counted by the mockers [^17]: and beware of all greed. Greed of money is too little: beware the greed of life. A dreadful greed, a fearful greed. Sometimes a man despises what he has, and says: I do not give false witness. I do not give it, you say to me? I take what you have. Take what I have: you do not take what I have within. For he had not remained poor who said: *The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away; as it pleased the Lord, so it was done; blessed therefore be the name of the Lord. Naked I came forth from my mother's womb, naked I shall return to the earth* [^18]. Naked outside, within clothed. Naked outside of rags, and rotten rags; within clothed. With what? *Let your priests be clothed with righteousness* [^19]. But what if he should say, when you have despised what you possess, what if he should say: I kill you? Answer him, if you have heard Christ: You will kill me? Better you kill my flesh, than I by a false tongue my soul. What will you do to me? You will kill the flesh: the soul goes forth free, at the end of the age to receive again even the flesh it despised. What, then, will you do to me? But if I give false witness for you, by my tongue I kill myself; and not in the flesh do I kill myself: *For the mouth that lies kills the soul* [^20]. Perhaps you do not say this. Why do you not say it? You want to live: do you want to live more than God has appointed? Surely you beware of all greed? God willed you to live up to this point, to which this man approached you. Perhaps he is going to kill you, to make you a martyr. Do not have a greed of living; and do you not have an eternity of dying? You see that everywhere that greed, when we want more than is needful, makes us sin? Let us beware of all greed, if we want to enjoy eternal wisdom.
