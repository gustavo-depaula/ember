# Sermon 228: On the Day of Easter

**1.** After the labor of the past night, since, although the spirit is willing, yet the flesh is weak[^1], I ought not to hold you long with a sermon, and yet I owe you a sermon. These days, on which after the passion of our Lord we sing to God *Alleluia*, we keep as festal in joy up to Pentecost, when the promised Holy Spirit was sent from heaven. Of these days, the seven or eight which are now kept are assigned to the sacraments of the infants. Those who a little before were called Competentes are now called Infants. They were called Competentes, because, by asking, they knocked at the maternal bowels, that they might be born: they are called Infants, because they have now been born to Christ, who before had been born to the world. In them is renewed what in you ought to be made firm: and you who are now faithful, offer them not examples by which they may perish, but by which they may advance. For those just born observe how you, born long ago, live. This is what those do who are born also according to Adam: first they are little ones; afterward, when they have begun to perceive the manners of their elders, they attend to what they may imitate. And since where the elder leads, the younger follows; it is to be desired that the elder go by a good way, lest, by following, both the elder and the younger perish. And so you, brothers, who in a certain manner are now, by the age of your regeneration, parents, I address you, and exhort you, so to live that you may rejoice, not perish, with those who imitate you. The just-born attends to some faithful drunkard; I fear lest he say to himself: Why is that one faithful, and drinks so much? He attends to some usurer, a sad giver, a fierce exactor, and says to himself: I will do it too. It is answered him: You are now faithful, do not do it; you are baptized, you are reborn, your hope is changed, let your manners be changed. And he: Why are this one and that one faithful? I do not wish to say other things; for who could recall them all? Therefore, my brothers, when you live badly, who are now faithful, you will render a bad account to God both of yourselves and of these.

**2.** Now I address them, that they may be grains on the threshing floor, that they may not follow the chaff which is carried about by the wind, with which they would perish; but may remain by the weight of love, that they may come to the kingdom of immortality. You, then, brothers, you sons, you new shoots of mother Church, I beseech you by that which you have received, to attend to him who called you, who loved you, who sought you when lost, who enlightened you when found, that you may not follow the ways of the lost, in which the name of the faithful errs: for it is not asked what they are called, but whether they are in harmony with their name. If he is born, where is the new life? If he is faithful, where is faith? I hear the name, let me recognize the reality too. Choose for yourselves those whom you may imitate, fearing God, entering the church of God with fear, hearing the word of God diligently, retaining it in memory, ruminating on it in thought, fulfilling it in deeds; choose those to imitate. Let not your heart say: And where shall we find such? Be such, and you will find such. Everything like clings to like: if you live as one lost, none but a lost one will join himself to you. Begin to live well, and you will see how many companions surround you, of how great a brotherhood you may be glad. Finally, do you not find what you may imitate? Be what another may imitate.

**3.** We owe today, at the altar of God, a sermon to the infants concerning the sacrament of the altar. We have treated to them of the sacrament of the Creed, which they ought to believe: we have treated of the sacrament of the Lord's Prayer, how they should ask; and of the sacrament of the font and of baptism. All these they have both heard disputed, and received handed over: but of the sacrament of the holy altar, which today they have seen, they have heard nothing as yet; today a sermon on this matter is owed them. Therefore this sermon ought to be brief, both on account of our labor, and on account of their edification.

[^1]: See Matt 26:41
