# Sermon 306/A: On the Feast of the Massa Candida

**1.** We are celebrating the solemn day of the blessed and many martyrs, that is, of the Massa Candida. *Blessed,* because *poor in spirit;* blessed, because *meek;* blessed, because *they mourn;* blessed, because *they hunger and thirst for righteousness;* blessed, because *merciful;* blessed, because *pure in heart;* blessed, because *they have suffered persecution*[^1], not in vain, not having a bad cause, but *for the sake of righteousness*[^2]. For it is the cause, not the punishment, that makes a martyr of Christ. He bears the punishment secure who prudently chooses his cause, so that what he suffers is for the sake of righteousness; lest he both suffer here, and be led from tribulations to the most wretched tribulations. Their soul was patient before the Lord, it waited for the Lord; it did not hasten to receive the reward. For this is to wait for the Lord: that you receive it when he has willed to give it. For he will give it: for either the truth does not deceive, or the Almighty will lack the means to give what he has promised, or the Eternal will fear some successor. Therefore he will give: only let our soul wait for the Lord. So, then, the soul of the martyrs waited, one soul of many; not whitened on the outside, but white within. Their soul, then, waited for the Lord: and the Lord was their helper and their protector. In their sufferings they hoped and endured: hoping for eternal things, they endured the passing. And when they have come, what will they receive? *They will be intoxicated by the abundance of your house, and you will give them drink from the torrent of your delight*[^3]. Let no one here think of the pleasure of the body: there is another pleasure, invisible, from an invisible fountain. Then mark what follows. As though you were asking from where that future pleasure will come, he added and said: *For with you is the fountain of life*[^4]. See the life that does not die, see the health that does not sicken. For *vain is the salvation of man*[^5]. There is certainly nothing in human affairs more precious than this health: and yet it is vain. How then are the rest to be held here? What are a man's riches, what are a man's delights, what are a man's powers, if the salvation of man is vain? Therefore, brothers, let us desire that salvation, let us ask for it with all our prayers, let us strive to come to it by our conduct; let us seek it by believing it, by hoping for it, by loving it. But if he grants this present health, let us give thanks. For surely it was to this health that the gift made to the ten lepers pertained; to this health it pertained that ten lepers were cleansed by the Lord Christ; and yet the grateful one is praised, the ungrateful are accused. So when we receive this health too from him, let us give thanks, and if need be, for his sake let us despise even what he has given, so that we may come to greater things. May it be with you, may it dwell in your hearts, may it work in your thoughts, may whatever truth you hear flourish in your good conduct.

[^1]: See Matt 5:3-10
[^2]: Matt 5:10
[^3]: Ps 36:8 (35:9)
[^4]: Ps 36:9 (35:10)
[^5]: Ps 60:11 (59:13)
