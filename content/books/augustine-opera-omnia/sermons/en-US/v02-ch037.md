# Sermon 49

**1** We have heard several holy readings as they were recited, and of these we ought to say what the Lord has deigned to grant. But every hearer of the readings remembers more what was read most recently, and expects something to be said of it by the treater of the word. Since, then, the holy Gospel was recited last, I do not doubt that your Charity expects to hear something of that vineyard, and the hired men, and the wage of a denarius. But I remember what I promised last Sunday. For when I wished to expound something of the holy prophet that had been read—and there had been read that, when a man asked with what sacrifices he might appease God, it was answered that God seeks nothing of him except to do judgment and justice and love mercy and be ready to walk with the Lord his God—I treated, as far as I could, of judgment, and the sermon was so prolonged that there remained no span of time in which I could discourse of the rest. Whence I promised that today I would speak of justice. But you who expected me to speak of the Gospel, do not think yourselves cheated. For the work in that vineyard is itself justice.

**2** Think, then, that you have been hired. Those who came as boys, let them think they were brought in at the first hour; those as youths, at the third; those as young men, at the sixth; those more mature, at the ninth; those decrepit, at the eleventh. Do not make excuses about the time. Hear the work you are to do, await the wage securely. And if you attend to what your Lord is like, do not be envious if the wage is equal. What the work is you know, but I will recall it. Hear what you know, and do what you hear. We said the work of God is justice. But the Lord Jesus, when asked what the work of God was, answered: *This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent*[^1]. Our devout Lord could have said: Justice is the work of God. Have we then, hired men, dared to presume anything against the head of the household? If justice is the work of God, as I said, how will it be the work of God, which the Lord said, to believe in him, unless to believe in him is itself justice? "But see," you say, "from the Lord we heard: *This is the work of God, that you believe in him*. From you we heard that the work of God is justice. Prove to us that to believe in Christ is this justice." Does it seem to you—since I am already answering one who asks and demands what is just—does it seem to you that to believe in Christ is not justice? What is it, then? Put a name on this work. Without doubt, if you consider well what you have heard, you will answer me: "This is called faith. To believe in Christ is called faith." I accept what you say, to believe in Christ is called faith. Hear too another place of Scripture: The just man lives by faith. Do justice, believe: *The just man lives by faith*[^2]. It is difficult for one to live badly who believes well. Believe with your whole heart, believe not limping, not hesitating, not arguing against faith itself by human suspicions. Faith [fides] is named from this, that what is said is done [fit]. Two syllables sound when "fides" is said. The first syllable is from "is done," the second from "what is said." I ask you, then, whether you believe? You say: "I believe." Do what you say, and it is faith. For I can hear the voice of one answering, I cannot see the heart of one believing. But did I hire you to the vineyard, I who cannot see the heart? Neither do I hire, nor do I impose the work, nor do I prepare the denarius as wage. I am your fellow worker. According to the strength which he deigns to grant, I labor in the vineyard. But with what mind I labor, he who hired me sees. *For to me*, says the Apostle, *it is a very small thing that I be judged by you*[^3]. And you can hear my voice, you cannot see my heart. Let us all set our heart before God to be seen, and do the work from the soul. Let us not offend the one who hires, that with a free brow we may receive the wage.

**3** And we too, dearly beloved, will see one another's hearts, but afterward. But now we still carry about the darkness of this mortality, and walk by the lamp of Scripture, as the apostle Peter says: *We have a more sure prophetic word, to which you do well to attend as to a lamp in a dark place, until the day dawns, and the morning star rises in your hearts*[^4]. Accordingly, dearly beloved, on account of that very faith by which we believe in God, in comparison with the unbelievers we are day. In unbelief we were night together with them, now light, the Apostle saying: *You were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^5]. Darkness in you, light in the Lord. Likewise in another place: *For you are all sons of light, and sons of day; we are not of the night, nor of the darkness. As in the day let us walk honorably*[^6]. Day, then, in comparison with the unbelievers. But in comparison with that day, when the dead will rise and this corruptible will put on incorruption and this mortal will put on immortality, we are still night. To us, as if already in the day, the apostle John says: *Beloved, we are children of God*[^7]. And yet, because it is still night, what follows? *And it does not yet appear what we shall be. We know that when he appears we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^8]. But this is the wage, not the work. *We shall see him as he is* is itself the wage. Then it will be day, than which there can be none clearer. Now, then, in this day already let us walk honorably; in this night still let us not judge one another. For see that the apostle Paul himself, who said, *As in the day let us walk honorably*, does not clash or sound dissonant from his fellow apostle Peter, who said: *To which you do well to attend*—namely, the divine word—*as to a lamp in a dark place, until the day dawns, and the morning star rises in your hearts*.

**4** See the apostle Paul saying this very thing: *And so do not judge anything before the time*[^9]. And when will it be the time? *Until the Lord comes, and enlightens the hidden things of darkness, and makes manifest the thoughts of the heart, and then there will be praise to each one from God*[^10]. What is *before the time*, except before you see one another's hearts? Attend, whether this is what I said. Hear for a little all the words of that sentence. *Do not judge anything before the time.* And when will it be the time? *Until the Lord comes and enlightens the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the thoughts of the heart, and then there will be praise to each one from God.* How will the darkness reproach you, when by the light you will be praised? Then hearts will be open, but now they lie hidden. Someone or other is suspected as if an enemy, and perhaps he is a friend. Another seems as if a friend, and is perhaps a hidden enemy. O darkness! He rages and loves; he flatters and hates. If I judge by their voices, avoiding the calm I run upon the rock: I flee the friend, I cling to the enemy. This the hidden heart did. There it must be believed, there within where it lies hidden, where it is hidden. To cultivate this you were hired. There, by believing, work together, where your fellow worker does not see you, but your Lord sees you. *The just man lives by faith*[^11]. Do this.

**5** Now of judgment I discoursed last Sunday, that you should judge yourself, and when you find yourself perverse, not flatter yourself but correct yourself, and become upright, and the upright God may please you. For the upright God does not please the perverse. Do you want the upright to please you? Be upright. Judge yourself, do not spare yourself. What in yourself deservedly displeases you, chastise, amend, correct. Let the holy Scripture be to you as a mirror. This mirror has a splendor not lying, a splendor not flattering, loving no one's person. You are handsome, you see yourself handsome there; you are foul, you see yourself foul there. But when, foul, you have approached and seen yourself foul there, do not accuse the mirror. Return to yourself; the mirror does not deceive you, do not you deceive yourself. Judge of yourself, be saddened over your foulness, that when you go away and depart sad, foul, corrected, you may be able to return handsome. When, then, you have judged yourself without flattery, judge also your neighbor with love. For there is what you judge, that which you see. For it can be that you see him evil whence you are defiled; it can be that your neighbor himself confesses his evil to you, and betrays to a friend what he had covered in his soul. As you see, judge. What you do not see, leave to God. But when you judge, love the man, hate the vice. Do not on account of the man love the vice, nor on account of the vice hate the man. The man is your neighbor; the vice is the enemy of your neighbor. Then you love your friend, if you hate what harms your friend. If you believe, you do it, because *the just man lives by faith*.

**6** What abounds in human affairs, this I say. Sometimes to your dearest friend someone is an enemy, who was the friend of both. Of three friends two begin to be enemies between themselves; what shall the one in the middle do who remained? He wants, exacts, demands of you that you hate with him the one he has begun to hate, and says these words to you: "You are not my friend, you who are the friend of my enemy." What word this man speaks to you, the same the other speaks to you. For you were three. You were three, two began to be discordant, you remained. If you join yourself to this one, you will have that one for an enemy; if to that one, this one; if to both, both will murmur. See the temptation; see the thorns in the vineyard to which we were hired. You expect perhaps to hear from me what you should do. Remain the friend of both. Let those who are discordant between themselves be concordant through you. If you hear any evils from the one about the other, do not betray them to the other, lest perhaps they later be friends who are now enemies, and their betrayers be betrayed to them. But I said this on account of men, not on account of the eyes of him who hired us. See, no one betrays you. God who sees, who judges you. You heard a word from one angry, from one grieving, from one inflamed. Let it die in you. Why is it betrayed, why brought forth? For if it is in you, it will not burst you. Speak indeed to your friend, who wants to make you the enemy of his friend; address and, as it were, treat a sick soul with the gentleness of medicine. Say to him: "Why do you want me to be his enemy?" He will answer: "Because he is my enemy." "Do you want me, then, to be the enemy of your enemy? I ought to be the enemy of your vice. This one, to whom you want to make me an enemy, is a man. There is another enemy of yours, to whom I ought to be an enemy, if I am your friend." He will answer: "Who is my other enemy?" "Your vice." He will answer: "What is my vice?" "The hatred by which you hate your friend." Be, then, like the physician. The physician does not love the sick man if he does not hate the sickness. To free the sick man, he persecutes the fever. Do not love the vices of your friends, if you love your friends.

**7** But I who say this, do you think I myself do what I say? My brothers, I do, if I do it first in myself. But I do it in myself, if I receive it from the Lord and do it. I hate my vices, I offer my heart to be healed by my physician. I persecute them as much as I can, I groan over them, I confess that they are in me, and see, I accuse myself. You who reproached me, correct yourself. This is justice, lest it be said to us: *You see the speck in your brother's eye, and do not see the beam in your own eye? Hypocrite, first cast out the beam from your own eye, and then you will see to cast out the speck from your brother's eye*[^12]. Anger is a speck; hatred is a beam. But nourish the speck and it becomes a beam. Anger grown old becomes hatred, a speck nourished becomes a beam. That the speck, then, may not become a beam, *let not the sun go down upon your anger*[^13]. Do you see, do you feel yourself livid with hatred, and do you reproach one who is angry? Take away hatred, and you rightly reproach anger. The speck is in his eye, the beam in yours. For if you hate, how do you see what to censure? The beam is in your eye. Why is the beam in your eye? Because you despised the speck born there. With it you slept, with it you rose. You cultivated it in yourself, you watered it with false suspicions, by believing the words of flatterers and of those bringing you evil words about a friend, you nourished the speck, you did not pluck it out. By your diligence you made a beam. Take the beam from your eye, do not hate your brother. Do you shudder, or do you not shudder? I say to you: Do not hate, and you are secure. And you answer me and say to me: "What is it to hate? And what evil is it that a man hates his enemy?" You hate your brother. But if you despise hatred, hear what you do not attend to: *He who hates his brother is a murderer*[^14]. He who hates is a murderer. You have not prepared poison. Can you now say: "What is it to me, to be a murderer?" *He who hates is a murderer.* You have not prepared poison. You have not gone forth with a sword to strike your enemy. You have not prepared a minister of the crime, not a place, not a time. Last of all, you have not done the very crime. You only hated, and you killed yourself before him. Learn, then, justice, that you hate nothing but vices, and love men. If you hold this, and do this justice, that you prefer even vicious men to be healed rather than condemned, you have done a good work in the vineyard. But exercise yourselves in this, my brothers.

**8** See, after the sermon the dismissal of the catechumens takes place. The faithful will remain. We will come to the place of prayer. You know to where we are about to approach. What will we first say to God? *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^15]. Act so that you forgive, act. For you will come to those words of the prayer. How will you say them, how will you not say them? Finally I ask: Will you say them, or will you not say them? You hate, and you say it? You will answer me: "Then I do not say it." You pray, and do not say it? You hate, and say it? You pray and do not say it? Come, answer quickly. So if you say it, you lie. If you do not say it, you merit nothing. Watch yourself, attend to yourself. Now you are about to pray, forgive from the whole heart. You want to quarrel with your enemy, first quarrel with your heart. Quarrel, I say, quarrel with your heart. Say to your heart: Do not hate. But that heart of yours, your soul, still hates. Say to your soul: Do not hate. "How will I pray, how will I say: *Forgive us our debts?* I can indeed say this, but what follows, how will I say it? *As we too.*" What? *As we too forgive.* Where is faith? Do what you say: *As we too.*

**9** But your soul does not want to forgive and is saddened, because you say to it: "Do not hate." Answer it: *Why are you sad, my soul, and why do you disturb me? Why do you disturb me?* or: *Why are you sad?* Do not hate, lest you destroy me. *Why do you disturb me? Hope in God*[^16]. You are sick, you gasp, you are wounded with sickness. You cannot take hatred from yourself: *Hope in God*, he is the physician. For you he hung on the wood, and is not yet avenged. Why do you want to be avenged? For you hated for this reason, that you might be avenged. See your Lord hanging, see him hanging and, as it were, from the wood as a tribunal commanding you. See him hanging, and for you, sick, making medicine from his own blood. See him hanging. Do you want to be avenged? Do you want to be avenged? See him hanging, hear him praying: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^17].

**10** "But he could do this," you say to me, "I cannot. For I am a man, he is God. So, that man God." God, then, why a man, if a man is not corrected? But see, I speak to you. O man, it is too much for you to imitate your Lord; attend to Stephen, your fellow servant. Surely Stephen, the holy one, was a man. Or was he God and man? Plainly he was a man. He was what you are. But what he did, only by the gift of him whom you too ask. Yet see what he did. He spoke to the Jews, he raged and he loved. I ought to show both, because I said: he raged; I said: and he loved. I ought to demonstrate both, both raging and loving. Hear him raging: *Stiff-necked.* These are the words of holy Stephen, when he addressed the Jews: *Stiff-necked, and uncircumcised in heart and ears, you have always resisted the Holy Spirit. Which of the Prophets did your fathers not kill?*[^18] You have heard him raging. I owe the other, hear him loving too. They, made angry, and burning the more grievously and rendering evil for good, ran to stones, began to stone the servant of God. Here prove, holy Stephen, your love. Here, here let us see you, here let us behold, here let us discern the conqueror of the devil and the one who triumphs. We heard you raging against the silent, let us see whether you love the raging. You raged against the silent, let us see whether you love those who stone you. For if you hated and could hate, now is the time when you are stoned. Then most of all you ought to hate. Let us see whether you render hardness of heart to the hard stones, to the stones that stone you. For the stones cast stones, the hard the hard. Those who received the law on stone cast stones.

**11** Let us see, dearly beloved, let us see, let us behold a great spectacle. Let us behold it, even to be set forth for tomorrow. Let us see. See, Stephen is stoned. Let him be set as if before our eyes. Come now, member of Christ, come now, athlete of Christ, look upon him who hung on the wood for you. He was being crucified, you were being stoned. He said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^19]. What you say, let me hear. Let me see you, lest perhaps I may be able to imitate even you. First the blessed Stephen, standing, prayed for himself, and said: *Lord Jesus, receive my spirit*[^20]. Having said this, he bent his knee, and with knee bent said: *Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge. Having said this, he fell asleep*[^21]. O happy sleep, and true rest! See what it is to rest, to pray for one's enemies. But for a little, I beg you, holy Stephen, expound to me this—I do not know what it is—why for yourself you prayed standing, and for your enemies you bent the knee. He will answer perhaps what we understand: "For myself I prayed standing, because for myself, who served God rightly, I did not labor in praying and obtaining. For myself I did not labor." He who prays for a just man does not labor. Therefore standing he prayed for himself. It came to praying for the Jews, for the killers of Christ, for the killers of the saints, for those who stoned him; he attended to how exceeding and great was their impiety, which could hardly be forgiven, and he bent the knee. Fix your knee in this vineyard, strong worker. Fix, I say, your knee in the work of this vineyard, most strong worker. Great is your work, excellent and much to be praised. Very deep you dug, who stripped the hatred of enemies from your heart. Let us turn to the Lord.
