# Sermon 388: On Giving Alms

**1.** This reading, brothers, which we have just heard from the holy Gospel, exhorts us to give alms; and it so exhorts that in his judgment the Lord reckons these alone, when done, to those on the right, and these alone, when not done, to those on the left. Not that the other deeds of men, whether good or evil, do not come into judgment; for it is written that all things shall come to judgment: and yet our Lord Jesus Christ, foretelling to us that his judgment is to come, chose nothing to admonish us with except alms alone. Will it not be said to those on the right: Because you lived chastely, because you did not seize the goods of others, because you confessed my faith even to blood? For all these good deeds must be honored in the judgment of Jesus Christ. Again, do you think it will not be said to those on the left: Because you were shameless, because you fornicated, because you seized the goods of others, because by your pride and evil ways you made my name blasphemed, and whatever else he enumerates? Yet our Lord, foretelling to us his judgment, is silent about all the other right deeds of the just, and deigned to mention alms alone. He was silent about all the evil deeds of the unjust, and judged that the barrenness of alms alone was to be rebuked—only to admonish us. Why this? Because all crimes are redeemed by alms. Therefore he praised that fruitfulness, and blamed and condemned this dryness.

**2.** But what you have heard, that all evil deeds are redeemed by alms, do not understand as certain perverse people understand it. For alms can profit you toward blotting out past sins, if you change your ways. But if you persist in the same evils, you do not corrupt the judge by your alms. I speak with reference to those crimes and outrages which all must now avoid who receive the body and blood of Christ. For the rest, it does not escape me that this mortal life, set in corruptible flesh, cannot be without sins: but those daily and light ones have also their daily washings. This is why we beat our breast, and say in prayer to the Lord our God: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^1]. For not without reason do we say this, nor indeed would the heavenly master himself teach this prayer, unless he foresaw us to be sinners. For he saw what we ought to beware, and he saw what is difficult to beware. But he judged it impossible to beware of all, who taught this daily prayer not to any chance Christians, but to the very rams, the Apostles. But where the Apostles beat their breast, and say: *Forgive us our debts*, that is, our sins, does some little ewe dare to be proud of righteousness? See what sort John the apostle was: he reclined on the breast of the Lord, he drank secrets from that lofty fountain of wisdom. For from there he drank what in the Gospel he poured forth: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^2]. And the Gospel constantly testifies that the Lord especially loved him. And yet he says: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^3]. Yet not because these things are said ought anyone to be a secure murderer, if, because of his daily fornications and robberies, he comes to say: *Forgive us our debts*. For what God established to be said daily for light sins, that man thinks can avail him for those grave ones from which he is unwilling to depart, but in which he desires to remain perpetually. Let him beat his breast about these too, let him do alms for these too, but with his life changed: let him depart from there, and draw near to here. But if he says in his soul: If I commit daily robberies, and if I defile myself with daily adulterous corruptions, and if I seek soothsayers, sacrifice to idols, consult astrologers, and do not depart at all from such a life, yet, by doing daily alms, I extinguish all sins: you do indeed extinguish them, but when you are extinguished. For this evil exchange of yours will be such that there will be no lack of one to say of you: *And yet a little while, and the sinner will be no more; and you will seek his place, and not find it. I have seen the impious exalted above the cedars of Lebanon: and I passed by, and behold, he was not; and I sought, and his place was not found*[^4]. Your sins, then, perish, but with you. For you will not be allowed to sin in hell, nor, when the eternal fire has begun to torture you, will you think of satisfying your lusts. Your sins, then, perish, but with you. But if you change your life, they perish, you are found, and it will be said of you: *He was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found*[^5].

[^1]: Matt 6:12
[^2]: John 1:1
[^3]: 1 John 1:8
[^4]: Ps 37:10, 35-36 (36:10, 35-36)
[^5]: Luke 15:24
