# Sermon 369

**1.** Our Savior, born of the Father without a day, through whom every day was made, willed to have on earth this birthday which we celebrate today. Whoever you are who marvel at this day, marvel rather at the Eternal One abiding before every day, creating every day, born on the present day, freeing from the malice of the day[^1]. Marvel still: she who bore is both mother and virgin; he whom she bore is both infant and Word. Deservedly the heavens spoke, the angels gave thanks, the shepherds rejoiced, the magi were changed, the kings were troubled, the little ones were crowned. Nurse, O mother, our food; nurse the bread coming from heaven and laid in the manger as the fodder of the pious beasts. For there *the ox knew its owner, and the ass the manger of its lord*[^2], namely the circumcision and the uncircumcision[^3], by clinging to the cornerstone[^4], of whom the firstfruits were the shepherds and the magi. Nurse him who made you such that he himself might be made in you, who both brought you the gift of fruitfulness in being conceived and did not take away the dignity of virginity in being born; who chose for himself, before he was born, both the womb from which he was born and the day on which he was born. And he himself founded what he chose, that he might come forth from there like a bridegroom from his chamber[^5], that he might be seen by mortal eyes, and by the increase of the yearly light attest that he, the light of minds, had come. The prophets sang that the founder of heaven and earth would be on earth with men[^6]; the angel announced that the Creator of flesh and spirit would come in the flesh. John from the womb greeted the Savior in the womb; the old man Simeon recognized God as an infant; the widow Anna recognized the virgin mother. These are the testimonies of your nativity, O Lord Jesus, before the waves were spread under you as you trod them, yielding to you as you commanded; before the wind at your bidding fell silent, the dead at your calling lived, the sun at your dying grew pale, the earth at your rising trembled, heaven at your ascending lay open: before you had done these and other wonders in the already youthful age of your body. Still you were carried in the hands of your mother, and already you were recognized as the Lord of the world. Yourself a little child of the seed of Israel, and yourself with us God Emmanuel[^7].

**2.** What is that generation of our Savior, by which he is coeternal with the begetting Father, when the world dreaded this one from the virgin, which pious faith recognized and held, but unbelief mocked, and pride, overcome, feared? What is that generation, by which *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^8]? What is this Word, which, about to speak, was not silent beforehand, by the speaking of which he who spoke was not silent? What is the Word without time, through which the times were made; the Word which no one's lips opened in beginning, or closed in finishing; the Word which has no beginning from the mouth of those speaking, and opens the mouths of the dumb; the Word which is not made eloquent by the tongues of the nations, and makes the tongues of infants eloquent? What, I say, is that generation, to which the Father by dying does not yield, because he does not precede it by living? From all intervals of places and times, from all distension of spaces, which we are accustomed to perceive either in days or in bodies, let us lift up our soul to him, as much as with his help we can, if in some way we may be able to grasp both the one born not anticipating him who begets, and the one begetting not following him who is begotten, the Father and the Son: neither fathers alike, nor sons alike, and alike eternal: neither both begetting, nor both being born, but the one not living without the other. And let us think, if we can, that the Father has begotten eternally, and the Son has been born eternally; if we cannot, let us believe it. That which we wish to say is not here, yet it is not placed far from each one of us: *for in him we live, and move, and are*[^9]. Let us transcend our flesh, in which parents live before their children; because, that they might be able to beget children, they grew, and with their children growing they now grow old; before their children were born the parents lived, because also, with the parents dead, the children are going to live. Let us transcend also our souls: they too bring forth something by thinking, which they have with them by knowing; but they can lose it by forgetting, because they did not have it before by not knowing. Let us transcend all corporeal, temporal, changeable things, that we may see above all things him through whom all things were made[^10]. Our ascent is in the heart, because also that to which we ascend is near. But we are far from him, in so far as we are unlike him. Let his likeness, then, ascend to him, which he made and remade in us, by which not yet perfected the weak gaze quivers, and cannot behold the ineffable brightness of the eternal light. Whose splendor, then, the edge of the mind does not yet grasp, *his generation who shall declare?*[^11] But *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^12].

**3.** This generation, then, whose day we celebrate today, this in which he deigned to come through Israel and to become Emmanuel: God with us in the weakness of the flesh, not with us in the iniquity of the heart; coming to us through that which he assumed from ours, and freeing us through that which he remained in his own—for the Lord visited his servants through mortal weakness that he might make them free through unchangeable truth—this generation, then, of which human fragility is somehow capable; not that which abides without time, without mother above all things; but this which was made in time, without father among all things: this son of a virgin and bridegroom of virgins, born of an uncorrupted mother and making fruitful by incorruptible truth, let us praise, love, adore, that in his mercy we may triumph over the cunning of the devil overcome. The devil crept up to deceive us through the corrupted mind of a woman: Christ came forth to free us through the uncorrupted flesh too of a woman.
