# Sermon 203: On the Epiphany of the Lord

**1.** "Epiphany," a word of the Greek tongue, in Latin can be called "manifestation." On this present day, then, the Redeemer of all nations, made manifest, made a solemnity for all nations. Whose nativity, then, we celebrated a very few days ago, his manifestation we celebrate today. Our Lord Jesus Christ, then, born thirteen days ago, is today reported to have been adored by the Magi. That it happened, the truth of the Gospel tells: but on what day it happened, the authority of this so illustrious solemnity everywhere proclaims. For it seemed just — as it truly is just — that, since those Magi were the first of the Gentiles to know Christ the Lord, and, not yet moved by his speech, followed the star appearing to them and visibly speaking for the infant Word, as it were the tongue of heaven[^1], the Gentiles should gratefully recognize the day of the salvation of their firstfruits, and should dedicate it to the Lord Christ with thanksgiving in solemn homage. For the firstfruits of the Jews unto the faith and revelation of Christ appeared in those shepherds, who, on the very day on which he was born, saw him by coming from close by. To the former the angels, to the latter the star, announced it. To the former it was said: *Glory to God in the highest*[^2]: in the latter was fulfilled: *The heavens declare the glory of God*[^3]. Both indeed, as the beginnings of two walls coming from different directions, of the circumcision and the uncircumcision, ran to the cornerstone: that it might be their peace, making the two one[^4].

**2.** Yet the former praised God, from the fact that they had seen Christ; but the latter even adored Christ when seen. In the former grace was earlier, in the latter humility was greater. Perhaps, then, those shepherds, being less guilty, exulted more eagerly over salvation: but those Magi, laden with many sins, more humbly sought pardon. This is that humility which the divine Scripture commends more in those who were of the Gentiles than in the Jews. For of the Gentiles was that centurion who, when he had received the Lord in his whole heart, yet said himself unworthy that the Lord should enter his house, and did not want his sick servant to be seen by him, but to be ordered well[^5]. So inwardly he held present in his heart the one whose presence he honorably recalled from his roof. Finally the Lord said: *I have not found such faith in Israel*[^6]. That Canaanite woman too was of the Gentiles, who, when she had heard herself called a dog by the Lord, and unworthy that the bread of the children should be sent to her, asked for the crumbs as a dog: and therefore deserved not to be one, because she did not deny what she had been. For she too heard from the Lord: *O woman, great is your faith*[^7]. Humility had made in her great faith; because she had made herself small.

**3.** The shepherds, then, come from close by to see, and the Magi come from afar to adore. This is the humility by which the wild olive deserved to be grafted into the olive[^8], and to bear olives against nature; because it deserved to change nature through grace. For when with this wild olive the whole world ran wild and grew bitter, made fat through the grace of grafting, it shone. For they come from the end of the earth, according to Jeremiah, saying: *Truly our fathers worshiped lies*[^9]. And they come not from one part of the world, but, as the Gospel according to Luke says, *from the East and the West, from the North and the South*[^10], to recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven. So the whole world, from its four parts, is called to faith by the grace of the Trinity. According to which number, when four is multiplied by three, the apostolic number twelve is consecrated; prefiguring, as it were, the salvation of the whole world from the four parts of the world, in the grace of the Trinity. For this number that dish too signified, which was shown to Peter full of all animals[^11], as it were of all nations. For it too, hung from heaven by four lines, was let down three times and taken up[^12]; so that four threes make twelve. Therefore perhaps, twelve days being added after the birthday of the Lord, the Magi, the firstfruits of the Gentiles, came to see and adore Christ, and deserved not only to receive their own salvation, but also to signify the salvation of all nations. Let us therefore most devoutly celebrate this day too; and the Lord Jesus, whom those firstfruits of ours adored lying in a lodging, let us adore dwelling in heaven. For they venerated in him as future what we venerate fulfilled. The firstfruits of the Gentiles adored him reaching for his mother's breasts: the nations adore him sitting at the right hand of God the Father.
