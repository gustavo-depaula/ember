# Sermon 122

**1.** What we have heard said by the Lord Jesus Christ to Nathanael, if we understand it well, does not pertain to him alone. For the Lord Jesus himself saw under the fig tree the whole human race. For in this place it is understood that by the fig tree he signified sin. Not everywhere does it signify this, but in this place, as I said, namely by that fittingness of signifying by which you know the first man, when he sinned, was covered with fig leaves [^1]. For with these leaves they covered their shame, when they blushed at their sin; and the members which God gave them, they themselves made shameful to themselves. For one ought not to blush at the work of God: but the cause of sin preceded the confusion. If iniquity had not preceded, nakedness would never blush. For they were naked, and were not confounded. For they had committed nothing for which they should be confounded. But why did I say this? That by the fig tree we may understand sin to have been signified. What is it, then: *When you were under the fig tree, I saw you?* When you were under sin, I saw you. And looking indeed at the event, Nathanael recalled that he had been under the fig tree, where Christ was not. But he was not there by bodily presence; but by spiritual knowledge where is he not? And because he knew that he had been alone under the fig tree, where the Lord Christ was not, when he said to him: *When you were under the fig tree, I saw you*; he both recognized the divinity in him, and exclaimed: *You are the king of Israel* [^2].

**2.** The Lord says: *Because I said to you: I saw you when you were under the fig tree, therefore you marvel; you will see greater things than these*. What are these greater things? And he said: *You will see heaven opened, and the angels of God ascending and descending upon the Son of man* [^3]. Let us recall the old history written in the holy Book, that is, in Genesis. When Jacob slept in a place, he put a stone at his head; and in dreams he saw a ladder reaching from earth all the way to heaven; and the Lord leaned upon it; and the angels were ascending and descending by it. This Jacob saw. The dream of a man would not be written, unless some great mystery were figured in it, and something great prophesied were understood in that vision. In short, Jacob himself, because he understood what he had seen, set a stone there, and poured oil over it [^4]. For you recognize chrism: recognize Christ too. For he himself is the stone which the builders rejected; he himself was made the head of the corner [^5]. He himself is the stone, of which he himself said: *Whoever stumbles on that stone will be shattered; but on whom that stone falls, it will crush him* [^6]. He is stumbled on as he lies: but he will fall upon them, when he comes from on high to judge the living and the dead. Woe to the Jews, because while Christ lay humbly, they stumbled on him. *This man*, they say, *is not from God, who breaks the Sabbath* [^7]. *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross* [^8]. Madman, the stone lies, and therefore you laugh. But when you laugh, you are blind; when you are blind, you stumble; when you stumble, you are shattered; when you have been shattered while he lies thus, afterward when he comes from on high you will be crushed. So Jacob anointed the stone. Did he make an idol? He signified, he did not adore. Now then hear, attend to this Nathanael, on whose occasion the Lord Jesus wished to expound to us the vision of Jacob.

**3.** You know, who have been instructed in the school of Christ, that Jacob himself is also Israel. There are two names: for he was one man. He received the first name, Jacob, when he was born, which is interpreted: Supplanter. But when those twins were born, the brother Esau was born first, and the hand of that younger one was found on the foot of the elder. He held the foot of his brother going before him in being born, afterward he himself too followed. And on account of this deed, because he held the heel of his brother, he was called Jacob [^9], that is, Supplanter. But afterward, when he was returning from Mesopotamia, an angel wrestled with him on the way. What strength can be compared, of an angel and a man? So it is a mystery, so it is a sacrament, so it is a prophecy, so it is a figure; so let us understand. For see too the manner of the wrestling. While he wrestled, Jacob prevailed over the angel. A great signification. And when the man had prevailed over the angel, he held him; yet that man held the one whom he had conquered. And he said to him: *I will not let you go, unless you bless me*. When the conqueror was blessed by the conquered, Christ was figured. So that angel, who is understood as the Lord Jesus, said to Jacob: *You will no longer be called Jacob, but your name will be Israel*; which is interpreted: Seeing God. Then he touched the sinew of his thigh, that is, the breadth of Jacob's thigh, and it withered for him; and Jacob was made lame [^10]. He is the one conquered. So much could the conquered one do, that he both touched the thigh, and made him lame. Therefore he was conquered willingly. For he had the power to lay down his strength, and the power to take it up again [^11]. The conquered one is not angry, because the crucified is not angry. For he even blessed him, saying: *You will not be called Jacob, but Israel*. Then the "supplanter" became "seeing God." And he touched, as I said, his thigh, and made him limp. Attend in Jacob to the people of the Jews, those thousands following and going before the mount of the Lord, who, joined to the Apostles, adored the Lord and cried: *Hosanna to the Son of David; blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord* [^12]. Behold Jacob blessed. Now he remained lame in those who are now Jews. For the breadth of the thigh signifies the multitude of the race. Of whom the Psalm, when it had prophesied that the nations would believe, says: *A people whom I did not know, served me, in the hearing of the ear it obeyed me* [^13]. I was not there, and I was heard; here I was, and I was killed. *A people whom I did not know, served me, in the hearing of the ear it obeyed me*. So faith is from hearing, but hearing through the word of Christ [^14]. And there follows: *Alien sons have lied to me*, of the Jews. *Alien sons have lied to me, alien sons have grown old, and have limped from their paths* [^15]. I have assigned Jacob to you, both blessed and lame.

**4.** But from this occasion we must not pass over what can perhaps of itself stir some of you; what it means, that, whereas the name of Abraham, the grandfather of this Jacob, was changed (for he too was first called Abram, and God changed his name for him, and said: *You will not be called Abram but Abraham* [^16], from that time he was not called Abram. Seek in the Scriptures, and you will see, before he received the other name, he was not called except Abram; after he received it, he was not called except Abraham. But this Jacob, when he received another name, heard the same words: *You will not be called Jacob, but you will be called Israel* [^17]. Seek the Scriptures, and see that both were always called, both Jacob and Israel. Abram having received the other name, he was not called other than Abraham; Jacob having received the other name, he was called both Jacob, and Israel. The name of Abraham had to be unfolded in this age; for here he was made the father of many nations, from which he received the name. But the name of Israel pertains to the other age, where we shall see God. So the people of God, the Christian people, in this time is both Jacob and Israel; Jacob in reality, Israel in hope. For the lesser people of the greater people is called the supplanter of the brother. Did we supplant the Jews? But we are called their supplanters, because for our cause they were supplanted. Unless they had been blinded, Christ would not be crucified; if Christ were not crucified, that precious blood would not be poured out; if that blood were not poured out, the world would not be redeemed. Since, then, their blindness profited us, therefore the elder was supplanted by the younger brother, and the younger was called "Supplanter." But how long is this?

**5.** A time will come, the end of the age will come, and all Israel will believe; not those who are now, but the sons of those who will be then. For now these, walking in their own ways, will go to their own places, will pass over to perpetual damnation. But when one whole people has been made, that will come to be which we sing: *I shall be satisfied, when your glory is made manifest* [^18]. When the promise comes which is promised us, that we may see face to face. Now we see through a mirror in a riddle and in part; but when both peoples, now purged, now raised up, now crowned, now changed into the immortal form and into perpetual incorruption, will see God face to face, and now there will not be Jacob, but only Israel; then the Lord will see him in the person of this holy Nathanael, and will say: *Behold, truly an Israelite, in whom there is no guile*. When you hear: *Behold, truly an Israelite*, let Israel come to your mind; when Israel comes to your mind, let his dream come to your mind, in which dream he saw the ladder from earth all the way to heaven, the Lord leaning over it, the angels of God ascending and descending. This dream Jacob saw. But after this he was called Israel: that is, a little later, coming from Mesopotamia and making his journey. If, then, Jacob saw the ladder, who was also called Israel; and this Nathanael too is *truly an Israelite, in whom there is no guile*; therefore, when he had marveled because the Lord said to him: *Under the fig tree I saw you*; he said to him: *You will see greater things than these*. And likewise he told him the dream of Jacob. To whom did he tell it? To the one he called an Israelite, in whom there was no guile. As though saying: Of him by whose name I called you, his very dream will appear in you; do not hasten to marvel, you will see greater things than these. *You will see heaven opened, and the angels of God ascending and descending to the Son of man* [^19]. Behold what Jacob saw: behold why Jacob poured oil over the stone; behold why Jacob the prophet signified and figured Christ. For that deed was a prophecy.

**6.** Now what you await, I know: what you want to hear from me, I understand. I will say this too briefly, as the Lord grants: *The angels ascending and descending to the Son of man* [^20]. How is it that if they descend to him, he is here; if they ascend to him, he is above? But if they both ascend to him and descend to him, he is both above and here. In no way can it come about that they ascend to him, and descend to him; unless he is both there to which they ascend, and here to which they descend. How do we prove that he is both there and here? Let Paul answer us, who was first Saul. He himself experienced that, when he was first a persecutor, and afterward was made a preacher: first Jacob, afterward Israel; who was himself too of the race of Israel, of the tribe of Benjamin [^21]. In him let us see Christ above, Christ below. First the very voice of the Lord from heaven shows this: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me* [^22]? Had Paul ascended into heaven? Had Paul at least thrown a stone into heaven? He persecuted the Christians, bound them, dragged them to be killed, sought them everywhere as they hid, and when they were found spared them by no reasoning. To him the Lord Christ said: *Saul, Saul*. From where does he cry? From heaven. Therefore he is above. *Why do you persecute me*? Therefore he is below. All things, though briefly, as I could, I have expounded to Your Charity. I have doled out what pertains to me: but what pertains to you, think of the poor. Turned toward the Lord, and so on.
