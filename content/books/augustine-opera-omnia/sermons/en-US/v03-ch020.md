# Sermon 64: A Treatise on the Birthday of the Holy Martyrs

**1.** We celebrate the memorial of the martyrs. Let us, then, admire the praises of the martyrs and imitate their deeds. All those things which you heard when the holy Gospel was read: *For they will hand you over to councils and in their synagogues they will scourge you*[^1], then what was said: *And brother will hand over brother to death, and father his son, and a man's enemies will be those of his own household*[^2], these things happen to the good and the evil. For the evils that men suffer on this earth are common to the good and the evil, just as the goods that men have on this earth are commonly the good's and the evil's. So the chorus of martyrs, seeing that many evil men suffer many evils on this earth, cried out to God with one voice: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from the unholy nation*[^3]. For when the evil and the good are both punished, what becomes of the good if the cause is not distinguished? The good man is punished here, but crowned with God. The evil man is both punished here, and tortured in the everlasting judgment. So if we love the holy martyrs, let us choose a cause for ourselves, that we may please God.

**2.** See how our Lord Jesus Christ forms his martyrs by his discipline. *I send*, he says, *you as sheep in the midst of wolves*[^4]. See what happens if one wolf comes among many sheep. However many thousands of sheep there are, when one wolf is sent into the midst, they are disturbed; and if not all are torn apart, yet all are terrified. What kind of reasoning, then, was this, what counsel, what power, what great divinity, not to admit a wolf to the sheep, but to send sheep among wolves! *I send*, he says, *you as sheep in the midst of wolves*; not, to the borders of the wolves, but *in the midst of the wolves.* So there was a host of wolves, few sheep, that many wolves might kill few sheep. The wolves were converted and became sheep.

**3.** Yet it is said to all, both those who then were, who heard the Lord, and those who would be, who through them would believe in the Lord, and, as they departed by dying, would follow them by being born, even to us and after us to the end of the age—it is said to all: *You will be hated by all nations on account of my name*[^5]. The future Church was indeed foretold throughout all nations. As we read it promised, so we behold it rendered. And all nations are Christian, and again all nations are non-Christian. Through the whole field wheat, through the whole field tares[^6]. So when you hear this said by our Lord Jesus Christ: *You will be hated by all men on account of my name*, hear it as wheat, this is said to the wheat. Consider with me, lest perhaps someone say in his mind: "This was said to the disciples of the Lord, when our Lord Jesus Christ sent them to preach his word among the nations. All nations hated them on account of his name. But now all nations glorify his name. Let us not think we are hated by all nations, but rather loved by all nations." O all you Christian nations, O wheat of the Lord, O catholic seedlings spread through the whole world, attend to yourselves and recognize that all nations hate you on account of the name of Christ! However many remain pagans, however many remain Jews, however many have become straying heretics—do not all hate us on account of the name of Christ? But suppose some even most wicked man exists, let him be noble, let him be powerful, let him shine with dignity, be exalted in power, let him will evil things, be able to do very much, and he too is hated by all men—but not on account of Christ. The voice indeed is similar, but the cause dissimilar. Therefore the Lord Jesus, because he knew it also happens to most wicked men that they are hated by all men, when he had said: *All hate you*, added: *on account of my name*, because he heard those who say: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from the unholy nation*[^7].

**4.** Let us hear, then, what he admonished who promised the crowns. He set forth a contest, but he who awaits those contending helps those laboring. What kind of contest, then, did he set forth? "Agon" in Latin is "contest." What kind of contest, then, did the Lord Jesus Christ proclaim? This is what he proclaimed: *Be cunning as serpents and simple as doves*[^8]. He who has understood this, who has held this, who has kept this, dies secure because he does not die. For no one ought to die secure except one who knows he so dies that death dies in him, and life is crowned.

**5.** Therefore, dearly beloved, it must be explained to you, although we have often spoken of this, what it is to be *simple as doves, cunning as serpents.* If the simplicity of doves is now enjoined on us, what does the cunning of the serpent do in the simplicity of the dove? I love in the dove that it has no gall. I fear in the serpent that it has venom.

**6.** Do not abhor the serpent in every respect. It has what you may hate, it has what you may imitate. For the serpent, when it has been weighed down by old age and feels the burden of age, squeezes itself through a narrow opening and puts off its old skin, that it may exult as new. Imitate it, you Christian, who hear Christ saying: *Enter by the narrow gate*[^9]. And the apostle Paul says to you: *Put off the old man with his acts, and put on the new, who is created according to God*[^10]. So you have what to imitate in the serpent: do not die for the old self. He who dies for some temporal advantage dies for the old self. He who dies for the advantage of human praise dies for the old self. But when you have put off all these old things, you have imitated the cunning of the serpent. Imitate it more strongly: keep your head. What is, keep your head? Keep Christ with you. Has any of you perhaps noticed, when it kills a serpent, how it exposes its whole body to the blows of the striker for the sake of its head? It does not wish to be struck there where it knows it has its life. And Christ is our life. For he himself said: *I am the way, the truth, and the life*[^11]. Hear the Apostle too: *The head of the man is Christ*[^12]. He, then, who keeps Christ in himself, keeps his head for himself.

**7.** But now what need is there to commend at length the simplicity of doves? For the venoms of the serpent had to be guarded against. There the imitation was perilous, where there was something to be feared. But imitate the dove securely. Notice how doves rejoice in society; everywhere they fly together, feed together, are unwilling to be alone, rejoice in communion. They are fervent with love, they murmur with groans of love, they beget children with kisses. *But as long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims away from the Lord*[^13]. *Blessed are those who mourn!*[^14] And if you wish to be a dove, say to your Lord: *My groaning is not hidden from you*[^15]. So when doves—for this too we very often notice—quarrel among themselves over their places, in a way it is a peaceable contention. Surely because they quarrel, are they therefore separated? They fly together, feed together, and the very quarrel is peaceable. See the quarrel of doves. The Apostle said: *But if anyone does not obey our word by the letter, mark that one and do not mingle with him*[^16]. Behold a quarrel. But notice that it is the quarrel of doves, not of wolves. At once he added: *And do not regard him as an enemy, but correct him as a brother*[^17]. The dove loves even when it strikes. The wolf hates even when it fawns.

**8.** Having, then, the simplicity of doves and imitating the cunning of serpents, celebrate the solemnities of the martyrs in sobriety of mind, not in drunkenness of belly. Speak praises to God. For he himself is our God, who is also the Lord of the martyrs, he himself our crowner, if we have wrestled well, who crowned those whom we long to imitate.
