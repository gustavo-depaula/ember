# Sermon 148

**1.** When the reading was read from the book entitled *The Acts of the Apostles*[^1], you noticed what happened to those who, having sold a piece of land, kept back part of the price of the land and laid it at the feet of the apostles as though it were the whole price. They were rebuked, and both at once breathed their last, the man and his wife. To some this rebuke seemed too severe, that people should die on account of money kept back from their own property. The Holy Spirit did not do this out of greed; rather, the Holy Spirit thus punished a lie. For you heard the words of the most blessed Peter, who said, *While it remained, did it not remain your own, and after it was sold, was it not in your power*[^2]? If you did not wish to sell, who would have forced you? If you wished to offer half, who would have demanded the whole? For if half was to be offered, half ought to have been declared. Half for the whole—this is the lie that had to be punished. Yet do not regard this rebuke as severe, brothers, this temporal death. And would that the punishment had gone only this far! For what great thing happens to mortals who are going to die in any case? But through their temporal punishment God willed his discipline to be known. We must believe, however, that after this life God spared them, for great is his mercy. Now concerning the deaths that come as punishment, the apostle Paul says in a certain place, rebuking those who were mistreating the body and blood of Christ, *For this reason many among you are weak and sick, and some have fallen asleep*[^3]—that is, as many as suffices to impose discipline. *Many among you have fallen asleep*, that is, have died. For they were being scourged by the Lord; they fell sick and died. And after these words he added, *For if we judged ourselves, we would not be judged by the Lord. But when we are judged, we are rebuked by the Lord, that we may not be condemned with the world*[^4]. What, then, if something like this happened to this man and his wife? They were rebuked by the scourge of death, that they might not be punished with eternal torment.

**2.** Let your Charity attend only to this, that if it displeased God for them to withhold part of the money they had vowed to God—and that money, surely, was needed for human use—how angry is God when chastity is vowed and not rendered, when virginity is vowed and not rendered? For it is vowed for the use of God, not for the use of human beings. What do I mean when I say "for the use of God"? That out of holy persons God makes himself a house, makes himself a temple, in which he may deign to dwell; and surely he wills his temple to remain holy. So to a consecrated virgin about to marry one may say what Peter said about the money: Did not your virginity remain your own while it remained, and before you vowed it, was it not in your power? But whoever does this, vows such things and does not render them, let them not suppose that they are rebuked with temporal deaths, but condemned to eternal fire.

[^1]: Acts 5:1-11
[^2]: Acts 5:4
[^3]: 1 Cor 11:30
[^4]: 1 Cor 11:31-32
