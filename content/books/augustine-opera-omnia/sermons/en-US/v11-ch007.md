# Sermon 142: Augm. (Dolbeau 7)

**1.** Concerning the burial of catechumens, the lord, father, and brother, since he deigns to, bids me make it known to your holiness. And in truth it is especially his care, but in that love of which we were speaking we share all things with you, that we may be sharers of Christ. Grief is wont to be somewhat—and pardonably—prone to ill will. For who would not forgive one who grieves and is troubled, if perhaps he speaks with ill will? Yet you all ought to know, dearly beloved, what many of you and almost all know, that according to the custom and discipline of the Church, the bodies of deceased catechumens ought not to be buried among the bodies of the faithful, where also the sacraments of the faithful are celebrated, nor can it be granted to anyone. Otherwise it will be nothing but a culpable respecting of persons. For why should it be granted to the richer and not granted to the poor, if there is there any solace for the dead? For the merits of the dead are regarded, not in the places of their bodies, but in the affections of their souls. My brethren, learn to think these things too, as the faithful do: for the sake of the sacraments, bodies cannot be placed where they ought not to be.

**2.** Yet we lament that the catechumen has departed hence, and we grieve over him who was in question. And from this we admonish you, brethren, that no one be certain that he will live tomorrow. Run to grace, change your ways: let this avail you for admonition. What was healthier than he, what more vigorous than his body? Suddenly he died. He was well, he is deceased, and would that he were deceased and not truly dead. For what shall I say, my brethren? Shall I flatter the man and say that catechumens too go where the faithful go? Do we so coax the griefs of men that we argue against the Gospel? We cannot, my brethren. One must run while alive, lest the dead be truly lamented and be truly dead. If men ran for the sacraments of the living as they run for the tombs of the dead, perhaps no one would reasonably be lamented: for even if he were lamented, he would be lamented with carnal affection. For he is not to be lamented who is allotted better things, the temptations of the world forsaken, and nowhere fearful, secure in Christ, not fearing the adversary devil, not shuddering at a slanderous man.

**3.** For perhaps that Lazarus was not buried, whose wounds the dogs licked: for God was silent about his burial. Nothing was said of him except that, when he had died, he was carried away into Abraham's bosom. It was not even said that he was buried. For he who, living and hungry, was despised, perhaps was also cast out unburied when dead. And yet he was carried away by the angels into Abraham's bosom. *But the rich man also died,* he says, *and was buried*[^1]. What did even a marble tomb profit the soul among the dead, thirsting for a drop from the fingertip and not receiving it? I do not wish to say more, my brethren; it is enough to have terrified thus far, lest we increase the grief of certain of our brethren who have been struck by this misfortune. For I ought not even to have said these very things, except that we were compelled to exhort and admonish you all.

**4.** Consider human frailty, my brethren; run while you live, that you may live; run while you live, lest you truly die. The discipline of Christ is not to be feared. He cries out: *My yoke is easy, and my burden is light*[^2], in this very passage which we were treating a little before: *Learn of me, for I am meek and humble of heart. For my yoke is easy, and my burden is light*[^3], and you argue against it and say: "I do not wish to be faithful yet"? "I cannot." What is "I cannot," but that the yoke of Christ is harsh, and the burden heavy? Your flesh, then, suggests the truth to you, and Christ lies? He says: *It is easy,* and your vanity says: "It is harsh." He says: *It is light,* and your vanity says: "It is heavy." Believe Christ rather, because both his yoke is easy and his burden light. Do not be afraid, submit your neck without fear. The yoke will be the easier for your neck, the more faithful the neck itself is. And so, brethren, we have said these things and admonished your love for two reasons: that no one ask this and be saddened if he does not obtain it, and that each of you, O catechumens, while you live, beware lest, being dead, you perish, and lest neither your own nor mother Church herself find how you may be helped.

[^1]: See Luke 16:22
[^2]: Matt 11:30
[^3]: Matt 11:29-30
