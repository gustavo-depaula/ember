# Sermon 236/A: On the Monday of Easter Week

**1.** During these days of holy Easter, as your Charity knows, brothers, the resurrection of the Lord according to all the Evangelists is solemnly recited. For they wrote their narratives in such a way that they said some things alike, while one left out some things, yet no one was in disagreement with the harmony of truth. All said that the Lord was crucified, buried, and rose on the third day; but how he appeared to the disciples—since he appeared in many ways—some said some things, which others left out; yet all wrote down the truth.

**2.** See, on the past night of the vigils it was read, if you remember, that the Lord appeared after the resurrection to the women, whom he himself first greeted, saying: *Hail. But they approached, and held his feet, and worshiped him*[^1]. Likewise today it was read that he appeared to two of his disciples walking on the road: *For they were walking with him, and did not recognize him; for their eyes were held, that they should not recognize him*[^2]; but recognition was being reserved for the breaking of the bread. For he came with them; he was received by them as a guest; he blessed the bread, and broke it, and they recognized him. So you recognize Christ, you who believe in Christ. But let your Charity consider what all the disciples were like before the Lord's resurrection. Let them forgive me; they were not yet faithful; afterward they became great, but before they were even inferior to us. For we believe that Christ rose, which they did not yet believe. But afterward they saw, they touched, they handled him with their own eyes and hands, and so believed, and their hearts were strengthened by the holy Scriptures. Those who drank, belched, and filled us too.

**3.** They were speaking, then, of these things among themselves, grieving for Christ's death as for a man's, and Jesus appeared to them, and added himself as a companion, a third, and asked what they were saying among themselves. They answered him: *Are you the only stranger in Jerusalem; do you not know what has happened here in these days; how the chief priests handed Jesus over to death, who was a great prophet?*[^3] O disciples, where was the Lord—now a prophet? Did he not himself fulfill all the prophets? See, brothers, how the disciples believed, but out of despair at the Lord's death they went back to the words that strangers used to say about Christ. You remember, beloved, that the Lord once said to his disciples: *Who do men say that the Son of man is?* and they answered him the words and opinions of strangers, not their own faith, and said: *Some say you are John the Baptist, others Elijah, others Jeremiah, or one of the prophets*[^4]. To this, then, the disciples went back, they lost their own faith; they began to hold the opinion of strangers. *He who was*, they say, *a prophet.* This strangers used to say about Christ, but what did the disciples say? When Christ said: *Who do you say that I am? Peter answered: You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.* And the Lord: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, for flesh and blood did not reveal it to you*—as it did to those who call me a prophet—*but my Father, who is in heaven; and I tell you: You are Peter*[^5]. You told me, I will tell you; you have given back the confession, hear the blessing. For the Lord had said of himself what was less, and Peter had told him what was more. For in the Lord Jesus Christ it was less that he was the Son of man; it was more that he was the Son of God. He said less who humbled himself; he said more whom the other exalted. *On this rock*, says the Lord, *I will build my Church*. On this confession, on this, that you have said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God, I will build my Church, and the gates of hell shall not prevail against it*[^6]. For the gates of hell had prevailed against these men; they had withdrawn from Peter; they had failed on the sand. Help, Lord, your disciples; break the bread, that you may be recognized; unless you gather them, they are lost. How did you ask? See, the disciples call you a prophet.

**4.** But then Jesus opened to them the Scriptures, because they had said in despair: *But we were hoping that he was the one to redeem Israel*[^7]. O disciples, you were hoping, now you do not hope. Come, robber, admonish the disciples. Why do you despair, because you saw him crucified, because you looked on him hanging, because you thought him weak? Such did the robber too, hanging on the cross, at once believing, recognize him—that sharer in punishment; but you forgot the author of life. Cry out, robber, from the cross; you, a criminal, convict the saints. What do they say? *We were hoping that he was the one to redeem Israel.* What does this one say? *Lord, remember me when you come into your kingdom*[^8]. You were hoping, then, that he was the one to redeem Israel. O disciples, if he is the one to redeem Israel, you have failed; but he restored you, he does not abandon. He who became your companion on the road, he himself became your road. But Peter was not there then, that Peter who said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^9]. He was not with them. He, thinking of Christ, wherever he was—because he was with them, before the Lord died, then he denied, but when the Lord looked at him he wept. But now, when the Lord was crucified and dead . . . For perhaps he thought, because the Jews insulted him by saying: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross, and we will believe in him*[^10]. When perhaps even the disciples were not insulting, but exhorting him to come down from the cross. After he did not do it, but handed over his spirit, and was seen dead on the wood, as other men die, wrapped, buried; when they too despaired, and among the despairing was Peter; after the resurrection, as the Evangelist Mark says: *He appeared to the women; he said: Go, tell my disciples, and Peter, that I have risen from the dead*[^11]. For the Lord had already shown himself to the faithful women; and they returned and brought the good news to the disciples, that they had seen a vision of angels, who said to them: *Why do you seek the living among the dead? He is not here: he has risen*[^12]; and they did not find his body in the tomb. The women said these things, and the men did not believe; they were announcing these things to the apostles, they were announcing to the heralds who he was. For when he was casting out wandering spirits from possessed bodies, those very spirits, twisted and afflicted with torments, said: *What have we to do with you, Jesus, Son of the most high God? Why have you come before the time to torment us?*[^13]
