# Sermon 29

**1** We have been admonished, and it has been commanded us by the Spirit of God, that we should confess to the Lord. And the reason given for confessing to the Lord is, *because he is good*[^1]. What is most profoundly considered is briefly said. *Confess*, it says, *to the Lord*. And as though we asked, "Why?" the answer is given: *Because he is good*. What more do you seek, or if you seek anything else, than the good? So great is the force of the good that even the evil seek the good. But the other things that are called good have it from some good that they are good. But if we ask from whom all good things have it that they are good, let us recall: *And God made all things, and behold they were very good*[^2]. Nothing, then, would be good, unless it were made by the good. And by what kind of good? One whom no one made. Nothing, then, would be good, unless it were made by a good that was not made. Heaven is good, but made good; the angels are good, but made good; the stars are good, the sun and moon, the alternation of night and day, the changes of seasons, the rolling of the ages, the course of years, the buds of trees and herbs, the natures of animals, and among all these the creature that gives praise, man. All are good, but made good. And good from God, not from themselves. He who made these things is good above all things, because no one made him good, but he is good of himself. Yet he is good not for himself alone, but for us too. *Confess*, then, *to the Lord, because he is good*.

**2** Confession is either of one praising or of one doing penance. For there are some little instructed who, when they hear of confession in the Scriptures, as though it could not be except of sins, at once beat their breasts, as if they were already being warned to confess sins. But that your Charity may know that confession does not pertain to sins alone, let us hear the one of whom we cannot doubt that he had no sin at all, crying out and saying: *I confess to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth*[^3]. Who said this? *He who did no sin, nor was deceit found in his mouth*[^4]. He who alone could most truly say: *Behold, the prince of the world comes, and in me he will find nothing*[^5]. And yet he confesses. But this confessor is a praiser, not a sinner. Finally, hear what he confesses. Hear the praises, and that praise itself is our salvation. For what does the Son without sin confess to God the Father? *I confess*, he says, *to you, Father, Lord of heaven and earth, that you have hidden these things from the wise and prudent, and revealed them to little ones*[^6]. He commended this praise of the Father, that he hid these things from the wise and prudent—that is, the proud and arrogant—and revealed them to little ones—that is, the weak and humble.

**3** But it is also true that there is a salutary confession of sins. Whence we heard in the psalm that was read first: *Set a guard, O Lord, over my mouth, and a door of restraint about my lips, that you may not incline my heart to evil words, to make excuses for sins*[^7]. He asks God to give a guard to his mouth. And of what thing he expounds and says. For there are men who greatly abound, who, when they begin to be accused, run to excuse themselves. But to excuse is to seek causes, and to knit together pretexts, why the sin may seem not to pertain to you. One says, "The devil did it to me"; another says, "Fortune did it to me"; another, "I was driven by fate"—no one lays it on himself. When you want to be your own excuser, your accuser triumphs over you. Do you want, then, to do what would grieve and make your accuser groan—that is, the devil? Do what you have heard, do what you have learned, and say to your God: *I said, O Lord, be gracious to me, heal my soul, for I have sinned against you*[^8]. *I*, he says, *I said it*, not the devil, not fortune, not fate. *I said it*: I do not excuse myself, but accuse myself. *I said: be gracious to me, heal my soul*. For whence is it sick? *Because I have sinned against you*.

**4** *Confess*, then, *to the Lord, because he is good*[^9]. If you want to praise, what do you praise more securely than the good? If you want to praise, if you want to have the confession of praise, what do you praise more securely than the good? If you want to confess your sins, to whom more safely than to the good? To a man, since he is evil, you confess and are condemned. To God, since he is good, you confess and are purified. If you attend to the confession of praise, whatever you are about to praise abundantly, your intention is occupied about this, to show that what you praise is good. For good things are rightly praised, as evil things are rightly blamed. Briefly has the praise of your Lord been told you: *He is good*. If you too are good, praise that from which you are good; if you are evil, praise that from which you may be good. For if you are good, you are good from him; if you are evil, you are evil from yourself. Flee from yourself and come to him who made you, because by fleeing yourself you follow yourself, and by following yourself you cling to him who made you.

**5** How many good things you seek, O evil man! Surely you are evil. Tell me what you want, except the good? You seek a horse, only a good one; you seek an estate, only a good one; you seek a house, only a good one; you seek a wife, only a good one; a tunic, only a good one; a boot, only a good one. Your soul alone evil! Are you not contrary to yourself, who seek good things while you are evil? If you seek good things, first be yourself what you seek. But if, evil, you have found many good things, what does it profit you, since you yourself have perished? Love your souls when they are good; hate your souls when they are evil. But by loving him from whom is every good, you will be good. Hating your evils, choose the good things.

**6** What is it to hate your evils? By doing penance to confess your sins. For everyone who does penance and by doing penance confesses his sins is angry at himself, and in a way, by doing penance, takes vengeance on himself for what displeases him. For God hates sin. If you too hate in yourself what God hates, then by some willing you are joined to God, while you hate in yourself what God too hates. Rage against yourself, that God may intercede for you and not condemn you. For sin without doubt must be punished. This is owed to sin: punishment, condemnation. Sin must be punished, either by you or by him. If it is punished by you, then it will be punished without you; but if it is not punished by you, it is punished together with you. *Confess*, then, *to the Lord, because he is good*[^10]. Praise as much as you can, love as much as you can: *Pour out your hearts before him; God is our helper, because he is good*[^11].
