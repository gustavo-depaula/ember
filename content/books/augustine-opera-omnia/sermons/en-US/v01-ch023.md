# Sermon 18: Of Saint Augustine, on the Verse of Psalm 49:

"God Shall Come Manifest"

**1.** For the exhorting of the minds of your Charity, receive gratefully a few things from the present psalm, which the Lord grants. Of our Lord Jesus Christ it was prophesied in this psalm, where we heard and sang, *God shall come manifest, our God, and he shall not keep silence*[^1]. For the Lord Christ himself, our God, the Son of God, at his first coming came hidden, at his second coming will come manifest. When he came hidden, he became known only to his servants; when he comes manifest, he will become known to the good and the wicked. When he came hidden, he came to be judged; when he comes manifest, he will come to judge. Finally, then, when he was being judged, he kept silence, and of his silence the Prophet had foretold, *As a sheep he was led to the slaughter, and as a lamb before the shearer, so he opened not his mouth*[^2]. But *God shall come manifest, our God, and he shall not keep silence.* Not as he kept silence when he was to be judged, so will he keep silence when he will be the judge. And now he does not keep silence, if there is one to hear, but it was said, *Then he shall not keep silence*, when they will acknowledge his voice who now despise it. For now, when the commandments of God are said, to some they become a matter for laughter. And since what God promised is not now shown, and what he threatens is not now seen, what he commands is mocked. For now the so-called happiness of this world the wicked too have; the so-called unhappiness of this world the good too have. People who believe present things and do not believe future things attend that these good and evil things of the present age are had indiscriminately by the good and the wicked. If they desire riches, they see that both the worst men and good men have riches. They see also, if they shudder at the poverty and miseries of this age, that not only the good but also the wicked labor in these miseries. And they say in their heart that God neither regards nor governs human affairs, but has altogether left us, in some innermost depth of this world, to be tossed by chances, and shows us no providence. And through this there arises in them a contempt of the commandment, because they do not see the manifestation of judgment.

**2.** Nevertheless, even now each one ought to attend that, when God wills, he regards and judges, he does not defer it for an hour; but when he wills, he defers. And whence this? Because if he never judged in the present, it would not be believed that God exists; if he judged everything in the present, he would reserve nothing for judgment. For to this end many things are reserved for judgment, and certain things are judged in the present, that those who are deferred may fear and be converted. For God does not love to condemn but to save, and therefore he is patient toward the wicked, that of the wicked he may make good. So the Apostle says, that *the anger of God shall be revealed upon all impiety*[^3], and *God will render to each according to his works*[^4]. But he admonishes and rebukes the man who despises, and says, *Or do you despise the riches of his goodness and long-suffering?*[^5] Because he is good to you, because he is long-suffering, because he is patient toward you, because he defers you and does not take you away, you despise, and think there is no judgment of God at all, not knowing *that the patience of God leads you to repentance. But you, according to the hardness of your heart, lay up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will render to each according to his works*[^6].

**3.** Whatever, then, a man does now he puts into a treasury, but he does not know what he will gather. As the rich who put into an earthly treasury, as it were, know what they gather, but do not know for whom they gather. For who will possess their riches after their death they utterly do not know, and sometimes their riches come to their enemies. And therefore each one defrauds himself, unwilling to eat that he may grow rich, that another may flow with and luxuriate in and squander his labors. As, then, they gather indeed knowing, but for whom they gather they do not know, so, on the contrary, in the heavenly treasury the good know what they gather, the wicked do not know what they gather. For the good man puts in the heavenly treasury all the works of mercy that he did toward the people he relieved, and he knows that the keeper is faithful who keeps for him all that he stores up. And he does not see them, but he is certain of his treasure, because neither is anything snatched away by a thief, nor invaded by an enemy, nor taken away by a foe, wicked and powerful, as though by suit, but it will always remain because it is kept by the most powerful Lord. For if people entrust their monies to a faithful servant and are secure, do they entrust their works of mercy to the powerful Lord and are anxious? They know, then, that whatever they store up, it is all safe there. Those who are faithful join faith to the power of their Lord. They believe him that he keeps it, and they find that he keeps it. For even people who gather money, do they see the very chest, or the money in the chest? They are always gathering and putting in, or burying and keeping. They do not see it, and yet, as it were, their conscience knows that it is there in that place where they put it. And perhaps a thief has already taken it, and he rejoices in vain who has stored it up emptily. But in the heavenly treasury, if we have put anything, we are both secure of the Lord's keeping, and suffer no thief at all, nor sustain any loss. But the wicked too themselves put into the treasury all their evil works, and God keeps them for them. This is what the Apostle said, *You lay up for yourself wrath in the day of wrath of the just judgment of God*[^7].

**4.** But since whatever evil they do is kept, though they do not know it, when our God shall come manifest and shall not keep silence, he will call to himself all the nations, as he says in the Gospel, and will separate some, setting them on the right, some on the left, and will now begin to handle the treasures of both, that he may find what each has put in. *Come,* he says, *blessed of my Father,* to those who are on the right, *receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the beginning of the world*[^8]. The kingdom of heaven, the everlasting kingdom, the fellowship with the angels, the eternal life where no one is born or dies, this receive. For when you put your works into the treasury, you were buying the kingdom of heaven. *Receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the beginning of the world.* He even shows them his treasures: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink; I was naked, and you clothed me; I was a stranger, and you took me in; I was in prison, and you came to me; I was sick, and you visited me*[^9]. And they answer him, Lord, when did we see you placed in these necessities, and ministered to you? And he, *When you did it to one of my least, you did it to me*[^10]. Because, then, when you did it to one of my least you did it to me, receive what you sent, possess what you bought. For me, the keeper, you believed for this. Then he will turn also to those who are on the left, and will show them his treasures empty of good work: *Go,* he says, *into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels. I was hungry, and you did not give me to eat*[^11]. Or, if you found anything in this treasury, or put in anything, consider, and it will be rendered to you. But, they themselves say, we never saw you hungry. And he, *When you did not do it to one of my least, neither did you do it to me*[^12]. For perhaps you did not do it to me for this reason, that you did not see me walking on earth. You are so evil that, if you saw me, like the Jews you would crucify me. For today the evil men who try, if it can be, to abolish the commandments of God, who try, if it can be, that there be no Churches where the commandments of God may be preached to them—would they not kill Christ himself, if they found him living on earth? But they will dare to say, as though to one who does not know the thoughts of men, *Lord, when did we see you hungry?*[^13] And he, *When you did not do it to one of my least, neither did you do it to me.* My least, the needy, I had set for you on earth. I, as the head, he will say, sat in heaven at the right hand of the Father, but my members labored on earth, my members were in need on earth. You would have given to my members, and what you gave would have reached the head. And you would have known that, when I set my least, the needy, on earth for you, I appointed them as porters for you to carry your works into my treasury. And you put nothing in their hands; therefore with me you found nothing.

**5.** Then, therefore, he will not keep silence, but will appear; therefore it was said, *He shall not keep silence*[^14]. But now the reader says it from the codex and it is despised; the expounder or disputant bishop says it from his own mouth, and it is mocked. Will it be so mocked, when it shall be said by the most powerful judge himself? Each one will receive what he did, whether good or evil. Then people will say, with a late and unfruitful repentance, "Oh, if we could live again and hear and do what we despised!" Then those will say, whom their iniquities will set over against them, as it says in the book of Wisdom, *What did pride profit us, and what did the boasting of riches confer on us? All have passed away like a shadow*[^15]. You see that they will repent, but this repentance is one of torment, not of healing. Do you wish to have a useful repentance? Have it now. For if you have it now, you will be corrected. When you have been corrected, that treasure of yours where your evil works were gathered will be poured out, and that other treasure of yours will be filled where all your good works are gathered. But perhaps, converted to God, you will die at once, and perhaps none of your works will be found in that treasury? You will plainly find your works there, because it is written, *Peace on earth to men of good will*[^16]. God does not note the ability, but crowns the will. He knows that you wished and could not; so he notes you, as though you had done what you wished. Therefore there is need that you be converted, lest by deferring you suddenly die, and nothing at all be found which you may have in the present and possess in the future. Turned to God.
