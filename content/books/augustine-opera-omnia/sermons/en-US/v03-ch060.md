# Sermon 93: On the Words of the Gospel, Matthew 25:1-13: "the Kingdom of Heaven Will Be Like Ten Virgins," and the Rest

**1. 1.** You who were present yesterday hold our promise: which today, with the Lord's help, is to be paid not to you alone, but also to the many more who have gathered. Who the ten virgins are, of whom five are prudent and five foolish, cannot easily be tracked out. Nevertheless, according to what the very reading contains, which today too I wanted to be read out to your Charity, as far as the Lord deigns to grant me understanding, this parable or comparison does not seem to me to pertain only to those who, by a proper and more excellent holiness, are named virgins in the Church, whom by a more usual term we are accustomed to call nuns: but, unless I am mistaken, this comparison pertains to the universal Church. Although even if we understood only those who are called nuns, are they ten? Far be it that so great a multitude of virgins be reduced to so small a number. But perhaps someone may say: What if there are many in name, and so few in truth, that scarcely ten are found? It is not so. For if he wanted only ten to be understood as good, he would not there show five as foolish. For if the virgins who are called are many, why are the doors of the great house closed against five?

**2. 2.** Let us understand, then, dearest ones, that this parable pertains to all of us, that is, to the wholly universal Church, not to the superiors alone, of whom we spoke yesterday; nor to the common people alone; but altogether to all. Why, then, five and five virgins? These five and five virgins are altogether all the souls of Christians. But, that we may tell you what we feel by God's inspiration, not just any souls, but such souls as have the Catholic faith, and seem to have good works in the Church of God: and yet of these five are prudent, and five foolish. Why, then, are they called five, and why virgins, let us first see; and then let us consider the rest. Every soul in a body is reckoned by the number five, because it uses five senses. For there is nothing that we perceive from the body, except by a five-part gate, either by seeing, or hearing, or smelling, or tasting, or touching. He, then, who abstains from unlawful sight, from unlawful hearing, from unlawful smell, from unlawful taste, from unlawful touch, on account of that very integrity, has received the name of virgin.

**2. 3.** But if it is good to abstain from unlawful movements of perceiving, and therefore each Christian soul has received the name of virgin; why are five admitted, and five repelled? They are both virgins, and they are repelled. It is too little that they are virgins: they also have lamps. Virgins, on account of abstinence from unlawful senses; they have lamps, on account of good works. Of which works the Lord says: *Let your works shine before men, that they may see your good deeds, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^1]. Likewise he says to the disciples: *Let your loins be girded, and your lamps burning*[^2]. In the girded loins, virginity: in the burning lamps, good works.

**3. 4.** Virginity is not usually said, of course, of married people: yet even there is the virginity of faith, which displays conjugal chastity. For, that your Holiness may know that not unfittingly, according to the soul and according to the integrity of faith—by which faith one abstains from unlawful things, and good works are done—each man or each woman, each soul, is called a virgin; the whole Church, which consists of virgins and boys, and married women and wived men, is called by one name a virgin. Whence do we prove this? Hear the Apostle saying, not to nuns alone, but to the wholly universal Church: *I have betrothed you to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ*[^3]. And because the corrupter of this virginity, the devil, must be guarded against, at once the Apostle himself, when he had said: *I have betrothed you to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ;* added and said: *But I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his cunning, so also your minds be corrupted from the chastity that is in Christ*[^4]. In body few have virginity: in heart all ought to have it. If, then, abstinence from unlawful things is good, whence virginity received its name, and good works are praiseworthy, which are signified by the lamps; why are five admitted, and five repelled? If she is both a virgin, and carries lamps, and yet is not admitted; where does he see himself who neither keeps virginity from unlawful things, nor, unwilling to have good works, walks in the darkness?

**4. 5.** Of these, then, my brothers, of these rather let us discourse. He who does not want to see what is evil, who does not want to hear what is evil, who turns his smell away from the unlawful reeks of sacrifices, turns his taste away from the unlawful foods of sacrifices, flees the embrace of another's wife, breaks bread for the hungry, brings a stranger into his house, clothes the naked, reconciles the quarrelsome, visits the sick, buries the dead: behold, a virgin, behold, he has lamps. What more do we seek? I still seek. What do you still seek? he says. I still seek: the holy Gospel has made me attentive. Even these very virgins and carriers of lamps, some he called prudent, some foolish. By what do we discern them? by what do we distinguish them? By the oil. The oil signifies something great, very great. Do you think it is not love? We say this in seeking, we do not rush to a verdict. Why love seems to me to be signified by the oil, I will tell you. The Apostle says: *I show you a still more eminent way*[^5]. What more eminent way does he show? *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal*[^6]. This is the eminent way, that is, love, which is rightly signified by the oil. For oil rises above all liquids. Put water, and pour oil over it, the oil rises above. Put oil, pour water over it, the oil rises above. If you keep the order, it wins: if you change the order, it wins. *Love never falls*[^7].

**5. 6.** What, then, brothers? Now let us discuss the five prudent virgins and the five foolish. They wanted to go to meet the bridegroom. What is it, to go to meet the bridegroom? To go with the heart, to await his coming. But he was delaying. While he delays, *they all slept.* What is it: *all?* Both the foolish and the wise, *they all dozed off, and slept*[^8]. Do we think this sleep is good? What is this sleep? Lest perhaps, the bridegroom delaying, because iniquity abounds, the love of many grows cold. Are we to understand this sleep so? It does not please me: I say why. Because there are prudent ones there: and surely when the Lord said: *Because iniquity has abounded, the love of many grows cold;* he subjoined and said: *But he who perseveres unto the end, this one will be saved*[^9]. Where do you want these prudent ones to be? Is it not among those who persevered unto the end? For no other reason, brothers, no other reason at all would they be admitted within, except that they persevered unto the end. The coldness of love, then, did not creep over them, love did not grow cold in them; but it is fervent unto the end. Because it is fervent unto the end, therefore the doors of the bridegroom opened: therefore it was said to them to enter, as to that best servant: *Enter into the joy of your Lord*[^10]. What, then, is it: *They all dozed off?* There is another sleep, which no one escapes. Do you not remember the Apostle saying: *But concerning those who sleep I do not want you to be ignorant, brothers*[^11], that is, concerning those who are dead? For why are they called sleeping, except that on their own day they are raised up again? Therefore *they all slept.* Do you think that because she is prudent, therefore she does not have to die? Be a foolish virgin, be a prudent virgin, all suffer the sleep of death.

**6. 7.** But sometimes men say to themselves: Behold, now the day of judgment comes, so great evils are happening, so great tribulations grow frequent; behold, all the things the Prophets said are almost completed; now the day of judgment is at hand. Those who say this, and say it faithfully, as it were go to meet the bridegroom with such thoughts. But behold, war upon war, tribulation upon tribulation, earthquake upon earthquake, famine upon famine, nation upon nation, and the bridegroom has not yet come. When, then, it is expected that he come, all those sleep who say: Behold, he comes, and the day of judgment finds us here. While he says it, he sleeps. Therefore let him keep watch unto his own sleep, and let him persevere in love unto his own sleep: let sleep find him awaiting him. For suppose he has slept. *Shall not he who sleeps add to it that he rise again*[^12]? Therefore *they all slept:* both of those prudent ones and of those foolish ones, all slept.

**7. 8.** *Behold, at midnight a cry was made*[^13]. What is it, *at midnight?* When it is not expected, when it is not at all believed. He put night for ignorance. Someone, as it were, reckons for himself: Behold, from Adam so many years have passed, and behold, six thousand years are completed, and at once, as certain interpreters have reckoned, at once the day of judgment will come: and the reckonings come and pass, and still the coming of the bridegroom is delayed, and the virgins who had gone to meet him sleep. And behold, while it is not expected, while it is said, Six thousand years were awaited, and behold, they have passed, whence do we now know when he will come? at midnight he will come. What is it, At midnight he will come? While you do not know, he will come. Why, while you do not know, will he come? Hear the Lord himself: *It is not yours to know the times, which the Father has put in his own power*[^14]. *The day of the Lord,* says the Apostle, *will come like a thief in the night*[^15]. Therefore watch in the night, lest you suffer the thief. For the sleep of death, whether you will or not, will come.

**7. 9.** But when the cry has been made at midnight. What is this cry, except that of which the Apostle says: *In the twinkling of an eye, at the last trumpet? For the trumpet will sound, and the dead will rise incorrupt, and we will be changed*[^16]. Finally, with the cry made at midnight, by which it will be cried out: *Behold, the bridegroom comes,* what follows? *They all arose.* What is it: *They all arose? The hour will come,* the Lord himself said, *when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice, and will come forth*[^17]. Therefore at the last trumpet they all arose. *But those prudent ones took oil with them in their vessels: but those foolish ones did not take oil with them*[^18]. What is it, *they did not take oil with them in their vessels?* What is it, *in their vessels?* In their hearts. Whence the Apostle says: *This is our glory, the testimony of our conscience*[^19]. There is the oil, great oil: this oil is of the gift of God. Finally, men can put oil within, they cannot create the olive. Behold, I have oil: did you create the oil? It is of the gift of God. You have oil, carry it with you. What is it, carry it with you? Have it within, there please God.

**8. 10.** For behold, these foolish virgins, who did not carry oil with them, by their abstinence by which they are called virgins, and by their good works, when they are seen to carry lamps, want to please men. And if they want to please men, and therefore do all these praiseworthy things, they do not carry oil with them. You, then, carry it with you, carry it within, where God sees: there carry the testimony of your conscience. But he who walks by another's testimony does not carry oil with him. If you therefore abstain from unlawful things, and do good works, in order to be praised by men; the oil is not within. Finally, when men begin not to praise, the lamps fail. Let your Charity, therefore, be attentive. Before those virgins slept, it is not said that their lamps were being extinguished. The lamps of the wise burned from the inner oil, from the security of conscience, from inner glory, from inmost love. Yet the lamps of those foolish ones also burned. Why did they burn then? Because the praises of men were not lacking. But after they arose, that is, in the resurrection from the dead, they began to trim their lamps, that is, to prepare to render to God an account of their works. And because then there is no one who praises, every man is occupied with his own case, there is no one then who does not think about himself: there were not, therefore, any who would sell oil; the lamps began to fail, and the foolish turned themselves to the five prudent: *Give us of your oil, because our lamps are being extinguished*[^20]. They were seeking what they were accustomed to, that is, to shine by another's oil, to walk by another's praises. *Give us of your oil, because our lamps are being extinguished.*

**8. 11.** But they: *Lest perhaps,* they say, *there be not enough for us and for you, go rather to those who sell, and buy for yourselves*[^21]. This is the answer not of counselors, but of mockers. Why of mockers? Because they were wise, because wisdom was in them. For they were not wise of themselves: but that wisdom was in them, of which it is written in a certain book, which says to its despisers, when they have come to the evils that it threatened them: *I too will laugh over your perdition*[^22]. What wonder is it that the wise mock the foolish? What is it to mock?

**9. 12.** *Go to those who sell, and buy for yourselves:* you who were not accustomed to live well, except because men praised you, who sold you oil. What is it, sold oil? They sold praises. Who sell praises, but flatterers? How much better would you not yield to flatterers, and carry oil within, and do all good works for the sake of a good conscience: then you would say: *The just man will amend me in mercy, and reprove me; but the oil of the sinner will not fatten my head*[^23]. Better, he says, let the just man amend me, let the just man reprove me, let the just man cuff me, let the just man correct me, than that the oil of the sinner fatten my head. What is the oil of the sinner, but the blandishments of a flatterer?

**9. 13.** *Go,* then, *to those who sell;* this you were accustomed to do. But we do not give to you. Why? *Lest perhaps there be not enough for us and for you.* What is it, *not be enough?* It is said not out of despair, but out of sober and devout humility. For although the good man has a good conscience; whence does he know how that one judges who is deceived by no one? He has a good conscience, crimes conceived in the heart do not tickle him; but on account of certain daily sins of human life, although the conscience be good, yet he says to God: *Forgive us our debts:* because he has done what follows: *As we too forgive our debtors*[^24]. He broke bread for the hungry from the heart, he clothed the naked from the heart: from inner oil he did good works, and yet in that judgment that very good conscience trembles.

**9. 14.** See what it is: *Give us oil.* They heard: *Go rather to those who sell*[^25]. Because you were accustomed to live well by human praises, you do not carry oil with you: but we do not give it; *lest perhaps there be not enough for us and for you.* For we scarcely judge about ourselves, how much less can we judge about you? What is it, We scarcely judge about ourselves? Because when the just king sits on his throne, who will boast that he has a chaste heart[^26]? Perhaps you find nothing in your conscience; and he finds it who sees better, whose divine gaze penetrates the deeper things: he perhaps sees something, finds something. How much better do you say to him: *Enter not into judgment with your servant*[^27]? Indeed, how much better do you say: *Forgive us our debts*[^28]? Because it is said to you too, on account of those torches, those lamps: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^29]. What then? did those women not do that? They did not do it before him. But how did they do it? As the Lord forbids, who said: *Beware of doing your righteousness before men, to be seen by them: otherwise you will have no reward with your Father who is in heaven. And do not be like the hypocrites when you pray. For they love to stand in the streets and pray, to be seen by men. Amen I say to you, they have received their reward*[^30]. They bought oil, they gave a price: they bought, they were not cheated of human praises; they sought human praises, they had them. Those human praises do not help them on the day of judgment. But how did those others do it? *Let your works shine before men, that they may see your good deeds, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^31]. He did not say: You. For you do not have the oil from yourself. Boast yourself, and say, I have it: but from him. For what do you have that you have not received[^32]? Therefore those women did so, those women so.

**9. 15.** But it is not to be wondered at, *when they go to buy,* when they seek those by whom they may be praised, and find none; when they seek those by whom they may be consoled, and find none; the door is opened, *the bridegroom comes* and the bride—then the Church glorified with Christ, that the individual members may gather themselves to the whole. *And they entered with him to the wedding, and the door was shut*[^33]. And those foolish ones came afterward: but did they buy oil, or did they find any from whom they might buy? Therefore they found the doors shut: they began to knock, but too late.

**10. 16.** It was said, it is true, it was not said deceptively: *Knock, and it will be opened to you*[^34]: but now while it is the time of mercy, not when it is the time of judgment. For these times cannot be confounded, since the Church sings mercy and judgment to her Lord[^35]. It is the time of mercy; do penance. Are you to do it in the time of judgment? You will be among those virgins against whom the door was shut. *Lord, Lord, open to us*[^36]. Did they not do penance, because they did not carry oil with them? And what did late penance profit them, when true wisdom mocked them? *The door,* then, *was shut.* And what was said to them? *I do not know you*[^37]. Does he not know them, who knows all things? What, then, is it: *I do not know you?* I disapprove of you, I reprobate you. In my art I do not recognize you; my art does not know vices: but this is the great thing, that it both does not know vices, and judges vices. It does not know them by doing them, it judges them by reproving them. So, then, *I do not know you.*

**10. 17.** The five prudent went, they entered. How many you are, my brothers, in the name of Christ; let there be in you five prudent ones, but do not be five men. Let there be in you five prudent ones, pertaining to that prudence of the number five. For the hour will come, and when we know not, it will come. At midnight it will come, watch. So the Gospel closed: *Watch, because you do not know the day nor the hour*[^38]. If, then, we are to sleep, how do we watch? Watch in heart, watch in faith, watch in hope, watch in love, watch in works: and when you have slept in body, the time will come for you to rise. But when you have risen, prepare your lamps. Then let them not be extinguished, then let them be quickened by the inner oil of conscience: then let that bridegroom embrace you with incorporeal bonds, then let him bring you into the house where you may never sleep, where your lamp may never be extinguished. But today we toil, and our lamps waver amid the winds and temptations of this world: but let our flame burn in strength, so that the wind of temptation may rather increase the fire than extinguish it.
