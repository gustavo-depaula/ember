# Sermon 72/A: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 12:41: "Behold, a Greater Than Jonah Is Here," and What Follows

**1.** The things that have been read from the holy Gospel, my brothers, if we desire to treat them all, the time scarcely suffices for the single ones: how much less does it suffice for all. That the prophet Jonah, who, cast into the sea, was received into the belly of a sea-beast, and on the third day vomited out alive, bore the figure of the Savior, who suffered and on the third day rose again, the Savior himself shows. The people of the Jews were accused in comparison with the Ninevites, since the Ninevites, to whom the prophet Jonah was sent to be rebuked, by doing penance appeased the wrath of God and merited mercy: *And behold*, he says, *a greater than Jonah is here*[^1], the Lord Christ wishing himself to be understood. Those heard a servant, and corrected their ways; these heard the Lord, and not only did not correct themselves, but moreover killed him. *The queen of the south will rise up*, he says, *in the judgment with this generation, and will condemn it; for she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and behold a greater than Solomon is here*[^2]. It was no great thing for Christ to be greater than Jonah, greater than Solomon: for he was the Lord, they were servants; but yet what kind of men are they who despised the Lord present, when foreigners heard his servants?

**2.** Then there follows: *But when the unclean spirit has gone out of a man, he walks through dry places seeking rest, and does not find it. Then he says: I will return into my house, whence I went out. And coming he finds it empty, swept with brooms, and adorned. Then he goes, and takes with him seven other spirits more wicked than himself, and entering they dwell there; and the last things of that man become worse than the first. So will it be for this most wicked generation*[^3]. That this may be understood, if it is fittingly expounded, the sermon will linger long; yet I will touch on it briefly, as far as the Lord grants, lest I dismiss you fasting from the understanding of this matter. When in the sacraments the remission of sins takes place, the house is cleansed; but a dweller is necessary, the Holy Spirit; but the Holy Spirit dwells only in the humble of heart. For God says: *Upon whom will my Spirit rest?* And he answers his own proposal: *Upon the humble, and the quiet, and the one who trembles at my words*[^4]. So when he is the dweller, he fills, rules, acts, bridles from evils, rouses to good things, makes justice sweet, that a man do good from love of the right, not from fear of punishment. This which I have said, a man is less fit to do by himself; but if he has the Holy Spirit as dweller, he finds him in all good things his helper too. But certain proud men, their sins forgiven them, if they presume to live well by the free choice of the human will alone, by that very pride exclude from themselves the Holy Spirit; and the house remains as it were cleansed of sins, but empty of all good things. Your sins are forgiven, you are rid of evils; but only the Holy Spirit will fill you with good things. Him pride repels. You presume on yourself, he leaves you; you trust in yourself, you are given over to yourself. But that craving, by which you were bad, repelled from the man, that is from your mind, when your sins were forgiven, wanders through deserts seeking rest; and not finding rest that craving returns to the house, finds it cleansed, brings with it seven other spirits more wicked than itself; and the last things of that man will be worse than the first were. It brings seven others with it. What is: *seven others?* Is then the unclean spirit himself sevenfold? What is this? By seven, totality is signified. He had gone whole, he comes whole; and would that he came alone! What is: *he brings with him seven others?* Those whom he did not have, when he was bad, he will have as others, falsely good. Attend, that, if I can, as much as I am helped, I may explain what I say. The Holy Spirit is commended by a sevenfold working, that there may be in us *the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, of counsel and fortitude, of knowledge and godliness, and the fear of God*[^5]. To this sevenfold good set, from the contrary, a sevenfold evil: the spirit of folly and error, the spirit of rashness and cowardice, the spirit of ignorance and impiety, and the spirit of pride against the fear of God. These are the seven wicked spirits: who are the seven others more wicked? The seven others more wicked are found in hypocrisy; one evil spirit of folly, another worse the simulation of wisdom; an evil spirit the spirit of error, another worse the simulation of truth; an evil spirit the spirit of rashness, another worse the simulation of counsel; an evil spirit the spirit of cowardice, another worse the simulation of fortitude; an evil spirit the spirit of ignorance, another worse the simulation of knowledge; an evil spirit the spirit of impiety, another worse the simulation of godliness; an evil spirit the spirit of elation, another worse the simulation of fear. Seven were not borne: who would bear fourteen? It is necessary, then, that, when to malice the simulation of truth has been added, the last things of the man be worse than the first were.

**3.** *As he was saying these things to the crowds*—I follow the Gospel—*his mother and his brothers stood outside, wishing to speak with him. A certain one announced to him, saying: Behold, your mother and your brothers are outside, they wish to speak with you. And he: Who is my mother? or who are my brothers? And stretching out his hand over his disciples he said: These are my mother and my brothers. And whoever does the will of my Father who is in heaven, he is to me brother, sister, and mother*[^6]. From this alone I would wish to speak; but, because I was unwilling to pass over the earlier things, I have, as I judge, consumed no small part of the time. For this which I have just now set forth has many folds and knots of question; how the Lord Christ piously despised his mother, not any mother whatever, but, the more a virgin mother, the more such a mother, to whom he so brought fecundity that he did not take away integrity: a mother conceiving a virgin, bearing a virgin, remaining a virgin perpetually. Such a mother he despised, lest a maternal affection insert itself into the work he was doing, and hinder him. For what was he doing? He was speaking to the peoples, destroying old men, building up new, freeing souls, loosing the bound, enlightening blind minds, doing a good work, fervent in a good work by act and word. Amid these things a carnal affection was announced to him. You have heard what he answered: why should I repeat it? Let mothers hear, lest by carnal affection they hinder the good works of their sons. For if they wish to hinder, and so rush in upon those acting that they at least interrupt what ought not to be deferred, they will be despised by their sons; I dare to say, they will be despised, by godliness despised. And when will the woman be angry—whether married or widow—when a son is intent in mind on a good work, and therefore despises a mother coming, when the Virgin Mary was despised? But you will say to me: "So do you compare my son to Christ?" I neither compare him to Christ, nor you to Mary. So the Lord Christ did not condemn maternal affection, but showed in himself a great example of despising a mother for the work of God. Both in speaking he was a teacher, and in despising he was a teacher; and therefore he deigned to despise a mother, that he might teach you to despise even a father for the work of God.

**4.** For could not the Lord Christ become man without a mother, who could without a father? If it was fitting, indeed because it was fitting, that he become man for the sake of man, who made man, consider and recall whence he made the first man himself. The first man was made without father, without mother. What he could fit to the first instructing of human affairs, could he not afterward fit to himself some such thing for the repairing of human affairs? Was it hard for the Wisdom of God, the Word of God, the Power of God, the only-begotten Son of God, hard to make a man, whom he might fit to himself, from wherever he wished? Angels presented themselves as men to men. Abraham fed the holy angels, and invited them as men; nor only saw, but also touched them, for he washed their feet[^7]. Were those things done by the angels as if by sportive phantasms? If, then, an angel could present a human form, true, when he wished, could not the Lord of angels, from wherever he wished, make for himself a true man whom he might assume? But he was unwilling for the man to have a father, lest through carnal desire he come to men; yet he willed a mother, that he might have a mother among men, whom, by despising for the work of God, he might teach men. He willed to take on the male sex in himself, and deigned to honor the female sex in his mother. For of old both the woman had sinned, and had given sin to the man to drink; both partners of the marriage were deceived by the fraud of the devil. If Christ came a man without the commendation of the female sex, women would despair of themselves, especially because through her man fell: he honored both, commended both, took up both. He was born of a woman. Do not despair, men: Christ deigned to be a man. Do not despair, women: Christ deigned to be born of a woman. To the salvation of Christ let each sex hasten: let the male come, let the female come—in faith there is neither male nor female[^8]. So Christ teaches you to despise your parents, to love your parents. For then you love your parents in order and piously, when you do not prefer your parents to God: *He who loves*—they are the words of the Lord—*he who loves father or mother more than me is not worthy of me*[^9]. By these words he seems, as it were, to have admonished you not to love: rather, if you attend, he admonished you to love. For he could have said: "He who loves father or mother is not worthy of me." He did not say this, lest he speak against the law which he gave, for he himself gave that law through his servant Moses, where it is written: *Honor your father and your mother*[^10]. He did not promulgate a contrary law, but commended that one; and he taught you order, he did not subvert godliness: *He who loves father or mother*, but *more than me.* Let him love, then, but not more than me: God is God, man is man. Love your parents, obey your parents, honor your parents; but, if God calls you to something greater, where parental affection can be a hindrance, keep the order, do not overturn love.

**5.** In so great a truth of the teaching of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, who would believe that the Manicheans sought a slander, by which they tried to assert that the Lord Jesus Christ had no mother at all? For this they savor, rather are foolish in, that the Lord Jesus did not have a human mother, against the Gospel, against the light of the Truth itself. And attend to what they argue from. Behold, they say, he himself says. What does he say? *Who is my mother? or who are my brothers?*[^11] He himself, they say, denies it, and do you wish to impose on him what he denies; he himself says: *Who is my mother, or who are my brothers*, and do you say: He has a mother? O fool, O contentious one, O deservedly hateful one! answer me, whence do you know that the Lord said: *Who is my mother, or who are my brothers?* You deny that Christ had a mother, and what you aim at, through this you try to teach, because he said: *Who is my mother, or my brothers?* If another should exist, and say that the Lord Christ did not say this at all, whence will you convict him? Answer, if you can, the man denying that Christ said this. Whence you will convict him, by that you are convicted. For did Christ himself indicate in your ear that he said this? Answer, that by your own mouth you may be convicted; answer, that you may convict that Christ said this. I know what he is going to say: "I will take the book, I will open the Gospel, I will recite his words written in the holy Gospel." Good, good: by the Gospel itself I will hold you, with the Gospel itself I will bind you, from the Gospel itself I will strangle you. In the Gospel itself recite what you think is for you: open, read. *Who is my mother?* Why he said this, read above. *A certain one announced to him: Behold, your mother and your brothers stand outside.* I do not yet press, I do not yet hold, I do not yet strangle; still you can say: That was a false messenger, he did not speak the truth, he suggested lies: therefore the Lord refuted the false messenger. For after his message he answered: *Who is my mother?* As if he said: "You say: Your mother stands outside; I say: *Who is my mother?* Whom, he says, do you wish us to believe? The messenger suggesting, or Christ rejecting what he had announced?" Hear, then, I still question you: only hold the Gospel, do not throw the book down behind you: hold it, give authority to the Gospel; which unless you give, there will be no way by which you may prove the Lord said: *Who is my mother?* But when you give worthy authority to the Gospel, see what I ask. A little before I had asked, whence you would know whether Christ said: *Who is my mother?* What had preceded? A certain messenger had said to Christ: *Your mother stands outside.* Before the messenger said this, or that the messenger might say this, what preceded? I compel you to read. I see you now fear to read. *The Lord answered, and said.* Who said it? I do not say: Who said: *who is my mother?* for you will answer, the Lord said it. *The Lord answered*, who said it? You will answer, the Evangelist said it. Did this Evangelist say a true thing, or a false thing? You are going to say: "What, a true thing or a false thing?" *The Lord answered, and said to him.* What the Evangelist said, did he say a true thing, or a false thing? If you are going to say that the Evangelist said a false thing, that the Lord answered; whence do you know that the Lord said *who is my mother?* But if for this reason you convict that the Lord said *who is my mother?*, because the Evangelist said that he said it, you do not convict that the Lord said this, unless you believe the Evangelist. Now if you believe the Evangelist (because you say nothing, if you do not believe the Evangelist), read above what the Evangelist himself said.

**6.** How long I make delays for you! how long I keep you in suspense! It is a kindness that you be quickly conquered. Attend, inspect, read. I see you are unwilling. Give the book, I read: *As he was saying these things to the crowds.* Who says this? The Evangelist, whom if you do not believe, Christ said nothing. If Christ said nothing, *who is my mother?* Christ did not say. But if *who is my mother?* Christ said, it is true what the Evangelist wrote. See what he said before: *As he was saying these things to the crowds, his mother and his brothers stood outside, wishing to speak with him.* That messenger, whom you can say lied, had not yet announced anything. See what he announced, attend to what the Evangelist set before: *As the Lord was saying these things to the crowds, his mother and his brothers stood outside.* Who says this? The Evangelist, whom you believe that the Lord said: *Who is my mother?* But if you do not believe these things said, as those, then the Lord did not say: *Who is my mother?* But truly the Lord said: *Who is my mother?* Believe, then, him who said that the Lord said: *Who is my mother?* For he who said that the Lord said: *Who is my mother?*, he himself said: *As he was saying these things, his mother stood outside.* Why, then, did he deny his mother? Far be it! understand: he did not deny her, but preferred to his mother something he was doing. Finally, the only reason is, to ask why the Lord said: *Who is my mother?* The first reason is, to see that he had one of whom he might say: *Who is my mother?* He had one: she stood outside, she wished to speak with him. Tell me, whence do you know? The Evangelist says it, whom if I do not believe, the Lord says nothing. So he had a mother. But what is: *Who is my mother?* For these things which I am doing, who is my mother? If you say to someone in peril, and having a father: "Let your father free you," whom that one knows is not fit to free his son, does he not answer you with the highest godliness, with whole truth: "What is my father? For this which I wish, for this which I now feel I have need of, what is my father?" So for that which Christ was doing, that he was loosing the bound, enlightening blind minds, building up inner men, fashioning for himself a spiritual temple, who is his mother? But, if you think for this reason that Christ did not have a mother on earth, because he said: *Who is my mother?*, neither did his disciples have fathers on earth, because the Lord himself said to them: *Do not call yourselves a father on earth.* They are the words of the Lord: *Do not call yourselves a father; for your one Father is God*[^12]. It is not that they did not have fathers: but where it comes to regeneration, let the Father of regeneration be sought; let not the father of generation be condemned, but let the Father of regeneration be preferred to him.

**7.** Behold, attend rather to that, my brothers, attend rather to that, I beseech you, which the Lord Christ said, stretching out his hand over his disciples: *These are my mother and my brothers; and whoever does the will of my Father who sent me, he is to me both brother and sister and mother*[^13]. Did not the Virgin Mary do the will of the Father, who by faith believed, by faith conceived, was chosen, from whom salvation might be born to us among men, was created by Christ before Christ was created in her? She did, holy Mary plainly did the will of the Father: and therefore it is more for Mary to have been a disciple of Christ than to have been the mother of Christ; it is more, it is more blessed to have been a disciple of Christ than to have been the mother of Christ. Therefore Mary was blessed, because, even before she bore him, she carried the Master in her womb. See whether it is not what I say. As the Lord passed by with crowds following, and doing divine miracles, a certain woman said: *Blessed the womb that bore you!*[^14] And the Lord, that happiness might not be sought in the flesh, what did he answer? *Rather, blessed are those who hear the word of God, and keep it*[^15]. Hence, then, Mary too was blessed, because she heard the word of God, and kept it: she kept the truth in her mind more than the flesh in her womb. The truth is Christ, the flesh is Christ: the truth Christ in the mind of Mary, the flesh Christ in the womb of Mary; what is in the mind is more than what is carried in the womb. Holy Mary, blessed Mary, but the Church is better than the Virgin Mary. Why? because Mary is a portion of the Church, a holy member, an excellent member, a supereminent member, but yet a member of the whole body. If a member of the whole body, surely the body is more than the member. The head is the Lord, and the whole Christ is head and body. What shall I say? We have a divine head, we have God as head.

**8.** So, dearly beloved, attend: you too are members of Christ, you too are the body of Christ. Attend to how you are what he says: *Behold my mother and my brothers.* How will you be the mother of Christ? *And whoever hears, and whoever does the will of my Father who is in heaven, he is to me brother, sister, and mother*[^16]. I understand brothers, I understand sisters: for there is one inheritance; and therefore the mercy of Christ, who, since he was the only one, was unwilling to be alone, willed us to be heirs of the Father, coheirs of himself. For such is that inheritance, which cannot be made narrow by the multitude of coheirs. I understand, then, that we are brothers of Christ, that holy and faithful women are sisters of Christ. How can we understand mothers of Christ? What then? do we dare to call ourselves mothers of Christ? Rather we dare to call ourselves mothers of Christ. For I said you are all his brothers, and would I not dare to call you his mother? But much less do I dare to deny what Christ said. Come, dearly beloved, attend how the Church is, what is manifest, the spouse of Christ; what is more difficult to understand, but yet is true, the mother of Christ. In her type the Virgin Mary went before. Whence, I ask you, is Mary the mother of Christ, except because she bore the members of Christ? You, to whom I speak, are members of Christ: who bore you? I hear the voice of your heart: "Mother Church." This mother, holy, honored, like Mary, both bears and is a virgin. That she bears, I prove through you: of her you are born; and she bears Christ, for you are the members of Christ. I have proved her bearing, I will prove her a virgin: the divine testimony does not desert me, does not desert me. Come forth to the people, blessed Paul, be a witness of my assertion; cry out, and say what I wish to say: *I betrothed you, to present you as a chaste virgin to one husband, Christ*[^17]. Where is that virginity? where is corruption feared? Let him say it, who said "virgin." *I betrothed you, to present you as a chaste virgin to one husband, Christ; but I fear*, he says, *lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his cunning, so your minds too*, he says, *be corrupted from the chastity that is in Christ*[^18]. Hold virginity in your minds: the virginity of the mind is the integrity of the catholic faith; where Eve was corrupted by the speech of the serpent, there the Church ought to be a virgin by the gift of the Almighty. So let them bear the members of Christ in the mind, as Mary, a virgin, bore Christ in the womb; and so you will be mothers of Christ. It is not far from you, it is not beyond you, it does not shrink from you: you were sons, be mothers too. Sons of the mother, when you were baptized, then you were born members of Christ: bring to the laver of baptism whom you can; that, as you were sons when you were born, so also, by leading others to be born, you may be able to be mothers of Christ.
