# Sermon 66: On the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 11:2-11: "When John Had Heard in Chains the Works of Christ," and What Follows

**1.** The reading of the holy Gospel set before us a question about John the Baptist. May the Lord help us to resolve it for you, as he resolved it for us. John was praised by the testimony of Christ, as you heard; and so praised that among those born of women none greater had risen. But greater than he was the one born of the Virgin[^1]. How much greater? Let the herald himself say how much difference there is between himself and his judge, whose herald he is. For John preceded Christ, both by being born and by preaching: but he preceded by serving, not by preferring himself. For the whole retinue too walks before the judge; and those who walk first are themselves the lesser. How great, then, was the testimony John bore to Christ? So great that he said he was not worthy to loose the strap of his sandal[^2]. What further? *We*, he says, *have received of his fullness*[^3]. He confessed himself a lamp kindled from him, and therefore fled to his feet, lest, seeking the heights, he be extinguished by the wind of pride. For he was so great that he was thought to be Christ: and if he were not his own witness that he was not himself, the error would remain, and he would be thought to be Christ. How humble! Honor was offered him by the people, and he himself spurned it. Men erred about his greatness, and he humbled himself. He did not wish to grow by the words of men, because he had grasped the Word of God.

**2.** This, then, John of Christ: Christ of John, what? Just now we heard: *He began to say to the crowds about John: What did you go out into the desert to see? a reed shaken by the wind?*[^4] Surely not; because John was not blown about by every wind of doctrine. *But what did you go out to see? a man clothed in soft garments?*[^5] No; because John was clothed in rough clothing: for he had a garment of camel hair, not of feathers. *But what did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes: and more than a prophet*[^6]. Why more than a prophet? They foretold the Lord who was to come, whom they desired to see, and did not see: but to this man was granted what they sought. John saw the Lord, saw him; he stretched out his finger toward him, and said: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sins of the world*[^7]: behold, he is here. He had already come, and was not recognized: and therefore in John himself there was error. Behold, he is here whom the Patriarchs desired to see, whom the Prophets foretold, whom the Law foreshadowed. *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sins of the world.* And he bore good testimony to the Lord, and the Lord to him: *Among those born of women*, says the Lord, *none has risen greater than John the Baptist: but he who is lesser in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he*[^8]; lesser in time, greater in majesty. Wishing himself to be understood, he said this. John is very great among men, than whom alone among men Christ is greater. It can also be distinguished and resolved thus: *Among those born of women none has risen greater than John the Baptist: but he who is lesser in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he.* Not as I said a little before. *But he who is lesser in the kingdom of heaven is greater:* he said the kingdom of heaven, where the angels are; so he who is lesser among the angels is greater than John. He commended the kingdom that we may desire it: he set forth the city whose citizens we long to be. What citizens are there? how great the citizens! Whoever is lesser there is greater than John. Than what John? Than whom none greater has risen among those born of women.

**3.** We have heard the true and good testimony both of John about Christ, and of Christ about John. What, then, does it mean, that John sent his disciples to him, when he was shut up in prison, now to be killed, and said to his disciples: *Go, say to him: Are you he who is to come, or do we await another?*[^9] Is this the whole praise? Has that praise become doubt? What do you say, John? to whom do you say it? what do you say? To the judge you say it, the herald you say it. You stretched out your finger, you showed him: you said: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sins of the world*[^10]. You said: *We have all received of his fullness*[^11]: you said: *I am not worthy to loose the strap of his sandal*[^12]. And now you say: *Are you he who comes, or do we await another?* Is he himself not the one? Who are you? Are you not his forerunner? Are you not the one of whom it was foretold: *Behold, I send my angel before your face, and he will prepare your way?*[^13] How do you prepare the way, and you err? So the disciples of John came: and the Lord to them: *Go, say to John: The blind see, the deaf hear, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the poor are evangelized: and blessed is he who is not scandalized in me*[^14]. Do not suspect that John was scandalized in Christ. And yet the words seem to sound thus: *Are you he who comes?* Question the works: *The blind see, the deaf hear, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the dead rise, the poor are evangelized:* and do you ask whether it is I? My works, he says, are my words. *Go, report back. And as they departed* ... Lest perhaps someone say: John was good at first, and the Spirit of God deserted him; therefore he said these things after their departure; after the departure of those whom John had sent, Christ praised John.

**4.** What, then, does the obscure question mean? Let the sun shine, from which that lamp was kindled. So utterly is the solution evident a solution. John had disciples apart: not separated, but a witness prepared. For it was fitting that such a one bear testimony to Christ, who himself too was gathering disciples, who could envy him, if he could not see. So because the disciples of John held their master John as a great thing; they heard from John the testimony about Christ, and marveled: about to die, he wished them to be confirmed by him. For without doubt they said among themselves: This man says such great things about him, he himself says these things not about himself. *Go, say to him:* not because I doubt, but that you may be instructed. *Go, say to him:* what I am wont to say, hear from him; you have heard the herald, be confirmed by the judge. *Go, say to him: Are you he who comes, or do we await another?* They went, and said it; for their own sake, not for John's. And for their sake Christ said: *The blind see, the lame walk, the deaf hear, the lepers are cleansed, the dead rise, the poor are evangelized.* You see me, recognize me: you see the deeds, recognize the doer. *And blessed is he who is not scandalized in me.* But I say this of you, not of John. For that we might know he did not say it of John: *As they departed he began to say to the crowds about John:* the truthful one, the Truth, spoke his true praises.

**5.** I think this question has been sufficiently resolved. Let it suffice, then, to have brought the sermon to its resolution. Have the poor in mind: act, you who have not yet acted: believe, you lose nothing; rather, this alone you lose, what you do not bring to the carriage. Now what you offered must be repaid to the poor, you who offered: and we have much less toward the total than you are wont to offer: shake off sloth. I have become a beggar of beggars: what is that to me? Let me be a beggar of beggars, that you may be numbered among the sons.
