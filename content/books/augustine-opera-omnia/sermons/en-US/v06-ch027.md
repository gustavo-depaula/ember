# Sermon 206: In Lent

**1.** By the yearly return of Lent the time has come on which our exhortation is owed to you: because you too owe to the Lord your works suited to the time: which yet can be useful not to the Lord, but to you. With prayers, fasts, alms, the other times too ought to be fervent for the Christian: yet this solemnity ought to rouse those who on other days are sluggish in these things; and those who on other days are eager for them ought now to exercise them more fervently. For the time of our humility is the life in this age, which these days signify, with the Lord Christ having suffered for us by dying once, as though about to suffer in the solemnity rolled round every year. For what was done once in the whole of time, that our life might be renewed, is celebrated every year, that it may be called to memory. If, then, with the affection of most true piety, throughout the whole time of this pilgrimage, in which we live amid temptations, we ought to be humble in heart: how much more on these days, in which we not only enact, by living, the very time of our humility, but also signify it, by celebrating? The humility of Christ taught us to be humble, because by dying he yielded to the ungodly: the loftiness of Christ makes us lofty, because by rising he went before the pious. *For if we have died with him*, says the Apostle, *we shall also live with him: if we endure, we shall also reign with him*[^1]. One of these now, as though his passion drawing near; the other after Easter, as though his resurrection completed, we celebrate with due devotion. For then, after the days of this humility, even the time of our loftiness, though it is not yet free to be enacted by seeing, is yet a delight to signify by premeditating. Now therefore let us groan in prayers more urgently: then we shall be more abundantly gladdened in praises.

**2.** But to our prayers, that by flying they may more easily reach God, let us add the feathers of piety, alms and fasts. From this the Christian mind understands how far it ought to be removed from the fraud of another's property; when it feels that it is like fraud, if it does not bestow its superfluities on the needy. The Lord says: *Give, and it will be given you; forgive, and it will be forgiven you*[^2]. These two kinds of alms, of giving and of forgiving, let us perform clemently and fervently, who pray to the Lord that goods may be bestowed on us, and evils not repaid us. *Give*, he says, *and it will be given you*. What is truer, what more just, than that he who refuses to give should cheat himself, and not receive? If shamelessly the farmer seeks a harvest where he knows he has not first sent the seed; how much more shamelessly does he seek of God, rich and giving, who was unwilling to hear the poor man asking? For in the poor man he willed to be fed, who does not hunger. Let us not, then, spurn our God in need in the poor, that being needy we may be satisfied in the rich one. We have the needy, and we are in need: let us give, then, that we may receive. Yet what is it that we give? And for this small, visible, temporal, and earthly thing, what is it that we desire to receive? *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man*[^3]. Unless he himself promised, it would be shameless to give these things and to wish to receive those: and unwilling to give even these: which yet, even these things, we would not have, unless he gave them who exhorts us to give. With what brow, then, do we hope in both for the giver, if in the least things we despise the commander? *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you*[^4]. That is, Pardon, and it will be pardoned you. Let fellow servant be reconciled to fellow servant; lest the servant be justly punished by the Lord[^5]. In this kind of alms no one is poor. He can do this so as to live forever, even he who, for the present, has not whence to live. It is given freely, it is heaped up by giving, and is not consumed except when it is not bestowed. Of whom, then, the enmities have lasted up to these days, let them be confounded and ended. Let them be ended, lest they end you: let them not be held, lest they hold you: let them be slain through the Redeemer, lest they slay the one who holds them.

**3.** Let your fasts not be such as the prophet condemns, saying: *This is not the fast I have chosen, says the Lord*[^6]. For he reproves the fasts of the quarrelsome: he seeks those of the pious. He reproves the oppressing: he seeks the releasing. He reproves the hostile: he seeks the liberating. For on this account, through these days, you rein in your desires from lawful things, that you may not commit unlawful ones. Let no one gorge himself, on any day, with wine, or with adultery, who on these days abstains from marriage. So our prayer, by humility and love, by fasting and giving, by self-restraint and forgiving, by bestowing goods and not repaying evils, by turning away from evil and doing good, seeks peace and obtains it[^7]. For prayer flies, supported by such feathers of virtues: and whither Christ our peace went before, it is more easily carried up to heaven.

[^1]: 2 Tim 2:11-12
[^2]: Luke 6:38,37
[^3]: 1 Cor 2:9
[^4]: Luke 6:37
[^5]: See Matt 18:23-35
[^6]: See Isa 58:5
[^7]: See Ps 34:14 (33:15)
