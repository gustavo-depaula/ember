# Sermon 298A

**1.** Let us hear, dearest brothers, this fisherman, chief of all the apostles, who through his faithful answer deserved to be the gatekeeper of the kingdom of heaven. He received the power to loose and bind because he discovered the Lord's hidden majesty. In his human person, Christ the Lord asks his disciples what popular opinion thinks of him, saying: *Who do people say that I, the Son of man, am?* But Peter, set aflame by the divine Spirit, answered not what he had been asked but what had been inspired from above: *You are*, he says, *the Christ, the Son of the living God.* The Lord says to him: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, because flesh and blood has not revealed this to you, but my Father who is in heaven.* Human perception and fleshly wisdom cannot behold the mystery of the Incarnation and the height of so profound a sacrament unless it is revealed by the Lord. See, brothers: the humble, poor apostle discovered the Lord's unsearchable riches by confessing the glory of Christ's name; he discovered heavenly riches by believing, not by disputing. For he always believed and never disputed, and his faith deserved such great grace that he even followed the Lord across the sea. When would he have trodden the waves if he had deliberated with human reason? When would he have pressed the footsteps of solid flesh upon the liquid element unless he had believed the Lord's words? He did not dispute, then, but believed. That is why God's deep mysteries were revealed to him. The Lord revealed the ineffable mysteries of faith to his disciples and made known to them the fragrance of the knowledge of him. The Lord revealed himself to fishermen so that the whole world might be caught in the Gospel's nets. See: everywhere fishermen teach wisdom, while grammarians and orators go astray following foolishness. Let us seek the Lord in simplicity of heart, dearest brothers, and believe that his coming may occur any day, so that, corrected from our vices and abandoning the world's vain hope, we may deserve to reach the prize of the heavenly calling.[^1]

**2.** We celebrate, then, the birthday of the holy apostles, the first and the last. I know that you know what I have said; but because you know, should we be silent, so that while you render what is yours, we remain without fault? For you render your solemn attendance, and we must offer a sermon of some kind, so that all of us may celebrate our devotion together. Peter, then, is the first of the apostles and Paul the last. But God, who sanctified them by calling them, himself crowned them in their suffering: the first and the last, the first before whom there is nothing, the last after whom there is nothing. No one precedes his origin or passes beyond his end. His origin has no beginning; his end has no end. Thus he who declared himself the first and the last in everlasting eternity equally crowned his apostles, the first and the last. Their life proclaims charity; their death consecrates the solemnity. One day met them for their crowning, one city for their burial. Their bodies are not separated on earth, whose merits are made equal in heaven. Living in the flesh, they planted churches by their speech; dying for Christ, they watered them with their blood. Praying, they help us through their merit.[^2]

**3.** It was fitting that they met on one day, so that those who shared the preaching of the city should have one lot in undivided charity. Certainly, as tradition has come down to us, blessed Peter suffered first—for the order of calling should also be preserved in death—and then most blessed Paul, last indeed among the apostles, but teacher of the nations. Paul, then, is small, like the fringe of the Lord's garment, yet a fringe that can save those who touch it. Both chose Rome, the chief city of the Roman name: Peter because of his primacy among the apostles, Paul because of his teaching of the nations. But they placed only their bodies there; they spread their merits everywhere, as we have just sung: *Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world.* Whoever in any part of the earth knows our Lord Jesus Christ must know Peter and Paul. This is what it means: *Their sound has gone out into all the earth.* Wherever they did not go on their own feet while living, they reached through their writings after death, from which the apostles' words are read to us daily. By them Catholic churches are instructed, heretics confounded, enemies refuted. Since, then, we could not have their bodies everywhere, we can hold fast to their words. Their bodies had necessarily to be laid in one place, but their powers are exercised everywhere.[^3]

[^1]: Matt 16:13; Matt 16:16; Matt 16:17; cf. Eph 3:8; cf. Matt 14:28–32; cf. 2 Cor 2:14; cf. Wis 1:1; cf. Phil 3:14.
[^2]: Cf. Rev 1:17; cf. 1 Cor 3:6.
[^3]: Cf. 2 Tim 1:11; cf. Matt 9:20, 22; Ps 19:4 (18:5 Vg).
