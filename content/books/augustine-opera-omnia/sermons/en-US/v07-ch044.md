# Sermon 263/A: On the Ascension of the Lord

**1.** Today our Lord Jesus Christ ascends into heaven; let our heart ascend with him. Let us hear the Apostle saying: *If you have risen with Christ, set your minds on the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; seek the things that are above, not the things that are on earth*[^1]. For as he ascended, and did not withdraw from us, so we too are already there with him, although what is promised to us has not yet been done in our body. He is already exalted above the heavens; yet he suffers on earth whatever of labors we, as his members, feel. To which thing he bore witness, crying from above: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^2] And: *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^3]. Why do we too not so labor on earth that, through the faith, hope, and love by which we are connected to him, we already rest with him in heaven? When he is there, he is also with us; and we, when we are here, are also with him. He himself does this both by divinity and by power and by love; but we, even if we cannot by divinity as he can, yet can by love, but in him. He did not withdraw from heaven, when from there he came down to us; nor did he withdraw from us, when he ascended again into heaven. For that he was there when he was here, he himself testifies thus: *No one*, he says, *has ascended into heaven, except the one who came down from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven*[^4]. He did not say: the Son of man who will be in heaven, but: *the Son of man who is in heaven.*

**2.** But that he is with us even when he is there, before he ascended he promised this, saying: *Behold, I am with you until the consummation of the age*[^5]. But we are there by name, since he himself said: *Rejoice, because your names are written in heaven*[^6]; although by our bodies and labors we wear down the earth, and are worn down by the earth. But when, after the resurrection of the body, we have begun to be in his glory, neither will these mortal things inhabit our body, nor will our affection be bent down to these; the whole gathers from here him who holds the firstfruits of our spirit. For not on this account is the perfect and angelic heavenly dwelling to be despaired of by us, because he said: *No one has ascended into heaven, except the one who came down from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven*; for he seems to have said it of himself alone, as if none of us could receive this; but it was said because of unity, because he is our head, and we are his body. This, then, no one but he, because we too are he, according to that by which he himself is the Son of man for our sake, and we are sons of God for his sake. For thus the Apostle says: *For as the body is one, and has many members, but all the members of the body, though they are many, are one body, so also is Christ*[^7]. He did not say: so is Christ; but he said: *so also is Christ.* Christ, then, is many members, one body. He came down, then, from heaven by mercy, nor does anyone ascend but he, since we too are in him by grace. And through this no one but Christ came down, no one but Christ ascends; not that the dignity of the head is confounded in the body, but that the unity of the body is not separated from the head. *For he does not say, To seeds, as of many, but as of one, In your seed, which is Christ*[^8]. Accordingly he calls Christ the seed of Abraham; and yet the same Apostle: *You, then*, he says, *are Abraham's seed*[^9]. If, then, not in seeds as of many, but as of one: and this is the seed of Abraham, which is Christ: and this is the seed of Abraham, which we are; when he ascends into heaven, we are not separated from him. He who came down from heaven does not envy us heaven, but in a way cries out: Be my members, if you wish to ascend into heaven. And by this very thing let us meanwhile be strengthened, let us be aflame for this with all our prayers; let us meditate on earth on this, that we are counted in heaven. Then about to put off the flesh of mortality, now let us put off the oldness of the mind: easily will the body be lifted into the heights of the heavens, if the burdens of sins do not press the spirit.

**3.** For this too moves some, when heretics slander, how the Lord came down without a body, when he ascended with a body; as if it were contrary to those words in which he says: *No one has ascended into heaven, except the one who came down from heaven.* The body, then, they say, which did not come down from heaven, how could it ascend into heaven? As if he had said: Nothing ascends into heaven, except what came down from heaven; but he said: *No one ascends, except the one who came down.* For this he referred to the person, not to the dress of the person. He came down without the garment of the body, he ascended with the garment of the body; yet no one ascends except the one who came down. For if he so fitted us to himself as his own members that, even when we are joined, he himself is the same; how much more can that body, which he took from the Virgin, not have another person in him? For who would say, whether of a mountain, or of a wall, or of any higher place, that not only the one who came down ascended, if, when he had come down stripped, he ascends clothed; or when he had come down unarmed, he ascends armed? As, then, of this it is said: *No one ascends except the one who came down*, although he ascended with that thing with which he did not come down; so no one ascends into heaven except Christ, because no one but Christ came down from heaven, although he came down without a body, and ascended with a body, we too being about to ascend not by our own power, but by our unity and his. *For two* are in one flesh: it is a great sacred sign in Christ and in the Church; hence he himself says: *Now no longer two, but one flesh*[^10].

**4.** And therefore he fasted when he was tempted, before death still needing food; but he ate and drank when he was being glorified, after the resurrection now not needing food. For there he showed in himself our labor, but here in us his consolation, defining each by forty days. For he fasted forty days, when he was tempted in the desert, as it is written in the Gospel, before the death of his flesh; and again he was forty days with the disciples, as Peter says in the Acts of the Apostles, going in and out, eating and drinking after the resurrection of his flesh. By which number forty seems to be signified the course of this age in those who are called to grace, through him who came not to abolish the law but to fulfill it. For there are ten precepts of the law, grace of Christ now spread through the world; and the world is fourfold, and ten multiplied by four make forty; since *those who have been redeemed by the Lord, he gathered them from the regions, from the East and West, and the North and the Sea*[^11]. Fasting, then, forty days before the death of the flesh, he as it were cried out: Abstain from the desires of this age; but eating and drinking forty days after the resurrection of the flesh, he as it were cried out: *Behold, I am with you until the consummation of the age*[^12]. For fasting is in the tribulation of the contest; since *he who strives in the contest abstains from all things*[^13]; but food is in the hope of peace, which will not be perfect, except when our body, whose redemption we await, has put on immortality; which, by not yet attaining, we glory in, but by hoping we are now fed. The Apostle shows that we do both at the same time, saying: *Rejoicing in hope, patient in tribulation*[^14]; as though the one were in food, the other in fasting. For at the same time, while we travel the Lord's way, let us both fast from the vanity of the present age, and be refreshed by the promise of the future: here not setting our heart, there feeding our heart on high.
