# Sermon 94a

**1.** Concerning the present passage of the holy Gospel, dearly beloved brothers, which the Lord wished to teach us, the Church of Christ certainly does not doubt that John is a martyr. He earned martyrdom even before the Lord's passion; he was born before, he suffered before—yet not as the author of salvation, but as the forerunner of the judge. For he went ahead of the Lord, himself taking on humble service, giving exaltation to the heavenly Master. On what ground do we say that John is a martyr? Was he perhaps seized by persecutors of Christians, brought forward, questioned, and did he confess Christ and suffer? For the other martyrs, after Christ's passion, can be called this. So on what ground is he a martyr? Because he was beheaded? For it is not the penalty that makes a martyr, but the cause. Because he offended a powerful woman? How did he offend? On what matter did he offend? By speaking the truth to the king, who had become her husband, that it was not lawful for him to have his brother's wife [^1]. The truth earned hatred, and by earning hatred it arrived at suffering and a crown. These are the fruits of the age to come. In the end self-indulgence dances, and innocence is condemned; but it is condemned by men and crowned by God almighty.

**2.** Therefore let no one say: I cannot be a martyr, because there is no persecution against Christians. You hear that John bore martyrdom; and, if you consider the truth, he died for Christ. How, you say, did he die for Christ, when he was not questioned about Christ, nor forced to deny Christ? Hear Christ himself saying: *I am the way, and the truth, and the life* [^2]. If Christ is the truth, then whoever is condemned for the truth suffers for Christ, and is lawfully crowned. Therefore let no one excuse himself: all times lie open to martyrs. Nor let anyone say that there is no persecution against Christians. The statement of the apostle Paul cannot be made void, because it is true; Christ spoke through him, he did not lie. For he says: *All who want to live devoutly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution* [^3]. *All*, he says: he excluded no one, he set no one apart. If you want to prove that what he said is true, begin to live devoutly in Christ, and you will see that what he says is true. Just because persecution from earthly kings has died down, does that mean the devil does not rage? That ancient enemy is always on watch against us: let us not sleep. He puts forward enticements, ambushes, sends in evil thoughts; he sets out gains and threatens losses, so as to provoke us to a worse fall. It comes to the crisis, and the evil of his suggestion is rejected with effort, so that present death is gladly undergone. Understand, brothers. If someone forces you—say, some nobleman who has power over your life—forces you to bear false witness, and does not say to you, Deny Christ, what do you think: to choose falsehood, or to die for the truth? And yet the persecutor says nothing else to you than, Deny Christ. For if Christ is the truth, as we have already said, then surely whoever denies the truth denies Christ. And everyone who speaks a lie denies the truth. The very one who gives false testimony—why does he? Because he is afraid, certainly. Is there not persecution against all Christians, when there is a struggle for the truth? Now each one is tested, and in his own case everyone is put to the trial.

**3.** But what would your enemy have done to you—he who threatens slaughter, thirsts for blood, is puffed up with power, lifted up like smoke—what would he have done, he who was forcing you to perjury and false witness? Weakness answers: He would kill me, it says.—He would not kill you.—I know for certain, he would kill me. If that is so, then I too will answer: You, brother, are the one who kills, you who give false witness. He would indeed kill, but only your body: what would he do to your soul? The house would be thrown down, the dweller would be crowned. See what your enemy would have done to you, if you had stood firm in the truth and had not given false witness: he would certainly kill, but only the body, not the soul. Hear your Lord giving you assurance with care: *Do not*, he says, *fear those who kill the body, and after that have nothing more they can do; but fear him who has the power to kill body and soul and to cast them into hell. Yes, I tell you, fear him* [^4]. Him John feared: he refused to keep silent about the truth, and he endured the wickedness of evil men; through an immoral woman he earned the king's hatred, and arrived at martyrdom.

**4.** For all who want to live devoutly in Christ are shaken by such persecution. For they suffer persecution either for the gain of the world or for fear of loss, either for the present life or because death is threatened; for this world is not without persecution. But one must distinguish who suffers for what; and they will be true martyrs, if there is a struggle for the truth, which is Christ, and they are lawfully crowned. For whoever suffers persecution for this world, which lies in evil, can endure only a temporal punishment.

**5.** Therefore, brothers, since we have learned this in the present reading—to fight for the truth even to death, and not to give false witness, not to perjure ourselves, to hold to justice in dangers (for it is no great thing to hold to justice in safety or amid pleasures)—let us always reckon that the devil, our tempter and persecutor, keeps watch against us; and in the name and with the help of the Lord our God let us keep watch more fervently against him, lest in anything he overcome us through the desire by which he is accustomed to tempt. For the one whom desire and fear do not conquer—these being the enemy's weapons by which men who set their hope in this world are entangled in various snares, so that they cannot prevail to hold the truth—the devil has, in the end, two doors through which he knocks to enter: first through desire, then through fear. If he finds both doors closed in the faithful, he passes on. And what, you ask, is desire, what is fear? Hear what it is: do not desire what passes away, and do not fear what fails and perishes in time; and then at last he will not find a nest where the enemy may dwell, because a struggle has been set before us until the end—not only for us, who stand or sit in the higher place and speak to you, but the struggle is set before all the members of Christ.

**6.** For this reason, to this very day in Numidia it is the custom to put servants of God under oath thus: If you should conquer. You see that such an oath of one who fights is not spoken without reason. For here too, where we are speaking, at Carthage, and in the whole proconsular and Byzacene province, and also in Tripoli, this is the customary phrase by which the servants of God adjure one another: By your crown. Which crown no one will receive unless he has conquered. And I too adjure you by your crown, that you fight against the devil with your whole heart; and, if we conquer together, together we will also be crowned. Why do you say to us, By your crown, and yet act wickedly, live wickedly? Live well, act well, keep good conduct both outside and within, and you will be our crown. The people of God, which is what you are, the Apostle was instructing when he said: *My joy and my crown, stand fast in the Lord* [^5]. So if the prosperity of the times smiles, stand fast in the Lord; and if the adversity of the times roars, be steadfast in the Lord. Do not fall away from him who always stands, and who, standing, awaits the one who fights, and helps you, so that by standing and pruning you may conquer, and at last come to him to be crowned.
