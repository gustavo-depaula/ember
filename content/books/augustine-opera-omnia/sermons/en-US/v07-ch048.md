# Sermon 265/B: On the Fortieth Day, the Ascension of the Lord

**1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ, after he rose from the dead, wishing by a most certain and most faithful attestation to show that he had risen in the same body in which he hung on the cross, was with his disciples for forty days, going in and out, eating and drinking. For so it was fitting both that the doubting be made firm, and that the truth of the Gospel be preached to posterity, and that to believers the future incorruption and immortality of his flesh in that eternal blessedness be shown, and that the malicious men who think and teach about the Lord otherwise than the truth holds be contradicted. For in that body he ascended rising into heaven, in which body dead he visited the underworld. For he placed in heaven that very dwelling of his now immortal flesh, which he himself fashioned for himself in the womb of the Virgin mother.

**2.** It seems indeed marvelous to some, that the Lord says in the Gospel: *No one has ascended into heaven, except the one who came down from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven*[^1]. How, they say, is the Son of man said to have come down from heaven, when he was here assumed in the womb of the Virgin? Those who say these things are not to be spurned, but to be taught; for I judge that they ask devoutly, but cannot yet understand what they ask. For they do not know that the divinity itself so assumed that humanity, that God and man became one person; and that humanity so cohered to that divinity, that there was one Christ, Word, soul, and flesh. And therefore it was said: *No one has ascended into heaven, except the one who came down from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven.*

**3.** For each substance imparts to the other the names of its own properties, both the divine to the human, and the human to the divine; so that both the Son of God is called man, and the Son of man is called God, yet the same Christ is both. For our Lord Jesus Christ himself so condescended to take up a man, that he did not disdain to call himself the Son of man, as we read in many places in the Gospel. For to blessed Peter he himself says: *Whom do men say the Son of man to be?* To whom Peter, with Christ the rock himself inspiring, answered: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^2]. See, here is that harper whom David signified: now he has appeared, because he touched the hearts of his own, and drew out the sound he wished, which would be known to all. And, terrifying the Jews about his last coming, in his passion: *From now on you will see*, he says, *the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven*[^3]. And in another place: *You will see the angels ascending and descending to the Son of man*[^4]. When he says: *ascending*, he shows himself to be in heaven; when he says: *descending*, he shows that he will never be lacking on earth either, as he also promised to the disciples when about to ascend into heaven, saying: *Behold, I am with you all the days until the consummation of the age*[^5].

**4.** But God so loved the human race, that he gave his only-begotten Son for the life of the world. For unless the Father handed over life, we would not have life; and unless life itself died, death would not be killed. For the Lord Christ himself is life, of whom the Evangelist John says: *This is the true God, and eternal life*[^6]. For he too, through the prophet, threatening death to death, says: *I will be your death, O death; I will be your bite, O underworld!*[^7] As if to say: I by dying will kill you, I will consume you, I will take from you all power, I will rescue the captives whom you held. You wished to hold me, the harmless one; it is just that you lose those whom you wished to hold.

**5.** Therefore both life died, and life remained, and life rose again, and by his death, killing death, conferred life on us. *Death* therefore *was swallowed up in the victory* of Christ, who is eternal life; therefore *he swallowed up death*, as the Apostle says, *that we might be heirs of life*[^8]. Through Christ, then, we have been made heirs of eternal life, through whom we have been freed from everlasting death, of whom we do not doubt that we are members. And so on the fortieth day, that is, today, the Lord Jesus ascended into heaven, the disciples contemplating and marveling: for while they stood and talked, suddenly a cloud received him, and he was taken from them into heaven.

[^1]: John 3:13
[^2]: Matt 16:13-16
[^3]: Matt 26:64
[^4]: John 1:51
[^5]: Matt 28:20
[^6]: 1 John 5:20
[^7]: Hos 13:14
[^8]: 1 Cor 15:54; 1 Pet 3:22; Titus 3:7
