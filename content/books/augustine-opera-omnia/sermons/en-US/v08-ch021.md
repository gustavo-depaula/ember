# Sermon 291: On the Birthday of John the Baptist

**1.** You do not need to be told what day we celebrate today, because you all heard when the Gospel was read. Today we received Saint John, the Lord’s forerunner, the barren woman’s son announcing the Virgin’s Son, yet a servant announcing the Lord. For because God made man was to come through a virgin, a wondrous man born of a barren woman preceded him, so that when the wondrous man declares himself unworthy to untie the strap of his sandal, God made man may be recognized. Marvel at John as much as you can: what you marvel at advances Christ’s cause. It advances Christ’s cause, I say, not because you bestow anything on Christ, but so that you may advance in Christ. Marvel at John, then, as much as you can. You have heard what you should marvel at. An angel announces him to his father, a priest. The angel takes away the unbelieving father’s voice; he remains mute, awaiting the recovery of his tongue at his son’s birth. A barren woman conceived, and an elderly woman conceived: a double infertility, barrenness and age. The angel declares what he will be; what is declared is fulfilled in him. And what is most marvelous, he is filled with the Holy Spirit even from his mother’s womb. Then, when holy Mary arrives, he leaps in the womb and greets with movements the one he could not greet with words. He is born and gives his father a voice; the father speaks and gives his son a name. Everyone marvels at such grace. For what else is it but grace? Where had this John earned God’s favor? Where had he earned God’s favor before there was anyone to earn it? O grace freely given!

**2.** Everyone marvels and stands amazed, and, moved in their hearts, they say what would be written for us to read: *What, do you think, will this child be? For the Lord’s hand is with him. What, do you think, will this child be?* He surpasses the limits of human nature. We know children, but *what, do you think, will this child be?* Why do you say, *What, do you think, will this child be? For the Lord’s hand is with him*. We already know that the Lord’s hand is with him, but we do not know what he will be. Surely he will be very great, having begun so greatly. What will he be, who is so great while so little? What will he be? Human weakness is bewildered; the hearts of all who consider him tremble: *What, do you think, will this child be?* He will be great; but what will the one greater than he be? This child will be very great; but what will the one greater than this great child be? If the one who has just begun to exist will be so great, what will the one who already was be? But what have I said: “Who already was”? Zechariah too was before John; much more were Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob before John. Heaven and earth certainly were before John. What will the one who was in the beginning be? For *in the beginning*—before John and before every human being—*God made heaven and earth*. But do you ask through what he made them? In the beginning God did not make the Word; rather, the Word was. *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was*—not just anything, but—*the Word was God. All things were made through him.* And in the last time the one who was came to be, so that what he had made might not perish. *What, do you think, will this child be? For the Lord’s hand is with him.* If the child will be so great because the Lord’s hand is with him, what of the Lord’s hand itself? For Christ is the Lord’s hand; the Son of God is God’s hand; the Word of God is God’s hand. For what is God’s hand except that through which all things were made? *What, do you think, will this child be? For the Lord’s hand is with him.* O human weakness, what will you do before the judge, when you are so at a loss before the herald? But what have I said here too? I return to considering ordinary human usage. What have I said? I have said “herald” and “judge”: the herald is a man, and the judge a man. I have spoken of what appeared; who could speak of what lay hidden? *The Word became flesh*, yet the Word was not changed into flesh. The Word became flesh by taking on what he was not, not by losing what he was. See, we have marveled at his herald’s birth, which we celebrate today; but let us look at the one for whose sake it occurred.

**3.** The angel Gabriel came to Zechariah, not to his wife Elizabeth, John’s mother. The angel Gabriel came, I say, to Zechariah, not to Elizabeth. Why? Because through Zechariah John was to come to be in Elizabeth. Thus the angel announcing John’s coming birth went not to the womb that would receive him, but to the source of the seed. He announced the future son of both, but announced him to the father. For John was to come from the marriage of a man and a woman. Now again that same Gabriel comes to Mary, not to Joseph. The angel came to the very one from whom that flesh was to begin, from whom it was to have its origin. But how did the angel foretell the future son to his father, the priest Zechariah? *Do not,* he said, *be afraid, Zechariah; your prayer has been heard.* What, my brothers and sisters? Had that priest entered the Holy of Holies to ask the Lord for children? Certainly not. Someone says, “How do you prove this? Zechariah did not disclose what he prayed for.” I will briefly say one thing: if he had asked for a son, he would have believed the announcement. The angel says a son will be born to him, and he does not believe? Had he really prayed for this? Who prays without hope? Or who does not believe while hoping? If you do not hope, why do you ask? If you hope, why do you not believe? What, then? *Your prayer,* he says, *has been heard; for behold, Elizabeth will conceive and bear you a son.* Why? Because *your prayer has been heard.* Suppose Zechariah said, “Why? Did I pray for this?” Surely the angel was neither mistaken nor deceiving when he said, *Your prayer has been heard; for behold, your wife will give birth.* But why was this said? Because he was offering sacrifice for the people: the priest was offering sacrifice for the people, the people were awaiting Christ, and John was announcing Christ.

**4.** But that same angel says to the Virgin Mary, *Hail,* *full of grace, the Lord is with you.* He who will be within you is already with you. *Blessed are you among women.* Holy Scripture attests that, according to the distinctive usage of the Hebrew language, all females are customarily called women. I say this so that those unaccustomed to hearing the Scriptures may not be surprised or offended. In one passage of Scripture the Lord clearly says, *Set apart the women who have not known a man*. Recall, indeed, our very origin: when Eve was made from the man’s side, what does Scripture say? *He took a rib from him and built it into a woman*. She is already called a woman, taken indeed from a man but not yet joined to a man. Therefore, when you hear the angel say, *Blessed are you among women,* understand it as though in our manner of speaking he had said, “Blessed are you among females.”

**5.** A son is promised to Zechariah; a son is also promised to holy Mary, and she too says almost the same words Zechariah had said. What had Zechariah said? *How can this happen to me? For I am an old man, and my wife is barren and advanced in years.* And what did holy Mary say? *How will this happen?* Similar words, different hearts. Let us hear the similar words with our ears, but learn the difference of the hearts from the angel’s declaration. David sinned, and when corrected by the prophet he said, *I have sinned.* Immediately he was told, *Your sin has been forgiven*. Saul sinned, and when corrected by the prophet he said, *I have sinned.* Yet his sin was not forgiven; God’s wrath remained upon him. What does this mean, except similar words, different hearts? For a human being hears the voice; God examines the heart. Thus the angel saw that Zechariah’s words contained not faith but doubt and despair; the angel revealed this by taking away his voice and condemning his unbelief. But holy Mary said, *How will this happen, since I do not know a man*? Recognize the Virgin’s resolve. When would she have said, *How will this happen?* if she intended to have intercourse with a man? For if it were to happen as it ordinarily happens with all infants, she would not have asked, “How will it happen?” But mindful of her resolve and conscious of her holy vow, knowing what she had vowed, she said, *How will this happen, since I do not know a man?* She knew of no way for children to be born except to married women who had intercourse with their husbands, something she had resolved not to experience. In saying, *How will this happen?* she asked about the manner; she did not doubt God’s omnipotence. *How will this happen?* What is the manner in which it will happen? “You announce a son to me; you find my mind ready. Tell me the manner.” For the holy Virgin could have feared, or at least not known, God’s plan for how he wished her to have a son, as though he had disapproved of her virginal vow. What if he had said, “Marry; join yourself to a man”? God would not say this, for he had accepted the Virgin’s vow as God. And what he accepted from her was what he himself had given. “Tell me, then, messenger of God: *How will this happen?*” See how the angel knows that she is asking, not distrusting. Because he saw her asking rather than distrusting, he did not refuse to instruct her. Hear how: your virginity will remain. Only believe the truth, preserve your virginity, receive its integrity. Because your faith is whole, your virginal integrity will remain untouched. Hear, then, how this will happen: *The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you.* Such shade knows no heat of lust. *Therefore,* because *the Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you,* because you conceive by faith, because through believing, not through intercourse, you will bear in your womb, *therefore the holy one to be born of you will be called the Son of God.*

**6.** Who are you who will afterward give birth? How did you deserve this? Where did you receive it from? How will the one who made you be made in you? Where, I ask, does so great a good come to you from? You are a virgin, you are holy, you have made a vow; yet what you have deserved is great—or rather, what you have received is great. For how did you deserve this? The one who made you is made in you; the one through whom you were made is made in you. Indeed, the Word of God, through whom heaven and earth were made, through whom all things were made, becomes flesh in you, taking on flesh without losing divinity. The Word is joined to flesh, the Word is united to flesh, and your womb is the bridal chamber of this great union. Your womb, I say, is the bridal chamber of this great union of the Word and flesh, from which *the bridegroom himself comes forth from his chamber*. Conceived, he finds you a virgin; born, he leaves you a virgin. He gives fruitfulness without taking away virginity. Where does this come to you from? I seem to question the Virgin boldly, and with this voice of mine almost intrusively to assail her modest ears. But I see the Virgin blushing, yet answering and admonishing me: “Do you ask me where this comes to me from? I am too modest to answer you about my own good. Hear the angel’s own greeting, and recognize your salvation in me. Believe the one I believed. Do you ask where this comes to me from? Let the angel answer.” Tell me, angel, where does this come to Mary from? “I have already said it in my greeting: *Hail, full of grace*.”
