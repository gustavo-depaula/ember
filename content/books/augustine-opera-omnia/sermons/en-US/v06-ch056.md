# Sermon 223/F: On the Paschal Vigil

**1.** The yearly solemnity of that night, dearest brothers, on which the Lord Jesus Christ rose from the dead, we celebrate by this vigil. I do not now teach, but I admonish you who know, lest you forget. For as the celebration itself, coming solemnly at its own time, does not bring it about that we should know something unwonted, but that we should not blot out by forgetting what we know — for it renews memory, it does not insert knowledge — so too our sermon, even if it is not brought forth to the ignorant, yet seeks attentive ones; because, even if it does not bring it about that you hear what you do not know, yet I wish it to bring it about that you recall with pleasure the things you know.

**2.** Let us watch, then, and pray, lest we enter into temptation[^1]: for *our adversary, like a roaring lion, goes about seeking whom he may devour*[^2]. But that Lion of the tribe of Judah, of whom it was foretold so long before: *You went up, lying down, you slept like a lion; who will rouse him?*[^3] He went up onto the cross, when he suffered, because he was hung up by the will of one showing mercy, not by the necessity of one sinning. He slept, lying down, when, *his head bowed, he gave up the Spirit*[^4]. But like a lion, because even in that very weakness he was strong; *for that which is weak of God is stronger than men*[^5]; finally, by dying he gave life, and consumed death by death. Who roused him, except the invisible God? For why "who"? because he does not appear to human sight, as also the only-begotten Word of God himself, with the Father, is invisible. He, then, roused him from the dead, and *gave him a name which is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee may bow*[^6], and the lion seeking to devour is conquered by the lion seeking to free.

**3.** Let us watch, then, and pray, lest we enter into temptation[^7]. Let us watch for him who of his own accord slept for us. For he slept, and took sleep; and rose, because the Lord took him up[^8], to whom he had said: *And rouse me up, and I will requite them*[^9]. Who, to the enemies about to rage and to insult him as he died, said in prophecy: *Will not he who sleeps add that he rise?*[^10] Therefore he who slept also awoke, and to himself awaking consecrated this vigil of ours. Let us watch and pray, lest we enter into temptation, since he watched, and was made *like a solitary sparrow on a roof*[^11]; therefore he rose, and flew on high, and for us, the only one, intercedes in heaven[^12]. To this one, so great an intercessor, let us give our prayers; he grants with the Father what he asked of the Father, because he is mediator and creator: mediator, to ask; creator, to bestow: mediator made in the world, creator through whom the world was made. With a most faithful and most trusting affection, then, with a sober mind let us watch for him, and put forward to him the Prayer which he taught; that what he commanded to be done, with his help we may be able to do; what he promised to be received, with his giving we may take.
