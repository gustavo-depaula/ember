# Sermon 54

On What Is Written in the Gospel of Matthew 5:16: "So Let Your Light Shine Before Men, That They May See Your Good Works, and Glorify Your Father Who Is in Heaven"; and on the Contrary, Matthew 6:1: "Take Heed That You Do Not Your Justice Before Men, to Be Seen by Them"

**1. 1.** It usually troubles many, dearly beloved, that our Lord Jesus Christ in the Gospel sermon, when he had first said: *Let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^1], afterward said: *Take heed that you do not your justice before men, to be seen by them*[^2]. For the mind of one who understands little is disturbed, and, desiring to obey the precept, is stretched between contrary and opposing things. For no one can obey even one master commanding contradictory things, just as no one can serve two masters: which the Savior himself attested in the same sermon[^3]. What, then, will the wavering mind do, when it judges that it cannot obey, and is afraid not to obey? For if it sets its good works in the light for men to behold, so as to do what is commanded: *So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*; it will think itself held guilty, because it acted against the precept where it is said: *Take heed that you do not your justice before men, to be seen by them.* And again, if, fearing and avoiding this, it has hidden the things it does well, it will not think itself serving him who commands and says: *Let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works.*

**2. 2.** But he who understands rightly fulfills both, and will serve the most universal Lord, who would not condemn a sluggish servant if he commanded things that could in no way be done. For hear Paul, the servant of Christ Jesus, called an apostle, set apart for the Gospel of God[^4], both doing and teaching both. See how his light shines before men, that they may see his good works. *We commend ourselves*, he says, *to every conscience of men in the sight of God*[^5]: and again: *For we provide good things, not only before God, but also before men*[^6]: and again: *Please all men in all things, as I too please all in all things*[^7]. See, on the other hand, how he takes heed not to do his justice before men, to be seen by them. *But let each one prove his own work*, he says; *and then he will have glory in himself, and not in another*[^8]: and again: *For this is our glory, the testimony of our conscience*[^9]: and that which is most manifest: *If*, he says, *I still pleased men, I would not be the servant of Christ*[^10]. But lest any of those who are troubled by the seemingly conflicting precepts of the Lord himself raise the question all the more against his Apostle, and say: "How do you say: *Please all men in all things, as I too please all in all things*: and you the same say: *If I still pleased men, I would not be the servant of Christ?*" May the Lord himself be present, who spoke even in his servant and apostle, and open his will to us, and grant us the ability to obey.

**2. 3.** For the very words of the Gospel carry their own explanations with them; nor do they close the mouths of the hungry, because they feed the hearts of those who knock. For one must consider whither the intention of the human heart is directed, and at what it looks. For if he who wishes his good works to be seen by men sets his glory and advantage before men, and seeks this in the sight of men, he has fulfilled none of what the Lord commanded on this matter: because he both took heed to do his justice before men, to be seen by them; and his light did not so shine before men that they should for this reason see his good works, that they might glorify the Father who is in heaven. For he wished himself to be glorified, not God; and he sought his own advantage, he did not love the will of the Lord. Of such men the Apostle says: *For all seek their own things, not the things of Jesus Christ*[^11]. And so the sentence was not ended there, where he says: *So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works*: but he at once added why it must be done: *that they may glorify*, he says, *your Father who is in heaven*: so that a man, when seen by men doing well, may have the intention of the good deed in his conscience, but may have the intention of becoming known only in the praises of God, for the advantage of those to whom he becomes known; whom it profits that God, who granted this to a man, should please them; and so that they may not despair that this can be granted to them too, if they wish. And so the other sentence, where he says: *Take heed that you do not your justice before men*, he ended nowhere else than where he said, *to be seen by them*: nor did he add: *And that they may glorify your Father who is in heaven*; but rather added: *Otherwise you will not have a reward with your Father who is in heaven*[^12]. For from this he shows that those who are such as he does not wish his faithful to be seek their reward in this very thing, that they are seen by men, that they place their good there; there delight the vanity of their heart, there are emptied and puffed up, there swell and waste away. For why does it not suffice to say: *Take heed that you do not your justice before men*; but he added, *to be seen by them*: except because there are certain ones who so do their justice before men, not that they may be seen by them, but that the works themselves may be seen, and the Father who is in heaven may be glorified, who deigned to grant these things to the wicked once justified?

**3. 4.** Those who are such do not reckon the justice as their own, but his by whose faith they live. Whence the Apostle too says: *That I may gain Christ, and be found in him, not having my own justice, which is of the Law, but that which is through the faith of Christ, which is from God, the justice from faith*[^13]: and in another place: *That we may be the justice of God in him*[^14]. Whence he also reproves the Jews thus: *Being ignorant*, he says, *of the justice of God, and wishing to establish their own, they have not been subject to the justice of God*[^15]. Whoever, then, so wishes his works to be seen by men that he may be glorified from whom he received the things that are seen in him, and so that those very ones who see may be provoked by the godliness of faith to imitate the good, his light truly shines before men: because the light of love radiates from him, not the smoke of pride is belched out; and in this very thing he takes heed not to do his justice before men, to be seen by them; because he neither reckons that justice his own, nor does it that he himself may be seen; but that he may be understood who is praised in the justified man, that he may bring about in the praiser too what is praised in another, that is, that he may make the praiser praiseworthy. Attend to the Apostle too, when he had said: *Please all men in all things, as I too please all in all things*; how he did not remain there, as if he had set there the end of his intention, that he might please men; otherwise he would have spoken falsely: *If I still pleased men, I would not be the servant of Christ*: but at once he added why he pleased men. *Not seeking*, he says, *what is useful to me, but what is useful to many, that they may be saved*[^16]. So he both did not please men for his own advantage, lest he be not the servant of Christ; and pleased men for their salvation, that he might be a fit dispenser of Christ: because his conscience sufficed for him before God, and from him there shone before men what they might imitate.
