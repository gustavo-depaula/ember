# Sermon 204D

**1.** Your Holiness remembers, most beloved brothers, that a few days ago we celebrated the Lord's birth. Today, then, we celebrate the day of the manifestation of him whose birthday we celebrated only a very few days ago. Not that he was hidden then either, for then too, [from] the day he was born, he was announced to the shepherds through the ministry of angels; they went and saw what they had heard. So then he was revealed to Israel's shepherds; today, however, to the magi of the nations. For the cornerstone had been born. From one side the shepherds, from the other the magi: these are the two walls seeking a kiss at the corner. The shepherds were Jews; the magi were from the gentiles. The shepherds signified the apostles; the magi, the nations. Thus both days belong to our celebration: let us worship Christ born, let us worship him revealed. Angels announced him 〈to the shepherds〉; a star announced him to the magi. Both the angels and the star were from heaven: *The heavens proclaim God's glory*.[^1]

**2.** Let us now review a little of the Gospel narrative. The magi see a new star in heaven: they marvel but do not know. They saw it for a long time and finally understood. How do we prove that they saw it for a long time? You noticed when it was \*read; if your ear and heart were there, recognize what I recall. Herod feared the infant king, not yet speaking but already terrifying him—the lion was terrifying the fox. Fearing, then, that the king of the Jews had been born, he asked the magi about the time of the star. Then he summoned the Jewish teachers of the law and asked where Christ was to be born. You see that he had long been expected. The teachers of the law answer: *In Bethlehem of Judea*. \*But reading is one thing, understanding another. They read; others understood: *In Bethlehem of Judea*. The magi heard the prophetic testimony cited on this matter and went to seek him in Bethlehem of Judea. They worshiped the one they had come to worship, and, warned in a dream, returned by another road. They did not return to Herod because they had been forbidden. When he learned this, that king who had been afraid sent and killed all the infants in that region *two years old and under, according to the time he had ascertained from the magi*. The star, then, had already been seen for two years without its owner being known; and those two years signified that double wall.[^2]

**3.** The magi hear, then, in which city he had been born. For we should believe that when the one whose star had been seen for so long was born, the magi [who] learned through some message that he whose sign appeared in heaven had been born on earth. They came to worship but did not know where. From the Jewish teachers they learned the city, never the house. What, then, do the Jews do? They read and answer; they send others and stay behind themselves. Like milestones on a road, they can instruct the traveler but cannot move from their place. What, then, were the magi to do? They would come to Bethlehem: were they to go around each house? The Jew showed the place; the star showed the house. For that star they had seen appeared to them and went before them, and they followed. The star stood over the place where the child Christ was, where the great one was little, where God was hidden, where the Word was an infant. They approached and worshiped without hesitation, for they understood to whom they bent the knee. They renounced their gods, sought Christ, and brought forth gifts.[^3]

**4.** Recognizing royal power, they brought forth gold; recognizing God, they brought forth incense; foreknowing that he would also die, they brought myrrh for burial too. Christ accepted gold: it was their honor to him, not his greed. Was his mother going to rejoice over the gold? All her joy was in Christ. Yet, my brothers, does Christ not receive gold now? Certainly many have spent their possessions on him. How does Christ receive gold? The hungry are fed, the naked clothed, strangers welcomed, captives ransomed, churches built. Christ, then, receives gold even now, and blessed is the one who gives. For the one who gives does not give without return: he gives at interest. Bring me a timid and scrupulous moneylender. What are you calculating? Why are you panting? Your whole concern is to give a little and receive much. Here you have Christ, to whom you may give little and from whom you may receive much. Give earth and receive heaven. You will give earth if you are not earth; but you will love earth if you are earth, and you will be told: *You are earth, and into earth you will go*.[^4]

**5.** Herod hears, rages, and seeks to kill the one he ought to worship. He slaughters infants. See: Christ does not yet teach and already has martyrs, martyrs his own age, an infant with infant martyrs. O blessed ones! They could already suffer for Christ when they did not yet know how to confess Christ. Why are you afraid, Herod? Christ's kingdom is not of this world; the one born seeks to compel you, not exclude you. Hear the psalm: *And now, kings, understand; be instructed, you who judge the earth*. You envy 〈…〉 to Christ who reigns, with fear, and exult in the Lord with trembling. When the authority questioned him and he was asked, *Are you the king of the Jews?*, what did he answer? *My kingdom is not of this world*. Leave the world, and you will find Christ the king. Understand that he who was worshiped in the manger is in heaven. He who was carried in his mother's hands contains the whole world; he ruled the angels in heaven and sucked at the breast in the lodging place. Let us, then, join this day to that day and think of Christ receiving gifts in the poor.[^5]
