# Sermon 387

An Admonition of Saint Augustine on That Which Is Written, "Agree with Your Adversary While You Are with Him on the Way," and on the Speck of Anger, Which, Nourished by False Suspicions, Becomes a Beam

**1.** Frequently in the holy Scriptures your love has heard, most beloved brothers, in what danger priests are set, if they refuse to fulfill that which the Apostle solemnly charges: *Preach the word, be urgent in season and out of season; reprove, rebuke, exhort, with all patience and teaching*[^1]. And because so heavy a weight presses upon our necks, to whom it is said: *If you do not announce to the wicked man his wickedness, I will require his blood at your hand*[^2], it is necessary for us to chastise the negligent, whoever they are, either privately or publicly. But when we rebuke, the one whom we rebuke, if he is wicked, attends to who rebukes him; and more easily, in his rebuker than in himself, does he willingly recognize what is to be corrected. And if he finds true things which he too may say against the one by whom he is rebuked, he rejoices. How much better it would be to rejoice over his own healing, when corrected, than over another's sickness, when rebuked! Behold, suppose what you say is true, suppose you have found something in the man by whom you were reproved; yet through him the truth was speaking to you, through the wicked, through the unjust the truth was speaking to you. You seek what to reprove in the man: rather find what you may reprove in the truth. Whether you will or not, it is your adversary, in which you find nothing to accuse. Make it your friend, if you can. Your adversary is the word of God: let a sinner utter it, let a righteous man utter it, it is the word of God, it is blameless. It is your adversary: *Agree with it while you are with it on the way*. The way is this life; the adversary of all the unjust is the word of God. Is it too little for you that, when it was abiding in its most blessed and most hidden seat, it came to you, to be with you on the way, and willed to accompany you, so that, while you walk and have it in your power, you may compose your case and say when you will finish the way? And when you have finished the way, there will be no one with whom you can compose your case; and *the adversary will hand you over to the judge, the judge to the officer, and the officer into prison. You will not go out from there until you have paid the last farthing*[^3]. The word of God is with you as an adversary on the way: you have it in your power, compose. What does this adversary seek of you? That you agree with it. What, except your salvation? It walks with its adversaries, and tells them to agree with it. Let it be done: the way is not yet finished. What was not done yesterday, let it be done today. The way is not yet finished: what do you wait for, until it is finished? When it has been finished, there will be no other where you may agree with the adversary. The judge remains, the officer and the prison. For many this way, when they promised themselves more years in it, was suddenly finished. But behold, suppose your way will be long, and your adversary always walks with you: are you not ashamed, for so long a time to have discord with such an adversary? The word of God, as far as in itself lies, is your friend; you make it your adversary. For it wishes you well, you on the contrary wish yourself ill. It commands: Do not steal; you steal; it commands: Do not commit adultery; you commit adultery; it commands: Do not commit fraud; you commit it; it forbids you to swear, you swear falsely; you do everything contrary to what it says, you make the word of God your enemy. And no wonder, when you are an enemy to yourself: *For he who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^4]. If, then, by loving iniquity you have hated your own soul, do you wonder that you have hated the word of God, which wishes your soul well?

**2.** Shall we, then, be silent and rebuke no one at all? Let us plainly rebuke, but first ourselves. You want to rebuke your neighbor: nothing is nearer to you than yourself. Why far off? You have yourself before you. For what does the Lord say through Scripture? *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^5]. If, then, you do not love yourself, how do you love your neighbor? You have taken the rule of love toward your neighbor from yourself. I love him, you will say. Therefore I say, first love yourself, and speak to yourself. But if you truly speak out of love, it is manifest that within, the word which proceeds has done something. But it is to be feared lest you do not love yourself, and want to rebuke another, and do this with hatred. But if you hate your brother, you charge him with lighter things than you do. *He who hates his brother is a murderer*[^6]. You heard the *Epistle of John* read today. Scripture said—lest men despise the things they have within in the heart, and accuse those things that are done through the body—now *he who hates his brother*, it said, *is a murderer*. The hand is not yet armed, the throat is not yet besieged, the ambush is not yet prepared, the poisons are not yet sought, and he is already held guilty in the eyes of God by the hatred conceived. The one he seeks to kill is still alive, and he is already judged to have killed him. If, then, you rebuke with hatred, do you, a murderer, dare to rebuke anyone? Or, because men do not seize you and lead you to a judge, do you therefore not recognize your crime in the eyes of the most high God and judge? If you will not recognize your crime, you will recognize your punishment: for he does not spare murderers. But I am correcting myself, you say, while I am on the way. Correct yourself, then, and then you will be able to rebuke your brother. You charge him with lighter things, you commit graver ones: *You see the speck in your brother's eye, but you do not see the beam in your own eye*[^7]. For the Lord said this because of men who rebuke with hatred. You rebuke the angry man, and you yourself waste away with hatred? Weigh in the balance of consideration anger and hatred. What is anger? A certain heat of the soul. For the present it displeases you. Now in you that anger has become old, and so has made hatred. Anger is a speck, a beam to be by growing: for just as a speck grows into a beam, so anger grown old becomes hatred. Now you hate, and you rebuke the angry man: now in him the speck displeases you, in yourself the beam still pleases. Do you want to know how great the difference is? We often find fathers angry with their sons: that a father hates his son you will hardly find. A father can be angry with the son whom he loves: he is angry and loves; it can be said: he hates and loves, it cannot be said. I have said this because of men who punish lesser things in others, and do not punish greater things in themselves.

**3.** These things, then, dearest brothers, thinking on them healthfully, let us make friendships with our adversary, while we are with him on the way: that is, let us agree with the word of God, while we are still in this life; because afterward, when we have passed from this age, no composition or any satisfaction will remain. The judge remains, and the officer, and the prison. So then, that we may be able, with the Lord's help, to fulfill all these things, let us love with all our heart not only friends, but also enemies; that there may be fulfilled in us that which is written: *Let the whole law be fulfilled in you in one saying: You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^8]; and that: *Love covers a multitude of sins*[^9]. Which thing may he himself, who is true love, deign to grant us, who with the Father and the Holy Spirit lives and reigns forever and ever. Amen.
