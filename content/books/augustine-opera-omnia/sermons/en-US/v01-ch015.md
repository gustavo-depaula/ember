# Sermon 14: A Sermon Delivered at Carthage

In the Basilica of the New Men on a Sunday

On the Verse of the Psalm: "to You the Poor Man Is Left,

You Will Be a Helper to the Orphan"

**1.** We have sung to the Lord and said, *To you the poor man is left, you will be a helper to the orphan*[^1]. Let us seek the poor man, let us seek the orphan. And let it not be a wonder that I admonish us to seek what we perceive and feel to be so abundant. Are not all things full of the poor? Are not all things full of orphans? Yet among all I seek the orphan. And first it must be shown to your Charity that what we suppose is not what we seek. For those who are poor, toward whom commands and almsgivings are made by God, of whom we confess it is written, *Shut up almsgiving in the heart of the poor, and it will pray for you to the Lord*[^2]—this kind of men indeed abounds, but this poor man is to be understood more deeply. This poor man is of that kind of which it was said, *Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^3]. There are poor who, having no money, scarcely finding daily food, are so in need of others' resources through pity that they do not even blush to beg; if of these it was said, *To you the poor man is left*[^4], what do we, who are not this, do? Therefore are we who are Christians not left to God? And what other hope is there for us, if we are not left to him who has not left us?

**2.** Learn, then, to be poor and to be left to God, O my fellow poor! He is rich, he is proud. For even in those riches which are commonly called riches, to which that common poverty is contrary—in those riches, then, nothing is so to be guarded against as the disease of pride. For he who has no money does not have very great resources, does not have wherewith to exalt himself. If, then, he who does not have wherewith to exalt himself is not praised for the fact that he does not exalt himself, let him who has be praised, if he does not exalt himself. Why, then, do I praise the humble poor man, who has nothing to be proud of? But who could bear one both destitute and proud? Praise the humble rich man, praise the rich man who is poor. Such does he wish who, writing to Timothy, says, *Charge the rich of this world not to be proud in mind*[^5]. I know what I say: charge them this. For they have riches inwardly persuading pride, they have riches in which they labor to be humble. Give me Zacchaeus, having great riches, a chief of the tax collectors, a confessor of his sins, short in stature, shorter in mind, climbing a tree that he might see the one passing by who for him was hanging on a tree; give me him saying, *Half of my goods I give to the poor*[^6]. But you are very rich, O Zacchaeus, very rich! Behold, you will give half; why will you keep half? Because *if I have taken anything from anyone, I restore fourfold*[^7].

**3.** But some beggar says to me, worn out by infirmity, covered with rags, languishing with hunger, answers me and says, "To me the kingdom of heaven is owed. For I am like that Lazarus who lay full of sores before the rich man's house, whose sores the dogs licked, and who sought to be filled with the crumbs that fell from the rich man's table. I am more like him," he says; "ours is the kind to which the kingdom of heaven is owed, not that kind which is clothed in purple and fine linen and feasts splendidly every day. For such was that man, before whose house the poor man, full of sores, lay. And see the ends of both. For it happened that the destitute man died and was carried by the angels into the bosom of Abraham. But the rich man died and was buried. For the poor man perhaps was not even buried. And what afterward? When that rich man was in torments in the lower regions, he lifted up his eyes, saw the poor man whom he had despised resting in Abraham's bosom. He desired a drop from him from whom that man had desired a crumb, and because he loved opulence, he did not find mercy. He wished to help his brothers, ever foolish, too late merciful; nothing at all that he asked did he receive. Let us distinguish, then," he says, "the poor and the rich. Why do you exhort me to understand other things? The poor are evident, the rich are evident."

**4.** Hear me, then, about what you have proposed, sir poor man. For when you say that you are that holy man full of sores, I fear that by being proud you may not be what you say. Do not despise the merciful rich, the humble rich and—to say more quickly what I said a little before—do not despise the rich who are poor. O poor man, be you too poor; poor, that is, humble! For if a rich man became humble, how much more ought a poor man to be humble! The poor man has nothing to be puffed up by, the rich man has something to wrestle with. Hear me, then. Be a true poor man, be devout, be humble. For if you glory in that ragged and ulcerous poverty, because such was he who lay destitute before the rich man's house, you attend that he was poor, and you do not attend to something else. "What," he says, "do I attend to?" Read the Scriptures, and you will find what I say. Lazarus was poor; he into whose bosom he was lifted was rich. *It happened,* it says, *that the destitute man died and was carried by the angels*[^8]. Whither? *Into the bosom of Abraham*, that is, into the secret place where Abraham was. For do not understand in a fleshly way that the poor man was lifted, as it were, into the bosom of Abraham's tunic. It was a bosom because it was a secret place. Whence it is said, *Render to our neighbors into their bosom*[^9]. What is, *into their bosom*? Into their secret places. What is, *Render into their bosom*? Torment their conscience. Read, or if you cannot read, hear when it is read, and see that Abraham was most opulent on earth, in gold, silver, household, cattle, possessions. And yet this rich man was poor, because he was humble. And he was humble: *For Abraham believed God, and it was reckoned to him as righteousness*[^10]. He was justified by the grace of God, not by his own presumption. He was faithful, he worked well. He was ordered to sacrifice his son, nor did he hesitate to offer to him what he had received, from whom he had received it. He was proved to God, was established as an example of faith. He was already known to God, but he had to be shown to us. He was not puffed up as though over his good works, because this rich man was poor. And that you may know that he was not puffed up as though over his good works (for he knew that whatever he had he had from God, and gloried not in himself but in the Lord), hear the apostle Paul: *For if Abraham was justified by works, he has glory, but not before God*[^11].

**5.** You see that, although the poor abound, we rightly seek a poor man. In a crowd we seek and scarcely find. A poor man meets me, and I seek a poor man. Meanwhile, stretch out your hand to the poor man whom you find. With your heart you seek the one you seek. You say, "I am poor like Lazarus." This humble rich man of mine does not say, "I am rich like Abraham." Therefore you exalt yourself, he humbles himself. Why are you puffed up, and do not imitate? "I," he says, "a poor man, was lifted into the bosom of Abraham." Do you not see that a rich man received the poor man? Do you not see that a rich man is the receiver of the poor man? For if you are proud against those who have money and deny that they belong to the kingdom of heaven, when perhaps in them is found the humility that is not found in you, do you not fear lest, when you are dead, Abraham say to you, "Depart from me, because you blasphemed me"?

**6.** Let us, then, admonish our rich, what the Apostle admonished. *Not to be proud in mind, nor to hope in the uncertainty of riches*[^12], we have been admonished. Those riches, which you think are full of delights, are full of dangers. The poor man was poor, and slept more securely; sleep came more easily to the hard ground than to the silvered bed. Attend to the cares of the rich, and compare the securities of the poor. But let this rich man hear, that he be not proud in mind nor hope in the uncertainty of riches. Let him use the world, as not using it. Let him know that he is walking a road and has entered into these riches as into an inn. Let him take refreshment, he is a traveler; let him refresh himself and pass on; he does not take with him what he found in the inn. Another will be a traveler, and he himself will have it but will not carry it off. All here will leave what here they acquired. *Naked,* he says, *I came forth from my mother's womb; naked I shall return to the earth. The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away*[^13]—he has not taken away, because *to you the poor man is left*[^14]—*Naked I came forth from my mother's womb, naked I shall return to the earth*[^15].

**7.** Hear another poor man: *We brought nothing into this world, but neither can we carry anything away. Having food and covering, let us be content with these. For those who wish to become rich fall into temptation and many desires, foolish and harmful, which plunge men into destruction and perdition. The root of all evils is avarice: which some, coveting, have strayed from the faith and entangled themselves in many sorrows*[^16]. Who are these who *have strayed from the faith and entangled themselves in many sorrows? Those who wish to become rich*. Now let that ragged man answer me. Let us see whether he does not wish to become rich; let us see, let us ask him, whether he does not wish to become rich; let him answer, let him not lie. I hear the tongue, but I question the conscience. Let him say whether he does not wish to become rich. But if he does, he has already fallen *into temptation and many desires, foolish and harmful*. For I do not say wealth, but *desires*. Whence? Because he wishes to become rich. What follows? *Many desires, foolish and harmful, which plunge men into destruction and perdition*[^17]. Do you see where you are? Why do you display to me no resources, when I convict you of so great desires? Behold, now compare the two. This one is rich, that one is poor. But this one is already rich, he does not wish to become rich. This one is rich, either from his parents or from gifts and inheritances. Let us suppose, let us grant: he is rich even from iniquities. Now he does not wish to add, he has imposed a measure, fixed a limit to his desire, now in his heart he is enlisted in the service of devotion.

**8.** "He is rich," you say. I answer, "He is rich." Again you, the accuser, answer and say, "He is rich from iniquity." What if he makes friends of the mammon of iniquity? The Lord knew what he was saying; certainly he did not err, when he commanded, *Make for yourselves friends of the mammon of iniquity, that they too may receive you into the eternal dwellings*[^18]. What if this rich man does this? Now he ends his desire, he practices devotion. You have nothing, but you wish to become rich. You will fall into temptation. But perhaps you became very poor and very destitute, because you had some paternal property, I know not what, which sustained you, and some slander of a rival took it away. You groan, I hear, you accuse the times. What you groan over, if you could, you would do. Or do we not see, or are not all things full of daily examples? Yesterday he was groaning, because he was losing his own; today, belonging to the greater man, he seizes another's.

**9.** We have found a true poor man, we have found one devout and humble, not trusting in himself, a true poor man, a member of the poor man who for our sake became poor, though he was rich. See our rich man, who for our sake *became poor, though he was rich*[^19]; see that rich one: *All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^20]. It is more to make gold than to have it. You are rich in gold, silver, cattle, household, lands, fruits: you could not create these for yourself. See that rich one: *All things were made through him*. See that poor one: *The Word was made flesh and dwelt among us*[^21]. Who can worthily think of his riches—how he makes, who is not made; how he creates, uncreated; forms, unformed; remaining, makes changeable things; eternal, makes temporal things? Who can worthily think of his riches? Let us think of his poverty, lest perhaps, being poor, we grasp even that. He is conceived in a virginal female womb, enclosed in his mother's inward parts. O poverty! He is born in a narrow lodging, wrapped in infant cloths, laid in a manger, becomes food for poor beasts of burden; then the Lord of heaven and earth, the creator of angels, the maker and founder of all things visible and invisible, sucks, wails, is nursed, grows, endures the ages, conceals his majesty; afterward is seized, despised, scourged, mocked, spat upon, buffeted, crowned with thorns, hung on wood, pierced with a lance. O poverty! Behold the head of the poor whom I seek, of which poor man we have found a true poor member.

**10.** Let us briefly seek the orphan, because we have grown weary in seeking the poor man. Lord Jesus, I seek the orphan, weary I seek. Answer quickly, that I may find. *Call no one,* he says, *father on earth*[^22]. The orphan on earth has found an immortal Father in heaven. *Call no one,* he says, *father on earth*. This orphan has been found. Let this orphan pray. Let us hear him and imitate him. What is his prayer? *For my father and my mother have left me*. *My father,* he says, *and my mother have left me; but the Lord took me up*[^23]. If, then, *blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven*[^24], *to you the poor man is left*. If *my father and my mother have left me, but the Lord took me up*, *you will be a helper to the orphan*[^25].
