# Sermon 306/B: A Sermon Delivered on the Twelfth of the Kalends of September on the Feast of the Martyr Quadratus

**1.** The Lord our God, to whom together we give thanks, has granted that we should see you and be seen by you. And if this is the reason why *our mouth was filled with joy and our tongue with exultation*[^1]—because we have seen one another in mortal flesh—what will our joy be when we see ourselves there, where we shall fear nothing about one another? The Apostle says: *rejoicing in hope*[^2]. Our joy, then, which is now, is in hope, not yet in reality. *But hope that is seen is not hope,* he says; *for what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience*[^3]. But if pilgrim companions rejoice with one another on the road, what joy will they obtain in the homeland? On this road the martyrs strove, and by striving they walked, and by walking they did not stick fast. For those who love walk; for we run to God not by steps but by affections. Our road, then, seeks those who walk. There are three kinds of men that it hates: the one who remains behind, the one who turns back, the one who strays. From these three evil kinds may our step, with the Lord's help, be claimed and defended. Now indeed, when we are walking, one walks more slowly, another more quickly; yet both walk. So those who remain behind must be roused, those who turn back must be called back, those who wander must be led onto the road, the slow must be exhorted, the swift imitated. He who does not advance has remained behind on the road; he who perhaps turns aside from a better resolution to what, being worse, he had left behind, has turned back; he who deserts the faith has strayed from the road. Let us be reasonably with the slow—but called on by the swifter—yet with those who are walking.

**2.** Who is it that does not advance? He who has thought himself wise; he who has said: It is enough for me to be what I am; he who has not heeded the one who said: *Forgetting what lies behind, and straining forward to what lies ahead, I press on according to my purpose toward the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus*[^4]. He called himself a runner, he called himself a follower; he did not remain behind, he did not look back, far be it that he strayed, he who taught the way itself, who both held and showed it. And that we might imitate his swiftness, he said: *Be imitators of me, as I also am of Christ*[^5]. We reckon, then, dearest brothers, that we are walking the road with you. If we are slow, go before us; we do not envy, we are looking for someone to follow. But if you think that we are walking swiftly, run with us. There is one thing toward which we all hasten, both those who walk more slowly and those more quickly. The Apostle himself says this: *But one thing I do: forgetting what lies behind, and straining forward to what lies ahead, I press on according to my purpose toward the prize of the upward call of God in Christ Jesus*[^6]. The order of the words is: *But one thing I do, I press on*[^7]. And in order to say this, what did he say above? *Brothers, I do not reckon myself to have laid hold of it*[^8]. Behold one who does not remain behind, who does not reckon himself to have laid hold; behold one who does not want to be a pilgrim forever, behold one who does not stick fast on the road, behold one who will rejoice in the homeland. *I,* he says. Who? *I* who *labored more than all of them*[^9]. And yet, where he says: *I labored more than all of them,* he did not say: I do not reckon myself to have laid hold. There the "I" is well placed, where there is room for humility, not for pride. *I,* he says, as far as concerns me, *do not reckon myself to have laid hold*[^10]. This is his own. But where he says: *I labored more than all of them,* there follows: *yet not I, but the grace of God with me*[^11]. Does not the grace of God lay hold? Rightly there the "I"; for not to lay hold belongs to our weakness; to lay hold belongs to the help of divine grace, not to human weakness. Who, then, is there to show us, who is there to teach us, who is there worthily to suggest to us, how it can be true—what nevertheless is true beyond doubt—that there is nothing of ours in us except sin? Let piety know this, let weakness accuse itself of this, let love long to be healed of this. *Not that I have already received it,* he says, *or am already perfect*[^12]. And then he added: *Brothers, I do not reckon myself to have laid hold*[^13]. And when he exhorted them to run, and to stretch the heart toward what lies ahead, he said: *Let as many of us as are perfect be of this mind*[^14]. He had said above: *Not that I have already received it or am already perfect;* and afterward he says: *Let as many of us as are perfect be of this mind*[^15]. You had said that you, so great an apostle, were imperfect; now you find many who are perfect, and you say: *Let as many of us as are perfect be of this mind.* There is, then, a perfection and a perfection.

**3.** He is a perfect traveler who is not yet a perfect arriver. The perfect traveler approaches well, walks well, keeps to the road; but still he is a traveler, he has not yet laid hold. For surely, if he walks, and walks on the road, he walks somewhere, and is trying to arrive at something. To where, then, he was trying to arrive, the Apostle had not yet laid hold. And he exhorts the perfect to know that they are not yet perfect, to know their imperfection. The perfection of the traveler is to know that he has not yet arrived at that toward which he tends; for he knows how much he has traversed, how much remains to him. Let us know, then, that we are not perfect, as many of us as are perfect, lest we remain imperfect. What shall we say, brothers? Was not the martyr Quadratus perfect? What is more perfect than a square (quadratus)? The sides are equal, the shape is equal on every side; whichever way you turn it, it stands, it does not topple. O beautiful name, showing the figure, and pointing to the thing to come! Quadratus was called this even before, and was not yet crowned; he had not yet appeared in the trial through which he might be that square; and yet, when he was named, the one predestined before the foundation of the world was foretold, and, so that he might be called this, this in him suffers along with him, that it might be fulfilled; and yet he was walking, and yet he was still on the road; and, as long as he was in this body, everything was feared—both that he might remain behind, and that he might turn back, and that he might go off course. But now he has run, he has finished the road, he has stood on solid ground; he has been built together by the craftsman of the Lord's ark, which by way of figure was commanded to be built of squared timbers. Now he fears no trial: he heard the one who called, and he too heard the one who summoned, he followed the savior, he carries the indweller. He despised the world when it flattered, he conquered it when it terrified, he escaped it when it raged. Great, brothers, is the glory of the martyrs, first in the Church; whatever others there are, they are second. For it was not without reason that it was said to certain people: *You have not yet resisted to the point of blood in your struggle against sin*[^16]. When does he endure, when does he bear the world raging, who cannot scorn it flattering?

**4.** The same Apostle says: *I speak in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh; for just as you presented your members to serve uncleanness unto iniquity, so now present your members to serve righteousness unto sanctification*[^17]. It is a very great thing that he seems to exhort. Let each one measure himself by these words of the Apostle: let him not stroke himself with flattery; let him weigh himself, and tell himself the truth. What does he expect to hear from me? Let him tell it to himself. I have set out to set before you a mirror, where each one may look at himself. I am not the brightness of the mirror, which reports his face to one who gazes at it. For I mean now those faces which we have within; those I can reach by the ear, I cannot see. But I do set before you a mirror: let each one look at himself, and report himself to himself. Take these very words of the Apostle, which I have recalled, in place of a mirror. *I speak in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh: just as you presented your members to serve uncleanness unto iniquity, so now present your members to serve righteousness unto sanctification*[^18]. What is the "so"? As that, so this too. When you presented your members as weapons of iniquity to sin for uncleanness, did it delight you? I ask: attend, answer. Did it delight you? I hear you answering, even while silent; for you would not do it, unless it delighted you. As, then, you presented your members to serve uncleanness unto iniquity, and did these things with delight, so let righteousness someday delight you. God does not want you to do it from fear, he says to you; for surely you did not do that from fear? *So,* he says, *so. Just as you presented your members to serve uncleanness unto iniquity, so now present your members to serve righteousness unto sanctification.* To righteousness you are driven by fear; to uncleanness you ran by love. What is more beautiful than righteousness, I ask you? It is worthy to be loved at least in the way uncleanness was loved. When you ran to uncleanness, you were forbidden, and you went; you offended your father, and you ran; you were ready to be disinherited, and not to be parted from its wickedness. What will you say? Righteousness requires of you this, which uncleanness had from you. You have heard the Gospel: *I have not come to bring peace upon the earth, but a sword*[^19]. He said he would separate children from their parents. So heed that sword. Perhaps you want to serve God, and your father forbids it? When you loved uncleanness, you ran even though your father forbade it; now righteousness forbids you to be its lover; here too you have found a father who forbids. Exert your liberty, you who then exerted your lust. You were ready then to be disinherited, and not to be parted from the wickedness of that uncleanness; be ready to be disinherited, and not to be parted from the beauty of righteousness. It is a great thing, but it is just. Who is there who would dare to say: But uncleanness ought to have been loved more than righteousness? Meanwhile righteousness makes a step for you. Surely, it says, I am unlike it; surely there is a very great distance between the darkness of that uncleanness and my light, between that wickedness and my beauty, between its comeliness and my comeliness; surely there is a very great distance. Meanwhile I set down a step. So I will it: for I am owed more, I am altogether owed more; by as much as I differ, by so much am I owed. But *I speak in human terms,* I defer the divine. Why do I defer the divine? *I speak in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh.* Therefore the "so," because I am still sparing your weakness. Therefore, just as you presented your members to serve uncleanness unto iniquity, so now indeed you owe more, but at least walk thus, arrive at this. But pass on from here too. Meanwhile *I speak in human terms*: but, as that, so this too.

**5.** Was Quadratus like this? Not merely like this, but more, and worthily more. For attend to those uncleannesses, and see what godliness and love, and the beauty of righteousness, and the sweetness of sanctification require of you more; attend to what they require of you more. The lover of uncleanness does not want his evil deeds to be known: he fears lest he be condemned for them, he fears prison for them, he fears the judge, he fears the executioner. He covets the chastity of another man's wife, he deceives the husband; he seeks darkness, he dreads a witness, he is afraid of the judge; he fears to be known, because he fears to be punished for it. But now that which the beauty of righteousness requires of you more—which the Apostle meanwhile deferred when he said: *I speak in human terms because of the weakness of your flesh*[^20]—hear it from the Lord: *What I tell you in the dark,* that is, in secret, *speak in the light, and what you hear whispered, proclaim upon the housetops*[^21]. Does an adulterer proclaim his disgrace upon the housetop? But why does he not only not proclaim it upon the housetop, but seek to be hidden by the roof? Why does he do this? Because the love of uncleanness could go only so far: for he fears to be uncovered, he fears to be punished. But these lovers of that invisible beauty, lovers of that comeliness in which is he who is *beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*[^22]; these lovers, then, of that beauty—from where comes it that they do not fear to proclaim upon the housetops what they have heard whispered? Ask from where the former fears: lest he be recognized and punished. Ask from where this latter does not fear: the Lord himself, going on, added it. For when he had said: *What I tell you in the darkness, say in the light, and what you hear whispered, proclaim upon the housetops,* he said: *Do not fear those who kill the body*[^23]—so that what you hear in the dark you may say in the light, so that what you hear whispered you may proclaim upon the housetops, *do not fear those who kill the body.* Let the adulterer fear those who kill the body: for when that adulterer has lost the body, he loses the workshop of his pleasures. Let him fear to lose the body who lives by the body; for all that he covets he works through the body; therefore in him pleasure does not suffice; he burns with desire, until he comes to the most unclean pleasure of the body. But you, man of God, if you have the eyes of the heart, by which you may see the beauty of love, by which you may see the beauty of godliness—if you have the eyes of the heart, see by what you may enjoy your beloved; for to enjoy her, you do not seek the members of the body. Let the lover of filthy pleasure fear that his body be destroyed; but *peace on earth to men of good will*[^24].

**6.** How far are you from this love, O Christian! Would that you might arrive even at that human level, and do good with delight, as before you sinned with delight. For if you do good with delight, if you believe in Christ with delight, if you enjoy his wisdom with delight to the measure of your capacity, if you hear and do the commandment with delight, then that human level has begun to be in you, on account of your weakness. You have begun now to have a good gift, but you have not yet fulfilled the Square. But, as I said, if you have arrived, draw near; there is still a road, do not remain behind. There is still something for you to do; do not fear, and hide your good deeds by being afraid. Your critics, your harassers, what do they say to you? You, a great apostle! your feet hang down from heaven for you: where do you come from? And you are afraid to say: From the Church, lest it be said to you: Are you not ashamed, you bearded man, to go where widows and little old women go? Lest you hear this, you are afraid to say: I was in church. How will you bear a persecutor, you who shudder at a mocker? And surely it is a time of peace. They ought to blush: so many blush who have come over, and so few do not blush who have stayed behind. And these have come over: to where? But those have stayed behind: where? These have come over to the light of peace; those have stayed behind in the darkness of confusion. Are you not ashamed to be ashamed of what should be gloried in? They are not ashamed of shameful things: and are you ashamed of things to be gloried in? And where is it, what you have heard: *Draw near to him and be enlightened, and your faces shall not blush*[^25]?

**7.** I have said these things, my brothers, because, as I know and greatly grieve, the tongues of a few pagans are feared—pagans not raging, but only mocking—and the minds of those who want to believe are held back, since they do not yield to the exhortations of Christians. And why more? or what am I to say? You see some pagan or other being harassed, lest he become a Christian, and you, a Christian, are silent; you count it a great thing that he spares you, that is, that he does not mock you. When he frightens that man off, do you say in your heart: Thanks be to God? He said nothing to me. You flee, not in the flesh, but in the mind. You stand there, and you flee; you fear lest the slanderous tongue be turned against you, and you do not come to the aid of the one whom you ought to gain for Christ. You do not help, you are silent; as I said, you flee not in the flesh but in the mind, you are a hireling, you see the wolf coming, and you flee. And what more am I to say? Just now we all heard it. Let the Lord frighten us: he who is to be loved is himself to be feared. *Whoever,* he says, *is ashamed of me before men* (and see when he was saying these things: when the world did not believe, but raged). *Whoever is ashamed of me before men, I will be ashamed of him before my Father who is in heaven; but whoever confesses me before men, I too will confess him before my Father who is in heaven*[^26]. Do you want to be denied by Christ, or to be confessed by Christ? You will keep your mocker far off, when you have made yourself a denier of Christ. What he promises will come: he who has shown forth so much—will he be found false about the day of judgment alone? Far be it. Let them keep their faithlessness—indeed, let them rather be rid of their faithlessness; but set yourselves before them as men to be imitated in confession, not to be overcome in silence. For if they find Christians who are stronger defenders of the weak, by the assertion of the faith, by the freedom of confessing, by the prudence of teaching, by the love of instructing, they will be silent, believe me; for they have nothing to say. It is an empty voice, a clanging cymbal; what has ceased in their temples has remained in their mouths.
