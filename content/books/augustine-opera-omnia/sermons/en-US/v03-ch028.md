# Sermon 70: Again on the Words of the Gospel of Matthew 11:28-30: "Come to Me, All You Who Labor and Are Burdened, and I Will Refresh You," and What Follows

**1.** It seems wonderful to some, my brothers, when they hear the Lord saying: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me that I am meek and humble of heart, and you will find rest for your souls. For my yoke is gentle, and my burden is light*[^1]: and they consider that those who put their neck under that yoke undaunted and received that burden on most gentle shoulders are harassed and exercised by so great difficulties of this world, that they seem to be called not from labors to rest, but from rest to labor; since the Apostle too says: *All who wish to live piously in Christ will suffer persecution*[^2]. So someone says: "How is the yoke gentle, and the burden light, since to bear that yoke and burden is nothing else than to live piously in Christ?" And how is it said: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you:* and not rather said, "Come, you who are at rest, that you may labor"? For he found even those at rest, whom he hired into the vineyard, to bear the heat of the day[^3]. And under that gentle yoke and light burden, we hear the Apostle say: *In all things commending ourselves as the ministers of God, in much patience, in tribulations, in necessities, in straits, in stripes*, etc.[^4] And in another place in the same Epistle: *From the Jews five times I received forty lashes less one. Three times I was beaten with rods, once I was stoned, three times I suffered shipwreck, night and day I was in the deep of the sea*[^5]; and the other perils which can indeed be numbered, but cannot be endured except with the help of the Holy Spirit.

**2.** So all those things which he mentioned, harsh and heavy, he endured more frequently and more abundantly: but surely the Holy Spirit was present to him, who, in the corruption of the outer man, renewed the inner from day to day[^6], and, having tasted the spiritual rest in the affluence of the delights of God, in the hope of future blessedness softened all present harsh things, and relieved all heavy things. Behold how sweet a yoke of Christ he bore, and how light a burden: so that all those things which, enumerated above as hard and monstrous, every hearer shudders at, he called a light tribulation; gazing with inner and faithful eyes at how great a price the future life is to be bought with temporal things—not to suffer the eternal labors of the impious, and to enjoy without any anxiety the eternal happiness of the just. Men allow themselves to be cut and burned, that the pains, not of an eternal but of a somewhat more lasting ulcer, may be redeemed at the price of sharper pains. In the languid and uncertain life of the briefest and final furlough, the soldier is worn down by most monstrous wars; restless in labors for more years perhaps than he will rest at leisure. By what tempests and storms, by what horrible and dreadful savagery of an unfavorable sky and sea are the merchants beset, that they may acquire windy riches, full of greater perils and tempests than those by which they were acquired? What heats, what colds, what perils from horses, from pits, from precipices, from rivers, from wild beasts do hunters endure? what labor of hungering and thirsting, what great straits of the cheapest and foulest food and drink, that they may catch a beast? and sometimes not even the flesh of the very beast, for which they endure these so great things, is necessary for their feasts. Although even if a boar and a stag be caught, it is more sweet to the mind of the hunter that it has been caught, than to the palate of the eater that it has been cooked. By how great torments of almost daily blows is the tender age of boys subjected? By how great troubles of vigils and abstinence are they exercised even in the schools, not for the sake of learning wisdom, but for the riches and honors of vanity, that they may learn to speak numbers, and letters, and eloquent fallacies?

**3.** But in all these things, those who do not love them suffer the same things as heavy: but those who love them suffer the same things indeed, but do not seem to suffer them as heavy. For all things savage and monstrous, love makes utterly easy and almost nothing. How much more certainly and easily, then, does love do toward true blessedness, what craving did, as far as it could, toward misery? How easily is any temporal adversity endured, that eternal punishment may be avoided, and eternal rest gained? Not without reason that vessel of election said with vast gladness: *The sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the glory to come, which will be revealed in us*[^7]. Behold whence that yoke is sweet, and the burden light. And if it is narrow for the few who choose it, yet it is easy for all who love it. The Psalmist says: *For the words of your lips I have kept hard ways*[^8]. But the things that are hard to those laboring, grow gentle for those same ones loving. On account of which it was so wrought by the dispensation of divine godliness, that the inner man, who is renewed from day to day[^9], no longer set under the Law, but already under grace, unburdened of the loads of innumerable observances—which was indeed a heavy yoke, but fittingly imposed on a hard neck—by the ease of simple faith, and good hope, and holy love, whatever of troubles that prince who was cast outside[^10] brought from without upon the outer man, it might become light by the inner joy. For nothing is so easy to a good will, as it is to itself: and this suffices for God. However much, then, this world rages, most truly the angels cried out at the birth of the Lord in flesh: *Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will*[^11]: because the yoke of him who was born is sweet, and the burden light. And as the Apostle says: *God is faithful, who does not let us be tempted beyond what we can bear; but makes with the temptation also a way out, that we may be able to endure*[^12].
