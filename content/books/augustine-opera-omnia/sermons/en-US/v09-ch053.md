# Sermon 335/H: On the Birthday of the Martyrs

**1.** All the solemnities of the most blessed martyrs admonish us how much the present life is to be despised, and how much the future is to be sought. Therefore our Lord Jesus Christ himself, the prince of martyrs, whose passion was the price of the martyrs, who was born not by the condition of a mortal but by the dignation of mercy, both willed to die and to rise again. For he would not have died, if he had not willed to die; nor would he have risen, unless he had died. So he willed both for this reason: by dying he commended to us the contempt of the present life; by rising, the desire of the future life. Behold in my passion and resurrection both of these for you, that is, both what you may endure in this life, and what you may desire in the future. So one life, laborious, full of troubles, trials, fears, and pains, by which this world runs its course, Christ showed us in his passion; but that life where no one will grieve, no one will fear, where no one will die, no one will be reconciled because no one will quarrel, he showed us by his resurrection, as though saying: Behold what you may suffer and what you may hope, that you may suffer the passions, that you may hope the resurrection. And what a resurrection! Not such as was the resurrection of Lazarus dying again. *Christ rising, rising from the dead,* as the Apostle says, *dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him*[^1]. I know that you desire such a life. For who would not desire it? Even the impious gentiles want to be immortal. But they do not believe that they can be immortal. For those who have not received the faith have lost the hope of immortality. So it is no great thing to desire immortality: for this is the desire even of the impious; but it is indeed great to believe that we will be immortal, and so to live that we may be able to come to that very immortality. Therefore every man wants to have, if he could, the power of an angel, but he does not want to have the righteousness of an angel. He wants to have immortality, and he does not want to have godliness. They want where one arrives, and they refuse the way by which one arrives.

**2.** So you, brothers, I admonish, exhort, and beseech, that as you devoutly celebrate the solemnity of the martyrs, so you love the holy conduct of the martyrs. They are martyrs, but they were men. By their prayers they help us, but they were what we are. So do not despair of their merits. For he who gave to them, can give also to us. For we do not worship them as gods, but honor them on account of God, but we worship that God who is both our Lord and theirs. They by his grace are his friends, let us be at least his servants. Nevertheless if we truly love the martyrs and follow their footsteps, why shall not we too be friends of God, by his grace, not by our merit? In our happiness he himself is praised, who makes us, from wretched, happy. For we through ourselves could make ourselves wretched; but to make ourselves happy we are not able. Let us run to him, let us supplicate him, and what they received, we too will receive.

**3.** Yesterday I exhorted Your Charity: whoever of you are catechumens, that you hasten to the washing of regeneration, all delays set aside; whoever were living damnably in sins and shameful things, uncleannesses, that you change your life, do penance, do not despair of life; whoever also are doing penance (and what is sweet to you is not the penance for sins but the license), that you change your life and be all prepared to be reconciled according to the will of God, since what God has willed is better for man, not what we will. Let us indeed ask him who is able to free us from all things, and to give us peace, as he freed the three boys from the furnace.
