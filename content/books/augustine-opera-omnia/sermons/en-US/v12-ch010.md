# Sermon 272C

**1.** We celebrate the annual solemnity of the day on which our Lord Jesus Christ sent the Holy Spirit, the Paraclete, whom he had previously promised to the disciples. For he had said: *He cannot come unless I go away*. Thus on the fortieth day after his Resurrection—the solemnity we celebrated ten days ago—he ascended into heaven; and, not forgetting his pledge, today he sent the Holy Spirit he had promised. The hundred and twenty who had gathered together and were faithfully awaiting that same promise of the Lord spoke in every language. For God was pleased at that time to signify the Holy Spirit's presence in this way: whoever received him spoke in every language.[^1]

**2.** First, then, let us briefly consider why he said when promising him: *He cannot come unless I go away*. For the Holy Spirit is not separable from God's Son, nor was he unable to come in the presence of him from whom he cannot depart. But the Lord was showing the still-weak disciples the form of a servant, in which *the Word became flesh and dwelt among us*. Even carnal people could look upon the Lord's flesh, whereas the form of God, in which he did not consider equality with God robbery, can be contemplated only by spiritual people. That is why he said: *He cannot come unless I go away*, as though saying, “As long as your carnal affection is occupied with my flesh, your spiritual vision does not receive my divinity.” For he had promised that through the Holy Spirit's gift they would be led into all truth, as it is written: *Cleansing their hearts by faith*. And *blessed are* *the pure in heart, for they shall see God*. Thus the Lord's bodily form withdrew from their bodily eyes, so that through the Holy Spirit the Lord's divinity might be made known to their hearts.[^2]

**3.** Next let us see why the sign of the Holy Spirit's presence then was that those who received him spoke in every language. It is not that the Holy Spirit is not given even now; yet those who receive him do not speak in every language, the manifest miracle through which he revealed himself at that time. We must understand, dearest brothers, that this is the Holy Spirit through whom *charity is poured into our hearts*. And because charity was to gather God's Church throughout the whole world, what even one person receiving the Holy Spirit could do then—speak in every language—the unity of the Church itself, gathered by the Holy Spirit, does now: it speaks in every language. Hence if anyone says to one of us, “You have received the Holy Spirit: why do you not speak in every language?” he ought to answer, “Certainly I speak in every language, because I am in that body of Christ, that is, the Church, which speaks in every language.” For what else did God signify then through the Holy Spirit's presence but that his Church would speak in every language? Thus what the Lord had promised was fulfilled: *No one puts new wine into old wineskins; rather, they put new wine into new wineskins, and both are preserved*. Rightly, then, when they were heard speaking in every language, some said: *These people are full of new wine*. For they had already become new wineskins, renewed by the grace of holiness, so that, filled with new wine, that is, the Holy Spirit, they might overflow in speaking every language and by that most manifest miracle prefigure the Catholic Church that would exist in the languages of all nations. Thus heretics are rightly believed not to have the Holy Spirit, since they are separated from the body that has deserved to resound in every language throughout all nations.[^3]

**4.** Celebrate this day, then, as members of the unity of Christ's body. For you do not celebrate in vain if you are what you celebrate, cleaving to that Church which the Lord fills with the Holy Spirit and recognizes as his own as she grows throughout the world, and by which he is recognized as her own, like a bridegroom and bride. He has not lost his own bride; no one substitutes another for her. For to you who dwell among all nations—that is, to Christ's Church, Christ's members, Christ's body, Christ's bride—the Apostle says: *Bearing with one another in love, striving to preserve the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace*. Notice that where he commanded us to bear with one another, he placed love; where he named the Spirit of unity, he showed the bond of peace. This is God's house, built of living stones, in which such a master of the household delights to dwell, whose eyes must not be offended by the ruin of division.[^4]

[^1]: John 16:7; cf. Acts 2:1–4.
[^2]: John 16:7; John 1:14; cf. Phil 2:7; cf. John 16:13; Acts 15:9; Matt 5:8.
[^3]: Rom 5:5; Matt 9:17; Luke 5:37; Acts 2:13.
[^4]: Eph 4:2–3; cf. 1 Pet 2:5.
