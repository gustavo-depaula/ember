# Sermon 265/E: On the Fortieth Day, the Ascension of the Lord, a Fragment

**1.** . . . poured out. I have repaid. Your price went out from his side. On the wood hung the purse of your price. I have repaid you, then, the blood of Christ. I have repaid the resurrection. I have repaid the Holy Spirit sent by him. I have repaid the Church spread through the whole world. I promised that idols would be broken, the temples of demons overthrown. It is done. It is repaid. I promised that the Church would have exercise through heresies and would not perish, and that, certain useless branches being cut off, the vine would be pruned, not amputated. And this I have repaid. It is done. I promised the blood and crowns of martyrs. I have repaid it. Of the day of judgment alone do you still hold me a debtor. Why do you hasten? That too will be repaid. And would that, as he will come, so he may find you ready!

**2.** Of Christ, then, we have sung: *You are the Lord most high over all the earth. You are greatly exalted above all gods*[^1]. For who is exalted except one who had been humbled? See him humbled and see him exalted. The Apostle tells you both. For he was exalted from the beginning, because *in the beginning was the Word.* This height lacks a beginning, lacks time, because *through him all things were made.* What, then, does the Apostle say of him? *Since he was in the form of God*, he says, *he did not think it robbery to be equal to God*; because it was nature, it was not robbery. For he did not claim for himself equality with God, but was always equal because born equal; therefore *since he was in the form of God, he did not think it robbery to be equal to God.* You have heard his ineffable height. Hear also his humility. *He emptied himself*, he says. How? Did he then lose what he was? No. And how *did he empty himself?* Not by losing what he was, but by taking up what he was not. Hear the Apostle himself expounding this. When he had said: *he emptied himself*, as though we asked how, he added: *taking the form of a servant*, not by losing what he was, but by taking up what he was not, existing in the form of God, afterward taking the form of a servant. The emptying, then, was to take up lowly things, not to lose lofty ones. *Taking the form of a servant* he emptied himself. Within the man God lay hidden. If what was within were seen, that man would not be crucified. If that man were not crucified, that blood would not be poured out. If that blood were not poured out, the world would not be redeemed. *He emptied himself*, then, *taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men*—for in the form of God he was not made, because *through him all things were made*, so that it should not be said: The Word was made. Therefore he who made was made, lest what he made should perish—. *Made in the likeness of men and found in fashion as a man. He humbled himself, made obedient unto death, even the death of the cross.* See to what extent humbled. And what follows? *For which reason God exalted him and gave him the name that is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven, on earth, and in the underworld, and every tongue confess that the Lord Jesus Christ is in the glory of God the Father*[^2]. *Greatly exalted above all gods, and you alone are most high over all the earth.*

**3.** *Greatly exalted above all gods.* Above what gods? For there are gods. The Psalm shows us: *God stood in the synagogue of gods*[^3], calling gods men born of man. But those are adopted sons. One is so by nature, the others by grace. One Son by nature, the rest by grace. Our Lord Jesus Christ Son by nature according to that which he is the Word. Who are the adopted? The rest who believed in him. Those are men, a man among them, but *greatly exalted above all gods.* There is much difference between you and them. You were born, they were born; but the condition is one thing, the mercy another. You died, they die; but the necessity is one thing, the kindness another. We all die by necessity, he alone died by kindness. We all are born by condition, he was born by mercy. *Greatly* therefore *exalted above all gods*, because you are a man and they are men, as it were they are compared to you, but the virginal birth is one thing, the desire-born conception another. You rose, they will rise, but it is one thing that was said: *His flesh did not see corruption* and another: *He was laid with his fathers and saw corruption*[^4]. The flesh of all who die is handed over to corruption; on the third day you rose, corruption could work nothing in you. In heaven you went before; at the right hand of the Father you sat; all confess you, angels, men, the heavens, the earth, the underworld.

**4.** *You are greatly exalted above all gods*, well was it said to him and most excellently through prophecy, because what we see fulfilled—for here too it is fulfilled: *Hear, daughter, and see—be exalted above the heavens, O God*, of this it was said: *You are greatly exalted above all gods.* To whom did he say this: *You are greatly exalted above all gods?* To Christ. And what of the Church? *And your glory over all the earth. Be exalted above the heavens, O God*[^5]. Christ we did not see. The apostles saw him. They were there. He led them to the Mount of Olives, and they asked him about the end of the age, and he said to them: It is not yours to know the times which the Father has set in his own power, but you will receive the power of the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem and in all Judea and to the ends of the earth. *These things being said, a cloud received him*[^6]. After these words he wished to say nothing. He willed those to be the last, which he might more strongly commend to our heart, about the future Church throughout the whole world, because many were going to make for themselves their own flocks, to gather disciples after themselves and make for themselves heresies and schisms in diverse places. There the vine itself holds the whole from which the branches are cut off. Where a branch is cut off, there it remains. The vine grows and takes up the whole, holds the whole. So the Church did.

**5.** Hence he granted the Church, as he promised, the bridegroom the bride. *Be exalted above the heavens, O God.* What God? Of whom did we speak today except of Christ: *Exalted above all gods and over the earth* his *glory?* What is your glory? Your Church, your spouse. Hence the Apostle says: *A man indeed ought not to veil his head, because he is the image and glory of God. But the woman is the glory of the man*[^7]. The wife is the glory of the husband. Who is the wife of that great king? The whole Church. He where? *Be exalted above the heavens, O God.* He where? *Greatly exalted above all gods.* She where? And over all the earth your glory. A great sacred sign! We have been invited to a wedding, and the wedding itself is we ourselves. In the weddings of men, the bride is one, the invited are others. We are both invited and are ourselves the bride. For we are the Church, and we are invited within the Church. And to what are we invited? If it is now done, beloved, if we now see it, if we now behold it fulfilled, if what we say cannot be denied: *Over all the earth your glory*, when he comes, what will we be? But only if we guard what we have received.

[^2]: Phil 2:6-11
[^3]: Ps 82:1 (81:1)
[^4]: Acts 13:37, 36
[^5]: Ps 57:5 (56:6)
[^6]: Acts 1:7-9
[^7]: 1 Cor 11:7
