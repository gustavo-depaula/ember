# Sermon 214/A: Augustine in His Explanation of the Creed

**1** ] Plainly, to believe too pertains to knowing.

**2** ] We do not believe whatever we know, but we know whatever we rightly believe.

**3** ] We know by seeing, we know by believing.

**4** ] For if a fellow citizen speaks with me, and in this a fellow countryman and provincial contradicts me, so as to harass me as though I did not know because I have not seen, I ask of him whence he knows that there is an Alexandria which he has not seen, that there are so many cities in which he has never been, which yet he so knows to exist that he would even securely swear it if he were challenged.

**5** ] Behold, I put the question: Whence do you know that there is an Alexandria? He will say: I believed it. I will answer: Whom did you believe? What else will he say but that he believed men? You, then, believed about Alexandria those who saw it, I about God those who prophesied. If you consider the truth, if you weigh the weights of the testimonies, I rather believed fit witnesses than you. For about Alexandria you believed those who, being there, could see it present to them, I believed those who said the things that are now done before they were to be.
