# Sermon 293/D: On the Birthday of Saint John the Baptist

**1.** Great is the Lord's own testimony about John the Baptist, not the Evangelist, whose birthday, a great day, we celebrate today. For our Savior, his Lord and ours, says of him—what else but the truth?—*Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist*. See whose solemnity is celebrated today: the one than whom no greater arose among those born of women. But the Lord himself added: *Yet the one who is lesser is greater than he in the kingdom of heaven*. What does lesser than he mean? Lesser in age, greater in power. John is the highest human being, but still human. Of the Lord Christ, however, it is far too little to say the highest human being, because he is God and human. We have stated the Lord's testimony about John; let us state John's testimony about the Lord. Recall and retain the Lord's testimony about John that I mentioned: *Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist.* This is what Jesus says of John. What does John say of Jesus? First see how the Lord's testimony about John was fulfilled. He himself was thought to be the Christ. When the first reading was recited from the *Acts of the Apostles,* you heard what was narrated and recalled that John said: *What do you suppose I am? I am not the Christ*. Human error suspected something more, but humility confessed what he was. See how easy it was to exploit human error and boast of himself as the Christ. He did not do it: rightly great, more powerful by confessing than by neighing proudly. Would he have had to persuade them that he was the Christ? They already thought it. He could have confirmed their opinion and deceived them about what he was by saying he was what he was not. And if he had done this, where would he be? *You sent to John,* the Lord Jesus says to the Jews; *he was a burning and shining lamp, and you wished to exult for a time in his light. But I have testimony greater than John's.* A good lamp! Rightly it fled beneath the rock's feet, lest the wind of pride extinguish it.

**2.** See, then, beloved, how great John's greatness was and how the Lord's saying about him was fulfilled: *Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist.* Christ was certainly Lord—indeed, he is Lord—who existed before John, before Abraham, before Adam, before heaven and earth, because *everything was made through him*. He was certainly Lord, God walked on earth, and the Word had become flesh; yet John is not numbered among his disciples. Recall the disciples' names from first to last. John is there, but the Evangelist, not the Baptist. As far as human sight was concerned, the Baptist was not Christ's disciple; indeed, he himself gathered his own disciples in Christ's time. Finally, the Jews taunted Christ's disciples about John's disciples, making accusations and saying: *Why do John's disciples fast, but your disciples do not?* Christ had disciples, and John had them too; John baptized, and Christ baptized too. It is true that *among those born of women* *no one* *has arisen greater than John the Baptist*; yet he gathered disciples as though a rival, as though against Christ, but nevertheless for Christ. Because he was so great that he could be thought the Christ, he ought to bear witness to Christ. The one to whom the highest human being bears witness is more than human. 'I am not what you think. Certainly I am great enough for you to think this; I am not what you think.' *And* *who are you?* he was asked. *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness: Prepare the Lord's way*. See the forerunner, welcome the advance messenger, fear the judge. *Prepare the Lord's way, make his paths straight; every mountain and hill will be humbled, and every valley raised; crooked things will become straight, rough things smooth ways, and all flesh will see God's salvation*. Not me, but God's salvation. All flesh will see God's salvation. The lamp bears witness to the day, because Christ is day. What is John? A lamp. But why was a lamp needed? Because the day was hidden. It was hidden until he suffered, for he would not have suffered unless hidden. *For if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*.

**3.** Yet lest they resist the day too much, the Jews are confounded by the lamp: they are sent to John. He said: *I am not he;* the one among you is greater than I. How much greater? Certainly the Lord Christ had said: *Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist*; *but the one who is lesser*—speaking of himself, because he came later in the flesh—*is greater than he* *in the kingdom of heaven*. He called himself lesser; he did not say how much greater he was. Certainly, lest I seem to conceal anything, because this too is a useful meaning that does not stray from truth, I will state it as well. In the Lord's words, *Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist, but the one who is lesser is greater than he in the kingdom of heaven,* another meaning emerges from another division of the words. If you divide them thus, *Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist; but the one who is lesser in the kingdom of heaven is greater than he,* you understand the holy angels to be in the kingdom of heaven, and the statement to mean that the lesser among the holy angels is greater than all human beings. Either meaning accords with faith, because both this is true, that Christ is lesser in age and greater in majesty, and that is true, that however great a human being is, he is less than the least angel. Nevertheless, let us hear how much greater John confessed the Lord Jesus to be. Because he himself said, *The one* *who comes after me is greater than I,* you hear 'greater than I' and ask how much greater. If he is silent, let us surmise, but certainly believe: for John is a slave, Christ the Lord. Let John say how much: *I am not worthy,* he says, *to untie* *his sandal strap.* How much he would have humbled himself if he said he was worthy! If Christ stands or sits and you untie his sandal strap, I recognize it: he is Lord, you a slave. 'This is too little,' John says; 'I am not worthy even of this.' Therefore, answer us, if possible, why you are not among his disciples, why you gathered disciples outside. 'I,' John says, 'am I not his disciple? I am, but where you do not see.' *The one* *who has the bride is the bridegroom.* These are John's words about the Lord Christ: *The one* *who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the bridegroom's friend stands and hears him*. Surely he is not his disciple? Certainly a slave with Christ standing over him, a friend by Christ's gift. For he also said to his disciples: *I will no longer call you slaves, but friends*. Yet see the disciple within, hidden in the sanctuary's secret place. The bridegroom's friend stands and hears him. Let him stand and hear him, because unless he had heard, he would have fallen and been like the one of whom the Lord himself says: *He was a murderer from the beginning and did not stand in the truth*. The devil did not stand in the truth; the fallen one who casts others down fell. But John stood and heard him. What follows? *And he rejoices with joy because of the bridegroom's voice*. 'He is the Word; I am the voice,' John says. He is a voice, *and he rejoices with joy because of the bridegroom's voice.* For you are the voice and he the Word; yet he is both Word and voice. Why is the Lord both word and voice? Because *the Word became flesh*. For just as the word our heart bears is within our heart and hidden from those outside us—as what I am going to say now is hidden from you but not from me—the word is already in my heart. For it to proceed to you, a voice is taken up, and what was hidden in me reaches you; yet when it reaches you, it does not depart from me. If this is true of my word, what of God's Word? The one with the Father was hidden with the Father. To come out to us, flesh was taken up like a voice; he proceeded to us and did not depart from the Father. The Jews heard John saying these things, and also saying: *We have all received from his fullness*. What does *we all* mean? Whoever was a patriarch, prophet, righteous person, or preacher, John himself, than whom *no greater arose among those born of women*—all drank from that fountain, and therefore poured forth such words.

**4.** Therefore, the hostile Jews are confounded by the lamp while the day is hidden. They certainly were confounded. See how one day they questioned the Lord himself and said: *How* *long will you hold our souls in suspense? If you are the Christ, tell us openly*. They were preparing an accusation, not seeking truth. 'How long,' they said, *will you hold our souls?* How long will you keep us suspended? *If* *you are the Christ, tell us openly.* He told them: *I too ask you one question: tell me, where does John's baptism come from? Is it from heaven or from human beings?* Wisdom flashed; imprudence was checked. Those who had advanced toward him returned into themselves. They advanced with accusations and returned with hesitation, saying among themselves or within themselves: *If* *we say, From heaven, he will answer, Why did you not believe him* when he bore witness to me? If you believed him, why question me? *If* *we say, From heaven,* he will answer this within himself: *Why did you not believe him? If we say, From human beings, we fear the crowds, lest they stone us*. For the whole Jewish crowd regarded John as a prophet. Therefore, because of the lamp before the day, seeing that either answer would be an answer against themselves, they replied: *We do not know*. Concerning what they knew, they replied: *We do not know.* And the Lord: *Neither will I tell you by what authority I do these things*. What could they say? What could they answer? Without doubt they were confounded, and the prophecy spoken in the Psalm was fulfilled: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ*. See when it was said and when fulfilled. To know when it was said, read the Psalm; when fulfilled, read the Gospel. *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ.* Why? *I will clothe his enemies in confusion*. O hard confusion! They are confounded daily and do not turn back.

**5.** But we, beloved, who have been grafted in place of the severed branches—for so the Apostle says—should hear that same Apostle and not think highly of ourselves. Whatever good we are, let us attribute to the good God; whatever evil we are, let us impute to ourselves. The person has an upright heart who in his good things praises only God and in his evils does not blaspheme God. For whatever God does with us he does knowingly. No one is better, wiser, or more powerful than he. He called us together; remember from where. He delivered us from idols, from slavery to demons, from so many sacrileges. How has this come to us? *How,* she says, *has it come to me that my Lord's mother should come to me?* Let us human beings be humbled as much as possible and glory only in the Lord, so that he may be exalted. Let us decrease, so that we may grow in him. See the highest human being, than whom *among those born of women no one greater has arisen.* What does he say of Christ? *He must increase, but I must decrease.* Let God increase and humanity decrease. How does the perfect one increase? What does God lack that he should increase? God increases in you when you understand him. Therefore, see the human being's humility and God's loftiness. *He must increase,* he says, *but I must decrease.* Both their birthdays indicated this. The matter takes place in the light; the sun itself is witness. From today the days diminish; from the Lord's birthday the days grow*. He must increase, but I must decrease.* For the Lord Jesus Christ, governor and author of creation, ruler of the world, maker of the stars, arranger of times, because he was born when he wished, chose for himself the day on which to be born, by which he might be well signified. He himself also gave his forerunner his day. In John he wished humanity to be understood, in himself God. Let humanity decrease and God increase: their sufferings indicate this. John was beheaded; Christ was exalted upon the cross. Therefore, brothers, I will conclude briefly with this. Many things can be said about Saint John the Baptist, but neither we are sufficient to speak them nor you to hear them. Therefore, I will now conclude briefly: let humanity be humbled and God exalted. *The one* *who glories should glory in the Lord*.
