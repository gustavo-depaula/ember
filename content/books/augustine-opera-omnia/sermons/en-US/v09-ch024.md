# Sermon 315: On the Solemnity of Stephen the Martyr

**1. 1.** How the most blessed Stephen was ordained a deacon, the seventh together with six others, and how he came to the heavenly crown, you heard when the reading itself was read. This first merit of the first Martyr was commended to Your Charity: that, whereas of other martyrs we scarcely find Acts which we can recite on their solemnities, this man's passion is in the canonical book. The Acts of the Apostles is a book of the Canon of the Scriptures. This book begins to be read from the Lord's day of Easter, as the custom of the Church holds. In this book, then, whose title is the Acts of the Apostles, you heard how seven deacons were chosen and ordained by the Apostles, among whom holy Stephen was. The Apostles first, the deacons after. And the first martyr is from the deacons rather than from the Apostles: the first victim from the lambs rather than from the rams.

**1. 2.** But how great a likeness of passion he had with his Lord and his Savior! False witnesses against this man, just as against that one: and about the very same matter. For you know and recall what the false witnesses said against the Lord Christ: *We heard him say, I destroy this temple, and after three days I build another new one*[^1]. But the Lord had not said this: but the falsehood wanted to be near to the truth. How are they false witnesses? They heard him say: *Destroy this temple, and after three days I will raise it up*[^2]. But the Evangelist says: *But he was speaking of the temple of his body*[^3]. The false witnesses, for what was said, *Destroy,* said, *I destroy.* They changed a little in the syllables: but the false witnesses were the worse, the more they wanted by slander to approach the truth. And what was objected against this man? *We heard him say that Jesus of Nazareth will destroy this temple, and will change the custom of the Law*[^4]. They bore false witness, and prophesied true things. Just as that Caiaphas, their master, the chief of the priests, giving counsel to the Jews, that Christ should be killed, said this: *It is expedient that one die, rather than that the whole nation perish*[^5]. But the Evangelist says: *This he said not of himself, but, being high priest of that year, he prophesied that Christ was to die for the nation*[^6]. What is this, brothers? Great is the power of truth. Men hate the truth, and prophesy the truth without knowing it. They do not act, but it is acted concerning them. So these false witnesses came forth like the false witnesses, but those for whose sake Christ was killed.

**2. 3.** They brought him into the council, that they might have a greater trial. But the friend of Christ, when he had stated his case, proclaimed the truth of his Lord. He was about to die: why should a godly tongue be silent before the impious? Why should he not die for the truth? Unequal to his Lord in this one thing, for the sake of a certain mystery, as far as concerns the likeness of the passion. For that one is God by the excellence of majesty. When the Lord was led to his passion, questioned, he preferred to be silent: this man was not silent. Why did that one prefer to be silent? Because it had been foretold of him: *Like a sheep he was led to the slaughter, and like a lamb before its shearer is silent, so he did not open his mouth*[^7]. But why did this man refuse to be silent? Because it had been said by the Lord himself: *What I tell you in the dark, say in the light; and what you hear in the ear, proclaim upon the housetops*[^8]. How did holy Stephen proclaim upon the housetop? Because he trampled the muddy house, the flesh. For he who does not fear death tramples the flesh. He first expounded to them from the beginning the law of God, from Abraham to Moses, to the giving of the law, to the entry into the land of promise; that he might commend that there was no true testimony from which they were stirring up slander. Then concerning Moses he gave them a great likeness to Christ. Moses, rejected by them, himself also freed them: rejected, he freed them. He did not return evil for evil: indeed he returned good for evil. So too the Lord Christ, rejected by the Jews, will himself afterward free them.

**3. 4.** But now he who dies, has died. The Jews whom you see will have the time of their deliverance, through the very one whom they rejected; but they do not know it. Now those who blaspheme perish: then there will be others, not these. When we say these things, we promise salvation not to others, but to themselves. The nation will be freed, not these. Attend and receive a likeness. Does not God now free the Gentiles? All nations believe in Christ, and from sons of the devil become sons of God. Yet those parents of ours, from whom we were born, who worshiped idols, perished with the idols.

**3. 5.** You heard, and you saw the spectacles of the heart. The sound was in your ears, the vision in your minds. You watched the great struggle of holy Stephen, who in the struggle was being stoned. Who? He who shortly before was teaching the law. What law was he teaching? The one which they received on tablets of stone. Deservedly made of stone, they stoned the friend of Christ. *Stiff-necked* (after he had taught, he began to rebuke), *and uncircumcised in heart and ears. Which of the prophets did your fathers not kill?*[^9] He seems to rage: a fierce tongue, a gentle heart. He cried out, and he loved. He raged, and he wanted them to be saved. Who would not believe him angry, who would not believe him inflamed with the torches of hatred, when he said: *Stiff-necked, and uncircumcised in heart and ears?* Meanwhile the Lord looked from heaven, and saw. Heaven was opened: he saw Jesus, as it were exhorting his athlete. Nor was he silent about what he saw: *Behold I see,* he says, *heaven opened, and the Son of man standing at the right hand of majesty*[^10]. They, hearing this, as though it were blasphemy that he had said, stopped their ears, ran to the stones. In the Psalm it had been said: *Like the deaf asp, that stops its ears*[^11]. They plainly displayed what had been foretold of them. He began to be stoned. Now attend to him raging, recall the hard words: *Stiff-necked, and uncircumcised in heart and ears.* As though he were an enemy: as if, were it possible, he desired to kill them all. Let him say this who does not see the heart. His heart lay hidden: but his last words were heard, and his hidden things were laid open, when he was being stoned. *Lord Jesus,* he says, *receive my spirit*[^12]. To you I have spoken: for you I die. *Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.* Because you have helped, he whom you receive has conquered. Receive my spirit, from the hand of those who hate yours. This holy Stephen said standing.

**4. 5.** And after this he bent the knee, and said: *Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge*[^13]. Where is the *Stiff-necked?* Is this the whole of what you were crying out? is this the whole of what you were raging? Outwardly you were crying out, and inwardly you were praying.

**4. 6.** *Lord Jesus, receive my spirit*[^14]: this standing. For he was demanding a debt, when he said: *Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.* He was demanding the debt that had been promised to the martyrs: the debt of which the Apostle says: *For I am already being offered, and the time of my dissolution is at hand. I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith: henceforth there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will render to me on that day*[^15]. He will render, he will render what he owes. He who was before a debtor of punishments, afterward began to hold God as a giver of rewards. From where was the apostle Paul a debtor of punishments? Because he was an enemy of the Church, because a persecutor. Hear him: *I am not worthy to be called an apostle, because I persecuted the Church of God*[^16]. *I am not worthy,* he says worthy. Why are you not worthy? To suffer punishments, to enter into the fires of Gehenna, to be tortured according to my merits, of this I was worthy: to be an apostle, I was not worthy. From where, then, came this to you, of which you were not worthy? There followed: *But by the grace of God I am what I am*[^17]. By my own evil I was what I was: by the gift of God I am what I am. So that afterward he might demand the debt, he first received the undeserved. What debt afterward? *There remains for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, will render to me on that day.* He will render to me, it is owed to me: first the undeserved. What? *I am not worthy to be called an apostle: but by the grace of God I am what I am.* So too holy Stephen: *Lord Jesus,* standing with confidence, because he had served well, fought well, had not yielded to the enemy, had trampled fear, had spurned the flesh, had conquered the world and the devil: hence he stood when he said: *Lord Jesus, receive my spirit.*

**4. 7.** When this man was demanding a good debt, the apostle Paul was heaping up for himself a debt. The one was asking a good debt: the other was adding to a bad debt. For what do you think, brothers? When Stephen was being stoned, you heard, but perhaps you did not notice, the false witnesses about to stone Stephen laid their garments at the feet of a certain young man, by name Saul.

**5. 7.** This Saul, and afterward Paul: the persecutor Saul, the preacher Paul. For "Saul" is a name from Saul. Saul was the persecutor of King David. Such was Saul toward David, as Saul toward Stephen. But afterward when he had been called from heaven; called, prostrated, changed, when he began as an apostle to preach the word of God; he changed his name, and called himself Paul. And why did he choose this? Because "Paul" is little, "Paul" is small. We are accustomed to speak so: I will see you after paulum, that is, after a little. From where, then, Paul? *I am the least of the Apostles*[^18]. Great, divine spectacles! He who was the persecutor at the killing of Stephen, was afterward made the preacher of the kingdom of heaven. How he raged in that killing, do you want to hear? He kept the garments of those stoning, that he might stone with the hands of all. So after holy Stephen, standing, demanded his debt, saying: *Lord Jesus, receive my spirit;* attending to his enemies, who by stoning him were making a bad debt for themselves, and were adding to that treasure of which the apostle Paul says: *But you, according to your hardness and impenitent heart, are storing up for yourself wrath on the day of wrath and revelation of the righteous judgment of God*[^19]: he attended to them, and pitied them, and bent the knee for them. For himself he stood, for them he bent the knees. He distinguished the righteous from the sinners: for the righteous, standing, he asked, because he was demanding a reward; for the sinners he bent the knees, because he knew how difficult it was to be heard for men so wicked. Although righteous, although set under the very crown, he did not presume, but bent the knee: not attending to what he himself was worthy to receive by asking, but to what they were worthy of, from whom he wanted to remove the dreadful punishments. *Lord,* he says, *do not lay this sin to their charge*[^20].

**5. 8.** What Stephen was, humble, Christ was, sublime: that he was bowed to the earth, this Christ was hung on the wood. For recall that he too said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^21]. He sat in the chair of the cross, and taught Stephen the rule of godliness. O good Teacher, you pronounced well, you taught well. Behold your disciple prays for his enemies, prays for those who stone him. He showed how the humble ought to imitate you the sublime, the creature the creator, the victim the mediator, man God and man: God, but yet on the cross man; God Christ, but on the cross man, when he said with a clear voice: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do.*

**6. 8.** That man says to himself: He prayed for his enemies, because he is Christ, because he is God, because he is the Only-begotten; what am I, to do this? If your Lord is too much for you, do you not know that Stephen is your fellow servant? God taught through Stephen, who was not emptied out. If you see that these things went before in the Gospel, my brothers, let no one say in his heart, Who does that? Behold, Stephen did it: from himself? did he do it from his own? But if he did it from the gift of God; surely he did not enter, and close it against you? Surely he did not cross the bridge, and cut it off? Is it too much for you? Ask, you too. The fountain flows, it has not dried up.

**6. 9.** And truly I say to Your Charity, my brothers: exercise yourselves, as much as you can, to display gentleness, even toward your enemies. Bridle the anger which goads you to vengeance. For anger is a scorpion. If it has stirred you with its inner flames, you think it some great thing, if you avenge yourself on your enemy. If you want to avenge yourself on your enemy, turn against your own anger: for it is your enemy, which kills your soul. O good man—for I do not want to say, bad man; I rather say what I want you to be, than what you are—O good man, what will your enemy do to you? what is he going to do, that he may be able to do much; that God should permit him the whole of what he desires? He desires to shed your blood. It is difficult indeed, and even enemies are rare, who rage to the point of death. Even enemies themselves are accustomed, when they have seen those they persecute afflicted, to turn their anger into mercy. You hardly find an enemy who rages to the point of death. But suppose, to the point of death. Set yourself such an enemy as rages to the point of death. What is he going to do? What the Jews did to Stephen: for himself punishment, for the other a crown. Will your enemy kill you as though you were not going to die, as though you were always going to live? Your enemy is going to do to you what at some time a fever was going to do: if he kills you, he will be like your fever. So will he harm you by killing you? No: rather, if you die well, and have loved him, he will add something to your heavenly reward. Do you not know how much those stoners bestowed on holy Stephen? Did they know that to him for his goodness a crown was to be rendered, to them for their malice a punishment? How much the devil bestowed? He himself made all the martyrs for us. But will he go from here? But of his benefits, what he did not want, that will be imputed to him which he himself desired, not what God was doing through him. So your enemy, whoever he is, to the point of death, will harm you nothing.

**7. 10.** See what anger harms. Acknowledge your enemy: acknowledge her with whom you fight in the theater of your breast. A narrow theater; but God watches: there tame your enemy. Do you want to see how truly she is your enemy? Now I show you. You are about to pray to God: the hour will come when you will say: *Our Father, who are in heaven*[^22]. You will come to that verse: *Forgive us our debts.* What follows? *As we also forgive our debtors*[^23]. There that enemy stands against you. She hedges the way of your prayer: she raises a wall, and there is no way for you to pass. You said all of it well: *Our Father.* You ran on: *Forgive us our debts.* And what afterward? *As we also forgive our debtors.* Behold, that adversary contradicts; not before the veil, but within: in the very sanctuary of your heart, there she cries out at you, contradicts. What an enemy, brothers, who contradicts? *As we also forgive.* You are not permitted to rage against your enemy: against her rage. *Better is he who conquers anger, than he who takes a city*[^24], Scripture says. What I have just said is written: *Better is he who conquers anger, than he who takes a city.* Does not a warring commander, when he comes to some enemies, and finds a fortified city, equipped with armed men, excellent, opposing him, if he takes it, if he conquers it, if he overthrows it, seek triumphs? But as Scripture says: *Better is he who conquers anger, than he who takes a city.* It is in your hand. You cannot destroy her, you can restrain her. If you are strong, conquer anger: and spare the city. I see you attentive, I know how well you have received it. May God be present to your contests, that it may profit you to have watched the struggle of so great a Martyr; that, as you saw him conquering and favored him conquering, so you too may conquer in your heart.
