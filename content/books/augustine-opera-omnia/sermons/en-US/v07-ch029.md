# Sermon 255: In the Days of Easter, on the Alleluia.

**1. 1.** Since the Lord willed that we should see your Charity in the *Alleluia*, we owe you a word about the *Alleluia.* Let me not be a burden, if I recall what you know: for we both say *Alleluia* daily, and daily delight in it. For you know that *Alleluia* is said in Latin "Praise God": and in this word, agreeing with the mouth and consenting in the heart, we exhort one another to praise God. A man praises him securely only when he has nothing to displease. And in this time of our pilgrimage, indeed, we say *Alleluia* for the comfort of our provisions for the journey; now *Alleluia* is for us the song of the traveler: but we make our way along a laborious road to the quiet homeland, where, all our actions withdrawn, nothing will remain but *Alleluia.*

**2. 2.** That sweetest part Mary had chosen for herself, who was at leisure, learned, praised: but Martha her sister had been occupied about many things. She was indeed doing a necessary thing, but one not to remain: she was doing a thing of the road, not yet of the homeland: she was doing a thing of the pilgrimage, not yet of the possession. For she had received the Lord, and those who were with him. And the Lord had flesh: and as for our sake he condescended to take flesh, so he condescended to hunger and to thirst. And from the fact that he condescended to hunger and to thirst, he condescended to be fed by those whom he himself enriched: he condescended to be received, not from need, but by grace. Martha, then, was doing what pertained to the need of the hungry and thirsty: with anxious busyness she was preparing what might be eaten and drunk by the saints and by the Holy of holies himself in her house. A great work, but transitory. Will there always be hunger and thirst? When we will cling to that most pure and most perfect goodness, there will be no reason to serve need. We will be blessed, lacking nothing; having much, seeking nothing. And what is it that we will have, that we will seek nothing? I have said: what you believe, you will afterward see. What we have said, then, having much and seeking nothing, that is, lacking nothing; what is it that we will have? To those serving him, worshiping him, believing in him, hoping in him, loving him, what will God give?

**3. 3.** For we see how much he gives in this time to those who distrust him, despair of him, are turned away from him, blaspheme him; we see how many gifts he bestows. For from him is, first, health: which is so sweet that it never comes into distaste. When he has this, what does the poor man lack? When he does not have it, whatever the rich man has, what does it profit him? From him, then, that is, from the Lord our God whom we worship, from the true God in whom we believe, and in whom we hope, and whom we love; from him is so great a thing, health. See, however, since health is a great thing, how he gives it both to the good and to the bad, both to his blasphemers and to his praisers. And what shall I say? Both are men. Even a bad man is better than any beast. He gives health even to beasts of burden and to serpents, down to flies and little worms he grants health; and he saves all who created all. To omit other things, then; because we find nothing better than health: God gives it not only to men, but also to beasts, as the Psalm says: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord; how your mercy is multiplied, O God*[^1]. For such you are, because you are God; that your goodness does not remain in the highest things, and abandon the lowest. It reaches from the angels down to the last and tiny animals. For it reaches from end to end, wisdom stretching mightily, and orders all things sweetly: and in that very ordering of his sweetness, health is sweet to all.

**4. 4.** Since, then, he gives so great a good both to the good and to the bad, both to men and to beasts; what is it, my brothers, that he keeps for the good? For he had already said: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord; how your mercy is multiplied, O God.* And then he added: *But the sons of men.* Who are these? As if there are other men, of whom a little before he had said: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord*; and others the sons of men. Are men a different thing from the sons of men, and the sons of men a different thing from men? What, then, does this distinction mean? Unless perhaps because men pertain to the man, the sons of men to the Son of man: men, to the man; the sons of men, to the Son of man. For there is a certain man who was not a son of man. For he who was first made was a man, he was not a son of man. What, then, came to us through the man, and what came to us through the Son of man? I recall what came through the man, and I quote the Apostle's words: *Through one man sin entered into the world, and through sin death: and so it passed into all men, in whom all sinned*[^2]. See what the man pledged us, see what we drink from our parent, and scarcely digest. If this through the man, what through the Son of man? *He did not spare*, he says, *his own Son.* If *he did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all; how will he not also with him give us all things?*[^3] Again, *As through the disobedience of one man many were made sinners; so also through the obedience of one man many will be made righteous*[^4]. Through that one, then, sin, through Christ righteousness. All sinners, then, pertain to the man: all the righteous to the Son of man. Why, then, do you marvel that sinners, that the impious, that the wicked, that despisers of God, turned away from God, lovers of the age, embracing iniquity, hating the truth, that is, men pertaining to the man; why do you marvel that they have this health, when you hear the Psalm: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord?* Lest men exalt themselves, because they have temporal health, the beasts were added to them. What is it, then, in which you rejoice, O man? Is it not common to you with your donkey, and with your hen, with any living thing in your house, with those sparrows? is not bodily health common to you with all these?

**5. 5.** *To the devout is promised the delight of the praise of God, which is not given to the impious.* Seek, then, what was promised to the sons of men, and hear what follows: *But the sons of men will hope under the shelter of your wings.* Meanwhile they will hope, as long as they are on the road. *But the sons of men will hope under the shelter of your wings. For by hope we have been saved*[^5]. This does not pertain to men and beasts, to hope under the shelter of God's wings. And see, hope suckles us, nourishes us, strengthens us, and in this laborious life consoles us; in that very hope we sing Alleluia. See how much joy hope has. What will the reality itself be? You ask what it will be? Hear what follows: *They will be inebriated with the abundance of your house*[^6]. Of this thing there is hope. We thirst, we hunger, it is necessary that we be filled: but on the road hunger, in the homeland fullness. When will we be filled? *I will be filled when your glory has been manifested*[^7]. But now the glory of our God, the glory of our Christ, is hidden: and with him our glory too is hidden. But *when Christ who is your life appears, then you also will appear with him in glory*[^8]. Then there will be Alleluia in reality: but now in hope. Hope sings it, love sings it now, love will sing it then too: but now love sings hungering, then love will sing enjoying. For what is *Alleluia*, my brothers? I told you, it is the praise of God. See, now you hear the word, and in hearing you delight, and in delight you praise. If you love the dew so, how will you love the fount itself? For what belching is to a belching stomach, that is praise to a sated heart. For if we praise what we believe, how will we praise when we see? See what Mary had chosen for herself: but it signified that life, she did not yet hold it.

**6. 6.** There are two lives: one pertaining to delight, the other pertaining to need. The one for need is laborious: the one for delight is full of pleasure. But enter within, do not seek delight outside: lest you swell with it, and not be able to enter through the narrow place. See how Mary saw the Lord in the flesh, and heard the Lord through the flesh, as you heard, when the Epistle to the Hebrews was read, as it were through a veil. There will be no veil, when we will see face to face. Mary, then, was sitting, that is, at leisure, and was hearing, and praising: but Martha was occupied about much service. And the Lord says to her: *Martha, Martha, you are occupied about many things: but one thing is necessary*[^9]. One true thing: many things will not be necessary. Before we reach the one, we need many things. Let the one extend us, lest the many distend us and tear us away from the one. The apostle Paul spoke of that very one, because he had not yet attained it. *I*, he says, *do not consider that I have grasped it. But one thing, forgetting the things behind, stretched out to the things that are before.* Not distended, but stretched out. For the one stretches out, it does not distend. Many things distend, the one stretches out. And how long does it stretch out? As long as we are here. When we have come, it gathers, it does not stretch out. *But one thing, forgetting the things behind, stretched out to the things that are before, according to the intention.* See the stretching out: never the distending. *According to the intention I follow, to the prize of the upward calling of God in Christ Jesus*[^10]. The order of the words is: *But one thing I follow.* Then, then, we will come, and enjoy the one; but that very one will be all things to us. For what did we say, brothers, when I began to speak? What is that much which we will have, when we will not be in need? what is the much which we will have? This I had proposed to say: what will God give us, that he will not give to them? *Let the impious be taken away, lest he see the brightness of God*[^11]. Therefore God will give us his brightness, which we may enjoy: from which the impious will be taken away, lest he see the brightness of God. The whole of our much that we will have, God himself will be. Greedy one, what were you seeking to receive? What does he seek from God, for whom God is not enough?

**7. 7.** But when it is said that we will have God, and we will be content with him alone, rather, we will so delight in him alone that we seek nothing else; because we will both enjoy him in the one himself, and enjoy him in one another: (for what are we, if we do not have God? or what else in ourselves ought we to love than God, either because we have him, or that we may have him?) when, then, it is said that the rest are taken away, and God alone will be the one in whom we delight; the soul as it were is straitened, which was accustomed to delight in many things; and the carnal soul, addicted to the flesh, entangled in carnal desires, having its wings smeared with the birdlime of bad desires, lest it fly to God, says to itself: What will there be for me, where I will not eat, where I will not drink, where I will not sleep with my wife? what kind of joy will there be for me? This joy of yours is from sickness, not from health. Surely your very flesh in this time is sometimes sick, sometimes healthy. Attend, that I may say something from which you may grasp an example of a thing I cannot speak. There are certain desires of the sick: they burn with desire either for some spring, or some fruit; and so they burn that they suppose that, if they recover, they ought to enjoy those very desires of theirs. Health comes, and the desire perishes. What it desired, it now loathes: because the fever was seeking this in it. And what kind of health is this, in which the sick man recovers?

**8. 7.** This health by which we are called healthy, what is it? But yet from this take an example. Since there are many desires of the sick, which this health takes away; as this health takes those away, so immortality takes all away: because our health is immortality. Recall the Apostle, and see what it will be: *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality*[^12]. And we will be equal to the angels of God. Surely those are not wretched, who do not feast? Are they not therefore more blessed, because they do not need these things? Or will any rich man be made equal to the angels? The angels are truly rich. What are riches called? Resources. The angels have great resources, who have great facilities. You hear when a rich man is praised: How great he is, he is a lord, he is rich, he is powerful. How great a thing, that he may go where he wishes, beasts of burden are at hand, supplies are at hand, servants are at hand, services are at hand. All these the rich man has: he goes where he wishes, he suffers no labor. The angel will be wherever he wishes: nor does he say: Harness; nor does he say: Saddle; which the rich say with pride, and want to swell over it, because they have those to whom to say, Harness and Saddle. Unhappy man, these words are of weakness, not of resources. Therefore we will not be in need: and so we will be blessed. For we will be full, but of our God: and all the things that we here desire as great, he himself will be to us. As a great thing you seek food here: God will be food to you. The embrace of the flesh you seek here: *But for me to cling to God is good*[^13]. Riches you seek here: how will all things be lacking to you, when you have him who made all things? And to make you secure by the words of the Apostle, of that life he said this: *That God may be all in all*[^14].
