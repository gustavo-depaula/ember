# Sermon 313/E: A Treatise on the Feast of Saint Cyprian

**1.** Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, in whom is our hope of eternal salvation, for this reason, though he was God, became man, lest man, made far from God, should think himself left in the distance and deserted. So, made the Mediator, he in a certain way filled up the time of distance, by which we were separated from God, so that through him we might not only not remain far off, but might even be able to draw near. For nothing is so joined as the Word and God; again, nothing is so joined as flesh and man. Since, then, the Word and God was far from flesh and man, the Word became flesh, and joined man to God. So this our Lord and Savior, the Son of God, the Word of God made flesh—not by conversion into flesh, but by the assumption of flesh—taught those who believe in him to live, taught them to die; to live without desire, to die without fear. He taught us to live, that we might not die forever; he taught us to die, that we might live forever. So out of the number of those whom he taught this, blessed Cyprian stood out; living as one knowing that he would die, and dying as one having it certain that he would rise again, commended to God by a twofold grace, that grace, namely, which he received from him to whom he was pleasing. But he was pleasing to him by his gift: for as far as concerned himself, he had that by which to displease, not by which to please; but, as it is written: *Where sin abounded, grace superabounded*[^1]. That truthful and true martyr, the servant of God, true by the gift of God, confesses in his writings what kind of man he was before; he does not forget what he was, lest he be ungrateful to him through whom he ceased to be such. So he is commended to God by a twofold grace, by the episcopate and by martyrdom. His episcopate defended and held unity; his martyrdom taught and fulfilled confession.

**2.** O how great, how wonderful is faith! Faith is something great, and where is it? For we see one another's faces, our shape, our clothing, and finally we perceive our words and voices with the ear; where is faith, of which I now speak? let it be shown to our eyes. Behold, faith is not seen: and yet that whole multitude which is seen here in the house of God, faith itself, which is not seen, has drawn. So faith is great, as the Lord too says in the Gospel: *According to your faith be it done to you*[^2]. Likewise the Lord our God himself, praising the faith of certain people, says: *I have not found such great faith in Israel*[^3]. So it is no wonder, if through faith which is not seen the life which is seen is despised, that the life which is not seen may be acquired. With this faith holy Cyprian was full: with this faith are filled not false but true Christians, who with their whole heart and with unshaken faith believe God. But the heretics and Donatists, who falsely boast that they belong to Cyprian, if they attended to his episcopate, would not separate themselves; if to his martyrdom, would not cast themselves headlong. He is not at all a disciple of Christ, he is not a companion of Cyprian, the heretic separated in heresy, or the Donatist cast headlong in death.

**3.** Let us consider, most beloved brothers, Christ teaching, Cyprian following; and those crying out from across the way that they are Christians, that they belong to Cyprian. Hear what Christ teaches: *My peace I give to you, my peace I leave to you*[^4]. The Donatist or heretic is not a disciple of Christ; the enemy of peace is not a disciple of Christ. See whether the Lord our God himself said this and did not do it, who said: *My peace I give to you, my peace I leave to you.* He tolerated Judas the devil among his disciples, he did not separate him; he was admitted to the Lord's supper, when he had already received the price of the Lord. For he wanted to sell Christ, he did not want to be redeemed by Christ. So our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ taught that separation is to be avoided, and division to be broken off, and peace and unity to be loved. Which peace he in a certain way leaves to his disciples, our Apostles, in his last testament. For, about to go to the Father, he said: *My peace I give to you, my peace I leave to you;* and he did not separate Judas from himself, who could not err, if he had wanted to separate him; for he would not separate an innocent man for a guilty one, or by separating the guilty desert the innocent. He knew what he was doing, and did not do it; he did not make separation, who commanded that peace be loved. He knew what he was doing, and did not do it; he who knew did not do it, lest he who did not know should dare to do it. Judas separated himself from the Lord. He was tolerated to the very end: he gave the kiss of peace who did not have peace; and yet he received the kiss of peace. By that kiss he was not joined, but condemned; for the Lord gave him the kiss as though he said to him: Behold what I have, behold what you do not have. For Christ was the head of his body singing and saying: *With those who hated peace I was peaceable*[^5]. So in that one tolerated to the end, the Lord Jesus Christ vehemently commended that separation is not to be made, but unity to be loved, and peace to be kept.

**4.** This we have said about keeping peace, on account of the heretics, who have separated themselves from the Catholic Church, and daily separate themselves, and falsely call themselves Catholics. So we have recalled this command of the Lord's peace on account of the heretics. But let us see what the Lord says also about martyrdom; we ought to recall it on account of the Donatists too, who cast themselves headlong; the Lord's teaching is to be commended. For the devil said to the Lord, when he tempted him—and the Lord was tempted, that we might learn to resist the tempter—so the devil said to him: *If you are the Son of God, throw yourself down*[^6]. For he had taken him up onto the pinnacle of the temple; he did not recognize his Lord, and was teaching the precipice as to a man. Not knowing it, he was tempting in the true Christ what he was preparing to persuade false Christians to do. For the Donatists are not false Christians, but are altogether not Christians, who hear what was suggested by the devil, and do not hear what was answered by Christ. For what did the Lord our Master and Savior answer the devil suggesting such things? *Get back, Satan, for it is written: you shall not tempt the Lord your God*[^7]. For the devil suggested from Scripture, and the Lord answered from Scripture. For the devil had said to the Lord: *For it is written: He has given his angels charge concerning you; in their hands they will lift you up, lest you strike your foot against a stone*[^8]. Throw yourself down, he says, and if you are the Son of God, the angels take you up: what do you fear? The Lord could both cast his flesh down, and not let it die; but what the devil suggested to the present Christ, Christ did not teach the future Christians such things. For this too the devil suggests to the Donatists, saying: Throw yourselves down, the angels take you up; by such a death you do not go to punishment, but proceed to a crown. They would be Christians, if they gave ear to Christ, and did not believe the devil, who first separated them from the peace of the Church, and afterward gave them over headlong. We ask them, and we say: If voluntary death delights you, and you think it beautiful, with no enemy pressing, no adversary killing, to die of your own accord, why do you run quickly to the precipice, never to the noose? There is another death at hand, and hanging by a noose rather keeps the limbs of the dying whole, than the precipice you choose; why then do you not hang yourselves in a noose, when you want to die? They answer: Far be it from us, let the noose be anathema; for Judas the traitor hanged himself in a noose. O wretched and unhappy men, what is this madness, to refuse to do what the traitor did, and to do what the master of the traitor, the devil, taught them? Judas, that he might betray Christ, as it is written: *Satan entered into his heart*[^9]; he who persuaded him to betray Christ, the same persuaded him to hang himself in a noose. For he repented that he had betrayed righteous blood, but his repentance had no hope; he repented, but despaired, he did not believe he would receive pardon. He did not come to him whom he had betrayed, and ask forgiveness; he did not ask forgiveness, he did not implore deliverance, he did not commend himself to be redeemed by his blood. For the Lord would not be cruel toward Judas, who was merciful toward the Jews. Judas sold Christ to be killed, the Jews bought Christ to be killed. Do you want to know that he would have spared the seller? He prayed for the buyers; hanging on the cross he said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do*[^10]. But those who crucified Christ, who wanted to remain in that sin, perished, because they refused to repent; they despaired of forgiveness, they did not merit pardon. But if any of them ran to the forgiveness of Christ himself to be freed, to whose shedding of blood they had run, incited by the devil, they repented, and merited forgiveness. They were baptized: raging they shed the blood of Christ, believing they drank it.

**5.** So the devil, who persuaded the heretics to separation, the Donatists to casting themselves headlong, the same persuaded Judas both to betrayal and to despair and to the noose. You then, Donatist, who want to kill yourself, who avoid the noose of the traitor, why do you not avoid the precipice of the devil? The devil persuaded Judas to tie his own neck; the same it is who suggested to the Lord that he throw himself down. So avoid the devil in both: as in the noose you avoid the devil, so in the precipice you ought not to consent to the devil. For the devil, suggesting the precipice, what did he hear from the Lord? *Get back, Satan!* O Donatist, say this to the devil, when he suggests the precipice to you; who has also filled you, that, cast headlong, you may be worshiped. For truly, brothers, both they cast themselves down, and they are cast down by their own perverse peoples. Those are the greater murderers, who gather with honor the bodies of those cast down, who catch the blood of those cast down, who honor their tombs, who get drunk at their graves. For those, seeing such honor offered to those cast down, others are inflamed to the precipice; those get drunk over them with wine, those are drunk with fury and the worst error. Against these blessed Cyprian, commending the peace of Christ, teaching his passion. Attend to both in him: he held peace in unity, he held martyrdom in confession. For our Lord Jesus Christ himself had surely come to suffer, and had foretold that he would suffer, and we would not be redeemed unless he had suffered; he had even said: *I have power to lay down my soul, and to take it up. No one takes it from me; I myself lay it down of myself*[^11]. And of the Father it had been said: *Who did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all*[^12]. And of our Lord Jesus Christ himself: *Who loved me, and handed himself over for me*[^13]. Since, then, he had come for this, that the Father should hand him over to the impious to redeem us, and he handed himself over to the impious to redeem us, yet after the supper given he withdrew to the mountain, he fled the eyes of his persecutors; he willed that men come to him, he did not bring himself forward of his own accord. Attend to Cyprian imitating him. You heard, when his passion was read, that he said to the proconsul: *Since discipline forbids that anyone offer himself.* See how he knew the discipline of suffering; he surely roused men to suffering; yet he did not cast them headlong, did not push them, that they should offer themselves of their own accord. And to blessed Cyprian himself men were sent, and he was led, yet he did not offer himself of his own accord. But the Donatists cast themselves headlong of their own accord, and come to men, and say: Kill us. Those say: We do not kill you. O mad, O perverse men! You come to this point: you are going to say you are a martyr, in order to make either a homicide or a murderer; so that they come to men, and arm them against themselves, and by terrifying compel them to kill. If they had a sound heart, they would both dread the precipice, and not commit homicide; but they do this, which their father the devil taught them, and their master Donatus instructed them. Against these blessed Cyprian bravely defended both unity and peace.

**6.** So men are sent to him: he is led to the proconsul, he stands before the tribunal of the judge, a man. For he had withdrawn into his gardens because of the persecution, as the Lord after supper to the Mount of Olives. The Lord on the Mount of Olives poured out and cherished the oil of peace; Cyprian in his gardens was nourishing a grain of mustard seed. He, led from there, stood in body before the proconsul, but in heart before the savior; he honored the human power, he did not deny the divine glory. First from there he was sent into exile. He confessed Christ, he went on to exile; from there, led to martyrdom, he was brought, like a branch to the pruning-knife, not of cutting off, but of purging. For the Lord said: *I am the vine, you are the branches, my Father is the husbandman. Every branch in me that bears fruit, my Father purges it, that it may bear more fruit; and every branch in me that does not bear fruit, he will cut it off, and it withers, and is cast into the fire*[^14]. So attend to the purged branch, Cyprian the martyr; attend to the branches cut off, the heretics and Donatists. Why do you say that you belong to this man, to this man bearing the fruit of peace and unity, purged by the knife of martyrdom to receive the crown of eternal salvation? Why do you compare yourselves to this man, heretics and Donatists, cut off by separation, defiled by casting yourselves headlong? Blessed Cyprian stands, confesses Christ, does not consent to that to which he is compelled; he receives the temporal sentence of the judiciary, he becomes a judge with Christ forever. He receives the sentence, and "Thanks be to God" rightly, because he confessed rightly. O mad Donatists! O rabid ones! Thanks be to God. They say they celebrate the birthday of Cyprian; their "Praise to God" all Christian men dread. For the Donatists are gathered for all their crimes; that they may cast themselves down: "Praise to God!" they shout; in the mouth: "Praise to God!" in deeds: "Hateful to God." And so whoever then, a Catholic Christian standing far off, hears: "Praise to God!" already trembles, already looks for where to flee, lest he see their headlong leaps. Behold how the Donatists have made bitter the praises of God.

**7.** But let us, attending to the teaching of Christ, set the example of Cyprian in our hearts, beseeching the Lord God, with the prayers of the saints also helping, that we may not fear such men, and may not be silent to them about the faith and hope that is in us. Let us, then, confess Christ, and not fear men, nor by fearing be silent. For blessed Cyprian too, leading his life among persecutors and gentiles, when he was among the worshipers of idols, did not fear the power of temporal rule, nor was silent that idols were not gods. Not only in churches was he not silent, but he also showed it in his writings. Did he say: I will consider the times; those who rule worship idols; I will refrain from confounding them, who though temporal are yet emperors? Was he silent? did the good pastor, seeing the wolf, flee? For what does it profit, if the pastor is present in body, and flees in heart? For he who is silent through fear flees in heart. For on the contrary God would say to him: *I have set you as a watchman*[^15]; you should speak, not be silent: but you were silent, lest you be killed. Had I not said: *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^16]? Behold, when you fear the death of your flesh, you have made so many deaths of your soul. So blessed Cyprian was not silent, neither to heretics, nor to Donatists, nor to the emperors themselves. He said, and securely said, and certain in the Lord his God he said, that the demons were not gods. We too say, that heretics are not martyrs, that the Circumcellions are not martyrs. Blessed Cyprian did not fear the worshipers of demons; let us not fear the gatherings of heretics, let us not fear the congregations of those cast headlong. Let us only pray to the Lord in mind, that no one have the counsel of being silent. And if they kill us for our faith, let us say: *Thanks be to God;* and if they do not kill us, how much is it that we are going to live here? And if we reach the last gray hairs, how much is it? Surely we are going to die? Surely on account of the few days of this life ought the ministry of our heart and tongue to rest? Far be it. But if this our life is necessary to Your Charity for the sake of this ministry, obtain it from the Lord; we cannot be silent. You can ask God that, if we are still necessary to you, he preserve us for you, and you for us; he is able to preserve all of us from threats, from wickednesses, from the plots of all enemies, from the perversities of all heretics; whom, because we wish well, and want to convert, he himself knows, who is the inspector and ruler of our heart. But perhaps they will repay us evil for good. Has it now begun to happen, that evil is repaid to the servants of God for good? It is an old thing, the examples stand; nor will this cease, nor will it cease until the end; and we are now in the very end of the age.

**8.** And so, my brothers, I entreat Your Charity, that you have a firm and prepared heart in God. And let us all pray to God, that the Lord lead us as he knows how, and guard our ways, both bodily and spiritual; for nothing will befall us or you that he has not willed. But do not think that the prayers of the saints for all of us before God can be empty; by whose prayers our prayers and yours too will not be vain before God. I will give you an example from holy Scripture. Tabitha, a widow woman, used to clothe widows: it happened that she died; word was sent to Peter the apostle, he came. There were shown to him the tunics which she had woven for the poor. The Lord was moved with mercy: he heard Peter, he restored the widow, a good worker, to the light. So, just as she was restored from the grave by the prayers of the widows, so too the Lord is able, by the prayers of blessed Cyprian and all the saints, to free us from every evil.
