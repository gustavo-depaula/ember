# Sermon 158

**1. 1.** We have heard the blessed Apostle exhorting and confirming us, when he said to us, *If God is for us, who is against us*[^1]? But for whom God is, he showed above, where he says, *But those whom he predestined, these he also called; and those whom he called, these he also justified; and those whom he justified, these he also glorified. What, then, shall we say to these things? If God is for us, who is against us*[^2]? God for us, to predestine us; God for us, to call us; God for us, to justify us; God for us, to glorify us. *If God is for us, who is against us*? He predestined us before we were; he called us when we were turned away; he justified us when we were sinners; he glorified us when we were mortal. *If God is for us, who is against us*? Against those predestined by God, called, justified, glorified, whoever wishes to be an adversary, let him prepare himself, if he can wage war against God. For where we have heard, *If God is for us, who is against us*? none harms us but the one who conquers God. And who is there who conquers the Almighty? Whoever wishes to struggle against him harms himself. This is what Christ cried from heaven to Paul, still Saul: *It does not profit you to kick against the goad*[^3]. Let him rage, let him rage as much as he can; he who kicks against the goad—does he not rage against himself?

**2. 2.** But in these four notable things which the Apostle commended, which pertain to those for whom God is—that is, predestination, calling, justification, glorification—in these four things, then, we ought to consider what we already have, and what we still await. For in those things we already have, let us praise God the giver; in those we do not yet have, let us hold him as debtor. For he became a debtor, not by receiving anything from us, but by promising what it pleased him. For in one way we say to a man, You owe me because I gave to you; and in another way we say, You owe me because you promised me. When you say, You owe me because I gave to you, a benefit proceeded from you, but lent, not given. But when you say, You owe me because you promised me, you gave nothing, and yet you demand. For the goodness of him who promised will give, lest faith be turned into malice. For he who deceives is evil; but do we say to God, Repay me, because I gave to you? What did we give to God, when all that we are, and what good we have, we have from him? We gave him nothing, then. There is no way for us to demand God as debtor by these words, especially as the Apostle says to us, *For who has known the mind of the Lord? Or who has been his counselor? Or who has first given to him, and it shall be repaid to him*[^4]? In that other way, then, we can demand of our Lord, so as to say: Pay what you promised, because we have done what you commanded; and this you did, because you helped us as we labored.

**3. 3.** Let no one, then, say: God called me for this reason, because I worshiped God. How would you have worshiped, if you had not been called? If God called you for this reason, because you worshiped God, then you first gave, and he repaid you. Does not the Apostle take this saying from you, when he says, *Or who has first given to him, and it shall be repaid to him*? But behold, when you were called, you at least already existed. How would you be predestined, except when you did not exist? What did you give to God, when you, who might give something, did not exist? What, then, did God do, when he predestined one who did not exist? What the Apostle says: *He who calls the things that are not as though they were*[^5]. If you already existed, you would not be predestined; unless you were turned away, you would not be called; unless you were ungodly, you would not be justified; unless you were earthly and abject, you would not be glorified. *Who*, then, *has first given to him, and it shall be repaid to him? For from him, and through him, and in him are all things*. What, then, do we render? *To him be glory*[^6]. Because we did not exist when we were predestined; because we were turned away when we were called; because we were sinners when we were justified; let us give thanks to God, lest we remain ungrateful.

**4. 4.** But we had proposed to consider, of these four things, what we have already attained, what we still await as to be obtained. For we have already been predestined, even before we existed. We have been called, when we were made Christians. So this too we already have. Justified—what? What is, justified? Do we dare to say that we already have this third thing? And will there be any of us who dares to say: I am righteous? For I think this is what "I am righteous" means: "I am not a sinner." If you dare to say this, John meets you: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^7]. What, then? Do we have nothing of righteousness? Or do we have it, but do not have all of it? Let us seek this, then. For if we have something, and do not have something; let what we have grow, and what we do not have will be fulfilled. For behold, people have been baptized, all their sins have been forgiven them, they have been justified from their sins; we cannot deny it: yet there remains the struggle with the flesh, there remains the struggle with the world, there remains the struggle with the devil. But whoever struggles sometimes strikes, sometimes is struck; sometimes conquers, sometimes is slain; how he comes out of the arena is watched. For *if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*. Likewise, if we say that we have nothing of righteousness, we lie against the gifts of God. For if we have nothing of righteousness, we do not even have faith; if we do not have faith, we are not Christians. But if we have faith, we already have something of righteousness. That something—do you wish to know how great it is? *The righteous lives by faith*; *the righteous*, I say, *lives by faith*[^8]; because he believes what he does not see.

**5. 5.** The fathers, the holy rams, the leaders, the apostles, when they announced him, not only saw with their eyes, but even handled with their hands; and yet the Lord, keeping for us the gift of faith, to one of his disciples handling, feeling, seeking the truth with his fingers and finding it, crying out, *My Lord and my God*; the Lord and God himself said, *Because you have seen, you have believed*. And looking upon us who were to come, *Blessed*, he said, *are those who have not seen and have believed*[^9]. We have not seen, we have heard, and we have believed. We have been foretold blessed, and do we have nothing of righteousness? The Lord came in the flesh to the Jews, and was killed; he did not come to us, and was received. *A people I did not know served me, at the hearing of the ear it obeyed me*[^10]. We are that people, and do we have nothing of righteousness? We surely have it. Let us be grateful for what we have: that what we do not have may be added, and that we may not lose what we have. So this third thing too is already at work in us. We have been justified: but this very righteousness, as we make progress, grows. And how it grows I will say, and as it were confer with you; so that each one of you, now established in this very justification—having, namely, received the forgiveness of sins through the washing of regeneration, having received the Holy Spirit, making progress from day to day—may see where he is, may draw near, make progress, and grow, until he is consummated, not so as to be ended, but so as to be perfected.

**6. 6.** A man begins from faith; what pertains to faith? To believe. But let this faith still be distinguished from the unclean spirits. What pertains to faith? To believe. But the apostle James says, *The demons too believe, and tremble*[^11]. If you only believe, and live without hope, or do not have love: *The demons too believe, and tremble*. What great thing is it, if you say that Christ is the Son of God? Peter said this, and heard, *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah*; the demons said this, and heard, *Be silent*. To him, blessed, it is said, *Because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^12]. But they hear, *Be silent*[^13]; and they say this same thing, and are driven off. It is one voice: but the Lord questions the root, not the flower. Hence it is said to the Hebrews, *Lest any root of bitterness, springing up, cause trouble, and through it many be defiled*[^14]. First, then, distinguish your faith from the faith of a demon. By what do you distinguish it? The demons said this fearing, Peter loving. Add, then, hope to faith. And what is hope, but from some goodness of conscience? And to hope itself add love. We have a way that surpasses all, the Apostle saying, *I show you a way that surpasses all: If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but do not have love, I have become like sounding brass, or a clanging cymbal*[^15]; and he enumerates the other good things, and confirms that without love they profit nothing. Let these, then, remain: faith, hope, love; but the greatest of these is love. Pursue love. Distinguish, then, your faith. You are already among the predestined, called, justified. The apostle Paul says, *Neither circumcision avails anything, nor uncircumcision; but faith*. Say still more, Apostle, add, distinguish; because *the demons too believe, and tremble*; so add and distinguish, for *the demons believe, and tremble* at what they hate. Distinguish, Apostle, and circumcise my faith, and distinguish my cause from an unholy nation[^16]. He plainly distinguishes, he distinguishes, he circumcises. And, *faith*, he says, *that works through love*[^17].

**7. 7.** Let each one, then, my brothers, look at himself within, weigh himself, test himself in all his deeds, his good works, which he does with love, not expecting a temporal reward, but the promise of God, the face of God. For whatever God promises you is worth nothing apart from God himself. God would not satisfy me at all, unless he promised me himself, God. What is the whole earth? What is the whole sea? What is the whole heaven? What are all the stars? What is the sun? What is the moon? What is the host of angels? I thirst for the Creator of all these: him I hunger for, him I thirst for, to him I say, *For with you is the fountain of life*[^18]. He says to me, *I am the bread that came down from heaven*[^19]. Let my pilgrimage hunger and thirst, that my presence may be satisfied. The world smiles with many things—beautiful, strong, varied: more beautiful is he who made them, stronger and more glorious he who made them, sweeter is he who made them. *I shall be satisfied when your glory is made manifest*[^20]. If, then, faith that works through love is in you, you already belong to the predestined, called, justified: let it grow, then, in you. For *faith that works through love* cannot be without hope. But when we have come, will faith still be there? Will it be said to us: Believe? Surely not. We shall see him, we shall contemplate him. *Beloved, we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be*. Because *it has not yet appeared*, therefore faith. *We are sons of God*, predestined, called, justified; *we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be*. Now, then, faith, before it appears what we shall be. *We know that when it has appeared, we shall be like him*. Is it because we believe? No. Why, then? *Because we shall see him as he is*[^21].

**8. 8.** What of hope? Will it be there? Hope will no longer exist, when the reality is present. For hope itself is necessary for the pilgrimage; it is what consoles on the way. For the traveler, when he labors in walking, bears the labor for this reason: because he hopes to arrive. Take from him the hope of arriving, and at once the strength of walking is broken. Therefore the hope that is here too pertains to the righteousness of our pilgrimage. Hear the Apostle himself: *Awaiting the adoption*, he says, *we ourselves groan within ourselves still*[^22]. Where there is groaning, that happiness cannot yet be spoken of, of which Scripture says, *Toil and groaning have passed away*[^23]. Therefore *still*, he says, *we groan within ourselves, awaiting the adoption, the redemption of our body*. Still we groan. Why? *For in hope we have been saved. But hope that is seen is not hope. For if anyone sees it, why does he hope? But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience*[^24]. In this patience, then, the martyrs were crowned; they longed for what they did not see, they despised what they bore. In this hope they said, *Who will separate us from the love of Christ? Tribulation? Or distress? Or persecution? Or famine? Or nakedness? Or the sword? For on account of you*. And where is the one for whose sake? *For on account of you*, he says, *we are put to death all the day*[^25]. *On account of you*. And where is, *Blessed are those who have not seen and have believed*[^26]? Behold where he is, he is in you, because faith itself too is in you yourself. Or does the Apostle deceive us, who says that *Christ dwells through faith in our hearts*[^27]? Now through faith, then through sight: now through faith, as long as we are on the way, as long as on pilgrimage. *For as long as we are in the body, we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord; for we walk by faith, not by sight*[^28].

**9. 9.** If this is faith, what will sight be? Hear what it will be: *That God may be all in all*[^29]. What is, *all*? Whatever you sought here, whatever you held for great here, he himself will be to you. What did you want here, what did you love? To eat and drink? He himself will be your food, he himself will be your drink. What did you want here? The frail, passing health of the body? He himself will be your immortality. What did you seek here? Riches? Greedy one, what suffices for you, if God himself does not suffice? But what did you love? Glory, honors? God will be your glory: to whom even now it is said, *My glory, and the lifter up of my head*[^30]. For he has already lifted up my head. Our head is Christ. But why do you marvel? Because the head and the other members will be lifted up; then God will be all in all. In this way we believe, in this way we hope: when we have come, we will hold; and there will then be sight, not faith: when we have come, we will hold, and there will then be the reality, not hope. What of love? Surely it too exists now, and then will not be? If we love by believing and not seeing, how shall we love by seeing and holding? Therefore love will be, but it will be perfect; as the Apostle says, *Faith, hope, love, these three; but the greatest of these is love*[^31]. Having this, and nourishing it in us, secure, with his help persevering in him, let us say, *Who will separate us from the love of Christ*? until he himself shows mercy, he himself perfects. *Tribulation? Or distress? Or famine? Or nakedness? Or peril? Or the sword? For on account of you we are put to death all the day, we are reckoned as sheep for the slaughter*. And who endures it? Who bears all things? *But in all these things we conquer*. By what? *Through him who loved us*[^32]. Therefore, *If God is for us, who is against us*[^33]?
