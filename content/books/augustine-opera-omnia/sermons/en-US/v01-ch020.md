# Sermon 16/A: A Sermon Delivered in the Basilica of the Elders

On a Sunday, the Fourteenth Day Before the Kalends of July

On the Response of Psalm 38:

"Hear My Prayer and My Supplication, O Lord"

And on the Woman Caught in Adultery

**1.** It is the mark of Christians to advance daily in God and always to rejoice in God or in his gifts. For the time of our pilgrimage is very brief, and our homeland is without time. For between eternity and time there is much distance. Here devotion is acquired, there one rests. Hence, in the daily days, let us, as good merchants, know what we have gained. For we ought not only to be eager in hearing, but watchful in working. This school, in which God alone is master, seeks good disciples, not runaways but eager ones. The Apostle says, *In zeal not slothful, fervent in spirit, rejoicing in hope*[^1]. In this school, then, brothers, we learn daily. One thing we learn in the commandments, another in the examples, another in the sacraments. These are medicines for our wounds, and supports for our zeal. Just now we responded, *Hear my prayer and my supplication, O Lord; give ear to*—that is, perceive with the ears—*my tears*[^2]. What do you think this man is going to ask, who first desires God to be favorable to him? What will he seek from him? Let us see, let us learn. Will he perhaps ask for riches? Perhaps for some happiness in this life? Let him tell us, then, what he is going to ask, who first prays to God. For he saw that what he could not have of himself, he could have from God. For he had heard, *Ask, and you will receive*[^3]. He knew, then, what he was going to ask, who first asked God; therefore, *Hear, O God, my prayer*. And as though asked, "What do you want, why do you knock, why do you cry, why do you interrupt? Let me hear what you want." "What do I want? Hear my will and perfect your work. What is my will? *I said, I will guard my ways, that I may not offend with my tongue*[^4]." A hard thing this man undertakes, but he does not doubt, since he first prayed to the Lord. For he knew the teaching of Paul: *Not I, but the grace of God with me*[^5].

**2.** *I said,* then, *I will guard my ways*[^6]. What ways? Earthly ones? Do we walk on the earth by the tongue? We walk on the earth either by our own feet or by another's. Either we are carried by animals, or we go by our own feet. What, then, is it? What way does this man seek, that he may not offend with his tongue? A great teaching. See, brothers. For is it that, just as in one hour we can take food and, refreshed, depart, so we can in one moment both speak and be silent? For just as we have eyes for seeing, ears for hearing, and the other senses for perceiving, so too the tongue for speaking. We have great need of the tongue. Either you are going to hear something so as to answer, or you are going to say something so as to teach. Will you say it with the eye, and not with the tongue? And if you are going to hear with the ear, you are going to answer with the tongue. What do we do with so useful a member? With it we ask God, with it we make satisfaction, with it we say praises, with it, singing with one voice in harmony, we sing to God, with it in the daily days we either become merciful when we speak with others, or give counsel. What shall we do now? Our very tongue performs a ministry for you. What do we do, that we may not offend with the tongue? Especially since it was said, *Death and life are in the hands of the tongue*[^7], and again it was said, *I have seen many falling by the edge of the sword, but not so as those who fell by the tongue*[^8]. Again it was said, *And the tongue is set among our members, which defiles our whole body*[^9]. Hence again the same Lord, *They have taught their tongue to speak a lie*[^10]. O, *they have taught it*! For habit makes one speak a lie; even if you do not wish, it speaks a lie. For just as, if you turn a wheel once, then, as you have pushed it with the hand, by that very shape and roundness, or by that unstable and as it were natural course, it runs, so too our tongue need not be taught to speak a lie. For now, set going of its own accord, it runs to that toward which it can more easily be moved. You have one thing in your heart; that turns out another, sometimes from habit. What will you do? See, brothers, what scales of the heart must be made, that the tongue may bring forth something! For it is not moved of its own accord; there is within one who moves it.

**3.** For there is a certain force, which moves both itself and its other ministries. There is need that he be good who rules, and, helped through grace, he conquers however evil a habit. Let the minister be good, and the ministry is at rest. A soldier has arms, but if he does nothing, the arms do nothing. So too the tongue among our members—they are the arms of our soul. Of it was said, *A restless evil*[^11]. O, *restless*! Who made this evil, except the restless one? Do not be restless, and this is not evil; do not stir it, and there is nothing to stir itself. For it is not a soul, that it should move; it is a body, it lies still. Do not move it, and it is not moved. But see how you move it. It is the very one with which many, eager for frauds and serving avarice, when they begin to transact some business, cast forth the member made for the praise of God, and there blaspheme God and say, "By Christ! I bought for so much, I sell for so much." Why? I have asked you. Swear to me, for how much do you sell? I have asked you: For how much do you sell? "I sell for ten coins, for twenty coins." By Christ you swear. Swear by your eyes, swear by your children, and at that very hour the conscience trembles. O impiety of the tongue! You despised the Creator, you guarded the creature. O *restless evil, full of deadly poison! With it we bless God and the Father*—but God and the Father, God on account of nature, Father on account of grace; *and with it we curse a man, who was made to the image of God*[^12]. Attend, brothers, to what you carry. Behold, I say what we carry, for I too am a man with you. But let us return.

**4.** *Hear, O God, my prayer*[^13]. Of this sort are those Jews whom we just read of in the Gospel. Certainly the tongue led them to death. For just now we heard in the Gospel: The Jews, it says, brought a certain woman, though a harlot, to the Lord, tempting him and saying, *Master, this woman was just now caught in adultery. In the law of Moses it is written that whoever is caught in adultery should be stoned: what do you say?*[^14] This the tongue said, but it did not acknowledge the Creator. These people were unwilling to pray, so as to say, *Deliver my soul from the deceitful tongue*[^15]. For they had come with guile; this is what they wished to do. For the Lord had come not to dissolve the law but to fulfill it, and to forgive sins. So the Jews said among themselves, "If he says, Let her be stoned, we will say to him, Where is it that you forgive sins? Are you not the one who says, *Your sins are forgiven you*[^16]? But if he says, Let her go, we will say, Where is it that you came to fulfill the law, not to dissolve it?" See the deceitful tongue toward God. He who had come as Redeemer, not condemner (for he had come to redeem what had perished), turned away from them, as though not wishing to look at them. That turning away from them is not idle. Something is understood in this turning away. As though he said, "You bring me a sinful woman, you sinners. If you think I ought to condemn sins, I begin with you." And he who had come to grant pardon of sins says, *Whoever of you knows himself to be without sin, let him first cast a stone at her*[^17]. O answer, or rather proposition! If they had wished to cast a stone at the sinful woman, at that very hour it would again be recited, *With what judgment you judge, judgment will be passed on you*[^18]. You have condemned, you will be condemned. Yet those people, even if they did not acknowledge the Creator, knew their own conscience. Setting face behind face by turns, so that they would not even wish to look at one another out of confusion, *from the elders*—this the Evangelist said—*to the least, all went out*[^19]. For the Holy Spirit had said, *All have turned aside, together they have become useless; there is none who does good, there is not even one*[^20].

**5.** And all went out. There remained the one alone and the woman alone; there remained the Creator and the creature; there remained misery and mercy; there remained she who acknowledged her own guilt, and he who forgave the sin. For this is what he writes on the earth when he turned. For he wrote on the earth. For when man sinned, it was said to him, *You are earth*[^21]. To the sinful woman, then, when he gave pardon, in giving it he wrote on the earth. He gave pardon; but, when he gave pardon, raising his face to her, *Has no one,* he said, *stoned you?* And she did not say, "Why? What have I done, Lord? Am I indeed guilty?" She did not say this, but said, *No one, Lord*[^22]. She accused herself. They could not prove it against her, they withdrew themselves. But she confessed, whose guilt the Lord did not fail to know, but whose faith and confession he was seeking. *Has no one stoned you?* And she, *No one, Lord*. And *No one* on account of the confession of sins, and *Lord* on account of the pardon of merits. *No one, Lord.* I acknowledge both. Who you are I know, what I am I know. For to you I confess. For I have heard, *Confess to the Lord, for he is good*[^23]. I know my confession, I know your mercy. This man said, *I will guard my ways, that I may not offend with my tongue*[^24]. They offended by acting deceitfully; she rather absolved herself by confessing. *Has no one stoned you?* And she, *No one,* and is silent. He again writes. Twice he wrote, this we heard, twice he wrote: once in giving pardon, again in renewing the commandments. For both happen, when we receive pardon. The Emperor has signed. When the document goes forth again, as though other commandments are again given. They are those by which we first heard in the Apostle that love commanded. For first we heard that very reading. Hence the Lord himself said this, *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole strength*; and, *You shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two commandments hang the whole Law and the Prophets*[^25].

**6.** Lest any of us should labor, two certain things were said: God and neighbor; he who made you, and that with which he made you. No one said to you, "Love the sun, love the moon, love the earth and whatever has been made." In these God is to be praised, the maker is to be blessed. *How magnified are your works! we say; you have made all things in wisdom*[^26]. They are yours, you have made all things. Thanks to you! But you have made us above all things. Thanks to you! For we are your image and likeness. Thanks to you! We sinned, we were sought. Thanks to you! We neglected, we were not neglected. Thanks to you! When we despised you, we were not despised; lest we should have forgotten your divinity and lost you, you took up even our humanity. Thanks to you! Where not thanks? *I said, then, I will guard my ways, that I may not offend with my tongue*[^27]. For that woman, when the charge of adultery was brought against her, received pardon, was freed. Is it burdensome to us that, through baptism, through confession, through grace, all receive pardon of all their sins? But let no one now say, "She received pardon. I am still a catechumen. I will commit adulteries, since I am going to receive pardon. Suppose me too to be one like that woman. She confessed, and was freed. Our God is good. And if I sin, I confess to him, and he will forgive me." You attend to his goodness, but consider his justice. As goodness toward pardon, so justice toward punishment. *I said, then, I will guard my ways, that I may not offend with my tongue.* I would like to know whether, in this time in which we minister a sermon to your Charity, no one offends with his tongue. In this time in which we are here, perhaps no one of us has spoken anything evil, but perhaps has thought something evil. Attend. *I said, I will guard my ways, that I may not offend with my tongue. Say truly: I have set a guard to my mouth while the sinner stands against me*[^28].

**7.** Attend. *I have set a guard to my mouth while the sinner stands against me*[^29]. Some wicked man stands against you, reviles you, says things you do not know. Do you set a guard to your mouth. *I said, I will guard my ways, that I may not offend with my tongue.* Let him speak. You, hear, and be silent. For there are two things. Either he speaks the truth, or what is false. If he speaks the truth, you made him speak it. And perhaps this is mercy. For when you are unwilling to hear what you did, God who has care of you tells you through someone what you did, that, confounded by that very shame, you may at some time have recourse to the medicine. Do not, then, repay evil for evil. For you do not know who speaks to you through him. Therefore, if he says something you did, recognize that you have obtained mercy, so that you either think yourself forgotten, or judge this to have been said for your confusion. If you did not do it, your conscience is free. What do you seek? Why are you angry about what you did not do? For what if he says to you, "Thief"? "Drunkard"? Quickly have recourse within to the secrets of conscience. See yourself within. Be the judge, be the examiner of yourself. Seek there, "Where do you think I put my sins which I did?" Or if there are none, say, "I did not do it." If your conscience says to you, "I did not do it," say, *This is our glory, the testimony of our conscience*[^30]. Did your conscience say this to you? Be silent, grieve over the one saying it to you. Say even you to God, "Father, forgive him, for he does not know what he says." Pray to God for him. *I said, I will guard my ways, that I may not offend with my tongue. I have set a guard to my mouth while the sinner stands against me.* For do not think that you then seem holy if no one tempts you. Then you are holy when you are not moved by any reviling, when you grieve over the one speaking, when you do not care what you suffer, but yet grieve over him from whom you suffer. There is all mercy. You grieve because he too is your brother, is your member. He rages against you, he is frenzied, he is sick. Grieve over him, do not rejoice. Only rejoice over the security of your conscience. Thence grieve. For you are a man. See that you too be not tempted. For it was said, Bear one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ. Now, when it says, "Be silent," afterward, when he has grown calm, say to him, "Brother, by your salvation, why did you say to me what I did not do? You have sinned against me, I pray God for you. I indeed grant you pardon, and pray for you to my God, against whom you did injury when you sinned against me. Do not do it further, do not be proud." I do not say, Repay him, O God, who said to me what I did not do. I do not wish to say this. *I have set a guard to my mouth while the sinner stands against me.*

**8.** *I became deaf and was humbled*—this follows—*and was silent from good things*[^31]. *I became deaf*: I did not hear him speaking. How far has that soul advanced, which, even within rejoicing in its brother's error and in the security of its own conscience, does not know that it is barked at outside! What kind of soul is that, how secure, how rejoicing! This is the soul that says to God, *I walked in the innocence of my heart, in the midst of your house*[^32]. Housebreakers were knocking at the doors, but the house was sound. *I became deaf and was humbled*, I did not stand proud against him. *And, humbled, I was silent from good things.* For it was not the time that I should say anything good. For it is the time that you be silent now. After he has become sincere, speak, then he will understand. Generally sick sons have beaten their fathers. Yet they, on account of the weakness of their sons, both were beaten and wept. What affection men show to their sons, that they may not die, awaiting the salvation of their sons! "But he is not my son," you say. But he is the work of God, the image of God, the son of God. If you despise him because he is not your son, do not despise him because he is the son of God, is your brother. "Therefore *I became deaf, and was humbled*. I was not proud, but *was silent from good things, and my sorrow was renewed*[^33], not over myself, but over him, because I did not do what he said. I had sorrow, but because he so said it. I had sorrow; for, having care of my brother, sorrow was renewed in me." This is the way. For this the Lord himself did, our Father, who was also called the Bridegroom. *The sons of the bridegroom will not fast,* he says, *as long as the bridegroom is with them*[^34]. He was beaten by frenzied sons, frenzied sons killed him. He prayed for them. Afterward they became sincere, they acknowledged and believed, and those who were unwilling to be cured by the physician were cured by the physician's disciple, for through Peter they were cured. For when Peter rebuked them, they said, *What shall we do?* Then Peter said, *Repent, and let each of you be baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ*[^35]. Just now they raged, just now they believed. You see what weakness and health do. When they were sick, they were borne with; when they were made well, they were redeemed. Hence we too, brothers, as often as we suffer such things, let us be silent. Let us hold to those two things: either he speaks the truth, or what is false. "And if he does not say it, and I did it, what then? Because he does not say it, and I did it, it is to be wished that he say it, that I may be confounded, because I did it: this is the mercy of God. But if he says this which I did not do, let me rejoice over myself from security, let me grieve over my brother's weakness. *My heart grew hot within me*[^36]. My heart grew hot within me from the love I have toward my brother. But *there is a time to speak, a time to be silent*[^37]. I could not do it just now. Hence Paul himself says, *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to fleshly*[^38]. Yet he spoke. How did he speak? Therefore *my heart grew hot within me, and in my meditation a fire blazes up*[^39]. There is in me the fire of love. I have no one to whom to say that he is weak. Let me, then, humble myself. There will perhaps at some time be a time when I can say something. Yet *forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^40]. Let me forgive him that which I do not have in my conscience. It is little that I have nothing in my conscience, but let me pray for him on account of his conscience."

**9.** This man had now run through, *I said, I will guard my ways. I will set a guard to my mouth. I was humbled*, and *in my meditation a fire blazes up*. Suddenly here he brings forth something else, greater, and after so many struggles and great labors hear what he says: *I spoke with my tongue*[^41]. For the tongue of the soul is the very motion of the will. For just as the tongue is a motion in the body, so the will is a motion in the soul. There is the first tongue; thence it is, thence it speaks to God. For this tongue performs a ministry to men placed outside; but that tongue, which is in the motion of the will, performs a ministry to him who remains within in his temple. That is the true tongue. Whence the Lord said that those who adore him ought to *adore in spirit and in truth*[^42]. That is the true tongue. *I spoke with my tongue: Make known to me, Lord, my end, and the number of my days, what it is, that I may know what is lacking to me*[^43]. If your Holiness attends, let it first understand the sentence, and so the Lord by his mercy, with which he is always accustomed, will give us, by your prayers, that we may be able to discuss this, since it is sufficiently hard. *I spoke with my tongue: make known to me, Lord, my end, and the number of my days, what it is, that I may know what is lacking to me.* See what he prays: *Make known to me, Lord, my end.* The end, brothers, is where we tend, where we are going to remain. That we went out of our houses was for us the end of coming to church. So our journey here is ended. From here again each one has an end of returning to his house. There he ends, where he was tending. Now, then, established in this pilgrimage, we have an end where we tend. Where, then, do we tend? To our homeland. What is our homeland? Jerusalem, the mother of the devout, the mother of the living. There we tend. He himself is our end. And because we did not know the way, he himself, a citizen of that city, made himself the way. We did not know which way to go. There were on the way certain windings, I know not what, thorny, stony, altogether hard. He himself came down here first, who is there the chief; he came down seeking the citizens of that city. For we had wandered astray, and, although we are citizens of Jerusalem, we became citizens of Babylon, we became sons of confusion: for Babylon is confusion. He came down here seeking his citizens, and became our citizen. We did not know that city, that province we did not know. But because we did not come to it, he came down here to his citizens, and himself became a citizen, not by consenting, but by taking on. He came down here. How did he come down? In the form of a servant. God walked here among us as a man. For if he had been only a man, he would not have led us to God. If he had been only God, he would not have been joined to men. He took on with us the equality of our condition, having with the Father the divinity. He took on with us temporality, having with the Father eternity. Here equal with us, there equal with the Father. Our citizen came down here, and said, "What do you do here, citizens of Jerusalem? The image and likeness of God is created only in Jerusalem. The statues of God are not set up in this life. Let us labor, let us return. By what way do we return? Behold, I lay myself down for you, I become for you the way, I will be for you the end. Imitate me." Make known to me, Lord, my end. Him we believe, who is our end.

**10.** God the Father now speaks: "I say to you, O soul whom I made, O man whom I made, I say to you, you had ended. What had you ended? You had perished. I sent to you one to seek you, I sent to you one to walk with you, I sent to you one to pardon. Thence he walked with feet, pardoned with hands. Thence, when after the resurrection he ascended, he showed hands, side, and feet: hands, whence he gave pardon of sins; feet, with which he announced peace to the forsaken; side, whence flowed the price of the redeemed." *The end, then, of the law is Christ, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^44]. Make known to me, Lord, my end. Now your end has been made known to you of the night. How has it been made known to you? Your end was poor, your end was humble, your end was struck with blows, your end was smeared with spittle, against your end false testimony was spoken. *I have set a guard to my mouth while the sinner stands against me.* He himself became for you the way. *Whoever says he abides in Christ ought himself to walk as he walked*[^45]. He is the way. Now let us walk, let us not fear, let us not wander astray. Let us not walk outside the way. For it was said, *Around the way they set scandals for me, and around the way they set a snare for me*[^46]. And behold that mercy. Lest you fall into the snare, you have that very mercy as the way. Make known to me, Lord, my end. Behold, you have the end. Imitate Christ the redeemer: *Be imitators of me, as I also am of Christ*[^47]. How did Paul imitate Christ? Attend to what he said: *In hunger and thirst, in cold and nakedness*, and the rest, down to, *Who is made to stumble, and I am not on fire?*[^48] *I became all things to all, that I might gain all*[^49]. *I have set a guard to my mouth while the sinner stands against me.* What are the words of Paul, brothers? *Who shall separate me from the love of Christ?* Attend to the end. *Who shall separate me from the love of Christ? Tribulation, or distress, or persecution, or nakedness, or peril?*[^50] O loving, O fervent, O running, O arriving man! What did this soul suffer? How did it burn, how did it teach? *Who shall separate me from the love of Christ? Distress?* and the rest, down to, *or the sword?* What did this man suffer? And lest anyone should think that he was proud about it, he said, *Brothers, I do not consider that I have laid hold of it*[^51].

**11.** Why this, *Make known to me, Lord, my end, and the number of my days, what it is*[^52]? How much have I to live here? Why know the day for yourself? *That I may know what is lacking to me.* *What is lacking*, but toward eternity. Now attend to Paul. After so many labors counted up, he says, I do not yet consider that I have *laid hold of it*[^53]. Hear him saying, *What is lacking to me.* Therefore let no one say, "I have now fasted much, labored much, given much. I have now done all the commandments of God. Yesterday I did, today I did." And still today will be, if you ever were. Yesterday always has a today; if you come to tomorrow, it will have a today; and if you come to ten years, there will be a today. Always say you today, *What is lacking to me.* For if Paul, in such great labors a heavenly soldier, if he, after such great exercise and after such great revelations, caught up to the third heaven, and heard ineffable words, and yet, lest he be exalted in the revelations, received a thorn of the flesh to humble him—who is there who can dare to say, "It is enough"? Therefore make known to me, Lord, my end. And behold, you have before you Christ the end. You have nothing more to seek. Since you have now believed, you have now acknowledged. But the matter is not in faith only, but in faith and work. Both are necessary. For even *the demons believe* (you have heard the Apostle) *and tremble*[^54], but it does not profit them that they believe. Faith alone is little, unless works too are joined: *Faith which works through love*[^55], says the Apostle. *Make known to me, Lord, my end, and the number of my days, what it is.* This is not said because, if each of us knew when he would die, he would resolve for himself, for example, to live well. Hence the master himself, wishing us to become anxious, is asked about the day and the hour, and says, *Of that day* and hour no one knows. For he was unwilling that they should know. Hence he said, *nor the Son*[^56]; that is, it is not expedient for you to know. For you would be negligent, not anxious. You will live the better the more anxious you are. Not that I do not know the day, for *all that the Father has is mine*[^57]. *Make known to me, Lord, my end, and the number of my days, what it is.* Make this known to me, that I may always be anxious, because I do not know when the thief will come, that I may know what is lacking to me.

**12.** There, then, brothers, let us be cautious, that we may know what is lacking to us. The temptation of Christians is the proving of Christians. For he who is tempted, it is shown to him what is lacking to him. There are two things: either it is shown to him what he has, or it is shown to him what is lacking. Abraham was tempted not that it might be shown to him what he lacked, but that it might be shown to us what we should imitate. But he was tempted in his son. What was that temptation? He longed for a son in advanced age, of whom there was now despair. Yet, when he heard the promise of God, he did not hesitate at all. He believed, received, merited, took him up. He was born, was nursed, was brought to maturity, was suckled. And it was said to him, *In your seed shall all nations be blessed*[^58]. He knew in what seed, for we have the testimony in the Gospel: *Abraham longed to see my day,* he says; *and he saw it, and rejoiced*[^59]. He knew, then. But after all those things which he had believed, he heard from God, *Abraham, offer me your son as a sacrifice*[^60]. He was tempted. Why? Did God not know his faith? But for our sake he deigned to show these things. For to us it is said, "Offer me a sacrifice from your purse," and we hesitate. What sacrifice? *Give alms, and behold, all things are clean to you*[^61]; and again, *I want mercy rather than sacrifice*[^62]. "Give something from your purse," it is said to you, and you tie it up. What if you were commanded about your son? Behold, about your purse you so hesitate; about your son what would you have done? *That I may know what is lacking to me.* I am going to say it not without sorrow and shame. Many women perhaps wish to serve God, and, if they are bold, say to their parents, "Let me go, I wish to be a virgin of God," or, "I wish to be a servant of God," and they hear, "Neither may you be saved, nor may you be saved. Truly you shall not do what you want. What I want, this you shall do." What if it were said to you, "Kill"? You are alive, eternal life is promised you, it is before you. And do you resist, and hesitate, and fight against it? Surely you are a Christian. "Why, sir, because I am a Christian, ought I therefore not to have grandchildren?" Ought you to have grandchildren? Do you know how much is lacking to you, that you fasted yesterday? Sing what this man said: *Make known to me, Lord, my end, and the number of my days, what it is, that I may know what is lacking to me.* For may God grant, and his mercy, that we be daily stirred, or tempted, or proved, or exercised, or that we advance. *Tribulation works patience, patience proof, proof hope. But hope does not confound*[^63].

**13.** Therefore, my brothers, let us wish daily to know who we are, lest, when we are secure, afterward the day come and nothing be found of what we thought, and it be said to us, *In hell who will confess to you?*[^64] Therefore, my brothers, let us give our effort daily to advancing in God, not sparing the perishable things which here we shall leave behind. Let us attend to the faith of Abraham, because he too was our father. Let us imitate his devotion, let us imitate his faith. If we are tempted in our children, let us not fear; if in our purse, let us not be terrified; if anything is brought upon us by the weaknesses of our body, let us place our hope in God. We are Christians, we are pilgrims. Let no one be terrified, the homeland is not here. He who wishes to have his homeland here will both lose this one, and not come to that one. As good sons, let us tend toward our homeland, that our course may be approved and brought through. Turned to the Lord.
