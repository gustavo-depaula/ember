# Sermon 380: On the Nativity of John the Baptist

**1.** For the quiet even a small voice is enough. But if you wish, brothers, to listen quietly, do not have your heart in your ears, but your ears in your heart. The Church hands down and believes that today has dawned for the festival of blessed John the Baptist. But of this day we ought to believe what the whole world recognizes without variation: yet because no one doubts that it is John's day—not the John who wrote the Gospel, but John the Baptist, the forerunner of the Lord, who appeared so much the greater the more he showed himself lowly, saying, when he himself was thought to be Christ, that he was not worthy to loose the strap of the sandal of the one whom he recognized as Lord, that he might deserve to be his friend. Some, however, think that the day of his passion is celebrated today. Let your holiness first know that it is the day of his nativity, not of his passion. For from the Gospel reading it is found that his nativity precedes the nativity of the Lord by six months. And since the consensus of the Church hands down the day of the Lord's nativity as the eighth day before the Kalends of January, it remains that today be understood as the day of John's nativity.

**2.** John, then, went before the Lord, not as a master before a disciple, but as a forerunner before a judge; not so as to impose authority, but so as to render service. But John's own testimony about this matter is thus: *He who comes after me has been made before me, because he was before me*[^1]. After John the Lord came by being born of the Virgin Mary, not of the Father's substance. For we have received two nativities of the Lord, one divine, the other human; yet both marvelous: that one without a mother, this one without a father; that one eternal, to create the temporal one; this one temporal, to bestow the eternal one. For he of whom John speaks—not the Baptist, but the Evangelist—since *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*, and since *all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^2]: he, so great in the form of God, equal to the Father, he fashioning the times without time, he the judge of the age, from no age before all ages, was made so small that he was born of a woman; but he remained so great that he was not separated from the Father. Rendering him obedience and testimony, as lamps to the coming day, all the prophets, announcing him beforehand, came before him by being born, and clung to him after him by believing. For it was fitting that the one to come be announced beforehand, the one who would work miracles: by which miracles, to those who understand well, he would be shown as God; but to those seeing with human sight he would appear as man, small to the small, but lowly to the proud: by his smallness teaching man to recognize himself as small, lest, not by growing great but by swelling up, he believe himself great. For pride is not greatness, but a swelling. So then, to heal this swelling of the human race, the physician himself, the medicine himself, not only applying a remedy but making himself the remedy, appeared among men a man, offering man to those who see, keeping God for those who believe. For the sight of his humanity healed the weak; the contemplation of his divinity seeks the strong. And there were not yet men who could see God in man, nor could they see anything but man; nor yet ought they to place their hope in man. What, then, was to be done? Man can see man, man ought not to follow man. God was to be followed, who could not be seen; man was not to be followed, who could be seen. So then, that there might be shown to man both one who could be seen by man and one whom man might follow, God became man. O man, for whose sake God became man, you ought to believe something great of yourself: but descend, that you may ascend; because God too, by descending, became man. Cleave to your medicine, imitate your master, recognize your Lord, embrace your brother, understand your God. This is what he, so great and so very small, did—a worm and not a man, but the one through whom man was made. This, then, is what he did: what of John, except what the Lord says of him, except what the Truthful one says of him, except what the Truth says of him? For if we ought to believe John himself about the truth, do we not believe the Truth about John?

**3.** Let the sharer of the truth first bear testimony to the truth itself; and let the Maker of man bear testimony to man. First let us hear what John says of Christ, and then what Christ says of John; let us hear the former, but understand the latter: let him who was born first speak first, but let him be confirmed by the one by whom he was made. *After me*, he says, *he comes, and has been made before me*[^3]. Here now, those who believe that the one through whom all things were made was made before the rest cavil at us with these words, or through these words, and they say: Behold, because he was made; John says, *After me he comes, and has been made before me*. Explain to me what is, *he has been made before me*. I speak their words, and I propose them to be examined. You are accustomed, they say, when something of this sort is said of Christ that shows him less than the Father, to say that the saying is to be referred to the man; so that, in that he is in the form of God, he is equal to the Father, but in that *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man*[^4], the Father is greater. What, then, will you say to this, that John says, *he has been made before me*? Hear what we say: but first see this, that the Apostle, distinguishing both, and yet commending one and the same in both, did not say, taking the form of God. But of the form of God what did he say? *Since he was in the form of God*[^5]: looking to him who was called before him, in whose Gospel is, *In the beginning was the Word*[^6]; indeed, looking to that light, whence John too said this. For John did not say, In the beginning God made the Word: he who could have said it thus, as Moses said—because he was speaking of a creature made by the Lord God: *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^7]. When, then, that one, if he held what the Arians hold, could have said, In the beginning God made the Word, he did not say this, but said, *In the beginning was the Word*. What was in the beginning was not made. For nothing preceded as part of the made Word. For all things that God made, he made by the Word: *He spoke, and they were made; he commanded, and they were created*[^8]. Between the one who says, Let it be made, and what was made by the one who says it, there is a very great distance: but if he is one who speaks, he has a Word; if he has a Word, he made through the Word; if he made through the Word, he did not make the Word.

**4.** Why, they say? Have you not heard, of the earth too: *The earth was invisible and unformed*[^9]? If, they say, you say the Word was not made because it was said, *The Word was*, then neither was the earth made, because it was said, *The earth was*. O blind and heretical madness! Attend, if you have wherewith to attend; hear, if you have wherewith to hear: lest the sound strike the ear in vain, whose heart the truth illumines. I am going to speak the words of the same Scripture which you found there when you read, and passed over when you quarreled. Surely for this reason you suppose that something of the kind was said of the Word of God, when it was said, *In the beginning was the Word*, such as was said of the earth, when it was said, *The earth was invisible and unformed*. I will read to you, in this book of Genesis, the earlier words; for before the writer, the servant of God, said, *The earth was*—so that it might appear that it had been made—he first said, *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*. Behold, I have first set forth to you the earth as made; and made, of course, by God, the Scripture sounding and breaking into the ears of the one who refuses: *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*. These had been made; but not yet adorned, not yet set in order: the earth not yet distinct, but yet already made. But lest you suppose that the earth was at once made in such a way that there was not in it what might be adorned, he went on to say: It had indeed been made, because *in the beginning God made heaven and earth; but the earth*, which God made, was still *invisible and unformed*[^10]. He showed you what sort it was, the thing that had been made; not that it was, the thing that had not been made. John, then, could have said: In the beginning God made the Word, but the Word was; just as, *In the beginning God made heaven and earth; but the earth was*: so that this would be the order of words: In the beginning God made the Word, and the Word was with God; so that we might understand that this Word was with God which God had made the Word. But now you hear: *In the beginning was the Word*. Why do you seek prior things, you who have been made more remote? For you have been made more remote in your error: you speak from the depths, and look toward the depths. I would wish, if you spoke from the depths, that you would stretch your heart upward, and cry out from the deep to the Lord: he would break the clouds of the gloom of your flesh, the light that came in lowliness would open the eyes of lowliness; you would see: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; this was in the beginning with God*[^11]. Let Paul too say: *Since he was in the form of God*[^12]. What does John say of the human nativity? *And the Word was made flesh*[^13]. Let Paul too say: *He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^14]. What, then, were you saying about the words of John the Baptist? Repeat them to me now, speak them now. What does John the Baptist say? *After me he comes*[^15]. I recognize a nativity. The one of the barren woman, the other of the Virgin. Barrenness was turned into fruitfulness; virginity remained after fruitfulness.

**5.** But John, he says, if he had said "made" according to this, that he was born of the Virgin, would not say, *he has been made before me*; because he had been made of the Virgin after him. Therefore, *he has been made before me*—what is it, except that in the beginning the Word had been made? For thus he was before John; for of the Virgin he was after John. Listen, and if you do not quarrel, take heed. For perhaps what you wish to understand is somewhat obscure, so that by quarreling you increase the smoke for yourself, by which you are hindered from understanding. First gaze at that Scripture which teaches you to become teachable. *Be meek to hear the word, that you may understand*[^16]. Bear with me, then, a little: perhaps we shall find how it was said, *he has been made before me*; provided, however, we do not understand that the Word, through whom all things were made, was made. How? he says. If I can, I will say it; if I cannot say it, yet it is not that there is no one who could say it. But I believe and hope that the majesty of that Only-begotten, who, although he was the Word, became an infant, will open the infancy of my tongue, and will bring about birth in the mouth, who brought about conception in the heart. Behold, I will say it as I can: understand as you can; and what you cannot understand, do not reprove, as though you were already great; but defer, that you may deserve to grow. Surely this moves you, how it was said: *He who comes after me has been made before me*[^17]. Let it move you indeed as one seeking, not as one quarreling. Surely I too seek with you: together we shall find, if together we seek; both of us shall receive, if both of us ask; to both of us it shall be opened, if both of us knock. *After me he comes*, he says: recognize his nativity of the Virgin Mary. *He has been made before me*: what is, *he has been made before me*? Understand, he has been set before me. He who comes after me has been made before me. As if two were walking on a journey, and one were slower, the other swifter, and the slower went ahead a little, but after a short while the swifter followed; the slower, going ahead, looks back at the swifter following, and says: He comes after me. And behold, as the other hastens, and draws near, and joins him, and passes him by, the first sees ahead of him the one he was looking back at as behind: surely, if he is in a way astonished and amazed at his swiftness, could not these be his words: Behold, the man was after me, and has been made before me? What is this: he who was after me has been made before me? He who was walking after me, by his swiftness it has come about that he is before me. For if, wherever you read, *he has been made*, you understand only that one was formed who did not exist, then you will say that even the Lord God was made, of whom it was said: *The Lord has been made a refuge for me*[^18]. *The Lord has been made a helper*[^19]; and: *He has been made my salvation*[^20]. How often "made"? And he himself made all things. So, then, understand the words with me. For the same John was not silent about that too, that the Word was; lest there you should think he meant the Word, when he said, *he has been made before me*: so that you may know it pertains to this: *he has been made before me*, because he went before me, because he was glorified more than I, because, while men gazed on me the forerunner, they recognized the Lord, whom by being born I had preceded, whom by serving I had announced; and their hope, their thronging, was upon the Lord; he was glorified as the Son of God, made before me, according to what the Apostle says: *Therefore God exalted him, and gave him the name which is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of those in heaven, on earth, and in the lower world*[^21]. His glory, therefore, did not begin to be, but became known. He has been made before John, because he went before John in honor.

**6.** But see whether deservedly. Ask John himself: He who comes after you, why has he been made before you? He who was following you, why has he been set before you? It follows: *Because he was before me*. This is: *In the beginning was the Word*[^22]. Therefore deservedly *he has been made before me, because he was before me*[^23]. Before John, before Abraham, before Adam, before heaven and earth, before angels, thrones, dominions, principalities, and powers. Why before? Because *all things were made through him*[^24]. Let the servant recognize his lowliness; let the Lord show his majesty. Let the same John say: *I am not worthy to loose the strap of his sandal*[^25]. He would humble himself greatly if he said: I am worthy. For what if he said he was worthy? to sit in judgment at the right hand of the Father? to come to judge the living and the dead? What if he said he was worthy to loose the strap of his sandal? Great is the lowliness, if of this the friend of the bridegroom is worthy. For he was about to say that he was the friend of the bridegroom: and lest in that friendship equality should perhaps be understood by some imprudent person, he calls himself a friend out of love, he casts himself down at the feet out of fear. And it is too little that he casts himself down at the feet; he does not even call himself worthy to loose the strap of the sandal. Lowly indeed, if worthy to loose; but because he says he is not even worthy to loose it, he is worthy to be exalted from lowliness. Let him say also more openly, let him say more distinctly, what is: *After me he comes, before me he has been made*. For he gave the reason too: *because he was before me*. Since: *In the beginning was the Word*; and: *Since he was in the form of God, he did not think it robbery to be equal with God*[^26]. *He must increase, but I must decrease*[^27]. He had to increase who came later, and he to decrease who came earlier. If he who came later increased, he was made before him by increasing. *He*, he says, *must increase, but I must decrease*. This is, *he has been made before me*. And how did Christ increase, John decrease? John preceded the Lord in age in the human nativity; both grew, the years passing, in bodily stature, and reached a certain measure as men do. But John was a man, Christ God and man. If we should understand that Christ increased in his divinity, we are absurd and err too greatly. For a thing increases that it may make progress. God has nothing wherein to increase: for if he has anything wherein to increase, he was less before he increased. Let us return to the form of the flesh. It grew with John; it did not grow when John was diminished. Let us refer it, then, perhaps to glory; and let us understand: *He must increase, but I must decrease*, according to this saying, that *after me he comes, and has been made before me*. For John bore the person of man, and spoke from the type of the human race, for the saving of which Christ had come. Now we had said, brothers, that the lowly God came to proud man. Let man recognize himself man, let God appear to man. Even if Christ came for this, that man might be humbled, and that out of lowliness man might grow: it was fitting that the glory of man now cease, and the glory of God be commended; so that man's hope might be in the glory of God, not in his own glory, the Apostle saying: *He who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^28]. Rightly, then, it is said to man: *What have you that you did not receive? But if you received it, why do you glory, as if you had not received it?*[^29] John, showing this type of human lowliness—because, in proportion as the opinion and fame of Christ grew (not the stature of Christ, not the majesty of Christ, not the wisdom, not the Word of God; but that fame, which began from the least and now holds the earth): the glory of Christ, not the glory of man, so that man may recognize his lowliness, and God may impart his divinity. For indeed the glory of God, brothers, is our glory. The more sweetly God is glorified, the more it profits us. For God will not be loftier because we honor him: let us humble ourselves, and we exalt him. For it is written: *I will exalt you, O Lord*[^30]. What is: *I will exalt you*? He was on earth, and you have placed him in heaven? And does man exalt God? What is: *I will exalt you*? I will confess you lofty. Let man, then, confess himself a man; let him first decrease, that he may increase. Let John, decreasing into lowliness, say that he is not worthy to loose the strap of the sandal, and let him understand that by participation he is made enlightened.

**7.** For this is what John the Evangelist said of him: *He was not the light*[^31]. Did he perhaps do an injury to John, because he says, *He was not the light*, when it was said by the Lord to the Apostles: *You are the light of the world*[^32]? Did the Apostles set themselves before John? They do not, lest they judge the Lord himself a liar, who said: *Among those born of women none has arisen greater than John the Baptist*[^33]. Not, of course, greater in the stature of the body, but in the grace of participated wisdom, in the grace of participated salvation. What, then, does it mean: *He was not the light, but came to bear witness to the light*[^34]? Why did he say this? I know, he says, what I say: to bear witness to the light. For it was the true light, distinct from John—not that John was not at all light, but so that it may be understood as said in comparison with this light: *He was not the light*; according to what he said, that *it was the true light*. How do you distinguish this true one? *Which enlightens*, he says, *every man coming into this world*[^35]. If every man, then John too. But, lest we seem to gather from the words what was not said—although it is to be understood as following—let John himself say: *We have all received of his fullness*[^36]. It was, then, the true light. John light enlightened, Christ light enlightening. For, that you may know John was light, let the Lord now bear testimony to him too. He bore testimony to his Lord, the testimony of truth about his Lord, which he had from his Lord. For he had received from his Lord, that he might speak of his Lord; but that the Lord should speak of his servant, he had not received from his servant: both in himself Christ spoke of John, and in John Christ spoke of himself. Let the truth itself, then, bear testimony; let us hear that John is light. It is said to the Jews about John: *He was a burning and shining lamp*[^37]. And surely a lamp is light, not as the day, but yet it is light. Therefore a lamp is lit that it may shine, and John was enlightened that he might speak. If, then, John was enlightened that he might speak, let him recognize himself a lamp, lest he be extinguished by the wind of pride. But perhaps the disciples are light, and John a lamp? For the Lord said of John: *He was a burning and shining lamp*; but of the disciples: *You are the light of the world*. Did he set his disciples before John? Are the faithful too whom Paul addresses to be set before John, to whom he says: *For you were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord*[^38]? So the Apostles are light, and the faithful are light, justified out of sinners, made faithful out of unbelievers: yet the disciples were not called a lamp. Attend: perhaps they were called so, and not in another place, but here: so that he might show in what way he called them light; because it was not the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world. Follow the words of the Gospel: *You are the light of the world. A city set on a mountain cannot be hidden*. But did he speak of a city, not of light? Follow further: *Neither do they light a lamp, and put it under a bushel*[^39]. You are such light as John is: an enlightened lamp. As one whose voice, or whose voice, had gone before: *You will light my lamp, O Lord; my God, enlighten my darkness*[^40]. What darkness in the Apostles? *We too were once*, etc., like that reprobate and blasphemer: *I who was formerly a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and an injurer*. Behold the darkness, let the lamp be lit: *But I obtained mercy*[^41]. And that you may know it was said of them: *Neither do they light a lamp, and put it under a bushel, but on a lampstand, that it may give light to all who are in the house*; immediately follows: *So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works*—but in lowliness, because man must decrease. But how does God increase? *And glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^42]. The Father is glorified, the Son is glorified: because the Father glorifies the Son, and the Son glorifies the Father. Let man, then, recognize himself lowly, let him recognize God on high humbled for his sake, so that man, who had been humbled in the confession of sin, may be exalted in the attainment of righteousness. These two: the Lord and John, lowliness and brightness: God lowly in brightness, man lowly in weakness; God lowly for the sake of man, man lowly for the sake of man. For God was lowly that he might profit man, man lowly that he might not harm himself.

**8.** Let us recognize, then, these two even in the very difference of their passions. We read that John suffered martyrdom for the truth: did he not also for Christ? Not for Christ, if Christ is not the truth. Not at all for his name, but yet for the truth itself. For John was not beheaded because he confessed Christ. But he was counseling temperance, he was counseling righteousness: he was saying, *It is not lawful for you to have your brother's wife*[^43]. For the Law that had commanded this had commanded it concerning those who died without children, that the brothers should take the wives of their brothers, and raise up seed for their brothers. Where this cause did not exist, it was nothing but lust. This lust John rebuked, the chaste man the unchastity: because that too which it prefigured is of this kind: *He must increase, but I must decrease*[^44]. It had been commanded, that if anyone died without seed, the one who was nearest of kin should take his wife, and raise up seed for his brother. For why had God commanded this, except that in that way might be signified the seed of the brother to be raised up to the brother's name? For it was commanded that he who was born from there should bear the name of the deceased. Christ died, the Apostles took his spouse, the Church. Those whom they begot of her they named not Paulians or Petrians, but Christians. Let both passions, then, speak these two things: *He must increase, but I must decrease*. He increased on the wood, this one was diminished by the iron. The passions too spoke this mystery; let the days too speak it. Christ is born, and they increase; John is born, and they decrease. Let the honor of man, then, decrease, let the honor of God increase; so that the honor of man may be found in the honor of God.
