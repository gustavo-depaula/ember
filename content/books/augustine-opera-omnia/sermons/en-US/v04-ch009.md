# Sermon 101

**1.** In the reading of the Gospel which has just been recited, we are admonished to ask what is the harvest of which the Lord says: *The harvest indeed is plentiful, but the laborers are few. Ask the Lord of the harvest to send laborers into his harvest* [^1]. Then he added to his twelve disciples, whom he also named Apostles, another seventy-two, and sent them all, as appears from his words, to a harvest already prepared. What, then, was that harvest? For that harvest was not among these Gentiles, where nothing had been sown. It remains, then, that we understand this harvest to have been among the people of the Jews. To that harvest the Lord of the harvest came, to that harvest he sent reapers: but to the Gentiles, not reapers, but sowers. Let us understand, then, that the harvest was made among the people of the Jews, the sowing among the peoples of the Gentiles. For from that harvest the Apostles were chosen. There, when it was being reaped, it was ripe; because there the Prophets had sown. It is a delight to look upon God's husbandry, and to take pleasure in his gifts, and in the laborers in his field. For in this husbandry he was working who said: *I have labored more than all of them* [^2]. But strength was given him for working by the Lord of the harvest: therefore he added: *Not I, but the grace of God with me* [^3]. For that he was engaged in husbandry he shows well enough, where he says: *I planted, Apollos watered* [^4]. And this Apostle, from Saul become Paul, that is, from the proud the least (for "Saul" is named from Saul: but "paulus" means little: whence, interpreting his own name in a way, he says: *I am the least of the Apostles* [^5]): this Paul, then, that is, the little and least, sent to the Gentiles, says he was sent especially to the Gentiles. He himself writes, we read, we believe, we preach. He himself says, then, in his Letter which is to the Galatians, that, already called by the Lord Jesus, he came to Jerusalem, and conferred the Gospel with the Apostles, and that right hands were given to him, and a sign of concord, a sign of agreement, that what they had learned from themselves differed from him in nothing. Then he says it pleased both him and them, that he should go to the Gentiles, but they to the circumcision [^6]; he the sower, they the reapers. Rightly too the Athenians, though not knowing it, told him his own name. For hearing the word from him: *Who is this*, they said, *sower of words* [^7]?

**2.** Attend, then, and let it delight you to look with me upon God's husbandry, and in it two harvests, one past, the other to come: the past among the people of the Jews, the one to come among the peoples of the Gentiles. Let us prove this: and from where, except from the Scripture of God the Lord of the harvest? Behold, we have it said here in the present passage: *The harvest is plentiful, but the laborers are few. Ask the Lord of the harvest to send laborers into his harvest* [^8]. But because in that harvest there would be Jewish opponents and persecutors: *Behold*, he says, *I send you as lambs in the midst of wolves* [^9]. Let us show something more plainly about this harvest in the Gospel according to John: at the well where the Lord sat wearied, great mysteries indeed were enacted, but the time is too short for all to be treated. But what pertains to the present matter, hear. For we undertook to show the harvest among the peoples in whom the Prophets had preached: for they were sowers, that the Apostles might be reapers. The Samaritan woman speaks with the Lord Jesus, and among other things, when the Lord had told her how God ought to be worshiped, she says: *We know that the Messiah is coming, who is called Christ, and he will teach us all things* [^10]. And the Lord to her: *I who speak with you am he*. Believe what you hear: why do you seek what you see? *I who speak with you am he* [^11]. But what she had said: *We know that the Messiah is coming*, whom Moses and the Prophets announced, *who is called Christ*. For now the harvest was in the ear. It had received the Prophets as sowers to bring it forth, it awaited the Apostles, ripe, as reapers. As soon as she heard this, she believed, and left her water jar, and ran in haste, and began to announce the Lord. The disciples had then gone to buy bread: returning, they found the Lord speaking with the woman, and they marveled. Yet they did not dare to say to him: *What, or why, do you speak with her* [^12]? They held their wonder within themselves, they pressed down their boldness in their heart. To this Samaritan woman, then, the name of Christ was not new; she already awaited him coming, she had already believed that he would come. Whence had she believed, unless Moses had sown? But hear this more expressly. The Lord then said to his disciples: *You say that summer is yet far off; lift up your eyes, and see the regions white for the harvest* [^13]. And he adds: *Others have labored, and you have entered into their labors* [^14]. Abraham labored, Isaac, Jacob, Moses, the Prophets; they labored by sowing; at the coming of the Lord the harvest was found ripe. The reapers, sent with the sickle of the Gospel, carried the sheaves to the Lord's threshing floor, where Stephen would be threshed.

**3.** But here that Paul comes forward, and is sent to the Gentiles. And he does not keep silent about this in commending the grace which he received especially and properly. For he says in his Scriptures that he was sent to preach the Gospel where Christ had not been named. But now, since that harvest has been made, and all the Jews who remained, let us attend to the harvest which we are. For it was sown by the Apostles and the Prophets. The Lord himself sowed (for he himself was in the Apostles, since Christ himself also reaped. For they did nothing without him: he is perfect without them. For he himself said to them: *Without me you can do nothing* [^15]). Christ, then, sowing now among the Gentiles, what does he say? *The sower went out to sow* [^16]. There reapers are sent to reap, here the sower went out to sow, tireless. For what did he fear, that some fell on the way, some on rocky places, some among thorns? If he feared those difficult soils, he would not come to the good soil. What is it to us, what have we now to do with disputing about the Jews, and speaking of the chaff? The eye to us—not on the way, not on the rock, not among the thorns, but in the good soil (*My heart is ready, O God* [^17])—from which come the thirtyfold, the sixtyfold, the hundredfold [^18]. That is less and that is more; but the whole is wheat. Let there be no wayside, where the enemy, like a bird, carries off the seed trampled by passers-by. Let there be no rock, where a little soil at once makes to sprout what cannot bear the sun. Let there be no thorns—the desires of the world, the cares of a vicious life. For what is worse than the care of life, which does not allow one to come to life? What more wretched, than by caring for life to lose life? What more unhappy, than by fearing death to fall into death? Let the thorns be rooted out, let the field be made ready, let the seeds be received, let the harvest be reached, let the granary be desired, let not the fire be feared.

**4.** It pertains to us, then—whom, such as we are, the Lord has set as laborers in his field—to say these things to you, to sow, to plant, to water, even to dig around some trees and apply a basket of dung; it pertains to us to do these things faithfully: to you, to receive faithfully: to the Lord, to help us as we work, you as you believe, all as we labor, but conquering the world in him. I have said, then, what pertains to you: I want to say what pertains to us. But perhaps it seems to some of you that I have said I want to say something superfluous, and speaking within himself in his thought he says: O if only he would now dismiss us! He has already said what pertains to us; what pertains to himself, what is it to us? I think it better that, in alternating and mutual love, we belong to you. For now you are one household, and we are all dispensers of that same household, we all belong to one Lord. Nor what I give do I give of my own; but of his from whom I too receive. For if I give of my own, I will give a lie. For *he who speaks a lie speaks of his own* [^19]. You ought, then, to hear what pertains to the dispenser, whether that in yourselves, if you find such, you may rejoice together, or that in this very thing you may be instructed. For how many in this people will be dispensers? And we too were there where you are: and we who now seem to measure out food from the higher place to our fellow servants, a few years ago in the lower place were receiving food with our fellow servants. A bishop, I speak to laymen: but from this I know to how many future bishops I am speaking.

**5.** So let us see how we ourselves understand what the Lord commanded those whom he was sending to preach the Gospel and to reap the prepared harvest. Let us see. *Do not*, he says, *carry a purse or a bag or sandals, and salute no one on the way. And into whatever house you enter, say: Peace to this house. If there is a son of peace there, your peace will rest upon him; if not, it will return to you* [^20]. All must be run through briefly. *Do not*, he says, *carry a purse*. What do we do? We carry a purse, we admit it, when we make a journey: we carry a little provision on the way. *Nor a bag*. Perhaps we do not carry a bag. *Nor sandals*. What? Did he command us to walk barefoot? You yourselves see: we walk shod. For we do not utter words and hide our feet; before your eyes we walk shod. And indeed, if anyone salutes us on the way and we do not salute him, we shall be judged proud; we are blasphemed unto the blaspheming of the Lord. So we salute men even on the way. That, indeed, is easy, when we enter a house, to say: *Peace to this house*. But just as we are pressed by the purse, by the sandals (let us look to the Lord himself, in case he might console us and grant us the understanding of these words. For even this, which we said is easy, that on entering a house we say, *Peace to this house*, than which nothing is easier), what follows, if we take it carnally, there too we are in peril. For what does he say? *Say: Peace to this house*. Nothing easier. But what follows? *If there is a son of peace in that house, your peace will rest upon him; if not, it will return to you*. What is this? How will peace return to me? Shall I then have it, if it returns; if it has rested, have I lost it? Far be this from the mind of the sound. So neither must that be taken carnally, and through this perhaps neither the purse, nor perhaps the sandals nor the bag, and most of all that where, if we take it simply, without examination, pride seems imposed upon us, that we salute no one on the way.

**6.** Let us attend to our Lord, the true example and help. Let us prove that he is the help: *Without me you can do nothing* [^21]; let us prove that he is the example: *Christ suffered for us*, Peter says, *leaving us an example, that we should follow his footsteps* [^22]. This Lord of ours had a money box on the way; that very money box he had entrusted to Judas. He endured a thief indeed. But I, with my Lord's leave, desiring to learn, say to my Lord: Lord, you endured Judas the thief; from where did you have what was to be stolen from you? Me, a wretched, weak man, you admonished not even to carry a purse. You carried a money box, and there was something for the thief to take. If you had not carried it, neither would he have found what to take away. What remains, except that he say to me: Understand what you hear: *Do not carry a purse*? What is the purse? Money shut up: hidden wisdom. What is: *Do not carry a purse?* Do not become wise in your own eyes. Receive the spirit: a fountain ought to be in you, not a purse; from which it may be poured out, not where it may be shut up. The bag is the same as the purse.

**7.** What are the sandals? What are the sandals we use? They are leather from carrion. They are the hides of dead animals, coverings for our feet. What, then, are we commanded? To renounce dead works. This he admonishes in the figure of Moses unto glory, when the Lord said to him: *Loose your sandals. For the place where you stand is holy ground* [^23]. What is so holy a ground as the Church of God? Standing in her, then, let us loose our sandals, let us renounce dead works. For about these sandals with which we walk shod, that same Lord of mine consoles me. If he had not been shod, John would not say of him: *I am not worthy to loose the strap of his sandal* [^24]. Let understanding obedience, then, look on; let proud hardness not creep in. I, he says, fulfill the Gospel, because I walk barefoot. You can. I cannot. What we have received together, let us guard. Let us burn with love: let us love one another. And so it will come about that I love your strength, and you bear my weakness.

**8.** But what seems good to you, who were unwilling to understand how these things were said, and are forced, through a perverse understanding, to calumniate the Lord himself about the money box and the sandals? What seems good to you? Does it then please you that, while making a journey, we meet our dear ones and do not salute them, the elders, nor return greeting to the younger? Do you for that reason fulfill the Gospel, because you salute and keep silent? Here indeed you will be like, not a traveler making a journey, but a milestone showing the way. So let us put off stupidity, let us understand the Lord's words, and salute no one on the way. For not without cause are we commanded this; otherwise he would not want us to do what he commanded. What, then, is: *Salute no one on the way*? It can indeed be taken simply thus; because he commanded us to do the enjoined things in haste, and said it thus: *Salute no one on the way*, as though saying: Pass over everything, until you complete what is enjoined, in that manner of speech by which sayings are wont to be heightened in the custom of speaking. Nor let us go far afield. In the same sermon a little later he says: *And you, Capernaum, who are exalted to heaven, will be brought down to hell* [^25]. What is: *Exalted to heaven?* Did the walls of that city touch the clouds, reach the stars? But what is: *You are exalted to heaven?* You seem too happy to yourself; too powerful, too proud you are. Just as, then, this was said for heightening: *You are exalted to heaven*, to that city which surely did not ascend or rise up to heaven, so for the heightening of haste it was said: Run so, do so what I have enjoined, that not even the least things should delay you in doing it; but, despising everything, hasten to the appointed end.

**9.** But there is another thing here that I rather think on. I do not pretend to ignore what pertains more to me, to all of us dispensers, but also to you the hearers. He who salutes speaks salvation. For the ancients too wrote thus in their letters: so-and-so to so-and-so, salvation. The salutation received its name from salvation. What, then, is: *Salute no one on the way?* Those who salute on the way salute by occasion (I see you have quickly understood; yet I ought not to finish now. For you have not all understood quickly. I saw some who understood by their voice; more I see who are still seeking in silence. But because we speak of the way, let us walk as on the way. You who are swift, await the slow, and walk together). What, then, did I say? He who salutes on the way salutes by occasion, he was not going to the one whom he salutes. He was doing something else, this other thing met him. He was seeking one thing, he found across his path another thing to do. What, then, is it to salute by occasion? To announce salvation by occasion. But what else is it to announce salvation, than to preach the Gospel? If, then, you preach, do this through love, not by occasion. For there are men who, seeking something else, announce the Gospel: of whom the Apostle, groaning, says: *For all seek their own, not the things of Jesus Christ* [^26]. And these saluted, that is, they announced salvation, they preached the Gospel, but they were seeking something else, and therefore they saluted by occasion. And what is this? If you are such, whoever you are, you do it: rather (not such whoever you are who do it, but perhaps some such one) who do it, if you are such, you do not do it, but it is done concerning you.

**10.** For the Apostle even admitted such men; yet he did not command that they should be such. And they themselves do something, and profit comes from them. They seek something else and announce Christ. What the announcer seeks, do not be concerned; hold what he announces. What he wants, do not attend to, it does not pertain to you. Hear salvation from his mouth; hold salvation from his mouth. Do not be the judge of his heart. But you see him seeking other things. What is that to you? Hear salvation. *What they say, do* [^27]. He made you secure. What? *What they say, do*. They do evil things: *What they do, do not do* [^28]. They do good things, they do not salute on the way, they do not announce the Gospel by occasion; be imitators of them, as they are of Christ. A good man preaches to you: pluck the grape from the vine. A wicked man preaches to you: pluck the grape hanging on the hedge. The cluster grew with its entangled tendril among the thorns, and it did not sprout from the thorns. Clearly, when you see something of the sort and are hungry, pluck carefully, lest, when you put your hand to the grape, you be torn by the thorns. This is what I say: hear what is good in such a way that you do not imitate evil manners. Let him preach by occasion, let him salute on the way; it will harm him that he did not hear the precept of Christ: *Salute no one on the way*; it will not harm you, who, whether from one passing by or one coming, hear salvation, hold salvation. Hear the Apostle, as I had promised, admitting these men: *For what then, so long as in every way, whether by occasion or in truth, Christ is announced? And in this I rejoice, but I will also rejoice. For I know that this turns out for my salvation through your prayer* [^29].

**11.** Therefore let such Apostles of Christ, preachers of the Gospel, not saluting on the way—that is, not seeking something else, but announcing the Gospel with genuine love—come to a house and say: *Peace to this house* [^30]. They do not say it with the mouth only; what they are full of, they pour out. They preach peace and they have peace. They are not such as those of whom it was said: *Peace, peace, and there is no peace* [^31]. What is: *Peace, peace, and there is no peace?* They preach it and do not have it; they praise it and do not love it; *they say, and do not do* [^32]. But nevertheless do you receive peace, whether by occasion or in truth Christ is announced. He, then, who is full of peace and salutes: *Peace to this house, if there is a son of peace there* [^33], his peace rests upon him; *if not*—for perhaps there is no son of peace there—he who saluted has lost nothing: *it will return*, he says, *to you* [^34]. That returns to you which did not depart from you. For this he wanted to say: It profits you that you announced; it profited him nothing who did not receive it. Not because he remained empty did you for that reason lose your reward. It is rendered to you for your will; it is rendered to you for the love which you expended. He will render it to you who made you secure with the angelic voice: *Peace on earth to men of good will* [^35].
