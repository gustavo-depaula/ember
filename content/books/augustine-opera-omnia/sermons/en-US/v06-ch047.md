# Sermon 220: On the Paschal Vigil

**1.** We know, brothers, and hold with most firm faith, that Christ died once for us[^1]; for sinners the righteous, for servants the Lord, for captives the free, for the sick the physician, for the wretched the blessed, for the needy the rich, for the lost the seeker, for the sold the redeemer, for the flock the shepherd, and, what is more wonderful than all, for the creature the Creator: keeping, however, what he always is, handing over what he was made; God hidden, man appearing; by power giving life, by weakness dying; by divinity unchangeable, by flesh passible: as the Apostle says: *Who was handed over for our trespasses, and rose for our justification*[^2]. That this was done once, you know well. And yet the solemnity, as though it were done often, in the rolling round of times repeats what the truth proclaims, with so many voices of the Scriptures, was done once. Nor yet are truth and solemnity contrary, so that the one lies and the other tells the truth. For what truth indicates was done once in fact, this solemnity, to be celebrated often, renews in pious hearts. Truth opens the things that were done, as they were done: but solemnity, not by doing them, but by celebrating them, does not permit the things past to pass away. Finally, *our Passover, Christ, was sacrificed*[^3]. He surely once slain, who *now dies no more, death will no longer have dominion over him*[^4]. Accordingly, according to the voice of truth, we say the Passover was done once, and will not be again hereafter: but according to the voice of solemnity, every year we say the Passover will come. So I think is to be understood what is written in the Psalm: *The thought of man will confess to you, and the remnants of thought will celebrate a solemn day for you*[^5]. For unless thought committed to memory what is said of things temporally done, it would find no remnants after the time. Therefore the thought of man, beholding the truth, confesses to the Lord: but the remnants of thought, which are in the memory, at the known times cease not to celebrate solemnities, lest the thought be judged ungrateful. To this pertains so illustrious a solemnity of this night, where by watching we work, as it were, the resurrection of the Lord through the remnants of thought, which, done once, we more truly confess by thinking. Whom, then, the proclaimed truth made learned, far be it that the deserted solemnity make irreligious. This made this night illustrious throughout the whole world. This shows the multitudes of the Christian peoples, this confounds the darkness of the Jews, this overthrows the idols of the pagans.

[^1]: See Rom 6:10
[^2]: Rom 4:25
[^3]: 1 Cor 5:7
[^4]: Rom 6:9
[^5]: Ps 76:10 (75:11)
