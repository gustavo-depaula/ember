# Sermon 338: On the Dedication of a Church

**1.** When good works, even those that are done for God's sake, are shown to men, when the good and religious do them, human praises are not sought, but things to be imitated are set forth. For the mercy that is done in a good work is twofold, bodily and spiritual. To the hungry, the thirsty, the naked, the stranger, help is given by bodily mercy: yet these same things, when they are shown, both provoke others to imitation, and feed their spirits and minds too. One is fed by the good work, another by the good example: for both hunger. The one wants to receive whereby he may be nourished, the other wants to see what he may imitate. The reading of the holy Gospel too, which was just recited, admonishes us of this truth. For to Christians believing in God, working well, expecting the hope of eternal life for their good works, it is said: *You are the light of the world*[^1]. And to the whole Church spread abroad everywhere it is said: *A city set on a mountain cannot be hidden*[^2]. *It will be,* he says, *in the last times the manifest mountain of the house of the Lord, prepared on the top of the mountains*[^3]. It is the mountain which grew from a small stone, and by growing filled the whole world. On it is built the Church, which cannot be hidden.

**2.** *Neither do they light a lamp and put it under a bushel, but on a lampstand, that it may give light to all who are in the house*[^4]. Well does the reading occur, when lampstands are dedicated, that there may be one who works, a lamp placed on the lampstand. For the lamp is the man who works well. But what is the lampstand? *But far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^5]. So he who does according to Christ, and does for the sake of Christ, that he may glory only in Christ, is a lampstand. Let him give light to all, let them see what they may imitate: let them not be lazy, not dry: let what they see profit them; let them not be seeing with the eyes, and blind in heart.

**3.** But lest perhaps it occur to someone, that the Lord as it were commands good works to be hidden, where he says: *Beware of doing your righteousness before men, to be seen by them; otherwise you will not have a reward with your Father who is in heaven*[^6]: this question must be resolved, that we may know how we obey the Lord, and may not be unable to obey him, when we hear him commanding the contrary. Here he says: *Let your works shine before men, that they may see your good deeds*[^7]: here he says: *Beware of doing your righteousness before men, to be seen by them.* Do you want to know how this question is to be resolved, and that, unless it is resolved, it will be troublesome if it remains unresolved? Certain men do well, and fear to be seen; and with all zeal, as much as they can, they cover their good works. They watch for when no one sees them: then they hand out something, fearing lest they offend against that precept where it was said: *Beware of doing your righteousness before men, to be seen by them.* But the Lord did not command good works to be hidden, but in good works not to think of human praise. Finally, when he said: *Beware of doing your righteousness before men:* where did he end it? *To be seen by them.* That they may do it for this reason, that they may be seen by men; let them seek this fruit of the good work, let them bear this away: let them expect nothing else, let them desire nothing of higher and heavenly profit. But if one does it only for this reason, that he may be praised: this the Lord forbade. *Beware of doing it.* How? *To be seen by them.* Beware of having this fruit, the sight of men.

**4.** But he commands our works to be seen, and says: *No one lights a lamp and puts it under a bushel, but on a lampstand, that it may give light to all who are in the house*[^8]. And: *So let your works shine before men, that they may see,* he says, *your good deeds.* And he did not stop there: but, *that they may glorify,* he added: *your Father, who is in heaven*[^9]. It is one thing to seek your own praise in a good work, another to seek the praise of God in a good work. When you seek your own praise, you have remained in the sight of men: when you seek the praise of God, you have acquired eternal glory. So let us so act, that we may not be seen by men, that is, let us so act, that we may not seek the sight of men as a reward: but let us so act, that from those seeing and imitating us we may seek the glory of God, and may know that if he did not make us such, we would be nothing.
