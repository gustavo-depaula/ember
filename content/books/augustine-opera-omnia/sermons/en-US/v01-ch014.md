# Sermon 13: A Sermon Delivered at the Table of Saint Cyprian

On the Sixth Day Before the Kalends of June

On the Psalm Where It Is Said:

"Be Instructed, All You Who Judge the Earth"

**1.** *Be instructed, all you who judge the earth*[^1]. To judge the earth is to tame the body. Let us hear the Apostle judging the earth: *I do not so fight,* he says, *as one beating the air, but I chastise my body and bring it into servitude, lest perhaps, preaching to others, I myself become a castaway*[^2]. Hear, then, earth, the one judging the earth, and judge the earth that you may not be earth. For if you judge the earth, you will be heaven, and you will recount the glory of the Lord made in you. For *the heavens recount the glory of God*[^3]. But if you do not judge the earth, you will be earth. But if you are earth, you will belong to him to whom it was said, *You shall eat earth*[^4]. Let the judges of the earth, then, hear. Let them chastise the body, bridle lusts, love wisdom, conquer desire. And that they may do this, let them be instructed.

**2.** But this is the sum of instruction: *Serve the Lord in fear and exult in him with trembling*[^5]—to exult in him, not in oneself: in him from whom is whatever you are, both that you are a man, and that you are just, if indeed you are now just. But if you think that you are indeed a man from him, but just from yourself, you do not serve the Lord in fear, nor exult in him with trembling, but in yourself in presumption. And what will happen to you, except what follows? *Lest at any time the Lord be angry and you perish,* it says, *from the just way*[^6]. For it does not say, "Lest at any time the Lord be angry, and you do not enter the just way," but, *you perish from the just way*. For now you seem just to yourself, by not seizing another's goods, not committing adultery, not committing murder, not bearing false witness against your neighbor, by honoring father and mother, by worshiping one God, by not serving idols and demons. From this way you will perish, if you presume these things to yourself, if you think these things are from yourself. For the unbelieving do not enter the just way; the proud perish from the just way. For what does it say? *Be instructed, you who judge the earth*[^7]. And lest perhaps you attribute to yourselves the strength and power by which you judge the earth, and believe it to be yours from yourselves, do not do so: *Serve the Lord in fear; exult,* not in yourselves in presumption, but in him in fear, *lest at any time the Lord be angry, and you perish from the just way, when his anger shall blaze up quickly*[^8]. What, then, is to be done that we may not perish from the just way? *Blessed are all who trust in him*[^9]. If blessed are those who trust in him, wretched are those who trust in themselves. *For cursed is every man who places his hope in man*[^10]; therefore not in yourself either, because you too are a man. For if you place your hope in another man, you will be disorderedly humble. But if you place your hope in yourself, you are dangerously proud. But what is the difference? Both are ruinous; neither of these is to be chosen. The disorderedly humble is not raised up, the dangerously proud is cast down.

**3.** Finally, let your Holiness know that these words were said for the refuting and consuming of that thinking by which each one trusts in himself: *Serve the Lord in fear and exult in him with trembling*[^11]; hear the Apostle saying these same words, and explaining why the sentence was said. Behold, the words are the Apostle's: *With fear and trembling work out your own salvation*[^12]. "Why, then, do I work out my salvation with fear and trembling, when it is in my power to work out my salvation?" Do you wish to hear why with fear and trembling? *For it is God who works in you*[^13]. Therefore with fear and trembling. Because what the humble obtains, the proud loses. If, then, it is God who works in us, why was it said, *Work out your own salvation*? Because he so works in us that we also work. *Be my helper*[^14]. He designates himself a worker too, who calls upon a helper. "But my will," he says, "is good." I grant it, yours. But by whom was it given, by whom roused? Do not hear me, ask the Apostle: *For it is God,* he says, *who works in you both to will*—he works in you both to will—*and to work, for his good pleasure*[^15]. What, then, was it that you arrogated to yourself? What was it that you went proud and perished? Return to your heart, find yourself evil, and that you may be good, call upon the Good. For nothing in you pleases God except what you have from God; but what you have from yourself displeases God. If you consider your good things, *what have you that you did not receive? But if you received, why do you glory as though you had not received?*[^16] He alone is the one who knows nothing but to give. He has no giver, who has no better. Than whom if you are inferior—rather, because you are inferior—be glad that you were made to his image, that you may be found in him, you who in yourself perished. For in yourself you could do nothing but lose yourself; you do not know how to find yourself, unless he who made you seeks you.

**4.** Yet let us also address those who judge the earth according to this visible and popular understanding. For kings, leaders, princes, judges judge the earth. Each one, according to the office he has received, judges the earth. But what is it that is said, "He judges the earth," except that he judges the men who are on the earth? For if you do not take "earth" properly except as that which you tread, then it was said to farmers, *You who judge the earth*[^17]. But further, if kings judge the earth, and whoever under kings receive power from kings, let these too be instructed, because earth judges earth, and ought to fear him who is in heaven, earth judging earth. For he judges his own equal, man judges man, mortal mortal, sinner sinner. For if that saying of the Lord should come forth into the midst, *Whoever is without sin, let him first cast a stone at her*[^18], does not everyone judging the earth suffer an earthquake? Let us recall the chapter of the Gospel. The Pharisees, tempting the Lord, brought before him a woman caught in adultery, for which sin the penalty had been defined by the law, namely the law given through Moses the servant of God. With this deceitful and fraudulent dilemma, then, the Pharisees came to the Lord, so that if he ordered the disgraced woman to be stoned, he would lose his gentleness, but if he forbade what the law had ordered, he would be held to have sinned against the law. But just as, when they questioned him about paying tribute to Caesar, he caught them by their own mouth, asking in turn whose was the coin produced, whose image and inscription it bore—those who questioned answering that the image of Caesar was on the coin, he answered them according to their own mouth, *Render to Caesar what is Caesar's, and to God what is God's*[^19], so that he might admonish that the image of God in man is to be rendered to God just as on a coin its image is rendered to Caesar. So too in the case of this adulteress he questioned the questioners, and therefore judged the judges. "I do not forbid," he says, "that she be stoned whom the law ordered to be stoned, but I ask by whom. For I do not resist, but I require a minister of the law." Hear, finally: "Do you wish to stone according to the Law? *Whoever is without sin, let him first cast a stone at her*[^20]."

**5.** But when he had heard these things, with his finger he *wrote on* the earth, that he might instruct the earth. But when he had said this to the Pharisees, he himself lifted up his eyes, and looked upon the earth and made it tremble. Accordingly, when he had said this, he again began to write on the earth. And they, pricked and made to tremble, departed one after another. O earthquake, where the earth was so moved that it even changed its place! When they had departed, then, the sinful woman and the Savior remained. The sick woman and the physician remained. The wretched woman and mercy remained. And looking at the woman he said, *Has no one condemned you?* And she, *No one, Lord*[^21]. But yet she was still anxious. For sinners did not dare to condemn, did not dare to stone the sinful woman, who, looking at themselves, found themselves alike. But the woman was still in great danger, because that judge too remained for her who was without sin. *Has no one,* he says, *condemned you?* And she, *No one, Lord*: if not even you, I am secure. To which anxiety the Lord answered with silent voice, *Neither will I condemn you*[^22]. Neither will I, although I am without sin, *neither will I condemn you*. From vengeance conscience held them back; toward succoring, mercy inclines me.

**6.** Attend to these things, and *be instructed, all you who judge the earth*[^23]. All, namely, because it is also to be understood thus of those of whom the Apostle says, *Let every soul be subject to the higher powers. For there is no power except from God. And those that are, are ordained of God. Whoever resists the power, resists the ordinance of God. For princes are not a terror to the good work, but to the evil. But do you wish not to fear the power? Do good, and you will have praise from it*[^24]. And if not from it, yet out of it. For either you act justly, and the just power will praise you; or, acting justly, even if an unjust power condemns you, the just God will crown you. And through this, hold to justice, live well; and whether it condemns or absolves, you will have praise out of it. That blessed one whose blood was shed here—did he not find praise from the very power before which, and by which, he was seen to be judged? He promised confession, remained in faith, did not fear death, shed his blood, conquered the devil.

**7.** Therefore, that you may not be of an unjust power, whoever of you men wish to have power over men, be instructed, lest you judge perversely, and perish in soul before you destroy anyone in flesh. You wish to be a judge—you cannot by merits, or by money. I do not yet reproach this. For perhaps you desire to benefit human affairs, and you buy office that you may benefit. That you may serve justice, you do not spare money. First, for your own sake, be a judge over yourself. First judge concerning yourself, that, secure about the inner chamber of your conscience, you may proceed against another. Return into yourself, attend to yourself, examine yourself, hear yourself. There I want to prove you a sound judge, where you do not seek a witness. You wish to proceed with power so that another may tell you about another what you do not know. First judge within. Did your conscience say nothing to you about yourself? If you are unwilling to deny it, it certainly said something. I do not wish to hear what it said; you who heard it, judge. It told you about yourself what you did, what you took, what you sinned. I would like to know what sentence you pronounced. If you heard well, if you heard rightly, if in hearing yourself you were just, if you ascended the tribunal of your own mind, if you suspended yourself before yourself on the rack of the heart, if you applied the grave torturers of fear, you heard well if you so heard, and without doubt by repenting you punished the sin. Behold, you both examined, and heard, and punished. And yet you spared yourself. So hear your neighbor too, if you are instructed as the psalm admonished: *Be instructed, you who judge the earth*[^25].

**8.** If you so hear your neighbor as you hear yourself too, you will pursue the sins, not the sinner. And if perhaps someone is hard toward correcting his sins, turned away from the fear of God, you will pursue this very thing in him, this very thing you will try to correct, this you will labor to destroy and take away, that the man may be saved, the sin condemned. For there are two names, man and sinner. God made the man, the man himself made the sinner. Let what man made perish, let what God made be freed. Do not, then, go as far as death, lest, when you pursue the sin, you destroy the man. Do not go as far as death, that there may be one to repent; let the man not be killed, that there may be one to be amended. Holding this love toward men in your heart, man, be a judge of the earth. And love to terrify, but love. If you are proud, be proud against sins, not against the man. Rage against that which displeases you even in yourself, not against him who was made just as you. From one workshop you come, you had one craftsman, one clay is your material. What do you lose by not loving him whom you judge? Since you lose justice by not loving him whom you judge. But let penalties be applied. I do not refuse, I do not forbid, but with the mind of one loving, with the mind of one cherishing, with the mind of one correcting.

**9.** For do you not instruct your son? And you first act so that, if it can be, he may be instructed by shame and generosity, may blush to offend his father, not fear him as a stern judge. You rejoice at such a son. But if perhaps he despises these things, you apply blows too, you strike fear into him, you inflict pain, but you seek his salvation. Many have been corrected by love, many by fear, but through the trembling of fear they came to love. *Be instructed, you who judge the earth*[^26]. Love and judge. For innocence is not so to be sought that discipline should perish. It is written, *Whoever casts away discipline is unhappy*[^27]. It can well be added to this sentence: as whoever casts away discipline is unhappy, so whoever denies discipline is cruel. I have dared to say something, my brothers, which I am compelled by the obscurity of the matter itself to expound to you somewhat more fully. I repeat what I said: Whoever casts away discipline is unhappy. This is manifest. Whoever does not give discipline is cruel. I hold it entirely, I hold it and show it: by striking, devout; by sparing, cruel. I set an example before your eyes. Where do I find one devout in striking? I do not go to others, I go to a father and son. A father even when he strikes, loves. And the boy does not want to be beaten. He despises the wish, he consults for the advantage. Why? Because he is a father, because he prepares an inheritance, because he nurtures a successor. Behold, by striking the father is devout, by striking he is merciful. Give me a man cruel in sparing. I do not depart from these persons, I set these very ones before my eyes. But if an untrained and undisciplined boy lives so as to perish, and the father overlooks it, the father spares, the father fears to offend his lost son by the harshness of discipline, is he not cruel in sparing? *Be instructed,* therefore, *you who judge the earth*[^28], and by judging well, expect a reward not from the earth but from him who made heaven and earth.
