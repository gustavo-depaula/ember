# Sermon 135

**1.** The Lord Jesus, as we heard when the holy Gospel was read, opened the eyes of a man who was born blind. Brothers, if we attend to our inherited penalty, the whole world is blind. Therefore Christ the enlightener came, because the devil had been the blinder. He made all to be born blind, who deceived the first man. Let them run to the enlightener, let them run, believe, receive clay made from spittle. The spittle is, as it were, the Word, the earth is the flesh. Let them wash their face in the pool of Siloam. But it pertained to the Evangelist to expound to us what Siloam signifies, and he says: *Which is interpreted: sent*. Who is the sent one, except he who said in that very reading: *I*, he says, *came, that I might do the works of him who sent me* [^1]? Behold Siloam; wash your face, be baptized, that you may be enlightened, and may see, you who before did not see.  
Behold, first to this, what was said, open your eyes: *I*, he says, *came that I might do the works of him who sent me*. Now here the Arian rises up, and says: Behold, you see that Christ did not do his own works, but the Father's who sent him. He would never say this, if he saw, that is, if he washed his face in the one who is sent, as in Siloam. What, then, do you say? Behold, he says, he himself said it. What did he say? *I came, that I might do the works of him who sent me*. Not, then, his own? No. And what is it that he himself says, he himself Siloam, he himself the sent one, he himself the Son, he himself the Only one whom you complain to be degenerate? What is it that he says: *All things which the Father has, are mine* [^2]? You say that he did another's works; because he said: *I will do the works of him who sent me*. I say that the Father had things that were another's; I speak according to your heart. Whence do you want to prescribe to me that Christ said: *I came, that I might do the works of him*, as though not mine, but *of him who sent me?*

**2.** I ask you, Lord Christ, resolve the question, end the contention. *All things*, he says, *which the Father has, are mine*. Therefore are they not the Father's, if they are yours? For he did not say: All things which the Father has, he gave me: although even if he had said this, he would have shown equality. But it is troublesome what he said: *All things which the Father has, are mine*. If you understand: All things which the Father has, are the Son's; all things which the Son has, are the Father's. Hear him in another place: *All mine is yours, and yours is mine* [^3]. The question is finished, about those things which the Father and the Son have; they have them in concord, do not you quarrel. The works of the Father which he calls his own works; because *yours is mine* too: because he calls the works of that Father, to whom he said: *All mine is yours, and yours is mine*. Therefore my works are yours, and your works are mine. *For whatever the Father does*: he himself said it, the Lord said it, the Only-begotten said it, the Son said it, the Truth said it. What did he say? *Whatever the Father does, these the Son too does likewise* [^4]. A great expression, a great truth, a great equality. *All things which the Father does, these the Son too does*. It would suffice: *All things which the Father does, these the Son too does*. It does not suffice; I add: *likewise*. Why do I add: *likewise?* Because those who do not understand, and who walk with eyes not yet opened, are wont to say: The Father did by commanding, the Son by obeying; therefore unlike. But if *likewise*, as he, so he; so the things he does, these this one does.

**3.** But the Father commands, he says, that the Son may do. You relish it carnally indeed, but without prejudice to the truth, I yield to you. Behold the Father commands, the Son obeys: is the Son for that reason not of his nature, because the one commands, the other obeys? Give me two men, father and son: they are two men; he who commands is a man; he who obeys is a man: he who commands and he who obeys have one and the same nature. Did not he who commands beget a son of his own nature? Did he who obeys, by obeying, lose his nature? Take, then, meanwhile, as you take two men, the Father commanding, the Son obeying, yet God and God. But at the same time these two are men, at the same time he is one God: this is a divine miracle. Meanwhile if you want me to acknowledge obedience with you, first acknowledge the nature with me. The Father begot this, which he himself is. If the Father begot something other than what he himself is, he did not beget a true Son; the Father says to the Son: *From the womb before the morning star I begot you* [^5]. What is: *before the morning star?* By the morning star times are signified. Therefore before times, before everything that is called "before"; before everything that is not, or before everything that is. For the Gospel does not say: In the beginning God made the Word; as it said: *In the beginning God made heaven and earth* [^6]; or: In the beginning the Word was born; or: In the beginning God begot the Word. But what does it say? *It was, it was, it was*. You hear: *It was*, believe. *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^7]. So often you hear: *It was*, do not seek time, because it always was. He, then, who always was, and always was with the Son, because God is able to beget without time; he said to the Son: *From the womb before the morning star I begot you*. What is: *from the womb?* Did God have a womb? Shall we think God to be arranged with bodily members? Far be it. And why did he say: *from the womb*, except that it might be understood that he begot of his own substance? Therefore from the womb came forth this, which he himself was who begot. For if he who begot was one thing, and what came forth from the womb was another; it is a monster, not a Son.

**4.** Therefore let the Son do the works of him who sent him, let the Father too do the works of the Son. Surely the Father willed, the Son effected. Behold I show that the Son wills, and the Father does. Where do you show it, you say? Now I show it. *Father, I will*. Now if I wanted to calumniate, behold the Son commands, and the Father does. What do you will? *That where I am, they too may be with me* [^8]. We have escaped, we shall be there where he is: there we shall be, we have escaped. Who blots out the Almighty's: *I will?* You hear the will of the power, hear also the power of the will. *As the Father*, he says, *raises the dead, and gives them life; so the Son too gives life to whom he wills* [^9]. *To whom he wills*. Do not say: The Son gives life to those whom the Father commands him to give life. *To whom he wills he gives life*. Therefore those whom the Father wills, and those whom he himself wills; because where there is one power, there is one will. Let us have, then, in a heart not blind, one and the same nature of the Father and the Son; because the Father is true, the Son is true. What he is, this he begot; because the begotten did not degenerate.

**5.** I do not know what may move someone in the words of him who was blind, and perhaps makes many who do not understand well despair. For among his other words, the same one whose eyes were opened says: *We know that God does not hear sinners* [^10]. What do we do, if God does not hear sinners? Do we dare to ask God, if he does not hear sinners? Give me one who asks, behold there is one who hears. Give me one who asks, examine the human race from the imperfect to the perfect. Ascend from spring to summer; for we have sung this: *The summer and the spring you made* [^11]. That is, both the spiritual now and those still carnal you made; because the Son too says: *Your eyes saw my imperfect being. My imperfect being* which is in my body, *your eyes saw*. And what then? Do those who are imperfect have hope? They have it plainly. Hear what follows: *And in your book all will be written* [^12]. But perhaps, brothers, the spiritual ask and are heard, because they are not sinners. What do the carnal do? what do they do? Will they perish? Will they not ask God? Far be it. Give me that Publican. Come, Publican, stand in the midst, show your hope, lest the weak lose hope. For behold the Publican went up with the Pharisee to pray, and with face cast down to the earth he said, standing afar off, beating his breast: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*. And he went down more justified, than that Pharisee [^13]. He who said: *Be merciful to me a sinner*; did he speak the truth, or falsehood? If he spoke the truth, he was a sinner; and he was heard, and was justified. What is it, then, that you said, you to whom the Lord opened the eyes: *We know that God does not hear sinners?* Behold, God hears sinners. But wash your inner face, let it come to pass in the heart, what came to pass in your face; and you will see that God hears sinners. The imagination of your heart deceived you. There is still that which he may do for you. Surely this man was cast out of the synagogue; he heard, came to him, and said to him: *Do you believe in the Son of God?* And he: *Who is he, Lord, that I may believe in him?* He saw, and did not see; he saw with his eyes, but did not yet see with his heart. The Lord said to him: *You both see him*, that is, with the eyes: *and he who speaks with you, he is the one. Then, prostrate, he adored him* [^14]. Then he washed the face of his heart.

**6.** Lie down, then, to prayers, sinners; confess your sins, pray that they may be blotted out, pray that they may be lessened, pray that, as you make progress, they may fail; yet do not despair, and pray, sinners. For who has not sinned? Begin from the priests. To the priests he said: *First offer sacrifices for your sins, and so for the people* [^15]. The sacrifices convicted the priests; so that if anyone said he was just and without sin, it would be answered him: I do not attend to what you say, but what you offer; your victim convicts you. Why do you offer for your sins, if you have no sins? Or do you lie to God in the sacrifice? But perhaps the priests of the old people were sinners, those of the new people are not sinners. Surely, brothers, because God willed it, I am his priest, I am a sinner, with you I beat my breast, with you I ask pardon, with you I hope God to be merciful. But perhaps the holy Apostles, the first, the chief rams of the flock, shepherds, members of the shepherd, perhaps they themselves had no sin. Truly they too had it, they had it: they are not angry, because they confess it. I would not dare. First hear the Lord himself saying to the Apostles: *So pray*. As those priests were convicted by sacrifices, so these by prayer. *So pray*. And among the other things he commanded them to pray, he set this too: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors* [^16]. What do the Apostles say? Daily they ask their debts to be forgiven them. They enter as debtors, go out absolved, and to prayer return as debtors. This life is not without sin, so that as often as one prays, so often sins are forgiven.

**7.** But what shall I say? Perhaps they, when they learned the prayer, were still weak. Perhaps someone will say this: When the Lord Jesus taught them the prayer, they were still little ones, were weak, were carnal; they were not yet spiritual, who do not have sin. What then, brothers, when made spiritual did they cease to pray? Christ ought, then, to have said: Now pray such things, and to give to the spiritual another prayer. There is one, it is the same, it is he who gave it; that, then, pray in the Church. But let us take away the controversy: when you say the holy Apostles were spiritual, until the Lord suffered they were carnal; this you are going to say. In short, what is true, while he hung they trembled, and then the Apostles despaired, when the thief believed. Peter dared to follow, when the Lord was led to the passion, dared to follow, who came to the house, and in the courtyard grew weary, stood at the fire, and grew cold: he stood at the fire, froze stiff with cold fear. Questioned by a maidservant, he denied Christ once; questioned again, he denied; questioned a third time, he denied [^17]. Thanks be to God, that the questioning ceased: if the questioning did not cease, the denial would long be repeated. Therefore after he rose, then he confirmed them, then they were made spiritual. Now then did they no longer have sin? The spiritual Apostles wrote spiritual Letters, sent them to the Churches: they had no sin, this you say. I do not believe you, I ask them. Say, holy Apostles, after the Lord rose, and confirmed you with the Holy Spirit sent from heaven, did you cease to have sin? Tell us, I beg. Let us hear, lest sinners despair, lest they cease to ask God, because they are not without sin. Tell us. One of them says it. And which? He whom the Lord loved more, and who reclined upon the breast of the Lord [^18], and drank the secret of the kingdom of heaven which he belched forth. When I ask him: Do you have sin, or not? He answers, and says: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*. But that John is the one who said: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^19]. See how great things he had transcended, that he might come to the Word. Such and so great a one, who flew like an eagle above the clouds, who with serenity of mind discerned: *In the beginning was the Word*; he himself said: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. But if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, and cleanse us from all iniquity* [^20]. Therefore pray.
