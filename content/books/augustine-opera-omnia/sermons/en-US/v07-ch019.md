# Sermon 246: In the Days of Easter, a Discourse Given on Thursday

**1.** In many ways the Lord Jesus appeared to his faithful after the resurrection; all the Evangelists had material from which to write, as the Spirit supplied to them the recollections of the things that they were to write. One said one thing, another another. Someone could leave out something true, not say something false. Reckon that all these things were said by one; truly one said them, because one Spirit was in them all. What have we heard today? That the disciples did not believe that Jesus had risen, and did not believe him when he himself had foretold this very thing. The matter is manifest, and is therefore written down, that we may give great thanks to God, because we believed in him whom we did not see on earth. To their eyes and hands it was scarcely persuaded what we believe.

**2.** You have heard that his disciple entered the tomb and *saw the linen cloths laid there and believed; for he did not yet know the Scriptures, that Jesus must rise from the dead*. So you have heard, so it was read: *He saw and believed; for he did not yet know the Scriptures*[^1]. Therefore it should have said: He saw and did not believe; for he did not yet know the Scriptures. What, then, does it mean: *He saw the linen cloths and believed?* What did he believe? What the woman had said: *They have taken away my Lord from the tomb.* For if you heard—rather, since you heard—this is what that woman had said: *They have taken away my Lord from the tomb, and I do not know where they have put him*[^2]. On hearing this they ran. He entered the tomb, saw the linen cloths, and believed what the woman had said; that Christ had been taken away from the tomb. Why did he believe that Christ had been taken away and stolen from the tomb? Why? *For he did not yet know the Scriptures, that Jesus must rise from the dead.* He had entered, he had not found him. He ought to have believed that he had risen, not that he had been stolen.

**3.** What, then, does it mean? We are wont to speak to you about it every year. But the reading itself is solemnly read, and let the sermon itself be solemnly rendered. Why did the Lord Christ say to the woman who now recognized him . . . For first he had said: *Whom do you seek? why do you weep?* But she thought him to be the gardener[^3]. And, if you consider, if we are his vegetables, Christ is the gardener. Is he not a gardener *who planted a grain of mustard seed*, that is, a very small and fervent seed? And it grew and ascended and made so great a tree that even the birds of the air rested in its branches. *If you have*, he himself says, *faith like a grain of mustard seed*[^4]. A grain of mustard seed seems small, nothing more contemptible in appearance, yet nothing stronger. What else is this but the greatest ardor and inmost power of faith in the Church? So she thought him the gardener and said to him: *Lord*—for honor's sake, because she was begging a favor, she therefore said Lord—*if you have taken him away, show me where you have put him, and I will take him away*[^5]. As if to say: He is necessary to me, not to you. O woman, you think a dead Christ necessary to you, recognize the living one. You seek the dead one, the Lord saw himself sought as dead. But the dead one would profit us nothing, unless he had risen from the dead. And he was sought as dead, he showed himself alive. How alive? He called her by name: *Mary*; and at once she, hearing her name: *Rabboni.* For a gardener could say: *Whom do you seek? why do you weep?* But *Mary* none could say except Christ. He called her by name, who called her to the kingdom of heaven. He said this name which he himself had written in his book: *Mary.* And she: *Rabboni*, which means *Master*[^6]. Now she had come to know him by whom she was enlightened to be known; now he who before was thought the gardener was seen to be Christ. And the Lord to her: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father*[^7].

**4.** What does this mean: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father*? If she could not touch him standing on earth, could she touch him sitting in heaven, as if he said: Now do not touch me, then touch me when I have ascended to the Father? Let your Charity recall yesterday's reading, when the Lord appeared to the disciples and they thought they were seeing a spirit. But he, wishing to take away this error from them, offered himself to be touched. What did he say? It was read yesterday, the sermon was from it. *Why are you troubled, and why do thoughts arise in your heart? See my hands and my feet, handle and see*[^8]. Surely he had now ascended to the Father, when he said: *handle and see*, offering himself to be touched by his disciples—not touched but handled, that there might be faith in the true flesh of the true body, that the solidity of the truth might be shown even to human touch? He offers himself, then, to be handled by the hands of the disciples, and says to the woman: *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father.* What does it mean? The men could touch him only on earth, the women had to touch him then in heaven: *For I have not yet ascended to my Father?* What, then, is it to touch, except to believe? For by faith we touch Christ, and it is better not to touch with the hand, and to touch by faith, than to handle with the hand and not to touch by faith. It was no great thing to touch Christ. The Jews touched him when they seized him, they touched him when they bound him, they touched him when they hung him up, they touched him even by touching badly, and what they touched they lost. Touch you by faith, O Catholic Church, by faith touch you. If you think Christ only a man, you have touched on earth. If you believe Christ the Lord equal to the Father, then you have touched when he ascended to the Father. So he ascends for us when we have understood him. Once, at that time, he ascended, but now he ascends daily. And for how many he does not ascend, for how many he lies on earth, how many say: He was not a man, how many say: He was a great man, how many say: He was a prophet, how many Christians have arisen who would say, like Photinus: He was a man, he had nothing more; but he surpassed all pious and holy men in the excellence of righteousness and wisdom, for he was not God. O Photinus, you have touched on earth, you hastened to touch, you rushed your verdict, you did not arrive at the truth, equal to the Father, and therefore did not arrive at the homeland, because you erred on the road.

**5.** Then let us hear his words: *I ascend to my Father and your Father, and to my God and your God*[^9]. Why not: to our Father and our God, but with a distinction: *my Father and your Father, and my God and your God*? *My Father*, because I am the only one, *your Father* by grace, not by nature. *My Father*, because he always was this, *your Father*, because I chose you. *My God and your God.* How is the Father God of Christ? He is his Father because he begot him. How is he his God? Because he also created him. He begot him, the only-begotten Word; he created him from the seed of David according to the flesh; therefore he is both Father of Christ and God of Christ; Father of Christ according to the divinity, God of Christ according to the weakness. Hear how God is of Christ, let us question the Psalm. Through the prophet he himself said: *From my mother's womb you are my God*[^10]. Before my mother's womb my Father, from my mother's womb my God. Why, then, is there a distinction there too, namely, *my Father and your Father*? There is a distinction because in one way he is the Father of the only-begotten Son, in another our Father. His Father by nature, ours by grace. He ought, then, to have said: to my Father and your Father and our God, because God, if he is God of a creature, and therefore of Christ because Christ too is a creature according to man. Father of Christ distinctly. Since he is the creator of Christ, the God of Christ, why distinctly, when according to man Christ is a creature, we too are creatures, according to man Christ is surely a servant—*taking the form of a servant*, says the Apostle[^11]—why, then: *my God and your God*, and there too distinctly? Distinctly plainly. For all of us our God formed by the propagation of sin. But he both was made man otherwise, he was born of a virgin, a woman conceived him not by desiring but by believing, he did not draw the propagation of sin from Adam. We were all born through sin; he was born without sin, who cleansed sins. Therefore there too a distinction: *my God and your God.* For you were created from seed, from male and female, you came from the desire of the flesh with the propagation of sin: *For who is clean in your sight? Not even an infant whose life is of one day upon the earth*[^12]. Finally, there is haste with infants, that there may be loosed in them what they added not by living, but drew by being born. Not so Christ. *My God and your God; my God* because of the likeness of sinful flesh; *your God* because of sinful flesh.

**6.** Thus far concerning the Gospel reading that relates to the resurrection of the Lord which the Evangelist John wrote, and it will be enough to have made a sermon, because other readings of the same Gospel of John concerning the same resurrection of the Lord will have to be read. For no one narrated his resurrection more copiously than holy John, so that it cannot be read in one day, but is read on another, is read again on a third, until whatever holy John wrote down about the Lord's resurrection is finished.
