# Sermon 201: On the Epiphany of the Lord

**1.** A very few days ago we celebrated the birthday of the Lord: but today we celebrate, with no less due solemnity, the manifestation by which he began to be made manifest to the Gentiles. On that day the Jewish shepherds saw him born: today the Magi, coming from the East, adored him. For that cornerstone had been born, the peace of two walls from the circumcision and the uncircumcision, not from a small diversity of those coming; that they might be joined in him who was made our peace, and made the two one[^1]. This was foreshadowed in the shepherds of the Jews and the Magi of the Gentiles. From there began what would bear fruit and grow in the whole world. So let us hold these two days, of the Nativity and the Manifestation of our Lord, most dear with spiritual joy. The Jewish shepherds were led to him by the angel's announcing, the Gentile Magi by the star's pointing. This star confounded the empty calculations and divinations of the astrologers, when to the worshipers of the stars it pointed out the Creator of heaven and earth as rather to be adored. For he himself, being born, declared a new star, who, being slain, darkened the ancient sun[^2]. By that light the faith of the Gentiles was begun; by that darkness the faithlessness of the Jews was accused. What was that star, which neither ever before appeared among the constellations, nor afterward remained to be pointed out? What was it, except a magnificent tongue of heaven, which would declare the glory of God[^3], which would proclaim the unwonted childbearing of a virgin with an unwonted brightness, to which, afterward not appearing, the Gospel would succeed throughout the whole world? What, finally, did the Magi say in coming? *Where is he who is born king of the Jews?* What is this? Had not so many kings of the Jews been born before? Why did they so greatly desire to know and adore a king of a foreign nation? *For we saw*, they say, *his star in the East, and we have come to adore him*[^4]. Would they have sought him with such devotion, longed for him with so great an affection of piety, unless they recognized him as the king of the Jews, who is also the king of the ages?

**2.** Hence Pilate too was surely breathed upon by some breeze of truth, when in his passion he wrote the title: *King of the Jews*: which the Jews tried to correct as faulty. To them he answered: *What I have written, I have written*[^5]: because it was foretold in the Psalm: *Do not corrupt the inscription of the title*[^6]. So let us mark this great and wonderful sacrament. The Magi were of the Gentiles, Pilate himself too was of the Gentiles: those saw a star in heaven, he wrote a title on wood: yet both sought or recognized not a king of the Gentiles, but of the Jews. But the Jews themselves neither followed the star nor consented to the title. So now there was foreshadowed what the Lord himself afterward said: *Many will come from the East and the West, and will recline with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob in the kingdom of heaven: but the sons of the kingdom will go into the outer darkness*[^7]. For the Magi had come from the East, Pilate from the West. Whence the former bore witness to the rising, that is, the being born, the latter to the setting, that is, the dying, of the king of the Jews: that with Abraham and Isaac and Jacob, from whom the Jews drew their origin, they might recline in the kingdom of heaven; not propagated from them through the flesh, but grafted into them through faith: so that he of whom the Apostle speaks, the wild olive to be grafted into the olive, was already foreshown. For this reason indeed, not a king of the Gentiles, but of the Jews, was sought or recognized by those same Gentiles; because the wild olive came to the olive, the olive did not come to the wild olive. Yet the branches to be broken off, that is, the unbelieving Jews, both answered the Magi inquiring where Christ would be born: *In Bethlehem of Judah*[^8]; and, when Pilate reproached them for wishing their king to be crucified, they raged most stubbornly. And so the Magi adored, the Jews pointing out the place of Christ's being born; because in the Scripture which the Jews received, we come to know Christ. Pilate, of the Gentiles, washed his hands, the Jews demanding the death of Christ[^9]; because with the blood which the Jews shed, we wash away our sins. But of Pilate's testimony through the title, in which he wrote that Christ is the king of the Jews, there is another place for discussing it: which is the time of the passion.

**3.** But now, as far as concerns the manifestation of the newborn Christ, of whose day of manifestation, which by a Greek word is named Epiphany, by which he began to be made manifest to the Gentiles when the Magi adored him, let us say the few things that remain. For again and again it is a delight to consider how, when the Magi inquired where Christ would be born, the Jews answered: *In Bethlehem of Judah*[^10]; and yet they themselves did not come to him, but, as they departed, the same star led the Magi to that place in which the infant was; that it might be shown that it could point out the city too, but for this had withdrawn itself a little, that the Jews might be questioned. For the Jews were questioned for this, that it might be shown that they carry the divine testimonies not to their own salvation and recognition, but to that of the Gentiles. For on this account that nation was driven from its kingdom and dispersed through the lands, that it might everywhere be compelled to become a witness of the faith of which it is the enemy. For, the temple, sacrifice, priesthood, and kingdom itself being lost, in a few old sacraments they keep their name and race; lest, mingled with the nations without distinction, they perish, and lose the testimony of truth: as Cain received a sign, that no one might kill him, who, envious and proud, killed his righteous brother[^11]. This, no doubt, can also be not unfittingly understood in the fifty-eighth psalm, where Christ speaks from the person of his body and says: *My God showed me concerning my enemies, Do not slay them, lest at some time they forget your law*[^12]. For in those enemies of the Christian faith it is shown to the Gentiles how Christ was prophesied: lest perhaps, when they had seen the prophecies fulfilled with so great a manifestation, they should think those Scriptures composed by the Christians, when the things foretold about Christ are recited, which are seen to be fulfilled. So the books are brought forth by the Jews, and thus God shows us concerning our enemies; whom for this reason he does not slay, that is, does not utterly destroy from the lands, lest they forget that law: which, by reading and by observing certain parts of it, however carnally, they remember, so that they take judgment for themselves and provide testimony for us.
