# Sermon 106

**1.** You have heard the holy Gospel, how the Lord Jesus, in what he was saying to the Pharisees, was surely admonishing his own disciples, lest they should think righteousness lies in cleansing of the body. For the Pharisees every day, before they dined, washed themselves with water: as though a daily washing could be the cleansing of the heart. Indeed, he showed what kind of men they were. He who saw them said it: for he discerned not only their face, but also their inner parts. Indeed, that you may know this, that Pharisee to whom Christ answered thought within himself, made no sound with his voice, and yet the Lord heard him. For within himself he reproved the Lord Christ, because he had come thus to his banquet unwashed. He was thinking, the Lord was hearing, and therefore answering. What, then, did he answer? *Now you Pharisees cleanse what is outside the dish: but within you are full of deceit and plunder* [^1]. O to come to dinner! how he did not spare the man by whom he had been invited? Rather, by chiding he spared him, that he might spare him, corrected, in the judgment. Then what did he show us? That Baptism too, which is applied once, cleanses through faith. But faith is within, not outside. Whence it is said and read in the Acts of the Apostles: *Cleansing their hearts by faith* [^2]. And the apostle Peter in his Letter speaks thus: *So*, he says, he gave you too a likeness from the ark of Noah, how eight souls were saved through water. And he added: *So you too in like fashion Baptism will save; not the putting off of the filth of the flesh, but the appeal of a good conscience* [^3]. This appeal of a good conscience the Pharisees despised, and they washed what was outside: within they remained most filthy.

**2.** And what did he say to them afterward? *Nevertheless give alms, and behold, all things are clean to you* [^4]. Almsgiving is praised: do it and prove it. But attend a little: it was said to the Pharisees. These Pharisees were Jews, the elite, as it were, of the Jews. For the more noble and learned were then called Pharisees. They had not been washed by the Baptism of Christ: in Christ the only-begotten Son of God, who walked among them, and was not recognized by them, they had not yet believed. How, then, does he say to them: *Give alms, and behold, all things are clean to you?* If the Pharisees heard him, and gave alms, then according to his word all things would be clean to them; what need was there that they should believe in him? But if they could not be cleansed unless believing in him who cleanses the heart by faith; what is: *Give alms, and behold, all things are clean to you?* Let us attend, and perhaps he himself explains it.  
When he had said these things, without doubt they thought that they did give alms. And how did they give? They tithed all their goods, from all their fruits they drew off the tenth part, and gave it. Not easily does any Christian do this. Behold what the Jews did. Not wheat only, but also wine and oil: nor this only, but even contemptible things, on account of the precept of God, cumin, rue, mint, and dill they tithed entirely: that is, they drew off the tenth part, and gave alms [^5]. I believe, then, that they called it back to themselves, and thought the Lord Christ spoke emptily, as though to those who did not give alms: when they themselves knew their own works, that they tithed even the most minute and contemptible of their fruits, and gave alms. They laughed at him within themselves, saying such things, as though to men who did not give alms.

**3.** The Lord, knowing this, at once added: *Nevertheless woe to you, Scribes and Pharisees, who tithe mint, cumin, and rue, and every herb* [^6]. That you may know it, I know your alms. Certainly these are your alms, these your tithes: you tithe even the minute and contemptible things of your fruits: *And you leave the weightier things of the Law, judgment and love* [^7]. Attend. You have left judgment and love, and you tithe herbs. This is not to give alms. *And these*, he says, *you ought to do, and not to omit those*. Which to do? *Judgment and love, justice and mercy; and not to omit those*. Do those: but set these first.

**4.** If these things are so, what did he say to them: *Give alms, and behold, all things are clean to you* [^8]? What is: *Give alms?* Do mercy. What is, Do mercy? If you understand, begin with yourself. For how are you merciful to another, if you are cruel to yourself? *Give alms, and all things are clean to you*. Give true alms. What is alms? Mercy. Hear the Scripture: *Have mercy on your soul, pleasing God* [^9]. Give alms, *have mercy on your soul, pleasing God*. Your soul begs before you, return to your conscience. Whoever you are who live badly, whoever you are who live faithlessly, return to your conscience: and there you find your soul begging, you find it needy, you find it poor, you find it wretched, you find it perhaps not even needy, but struck dumb by want. For if it begs, it hungers for righteousness. When you have found your soul such (these things are within in your heart), first give alms, give it bread. What bread? If the Pharisee asked, the Lord would say to him: Give alms with your soul. For this he said to him; but he did not understand, when he related to them the alms they gave, and they thought they were hidden from Christ; and he said to them: I know that you do them; you tithe mint and dill, cumin and rue: but I speak of other alms: you despise judgment and love. In judgment and love give alms with your soul. What is, in judgment? Look, and find; be displeased with yourself, pass sentence on yourself. And what is love? *Love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and your whole soul, and your whole mind; love your neighbor as yourself* [^10]; and you have done mercy first with your soul, in your conscience. But if you pass over this alms, give what you will, bestow as much as you will; draw off from your fruits, not tithes, but halves; give nine parts, and leave one for yourself: you do nothing, when you do not do it with yourself, and with yourself you are poor. Let your soul be fed, lest it perish of hunger. Give it bread. What bread, he says? He himself speaks with you. If you would hear, and understand, and believe the Lord, he himself would say to you: *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven* [^11]. Would you not first give this bread to your soul, and do alms with it? If, then, you believe, you ought to act so as first to feed your soul. Believe in Christ; and the things that are within will be cleansed, and the things that are outside will be clean. Turned toward the Lord, and so on.
