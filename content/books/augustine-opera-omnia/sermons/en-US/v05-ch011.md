# Sermon 157

**1. 1.** As your Holiness remembers that the Apostle said, dearly beloved brothers, *In hope we have been saved. But hope*, he says, *that is seen is not hope; for who hopes for what he sees? But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience*[^1]. Hence the Lord our God himself admonishes us to offer you an exhortatory and consolatory sermon—he to whom it is said in the Psalm, *You are my hope, my portion in the land of the living*[^2]. He himself, I say, who is our hope in the land of the living, bids that we address you in this land of the dying; that you may look not on the things that are seen, but on the things that are not seen. For the things that are seen are temporal, but the things that are not seen are eternal[^3]. Because, then, we hope for what we do not see, and wait for it with patience; rightly is it said to us in the Psalm, *Wait for the Lord, act manfully, and let your heart be strengthened, and wait for the Lord*[^4]. For the promises of the world always deceive; but the promises of God never deceive. But because the world seems about to give here what it promises—that is, in this land of the dying, in which we now are; while God is going to give us what he promises in the land of the living—many grow weary of waiting for the truthful one, and are not ashamed to love the deceiver. Of such Scripture says, *Woe to those who have lost endurance, and have turned aside into crooked ways*[^5]. For even to those who act manfully and, with strengthened heart, wait for God, the sons of everlasting death do not cease to make insults, flaunting their temporal delights, which for a time sweeten their throats, but afterward they will find them more bitter than gall. For they say to us: Where is what is promised to you after this life? Who has returned here from there and shown that the things you believe are true? Behold, we rejoice in the abundance of our pleasures, because what we see, we hope for; but you are tormented in the labors of continence, by believing what you do not see. Then they add what the Apostle mentioned, *Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we shall die*. But see what he himself warned must be guarded against: *Evil conversations*, he says, *corrupt good morals. Be soberly righteous, and do not sin*[^6].

**2. 2.** Beware, then, brothers, lest by such conversations your morals be corrupted, your hope overthrown, your patience unnerved, and you turn aside into crooked ways. Rather, being gentle and meek, keep the right ways, which the Lord teaches you; of which the Psalm says, *He will direct the gentle in judgment, he will teach the meek his ways*[^7]. For patience in the labors of this life, without which the hope of the future life cannot be kept, no one can perpetually retain except the gentle and meek; who do not resist the will of God, whose yoke is easy and burden light[^8], but who believe God, and hope in him, and love him. For thus, being gentle and meek, you will not only love his consolations, but will also bear his scourges like good sons; so that, since you hope for what you do not see, you may wait for it with patience. So act, so walk. For you walk in Christ, who said, *I am the way*[^9]. Learn how to walk in him, not only by his word, but also by his example. For the Father did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all[^10]; surely not unwilling, nor refusing, but equally willing; because the will of the Father and of the Son is one according to the equality of the form of God, in which, since he was, he did not consider it robbery to be equal to God; and singularly obedient, according to which he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant[^11]. *For he himself loved us, and handed himself over for us, an offering and sacrifice to God for an odor of sweetness*[^12]. So, then, the Father did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all, that the Son himself too might hand himself over for us.

**3. 3.** Handed over, then, was that Most High one, through whom all things were made; handed over, on account of the form of a servant, to the reproach of men and the abjection of the people, to insult, to scourges, to the death of the cross, he taught us by the example of his passion with how great patience we should walk in him; and he strengthened us by the example of his resurrection as to what we ought to hope patiently from him. *For if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience*[^13]. We hope indeed for what we do not see; but we are the body of that head, in whom what we hope for is already accomplished. For of him it was said that he is *the head of the body, the Church, the firstborn, himself holding the primacy*[^14]. And of us it is written, *But you are the body of Christ and members*[^15]. *But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience*, secure; since he who rose again is our head, he keeps our hope. And because before he rose our head was scourged, he strengthened our patience. For it is written, *For whom the Lord loves he rebukes; and he scourges every son whom he receives*[^16]. Let us therefore not fail under the scourge, that we may rejoice in the resurrection. For so true is it that he scourges every son whom he receives, that he did not spare even his Only-begotten, but handed him over for us all. Looking, then, on him who was scourged without the desert of sin, who died for our offenses and rose again for our justification[^17], let us not fear to be cast away when scourged; but rather let us be confident that we will be received when justified.

**4. 4.** For although the fullness of our joy has not yet come, yet not even now are we left without joy; because in hope we have been saved. Therefore the Apostle himself, who says, *If we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience*[^18], says in another place, *Rejoicing in hope, patient in tribulation*[^19]. *Having, then, such a hope, let us use much boldness*[^20]; and let our speech be seasoned with salt, that we may know how we ought to answer each one[^21]. For it must be said to those who, having lost—or never having taken up—endurance, dare even to insult us as we wait for the Lord (because, hoping for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience), when they ought rather to imitate us: Where are your delights, for which you walk in crooked ways? We do not say: Where will they be, when this life has passed away; but now: Where are they? Since today has taken away yesterday, and tomorrow will take away today, what of the things you love does not run by and fly away? What does not flee almost before it is grasped, since of this very day not even an hour can be retained? For the second is shut out by the third, just as the first was shut out by the second. Of that one hour itself, which seems present, nothing is present: for all its parts and all its moments are fleeting.

**5. 5.** Why does a man sin, if he is not blinded when he sins—or if only he would take heed when he sins? He could see that pleasure, soon to pass away, is desired without prudence; or, when it has passed, is thought on with repentance. You mock us because we hope for eternal things which we do not see; while you, subject to the temporal things that are seen, do not know what kind of day will dawn for you tomorrow—which, often hoping it good, you find evil; nor, if it is good, will you be able to hold it lest it flee. You mock us because we hope for eternal things; which, when they have come, will not pass away; because they themselves do not come, but always remain; but we shall come to them, when, by the Lord's way, we have passed through these things that pass away. But from you temporal things never cease at some time to be hoped for, and yet, hoped for, they often deceive you; nor do they cease to inflame you as they come, to corrupt as they arrive, to torment as they pass away. Are they not the very things that, when coveted, burn; when obtained, become worthless; when lost, vanish away? We too use them according to the necessity of our pilgrimage; but we do not fix our joys in them, lest, as they slip away, we be undermined. For we use this world as though not using it[^22], that we may come to the one who made this world, and remain in him, fully enjoying his eternity.

**6. 6.** But what is it that you say: Who came here from there, and who has shown men what goes on in the underworld? And here too he closed your mouth who raised a dead man on the fourth day[^23], and, no longer to die, rose on the third day, and before he died told what kind of life receives the dying—as one from whom nothing was hidden—both in the poor man at rest and in the rich man burning[^24]. But those do not believe these things who say: Who has returned here from there? They wish to seem about to believe, if one of their parents should come back to life. But cursed is everyone who places his hope in man[^25]. Therefore God, made man, willed to die and rise again; that both what was to come for man might be shown in man's flesh, and yet it might be believed of God, not of man. And surely the Church of the faithful, spread throughout the whole world, is now before their eyes. Let them read that it was promised, so many ages ago, to one man, who against hope believed in hope, that he might become the father of many nations[^26]. What, then, was promised to Abraham alone, believing, we now see fulfilled; and do we despair that what is promised to the whole believing world will come? Let them now go and say, *Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we shall die*[^27]. They say they will still die tomorrow, but truth, while they say these things, finds them already dead. But you, brothers, sons of the resurrection, citizens of the holy angels, heirs of God and co-heirs of Christ, beware of imitating those who tomorrow die by breathing their last, and today are buried by drinking. But, as the same Apostle says, lest evil conversations corrupt your good morals, *be soberly righteous, and do not sin*[^28]; walking the narrow but sure way that leads to the breadth of the heavenly Jerusalem, which is our eternal mother; most firmly hope for what you do not see, patiently wait for what you do not yet have; because you most faithfully hold Christ, the truthful one who promises.
