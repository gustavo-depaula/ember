# Sermon 324: In Which Augustine Completes the Part of the Sermon Just Preceding, Interrupted by the Miracle

**1.** The sermon of yesterday, which was interrupted by a greater joy, ought to be completed by us. For I had set out, and had begun to speak to Your Charity, why these brothers seem to me to have been directed by divine authority to this city, that here in them the long-desired and long-awaited healing might be fulfilled. And wanting to say this, I had first begun to commend to Your Charity the holy places in which they were not healed, and from which they were directed to us. And I spoke of Ancona, a city of Italy: I had begun to speak of Uzalis, a city which is in Africa (it has as bishop my brother, whom you know, Evodius); because the fame of the same martyr and of his works had driven them to come to that city too. There was not given what could have been given, that here it might be given where it ought to have been given. But when I wished briefly to recall the divine works through the holy Martyr, the others being omitted, I had set out to tell one: which, as I tell it, the health of that girl restored, a sudden tumult of gladness arose, and compelled us to end the sermon otherwise. So we know that such a miracle was done there among many others, which surely cannot all be recalled. A certain woman lost in her lap a sick catechumen son, an infant at the breast. When she had seen him lost and irreparably perished, she began to weep for him more faithfully than as a mother. For she did not desire her son's life except in the world to come, and she lamented that this had been taken from her and had perished. Filled with the affection of confidence, she took him dead, and ran to the memorial of the blessed martyr Stephen, and began to demand her son from him, and to say: Holy martyr, you see no consolation has remained to me. For I cannot say that my son has gone before me, whom you know has perished: for you see why I lament. Give back my son, that I may have him before the sight of your crowner. When she prayed these and such things, with tears in a way not asking, but, as I said, demanding, her son came back to life. And because she had said, You know why I seek him: God too wanted to show her true mind. At once she took him to the presbyters, he was baptized, he was sanctified, he was anointed, hands were laid on him, all the sacraments completed, he was taken up. But she led him away with such a countenance, as though she were leading him not to the rest of the tomb, but to the bosom of the martyr Stephen. The faithful heart of the woman was proved. Where, then, God did such a miracle through his Martyr, could he not heal these men there? And yet they were directed here to us. Turned to the Lord, etc.
