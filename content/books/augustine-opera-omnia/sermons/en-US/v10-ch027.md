# Sermon 353: On the Day of the Octave of the Infants: Whom He Exhorts in the Words of the Apostle Peter, "Laying Aside, Then, All Malice," and the Rest

**1. 1.** The sermon of an anxious shepherd addresses the ears and minds of all whom our care embraces: yet it is now directed properly to you, whose recent infancy is marked by the cradles of the Sacraments of spiritual generation. For to you especially the divine eloquence speaks coaxingly through the apostle Peter thus: *Laying aside, then*, he says, *all malice and all guile and flattery and envy and detraction, like newborn infants desire the rational and innocent milk; that in it you may grow unto salvation, if you have tasted that the Lord is sweet*[^1]. Accordingly, because you have tasted, we are witnesses: we ministered to you this sweetness in the office of nurse. Act, therefore, admonished, after the likeness of holy infancy, lay aside malice, guile, flattery and envy and detraction. This innocence you ought so to hold that by growing you do not lose it. What is malice, except the love of harming? What is guile, except to do one thing and feign another? What is flattery, except seduction by deceitful praise? What is detraction, except a rebuke more biting than truthful? Malice delights in another's evil: envy is tortured even by another's good: guile doubles the heart: flattery doubles the tongue: detraction wounds the reputation. But the innocence of this your holiness, since it is the daughter of love, *does not rejoice over iniquity, but rejoices together with the truth*[^2]. Simple as a dove, and cunning as a serpent[^3], not from zeal to harm, but to beware of the one harming.

**2. 1.** To this I exhort you. *For of such is the kingdom of heaven*[^4], namely of the humble, that is, of the spiritually little ones. Do not despise it, do not shrink from it. This littleness belongs to the great. But deceitful pride is the greatness of the weak; which, when it has taken possession of the mind, by raising up casts down, by puffing up empties out, by distending scatters. The one who harms cannot be humble, the proud cannot be innocent. I speak of that humility which does not wish to excel in perishing things, but truly thinks of something eternal, to which it may come not by its own strength, but helped. This cannot wish evil to anyone, by which its own good is in no way increased. But further, pride at once gives birth to envy. For who is envious who does not wish evil to him by whose good he is tortured? Therefore envy too consequently gives birth to malice: whence proceeds both guile and flattery and detraction, and every evil work which you would not wish to suffer from another. With pious humility preserved, then, which in the holy Scriptures is proved to be holy infancy, you will be secure about the immortality of the blessed: *For of such is the kingdom of heaven*.

**2. 2.** Further, he who is not proud toward men, much more ought he not to be defiant against God: since, if no one is to do to another what he does not wish to suffer from another, and no man wishes to suffer disobedience from one who has been subjected to his authority; how much more must it be guarded against, lest anyone exist toward God such as he does not wish a man to be toward himself?

**3. 2.** Accordingly they deceive their own souls who think it enough if they do to no man what they do not wish to be done to themselves, and yet so corrupt themselves with a luxurious life that they attempt to do to God what they do not wish to be done to themselves by a man. For they do not wish their house to be perverted by anyone, who in their own selves pervert the house of God by a pitiable blindness, deaf against the Apostle crying out: *Do you not know that you are the temple of God, and the Spirit of God dwells in you? But if anyone corrupts the temple of God, God will corrupt him. For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^5]. Let no one deceive himself. Why, then, do they think they preserve their innocence toward other men, when they so harm themselves that they lack God as inhabitant, and are punished by him as avenger? Hence too it comes about that, fallen and dissolved through harmful pleasures, they not only cease to be temples of God, but even become ruins, in which evil demons dwell, to whom they begin to supplicate, and which they begin to worship: and there are made for them, as it was said, *the last things worse than the first*[^6]. Whence you, regenerated by an immortal seed, just as above, on account of the malicious desires of harming, by which there is done to men what they hate; so afterward, on account of base and unlawful delights of the flesh and wicked sacrileges, by which men seem not to harm men, by not doing to them what they do not wish, but by not obeying God to whom all things are subject, do themselves do to the Lord of lords what they do not wish to be done to themselves by their own little servants—you the same apostle Peter addresses, saying: *Christ, then, having suffered through the flesh, you too arm yourselves with the same thought*[^7].

**4. 2.** *Because he who has died in the flesh has ceased to sin, that he may now live the rest of his time in the flesh, not by the desires of men, but by the will of God. For the past time is enough to have consummated the will of the gentiles in lusts, and pleasures, and carousings, in drinking and the abominable services of idols*[^8]. For the past time is enough to have served the muddy works of sins, as it were the domination of the Egyptians. Now the Red Sea, namely Baptism consecrated by the blood of Christ, has cast down the true Pharaoh, has destroyed the Egyptians[^9]: fear nothing of your past sins, as of enemies pursuing from behind. For the rest, think to traverse the desert of this life, and to come to the land of promise, the heavenly Jerusalem, the land of the living: lest, by contempt of the word of God, as it were by disgust at the manna, your hearts, as it were your inner mouths, become foolish; lest, craving the Egyptian foods, you murmur about the heavenly nourishments: lest you fornicate, as some of them fornicated; and lest you tempt Christ, as some of them tempted[^10]. If, while you thirst for the faith of the gentiles, some bitterness of those resisting meets you, as of those waters which Israel could not drink[^11]; let it grow sweet by imitating the patience of the Lord, as it were by the wood of the cross cast in. If the serpent's temptation bites; by beholding that lifting up of the serpent[^12], as of death conquered and triumphed over in the flesh of the Lord, let it be healed by the same medicine of the cross. If the adversary Amalekite tries to block and hinder your journey, let him be overcome by the most persevering stretching out of the arms, the sign of the same cross[^13]. Be true and genuine Christians: do not imitate those who are Christians in name, empty in deed. Again I say, and it must often be said: *The past time is enough to have consummated the will of the gentiles.* Detest and turn away from dogs returned to their own vomit: detest and turn away from the cleaned and empty house, into which seven other worse spirits are brought, so that the last things of a man are worse than the first were[^14]. Hold your cleanser as your inhabitant. *For commanding, we ask*, that you not receive the grace of God in vain. *For the past time is enough to have consummated the will of the gentiles*[^15]. Hear also the apostle Paul: *I speak humanly on account of the weakness of your flesh. For as you presented your members to serve uncleanness and iniquity unto injustice: so now present your members to serve justice unto sanctification*[^16].
