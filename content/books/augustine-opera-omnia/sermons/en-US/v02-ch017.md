# Sermon 30

**1** Without doubt, brothers, a certain heavy burden and a heavy yoke of iniquity he desired to avoid, who said to God: *Direct my steps according to your word, and let not any iniquity have dominion over me*[^1]. Let us see, then, when iniquity has dominion over a man, that we may understand whom we have heard praying, and what we ourselves too have prayed in responding. For all of us, as I judge, with a devout and true heart responded to the holy psalm, praying and saying to the Lord our God: *Direct my steps according to your word, and let not any iniquity have dominion over me*. From the dominion of this most wicked mistress we have been redeemed by precious blood. And what did it profit to have received a law that commands and threatens but does not help, so that under it we were guilty before the grace of God? In vain the law threatens, when iniquity has dominion. For the law is not corporeal, not carnal. But since God, who gave the law, is spirit, without doubt the law is spiritual. But what does the Apostle say? *For we know that the law is spiritual, but I am carnal, sold under sin*[^2]. Do not marvel, then, O you sold under sin, if the one to whom you have been sold has dominion over you. Hear the apostle John: *Sin is iniquity*[^3]. Against such a mistress, then, we call upon the Lord, when we say: *Let not any iniquity have dominion over me*.

**2** The one who was sold cries out; let the redeemer hear. Man himself sold himself, through free choice, under the dominion of iniquity, and received as his price a slight pleasure from the forbidden tree. He himself, then, cries out: *Direct my paths*, which I have made crooked; *direct my steps*, which I myself by my own choice have depraved. *Direct them according to your word*. What is *direct them according to your word*? That my steps may be straight, because your word is straight. "I," he says, "have been made crooked under the weight of iniquity, but your word is the rule of truth. Therefore correct me, who am crooked, as by a rule—that is, by the straight word. *Direct* then *my steps according to your word, and let not any iniquity have dominion over me*. I have sold myself, redeem me. I have sold myself by my own choice, redeem me with your blood." In the seller let pride blush, in the redeemer let grace glory. For *God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble*[^4].

**3** *For the law is spiritual, but I am carnal, sold under sin. For what I work I do not know; for I do not do what I want*[^5]. *I do not do what I want*, the carnal man says. He accuses not the law but himself. For the spiritual law has no fault. The carnal man, sold, falls into fault. He does not do what he wants. When he wants to, he cannot, because when he was able, he was unwilling. By willing evil, he lost the ability to do good. And now the captive speaks, and the captive says: *I do not do what I want. For I do not do the good I want, but the evil I hate, this I do*[^6]. *Not what I want*. And against this a man says: "You do indeed want." *I do not do what I want*. "You do altogether what you want." *I do not do what I want*. Believe me, my brother, *I do not do what I want*. "Oh, if you wanted, you would do it; because you do not want, you do not do the good." *I do not do what I want*. Believe me, my brother, I know what is done in me: *I do not do what I want*. You who contradict grace are no judge of my conscience. I know myself, that I do not do what I want, and you say, "You do what you want"? *No one knows what is done in a man, except the spirit of the man that is in him*[^7].

**4** And you are a man. If you will not believe me, attend to yourself. Do you so live in this corruptible body, which weighs down the soul, that the flesh does not lust against your spirit, and the spirit against the flesh? Is that quarrel not in you? Is there no concupiscence of the flesh in you that resists the law of the mind? If nothing in you resists the other, see where the whole of it is. If your spirit does not dissent from the flesh that lusts against it, see whether perhaps your mind wholly consents to the flesh; see whether perhaps there is no war for this reason, that the peace is perverse. Perhaps you wholly consent to the flesh, and there is no quarrel. What hope have you that you can ever conquer, you who have not yet begun to fight? But if you delight in the law of God according to the inner man, but see another law in your members fighting against the law of your mind—if in this you delight, by this you are bound, free in mind, a servant in flesh—if it is now so, have compassion rather on the man who says, *I do not do what I want*[^8]. For do you not want that concupiscence which resists your mind to be wholly absent from you? You are a man of evil wish, if you do not want to be rid of such an adversary. I confess to you that whatever in me rebels against my mind and quarrels with me with a contrary delight—whatever of the sort is in me—I want to kill it utterly. And if perhaps, with the Lord's help, I do not consent to it, I do not want to have one to quarrel with. It is far more desirable to me not to have an enemy than to conquer one. For not even that very thing, that the flesh lusts against the spirit—is it not mine? Or am I really compounded of a contrary nature? Both that is mine, and that I do not consent to it is mine. The free part somewhat resists the remnants of slavery. I want the whole to be healthy, because I am the whole. I do not want my flesh to be separated from me forever, as though foreign, but to be wholly healed with me. If you do not want this, I do not know what you think of the flesh. I judge you to think it comes from I-know-not-where, as though from a contrary race. That is false, it is heretical, it is blasphemous: of mind and of flesh there is one craftsman. He himself, when he created man, made both, joined both. He made the flesh subject to the soul, the soul to himself. If this latter always stood under its Lord, the former too would always obey its mistress. Do not marvel, then, if the soul which deserted the higher suffers punishment through the lower. *For the flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh. For these*, he says, *are opposed to one another, so that you do not do the things you want*[^9]. Hence this man too: *I do not do what I want*[^10]. For the flesh lusts against the spirit, and I do not want it to lust. I count it a great thing if I do not consent; yet I long to be rid of it. Therefore, *I do not do what I want*. For I want the flesh not to lust against the spirit, and I cannot. This is what I said: *I do not do what I want*.

**5** Why do you bring a false charge against me? I say, *I do not do what I want*, and you say, "You do what you want." Why do you bring a false charge against me? Ungrateful to the physician, why do you bring a false charge against the sick man? Allow me to ask the physician. *Redeem me from the false charges of men, and I will keep your commandments*[^11]. I will keep them through your redemption, not through my own power. Therefore I do not claim for myself the health I do not yet have, because I ask the physician. But you, defender of nature—would that you were such, that you did not apply a false defense as to a healthy nature, but asked the physician for one not yet healthy—but now you, defender of nature, or rather assailant, while you praise the Creator as though for a healthy nature, shut out the Savior from the sick. He who created heals the one falling through itself, raises it up through himself. This is faith itself, this is truth, this is the foundation of the Christian faith. One and one. One man through whom ruin, another man through whom the structure. Through that one ruin, through this one the structure. He fell who did not abide; he raises up who did not fall. He fell because he forsook the one who abides; the one who abides came down to the one lying prostrate.

**6** If, then, the flesh lusts against the spirit, so that in this very thing you do not do what you want—because you want it not to lust and you cannot—at least hold on, by the grace of the Lord, to the will, and persevere in his help. Say to him what you sing: *Direct my steps according to your word, and let not any iniquity have dominion over me*[^12]. What is *Let not any iniquity have dominion over me*? Hear the Apostle: *Let not sin reign in your mortal body*[^13]. What is *reign*? So as to obey its desires. He did not say, "Do not have evil desires." For how in this mortal flesh, where the flesh lusts against the spirit and the spirit against the flesh, shall I not have evil desires? Do this, then: *Let not sin reign in your mortal body, so as to obey its desires*. And if there are desires, let them not be obeyed, lest iniquity have dominion. *Nor present your members as weapons of iniquity to sin*[^14]. Let not your members become weapons of iniquity, and no iniquity will have dominion over you. But this too, that your members not become weapons of iniquity—do you furnish it to yourself by your own powers? This very thing, I say, this very thing, that your members do not become weapons of iniquity—do you furnish it to yourself by your own powers? For when your members do not become weapons of iniquity, there is indeed iniquity in your members, in illicit desires, but it does not reign. How does it reign, when it has no weapons? Your part, your flesh, the concupiscence of your flesh, rebels against you in weakness. This weakness is a tyrant. If you want to be the conqueror of the tyrant, call upon Christ as your commander.

**7** For I know what you were going to say to me, or what you now say within yourself. Whoever you are who are such here and hear me, I know what iniquity says to you within. For you are still under the yoke of iniquity, when you do not acknowledge the price of the Redeemer. I know what you say to yourself: "See, my flesh lusts against my spirit. It lusts after adultery, but I do not consent, I do not assent, I do not decide for it. Not only do I not do it, but I do not even consent to do it. Not only do I not perpetrate it outwardly through the flesh, but I do not even follow the rebelling flesh in my very mind. Do I consent to what fights me, do I yield to what struggles? I do not. See, no iniquity has dominion over me." Is it so? Is it true? Give thanks, if it is so, to the one who granted you that it should be so. Do not claim this for yourself, lest you lose what you received and begin to ask in vain. Do you not fear: *God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble*[^15]?

**8** Do you, then, furnish to yourself that iniquity not have dominion over you? If this presumption of yours is true, our prayer is vain, in which we said to God: *Let not any iniquity have dominion over me*[^16]. Did you sing these words today, or not? Were you here when we all said: *Direct my steps according to your word, and let not any iniquity have dominion over me*? You were here, you sang these words, I think you will not deny it. Therefore you sang among the people of God and asked God, saying: *Direct my paths according to your word, let not any iniquity have dominion over me*. If you furnished this to yourself, why did you ask along with me? I hold you praying, I hold you calling upon him, I convict you laboring. Let us, then, hear together the one who says: *Come to me, all you who labor*[^17]. Let us hear and come. What is "let us come"? Let us advance by believing, let us draw near by giving thanks, let us arrive by persevering. Let us come to the one who says: *Come to me, all you who labor*. You labor, and I labor. Let us hear him, let us come to him. Why do we quarrel between ourselves? Let us both hear, because we both labor. Why do we quarrel between ourselves? Is it that we may not hear the physician who calls? O unhappy weakness! The physician calls to himself, and the sick man is occupied with quarrels. See what he says in calling: *Come to me, all you who labor*. Where do you labor, except under the burdens of sins, except under the yoke of the evil mistress iniquity? *Come*, then, *to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you*[^18]. I who made you, I will refresh you. I, he says, will refresh you, because *without me you can do nothing*[^19].

**9** How will I refresh you? *Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me*[^20]. What do we learn from you? We know you, Lord, in the beginning the Word, the Word God, the Word with God: we know all things made through you, both what we see and what we do not see. What do we learn from you? For we, your disciples, are not, like the disciples of a craftsman and builder, going to found another world. You founded one world, you made heaven and earth, you adorned both with their creatures and ornaments. What do we learn from you? *Learn*, he says, *from me*. When I was in the beginning God with God, I created you. This I do not want you to learn from me. But I became what I made, lest the one I made should perish. Whence did I become what I made? *He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man, he humbled himself*[^21]. This learn from me. *For he humbled himself*, he says. *Learn from me, for I am gentle and humble of heart*[^22]. For I do not teach you this, he says, as though you had ever been in the form of God, not counting it robbery to be equal to God. To that one alone was it proper; to him it was no robbery, to whom it was nature. In the equality of the Father he was born of the Father. Yet what did he do for your sake? *He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in habit as a man*[^23]. See, for your sake God was made man, and will you not acknowledge yourself, when you are a man? See, for your sake he was made a man without sin, and will you not acknowledge yourself, when you are with sin? That you may come to the one who says: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you; take my yoke upon you*[^24].

**10** Have you taken up this yoke? Have you? Have you felt that you have a rider? Have you taken up this yoke? You say, "I have taken it up." Do you feel that you have a rider? Do you feel that you have a guide? "I feel it," you say. Then say to him: *Direct my steps according to your word*[^25]. He guides you under his yoke and under his burden. For that his burden may be light to you and his yoke sweet, he has breathed love into you. To the one who loves it is sweet; to the one who does not love it is hard. To the one who loves it is sweet: *The Lord gave sweetness*[^26]. Or perhaps, because you came hearing *Come to me*, do you intend to claim this very thing for yourself, that you came? "See," you say, "I came to him by my own choice, by my own will. Because I came, he refreshes me. Because I came, he lays his sweet yoke upon me. Because he gives love, he lays his burden light upon me, loving and cherishing it. All these things he did in me, but because I came to him." So then you suppose that, because you came, you furnished this to yourself? For *what do you have that you did not receive?*[^27] How did you come? By believing you came, but you have not yet arrived. We are still on the way. We came, but have not yet arrived. *Serve the Lord in fear, and exult before him with trembling, lest at any time the Lord be angry, and you perish from the just way*[^28]. Fear lest, when you claim for yourself that the just way was found by you, by that very arrogance you perish from the just way. "I," he says, "came; by my own choice I came, by my own will I came." Why do you swell up? Why do you puff up? Do you want to know that this too was furnished to you? Hear him calling: *No one comes to me unless the Father who sent me draws him*[^29]. Let us turn to the Lord.
