# Sermon 29/A

**1** *Confess to the Lord, because he is good, because his mercy is forever*[^1]. What the Holy Spirit exhorted us in the words of the psalm, to which with one mouth and one heart we answered *alleluia*, which in Latin is rendered *praise the Lord*, this the same Holy Spirit exhorts you also through our voice: *Confess to the Lord, because he is good, because his mercy is forever*. Whether you sing his gifts in praise, or pour out your sins in groaning, *confess to the Lord, because he is good, because his mercy is forever*. For not only the recollection of our sins, but also the praise of our Lord, is called confession, because, even if we do one of these, we do not do it without the other. For we both accuse our iniquity with hope of his mercy, and praise his mercy with the recollection of our iniquity. Let us confess, then, *to the Lord, because he is good, because his mercy is forever*. To some, some creature seems evil, because it offends the senses of the unskilled. But it is false. For God made all things very good, *because he is good*. To some, God seems unjust, because often his faithful suffer many hard and harsh things in this temporal life. But those to whom this seems are deceived. For *he scourges every son*, not whom he rejects, but *whom he receives*[^2], *because his mercy is forever*.

**2** Let us confess, then, *to the Lord, because he is good, because his mercy is forever*[^3]. Let us say to the Lord our God: Wonderful are your works, *in wisdom you have made them all*[^4]. Just are your judgments, *for iniquity you have instructed man. Before I was humbled, I sinned*[^5]. Let us say these things in confession, because, if some things are adverse to the punishment of our mortality, yet he does good, *because he is good*. And if we are amended by temporal pains and labors, *he will not be angry to the end, nor be indignant forever*[^6], *because his mercy is forever*. What is so good as our God? Men blaspheme, and not only are not humbled, but even take pride in their crimes. And he *makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^7]. What is so merciful as our God? Men persevere in their disgraceful and criminal deeds, and he does not cease to call them to conversion. What is so good as our God, from whom even in distress we receive such great consolations? What is so merciful as our God, even whose future sentence we, being changed, change? Let us confess *to the Lord, because he is good, because his mercy is forever*. Not the praises of all things are confessions, but the praises of the Lord our God. For if it was most truly said, *How good is the God of Israel to the upright in heart*[^8], surely he seems evil to the perverse in heart. But what man becomes upright except from being perverse, so that, as though convicted, he begins to praise what before he reproached, and to marvel at what before he despised, confessing to the Lord, that to him now upright the one is good who seemed evil to him when perverse? And since by his own malice he was perverse, but by God's grace he has been corrected, he ought at the same time to confess that *his mercy is forever*. We evil, he good; we good from him, evil from ourselves; he good to us when good, he good to us when evil. We raging against ourselves, he merciful toward us. He calls that we may be converted; he waits until we are converted; he forgives if we are converted; he crowns if we do not turn away.

**3** Let us confess *to the Lord, because he is good, because his mercy is forever*[^9]. The confession even of sins has always seemed something to be feared by men, but before a human judge. For very often it is by scourges and various beatings, by claws and fires too, that a confession is dragged out of the mouth. And sometimes the limbs yield to the torments before the mind yields to the pains, so that the frame of the body is dissolved before the secret of the crime is brought to light. The executioners press on, all kinds of torments multiply. But in vain are the inner parts opened by lacerating, when the conscience is shut by denying. But why does a man, amid such torments, fear to confess, except that those who confess are usually punished? He who confesses to a man is punished; he who confesses to God is freed. And no wonder. A man asks of a man what the one who asks does not know. But God, who warns us to confess, knows what we did not want to confess, nor does he then learn it when we have confessed. How much more, then, does he free those who confess from eternal death, who spared us from temporal death for our iniquities, which he knew even before we confessed them.

**4** You say perhaps: "What does God ask of me, that I should confess what he already knows? For a man, when he asks this of a man, does not know it." What do you think, except that God wills your sin, by your acknowledging it, to be punished, and by his forgiving it, to be loosed? For how do you long for him to forgive what you do not want to acknowledge? Hear the psalm, and if you are awake, recognize there your own voice: *My sin*, it says, *I made known, and my crime I did not cover. I said, I will declare against myself my offense to the Lord, and you forgave the impiety of my heart*[^10]. Hear in another psalm: *For I acknowledge my iniquity, and my offense is ever before me*[^11]. And so not shamelessly did he say to God: *Turn your face away from my sins*[^12]. For then God deigns to turn his face away from a man's sin, when the man himself takes care to turn his face toward his own sin, so that in the ears of God he may say: *And my sin is ever before me*. Nor is God said to turn his face away because he does not know it, but because he forgives it. If, then, you fear, O man, to confess to a human judge, because he is evil, or because he is compelled to fulfill the severity of the law, confess securely to the Lord, *because he is good, because his mercy is forever*.
