# Sermon 57: Again on the Lord's Prayer in Matthew 6, to the Candidates for Baptism

**1. 1.** The order of your edification is that you first learn what you believe, and afterward what you ask. For thus the Apostle says: *It shall be: everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord shall be saved*[^1]. The blessed Paul set down this testimony from the prophet: because these times were foretold through the prophet, when all would call upon God: *He who calls upon the name of the Lord shall be saved.* And he added: *But how will they call upon him in whom they have not believed? Or how will they believe him whom they have not heard? But how will they hear without a preacher? Or how will they preach, unless they are sent?*[^2] So preachers were sent, they preached Christ. As they preached, the peoples heard; by hearing they believed; by believing they called upon him. Because, then, it was most rightly and most truly said: *How will they call upon him in whom they have not believed?*, therefore you first learned what you should believe: today you have learned to call upon him in whom you have believed.

**2. 2.** The Son of God, our Lord Jesus Christ, taught us a prayer: and although he himself is the Lord, as you received and recited in the Creed, the only Son of God, yet he was unwilling to be alone. He is the only one, and was unwilling to be alone: he deigned to have brothers. For to whom does he say: *Say: Our Father, who are in heaven?*[^3] Whom did he wish us to call our Father, except his Father? Did he then envy us? Parents, when they have begotten one son, two, three, now fear to beget, lest they make the others beg. But because such is the inheritance which he promises us, that many obtain it and no one suffers narrowness; therefore he called the peoples of the nations into his brotherhood, and the Only one has innumerable brothers who say: *Our Father, who are in heaven.* Those who were before us said these things: those who will be after us will say them. See how many brothers the Only one has in his grace, sharing with them the inheritance, for whom he endured death. We had a father and mother on earth, that we might be born unto labors and death: we have found other parents, God the Father and the Church the mother, by whom we may be born unto eternal life. Let us consider, dearly beloved, whose sons we have begun to be: and let us so live as befits those who have such a Father. See, our Creator has deigned to be our Father.

**3. 3.** We have heard whom we ought to call upon, in what hope of an eternal inheritance we have begun to have a Father in heaven: let us hear what we ask of him. Of such a Father, what are we going to ask? Do we not ask of him, both today and yesterday and the day before, for rain? It is nothing great that we have asked of such a Father: and yet you see with what groaning, with what desire, we ask for rain, when death is feared, and that is feared which no one can escape. For every man, sooner or later, must die: and we groan, ask, are in travail, cry out to God, that we may die a little later. How much more ought we to cry out to him, that we may come where we never die?

**4. 4.** Therefore: *Hallowed be your name*[^4], was said. This too we ask of him, that his name be hallowed in us: for it is always holy. But how is his name hallowed in us, except while he makes us holy? For we were not holy, and through his name we are made holy: but he himself is always holy, and his name is always holy. We ask for ourselves, not for God. For we do not wish well for God, to whom no evil can ever come. But we wish good for ourselves, that his holy name be hallowed: that what is always holy may be hallowed in us.

**5. 5.** *Your kingdom come*[^5]. Whether we ask it or not, it is going to come. God indeed has an everlasting kingdom. For when did he not reign? When did he begin to reign? Whereas his kingdom has no beginning, neither will it have an end. But that you may know that this too we pray for ourselves, not for God (for we do not say: *Your kingdom come*, as if wishing that God may reign); we ourselves will be his kingdom, if, believing in him, we advance in him. All the faithful, redeemed by the blood of his Only one, will be his kingdom. But that kingdom is going to come when the resurrection of the dead has taken place: for then he himself will come. And when the dead have risen, he will divide them, as he himself says, and will set some on the right, others on the left. He will say to those who will be on the right: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*[^6]. This is what we wish and ask, when we say: *Your kingdom come*, that it may come to us. For if we are reprobate, that kingdom will come to others, not to us. But if we are in that number who belong to the members of his only-begotten Son, his kingdom will come to us: and it will not delay. For do as many ages remain as have passed? The apostle John said: *Little children, it is the last hour*[^7]. But for that great day the hour is long: and see how many years that last hour itself draws out. Yet let it be for you as one who keeps watch, sleeps, rises, and reigns. Now let us watch, in death we shall sleep, in the end we shall rise, without end we shall reign.

**6. 6.** *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth*[^8]. Thirdly we ask: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth.* And this too we wish well for ourselves. For the will of God must necessarily be done. The will of God is that the good reign, the evil be condemned. Can this will not be done? But what good do we wish for ourselves, when we say: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth?* Hear. For this petition can be understood in many ways, and many things must be pondered in this petition; when we ask God: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth.* As your angels do not offend you, so may we too not offend you. Again, how is it understood: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth?* All the holy Patriarchs, all the Prophets, all the Apostles, all the spiritual are, as it were, heaven to God: but we, in comparison with them, are earth. *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth*: as in them, so also in us. Again: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth.* The Church of God is heaven, its enemies are earth. We wish well for our enemies, that they too may believe and become Christians: and may the will of God be done, as in heaven, so also on earth. Again: *Your will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth.* Our spirit is heaven, the flesh is earth: as our spirit is renewed by believing, so may the flesh be renewed by rising again: and may the will of God be done, as in heaven, so also on earth. Again, our mind, by which we see the truth and delight in the truth itself, is heaven. Behold heaven: *I delight in the law of God according to the inner man*[^9]. What is earth? *I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind*[^10]. When this fight has passed, and a full concord of flesh and spirit has been made, the will of God will be done, as in heaven, so also on earth. When we say this petition, let us think of all these things, and ask all these of the Father. But all these things, dearly beloved, the three we have spoken of, these three petitions pertain to eternal life. For that the name of our God is hallowed in us will be eternal. That his kingdom will come, where we shall live forever, will be eternal. That his will is done, as in heaven, so also on earth, in all the ways I have explained, will be eternal.

**7. 7.** There remain the petitions for this life of our pilgrimage: therefore there follows: *Give us this day our daily bread*[^11]. Give eternal things, give temporal things. You promised a kingdom, do not deny support. You will give with you everlasting adornment, give on earth temporal nourishment. Therefore *daily*, therefore *this day*, that is, in this time. When this life has passed, will we ask for daily bread? For then it will not be called *daily*, but *today*. Now it is called *daily*, when a day passes and another day comes. Will it be called *daily*, when there will be one eternal day? Surely this petition about daily bread must be understood in two ways: either for the necessity of carnal sustenance, or also for the necessity of spiritual nourishment. The necessity of carnal food, on account of daily sustenance, without which we cannot live. There is sustenance and clothing, but the whole is understood from a part. When we ask for bread, we receive all things there. The faithful know also the spiritual nourishment, which you too are going to know, when you receive it from the altar of God. This too will be a daily bread, necessary for this life. For are we going to receive the Eucharist, when we have come to Christ himself, and have begun to reign with him forever? Therefore the Eucharist is our daily bread: but let us so receive it that we are refreshed not only in belly but also in mind. For the very power that is understood there is unity, that, brought back into his body, made his members, we may be what we receive. Then it will truly be our daily bread. And what I treat for you is daily bread: and the readings you hear daily in church are daily bread: and the hymns you hear and say are daily bread. For these things are necessary for our pilgrimage. Are we, when we have arrived there, going to hear a book read? We shall see the Word himself, hear the Word himself, eat him, drink him, as the angels do now. Do the angels need books, or disputants, or readers? Far be it. By seeing they read: for they see the Truth itself, and are satisfied from that fountain whence we are bedewed. It has been said, then, of daily bread; because in this life this petition is necessary for us.

**8. 8.** *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^12]. Is it necessary anywhere but here? For there we shall have no debts. For what are debts, except sins? Behold, you will be baptized, all your sins there will be blotted out: none at all will remain there. If at any time you did, committed, said, desired, or thought anything evil, all will be blotted out. And yet, if there were security in this latter life, we would not learn such a prayer, where we would say: *Forgive us our debts.* But let us plainly do what follows: *As we too forgive our debtors.* Most of all, then, you who are about to enter to receive full pardon of your debts, see that you hold nothing in your hearts against another, that you may go out from there secure, as it were free and absolved from all debts; and do not begin to wish to avenge yourselves on your enemies who did you injuries before. Forgive, as it is forgiven you. God did injury to no one, and yet he forgives who owes nothing. How ought he to forgive to whom it is forgiven, when he forgives all things who does not owe what should be forgiven him?

**9. 9.** *Lead us not into temptation; but deliver us from evil*[^13]. Will this too be necessary in that life? It is not said: *Lead us not into temptation*, except where there can be temptation. In the book of holy Job we read: *Is not the life of man upon earth a temptation?*[^14] What, then, do we ask? Hear what. The apostle James says: *Let no one, when he is tempted, say that he is tempted by God*[^15]. He spoke of that evil temptation by which one is deceived and subjugated to the devil; that he called temptation. For there is another temptation, which is called proving: of this temptation it is written: *The Lord your God tempts you, that he may know whether you love him*[^16]. What is *that he may know?* That he may make you know: for he himself knows. In that temptation by which one is deceived and seduced, God tempts no one: but plainly, by his deep and hidden judgment, he deserts certain ones. When he has deserted them, the tempter finds what to do. For he does not find a wrestler against himself, but at once shows himself a possessor, if God deserts. Lest, then, he desert us, therefore we say: *Lead us not into temptation. For each one is tempted*, says the same apostle James, *drawn away and enticed by his own desire: then desire, when it has conceived, brings forth sin; but sin, when it has been completed, begets death*[^17]. What did he teach us? That we fight against our desires. For in holy Baptism you are going to have your sins forgiven: the desires will remain, with which, regenerated, you may fight. For the conflict remains in yourselves. Let no enemy from without be feared: conquer yourself, and the world is conquered. What will the external tempter do to you, whether the devil or the minister of the devil? Whatever man sets forth gain, to seduce you, let him not find avarice in you: what does the one who sets forth gain do? But if avarice is found in you, at the sight of gain you burn, you are caught in the snare of vicious bait. But if he has not found avarice in you, the snare remained stretched out in vain. The tempter sets before you a most beautiful woman: let chastity be present within, and iniquity without is conquered. So that the displayed beauty of another's wife may not catch you, fight within with your own lust. You do not feel your enemy, but you feel your own desire. You do not see the devil, but you see what delights you. Conquer within what you yourself feel. Fight, fight; because he who regenerated you is the judge: he has set forth the wrestling-match, he prepares the crown. But because without doubt you will be conquered, if you do not have him as helper, if he deserts you; therefore you set forth in the Prayer: *Lead us not into temptation.* The anger of the judge has handed over certain ones to their own desires: and the Apostle says that: *God handed them over to the desire of their heart*[^18]. How did he hand them over? Not by compelling, but by deserting.

**10. 10.** *Deliver us from evil*: this can pertain to the same sentence. It is so, that you may understand one sentence: *Lead us not into temptation; but deliver us from evil.* Therefore he added *but*: to show that this whole pertains to one sentence, *lead us not into temptation; but deliver us from evil.* How? Let me set forth each part: *Lead us not into temptation; but deliver us from evil.* By delivering us from evil, he does not lead us into temptation: by not leading us into temptation, he delivers us from evil.

**11. 11.** But it is a great temptation, dearly beloved, a great temptation in this life, when that of ours is tempted by which we merit pardon, if anywhere, tempted, we have fallen. It is a horrendous temptation, when that is taken from us whereby we could be healed of the wounds of other temptations. I know you have not yet understood: be present in mind, that you may understand. Suppose avarice tempts, and someone is conquered (because even a wrestler and a good fighter is sometimes wounded) in some one temptation: avarice conquered the man, even a good wrestler; and made him in some way avaricious. Lust passed by, it did not lead to defilement, it did not reach adultery. For when that happens, the man must even be prohibited from adultery. But he saw a woman so as to desire her, he thought something more delightful than he ought; he received the fight, even the best fighter was struck: but he did not consent, he beat back the wanton motion, he chastised it with the bitterness of grief, he beat it back and conquered. Yet by the very fact that he had fallen, he has whence to say: *Forgive us our debts.* So with all the other temptations, it is difficult that there not be something whence we may say: *Forgive us our debts.* What, then, is that horrendous temptation which I set forth, troublesome, fearful, to be avoided with all our strength, with all our virtue? What is it? When it is dealt with us, that we avenge ourselves. Anger is exasperated; and a man gnashes to be avenged: a horrendous temptation. For whence you were going to receive pardon for the other offenses, this you lose. If you had sinned in other senses, in other desires, from this it was to be healed, because you were going to say: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors.* He who incites you to avenge yourself will lose for you what you were going to say: *As we too forgive our debtors.* When that is lost, all the rest will be held: nothing at all is forgiven.

**12. 12.** Knowing this dangerous temptation in this life, our Lord, Master, and Savior, when he taught us in this Prayer six or seven petitions, took up none for himself on which to discourse, and which to commend to us more vehemently, except this one. Did we not say: *Our Father, who are in heaven*, and what follows? Why, after the Prayer was finished, did he not discourse to us on something from it—either what he set at the head, or what he concluded at the end, or what he placed in the middle? For if the name of God is not hallowed in you, or if you do not belong to the kingdom of God, or if the will of God is not done in you as in heaven, or if God does not guard you lest you enter into temptation: why none of these? But what? *Amen I say to you, that if you forgive men their sins*[^19]: on account of that: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors.* Passing over all the petitions which he taught us, he taught this one most of all. Those things did not need to be much commended, in which, if one is a sinner, he may recognize whence he is healed: that must be commended, in which, if you have sinned, there is nothing else whence you may be healed. For this you ought to say: *Forgive us our debts.* What debts? They are not lacking: we are men. I have spoken a little more than I ought, I have said something I ought not, I have laughed more than I ought, I have drunk more than I ought, I have eaten more than I ought, I have heard willingly what I ought not, I have seen willingly what I ought not, I have thought willingly what I ought not: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors.* You have perished, if you have lost this.

**13. 13.** See, my brothers; see, my children; see, sons of God; see, because I say to you. Fight with your heart, as far as you can. And if you see your anger standing against you, ask God against it: may God make you a conqueror of yourself; may God make you a conqueror, not of your enemy without, but of your soul within. For he will be present and will do it. He wills more that we ask this of him than rain. For you see, dearly beloved, how many petitions the Lord Christ taught us, and scarcely is one found there that sounds of daily bread: that all we think of, we may think of on account of the future life. For why do we fear that he who promised will not furnish it to us, who said: *Seek first the kingdom and justice of God, and all these things will be added to you? For your Father knows that these things are necessary for you, before you ask him. Seek first the kingdom and justice of God, and all these things will be added to you*[^20]. For many have even been tempted by hunger, and found gold, and were not deserted by God. They would perish of hunger, if the inner daily bread deserted their heart. Let us hunger for that most of all. *For blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they shall be filled*[^21]. But he can mercifully behold our weakness, and see us, as it was said: *Remember that we are dust*[^22]. He who made man of dust and gave him life, for this thing he formed gave his Only one to death. How much he loves us, who can explain, who can even worthily conceive?
