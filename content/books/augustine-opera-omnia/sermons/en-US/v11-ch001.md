# Sermon 214/A: (Dolbeau 1)

[**1**] Clearly, even believing belongs to knowing.

[**2**] We do not know everything we believe, but we do know everything we rightly believe.

[**3**] We know by seeing, and we know by believing.

[**4**] For if a fellow citizen speaks with me and, on this point, a man of my own region and province contradicts me, harassing me as though I were ignorant because I have not seen it, I ask him how he knows that Alexandria exists, which he has not seen, and that there are so many cities in which he has never been, which he nonetheless knows to exist so surely that he would even swear to it without hesitation if challenged.

[**5**] See, I put the question back to him: How do you know that Alexandria exists? He will say: I have believed it. I will reply: Whom did you believe? What else will he say but that he believed other people? You, then, believed those who saw Alexandria; I believed those who prophesied about God. If you consider the truth, if you weigh the force of the testimonies, I have believed more reliable witnesses than you. For about Alexandria you believed those who, being there, were able to see it present before them; I believed those who said that the things now being done would come to pass before they existed.
