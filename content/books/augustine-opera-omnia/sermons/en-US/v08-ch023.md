# Sermon 293: On the Nativity of John the Baptist

**1.** Today we celebrate the solemnity of Saint John, whose birth we heard with wonder as the Gospel was read. How great is the judge's glory, if his herald's is so great? What will the one coming as the way be like, if the one preparing the way is like this? The Church observes John's birth as in some way consecrated. No one among the fathers is found whose birth we celebrate solemnly; we celebrate John's, and we also celebrate Christ's. This cannot be without meaning. Even if our explanation falls short of the dignity of so great a matter, it is nevertheless considered more fruitfully and deeply. John is born of a barren old woman; Christ is born of a young virgin. Barrenness gives birth to John; virgin integrity to Christ. At John's birth the parents were not of a suitable age; at Christ's birth there was no marital embrace. The former is announced by an angel's proclamation; the latter is conceived at an angel's announcement. John's future birth is not believed, and his father becomes mute; Christ is believed and conceived through faith. Faith first arrives in the virgin's heart, and fruitfulness in the mother's womb follows. Yet the words are nearly the same: Zechariah's, when the angel announced John, *By what shall I know this? For I am old, and my wife is advanced in her days*; and holy Mary's, when the angel announced her future childbearing, *How will this happen, since I do not know a man?* Nearly the same words. To him it is said: *See, you will be silent and unable to speak until these things happen, because you did not believe my words, which will be fulfilled in their time.* But to her: *The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the Most High's power will overshadow you; therefore, the Holy One born from you will be called God's Son*. He is corrected; she is instructed. To him it is said, 'Because you did not believe'; to her, 'Receive what you asked.' The words are almost the same: *By what shall I know this?* and *How will this happen?* But it was not hidden from the one who heard their words and saw their hearts. The thought lay hidden in each one's words, but it was hidden from human beings, not from the angel—or rather, not from the one speaking through the angel. Finally, John is born when daylight is already diminishing and the night begins to grow; Christ is born when the night decreases and the day increases. As though considering this sign in both their births, John himself says: *He must increase, but I must decrease*. We have proposed questions to investigate and announced matters to discuss. But I have said this beforehand: even if we are insufficient in ability or time to search all the recesses of so great a mystery, the one speaking within you, even when we are absent, will teach you better—the one you think of devoutly, whom you have received in your heart, whose temples you have become.

**2.** John therefore appears as a kind of boundary set between the two Testaments, the Old and the New. For the Lord himself testifies that he is, as I said, in some way a boundary, saying: *The Law and the Prophets until John the Baptist*. Thus he bears the person of antiquity and the proclamation of newness. Because he represents antiquity, he is born of old people; because he represents newness, he is declared a prophet within his mother's womb. For before he was born, at holy Mary's arrival, he exulted in his mother's womb. Already he had been designated there, designated before being born. Whose forerunner he would be is shown before that one was seen by him. These things are divine and exceed the measure of human frailty. Finally, he is born, receives a name, and his father's tongue is released. Relate what happened to an image signifying realities; only do not suppose it did not happen because perhaps you will explain what it signified. Relate what happened to the signification of realities and see a great mystery. Zechariah is silent and loses his voice until John, the Lord's forerunner, is born and releases his voice. What is Zechariah's silence but hidden prophecy, in some way concealed and closed before Christ's preaching? It is opened at his coming; it becomes clear when the one prophesied is about to come. The opening of Zechariah's voice at John's birth is what the tearing of the veil is at Christ's Cross. If John announced himself, he would not open Zechariah's mouth. The tongue is released because the voice is born; for when John was already announcing the Lord, he was asked: *Who are you?* And he answered: *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness*.

**3.** John is the voice, but the Lord *was the Word in the beginning*. John is a voice for a time; Christ is the eternal Word in the beginning. Remove the word: what is a voice? Where there is no meaning, there is empty noise. A voice without a word strikes the ear but does not build up the heart. Yet in the building up of our own heart, let us observe the order of things. If I think of what to say, the word is already in my heart; but wanting to speak to you, I seek how what is already in my heart may also be in yours. Seeking how the word already in my heart may reach you and settle in your heart, I take up a voice, and with that voice I speak to you. The voice's sound carries the word's meaning to you. When the voice's sound has carried the word's meaning to you, the sound itself passes away, but the word the sound conveyed to you is now in your heart and has not departed from mine. Therefore, after the word has been transmitted to you, does not the sound itself seem to say: *He must increase, but I must decrease*? The voice's sound rang out in service and departed, as though saying: *This joy of mine has been fulfilled*. Let us hold on to the word; let us not lose the word conceived in our inmost being. Do you want to see the voice passing away and the Word's divinity remaining? Where is John's baptism now? It served and departed. Christ's baptism is now celebrated everywhere. We all believe in Christ and hope for salvation in Christ: this is what the voice proclaimed. For because it is difficult to distinguish word from voice, John himself was thought to be the Christ. The voice was thought to be the Word, but the voice recognized itself so as not to offend the Word. *I am not,* he says, *the Christ, nor Elijah, nor the prophet.* The reply was: *Who are you, then? I am,* he says, *the voice of one crying in the wilderness: Prepare the Lord's way*. *The voice of one crying in the wilderness:* the voice of one breaking the silence. *Prepare the Lord's way,* as though saying, 'I sound for this reason: to bring him into your heart. But he does not deign to come where I am to bring him unless you prepare the way.' What does *Prepare the way* mean except, 'Make fitting supplication'? What does *Prepare the way* mean except, 'Think humbly'? Receive the example of humility from him. He is thought to be the Christ; he says he is not what he is thought to be, and does not take another's error for his own pride. If he had said, 'I am the Christ,' how readily he would have been believed, when he was believed before saying it! He did not say it. He recognized himself, distinguished himself, and humbled himself. He saw where his salvation lay; he understood himself to be a lamp and feared being extinguished by the wind of pride.

**4.** For this arrangement pleased God: that a man of such grace should bear witness to Christ that he himself could be thought the Christ. Indeed, *among those born of women,* as Christ himself said, *no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist*. If no human being was greater than this man, the one greater than he is more than a human being. Christ's testimony about himself is great, but to sore and weak eyes the day gives little testimony about itself. Weak eyes are frightened of the day but can bear a lamp. Therefore, the coming day sent a lamp ahead. He sent a lamp ahead into believers' hearts to confound unbelievers' hearts. *I have prepared,* he says, *a lamp for my Christ.* It is God the Father speaking in prophecy: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ:* John as the Savior's herald, the coming judge's forerunner, the future bridegroom's friend. *I have prepared,* he says, *a lamp for my Christ.* Why have you prepared it? *I will clothe his enemies in confusion, but upon him my sanctification will flourish*. How were his enemies clothed in confusion through this lamp? Let us search the Gospel. The Jews, accusing the Lord, said: *By what authority do you do these things? If you are the Christ, tell us openly.* They sought grounds for accusation, not faith; a means of setting a trap, not of being delivered. Indeed, pay attention to what the one who saw their hearts answered, about to confound them through the lamp. *I too,* he says, *will ask you one question: tell me, where does John's baptism come from? From heaven or from human beings?* Immediately struck back and forced to grope, although the day was shining only faintly, they could not gaze upon that brightness and took refuge in their hearts' darkness. There they began to be troubled among themselves, stumbling and falling. *If we say,* they said—this was within themselves, where they thought, but where he saw—*if we say,* they said, *It is from heaven, he will tell us, Why, then, did you not believe him?* For John had borne witness to Christ the Lord. *But if we say, From human beings, the people will stone us,* because John was regarded as a great prophet. And they said: *We do not know.* You do not know: you are in darkness; you lose the light. How much better, if darkness is present in a human heart, to admit the light rather than lose it! When they said, *We do not know,* the Lord said: *Neither do I tell you by what authority I do these things*. For I know with what heart you said, *We do not know:* unwilling to be taught, but afraid to confess.

**5.** This divine ordering—as far as a human being can search it out, a better one better, a lesser one less well—this divine ordering reveals a great sacrament to us. For Christ was going to come in the flesh: not just anyone, not an angel, not an envoy, but *he himself will come and save them*. No ordinary person was going to come; and yet how was he going to come? To be born in mortal flesh, to become a small infant, to be placed in a manger, wrapped in swaddling clothes, nourished with milk, to grow through the stages of life, and finally even to be slain by death. All these things, then, are signs of humility and a pattern of exceedingly great humility. Whose humility is this? The Most High's. How high is he? Do not seek on earth; pass beyond even the stars. When you come to the heavenly armies of angels, you will hear them say, 'Pass beyond us too.' When you come to thrones, dominions, principalities, and powers, you will hear, 'Pass beyond us too; we too were made: *Everything was made through him.*' Pass beyond the whole creation. Whatever has been created or established, whatever is changeable, bodily or incorporeal, pass beyond it all. You cannot yet do so by seeing; pass beyond by believing. Reach the Creator, and meanwhile, with faith going before you and leading you, reach the Creator. There see: *In the beginning was the Word.* For he was not made at some time; rather, he was in the beginning. It is not as with creation, of which it is said: *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*. There is no time when what was in the beginning did not exist. Therefore, the one who *was in the beginning, and the Word was with God, and* that very *Word was God,* and *everything was made through him, and without him nothing was made,* and *in whom what was made is life,* came to us. To whom? To the worthy? Far from it: to the unworthy. For *Christ died for the ungodly* and unworthy, but he was worthy. We were unworthy of his mercy, but he was worthy to show mercy, to whom it might be said: *Because of your mercy, Lord, deliver us.* Not because of our preceding merits, but *because of your mercy, Lord, deliver us, and be merciful to our sins because of your name*, not because of our merit. For it is not because of the merit of sins, but *because of your name.* What sins merit is certainly punishment, not reward. Therefore, *because of your name.* See whom he came to; see how great the one who came is. How did he come to us? Clearly *the Word* *became flesh* to dwell among us. For if he came in his divinity alone, who could bear him, grasp him, or receive him? But he took on what we were so that we might not remain what we were—what we were by nature, not by fault. Because he came to human beings, he came as a human being; but because he came to sinners, he did not come as a sinner. Of these two, human nature and human fault, he took on one and healed the other. For if he himself took on our iniquity as his own, he too would seek a savior. He did take it on to bear and heal it, but not to possess it; and he appeared as a man among people, God concealed.

**6.** Who, then, should bear witness to this day concealed in a kind of cloud of flesh? Give a lamp to testify to the day, but make this lamp so great that whoever surpasses it must be the day: *Among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist*. O ineffable ordering! Brothers, when I think of these things, I marvel greatly at what John says about Christ, with the Gospel as witness: *I am not worthy,* he says, *to untie his sandal strap*. What humbler thing could be said? What is more exalted than Christ? What humbler than the crucified one? *The one who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the bridegroom's friend stands and hears him, and rejoices with joy because of the bridegroom's voice*, not because of his own. *We,* he says, *have all received from his fullness*. What great things he says about Christ! How splendid, how exalted, how worthy—if anything worthy of him can be said by anyone! Yet he does not walk among the Lord's disciples; he did not follow him like Peter, Andrew, John, and the others. Rather, he himself also gathered disciples, and although the Lord was present here with his disciples, John too had disciples. They were called John's disciples. The Lord himself was asked: *Why do John's disciples fast, but your disciples do not?* This was undoubtedly necessary for a faithful forerunner: that Christ be proclaimed by one who might be thought a rival. John had disciples, and Christ had disciples; he taught as though outside, but clung to him as a witness. Therefore, *among those born of women no one has arisen greater than John the Baptist.* There were prophets who had disciples, but not while Christ was present. There were great Apostles afterward, but because they were Christ's disciples, not because they could have disciples alongside Christ. John has disciples, gathers them, and baptizes. What do we think? Is he outside or inside? In reality, certainly inside, to be delivered by God as a human being; in appearance, as though outside, so that he might be believed as a witness. Consider this very point. Peter, Andrew, John, and the others, for example, bore witness to the Lord, but they could be told, 'You praise the one you follow; you proclaim the one to whom you cling.' Let the lamp come to confound the enemies; let him gather disciples. Christ has them, and John has them too. Christ baptizes, and John baptizes too. People come to John and tell him: *The one to whom you bore witness—see, he baptizes, and everyone comes to him*, so that, as though an envious rival, he might say something evil about Christ. But there the lamp burns more safely, shines more clearly, and is enlivened; the more distinct it is, the more secure. *I have already,* he says, *told you that I am not the Christ. The one who has the bride is the bridegroom; the one who comes from heaven is above everyone*. Then believers marveled at Christ; then indeed enemies were confounded, when the one who could be thought envious was in a way compelled to proclaim him. For the slave is compelled to acknowledge the Lord, the creature to bear witness to the Creator—or rather, he is not compelled but does it willingly. For he is a friend, not envious; his zeal is for the bridegroom, not himself.

**7.** The bridegroom's friends do this. At human weddings there is a certain custom: besides the other friends, a best man is also appointed, a closer friend who knows the secret of the bedchamber. But here there is a difference, indeed a great difference. What one man is to another as best man at human weddings, John is to Christ; and this same Christ the bridegroom is God, the mediator between God and human beings—but insofar as he is human. For insofar as he is God, he is not mediator but equal to the Father, the same thing as the Father, one God with the Father. How could that sublimity be mediator, from which we lay separated so far away? To stand between, let him take on something he was not; but so that we may arrive, let him remain what he was. For see, God is above us, and we are beneath him, and many spaces lie between; especially the interval of sin separates and casts us far away. Across so great a distance, when we had to come to God, by what way were we to come? God himself remains God; humanity is added to God, and one person results—not a demigod, as though God in his divine part and human in his human part, but wholly God and wholly human. God the deliverer, man the mediator: so that through him we may come to him, not through another, nor to someone other than him, but through what we are in him to the one through whom we were made. Therefore, although the Apostle knew Christ was God—for he himself said of him, while speaking of the Jews' prior privileges: *Theirs are the fathers, and from them is Christ according to the flesh, who is God above all, blessed forever*—although, then, he knew him as God, God above all, and above all precisely because everything was made through him, when he came to commend the mediator, he did not say God. For he is mediator not because he is God, but because he became human. This is our deliverance. *For there is one God.* You certainly listen as Catholics, instructed and attentive: *One God.* Is that the Father alone? The Son alone? The Holy Spirit alone? Certainly the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are one God. Therefore: *One God, and one mediator between God and human beings, the man Christ Jesus*. If he said, 'One God, and one mediator between God and human beings, Christ Jesus,' he might be understood as a lesser God. For he would seem separated from the Trinity's divinity if there were one God and one mediator between God and human beings, Jesus Christ, as though he were not the God said to be one. But because the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are there in God's unity, let divinity hold the unity and humanity take on the mediation.

**8.** Through this mediation the whole mass of the human race, alienated from God through Adam, is reconciled to him. *For through Adam sin entered the world, and death through sin; and so it passed to all people, in whom all sinned*. Who could be rescued from this? Who could be separated from this mass of wrath for mercy? *For who distinguishes you? What do you have that you did not receive?* Therefore, merits do not distinguish us, but grace. For if it is merits, it is a debt; if a debt, it is not gratuitous; if not gratuitous, it is not grace. The Apostle himself said this: *But if by grace, it is no longer from works; otherwise, grace is no longer grace*. Through one we are saved: greater and lesser, old and young, small children and infants; through one we are saved. *For there is one God, and one mediator between God and human beings, the man Christ Jesus. Through one man came death, and through one the resurrection of the dead. As in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive*.

**9.** Here someone meets me and says: How *all?* What, then, of those who will be sent into hell, condemned with the devil, and tormented in eternal fires? How *all* and *all?* Because no one comes to death except through Adam, and no one to life except through Christ. If there were another through whom we came to death, not all would die in Adam. If there were another through whom we came to life, not all would be made alive in Christ.

**10.** What, then, someone says, does even an infant need a deliverer? Certainly he does. His mother bears witness, running faithfully to church with her little one to be baptized. Mother Church herself bears witness, receiving the little one to be washed and either sent away delivered or nurtured in piety. Who would dare testify against so great a mother? Finally, in the little one himself, his crying bears witness to his misery. Weak nature, understanding little, testifies as much as it can: it begins not with laughter but with crying. Recognize one in misery; extend help. Let everyone be clothed with heartfelt mercy. The less they can act for themselves, the more mercifully let us speak for the little ones. The Church is accustomed to assist orphans in protecting their property. Let us all speak for the little ones; let everyone give them help, lest they lose their heavenly inheritance. For their sake too their Lord became a little child. How did those who deserved to be the first killed for him fail to belong to his deliverance?

**11.** Finally, when the approaching birth of the Lord Savior himself was foretold, it was said: *They will call his name Jesus, for he will save his people from their sins*. We hold to Jesus; we have the interpretation of this name. Why Jesus, which in Latin is called 'Savior'? Why Jesus? *For he will save his people.* But Moses saved his people with a mighty hand and the Most High's help from the Egyptians' persecution and domination. Joshua son of Nun also saved his people from persecutors and the wars of nations. The judges saved the people by delivering them from the Philistines; kings did so too, delivering them from the domination of nations barking all around. Jesus does not save in this way, but *from their sins. They will call his name Jesus.* Why? *For he will save his people.* From what? *From their sins.* Now I ask about the little one brought to church to become a Christian, to be baptized. I suppose this is so that he may be among Jesus's people. Which Jesus? The one who makes *his people* *safe from their sins.* If he has nothing needing salvation within him, let him be taken away from here. Why do we not tell mothers, 'Take these little ones away from here'? For Jesus is savior; if these have nothing needing salvation within them, take them away. *The healthy have no need of a physician, but the sick do*. Will anyone dare tell me, with the little one in such danger: 'He is Jesus for me, but not for this one'? So he is Jesus for you but not for this one? Does this one not come to Jesus? Is no response made for him that he may believe in Jesus? Are we instituting another baptism for little ones, in which sins are not forgiven? Certainly, if this little one could speak for himself, he would repel the opponent's voice and cry: 'Give me Christ's life; I died in Adam. Give me Christ's life, *in whose sight not even an infant whose life on earth is one day is clean*.' No one would deny grace to these, even if giving from his own resources. Let mercy be shown to those in misery. Why is their innocence praised beyond measure? Let it find a savior; let them not yet experience a flatterer. Certainly, with infants in such danger, we ought not even dispute, lest we seem to delay their salvation even by disputing. Let the child be brought, washed, delivered, and made alive. *As in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive.* He found no way to come into this age's life except through Adam; he will find no way to escape the coming age's punishment except through Christ. Why do you close the only door? *For there is one God, and one mediator between God and human beings, the man Christ Jesus.* Listen; he cries to you: *The healthy have no need of a physician, but the sick do.* Why do you call this one healthy, except because you contradict the physician?

**12.** 'Is it so,' he says, 'that even John, of whom you were speaking, was born with sin?' Certainly you have found someone born apart from sin if you find someone born apart from Adam. You cannot tear this statement from believers' hands: *Through one man came death, and through one the resurrection of the dead. As in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive*. *Through one man sin entered the world, and death through sin, and so it passed to all people*. If these were my own words, could I express this statement more distinctly, more evidently, or more fully? *So it passed to all people, in whom all sinned.* Now make an exception for John. If you separate him from human beings, from that course of human propagation, from the embrace of male and female, you will also separate him from this statement. For the one who wished to be separate from it deigned to come through a virgin. Why do you compel me to examine John's merits? In the womb he greeted the Lord; but I think he greeted the one from whom he desired salvation. He does not seek your most destructive defense. Conscious of the common weakness, he said to the Lord approaching his baptism: *I ought to be baptized by you*. For the Lord was coming to commend humility in baptism too and to consecrate the sacrament itself. As a young man he received baptism as he had received circumcision as an infant. He received remedies to be commended, not wounds. But why would John say, *I ought to be baptized by you,* if he were utterly clean of every fault, if there were nothing in him to be healed or cleansed? He calls himself a debtor, and you clear him so that his debts may not be forgiven. *I ought,* he says, *to be baptized by you:* I need it; it is necessary for me. And this was granted him there. For when the Lord was in the water, John was not outside the water. Why say more? Henceforth, if possible, let the opposing disputant cease, because the Savior himself delivered his herald too.
