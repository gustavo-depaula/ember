# Sermon 144

**1.** When our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ was saying many things about the coming of the Holy Spirit, whom he promised he would send and did send, he said among other things, *He will convict the world of sin, and of righteousness, and of judgment*. Nor, when he had said this, did he pass over into something else; but he deigned to set forth these very things somewhat more expressly. *Of sin indeed*, he says, *because they did not believe in me; of righteousness, because I go to the Father; of judgment, because the prince of this world has now been judged*[^1]. So there arises in us a desire to understand why the sin of men, as though it were the only one, should be not to believe in Christ, so that he speaks of this one alone, of which the Holy Spirit would convict the world; if, however, it is manifest that besides this unbelief there are many other sins of men, why the Holy Spirit convicts the world of this one alone. Is it because all sins are held through unbelief and are forgiven through faith, that therefore God charges this one above the rest, through which it comes about that the rest are not loosed, so long as the proud man does not believe in the humble God? For so it is written, *God resists the proud, but to the humble he gives grace*[^2]. For the grace of God is the gift of God. And the greatest gift is the Holy Spirit himself; and therefore he is called grace. For since all had sinned and needed the glory of God[^3]—because through one man sin entered the world, and through sin death[^4], in whom all sinned—therefore it is grace, because it is given freely. It is given freely because it is not repaid as a wage after a weighing of merits, but is given as a gift after the pardon of offenses.

**2.** Of sin, then, the unbelievers are convicted, that is, the lovers of the world; for they are signified by the name of the world. For when it is said, *He will convict the world of sin*[^5], it is of nothing other than that they did not believe in Christ. Finally, if this sin be not present, no sins will remain, because, the righteous living by faith, all are loosed. But there is a great difference whether one believes that he is Christ, and whether one believes in Christ. For even the demons believed that he was Christ, yet the demons did not believe in Christ. For he believes in Christ who both hopes in Christ and loves Christ. For if he has faith without hope and without love, he believes that he is Christ; he does not believe in Christ. So he who believes in Christ, by believing in Christ, Christ comes into him, and in a manner is united to him, and is made a member in his body. This cannot happen unless both hope and love are joined to it.  
What also does he mean when he says, *Of righteousness, because I go to the Father*[^6]? And first it must be asked: if the world is convicted of sin, why also of righteousness? For of what could it rightly be convicted concerning righteousness? Or is the world convicted of its own sin, but of the righteousness of Christ? I do not see what else can be understood; since, *Of sin*, he says, *because they did not believe in me; of righteousness, because I go to the Father*. They did not believe, he himself goes to the Father. So it is their sin, but his righteousness. But why did he wish to name righteousness only in this, that he goes to the Father? Is it not righteousness, then, that he came here from the Father? Or is it rather mercy that he came from the Father to us, but righteousness that he goes to the Father?

**3.** So, brothers, I think it expedient that, in so great a depth of the Scriptures, in which perhaps something hidden lies concealed in the words that ought to be opened up at the right time, we should, as it were, faithfully inquire together, that we may deserve to find profitably. Why, then, does he call this righteousness, that he goes to the Father, and not also that he comes from the Father? Is it because it is mercy that he comes, therefore it is righteousness that he goes; so that in us too we may learn that righteousness cannot be fulfilled, if we are slow to advance mercy first, not seeking the things that are our own but also the things of others? When the Apostle had given this admonition, he immediately joined the example of the Lord himself: *Doing nothing*, he says, *through strife, nor through vainglory; but in humility of mind esteeming one another superior to himself; each one looking not to his own things, but also to the things of others*. Then he at once added, *Have this mind in you, which was also in Christ Jesus; who, though he was in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal to God; but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man; he humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even death on a cross*. This is the mercy by which he came from the Father. What, then, is the righteousness by which he goes to the Father? It follows and says, *Therefore God also exalted him, and gave him the name which is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven, on earth, and under the earth, and every tongue confess that the Lord Jesus Christ is in the glory of God the Father*[^7]. This is the righteousness by which he goes to the Father.

**4.** But if he alone goes to the Father, what does it profit us? Why is the world convicted by the Holy Spirit of this righteousness? And yet, unless he alone went to the Father, he would not say in another place, *No one ascends into heaven, except him who descended from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven*[^8]. But the apostle Paul also says, *For our citizenship is in heaven*[^9]. But why this? Because he likewise says, *If you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; savor the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth. For you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^10]. How, then, does he alone? Or is he alone for this reason, because Christ is one with all his members, as a head with its body? And what is his body, except the Church? As the same teacher says, *Now you are the body of Christ, and members in part*[^11]. Since, then, we had fallen, and for our sake he himself descended, what is, *No one ascends, except him who descended*—except that no one ascends except one made one with him, and as it were a member fitted into the body of him who descended? So too he says to the disciples, *For without me you can do nothing*[^12]. For he is one with the Father in one way, and one with us in another. He is one with the Father, because the substance of Father and Son is one; he is one with the Father, because, *though he was in the form of God, he did not consider it robbery to be equal to God*. But he was made one with us, because *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*; he was made one with us according to the seed of Abraham, in which all nations will be blessed. When he had recalled this, the Apostle says, *He does not say: And to seeds, as of many; but as of one: And to your seed, which is Christ*. And because we too belong to that which is Christ, since we are incorporated together and cleave to that head, Christ is one; and because to us also he says, *So you are the seed of Abraham, heirs according to the promise*[^13]. For if the seed of Abraham is one, and that one seed of Abraham is understood to be none other than Christ; and this seed of Abraham is also we ourselves; then this whole, that is, head and body, is one Christ.

**5.** And therefore we ought not to think ourselves separated from that righteousness which the Lord himself recalls, saying, *Of righteousness, because I go to the Father*[^14]. For with Christ we too have risen, and we are with our head Christ, for the present in faith and hope; but our hope will be fulfilled in the final resurrection of the dead. And when our hope is fulfilled, then our justification too will be fulfilled. And about to fulfill it, the Lord showed in his flesh (that is, in our head) what we ought to hope for, in which flesh he rose and ascended to the Father. For so it is written, *He was handed over for our offenses, and rose again for our justification*[^15]. The world, then, is convicted *of sin*, in those who do not believe in Christ; *and of righteousness*, in those who rise in the members of Christ. Hence it was said, *That we might be the righteousness of God in him*[^16]. For if not in him, in no way righteousness. But if in him, the whole goes with us to the Father, and this perfect righteousness will be fulfilled in us. For this reason too the world is convicted *of judgment*, *because the prince of this world has now been judged*[^17]; that is, the devil, the prince of the wicked, who dwell in their heart only in this world, which they love, and therefore are called the world; just as our citizenship is in heaven, if we have risen with Christ. So, just as Christ is one with us, that is, with his body, so the devil is one, as it were with a body of his own, with all the ungodly to whom he is head. Therefore, just as we are not separated from the righteousness of which the Lord said, *Because I go to the Father*, so the ungodly are not separated from that judgment of which he said, *Because the prince of this world has now been judged*.
