# Sermon 127

**1.** Our hope, brothers, is not of this time, nor of this world, nor in that happiness by which men are blinded who forget God. This we ought first to know and hold with a Christian heart, that we have not been made Christians for the goods of the present time; but for something else, which God already promises, and man does not yet grasp. For of this good it was said: *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has ascended into the heart of man, which God has prepared for those who love him* [^1]. Therefore because this good, so great, so glorious, so ineffable, did not find a man to perceive it, it held God to promise it. For now what is promised him, blind man in heart does not perceive; nor can it be shown him in the present, what he himself to whom it is promised will be. For even a newborn infant, if it could understand the words of one speaking, when it itself could neither speak, nor walk, nor do anything, but as we see it weak, lying, in need of another's aid, could only understand one who spoke to it, and said to it: Behold, just as you see me walking, working, speaking, after a few years you will be such; attending to itself and to him, although it saw what was promised; yet considering its weakness, it would not believe, and yet would see what was promised. But to us, lying as it were as infants in this flesh and weakness, what is promised is both great, and is not seen; and faith is raised up by which we believe what we do not see, that we may deserve to see what we believe. Whoever mocks this faith, so as to think therefore that he ought not to believe, because he does not see; when there comes what he did not believe, he blushes; confounded he is separated, separated he is condemned. But he who has believed, is set apart to the right hand, and will stand with great confidence and gladness among those to whom it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the origin of the world*. But the Lord concluded, when he said these words, thus: *Those will go into the eternal burning, but the just into eternal life* [^2]. This is the eternal life, which is promised us.  
Because men love to live in this earth, life was promised them; and because they greatly fear to die, eternal was promised them. What do you love? To live. This you will have. What do you fear? To die. You will not suffer it. This seemed to suffice for human weakness, that it should be said: You will have eternal life. The human mind grasps this, somehow from what it does, it grasps what is to come. But how much does it grasp from this little that it does? Because it lives, and does not want to die; it loves eternal life, it wants always to live, never to die. But even those who will be tortured in penalties have a will to die, and cannot. It is not, then, a great thing to live long, or to live always: but it is a great thing to live blessedly.

**2.** Let us love eternal life, and from this let us know how much for eternal life we ought to labor, when we see men, lovers of the present temporal and ending life, so labor for it, that when the fear of death comes, they do whatever they can, not to take away, but to defer death. How much a man labors, when death threatens, by fleeing, hiding, giving whatever he has, and redeeming himself, laboring, sustaining torments and troubles, calling in physicians, and whatever else a man can do? See that, his labors and means consumed, he can bring it about that he live a little while; that he live always, he cannot. If, then, with so great labor, so great effort, so great expenses, so great insistence, so great watchfulness, so great care it is brought about that one may live a little longer; how must it be brought about that one may live always? And if those are called prudent who in every way act to defer death, and live a few days, lest they lose a few days, how foolish are those who so live as to lose the eternal day?  
This alone, then, can be promised us, that the gift of God may somehow grow sweet to us, from this which we now have: because of his gift we have it that we may live, that we may be safe. Let us, then, set before our eyes such a life, when eternal is promised, that we may remove from it whatever troublesome thing we suffer here. For we find more easily what is not there, than what is there. Behold, here we live, we shall live there too. We are safe when here we are not sick, nor does anything hurt in the body; we shall be safe there too. And when it is well with us in this life, we suffer no penalties; we shall suffer none there either. Set, then, here a man living, safe, suffering no penalties; if anyone should grant him that he be always so, and this good not cease, how much would he rejoice? How much would he be exalted? How would joy without penalty, without torment, without end of life, not be contained in him? If God promised us only this, which I have said, which now, in such words as I could, I have described and commended; for how much would it have to be bought, if it were for sale, how much would have to be given, that it might be bought?

**3.** Whatever you had would suffice, even if you possessed the whole world. And yet it is for sale: buy it if you will. Nor be much in a heat about a great thing on account of the greatness of the price. It is worth as much as you have. So to buy something great and precious you would prepare gold, or silver, or money, or some fruits of flocks or crops, which would be born in your possession, that you might buy this something great and glorious, by which you would live happy in this earth. And this buy, if you will. Do not ask what you have, but what kind you are. This thing is worth you. It is worth as much as you are. Give yourself, and you will have it. Why are you troubled? Why are you in a heat? Are you going to seek yourself, or buy yourself? Behold, you who are, what kind you are, give yourself to that thing, and you will have it. But I am evil, you will say, and perhaps it does not accept me. By giving yourself to it, you will be good. That you give yourself to this faith and promise, this is to be good. But when you have been good, you will be the price of that thing itself, and you will have, not what I said alone, safety, soundness, life, and life without end; not this alone you will have, I take away still other things. There will not be there to grow weary and to sleep: there will not be there to hunger and to thirst; there will not be there to grow and to grow old; because there will not even be to be born, where whole numbers abide. The number that is, is itself; nor is there need that it be increased, because it does not there come about that it be diminished. Behold how much I have taken away, and I have not yet said what will be there. Behold now there is life, now there is soundness, now no penalty, no hunger, no thirst, no defect, none of these; and yet I have not yet said: *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has ascended into the heart of man* [^3]. For if I have said it, what is written is false: *What eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has ascended into the heart of man*. For whence would it ascend into my heart, that I should say: *What has not ascended into the heart of man?* It is believed, and is not seen: not only is it not seen, but it is not even spoken. How, then, is it believed, if it is not spoken? Who believes what he does not hear? But if he hears it that he may believe it, it is spoken; if it is spoken, it is thought; if it is thought and spoken, it enters into the ears of men. And because it would not be spoken unless it were thought, it also *ascends into the heart of man*. Now behold this question of so great a thing troubles us, so that we cannot explain the question in words. Who explains the thing?

**4.** Let us attend, then, to the Gospel, just now the Lord was speaking, and let us do what he himself said: *He who believes in me*, he says, *makes a passage from death to life, and does not come into judgment. Amen I say to you, that the hour will come, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God; and those who hear, will live. For as the Father has life in himself, so he gave the Son to have life in himself* [^4]. By begetting he gave; because he begot, he gave. For the Son is of the Father, the Father is not of the Son, but the Father is the Father of the Son, and the Son is the Son of the Father. Yet the Son is begotten of the Father, not the Father of the Son, and always the Son; therefore always begotten. Who grasps this, always begotten? For every man, when he hears begotten, it occurs to him: therefore there was a time, when this one who was begotten was not. What, then, do we say? Not so: there was not a time before the Son, because *all things were made through him* [^5]. If *all things were made through him*; even times were made through him; how could there be times before the Son, through whom times were made? Take away, then, all times, the Son always with the Father. If the Son is always with the Father, and yet the Son, always begotten; if always begotten, always with the begetter was the one who was begotten.  
I never saw this, you will say, anyone begetting, and always with him whom he begot; but the one who begot preceded, and the one who was begotten followed in time. You say well: I never saw this, because this pertains to that, *which eye has not seen*. You ask how it may be said? It cannot be said: because *neither has ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man* [^6]. Let it be believed, and worshiped. When it is believed, it is worshiped; when it is worshiped, it is grown; when it is grown, it is grasped. For still in this flesh; as long as we are pilgrims from the Lord, to the holy angels, who see these things, we are infants, to be suckled by faith, to be fed by sight. For so the Apostle says: *As long as we are in the body, we are pilgrims from the Lord. For we walk by faith, not by sight* [^7]. We are to come to sight, which is so promised us through John in his Letter: *Most beloved, we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be*. *We are sons of God* already through grace, through faith, through the sacrament, through the blood of Christ, through the redemption of the Savior: *We are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared, what we shall be. We know that when it appears, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is* [^8].

**5.** Behold for what we are nourished to grasp, behold for what we are nourished to perceive, to eat; yet so that what is eaten is not diminished, and he who eats is invigorated. For now food invigorates by being eaten; but the food that is eaten is diminished, but when we have begun to eat justice, to eat wisdom, to eat that immortal food; we are both invigorated, and that food is not diminished. For if the eye knows how to be fed by light, nor yet diminishes the light; for the light will not be less, because it is seen by more; it feeds the eyes of more, and yet is as great as it was; and they are fed, and it is not diminished; if God gave this to the light, which he made for the eyes of the flesh, what is he himself, light to the eyes of the heart? If, then, some great food were praised to you, which you were going to eat, you would prepare your belly; God is praised to you, prepare your mind.  
Behold what your Lord says to you: *The hour will come*, he says, *and now is. The hour will come, and that hour is now*, when: what? *When the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear, will live* [^9]. Those, then, who do not hear, will not live. What is: *Those who hear?* Those who obey. What is: *Those who hear?* Those who believe and comply, they will live. So before they believed and obeyed, they lay dead; and they walked, and were dead. What were they worth, because they walked dead? And yet if anyone among them died in body, they would run, prepare a tomb, wrap, carry, bury, the dead the dead, of whom it was said: *Let the dead bury their dead* [^10]. Such dead are so raised by the word of God, that they may live in faith. Those who were dead in unbelief, are roused by the word. Of that hour the Lord said: *The hour will come, and now is*. For by his word he raised the unbelieving dead; of whom the Apostle says: *Rise, you who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ will enlighten you* [^11]. This is the resurrection of minds, this is the resurrection of the inner man, this is the resurrection of the soul.

**6.** But this is not the only one, there remains also that of the body. He who rises in the soul, for his own good rises in the body. For not all rise in the soul: all are to rise in the body. In the soul, I say, not all rise, but those who believe and obey; because: *Those who hear, will live*. But as the Apostle says: *Faith is not of all* [^12]. If, then, faith is not of all, not all rise in the soul. When the hour of the resurrection of the body comes, all will rise; be they good, be they evil: all will rise. But he who first rises in the soul, for his own good rises in the body; he who does not first rise in the soul, to his own harm rises in the body. He who rises in the soul, rises in the body unto life; he who does not rise in the soul, rises in the body unto penalty. Because, then, the Lord commended to us this resurrection of souls, to which we all ought to hasten, in which to labor that we may live, and by living persevere unto the end; it remained that he commend to us also the resurrection of bodies, which is to be at the end of the age. But hear how he commended this too.  
When he had said: *Amen I say to you, that the hour will come, and now is, when the dead*, that is, the unbelieving, *will hear the voice of the Son of God*, that is, the Gospel, *and those who hear*, that is, who obey, *will live*, that is, will be justified, and will be unbelieving no longer; when, then, he had said this, since he saw that we must be taught also about the resurrection of the flesh, and not so left, he went on and said: *For as the Father has life in himself, so he gave the Son to have life in himself*. This to raise minds, this to give life to minds. Then he added: *And he gave him power to do judgment too, because he is the Son of man* [^13]. This Son of God is the Son of man. For if the Son of God remained the Son of God, and did not become the Son of man, he would not free the sons of men. He himself who had made man, was made what he made, lest what he made perish. But he was so made man, that he remained the Son of God. For he was made man by taking on what he was not, not by losing what he was; remaining God, he was made man. He took you on, he was not consumed in you. Such, then, came he to us, the Son of God the Son of man, doing and made, creator and created; creator of the mother, created of the mother: such came he to us. According to that by which he is the Son of God, he said: *The hour will come, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God*. He did not say: "the Son of man"; for he was commending the truth, in which he is equal to the Father. *And those who hear, will live. For as the Father has life in himself, so he gave the Son to have life in himself*; not by participating, but: *in himself*. For we do not have life in ourselves, but in our God. But that Father has life in himself; and he begot such a Son, who would have life in himself; he would not become a partaker of life, but he himself would be life, of which life we would be partakers; plainly so that he might have life in himself, and himself be life. But that he might become the Son of man, he received from us. The Son of God in himself; that he might be the Son of man, he received from us. Of his own the Son of God, of ours the Son of man. What is less, he received from us; what is more, he gave us. For he even died from that by which he is the Son of man, not according to that by which he is the Son of God. Yet the Son of God died: but according to the flesh he died, not according to the Word which was made flesh, and dwelt among us [^14]. Therefore that he died, he died of ours; that we live, we live of his. Neither could he die of his own, nor we live of our own. This, then, as God, as the Only-begotten, as equal to the begetter, the Lord Jesus commended to us, that if we hear we shall live.

**7.** But: *And*, he says, *he gave him power to do judgment too, because he is the Son of man* [^15]. Therefore that form is to come unto judgment. The form of man is to come unto judgment; therefore he says: *He gave him power to do judgment too, because he is the Son of man*. The judge here will be the Son of man; that form will here judge which was judged. Hear, and understand, the Prophet had already said this: *They will see him whom they pierced* [^16]. They will see that very form, which they struck with a lance. He will sit as judge, who stood under a judge. He will condemn the true guilty, who was made a false guilty one. He himself will come, that form will come. This too you have in the Gospel: when before the eyes of his disciples he went into heaven, they stood and attended, and the angelic voice sounded: *Men of Galilee, why do you stand*? and so on. *This Jesus will so come, as you see him going into heaven* [^17]. What is: *he will so come?* He will come in that very form: *For he gave him power to do judgment, because he is the Son of man*. But see for what reason this was fitting, and this was right, that those to be judged should see the judge. For both the good and the evil were to be judged. But *blessed are the pure of heart, for they will see God* [^18]. It remained that in the judgment the form of servant be shown both to the good and the evil, the form of God be kept for the good alone.  
For what will the good receive? Behold, now I say: what a little before I did not say; and yet by saying it I do not say it. For I said that there we shall be safe, we shall be sound, we shall be living, we shall be without penalties, we shall be without hunger and thirst, we shall be without defect, we shall be without the loss of our eyes. All this I said; and what more we shall have, I did not say.

**8.** We shall see God. But this will be so great, and so great a thing it will be, that in comparison with it the whole is nothing. I said that we shall be living, that we shall be safe and sound, that we shall not suffer hunger and thirst, that we shall not fall into weariness, that sleep will not press us. All this, what is it to that happiness, by which we shall see God? Because, then, God himself as he is cannot now be shown, whom yet we shall see; therefore *what eye has not seen, nor ear heard* [^19], this the good will see, this the devout will see, this the merciful will see, this the faithful will see, this they will see who will have a good lot in the resurrection of the body, because they had a good obedience in the resurrection of the heart.  
So will the evil too see God? Of whom Isaiah says: *Let the impious be taken away, lest he see the glory of God* [^20]. So both the impious and the devout will see that form; and when the sentence has been said: *Let the impious be taken away, lest he see the glory of God*, it remains that concerning the devout and good there be fulfilled what the Lord himself promised, when he was here in the flesh, and was seen not only by the good, but also by the evil. He spoke among the good and the evil, and conspicuous to all, God hidden, man manifest; God ruling men, man appearing among men; he spoke, then, among them, and said: *He who loves me, keeps my commandments; and he who loves me, will be loved by my Father, and I will love him*. And as though it were said to him: And what will you give him? *And I will show*, he says, *myself to him* [^21]. When did he say this? When he was seen by men. When did he say this? When he was seen even by those by whom he was not loved. How, then, was he going to show himself to those who loved him, except such as those who loved him did not see? Therefore because the form of God was kept, the form of man was shown; through the form of man speaking to men, conspicuous and visible, both to the good and the evil he showed himself, for those who loved him he kept himself.

**9.** When will he show himself to those who love him? After the resurrection of the body, when the impious will be taken away, lest he see the glory of God. For then: *When he appears, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is* [^22]. This is eternal life. For all that we were saying is nothing to that life. That we live, what is it? That we are safe, what is it? That we shall see God: a great thing. This is eternal life; he himself said it: *But this is eternal life, that they may know you the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you sent* [^23]. This is eternal life, that they may know, see, grasp, know what they had believed, perceive what they could not yet grasp. Now let the mind see what eye had not seen, nor ear heard, nor had ascended into the heart of man; this will be said to them at the end: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the beginning of the world. Those*, then, *evil ones will go into the eternal burning. But the just* where? *Into eternal life* [^24]. What is: "eternal life"? *This is eternal life, that they may know you the only true God, and him whom you sent, Jesus Christ*.

**10.** Speaking, then, of the future resurrection of the body, and not leaving us, he said: *He gave him power to do judgment too, because he is the Son of man. Do not marvel at this, because the hour will come*. There he did not add: *and now is*; because that hour will be afterward, because that hour will be at the end of the age, because that hour will be the last, will be at the last trumpet. *Do not marvel*, because I said this: *He gave him power to do judgment too, because he is the Son of man. Do not marvel*. I said this, because a man must be judged by men. By what men to be judged? Those whom he finds living? Not only. But what? *The hour will come, when those who are in the tombs*. How did he express those dead in the flesh? *Those who are in the tombs*, whose corpses lie buried, whose ashes are covered, whose bones are scattered, whose flesh now is not, and yet to God is whole. *The hour will come, when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice, and all will come forth* [^25]. Be they good, be they evil, they will hear the voice, and will come out. All the chains of hell will be broken: all that has perished, rather is thought to have perished, will be restored. For if God made man who was not, can he not repair what was?

**11.** I think that when it is said: God will raise the dead, no incredible thing is said, because it is said of God, not of man. It is a great thing that will come to pass, and an incredible thing that will come to pass. But let it not be incredible, because see who does it. He is said to be the one who will raise you, who created you. You were not, and you are; and made, will you not be? Far be it, do not believe it. God did something more wonderful, when he made what was not: and yet he made what was not; and is he not believed to be about to repair what was, by those very ones whom he made what they were not? Is this what we repay to God, who were not, and were made? Is this what we repay him, that we believe he cannot raise up what he made? Is this the reward which his creature renders him? Did I make you for this, God says to you, O man, before you were, that you would not believe me that you will be what you were, you who could be what you were not? But behold, he says, in the tomb which I see, there is ash, there are ashes, there are bones; and will this too receive life, skin, flesh, and rise again? What, this ash, these bones, which I see in the tomb? Either in the tomb you see ash, you see bones; in your mother's womb there was nothing. This you see, either they are ash, or they are bones; you, before you were, were neither ash, nor were there bones; and yet you were made, when you were not at all: and do you not believe that these bones (in whatever way they are, of whatever kind they are, yet they are) will receive the form they had, when you have received what you did not have? Believe: for if you believe this, then your soul will be raised. And if your soul is raised now: *The hour will come, and now is*; then for your good your flesh will rise, when the hour will come, that all who are in the tombs may hear his voice, and come forth. For not because you hear and come forth, ought you now to rejoice: hear what follows: *Those who did good things, unto the resurrection of life; but those who did evil things, unto the resurrection of judgment* [^26].
