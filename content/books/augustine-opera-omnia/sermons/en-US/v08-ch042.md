# Sermon 301: On the Feast of the Holy Maccabees

**1. 1.** A great spectacle has been set before the eyes of our faith. With the ear we heard, with the heart we saw, a mother wishing her sons to end this life before her: with wishes far contrary to human custom. For all men wish their sons to precede them in departing from this life, not to follow: but she wished to die last. For she was not losing her sons, but sending them ahead: nor did she look at what life they were ending, but what one they were beginning. For they ceased to live where one day they were going to die; and they began to live, to live without end. It is a small thing to have been a spectator; we rather marveled at her as an encourager. More fruitful in virtues than in offspring: seeing them contend, in all of whom she herself contended; and in all of them conquering, she herself conquered. One woman, one mother—how she set before our eyes the one mother, holy Church, everywhere exhorting her sons to die for the name of him by whom she conceived and bore them! Thus, the world filled with the blood of the martyrs, with the seeds cast forth, the crop of the Church sprang up. Whence is this to man, except because *the salvation of the just is from the Lord, and he is their protector in the time of tribulation?*[^1]

**1. 2.** We have seen, we have known, that the Lord was the protector in the time of tribulation of those three men, who walked unharmed among the fires, and praised the Lord without any injury[^2]. Where the man raged, the flame spared.

**2. 2.** We have seen, we have known, how the salvation of those just men was from the Lord, that they were cast into the fire, and converted that harsh king, whom by speaking they had provoked, by living. For he believed in their God, and set forth an edict, that whoever blasphemed the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego should go to destruction, and his house to plunder[^3]. How unlike the second command to the first! What was the first command? Let him perish who has not adored the golden statue. What the second? Let him perish who has blasphemed the true God. The faithful men, unchanged, changed the unfaithful man. They did not allow him to stand in faithlessness, because they themselves stood in faith. So their salvation was manifestly from the Lord. When they did not burn and praised, the Lord was present. When these burned, confessed, yet died, where was the Lord? Or were those perhaps just, and these sinners? For we heard these a little while ago, when their suffering was read, confessing their sins, and saying that they suffered all those things by the Lord's anger against them, but also by their fathers' deserving[^4]. What of those? Read, and you will see that they too confess their own sins, and say that they deservedly suffer. Equally just, equally confessors of their sins; and just for this reason just, because equally confessors of their sins. Therefore irreproachable, because not lying. *For if we say*, says John, *that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us. But if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all iniquity*[^5]. So to the just belongs the confession of sins: to the proud belongs the defense of their merits. Equally just, then, in confessing their sins, equally giving glory to God, equally ready to die for his laws.

**3. 2.** Why are those freed from the fires, these consumed by the fires? Was God then present to those, and had he deserted these? Far be it: rather he was present to both; to those openly, to these in secret. Those he freed visibly: these he crowned invisibly. Those indeed were freed from death; but they remained in the temptation of this life: freed from the fire, reserved for perils; one tyrant conquered, still to contend with the devil. My brethren, understand as Christians. The Maccabees were freed better and more safely. By those three men, the rest remaining, that one temptation was overcome: by these this life was ended, which is wholly a temptation. Then by the divine judgment, hidden no doubt, but yet just, Nebuchadnezzar deserved to be converted, Antiochus deserved to be hardened. The one found mercy, the other increased his pride.

**4. 3.** But how much, and how far, did he increase his pride? *I have seen the impious exalted above the cedars of Lebanon*[^6]. How far? how long? *I passed by, and behold, he was not: and I sought him, and his place was not found*[^7]. Well: you sought, and you did not find; because you passed by. Do you wish to see the impious not to be? do you wish to seek him, and not find his place? Pass by. What do I say, Pass by? Do not be alarmed: I did not say, Die. For you thought I had said, Pass out of this life: and so you were alarmed, because you have not passed by. What is it, you have not passed by? You have not passed by the enticements of temporal happiness through lifting up of heart; you have not passed by the blandishments of the flesh; you have not passed by the suggestions of the age that tickle the heart and instill the fear of human miseries. Therefore you think there is happiness in this world, you do not think there is calamity in this world. The happiness of the kingdom of heaven has not touched your heart; from it no breath of refreshment has been sprinkled on your fevers. When it is said to you, The happiness of the world is false; even if you do not dare to say so, yet I see in your heart, perhaps you twist your mouth, you sneer, you mock, and you say to yourself: Oh, that it may be well with me here! afterward what will be, I do not know. And it is no small thing that you even say I do not know: lest perhaps you say even this: *The time of our life is short and wearisome, and there is no return at the end of a man, and there is no one known to have returned from the underworld*[^8]. At least say, I do not know. The confession of ignorance is a step toward knowledge. So let me address you, as if you said to me, I do not know what will be after death: I am wholly ignorant whether the just will be blessed, and sinners wretched; or whether both alike will not be. Yet, though you do not know it, you will not dare to say that after death sinners will be blessed, the just wretched. You cannot say that, though you suppose both will alike not be, yet the impious will be in a better state, and the just in evils after death. Nor can your ignorance suggest this to you. So you can say: Whether it is well with the just after death, and ill with the impious after death, or whether both alike will be without sensation, I do not know. Oh, that it may be well with me here, while I live, while I feel! You see that you have not yet passed by. These, I say, these earthly, dusty, smoky, vaporous, carnal, mortal thoughts you have not yet passed by. Therefore the impious seems to you exalted above the cedars of Lebanon: therefore you seek his place, and find it, because you have not passed by.

**5. 4.** You seek his place, and find it; but here. He has his place in this age. For not in vain would he be created by God who foreknows, nor in vain nourished, nor in vain would the sun rise upon him and the rain be poured out, nor in vain would so great a patience of God spare him, malicious and living ill. This is not in vain: he has his place here. And even if we cannot find out all things, yet all things are known to God, who knows how to order all. Behold—to be silent about others—what a place this wretched Antiochus had here. Through him the people of God was scourged and tested: through him these holy young men were crowned. So he had his place here. He was evil; but he was used well by one who cannot be evil. For just as evil men use good creatures ill, so the good Creator uses evil men well. He knows what to do with them, who created the whole human race. The goldsmith carries, the goldsmith weighs, the goldsmith balances. The painter knows where to put the black color, that the painting may be comely; and does God not know where to put the sinner, that the creation may be ordered?

**6. 4.** Unless God in the ages past had preserved sinners by his patience, whence would so many faithful be born today? Some evil ones are preserved, that good ones may be born from them. The good are by the grace of God: for the whole lump of sin is condemned. What is more wicked than the devil? and from his wickedness how many good things has God wrought? The blood of the Redeemer would not have been poured out for our salvation, except through the wickedness of the deserter. Read the Gospel, and see what is written there: *The devil put it into the heart of Judas to betray Christ*[^9]. The devil evil, Judas evil: as the instrument-maker, such the instrument. So the devil used his vessel ill: the Lord used both well. They strove for our ruin: God deigned to turn this to our salvation.

**6. 5.** Judas betrayed Christ, and was condemned. Judas betrayed, and is condemned: the Father betrayed the Son, and is glorified. Judas, I say, betrayed his master, and is condemned: the Son betrayed himself, and is praised. How Judas betrayed Christ, we all know: perhaps you await to hear how the Father betrayed the Son. This too you know: but I will recall it, that you may remember. Hear the apostle saying of God the Father: *He who did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all*[^10]. Hear also of the Son: *Who*, he says, *loved me, and handed himself over for me*[^11]. See now two betrayers: the Father a betrayer of the Son, the Son a betrayer of himself, but both a savior, because both a creator. So what did Judas do? What good did he do? Good was done through him, he did not himself do good. For Judas did not say: I will betray Christ, that the human race may be freed. In Judas avarice betrayed; in God, mercy. Nothing was rendered to Judas except what he did, not what God did through him.

**7. 6.** Why have we said these things? Because in this age there is a place for the impious: and the Lord well knows who are his[^12]; and he knows what to do for them, out of those who are not his. But you, if you pass by, if you trample on earthly things, if you have not answered in vain that you have your heart on high; by passing by you will seek the place of the impious, and not find it. For in that future life what place is there for the impious? Do we still have need to be exercised by evils? Does gold still need to be purified by chaff? For the whole world is the goldsmith's furnace. There the just are like gold: there the impious are like chaff. There tribulation is like fire: there God is like the goldsmith. The pious praises God, the gold glows: the impious blasphemes God, the chaff smokes. At one tribulation, as at one fire, the one is purified, the other laid waste: but God the goldsmith is praised in both.

**8. 7.** I will speak, dearly beloved, exhorting you and myself. With the Lord's help let us pass beyond carnal thoughts, let us have our heart on high, let us think of the future life: where, when your heart is, you have passed by. Where is the impious? He will not be there. Here he was necessary: there you will seek him, and not find his place. So when you see, brethren, you who live by faith, whose heart is upright, who hope for the future and likewise true and everlasting happiness; when you see men rejoicing and glad in that false and deceptive happiness, if you are pious, grieve; if you are sound, weep. For so too the one whose feet were moved reproached himself, because he had begun to accuse God, and was already there; but he was almost there, he was little short of it. He did not deny God knowledge: but as it were with feet moved he wavered. What is to waver? To doubt. But when he reproached himself for what he had not held aright, what did he say? Why were my feet troubled? *Because I was envious*, he says, *of the sinners, seeing the peace of sinners*[^13]. Because I saw the wicked rich, I was envious; and I said that I had lost righteousness, and *without cause I justified my heart, and washed my hands among the innocent*[^14]. And while I doubt, thus I began to understand. *Thus I began*, he says, *to understand: this is a labor before me*[^15]. A great labor, to solve this question. Truly it is a labor. It is well with him, and he is evil; it is ill with him, and he is good: and over both God is judge. So the just judge gives good things to the evil, and evil things to the good. *It is a labor before me*. But how long is it a labor? *Until I enter into the sanctuary of God, and understand the last things*[^16]. So if you understand the last things, there will be the rest of finding, the labor of the question will perish.

**9. 8.** Understand the last things, where no evil man will be happy, no good man unhappy. For what does he say? *For what is there for me in heaven?* Afterward I knew what is there for me in heaven, when I entered into the sanctuary of God, and understood the last things. *For what is there for me in heaven?* Incorruption, eternity, immortality, no pain, no fear, no end of blessedness. *What* then *is there for me in heaven?* what is kept for me in heaven? *And from you what have I wished upon earth?*[^17] *For what is there for me in heaven?* What? shall I say what? When shall I unfold what? Therefore, marveling, he said this, not unfolding it: *For what is there for me?* he says. Why do you not say what? How shall I say *what eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man?*[^18] Trample down below the what; because it is nothing: hope above for the what; because it cannot be unfolded. And having this faith, do not be envious of sinners, when you see them as it were happy, falsely happy, truly unhappy. And do you *rejoice in the Lord*[^19]. And if perhaps you have for a time riches, honors, powers, do not on that account think yourselves happy.

**10. 8.** To one who knows how to rejoice in the Lord, and understands the last things, the happiness of the world is not an honor, but a burden. The man happy according to the age is in peril, lest by that very happiness he be corrupted, not in body, but in soul. For that happiness is false. Such men, even if they seem to be something in this age, do not rejoice in it, but delight in the commandments of the Lord. Then what God commands is preferred to the world, both flattering and threatening: everything visible is trampled, passed by; passed by in thought, not by walking. I did not say, everything visible; for it is easy to pass over what you trample: but I said, everything changeable is passed by. Since whatever is visible is changeable: but not whatever is changeable is visible; for the mind too is changeable, and yet invisible. Pass over all that is seen; pass over also what is not seen, and yet is changed: that you may come to him who is neither seen nor changed. When you have come to him, you will come to God.

**11. 9.** But now walk by faith, order your ways. He is far on high: nourish your wings. Believe what you cannot yet see, that you may deserve to see what you believe. Let us live as pilgrims, let us think of passing on; and we shall sin less. Let us rather give thanks to the Lord our God, because he willed the last day of this life to be both short and uncertain. From earliest infancy to decrepit old age is a brief span. He who had lived so long, what would it have profited him if Adam had died today? What is long, where there is an end? No one calls back yesterday: today is pressed by tomorrow to pass away. In this very small span let us live well, and go there whence we shall not pass away. And now, even as we speak, surely we are passing away. Words run, they fly from the mouth: so our deeds, so our honors, so our misery, so this happiness of ours. The whole passes away: but let us not be alarmed; *The word of the Lord abides forever*[^20].
