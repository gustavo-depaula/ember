# Sermon 102

**1.** What our Lord Jesus Christ spoke to his disciples at that time was being written down, and was being prepared for us to hear. So we have heard his words. For what would it profit us, if he were seen and not heard? Nor does it now harm us at all, that he is not seen, and yet is heard. He says, then: *Whoever despises you despises me* [^1]. If he said only to the Apostles: *Whoever despises you despises me*; then despise us. But if his word has reached us, and called us, and set us in their place; see that you do not despise us, lest the injury reach him which you do to us. For if you do not fear us, fear him who said: *Whoever despises you despises me*. But what do we say to you, we who do not want to be despised by you, except that we may rejoice in your good conduct? Let your good works be the solaces of our perils. Live well, lest you die badly.  
And in these words I have said: Live well, lest you die badly, do not consider those who perhaps lived badly and died in their beds; and the pomp of their funeral was conducted, and they were laid in costly sarcophagi, in tombs most beautifully and laboriously built: and because perhaps each of you says to himself, I would like to die so, do not think I meant to say something vain; for I said that I want you to live well, lest you die badly.

**2.** On the contrary, perhaps someone comes to mind who both lived well and, according to the opinion of men, died badly: perhaps he died in a collapse, perhaps he died in a shipwreck, perhaps he died by beasts; and every carnal man says in his heart: What is it to live well? Behold, that man lived so, and died so. *Return, then, to the heart* [^2]; and if you are faithful, you will find Christ there; he himself speaks to you there. For I cry out: but he in silence teaches more. I speak through the sound of speech: he speaks within through the fear of thought. So let him plant in your heart my word: since I have dared to say, Live well, lest you die badly. Behold, since faith is in your hearts, and Christ is there, he himself can teach what I desire to sound forth.  
Recall in the Gospel that rich man, and that poor man: the rich man clothed in purple and fine linen, and fattened with daily banquets; but that poor man lying before the rich man's gate, hungry, and seeking the crumbs from his table, full of sores, licked by dogs [^3]. Recall, then: from where do you recall, except because Christ is there in your hearts? Tell me what you have asked him within, and he has answered you. For it follows, and he says: *It came to pass that the poor man died, and was carried by the angels into Abraham's bosom. But the rich man also died, and was buried in hell. And when he was in torments, he lifted up his eyes, and saw Lazarus resting in Abraham's bosom. Then he cried out, saying: Father Abraham, have mercy on me, and send Lazarus to dip his finger in water, and let it drip on my tongue, for I am tormented in this flame* [^4]. Proud in time, a beggar in hell. For that poor man came to a crumb: but he did not come to a drop. Of these two, then, tell me, who died well, and who died badly? Do not question your eyes, return to the heart. For if you question your eyes, they answer you falsely. For very splendid and worldly-painted are the things that could be displayed for that dying rich man. What troops of mourning menservants and maidservants there could be? what pomp of clients? what splendor of funeral? what costliness of burial? I believe him buried under spices. What then shall we say, brothers—that he died well, or died badly? If you question your eyes, he died most excellently: if you seek your inner master, he died most wretchedly.

**3.** If, then, the proud who hoard their goods, and bestow nothing from them on the poor, die thus: how do they die who seize the goods of others? So I spoke truly: Live well, lest you die badly, lest you die as that rich man did. The time after death alone proves a death evil. On the contrary, attend to that poor man: not with the eyes, for you will err; let faith attend, let the heart see. Set him before your eyes, lying on the ground, full of sores, dogs coming and licking his sores. But when you recall him such before your eyes, at once you spit, you turn away your face, you stop your nostrils. See with the eyes of the heart. He died, and was carried by the angels into Abraham's bosom. The rich man's household was seen mourning: the angels were not seen rejoicing. What, then, did Abraham answer the rich man? *Remember, son, that you received good things in your life* [^5]. You thought nothing good, except what you had in this life. You received them: but the days passed: and you lost it all, and you remained to be tormented in hell.

**4.** It is fitting, then, brothers, that these things be said to you. Attend to the poor, whether lying or walking about: attend to the poor, do good works. You who are accustomed to, do it: and you who are not accustomed to, do it. Let the number of those who do good works increase: since the number of the faithful also increases. What you do, how great a good it is you do not yet see: because the countryman too, when he sows, does not see the crop, but entrusts it to the earth. Why do you not trust God? Our harvest will come. Reckon that, though laboring now, we act, laboring we work, ready to receive, as it is written: *Going they went and wept, casting their seeds; but coming they will come with exultation, carrying their sheaves* [^6].

[^2]: See Isa 46:8
[^3]: See Lk 16:19-21
[^4]: See Lk 16:22-24
[^5]: See Lk 16:25
