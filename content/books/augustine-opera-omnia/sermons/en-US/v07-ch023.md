# Sermon 250: In the Days of Easter

**1.** The Lord Jesus, choosing the weak things of the world to confound the strong, and gathering his Church from the whole world, did not begin from emperors or from senators but from fishermen. For if those first chosen had been of any high rank, they would dare to ascribe this to themselves, not to the grace of God. This secret counsel of God, this counsel of our Savior, the Apostle expounds where he says: *For consider your calling, brothers*—they are the Apostle's words—*for consider your calling, brothers, that not many are wise according to the flesh, not many powerful, not many noble, but God chose the weak things of the world to confound the strong, and the ignoble things of the world and the contemptible things God chose, and the things that are not, as though they were, that the things that are might be brought to nothing; so that no flesh may glory before him*[^1]. This too the prophet said: *Every valley will be filled, and every mountain and hill will be brought low, the level of the plain will be established*[^2]. Finally, today the noble and the ignoble, the learned and the unskilled, the poor and the rich come alike to the grace of the Lord. To receive this grace, pride does not set itself before humility, before one who knows nothing, has nothing, is worth nothing. But what did he say to them? *Come after me, and I will make you become fishers of men*[^3]. If those fishermen had not gone before, who would have caught us? Now any orator whatever is great if he can well expound what a fisherman wrote.

**2.** Since, then, the Lord Jesus Christ had chosen fishers of fish and made them fishers of men, in their very fishings too he willed to admonish us something about the calling of the peoples. Attend to two fishings, distinguished by a necessary distinction; one when the Lord chose them from fishermen and made them his disciples, the other which we just now heard when the holy Gospel was read after the resurrection of the Lord Jesus Christ; the former before the resurrection, the latter after the resurrection. What is the difference between these fishings we ought greatly to attend to; it is the boat of our instruction. The new preaching of the Gospel; then it found them fishing; he approached and said to them: *Cast the nets.* They said: *The whole night we caught nothing*, we labored in vain. *See, in your name we will cast the nets.* They cast them and caught so many that they filled two boats, which were so pressed by the multitude of fish that they almost sank. Then by that very multitude of fish the nets were broken. Then he said to them: *Come after me, and I will make you fishers of men.* Leaving then the nets and the boats they followed Christ[^4]. Now after the resurrection the Lord Christ showed us another fishing, differing from that earlier one. For then he said: *Cast the nets*; neither "on the left" nor "on the right side," but only: *Cast the nets.* For if he said: "on the left," he would signify the bad only, "on the right," the good only. Because, then, he said neither "on the right" nor "on the left," good and bad are signified, of whom the Gospel speaks in another place, that the head of the household, when the supper was prepared, sent his servants and *they brought in those whom they could find, good and bad, and the wedding was filled with guests reclining*[^5]. Such is the Church now, full of good and bad. The Church is filled with a multitude but that multitude sometimes presses and tries to bring it near to shipwreck. The multitude of those living badly troubles those living well, so troubles them that one who lives well thinks himself a fool, when he attends to others living badly, especially because, by the goods of this world, many are found prosperous who are harmful, many are found wretched who are innocent. And how to be feared it is lest he be pressed by shipwreck and sink! How to be feared it is, beloved, lest he who lives well say: What does it profit me that I live well? For see, that man lives badly and is more honored than I. What does it profit me that I live well? In danger, I fear lest he sink. Let me address him, lest he who lives well sink more deeply. You live well, do not grow weary and look back. The promise of your Lord is true. *He who perseveres to the end, he will be saved*[^6].—You attend, you say, to the fact that another lives badly and is prosperous!—You are deceived, he is wretched, and is the more wretched the more prosperous he seems to himself. It is madness that he does not recognize his misery. If you saw a feverish man laughing, you would mourn the madman. What was promised you has not yet come. He who seems more prosperous than you is fed on visible and temporal things, rejoices in them; he neither brought them, nor will carry them off, he entered naked, he will go out naked; from false joys he will come to true sorrows. But to you, what was promised has not yet come; endure that you may arrive; persevere lest by failing you defraud yourself, for God cannot deceive you. See, I have said briefly, lest the boats sink. Something else more detestable was added to that fishing, that the nets were broken. The nets were broken, heresies arose. For what else are schisms but rents? So the first fishing is to be borne and endured, that no one be worn out with weariness, although it is written: *Weariness held me because of sinners who forsake your law*[^7]. The boat cries that it is pressed by the multitude, as though the ship itself has this voice: Weariness held me because of sinners who forsake your law. Even if you are pressed, see always that you do not sink. The bad are to be tolerated now, not separated. *Mercy and judgment we will sing to the Lord*[^8]; first mercy is paid out and afterward judgment is exercised; the separation will be made in the judgment. Now let the good hear me and be better, let the bad also hear and be good; while it is the time of repentance, not yet of the verdict. Let us pass from this fishing that has joys mixed with tears; joys because the good are gathered, mixed with tears because the bad are scarcely borne.

**3.** Let us turn our mind to that latest fishing; there let us be refreshed, there let us be consoled. And therefore it was made after the Lord's resurrection, because thus he signified the Church such as it will be after the resurrection. See, it is said to the disciples fishing—the Lord says it, who said it before, both he himself and afterward, but before what they should cast, now where they should cast, that is, on the right side of the boat. So now those are caught who will stand on the right, those are caught to whom it was said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*[^9]. They cast and catch. There the number was not given in the first fishing, only the multitude was given, the number was not defined. For many are over the number now, that is, they come, enter, fill the churches; the very ones who fill the theaters fill the church; over the number they fill it; to that number which will be in eternal life they do not belong, unless they are changed while they live. And are all changed? How? Not even all the good persevere. Therefore it was said to them: *He who perseveres to the end, he will be saved*[^10]. And to those who are still bad it is said: *I do not want the death of the sinner, but that he be converted and live*[^11]. Therefore there the number was not given because many were made over the number, as the Psalm says: *I have announced and spoken, they are multiplied beyond number*[^12]. Now on the right, not over the number; one hundred fifty-three are the fish but great. For this was said: *And though they were great, the net was not torn*[^13]. For then there will be the congregation of the saints, there will not be the divisions and rending of heretics, there will be peace and there will be perfect unity, no one will be less, no one more, the whole number. But they are very few, if there are one hundred fifty-three. Far be it from us that there be so few only in this congregation, how much more in the universal Church of God. The Apocalypse of the same blessed John the Evangelist shows that there was seen so great a multitude of saints, happy in that eternity, that no one can count it. So you have it there written. And yet all belong to this number of one hundred fifty-three. To a greater fewness I want to reduce this number. They are one hundred fifty-three, let us make them fewer, they are seventeen. These one hundred fifty-three are seventeen. Why ten? why seven? Ten because of the law, seven because of the Spirit. For the form of seven is because of the perfection that is preached in the gifts of the Holy Spirit. *He will rest*, says the blessed prophet Isaiah, *he will rest*, he says, *upon him, the Holy Spirit.* And when he had said Holy Spirit, he enumerated seven powers: *the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and fortitude*, you have four. *The Spirit of knowledge and piety, the Spirit of the fear of the Lord*[^14]. He began from wisdom, he ended at fear, as though descending he spoke from the highest to the lowest, from wisdom to fear. From the lowest to the highest: from fear to wisdom. *For the beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord*[^15]. This is the gift of grace; with this sevenfold power the Holy Spirit works in the beloved of God so that the law may avail something there. For if you take away the Spirit, what does the law avail? It makes a transgressor, therefore it is said: *The letter kills*[^16]. It commands and does not do it. It did not kill before it was commanded you, and if providence held you a sinner, yet it did not hold you a transgressor. It is commanded and you do not do it, it is forbidden and you do it, see, the letter kills. Now the law has ten precepts. The first precept of the law is: that one God is to be worshiped, no other, no idol to be made. The second precept is: You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain. The third precept is: Observe the Sabbath day spiritually, not carnally as the Jews. These three precepts pertain to the love of God, but because *on these two precepts*, he says, *hang the whole Law and the Prophets*[^17], that is, on the love of God and on the love of neighbor. When you have heard what pertains to the love of God: unity, truth, rest; attend to what pertains to the love of neighbor. *Honor your father and your mother*; you have the fourth precept. *You shall not commit adultery*; you have the fifth. *You shall not commit murder*; you have the sixth. *You shall not commit theft*; you have the seventh. *You shall not bear false witness*; you have the eighth. *You shall not covet your neighbor's goods*; you have the ninth. *You shall not covet your neighbor's wife*; you have the tenth[^18]. He who says: *You shall not covet* strikes the inner parts, beats the inward parts where desire does its business. See, this law is in ten. What does it profit when you have learned it and not done it? You will be a transgressor. But that you may do it, help is necessary. From where help? From the Spirit. *The letter kills, but the Spirit gives life*[^19]. Let the seven be added to the ten, they become seventeen. In this number is the whole multitude of the blessed. But how they arrive at one hundred fifty-three, I am now wont to tell you and many anticipate me, yet the sermon must be solemnly rendered. Many have forgotten, some have not even heard. But those who have not forgotten and have heard, let them patiently bear that others too be reminded and instructed. When two walk a road, one quicker and the other slower, it is in the power of the quicker that the companion not be abandoned. He who hears what he knew loses nothing, and in that he loses nothing he ought even to rejoice, and because he who did not know is instructed. Count seventeen from one up to seventeen so that you add them all and you will arrive at one hundred fifty-three. What do you expect from me? Count for yourselves.
