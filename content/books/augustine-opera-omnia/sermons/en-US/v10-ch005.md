# Sermon 343: A Treatise on Susanna and Joseph

**1.** May the divine readings and the holy oracles of God, which have sounded in our ears, make a nest in our minds. May they not fly away and pass on, or settle and depart, but bring forth something. For if *the sparrow finds a house for itself, and the turtledove a nest where she may lay her young*[^1], how much more the sparrow that is the Word of God, and the turtledove that is the mercy of God! We have heard the reading about Susanna. Let conjugal chastity be built up, and let it rest on so firm a foundation and be walled around so strongly that it both repels those who lie in wait and convicts false witnesses. The chaste woman would have remained doomed to die, had he not been present who saw what was hidden from those judging. Her words were recorded, which she had *in the garden*, that is, in her own pleasure-garden—words which no man heard except those two alone who lay in wait for the modesty of another's wife, and were plotting false testimony against her as she resisted. They alone heard what was said: *I am hemmed in on every side. For if I do this, it is death to me; but if I do not do it, I shall not escape your hands. But it is better for me not to escape your hands than to sin in the sight of God*[^2]. She despised what she heard, because she feared him whom she did not see, to whose divine eyes she herself was nevertheless visible. For it was not the case that, as she did not see God, so she was not seen by God. God saw what he was building, he looked upon his own work, he dwelt in his own temple; there he was, he himself answered those who lay in wait. For if the giver of chastity had abandoned her, chastity would have perished. She said, then: *I am hemmed in on every side*. But she waited for him who would save her from faintheartedness and from the storm of false witnesses, as it were of evil winds. Yet in those winds and waves chastity made no shipwreck, because the Lord steered. There was an outcry, there was a coming, a proceeding; the case came to judgment. The household of Susanna had believed the false elders against their mistress. And although her life, blameless and unstained in its past conduct, seemed to bear suitable testimony to her modesty, yet it seemed irreligious not to believe those elders. No such report had ever sounded concerning Susanna. Those, then, were false witnesses, but known to God. The household believed one thing, the Lord saw another. But what the Lord saw, men did not know. The elders seemed to be believed. Therefore she had to die. But if the flesh died, chastity would be crowned. The Lord was present to her praying, he heard her whom he knew. He did not abandon her to death, whom he aided not to be defiled. *The Lord stirred up the Holy Spirit*[^3] of Daniel, still youthful in age, but robust in piety. Because, then, the prophetic Spirit was in him, he at once saw the deceit of the most wicked elders. But what he beheld had to be provided for, how it might be shown to the rest. *They are false*, he said, *witnesses. Return to judgment*[^4]. But that they were false, he knew to whom the prophetic Spirit had revealed it. Those who did not know had to be taught. Therefore, if the judges had to be taught, the witnesses without doubt had to be convicted. Convicting them, then, aiming at the falsehood of the testimony which he himself already knew, he ordered them to be separated from one another. He questioned each one. For both could have one lust, but they could not contrive one plan. One was asked under which tree he had caught the adulterers. He answered: *Under a mastic tree*[^5]. The other was asked. He answered: *Under a holm oak*[^6]. The disagreement of the testimonies laid open the truth, set chastity free.

**2.** And chastity indeed, brothers, as I have already said, would be set free and crowned, even if the flesh, doomed at some time to die, had died at that judgment. For we are all doomed to die, nor does anyone who wishes to escape bring it about that he takes death away, but that he postpones it. This debt holds us all. We shall all repay this, which we drew from Adam. And as for our not wishing to die, no security is given by the exactor of this debt, but a delay is requested. Susanna, then, a religious woman and a chaste wife, was surely doomed to die at some time. And if that some time had been then, what harm would it have done to her chastity? The flesh would be laid in the tomb, chastity would be rendered to God, would be crowned by God. For do you think, brothers, that it pertains to a great merit if false witnesses do not prevail over an innocent person? It is no great merit if false testimony does not prevail against an innocent person. It would be a great merit if it had not prevailed against the Lord. Our Lord Jesus Christ himself was crucified by the tongue of false witnesses. But even those false witnesses indeed prevailed for an hour; what harm did they do to him who would rise again? And so by his example the Lord our God, in his flesh, in his weakness, and in the form of a servant which he took up to set the servant free, to seek the fugitive, to redeem the captive, to loose the bound, to make of a servant a brother—coming for this in the form of a servant, he showed the servant an example, that he should not dread false witnesses, and, when they are believed, should not fear. For they can make a bad reputation, they cannot kill the conscience. Three men were freed from the furnace of burning fire. Their God was present; they walked among the harmless fires, burning around them and not consuming, and in that very walking they spoke praises to God, and came out unharmed as they had been thrown in[^7]. Their God, then, was present. Was the God of the Maccabees absent[^8]? Those escaped, these were soon burned: both were tested. These were consumed in the flesh, those were unharmed in the flesh: both were crowned. So that the three men might escape the flames, it was granted to Nebuchadnezzar that he should believe in their God. For he who could free them openly could also crown them secretly. But if he had crowned them secretly, he would not have freed the king who had raged. The salvation of their body became the salvation of his soul. They escaped by praising God, but escaped present fires. He escaped by believing in God, but escaped eternal hells. More, then, was granted to him than to them. But Antiochus was not worthy that such things should be granted to him by whom the Maccabees were tortured. Therefore he exulted when they were consumed by fire and torments, but *he who exalts himself shall be humbled*[^9].

**3.** He, then, who freed Susanna, a chaste woman, a faithful wife, from the false testimony of the elders[^10], he himself also freed the virgin Mary from the false suspicion of her husband. That virgin, then, was found pregnant, to whom no man had come[^11]. Her womb indeed had swelled with offspring, but her virginal integrity had remained. By faith she had conceived the sower of faith. She had taken the Lord into her own body; she had not permitted her body to be violated. Yet to her husband, as to a man, it came as a suspicion. He believed to be from elsewhere what he knew was not from himself, and he suspected the adultery itself to be from elsewhere. He is corrected by an angel. Why was he worthy to be corrected by an angel? Because in him there was no malevolent suspicion, of the sort the Apostle says are born among brothers, malevolent suspicions[^12]. Malevolent suspicions belong to those who slander, benevolent suspicions belong to those who govern. One may have a bad suspicion about a son, but one may not slander a son. You suspect evil, but you hope to find good. He who suspects benevolently desires to be proved wrong; for then he rejoices rightly, when what he badly suspected is found false. Such was Joseph toward his spouse, with whom he was not joined in body, but yet had already been joined in faith. The Virgin, then, also came under a false suspicion. But as for Susanna the Spirit was present in Daniel, so for Mary the angel was present to Joseph: *Do not fear to take Mary your wife. For that which is born of her is of the Holy Spirit*[^13]. The suspicion was taken away, because redemption was found.

**4.** A little while ago married women were rejoicing over Susanna. Let virgins rejoice over Mary. Let both hold to chastity, the former conjugal, the latter virginal. For both chastities have merit with God. And if the virginal is greater, the conjugal less, yet both are pleasing to God, because each is a gift of God. All arrive at eternal life, but in eternal life not all obtain the same honor, the same dignity, the same merit. Eternal life and the kingdom of God will be such as—for the sake of a likeness—what we call the sky. In the sky are all the stars: so too in the kingdom of God all the good faithful will be. Eternal life will be equal for all. For there one does not live more, another less, since we shall all live without end. It is the same denarius that the workers are going to receive, whether those who labored in the vineyard or those who came at the eleventh hour[^14]; that denarius is eternal life, which is equal for all. But gaze at the sky, recall the Apostle: *There are other heavenly bodies, and other earthly. There is one glory of the sun, and another glory of the moon, and another glory of the stars. For star differs from star in glory; so also the resurrection of the dead*[^15]. Therefore let each one, my brothers, according to the gift he has received, strive in this age, that he may rejoice in the one to come. Are you married? It is a lower life, a lower reward is hoped for, yet the eternal kingdom is not despaired of. You must keep the conjugal precepts. For what? Because you have a wife, ought you not to recognize that you are on pilgrimage in this world[^16]? Ought you not to consider that you are doomed to die, doomed to depart from the bed of pleasure? And see whither you are heading, whether to the torment of calamity or to the reward of eternity. Consider, then, keep what you have received, carry your burden through, because it is light if you love, heavy if you hate. For not in vain did the Lord say (or was he speaking, when he said this, only to the continent?): *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you. Take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart, and you shall find rest for your souls* (not for your flesh, but for your souls); *for my yoke is gentle, and my burden is light*[^17]—light to the one who loves, heavy to the one who refuses. Have you taken the Lord's yoke on your neck? It is gentle, if you strive well; harsh, if you struggle against it. Temptations surround the conjugal life. For was that Susanna therefore not tempted in her very modesty because she was joined to a husband? Are these alone, who are joined to husbands, not tempted in this respect? Behold, Susanna was another's wife, she had a husband. Yet she was tempted. She was tossed in a storm: *I am hemmed in*, she said, *on every side*[^18]. For she feared to die at the hands of false witnesses, but she feared utterly to die at the hands of God the true judge. For at the hands of false witnesses she would die for a time, but at the hands of God the judge she would be punished forever. She weighed it, she chose. First she feared, and weighed it. She weighed it and chose. She chose and conquered. She taught religious married women. She taught them to resist the tempter, taught them to fight, taught them to labor, taught them to implore help.

**5.** If Scripture is a witness concerning a tempted woman, has it abandoned men? Has it permitted them to lack an example for imitation? We were beholding Susanna tempted by men desiring her corruption. We were beholding her striving. That reading was the theater of our heart; we awaited God's athlete, the chaste spirit, we saw the adversary striving. Let us triumph over the conquered together with the conqueress. Religious wives have their building, they have what to imitate. Let them owe to God what they preserve, not to man. For then they preserve it, if they owe it to God. Then they preserve it, if they owe it to him who sees what they preserve, which even the husband does not see. For often the husband is absent, God is always present. And sometimes, because the husband is a man, he suspects what is false. Then let the woman pray for her husband who suspects what is false. Let her pray that he may be saved, not that he may be condemned. For the false suspicion of the husband does not close the eyes of God. Her conscience is naked before him who creates it. For he frees forever her who is for a time oppressed. But let her pray for her husband, and take care not only to have a good life, but also an unharmed reputation. For chastity itself frees a good life, so that it is not condemned. But a good reputation frees others, lest by falsely suspecting they fall, and perhaps fall into sin, while they judge what they do not see; just as those men fell, and holy Daniel—indeed, through Daniel the Lord—freed those judges, rather than Susanna, from an inner death. For he freed her, lest she be condemned for a time. But he freed those men, lest, by judging badly and condemning an innocent woman, they fall into the eternal punishment of that judge whom no one can corrupt, from whom no one can hide himself.

**6.** I was speaking, then, about men, because they too have not been left without an example. Chaste men, men fearing God, men for whom their own wives suffice, men who do not violate what you do not wish to be violated for yourselves, men who render the faith you have demanded—you too watch, as I recall it, what your wives watched as the reader recited it. Divine Scripture has not left you either without an example. Those women were hearing of Susanna, and rejoicing in her conquest. You, attend to Joseph. Not that Joseph to whom the virgin Mary was betrothed, who bore Christ, for he was tested by suspicion and soon healed by the angel. Holy Scripture attests to another Joseph, whom a shameless woman tempted. An unchaste woman loved a handsome man, and with a perverse mind, where she had no eyes, where spiritual and invisible beauty might be seen. The one she loved as handsome she did not wish to be chaste. She loved a stranger, she loved her husband's servant. But did she love him as keeping faith with his lord? Or do you think she loved him rather than herself? Not even him. If she loved, why did she wish to destroy him? Behold, I have proved that she did not love. She burned with the poison of lust, she did not shine with the flame of love. But he knew how to see what she did not know. He was more beautiful within than without, more beautiful in the light of the heart than in the skin of the flesh. Where that woman's eyes did not penetrate, there he himself enjoyed his own beauty. Beholding, then, the inner beauty of chastity, when would he permit it to be stained, when to be violated by that woman's temptation? She loved. But he loved too. And what he loved was more than what she loved. Because he saw what she did not see.

**7.** If you wish to see in any way the spiritual beauty of modesty, if you have any sort of eyes for it, for the sake of an example I propose something to you: you love it in your own wife. Do not hate in another's wife what you love in your own. What do you love in your own? Chastity. This you hate in another, which you love in your own. This you hate in another, with whom by lying you wish to destroy her chastity. What you love in your own, this you wish to slay in another? What you love in your own, this you wish to destroy in another? How will you have the reasoning of piety, you murderer of chastity? Preserve, then, in another's wife what you desire to be preserved in your own. Love chastity itself rather. But perhaps you suppose yourself a lover of your wife's flesh, not of her chastity. A sordid thought indeed, but I do not let you go without an example. For I think that you love chastity in your spouse more than the flesh. But that I may show you most absolutely to be a lover of chastity: you love it in your daughter. What man is there who does not want his daughters to be chaste? What man who does not rejoice with his daughters' chastity? Do you love the flesh there too? Do you desire a beautiful body, where you shudder at one unchaste? Behold, I have proved you a lover of chastity. If, then, I have shown you to be a lover of chastity, why are you offended, so as not to love it in yourself? Behold, you have a shortcut. Love in yourself what you love in your daughter. Love this in another's wife, because your daughter too will be another's wife. Love, then, chastity in yourself too. If you love another's wife, you will not at once have her. If you love chastity, you will soon have it. Love chastity, then, that you may have eternal happiness.

**8.** But perhaps you will be tempted. A shameless woman will love you. She will find you in solitude, she will try to wring an embrace from you. If you refuse, she will threaten punishment by defaming you. This the false elders did to Susanna. This his lord's wife did to holy Joseph. But attend to him whom both Susanna and Joseph attended to. Not because there is no witness is God not there. Joseph did not wish to offend his eyes, the eyes of his Lord who was present. He did not wish to consent to the shameless woman for an unlawful union. He repelled another's lust, he embraced his own modesty. Yet she did what she had threatened. She lied to her husband, she was believed by her husband. God was still patient. But Joseph is kept in prison as though guilty, by whom God was not offended. But neither there was God absent, because he was not guilty. The Lord was present to Joseph patiently. That he did not come quickly to his aid, he deferred for greater rewards[^19]. He gladdened with merit the one whom he exercised with punishment. For holy Joseph himself had to suffer something even hard, that is, bitter, for chastity itself. If perhaps he loved that shameless woman, he would be prepared to endure hard things for her. And she would not prove her love toward him unless on his account she beheld such troubles or hardships, and repaid love (or rather, not love, but evil desire) in turn. She would burn for him in return, because she saw him burning with so great a love for her that on that account he would not refuse to bear whatever punishments. If this for a shameless woman, how much more for chastity itself! Well, then, does God sometimes defer his help, that he may test a man, that he may exercise a man, that the man himself may become known to himself. For nothing is hidden from God.

**9.** This, then, I would admonish to your love, brothers: that above all you set, above the carnal concupiscences and worldly joys and the empty and fleeting pomp and vapor of this present life, the splendor and beauty of wisdom; that you set above them the sweetness and pleasantness of wisdom; that you set above them the splendor of modesty, the beauty of chastity. All these things are hidden in the heavenly treasure. Naked before the eyes of God, these are precious gems; they shine greatly. If you have eyes, you see them. Set these, then, above the various and unlawful delights. And if temptation goes so far that you even suffer trouble, my brothers, who would not suffer for his money-bag? Who would not suffer for his field, for one boundary-stone of his field? If for these things you suffer, which you do not have in your power—as long as you wish to keep them, and to whom you wish you bequeath them, but they are often lost while we live, often after our death possessed by those whom we hate—if for these goods (if indeed those things are to be called goods which do not make men good) men suffer such great evils with an even mind, why are they slothful for the faith? Why are they timid for the heavenly treasure, for those riches which not even shipwrecks can take from us? For the just man comes out of shipwreck rich and naked.

**10.** Of these riches holy Job was full. All those things had perished at one blow, nothing had remained in his house, by which a little before he seemed wealthy. Suddenly a beggar, on a dunghill, swarming with worms from head to foot. What more wretched than this misery? What more happy than that inner happiness? He had lost all those things which God had given, but he had God himself, who had given all things. *Naked*, he said, *I came forth from my mother's womb, naked I shall return to the earth. The Lord gave, the Lord has taken away. As it has pleased the Lord, so has it been done. Blessed be the name of the Lord*[^20]. Is he surely poor? Surely he has nothing? If nothing remains, from what treasure were these gems of the praise of God brought forth? Afterward the tempter approached even to the flesh. Having taken away everything, he left a tempting woman. He let Eve go, but that man was not Adam. And there what was he found to be? How did he answer his wife suggesting blasphemy? *You have spoken*, he said, *like one of the foolish women. If we have received good things from the hand of the Lord, why do we not bear the evil?*[^21] O man rotten and whole! O foul and beautiful! O wounded and healthy! O sitting on a dunghill, and reigning in heaven! If we love, let us imitate. That we may imitate, let us labor. And if in our labor we grow faint, let us implore help. He helps the one striving, who appointed the contest. For God does not watch you striving as the crowd watches a charioteer; the crowd wished to shout, it did not know how to help. God does not watch you striving as the master of the games watches the athlete; he prepares a leafy crown, he does not know how to supply strength to the one laboring—nor can he; for he is a man, not God. And perhaps, while he watches, he labors more by sitting than the other by wrestling. For God, when he watches his contestants, helps them when invoked. For the voice of his athlete is in the Psalm: *If I said, My foot has slipped, your mercy, O Lord, was helping me*[^22]. Let us not, then, be slothful, my brothers; let us ask, seek, knock. *For everyone who asks receives, and the one seeking will find, and to the one knocking it will be opened*[^23].
