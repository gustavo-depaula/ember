# Sermon 211/A: In Lent

**1.** Our Lord Jesus Christ commended to us, in his passion, the labors and afflictions of the present age; in his resurrection he commended the eternal and blessed life of the age to come. Let us endure things present, let us hope for things future. Therefore in these days we enact days signifying the labors of the present age, wearing out our souls in fasts and observance; but in the days to come we signify the days of the age to come. We are not yet there. I said "we signify," not "we hold." For up to the passion there is affliction, after the resurrection praise.

**2.** For in that life our very business will be, in the kingdom of God: to see, to love, to praise. For what shall we do there? In this age there are works of necessity, and others of iniquity. What are the works of necessity? To sow, to plow, to plant vines, to sail, to grind, to cook, to weave, and whatever like things of necessity; and that good work of ours is of necessity. To break bread for the hungry is not your necessity; but it is his, for whom you break bread. To receive the stranger, to clothe the naked, to redeem the captive, to visit the sick, to give counsel to one deliberating, to free the oppressed, all these pertain to alms; they are works of necessity. What are those of iniquity? To steal, to rob, to get drunk, to play at dice, to exact usury; and who counts all the deeds of iniquity? In that kingdom there will be no works of necessity, because there will be no misery there; there will be no works of iniquity, because there will be no trouble there. Where there is no misery, there are no works of necessity; where there is no malice, there are no works of iniquity. For what will you do to eat, when no one hungers? Those very works of alms, how will you do them? For whom do you break bread, where no one is in want? What sick man do you visit, where perpetual health endures? Whom do you bury dead, where immortality never dies? The works of necessity perish; but the works of iniquity, if you do them here, you do not come there. What shall we do there, tell me? Sleep? For here men, having nothing to do, sleep; there there is no sleep, because there is no failing. Not, then, about to do works of necessity, nor to sleep, what shall we do? Let no one dread weariness, let no one think that even there there will be weariness. Do you now weary of being healthy? All things in this age have loathing; health has no loathing. If health has no loathing, will immortality have loathing? What, then, will be our action? *Amen* and *Alleluia*. For here we do one thing, there another, I do not say by day and by night, but by a day without end: which the powers of the heavens, the Seraphim, now say without weariness: *Holy, holy, holy, Lord God of hosts*[^1]. This they say without weariness. Does the beating of your vein now grow weary? As long as you live, your vein has its beating. You do something, and by tiring you are tired, and you rest, and return to work, and your vein is not tired. As your vein is not tired in your health, so your tongue and your heart will not be tired in the praises of God, in your immortality. Hear the testimony of your business. What is "your business"? This business is leisure. A leisurely business — what? To praise the Lord. Hear hence the sentence: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house*. The Psalm says this: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house*. And as though we asked whence blessed: "will they have much gold"? Those who have much gold are much wretched. Blessed are those who *dwell in your house*. Whence blessed? This is their blessedness: *forever and ever they will praise you*[^2].

[^1]: Isa 6:3
[^2]: Ps 84:4 (83:5)
