# Book V

## The custom is proved, with Cyprian as witness.

**1.** 1. That the ancient custom of the Catholic Church is now maintained, whereby those who come from heretics or schismatics, if they have received a baptism consecrated with gospel words, are not baptized anew—of this we use blessed Cyprian as witness. For he himself set out a question to himself, taken indeed from the mouth of the brethren, whether they were seeking the truth or contending on behalf of the truth. For in the course of his disputations, by which he wished to show that heretics must be baptized anew—about which we have said enough for the present in the foregoing books—he says: *But someone says: What then will become of those who in the past, coming to the Church from heresy, were admitted without baptism?*[^1]

Here the whole cause of the Donatists, with whom we have our conflict on this question, is utterly shipwrecked. For if those who, coming from heretics, were thus received did not truly have baptism, and their sins were upon them[^2], then, when communion was given to such persons—whether by those who were before Cyprian or by Cyprian himself[^3]—one of two things must necessarily be said: either that the Church then perished, stained by communion with such people, or that another's sins, even when known, do no harm to one who remains in unity.

But since they cannot say the former—that the Church then perished through the contagion of communion with those who, as Cyprian says, were admitted to it without baptism (otherwise they will not even be able to assert their own origin, if the Church perished then; for there are more than forty years between Cyprian's passion and the burning of the divine books, from which these men, hurling out the smoke of their slanders, found occasion for making a schism, as the order of the consuls shows)—it remains that they must confess that the unity of Christ is contaminated by no such communion even with known evildoers. And when they have confessed this, they will find no cause why they should contend that they had to separate themselves from the Churches throughout the world, which we read were likewise founded through the Apostles; because neither could those Churches perish through admixture with evildoers of any kind, and these men, who would not have perished had they remained in unity with them, by separating themselves from them and breaking the bond of peace, perished in schism.

For the most evident sacrilege of schism stands out, if there was no cause for separation. And it appears that there was no cause for separation, if even known evildoers do not stain the good in unity. But that the good are not stained in unity even by known evildoers we teach with Cyprian as witness, who says that *in the past those coming to the Church from heresy were admitted without baptism*[^4]; yet their wicked acts of sacrilege, which were upon them because they had not been remitted by baptism, if they could not pollute and destroy the holiness of the Church, then the Church can perish by no contagion of evildoers. Therefore, if they agree that Cyprian spoke the truth, they are convicted by his testimony of the crime of schism; if they contend that Cyprian spoke falsely, let them not use him as a witness in the question of baptism.

## The argument from Cyprian continues.

**2.** 2. But now let us carry through what we have undertaken: to converse with blessed Cyprian, a man of peace.

When he had set against himself what he knew was said by the brethren—*What then will become of those who in the past, coming to the Church from heresy, were admitted without baptism?*—he says: *The Lord is able by his mercy to grant indulgence, and not to separate from the gifts of his Church those who, having been admitted to the Church in simplicity, have fallen asleep in the Church*[^5]. He rightly presumed that the love of unity could cover a multitude of sins. And if they did have baptism, and those who judged that they must be baptized anew did not think rightly, the love of unity covered that error[^6], as long as there was present this human temptation, not a diabolical secession; until, as the Apostle says, the Lord should reveal to them if they thought otherwise in any matter[^7]. And therefore woe to those who, torn from unity by a sacrilegious cutting-off, rebaptize—if baptism exists both among us and among them; or, if it is in the Catholic Church alone, do not baptize at all. Whether, then, they rebaptize or do not baptize, they are not in the bond of peace; so let them apply medicine to whichever of these wounds is theirs.

But as for us, if we admit to the Church without baptism, we are in the number of those whom Cyprian presumed could be pardoned for the sake of preserving unity. But if (as I think is already clear from what we have said in the foregoing books) the integrity of Christian baptism can exist even in the perversity of heretics, then those who in those times rebaptized, and yet did not withdraw from the framework of unity, could by the same love of peace belong to pardon, the love by which Cyprian testifies that even those admitted without baptism could not be separated from the gifts of the Church. Further, if it is true that among heretics and schismatics there is no baptism of Christ, how much less would another's sins harm those set in unity, if even one's own sins were loosed for those coming to unity and admitted without baptism. For if, with Cyprian as witness, the bond of unity avails so much, how could those be harmed by another's sins who were unwilling to withdraw from unity, if even the unbaptized would not perish by their own sins, provided they wished to come to it out of heresy?

## The authority of Cyprian contradicts the Donatists.

**3.** 3. But what Cyprian adds, saying, *It is not the case that, because at some time there was error, error must therefore always continue; since it better befits men who are wise and fear God to follow gladly and without hesitation the truth once disclosed and discerned, than to struggle stubbornly and obstinately against brethren and fellow priests on behalf of heretics*[^8]—he says most truly, and he opposes none rather than himself who resists the most evident truth[^9].

But through all that we have now said at such length, it appears clearly enough, as I judge, and it is certain, that the baptism of Christ cannot be violated by the perversity of heretics when it is given and received among them. But even if it is not yet certain—at least that it is still doubtful, anyone who has weighed what has been said, even while still holding his own view, will confess. We do not, therefore, resist the most evident truth; rather, either we contend on behalf of a manifest truth, as I judge, or at any rate, as those may suppose who think this question not yet resolved, we are still seeking the truth. And so, if the matter stands otherwise than we say, we receive those baptized by heretics with the same simplicity with which those received them whom Cyprian, for the sake of unity, presumed to belong to pardon.

But if the baptism of Christ, as the many things now said indicate, can be whole even in one whose life or faith is not whole—whether of those who seem to be within and yet do not belong to the members of that one dove[^10], or of those who so do not belong to it that they are even openly outside—then those who in those times repeated it earned the same pardon, for the sake of the love of unity, which Cyprian believed those had earned, for the sake of such love, whom he supposed to have been admitted without baptism. Therefore those who, with no cause existing (since, as the same Cyprian shows, evildoers cannot harm the good in unity), cut themselves off from the love of that same unity, lost all place of pardon; and since they would perish by the very crime of schism, even if after coming to the Catholic Church they did not rebaptize—how great a punishment do they deserve, who either try to give to Catholics, who already have it, what Cyprian affirms they do not have, or, as the matter itself makes manifest, charge the Catholic Church with not having what they themselves also have?

## No cause for separation exists, on the authority of Cyprian.

**4.** 4. But because now, as I had begun to say, we have undertaken to converse with Cyprian's letters, I would not, as I think, seem even to him, were he present, to *struggle stubbornly and obstinately against brethren and fellow priests on behalf of heretics*[^11], when he took in the great considerations that move us, why we should believe that even among heretics, perverted in their malignant error, the baptism of Christ can nonetheless be in itself most reverend and most holy. And since he himself also testifies—whose testimony is of great weight for us—that such people were customarily admitted in the past in this way, whoever, moved by his words, does not doubt that heretics must be baptized anew, let him reckon those, to whom this has not yet been proven on account of the many things urged against it, to be such as those were in the past who simply admitted persons baptized in heresy, with only their own error corrected, and were able, together with them, to be saved through the bond of unity.

But whoever, from the Church's past custom, and from the later strength of a plenary council, and from so many and so great testimonies of the holy Scriptures, and from the many documents of Cyprian himself, and from the clear reasons of truth, understands that the baptism of Christ, consecrated with gospel words, is not made perverse by the perversity of any man whatever, let him understand that those to whom another view then seemed right, with love preserved, could be saved by that same bond of unity. And thereby it must at the same time be understood that those whom no tares, no chaff, could stain in the society of the Church spread throughout the whole world—had they themselves wished to be wheat—and who therefore, with no cause existing, broke away from that same bond of unity;

whichever of those two things be true—whether what Cyprian then held, or what the whole body of the Catholic Church, from which he did not withdraw, maintained—those most evidently set outside, in the manifest sacrilege of schism, cannot be saved; and all that they have of the divine sacraments and of the bounty of the one lawful Husband, as long as they are such, avails rather to their confusion than to their salvation.

## Baptism is never to be repeated.

**5.** 5. Wherefore, even if heretics truly wished, with their error corrected, to come to the Church because they thought they did not have baptism unless they received it in the Catholic Church, not even so ought we to consent to a repetition of baptism for them; rather they should be taught that the integrity of baptism does not profit their perversity if they refuse to be corrected; that the whole baptism was not violated by their perversity as long as they refused to be corrected; and that it is not because they wish to be corrected that a better baptism comes to be in them; but that they themselves depart from malignity, while that which was formerly in them unto destruction begins now to profit unto salvation. For by learning these things they will both desire salvation in Catholic unity, and will not consider that to be their own which is Christ's, and will not mingle the sacrament of truth, though set within them, with their own error.

## Some Donatists too dread being rebaptized.

**5.** 6. To this is added that men so detest, by some hidden inspiration of God, that anyone should receive baptism again, which he had already received wherever it was, that the heretics themselves, when they dispute about it, rub their brow in shame; and nearly all their laity who have grown old among them, and have conceived a fierce stubbornness against the Catholic Church, confess that this alone displeases them there; and many who wish to cross over to them in order to gain some worldly advantages or to avoid some disadvantages strive by secret efforts to have this granted them as a kind of special and private favor, that they not be rebaptized; and some, believing their other empty errors and false accusations against the Catholic Church, are held back by this one thing, that they are unwilling to be joined to them lest they be compelled to be rebaptized.

Fearing this feeling, which thoroughly possesses the hearts of all men, these Donatists chose rather to accept the baptism that was given among the Maximianists, whom they had condemned, and in that way to cut out their own tongues and stop up their own mouths, than to baptize anew so many people of Musti and Assuras and the other communities, whom, along with Felicianus and Praetextatus and the others they had condemned and who were returning to them, they received back.

## The horror of this deed sits deep in everyone's mind.

**6.** 7. For since this happens rarely, in individual cases, amid great intervals of place and time, the horror of the deed is not so felt; but if there were suddenly gathered together all those whom over so long a time—whether under the pressing perils of death or through the Paschal solemnities—the aforesaid Maximianists had baptized, and they were told that they must be baptized again, since what they had received in the sacrilege of schism was nothing: this indeed is what the stubbornness of their own error would compel them to say, that they might be able, by some false shadow of constancy, to cover the rigor and ice of their hardness against the warmth of truth; but because they could not bear this, and what was done in the case of so many people not even those who did it could tolerate—especially since these same men would rebaptize on Primianus's side those whom they had already baptized on Maximianus's side—the baptism of those people was accepted, and the swollen pride of these men was checked.

They would by no means have chosen to do this, had they not judged that the horror of men at a repeated washing was more opposed to them than the consideration of a lost defense. I have said this not because we ought to be deterred by human feeling, if truth compelled that those coming from heretics be baptized anew; but because holy Cyprian says *that by this very thing the heretics could have been driven all the more to the necessity of coming, if they were to be baptized again in the Catholic Church*[^12]; on this account I wished to recall how great a horror of this deed sits in the minds of nearly all, which I would believe to have been divinely instilled, so that against any disputations whatever which the weak cannot resolve, the Church might be fortified by that very horror.

## Certainly heretics possess baptism, but not justly and lawfully.

**7.** 8. Indeed, when I consider Cyprian's very words, I am prompted to say some things very necessary for settling a question of this kind. *For if they see*, he says, *it decreed and established by our judgment and sentence that the baptism with which they are baptized there is reckoned just and lawful, they will think that they also possess the Church and the other gifts of the Church justly and lawfully*[^13]. He does not say: They will think that they possess the gifts of the Church; but, *that they possess them justly and lawfully*. Now we grant that they do not possess baptism justly and lawfully; but we cannot say that they do not possess it, since we recognize the Lord's sacrament in the gospel words. They have, therefore, a lawful baptism, but they do not have it lawfully. For whoever has it both in Catholic unity and living worthily of it, both has it lawful and has it lawfully; but whoever has it either within the Catholic Church, like chaff mixed with wheat, or outside, like chaff carried off by the wind, has this baptism lawful indeed, but not lawfully. For he has it in the manner in which he uses it; and he does not use it lawfully who uses it against the law, which everyone does who, having been baptized, lives in ruin, whether within or without.

## Of baptism too it can be said: it is good.

**8.** 9. Wherefore, just as the Apostle said of the law, *The law is good, if anyone uses it lawfully*[^14], so of baptism we can rightly say: Baptism is good, if anyone uses it lawfully. And just as those who did not use the law lawfully did not then make it cease to be good, or to be nothing at all, so in no way does anyone who, whether because he lives in heresy or because he lives in the worst morals, does not use baptism lawfully make it cease to be good, or to be no baptism at all. And therefore, when he is converted either to Catholic unity or to a life worthy of so great a sacrament, he does not begin to have another lawful baptism, but begins to have that same baptism lawfully. Nor does the remission of irrevocable sins follow baptism unless it is had not only lawful but also held lawfully; and yet, if it is not held lawfully, and sins are either not remitted or, once remitted, return, the sacrament of baptism in the baptized is not on that account either evil or nothing.

For just as Judas, to whom the Lord gave the morsel, gave place to the devil within himself not by receiving something evil, but by receiving it wickedly[^15], so whoever receives the Lord's sacrament unworthily does not bring it about, because he himself is evil, that it should be evil, or, because he does not receive it unto salvation, that he should have received nothing. For it was nonetheless the Body of the Lord and the Blood of the Lord even for those to whom the Apostle said, *Whoever eats and drinks unworthily eats and drinks judgment upon himself*[^16]. Let not heretics, then, seek in the Catholic Church what they have, but what they do not have, that is, the end of the commandment, without which many holy things can be had but cannot profit. *Now the end of the commandment is love from a pure heart, and a good conscience, and faith unfeigned*[^17]. As for the sacrament of the washing, let them hasten to the unity and truth of the Catholic Church, not that they may have it, if they have already been washed with that same washing, though in heresy, but that they may have it to their benefit.

## The baptism of John.

**9.** 10. Now we must see what is said about the baptism of John[^18]. For *we read in the Acts of the Apostles that those who had already been baptized with the baptism of John were baptized by Paul*[^19], for no other reason than that the baptism of John was not the baptism of Christ, but was granted to John by Christ in such a way that it would properly be called John's, as John himself says: *A man can receive nothing, unless it has been given him from heaven*[^20]. And lest perhaps it might seem that he had received this from God the Father in such a way that he did not receive it from the Son, speaking again of Christ himself he says: *Of his fullness we have all received*[^21].

But John received this by the grace of a fixed dispensation, not to last long, but only as far as was enough to prepare the way for the Lord, whose forerunner he had to be. And as the Lord was to enter that way humbly and to lead to excellence those who humbly followed him, so, just as he washed the feet of his servants[^22], he willed to be washed with a servant's baptism[^23]. For as he subjected himself to the feet of those whom he himself directed, so he subjected himself to John's office, which he himself had bestowed; that all might understand with how great a sacrilege of pride anyone would despise the baptism he ought to receive from the Lord, when the Lord himself had received what he had himself bestowed upon a servant so that the servant might give it as his own; and that, since John, than whom no one greater has arisen among those born of women[^24], bore such testimony to Christ that he confessed himself unworthy to loose the strap of his sandal[^25], Christ, by receiving John's baptism, should be found most humble among men, and, by taking away the place of John's baptism, should be believed to be the most high God—the same teacher of humility and giver of loftiness.

## Further on the baptism of John.

**9.** 11. For to none of the prophets, to no man at all, do we read in the divine Scriptures that it was granted to baptize in the water of repentance unto the remission of sins[^26], as was granted to John; by which marvelous grace, holding the hearts of the peoples in suspense upon himself, he would prepare in them the way[^27] for him whom he proclaimed to be so much greater than himself. But the Lord Jesus Christ cleanses the Church with such a baptism that, once it is received, no other is required; whereas John was pre-washing with such a baptism that, once it was received, the Lord's baptism too was still necessary—not that the latter should be repeated, but that to those who had received the baptism of John the baptism of Christ also, for which John was preparing the way, should be handed on.

For if the humility of Christ were not to be commended, there would have been no need even of John's baptism; and again, if there were an end in John, there would be no need of Christ's baptism after John's baptism. But because *Christ is the end of the law, unto righteousness to everyone who believes*[^28], by John was shown the one to whom one was to proceed; and when one has reached him, there one remains. The same John, therefore, proclaimed both the loftiness of the Lord, when he set him far above himself, and his humility, when he baptized him as though he were the lowest. But if John had baptized Christ alone, John would be thought the dispenser of a better baptism—the one with which Christ alone was washed—than is the baptism of Christ with which Christians are washed; and again, if everyone had first to be baptized with the baptism of John and then with that of Christ, the baptism of Christ would rightly seem less full and less perfect, as not sufficing for salvation. Wherefore the Lord was both baptized with the baptism of John, that he might bend the proud necks of men to his saving baptism; and he was not the only one washed with that baptism, lest by this very thing he should show the former to be superior, in that he alone had been deemed worthy to be baptized with it; and he did not allow it to continue beyond that, lest this one baptism, with which he himself baptizes, should seem to need another preceding it.

## Whether the baptism of John remits sins.

**10.** 12. I ask, therefore: if sins were remitted by the baptism of John, what more could have been bestowed through the baptism of Christ on those whom the apostle Paul, after the baptism of John, willed to be baptized with the baptism of Christ[^29]? But if sins were not remitted by John's baptism, were they then better than John in Cyprian's times—those of whom he himself says that they seized estates by treacherous frauds and increased their gains by multiplying usury[^30]—and yet, when these baptized, remission of sins took place? Or was it because the unity of the Church held them? What then? Was John not in unity, that friend of the bridegroom[^31], the preparer of the Lord's way, and the baptizer of the Lord himself? What madman would say this? Wherefore, although I believe that John baptized in the water of repentance[^32] unto the remission of sins in such a way that to those baptized by him sins were remitted in hope, but in reality this took place in the Lord's baptism—just as the resurrection that is awaited at the end has been wrought in us in hope, as the Apostle says, *For he has raised us up together and made us sit together in the heavenly places*[^33], and the same says, *For in hope we were saved*[^34]; for John himself, though he says, *I indeed baptize you in the water of repentance, unto the remission of sins*[^35], yet, seeing the Lord, says, *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sins of the world*[^36]—nevertheless, lest anyone contend that sins were remitted even in John's baptism, but that some fuller sanctification was conferred through the baptism of Christ on those whom Paul commanded to be baptized anew, I do not argue contentiously.

## Why baptism took place after John, and why

**11.** 13. For what most pertains to the matter at hand must be considered (however the baptism of John may stand, since it is manifest that he belonged to the unity of Christ): what cause brought it about that people had to be baptized after holy John, and did not have to be after avaricious bishops. For no one denies that in the Lord's field John was wheat, and, if it can be no more, then in a hundredfold abundance. Likewise no one doubts that avarice, which is the service of idols[^37], is counted among the chaff in the Lord's harvest. Why, then, is one baptized after the wheat and not baptized after the chaff? If Paul baptized after John because he was better than John, why did not Cyprian also baptize after his usurer colleagues, than whom he was better beyond comparison? If Cyprian did not baptize after such colleagues because they were in unity with him, then neither ought Paul to have baptized after John, since they were held within the same unity. Or do cheats and the rapacious perhaps belong to the members of that one dove[^38], and he does not belong to whom the very power of the Lord Jesus Christ was shown by the Holy Spirit descending in the appearance of a dove[^39]? On the contrary, he belongs to it intimately, while those, whether by the occasion of some scandal or by the final winnowing to be separated from the wheat, in no way belong; and yet baptism took place after him, and is not done after these. What, then, is the cause, except that the baptism which Paul commanded them to receive was not this one which was given through John? And therefore, in that same unity of the Church, the baptism of Christ, if it is handed on by a usurer baptizing, cannot be repeated; but those who received the baptism of John even from John himself had afterward to be baptized with the baptism of Christ.

. . . baptism does not take place after Christ.

**12.** 14. Accordingly, I too, using the very words of blessed Cyprian, can turn the hearts of my hearers to the consideration of a certain marvel, if I say: *That John, held to be the greatest among the prophets; he who, while still in his mother's womb, was filled with divine grace; he who was sustained by the spirit and power of Elijah, who was not the Lord's adversary but his forerunner and herald; who not only foretold the Lord in words but also pointed him out to the eyes; who baptized Christ himself, through whom the rest are baptized*[^40]—he was not deemed worthy to baptize in such a way that those baptized by him should not be baptized after him; and after the avaricious, the cheats, the rapacious, the usurers, will no one think that anyone in the Church is to be baptized?

When I have invidiously made this outcry, is it not answered me: Why do you think this unworthy, as though either John were dishonored or the avaricious man honored? But it was not fitting that his baptism should be repeated, of whom the same John says, *This is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*[^41]. For through whatever minister it is given, it is the baptism of him of whom it was said, *This is he who baptizes.* But not even John's own baptism was repeated, when the apostle Paul commanded those baptized by him to be baptized in Christ. For what they had not received from the friend of the bridegroom[^42], this they had to receive from the bridegroom himself, of whom that friend had said, *This is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit.*

## The baptism of Christ.

**13.** 15. For the Lord Jesus could, if he had willed, have given the power of his baptism to some one or to certain chief servants of his, whom he had already made his friends, to whom he says, *I will no longer call you servants, but friends*[^43]; so that, just as Aaron was shown to be priest by the rod that flowered[^44], so in his Church, where more and greater miracles have been done, ministers of more excellent holiness and dispensers of the mysteries, who alone ought to baptize, might be shown by some sign. But if this were done, their baptism, though assigned to them by the Lord, would even so now be called theirs, as the baptism of John was. Therefore Paul gives thanks to God that he had baptized none of them, who, as though forgetful in whose name they had been baptized, divided themselves under the names of men[^45].

For since baptism given through a contemptible man avails as much as that given through an Apostle, it is recognized as belonging neither to the one nor to the other, but to Christ; which John testifies that he had learned, in the case of the Lord himself, through that appearance of the dove. For in what other respect he may have said, *And I did not know him*[^46], I do not plainly see. For if he did not know him at all, he would not have said to him as he came to his baptism, *I ought to be baptized by you*[^47]. What, then, does he mean when he says, *Because I saw the Spirit descending as a dove from heaven, and it remained upon him. And I did not know him; but he who sent me to baptize in water, he himself said to me: Upon whom you shall see the Spirit descending from heaven and remaining upon him, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*[^48].

The dove had certainly descended upon the baptized one. Yet earlier, to him coming to be baptized, John had said, *I ought to be baptized by you*[^49]. Already, then, he knew him. What, therefore, does he mean by *I did not know him; but he who sent me to baptize in water, he himself said to me: Upon whom you shall see the Spirit descending from heaven and remaining upon him, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*[^50]—since this took place after he was baptized—unless that in one respect he knew him, and in another respect did not yet know him?

He knew, namely, the bridegroom, the Son of God, of whose fullness all would receive[^51]; but because he himself had so received from his fullness the power of baptizing that it was called John's baptism, he did not know whether he was going to give it thus to others as well, or rather to have his own baptism in such a way that, through whatever minister it was given—whether of more excellent grace or of lower, whether through a man of hundredfold fruit or of sixtyfold or of thirtyfold, whether through the wheat or through the chaff—it would be recognized as belonging to none but that one; and this he learned through the Spirit, like a dove descending and remaining upon him.

## Whether one may be baptized after heretics.

**14.** 16. And so we find it said by the Apostles, both *my glory*[^52]—though, of course, in the Lord—and *my ministry*[^53], and *my understanding*[^54], and *my gospel*[^55], though, of course, imparted and given by the Lord; but "my baptism" none of them said at all. For the glory of all is not equal, nor do all minister equally, nor are all endowed with equal understanding, and in evangelizing one works better than another, and therefore one can be called more learned than another and saving by his very teaching; but one cannot be called more or less baptized than another, whether he is baptized by a lesser or by a greater minister.

Accordingly, since *the works of the flesh are manifest, which are fornications, uncleannesses, debaucheries, the service of idols, sorceries, enmities, contentions, jealousies, animosities, dissensions, heresies, envyings, drunkennesses, carousings, and the like*[^56]—if it is said as something marvelous, "People were baptized after John, and after heretics they are not baptized," why is it not said as something marvelous, "People were baptized after John, and after the envious they are not baptized"—since Cyprian himself testifies in his Letter on Jealousy and Envy that to be envious is to be on the devil's side, and Cyprian himself, from the apostle Paul, as we have already shown, makes plain that there were envious heralds of Christ in the Church of Christ in those very apostolic times[^57]?

## The baptism of John.

**15.** 17. Since, therefore, the baptism of John was not the same as the baptism of Christ, I think it has been sufficiently shown; and therefore no argument can be drawn from it that baptism should be done after heretics on the ground that it was done after John, since John was not a heretic and could have a baptism that, though granted by Christ, was nonetheless not properly Christ's, since he had the love of Christ; whereas a heretic can have the baptism of Christ together with the perversity of the devil, just as within the Church another can have the baptism of Christ together with the envy of the devil.

## The argument continues.

**15.** 18. But, it is said, all the more must one be baptized after a heretic, since John was not a heretic, and yet baptism was done after him[^58]. Just so, someone says, all the more must one be baptized after a drunkard, since John was sober, and yet baptism was done after him[^59]; and we shall have nothing to answer him, except that the baptism of Christ was handed on to those baptized by John who did not have it; but in those in whom there is the baptism of Christ, by no perversities of theirs whatever can it come about that the baptism of Christ is not in them.

## What John had and gave he received from Christ.

**15.** 19. It was not, therefore, that *a heretic could obtain the right of baptism because he baptized first*[^60]; but because he did not baptize with his own baptism. And even if he did not have the right of baptizing, nonetheless what he gave is Christ's, and what the other received is Christ's. For many things are given contrary to right, and yet they are not on that account said to be either nothing or not given. For neither does he who renounces the world in words only and not in deeds receive baptism by right; and yet he receives it. For Cyprian recalls that there were such people in the Church in his times, and we experience it, and groan.

## Whether baptism can be separated from the Church.

**15.** 20. But it is strange how it can be said that *baptism and the Church cannot be separated from each other and divided at all*[^61]. For if baptism remains inseparably in the baptized, how can the baptized be separated from the Church and baptism cannot? But that baptism remains inseparably in the baptized is manifest; for into whatever depth of evils and into whatever horrible abyss of sins the baptized may rush, even to apostatic ruin, he does not lose baptism; and therefore, to one returning through repentance, it is not given back, because he is judged not to have been able to lose it. But who would doubt that the baptized can be separated from the Church? From there, indeed, have come all the heresies that deceive under the Christian name.

## This question is answered.

**16.** 20. Wherefore, since it is manifest that baptism is in the baptized, when the baptized is separated from the Church, the baptism that is in him is, of course, separated along with him. And therefore not all who hold baptism hold also the Church, just as not all who hold the Church hold also eternal life. Or, if we say that none hold the Church except those who keep the divine commandments, we grant that there are already many who hold baptism and do not hold the Church.

## A heretic does not have priority in baptism.

**16.** 21. Wherefore *a heretic does not seize baptism beforehand*[^62], since he took it from the Church. Nor could he lose, when he withdrew, that which we say the Church no longer has, and yet we grant that he has baptism[^63]. Nor does anyone *take priority from himself and assign it to the heretic*, because he says that the heretic carried off with himself what he would give not lawfully, but would yet give lawful, and would no longer have lawfully, but would have lawful.

But priority lies only in holy conduct and a good life, to which belong all those of whom, as of members, that bride consists who has neither spot nor wrinkle[^64], and that dove[^65] groaning amid the wickedness of many crows—unless, perhaps, since Esau lost priority on account of his craving for a lentil dish[^66], we are to think that cheats, the rapacious, usurers, the envious, the drunken, and the rest of this kind hold priority—such as Cyprian groaned over, even in letters, as being in the Church of his time[^67]. Wherefore, either to hold the Church is not the same as to hold priority in divine things; or, if everyone who holds the Church also holds priority, then all those wicked persons do not hold the Church who nevertheless are seen within both to give baptism and to have it, which is denied by none of us. For who would say that they have priority in divine things, except one who has no taste for anything divine?

## The conclusion of Cyprian's letter.

**17.** 22. But now, all things being considered and thoroughly treated, we have come to those peaceful words of Cyprian, that is, to the end of the letter, which, as I read and often repeat them, do not satisfy me—so great is the pleasantness of brotherly love that breathes from them, so great the sweetness of love that overflows. *These things*, he says, *we have written back to you briefly, according to our mediocrity, dearest brother, prescribing to no one and prejudging no one, so as to keep each bishop from doing what he thinks right, having the free power of his own judgment. We, as far as in us lies, do not contend with our colleagues and fellow bishops on account of heretics, with whom we hold concord and the Lord's peace; especially since the Apostle also says, 'But if anyone is minded to be contentious, we have no such custom, neither the Church of God'*[^68]. *Love of spirit, the honor of our college, the bond of faith, the concord of the priesthood are preserved by us patiently and gently. For this reason also we have written, as far as our mediocrity was able, the Lord permitting and inspiring, a little book on the Good of Patience, which we have sent to you for our mutual love*[^69].

## Praises of Cyprian.

**17.** 23. In these words many things are to be considered, in which the radiance of Christian love shines forth in this man who loved the beauty of the house of the Lord and the place of the tabernacle of his dwelling[^70].

First, because he did not keep silent about what he thought; next, because he put it forth so gently and peacefully; because he held ecclesiastical peace with those who thought otherwise; because he understood that there is such great soundness in the bond of unity; because he so loved it and soberly guarded it; because he saw and felt that even those who think differently can do so with love preserved—for he would not say that he held divine concord and the Lord's peace with the wicked, since a good man can indeed have peace toward the wicked, but cannot hold with them a peace which they themselves do not hold; finally, because, prescribing to no one and prejudging no one, so as to keep each bishop from doing what he thinks right, having the free power of his own judgment, he gave even to us, such as we are, a place for treating these matters peacefully with him.

For he is present, not only through his letters, but also through that very love which flourished most in him and could never die. Desiring, therefore, to cling and be cemented to him, if I am not hindered by the unfittingness of my sins, aided by his prayers, I shall learn, if I can, from his letters, with how great peace and with how great consolation the Lord governed his Church through him;

and, clothed in the bowels of humility through the affection of his discourse, if I hold any view more truly together with the whole world, I shall not set my own heart above him, even in this very thing, that, though thinking otherwise, he was nonetheless not torn away from the whole world.

For a greater strength of virtue stood out in him, while this question still wavered undebated, in that, thinking otherwise than many of his colleagues, he maintained such great moderation that he did not mutilate the holy society of the Church of God with any blemish of schism, than if, without that virtue, he had held all his views not only truly but also in agreement with others.

For I do not please him if I should attempt to set his genius and his power of speech and his abundance of teaching above the holy council of all the nations, in which he assuredly took part through the unity of the Spirit—especially now that he is set in such a light of truth, where he most certainly perceives what he here most peaceably was seeking.

For out of that abundance he derides these words of ours, which seem to be, as it were, the babblings of infancy; there he sees by what rule of devotion he here acted, so that nothing in the Church was dearer to him than unity; there too he beholds, with ineffable delight, with how provident and most merciful a dispensation the Lord, in order to cure our swellings of pride, chose the foolish things of the world to confound the wise[^71];

and that he might so wholesomely arrange all things in the orders of the members of his Church, lest men should say that they had been chosen for the gospel's help on account of their genius or their letters—of whose gift they were as yet ignorant that they had them—and should thereby be puffed up with pestilent pride. How Cyprian rejoices, how much more serenely he beholds in that light, for how great a salvation of the human race it was brought about that something might be found which could deservedly be reproved, though in the Christian and devout writings of orators, and not be found in the writings of fishermen!

Wholly secure as I am about this joy of that holy soul, I dare neither think nor say that my own writings are free from all error; nor do I set my own opinion above his in that view in which it seemed right to him that those coming from heretics should be received otherwise than they either were received in the past, as he himself testifies, or are now received, as the reasonable custom has been confirmed by the plenary council[^72] of the whole Christian world; but I set above his opinion that of the holy Catholic Church, which he so loved and loves, in which he brought forth so abundant a fruit with endurance; whose universal body he himself was not, but in whose universal body he remained; whose root he never deserted, but, that he might be more fruitful in whose root[^73], he was pruned by the heavenly husbandman; for whose peace and salvation, lest the wheat be uprooted together with the tares, he both rebuked, with the liberty of truth, such great evils of men set together with him in unity, and bore them, with the strength of love[^74].

## The outwardly dead and the inwardly dead.

**18.** 24. Whence the same man most copiously admonishes us that many who are dead in their offenses and sins, although they do not belong to the society of Christ and to the members of that one innocent and simple dove[^75] (which, if it alone baptized, those would assuredly not baptize), are nevertheless seen, in appearance, within, both to be baptized and to baptize. And that in them, though dead, his baptism lives—his who does not die, and over whom death shall no longer have dominion[^76].

Since, therefore, there are dead men even within, and not hidden ones (for Cyprian would not say such great things about them), who either do not belong to that living dove or do not yet belong to it; and there are dead men outside, who more manifestly either do not belong to it or do not yet belong to it; and since it is true that *one who does not himself live cannot give life to another*[^77]: it is manifest that those who within are baptized by such men, if they come with a true conversion of heart, are given life by him whose baptism it is. But if they renounce the world in words and not in deeds[^78], such as Cyprian testifies are within as well, neither are they given life unless they are converted, and yet they have a true baptism, even if they are not converted. Whence it is likewise manifest that even the outwardly dead, although *they neither live nor give life*, nevertheless have a living baptism, which then profits them unto life, if they are converted to peace.

## Whether the old heresies rebaptized.

**19.** 25. Wherefore those who then received people coming from heresies in the same baptism of Christ which they had received outside, and said that *they were following the old custom*, as even now the Church receives them—in vain was it said against them that *among the old heresies and schisms the first beginnings were still in their early stage, so that those were there who were withdrawing from the Church, and had first been baptized here, whom, then returning to the Church and doing penance, it was not necessary to baptize*[^79].

For as soon as each heresy arose and went out from the congregation of Catholic communion, it could—I do not say on another day, but on that very day—baptize any who rushed into it. And therefore, if this was the old custom, that they should be received in this way (which not even those who argued against it could deny), it can be in doubt to no one who attends a little more carefully that those too were received in this way who were baptized outside in heresies.

## On the truly straying sheep.

**19.** 26. But what reason there is in this I do not see: that one is said to be *not yet a straying sheep*[^80] to whom, while seeking Christian salvation, it has happened to fall into the error of heretics and to be baptized among them; while one is said to be already made a sheep, within the Catholic Church itself, who has renounced the world in words only and not in deeds[^81], and in that falsity of heart has received baptism.

Or, if even such a one does not become a sheep until he turns to God with a true heart: just as this man is not baptized when he becomes a sheep, if he was already baptized, but the sheep does not yet pray; so too that man who comes from heretics that he may become a sheep is not then to be baptized, if among them he had already been baptized with the same baptism, although he was not yet a sheep. Wherefore, since all the wicked even within—the avaricious, the envious, the drunken, and those living against Christian discipline—can deservedly be called liars, and darksome, and dead, and antichrists, do they nonetheless on that account not baptize, because *there can be nothing in common between a lie and the truth*[^82]*, between darkness and light, between death and immortality, between Antichrist and Christ*[^83]?

## No sin makes the sacrament perverse.

**19.** 27. He therefore does not presume *upon custom alone, but* also *upon the reason of truth*, who says that the sacrament of God is made perverse by the perversity of no men, since it is declared to be present even in the perverse. Certainly the apostle John says most plainly, *He who hates his brother is in darkness even until now*[^84]; and again, *He who hates his brother is a murderer*[^85]: why, then, do such men baptize within, whom Cyprian himself recalls to have been in malevolent envy[^86]?

**20.** 27. How does a murderer cleanse and sanctify the water? How does darkness bless the oil? But if God is present to his sacraments and his words, by whatever sort of men they are administered, then both the sacraments of God are everywhere upright, and the wicked men to whom they profit nothing are everywhere perverse.

## Whether a baptized heretic belongs to the Church.

**20.** 28. But what sort of thing is this, that *a heretic is thought not to have baptism because he does not have the Church*[^87]? And surely *when he is baptized, he is also questioned about the holy Church*[^88]. As though, indeed, the man who within renounces the world not in deeds but in words[^89] were not questioned about this in baptism. As, therefore, this man's false answer does not bring it about that the baptism he receives is no baptism, so too that man's false answer about the holy Church does not bring it about that the baptism he receives is no baptism;

and just as that man, if afterward he truly fulfills what he had falsely answered, does not have baptism repeated for him, but his life is corrected; so too that man, if he afterward comes to the Church, about which, when questioned, he had answered falsely because he thought he had it while he did not, has given to him the very Church which he did not have, and what he had received is not repeated. But why God can still *sanctify oil* at words proceeding from the mouth of a murderer[^90], and *cannot do so at the altar that heretics have set up*[^91], I do not know; unless perhaps a man's heart falsely converted within does not hinder, while wood falsely set up outside does hinder him from deigning to be present to his sacraments—he who is hindered by no falsehoods of men.

If, then, what was said in the Gospel, *God does not hear a sinner*[^92], avails to this, that the sacraments are not celebrated through a sinner, how does he hear a murderer praying, whether over the water of baptism, or over oil, or over the Eucharist, or over the heads of those on whom hands are laid? Yet all these things are both done and avail even through murderers, that is, through those who hate their brothers, even within the Church itself. Since *no one can give what he does not have*[^93], how does a murderer give the Holy Spirit[^94]? And yet he himself within does also baptize. God, therefore, gives the Holy Spirit even when that very man baptizes.

## What happens if baptism is perhaps received from a heretic.

**21.** 29. But as for what he says, *He who comes to the Church must be baptized and renewed, that within he may be sanctified by the holy*[^95]—what will he do, if even within he runs into men who are not holy? Or is a murderer perhaps holy? And if he is baptized in the Church for this reason, that *he may lay aside even this very thing, that a man coming to God, while he seeks a priest, ran by the fraud of error into a sacrilegious one*: where shall he afterward lay it aside, if perhaps within the Church itself, while he seeks a man of God, he runs by the fraud of error into a murderer[^96]? If *there cannot be in a man something empty and something prevailing*, why can there be in a murderer a holy sacrament, and not a holy heart? If *whoever cannot give the Holy Spirit cannot baptize either*[^97], why does a murderer baptize within? Or how does a murderer have the Holy Spirit, since everyone who has the Holy Spirit is enlightened, while *he who hates his brother is in darkness even until now*[^98]? If *because there is one baptism and one spirit*[^99], those therefore cannot have one baptism who do not have one spirit, why do the innocent man and the murderer within have one baptism and not have the same spirit?

Thus, then, a heretic and a Catholic can have one baptism and not have one Church, just as within the Catholic Church the innocent man and the murderer can have one baptism and not have one spirit; for just as there is one baptism, so there is one Spirit and one Church. So it comes about that in each person what he has is to be recognized, and what he does not have is to be given to him[^100].

If *nothing that those do can be ratified and firm with the Lord whom the Lord declares to be his enemies and adversaries*, why is the baptism firm which murderers hand on? Or do we not call murderers enemies and adversaries of the Lord? But *he who hates his brother is a murderer*[^101]. How, then, did those baptize who hated Paul, the servant of Christ Jesus, and thereby hated Jesus too, who himself said even to Saul, *Why do you persecute me?*[^102], when he was persecuting his servants—and who at the end will himself say, *Inasmuch as you did it not to one of the least of mine, you did it not to me*[^103]?

Wherefore all who go out from us are not of us; but not all who are with us are of us: just as, when the threshing floor is threshed, whatever flies off from it is not wheat, but not whatever is there is wheat. And therefore John too says, *They went out from us, but they were not of us. For if they had been of us, they would surely have remained with us*[^104].

Wherefore God gives the sacrament of grace even through the wicked; but grace itself only through himself or through his saints. And therefore he works the remission of sins either through himself or through the members of that dove, to whom he says, *If you forgive anyone, they will be forgiven; if you retain anyone, they will be retained*[^105].

But baptism, which is the sacrament of the remission of sins, since it is in doubt to no one that even murderers can have it—who are in darkness even until now, because fraternal hatred has not been shut out from their hearts: whether no sins have been remitted to them, if they were baptized with no change of heart for the better, or, once remitted, returned at once—we recognize, by itself, because it is God's, to be holy, and, whether it is handed on or received by such men, to be violated by the perversity of none, whether within or without.

## Heretics can baptize, but not remit sins.

**22.** 30. Accordingly, we agree with Cyprian that *heretics cannot give remission*, but can give baptism—which indeed avails both to those who give and to those who receive it unto destruction, as to those who use so great a gift of God wickedly: just as the malicious and the envious too, whom he himself testifies to be within, cannot give remission of sins, although we all confess that they can give baptism. For if of those who had sinned against us it was said, *If you do not forgive men their sins, neither will your Father forgive you your sins*[^106], how much more is it impossible that sins should be forgiven those who hate the brothers by whom they are loved, and are baptized in that very hatred—to whom, nevertheless, when they are afterward corrected, baptism is not given again, but the very pardon which they then did not deserve to receive is bestowed in true conversion.

And therefore the things that Cyprian wrote to Quintus, and those that, together with his colleagues Liberalis, Caldonius, Junius, and the rest, he wrote to Saturninus, Maximus, and others, when well considered, are in no way to be brought forth against the agreement of the whole Catholic Church, of which they rejoiced to be members, and from which they neither cut themselves off, nor, though thinking differently, allowed themselves to be cut off; until at length, in the will of the Lord, through a plenary council, though after many years, what was more right shone forth, established by no novelty, but strengthened by antiquity.

## What Pope Stephen did.

**23.** 31. Cyprian also writes to Pompeius[^107] about this same matter, where he openly indicates that Stephen, whom we have learned was then bishop of the Roman Church, not only did not agree with him on these things, but even wrote and gave instructions to the contrary. And Stephen certainly did not *have communion with heretics*[^108] for the reason that he did not dare to disapprove the baptism of Christ, which he recognized to have remained whole in their perversity. For that those who think wrongly about God do not have baptism—that this can happen even within—has, I think, already been sufficiently argued. *The Apostles, indeed, gave no instructions about this*[^109]; but that custom which was set against Cyprian must be believed to have taken its origin from their tradition, as there are many things which the whole Church holds and which are therefore rightly believed to have been commanded by the Apostles, although they are not found in writing.

## Heretics are condemned by themselves.

**23.** 32. But it is written of heretics *that they are condemned by themselves*[^110]. What then? Are not those also condemned by themselves to whom it was said, *For in that in which you judge another, you condemn yourself*[^111]? And to these the Apostle says, *You who preach that one must not steal, do steal*[^112], and so on. And such, assuredly, were those who, though they were bishops and set in Catholic unity with Cyprian himself, seized estates by treacherous frauds[^113], while preaching to the people the words of the Apostle who says, *Nor will the rapacious possess the kingdom of God*[^114].

## Baptism is common to true and to feigned Christians, but love is not.

**23.** 33. Wherefore I shall not delay to run briefly through the other statements of that very letter which was written to Pompeius, according to the same rules.

By what passage of the holy Scriptures is it shown *that it is against the commandment of God that those coming from heretics, if they have already received there the baptism of Christ, are not baptized*? But it is plainly shown that many false Christians, although they do not have the same love as the saints—without which whatever holy things could be had profit nothing—nevertheless have baptism in common with the saints: which has already been sufficiently and most abundantly demonstrated.

He said that *the Church and the Spirit and baptism cannot be separated from one another, and therefore those who are separated from the Church and from the Holy Spirit* he would have *understood to be separated also from baptism*. But if this is so, then, when anyone has received baptism in the Catholic Church, it remains in him only so long as he himself remains in the Church; but if he withdraws from it, he withdraws from baptism[^115]—which is not so. For the reason it is not given back to one returning is that, when he withdrew, he did not lose it. But just as the Holy Spirit, as the beloved sons have it, the malicious sons do not have, and yet they have baptism, so too the Church, as Catholics have it, heretics do not have, and yet they have baptism. For *the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee the feigned*[^116], and yet baptism will not flee him. And so, just as baptism can exist even where the Holy Spirit takes himself away, so baptism can exist where the Church is not.

But *the laying on of hands*[^117], if it were not *applied to one coming from heresy*, he would be judged to be, as it were, free from all fault: but for the sake of the bond of love, which is the greatest gift of the Holy Spirit, without which whatever other holy things have been in a man do not avail unto salvation, the hand is laid on heretics who are corrected.

## Sinners have baptism, but not the Holy Spirit.

**24.** 34. I recall having already discussed sufficiently *the temple of God*[^118] and how this is to be understood: *As many of you as were baptized in Christ have put on Christ*[^119][^120]. For the avaricious are not the temple of God, since it is written, *What fellowship has the temple of God with idols?*[^121], and Cyprian took up the testimony of Paul that avarice is idolatry[^122]. But men put on Christ sometimes only as far as the reception of the sacrament, sometimes even as far as the sanctification of their life; and that first can be common to both the good and the wicked, but this second is proper to the good and the devout. Wherefore, if *baptism cannot exist without the Spirit*, then heretics too have the Spirit; but unto destruction, not unto salvation, as Saul had it[^123]. For through the name of Christ, in the Holy Spirit[^124], demons are cast out, which even that man could do who was outside the Church, about whom the disciples reported to the Lord[^125]. As the avaricious have it, who nevertheless are not the temple of God, since *what fellowship has the temple of God with idols?*[^126] But if the avaricious do not have the Spirit of God, and yet have baptism, then baptism can exist without the Spirit.

## Whether heresy can beget children.

**24.** 35. If *heresy cannot beget children to God through Christ because it is not the bride of Christ*[^127], neither can that crowd of the wicked set within the Church, since it too is not the bride of Christ. For the bride of Christ is designated as without spot and wrinkle[^128]. Therefore either not all the baptized are sons of God, or even one who is not the bride can beget sons of God. But just as it is asked *whether one has been born spiritually who received the baptism of Christ among heretics*[^129], so it can be asked whether one has been born spiritually who received the baptism of Christ in the Catholic Church without being turned to God with a true heart; and yet he did not on that account fail to receive baptism.

## In the dispute with Stephen the peace of Christ prevailed.

**25.** 36. The things that, provoked, he poured out against Stephen I do not wish to review, both because there is no need, since the very same things are said which have already been sufficiently discussed, and because it is better to pass over those things which carried the peril of pernicious dissension. Stephen, indeed, had even thought that those should be cut off from communion who were trying to overturn the ancient custom regarding the receiving of heretics; but Cyprian, moved by the very difficulty of the question, and most abundantly endowed with the holy bowels of love, judged that one must remain in unity with those who thought differently. And so, although he is indignant rather hotly, yet in a brotherly way, the peace of Christ nevertheless prevailed in their hearts, so that in such a dispute no evil of schism arose between them. Nor did *heresies and schisms grow up more abundantly from this*[^130]; for what in them is Christ's is approved, but what is their own is disapproved. For rather have those who held this law of rebaptizing been cut into more fragments.

## The teachings of the Apostles must always be obeyed.

**26.** 37. And now, when he says that *a bishop ought to be teachable*[^131], and adds, *And that man is teachable who is gentle and mild toward the patience of learning; for bishops must not only teach but also learn, since he too teaches better who daily grows and advances by learning better things*[^132]—by these words the holy man, endowed with devout love, sufficiently indicates that we need not fear so to read his letters that, if afterward the Church has confirmed anything ascertained by fuller and longer inquiries, we should not waver; for just as there were many things that the learned Cyprian might teach, so there was also something that the teachable Cyprian might learn. And what he admonishes us, *that we run back to the source, that is, to the apostolic tradition, and from there direct the channel into our own times*[^133], is most excellent and is without doubt to be done. *It has therefore been handed down to us*, as he himself recalls, by the Apostles, *that there is one God, and one Christ, and one hope, and one faith, and one Church, and one baptism*[^134].

Since, then, in the very times of the Apostles we find that there were some who did not have one hope, and yet had one baptism, from that very source the truth is led down to us in such a way that it appears it can come about that, while there is one Church, just as there is one hope and one baptism, nevertheless those have one baptism who do not have one Church—just as even in those times it could come about that those had one baptism who did not have one hope. For how did those have one hope together with the saints and the righteous who said, *Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we shall die*[^135], saying that there was no resurrection of the dead? And yet there were among them those to whom the same Apostle says, *Was Paul crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^136] For he writes to them most plainly, saying, *How do some among you say that there is no resurrection of the dead?*[^137]

## How the Church is described in the Song of Songs.

**27.** 38. And as for the fact that in the Song of Songs the Church is described thus: *A garden enclosed, my sister, my bride, a sealed fountain, a well of living water, a paradise with the fruit of apple trees*[^138]—this I do not dare to understand except of the holy and the righteous, not of the avaricious and the cheats, and the rapacious, and usurers, and the drunken, and the envious; whom nevertheless, as I have often recalled, we learn and teach more abundantly from Cyprian's own letters[^139] to have had baptism in common with the righteous, with whom they certainly did not have love in common.

For let someone tell me *how they crept into the garden enclosed and the sealed fountain*, whom Cyprian testifies to have renounced the world in words only and not in deeds[^140], and yet to have been within. For if they too are there, and they too are the bride of Christ, is she then truly such, without spot and wrinkle[^141], and is that beautiful dove[^142] disfigured by such a part of her members? Or are these the thorns, in the midst of which she is like the *lily* that is spoken of in the same Song[^143]?

In so far, then, as she is a *lily*, in so far is she also a *garden enclosed* and a *sealed fountain*: namely, in those righteous who are Jews in secret by the circumcision of the heart[^144] (for *all the beauty of the king's daughter is within*[^145]), among whom is the fixed number of the saints, predestined before the foundation of the world. But that multitude of thorns, whether by hidden or by open separations, lies outside, beyond the number. *I have declared and have spoken; they are multiplied beyond number*[^146]. That number, then, of the righteous, who are called according to his purpose[^147], of whom it was said, *The Lord knows those who are his*[^148], is itself the *garden enclosed, the sealed fountain, the well of living water, the paradise with the fruit of apple trees*[^149].

Of this number some live spiritually and enter upon the surpassing way of love[^150]; and when they instruct, in the spirit of gentleness, a man overtaken in some offense, they take heed lest they themselves also be tempted[^151]; and when perhaps they themselves too are overtaken, the affection of love is somewhat repressed in them, but not extinguished, and, rising up and burning again, is restored to its former course. For they know how to say, *My soul slumbered for weariness; strengthen me in your words*[^152]. But when they hold any view otherwise, that too God will reveal to them, as they remain in the ardor of love and do not break the bond of peace[^153].

But some, still carnal and animal, earnestly pursue their own advances, and, that they may become fit for the food of the spiritual, are nourished with the milk of the holy mysteries; the things that in depraved morals are manifest even to popular judgment they shun in the fear of God;

and they strive most vigilantly to be less and less delighted by earthly and temporal things; the rule of faith, diligently inquired into, they hold most firmly, and if they deviate from it at all, they are quickly corrected by Catholic authority, although in its words, on account of a carnal understanding, they still waver amid various crowdings of imaginings.

There are also some of that number who as yet live wickedly, or even lie in heresies or in the superstitions of the Gentiles; and yet even there *the Lord knows those who are his.* For in that ineffable foreknowledge of God, many who seem to be outside are within, and many who seem to be within are outside. Of all those, then, who, so to speak, are inwardly and secretly within, that *garden enclosed, sealed fountain, well of living water, paradise with the fruit of apple trees*[^154] consists. Of these the gifts granted by God are partly proper, such as, in this present time, untiring love, and in the world to come, eternal life; but partly common with the wicked and perverse, such as all the rest, among which are also the most holy mysteries.

## On the ark of Noah and on baptism.

**28.** 39. Hence, then, the consideration of that ark, of which Noah was the builder and steersman[^155], is now set before us more easily and readily. For Peter says, *In the ark of Noah a few, that is, eight souls of men, were saved through water; which in a like form baptism also saves you—not the putting off of the filth of the flesh, but the appeal of a good conscience*[^156].

Wherefore, if there appear to men, baptized in Catholic unity, those who renounce the world in words only and not in deeds[^157], how do they belong to the mystery of this ark, in whom there is no appeal of a good conscience? Or how are they saved through water, who, using holy baptism wickedly, though they seem to be within, persevere even to the end of life in disgraceful and ruined morals? Or how were those not saved through water whom Cyprian himself recalls to have been in the past simply admitted into the Church with the baptism they had received in heresy?

For the same unity of the ark saved them, in which no one was saved except through water. For he himself says, *The Lord is able by his mercy to grant indulgence, and not to separate from the gifts of his Church those who, having been simply admitted to the Church, have fallen asleep in the Church*[^158]. If not through water, how in the ark? If not in the ark, how in the Church?

But if in the Church, then certainly in the ark; and if in the ark, then certainly through water. It can happen, therefore, that even some baptized outside are, by the foreknowledge of God, more truly reckoned baptized within, because there the water begins to profit them unto salvation; for they cannot otherwise be said to have been saved in the ark except through water;

and again, some who seemed to be baptized within are, by the same foreknowledge of God, more truly reckoned baptized outside; for, using baptism wickedly, they die through the water—which then happened to none except one who was outside the ark.

Certainly it is manifest that what is said—within and outside, in the Church—is to be thought of in the heart, not in the body; since all who are within in heart are saved, in the unity of the ark, through the same water by which all who are outside in heart die, as adversaries of unity, whether they are also outside in body or not.

As, therefore, not another but the same water both saved those set in the ark and destroyed those set outside the ark, so by not another but the same baptism both good Catholics are saved and wicked Catholics or heretics perish.

But what the most blessed Cyprian thinks about the Catholic Church, and how by his authority heretics are utterly crushed—although I have said much, I have nevertheless determined, if it please the Lord, to say separately, somewhat more abundantly and plainly, when I have first said about his council what I think is owed by me, which in the will of God I shall take up in the following volume.
