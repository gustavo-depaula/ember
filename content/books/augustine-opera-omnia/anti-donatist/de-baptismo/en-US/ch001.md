# Revisions II

## Chapter 18 (45): Baptism, in seven books

Against the Donatists, who were struggling to defend themselves by the authority of the most blessed bishop and martyr Cyprian, I wrote seven books, *On Baptism*. In them I taught that nothing is so effective for refuting the Donatists and for shutting their mouths completely, lest they defend their schism against the Catholic Church, as the writings and the conduct of Cyprian.

But wherever in these books I have mentioned[^1] the *Church not having spot or wrinkle*[^2]*,* it is not to be understood as though it were already such, but as one that is being prepared to be such, when it shall also appear in glory. For now, on account of certain forms of ignorance and weakness in its members, it has reason to say daily, as a whole: *Forgive us our debts*[^3].

In the fourth book, when I said that suffering could take the place of baptism,[^4] the example of the thief[^5] that I offered was not quite apropos, because it is uncertain that he was not baptized.[^6]

In the seventh book, concerning the gold and silver vessels set in a great house, I followed the view of Cyprian, who took these in a good sense, but the wooden and earthen vessels in a bad sense, referring to the former what is said, *some indeed for honor,* and to the latter what is said, *but others for dishonor*[^7]*.* But I more approve what I afterward found or noticed in Tyconius: that in both kinds some are to be understood as *for honor*, not the gold and silver alone, and again that in both kinds some are *for dishonor*, not the wooden and earthen alone.[^8]

This work begins thus: *In those books which against the letter of Parmenian.*

[^1]: De bapt. 1, 17, 26; 3, 18, 23; 4, 3, 5; 7, 10, 19.
[^2]: Eph 5:27.
[^3]: Matt 6:12. See De haeres. 88; De gestis Pelag. 12, 27.
[^4]: De bapt. 4, 22, 29.
[^5]: See Luke 23:40-43.
[^6]: See Revisions I, 26, question 62; II, 55, 3.
[^7]: Cyprian, Ep. 54, 3; see 2 Tim 2:20.
[^8]: Tyconius, De sept. regul. 7; see Ep. 41, 2; QQ. in Hept. 2, 102.
