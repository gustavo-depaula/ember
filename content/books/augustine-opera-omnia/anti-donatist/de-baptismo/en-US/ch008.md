# Book VII

## Cyprian erred, but he loved unity.

**1.** 1. Let us not be troublesome to our readers if we discuss the same things in varied ways and often. For although the holy Catholic Church, in all nations, is fortified by the authority of ancient custom and of a plenary council against the things that, in this question about baptism—whether the same baptism that is in the Catholic Church can be also among heretics or schismatics—spread some fog, nevertheless, because to men not to be despised, and especially to Cyprian, set in that very unity, it once seemed otherwise, those who are far removed from his love try to use his authority against us;

therefore we are compelled, by the opportunity of treating and considering all that we find about this matter in his council and his letters, to turn over this same question somewhat longer, as it were, in our hands, and to show how much more truly it pleased the universal body of the Catholic Church that heretics or schismatics who have already received the baptism of Christ there whence they come should be admitted into Catholic communion along with it, corrected from their error, rooted and grounded in love[^1]; so that, as regards the sacrament of baptism, what was lacking may not be supplied, but what was present may profit.

And blessed Cyprian indeed, now that the body which is corrupted no longer weighs down the soul, nor the earthly habitation presses down the mind that thinks of many things[^2], perceives more serenely the truth which he deserved to attain through love. Let him therefore help us by his prayers, as we labor in the mortality of this flesh, as in a foggy cloud, that, the Lord granting, we may, as far as we can, imitate his good qualities.

But if he thought anything otherwise, and persuaded certain brothers and colleagues of it—which he now, with the one he loved revealing it, already perceives—let us, far unequal to his merits, yet following, in our weakness, the authority of the Catholic Church, of which he himself is a distinguished and most dear member, unravel the matter against heretics or schismatics: whom, cut off from the unity which he held, and withering from the love by which he flourished, and slipped from the humility by which he stood, he disapproves and condemns the more, the more he knows that they wish to search out what he wrote in order to lay snares, and are unwilling to imitate what he did in order to make peace.

Just as those who call themselves Nazarene Christians, and circumcise the carnal foreskin in the Jewish manner, born heretics out of that error from which Peter, deviating from Paul, was called back[^3], persist in it even to this day: just as, then, those, after Peter, crowned in the primacy of the Apostles with the glory of martyrdom, remained cut off from the Church in their perversity; so these, after Cyprian, received, through the abundance of his love, into the lot of the saints by the glory of his passion, acknowledge themselves to be exiles from unity, and set up against the homeland of unity, on behalf of their slanders, a citizen of unity.

And so now let us look, by the same method, at the remaining opinions of that council.

## Some favor heretics and oppose Christians.

**2.** 2. Marcus of Mactaris said: *It is not to be wondered at if heretics, the enemies and assailants of the truth, vindicate to themselves a thing of another's power and condescension; this is to be wondered at, that certain of our own, betrayers of the truth, support heretics and oppose Christians. Therefore we have decreed that heretics are to be baptized*[^4].

## Christians love unity.

**2.** 3. To him we answer: On the contrary, it is rather to be wondered at, and to be proclaimed with great praise, that these men so loved unity that those whom they thought betrayers of the truth, while persevering in unity with them, they did not fear to be polluted by. For when this man had said, *This is to be wondered at, that certain of our own, betrayers of the truth, support heretics and oppose Christians*, he seemed about to say: Therefore we decree that they are not to be communicated with. He did not say this; but he said, *Therefore we decree that heretics are to be baptized*: preserving what the peaceful Cyprian had built beforehand, saying, *Judging no one, nor removing anyone from the right of communion if he should think differently*[^5]. Since, then, the Donatists slander us and call us traitors, if there should arise some Jew or pagan who, having read this council, should call both us and them, according to their rules, betrayers of unity, I would like to know, for the refuting and washing away of so grave a charge, how our common cause is to be pleaded. These men call those traitors whom neither at that time could they convict, nor now can they show to be such, and they themselves are shown rather to be in the same crime.

But what is that to us? About these betrayers, certainly, what shall we say? For if we, though falsely, are called traitors for the reason that they charge us with having succeeded traitors in the same communion, we have all succeeded those betrayers, because the party of Donatus had not yet divided itself from unity in the times of blessed Cyprian. For after his passion, forty years and more having passed, the handing over of the books took place, whence they began to be called traitors. If, then, we are traitors for the reason that we sprang from traitors, as they suppose or feign, both of us derive our origin from those betrayers. For there is no saying that these men did not communicate with them, since they call them their own; which the council states, which they themselves most of all bring forward: *Certain of our own*, it says, *betrayers of the truth, support heretics*[^6].

There is added also the testimony of Cyprian, sufficiently demonstrating that he remained in communion with them, by saying, *Judging no one, nor removing anyone from the right of communion if he should think differently.* For it was they who thought differently, whom this man calls betrayers, namely because they supported heretics by receiving them into the Church without baptism. That this too was the custom, that they should be so received, both Cyprian himself in many places, and several bishops in this council, make plain. Whence it appears that, if heretics do not have baptism, the Church of Christ of those times was full of betrayers, who, supporting them, received them in this way.

Let the common cause, then, be pleaded against the crime of betrayal which they cannot deny, and there our cause will be pleaded against the crime of treachery which they could not prove. But let us plead as though they had proved it: what we shall both answer to those who object to us the betrayal of our forebears, this we shall answer to these men who object to us the treachery of our forebears. For if, through the treachery of our forebears, from whom these men divided themselves, we are dead, then likewise, through the betrayal of the forebears who were the parents both of us and of them, both we and they are dead. But because they say that they live, they believe that that betrayal does not pertain to them: then neither does that treachery pertain to us. And that betrayal indeed, according to them, is certain; but according to us, neither is the earlier betrayal true—for we say that even heretics can have the baptism of Christ—nor the later treachery, because in that cause they themselves are overcome. These men, then, have no reason to be separated from us by the wicked crime of schism: because our forebears, if they were not traitors, as we say, then it in no way pertains to us at all; but if they were traitors, as they themselves say, it pertains to us just as little as those betrayers pertain either to us or to them. And through this, since from the iniquity of our forebears there is no crime of ours, from their own schism the crime of those men is certain.

## Heretics ought to fear the judgment.

**3.** 4. Satius of Sicilibba said: *If sins are forgiven to heretics in their own baptism, they come to the Church without cause. For since on the day of judgment it is sins that are punished, there is nothing that heretics can fear concerning the judgment of Christ, if they have obtained remission of sins*[^7].

**3.** 5. This too could be our opinion; but let its author see in what spirit it was said. Yet it is so modified in its words that I am not loath to consent and subscribe to it in the spirit in which I too hold that heretics can indeed have the baptism of Christ, but do not have remission of sins. For he does not say: If heretics baptize or are baptized; but, *If sins are forgiven to heretics*, he says, *in their own baptism, they come to the Church without cause.*

For let us put, in place of heretics, those whom Cyprian knew in the Church, renouncing the world in words only and not in deeds[^8]; in as many words we too can most truly state this opinion: If sins are forgiven, in their own baptism, to those falsely converted, they are afterward led to a true conversion without cause. For since on the day of judgment it is sins that are punished, there is nothing that those who renounced the world in words only and not in deeds can fear concerning the judgment of Christ, if they have obtained remission of sins.

But this reasoning is completed by such a connection that we add, saying: But they ought to fear the judgment of Christ, and at some time be converted with a true heart; and when they have done this, it is certainly not necessary that they be baptized again. They were able, therefore, to receive baptism and not receive remission of sins, or, their sins remitted, at once to be burdened again. So, then, are heretics too.

## To rebaptize is against reason.

**4.** 6. Victor of Gor said: *Since sins are remitted only in the baptism of the Church, whoever admits a heretic to communion without baptism does both things against reason: he neither purifies heretics nor stains Christians*[^9].

## The baptism of the Church is among heretics.

**4.** 7. To him we answer that the baptism of the Church is even among heretics, although they themselves are not in the Church: just as the water of Paradise is in the land of Egypt, although it is not in Paradise. We do not, therefore, admit heretics to communion without baptism; and because they come corrected from their perversity, we receive not their sins, but the sacraments of Christ; and concerning the remission of sins, what we said above, we say here too. As for what he put at the end, saying, *he does both things against reason: he neither cleanses heretics nor stains Christians*, this Cyprian himself, with his colleagues who agreed with him, rejected first and most of all; for he did not believe himself to be stained, when he decreed that such men were to be communicated with for the sake of the bond of peace, saying, *Judging no one, nor removing anyone from the right of communion if he should think differently*[^10].

Or, if heretics admitted to communion, if they are not baptized, stain it, then the whole Church was stained through that custom which is so often recalled here. And just as these men call us traitors on account of our forebears, in whom they could prove nothing of the kind when they made the charge; if everyone becomes what he is with whom he communicates, then all became heretics at that time. But if anyone says this, he is mad; therefore what this man says is false: *Whoever admits a heretic to communion without baptism neither purifies heretics nor stains Christians.* Or, if this is true, they were not admitted without baptism, but had the baptism of Christ, although given and received among heretics—those who, according to that custom which these men confess, were so admitted: and through this, rightly even now are they so admitted.

## To baptize heretics is to communicate with them.

**5.** 8. Aurelius of Utica said: *Since the Apostle says that one must not communicate with another's sins*[^11], *what else does he do but communicate with another's sins, who communicates with heretics without the baptism of the Church? And therefore I judge that heretics are to be baptized, that they may receive remission of sins, and so be communicated with*[^12].

**5.** 9. It is answered: Then Cyprian and all these men communicated with another's sins, because they remained in communion with such people, when they removed from the right of communion no one who thought differently[^13]. Where, then, is the Church? Then, to be silent about heretics—since the words of this opinion can be said also of other sinners, such as Cyprian saw, groaned over, reproved, and bore with among his own people in the Church[^14]—where is the Church, which, according to these words, is thought already then to have perished by the contagion of sins?

But if, as the most firm truth holds, the Church remained and remains, then the communion of sins which the Apostle forbids[^15] is to be understood only as consent to them. But let heretics be baptized again, that they may receive remission of sins, if the perverse and envious are baptized again—who, while they renounced the world in words and not in deeds[^16], were able indeed to receive baptism, but did not obtain remission of sins, the Lord saying, *If you do not forgive, neither will your Father forgive you*[^17].

## To approve heretical baptism is to disapprove our own.

**6.** 10. Iambus of Germaniciana said: *Those who approve the baptism of heretics disapprove our own; so that they deny that those outside the Church—I will not say washed, but defiled—ought to be baptized in the Church*[^18].

## Baptism is neither the heretics' nor ours, but Christ's.

**6.** 11. To him it is answered that none of us approves the baptism of heretics, but that of Christ, though among heretics as among outer chaff, just as among other iniquitous men as among inner chaff. For if those who are baptized outside the Church are not washed, but defiled, then assuredly those who are baptized outside the rock, on which the Church is built, are not washed, but defiled. But all are outside that same rock who hear the words of Christ and do not do them. Or, if they are indeed washed by baptism, but remain defiled by their iniquities, from which they were unwilling to be changed for the better: so too are heretics.

## Light cannot agree with darkness.

**7.** 12. Lucianus of Rucuma said: *It is written: " And God saw the light, that it was good, and divided between the light and the darkness "*[^19]. *If light and darkness can agree, there can be something in common between us and heretics. Therefore I judge that heretics are to be baptized*[^20].

## This holds of all the unrighteous.

**7.** 13. To him it is answered: If light and darkness can agree, there can be something in common between the righteous and the unrighteous. Let him, then, judge that those unrighteous men are to be baptized anew whom Cyprian reproved in the Church itself; or, if those are not unrighteous who renounce the world in words and not in deeds[^21], let him say so who can.

## Heresy is not the Church.

**8.** 14. Pelagianus of Luperciana said: *It is written: " Either God is God, or Baal is God "*[^22]. *So now too, either the Church is the Church, or heresy is the Church: now if heresy is not the Church, how can the baptism of the Church be among heretics?*[^23]

**8.** 15. To him we can answer thus: Either Paradise is Paradise, or Egypt is Paradise: now if Egypt is not Paradise, how can the water of Paradise be in Egypt? But it will be said to us: It reaches there too by flowing out. So, then, does baptism reach heretics. Likewise we say: Either the rock is the Church, or the sand is the Church. Now, because the sand is not the Church, how can baptism be among those who build upon the sand, by hearing the words of Christ and not doing them[^24]? And yet it is. So, then, is it also among heretics.

## Baptism is in the Catholic Church alone.

**9.** 16. Iader of Midila said[^25]: *We know that there is but one baptism in the Catholic Church; and therefore we ought not to admit a heretic unless he has been baptized among us, lest he think himself baptized outside the Catholic Church.*

**9.** 17. To him it is answered that, if this were said of those iniquitous men who are outside the rock, it would certainly be said falsely. So, then, is it of heretics.

**10.** 18. Likewise Felix of Marazana said[^26]: *One faith, one baptism*[^27]; *but of the Catholic Church, to which alone it is permitted to baptize.*

**10.** 19. To him it is answered: What if another were to say thus: One faith, one baptism; but of the righteous only, to whom alone it is permitted to baptize? Just as these words would be refuted, so let that opinion be refuted. Or do the unrighteous too, not changed even in baptism itself, while they renounce the world in words only and not in deeds[^28], belong to the members of the Church? Let them see whether she is that rock, whether she is that dove, whether she is that bride without spot and wrinkle[^29].

## Whether those who do not believe God have baptism.

**11.** 20. Paulus of Obba said: *It does not move me if someone does not vindicate the faith and truth of the Church, since the Apostle says, " For what? Because some of them have fallen away from the faith, has their faithlessness made void the faith of God? By no means: for God is truthful, but every man a liar "*[^30]. *But if God is truthful, how can the truth of baptism be among heretics, among whom God is not?*[^31]

**11.** 21. To him it is answered: What? Is God among the avaricious? And yet there is baptism: so too among heretics. For those among whom God is, are the temple of God[^32]. *But what agreement has the temple of God with idols?*[^33] Further, that avarice is idolatry Paul holds[^34], Cyprian agrees[^35]; and that same Cyprian dwells, with the great reward of his endurance, among rapacious colleagues who nevertheless baptized.

## Heretics and sinners do not give remission of sins.

**12.** 22. Pomponius of Dionysiana said[^36]: *It is manifest that heretics cannot baptize and give remission of sins, since they do not have the power either to loose or to bind anything on earth*[^37].

**12.** 23. It is answered: Neither do murderers have this power, that is, those who hate the brothers[^38]. For it was not to such men that it was said, *If you forgive anyone their sins, they will be forgiven him; if you retain anyone, they will be retained*[^39]. And yet they baptize, and Paul tolerates them[^40] in the same communion of baptism, and Cyprian recognizes them[^41].

## To have baptism in common with heretics is to contaminate the Bride of Christ.

**13.** 24. Venantius of Tinisa[^42] said: *If a husband, setting out abroad*[^43], *had entrusted his wife to his friend to be kept, that friend would preserve her, entrusted to him, with all the diligence he could, lest her chastity and holiness be adulterated by anyone. Christ, our Lord and God, setting out to the Father, entrusted his bride to us; do we keep her uncorrupted and inviolate, or do we betray her integrity and chastity to adulterers and corrupters? For whoever makes the baptism of the Church common with heretics betrays the bride of Christ to adulterers.*

**13.** 25. We answer: What of those who, when they are baptized, are converted to God with the lips, not the heart—do they not bear an adulterous mind? Are they not themselves lovers of the world, who renounced it not in deeds but in words[^44], and therefore corrupt good morals by evil conversations[^45], saying, *Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we shall die*[^46]? Was not the apostolic word watchful against these too, where it says, *But I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his cunning, so your minds too may be corrupted from the chastity that is in Christ*[^47]? Since, then, Cyprian had the baptism of Christ in common with such men, was he therefore betraying the bride of Christ to adulterers, and not rather recognizing the bridegroom's necklace[^48] even on an adulteress?

## There are not two baptisms.

**14.** 26. Aymnus of Ausuagiga said: *We have received one baptism, and that we practice; but whoever says that it is permitted to heretics too to baptize, he himself makes two baptisms*[^49].

**14.** 27. To him it is answered: Why does not he too make two baptisms who says that the iniquitous also baptize? For since the righteous and the iniquitous are contrary to one another, the baptism which the righteous give—such as Paul was, or such as Cyprian was—is not contrary to the baptism which those iniquitous men gave who hated Paul[^50], whom Cyprian understands to be not heretics but wicked Catholics[^51]: and since the continence which was in Cyprian and the avarice which was in Cyprian's colleagues are contrary to one another, nevertheless the baptism which Cyprian gave was not contrary to the baptism which they gave, but one and the same: because he baptizes of whom it was said, *This is he who baptizes*[^52].

## If heretics baptize, they are excused.

**15.** 28. Saturninus of Victoriana[^53] said: *If heretics are permitted to baptize, they are excused and defended in doing unlawful things; nor do I see why either Christ called them his adversaries, or the Apostle called them antichrists*[^54].

**15.** 29. To him it is answered: We say that it is not permitted to heretics to baptize in the same way that we say it is not permitted to cheats to baptize. For not to a heretic only, but to a sinner, the Lord says, *Why do you recount my justifications, and take up my covenant in your mouth?*[^55]—to this one certainly he said, *If you saw a thief, you ran along with him*[^56]. How much worse, then, were those who did not run along with thieves, but themselves seized estates by treacherous frauds[^57]? But Cyprian did not run along with them, although he tolerated them in the Catholic crop, lest the wheat be uprooted along with them. And yet even the baptism which they gave was the same; because it was not theirs, but Christ's. As, therefore, these men, although the baptism of Christ is recognized in them, are nevertheless not excused and defended in doing unlawful things, and Christ rightly calls them his adversaries, because, persevering in such things, they are to hear, *Depart from me, you who work iniquity*[^58]—whence they are called antichrists, because they are contrary to Christ, while they live against what he commands: so too are heretics.

## The example of Marcion.

**16.** 30. Another Saturninus, of Tucca, said[^59]: *The Gentiles, although they worship idols, nevertheless know and confess the most high God, the Father, the Creator. Against him Marcion blasphemes, and some are not ashamed to approve the baptism of Marcion*[^60]. *How do such priests either keep or vindicate the priesthood of God, who do not baptize the enemies of God, and thus communicate with them?*

**16.** 31. It is answered: Indeed, when such things are said, the measure is exceeded, and it is not considered that they themselves too communicated with such people, *judging no one, nor removing anyone from the right of communion if they thought differently*[^61]. But this man has, in this very opinion of his, something by which he might be admonished, if he noticed, that in each person what is depraved is to be corrected, but what is upright is to be approved: since he says, *The Gentiles, although they worship idols, nevertheless know and confess the most high God, the Father, the Creator.*

If, then, such a Gentile came to him, would he wish to correct and change in him the fact that he confessed and knew God the Father, the Creator? But he would amend in him idolatry, the evil he had; and would hand on the Christian sacraments, which he did not have: and if he knew anything upright in him, he would approve it; if he found anything perverse, he would amend it; if anything was lacking, he would give it. So too in a Marcionist heretic he would recognize the integrity of baptism, correct his perversity, teach the Catholic truth.

## Not to baptize heretics is to communicate with a sinner.

**17.** 32. Marcellus of Zama said: *Since sins are remitted only in the baptism of the Church, whoever does not baptize a heretic communicates with a sinner*[^62].

**17.** 33. What of him who communicates with the one doing this—does he not communicate with a sinner? But what else did all do who judged no one, nor removed from the right of communion one thinking differently[^63]? Where, then, is the Church? Or do these things not harm those who are patient and who tolerate the tares lest the wheat be uprooted[^64]? Let these men learn, then, who, by vainly separating themselves from the whole world, committed the sacrilege of schism, why they have in their mouth the opinion of Cyprian who do not have in their heart the patience of Cyprian. But to this Marcellus it is answered, from what was said above about baptism and about remission of sins, how baptism can be in a man, although remission of sins is not in him.

## If one is not rebaptized, the heresy is greater.

**18.** 34. Irenaeus of Ululi said: *If the Church does not baptize a heretic for the reason that he is said to be already baptized, the heresy is greater*[^65].

**18.** 35. It is answered: In this way it could be said: If the Church does not baptize an avaricious man for the reason that he is said to be already baptized, the avarice is greater. But this is false: so, then, is that.

## Let one who does not rebaptize heretics prove that the Church is in heresy.

**19.** 36. Donatus of Cibaliana said: *I know one Church and one baptism of hers. If there is one who says that the grace of baptism is among heretics, it is first necessary that he show and prove that the Church is there*[^66].

**19.** 37. To him it is answered: If you say that the grace of baptism is that which baptism is, it is among heretics; but if baptism is the sacrament of grace, while grace itself is the abolition of sins, then the grace of baptism is not among heretics. But baptism is one and the Church is one in the same way that hope is one[^67]. As, therefore, the good and the wicked, not having one hope, can nevertheless have one baptism, so those for whom the Church is not common can have baptism in common.

## Peter is to be imitated.

**20.** 38. Zosimus of Tharassa[^68] said: *The truth having been revealed, let error yield to the truth: for Peter too, who at first circumcised, yielded to Paul preaching the truth*[^69].

**20.** 39. It is answered: This too can be our opinion, and so it happened in this question about baptism. For when the truth was afterward revealed more clearly, error yielded to the truth, when that most wholesome custom was confirmed even by the authority of a plenary council. Yet it is well that they so often remembered that even Peter, the first of the Apostles, could think otherwise than the truth required; which we believe happened to Cyprian too, without any reproach to him—whoever of us love Cyprian: because it is not right that he be loved with a greater love than Peter.

## Heresy is not from heaven.

**21.** 40. Julianus of Telepte[^70] said: *It is written: No one can receive anything, unless it has been given him from heaven*[^71]. *If heresy is from heaven, it can give baptism.*

**21.** 41. Let him hear another saying too: If avarice is from heaven, it can give baptism. And yet the avaricious do give it: so, then, do heretics.

## Do not make the heretic a Christian.

**22.** 42. Faustus of Timida Regia said[^72]: *Let those who patronize heretics not flatter themselves. Whoever intercedes for heretics by ecclesiastical baptism makes them Christians and us heretics.*

**22.** 43. To him it is answered: If anyone should say that one who, when he received baptism, did not receive remission of sins because he held fraternal hatred in his heart, is not to be baptized again when he dismisses that hatred from his heart—does he then intercede by ecclesiastical baptism for murderers, or make them righteous and us murderers? Let him, then, understand this of heretics too.

## Some set heretics above bishops.

**23.** 44. Geminius of Furni said[^73]: *Some of our colleagues can set heretics above themselves; they cannot set them above us: and therefore what we have once decreed, we hold; that we baptize those coming from heretics.*

**23.** 45. And here he confesses most plainly that some of his colleagues thought differently: whence the love of unity is confirmed again and again, in that they were not separated from one another by any schism, until to one of the two parties, if they thought anything otherwise, God should reveal it[^74]. But to him it is answered that his colleagues did not set heretics above themselves; rather, they recognized the baptism of Christ, just as it is recognized in the avaricious, in cheats, in the rapacious, in murderers, so also in heretics.

## The synagogue of Satan does not have baptism.

**24.** 46. Rogatianus of Nova said[^75]: *Christ instituted the Church, the devil heresy: how can the synagogue of Satan have the baptism of Christ?*

**24.** 47. To him it is answered: Because Christ instituted the devout, and the devil the envious, can it therefore not have the baptism of Christ, which, in the envious, is convicted of being a part of the devil?

## A comparison with Judas.

**25.** 48. Therapius of Bulla said[^76]: *Whoever grants and betrays the baptism of the Church to heretics, what else does he stand forth as but a Judas to the bride of Christ?*

**25.** 49. O great conviction of the schismatics, who separated themselves by a wicked sacrilege from the inheritance of Christ spread throughout the whole world! For if Cyprian communicated with such men as the traitor Judas was, and yet was not stained by them; or, if he was stained, then all became Judases; therefore even now all are Judases; or, if they are not, then the crimes of the former do not pertain to those who came after, although propagated from the same communion.

Why, then, do they object to us traitors whom they have not convicted, and not object to themselves Judas, with whom Cyprian and his colleagues communicated? Behold the council in which these men are wont to glory. We indeed say that he who approves the baptism of Christ even in heretics does not thereby betray the baptism of the Church to heretics, just as he who approves the baptism of Christ even in murderers does not betray the baptism of the Church to murderers; but because these men, from this council, aim to prescribe to us what we ought to think, let them themselves first agree with it.

Behold, those were compared to the traitor Judas who said that heretics, although baptized in heresy, ought not to be baptized again. But Cyprian communicated with such men, who said, *Judging no one, nor removing anyone from the right of communion if he should think differently*[^77]. That such men had also been in the Church before is made plain by that opinion where he says, *But someone says: What then will become of those who in the past were admitted into the Church without baptism?*[^78] That such was the custom of the Church, they themselves, who hold this council, often recall. If, then, *whoever does this stands forth as nothing else but a Judas to the bride of Christ*, as that opinion was pronounced—and Judas, as the Gospel teaches, was a traitor—then all communicated with traitors who at that time said these things too, and, before they said these things, all had become traitors through that custom which the Church then retained. All, then—that is, both we and they, because we are propagated from that unity—are traitors.

But we are defended in two ways: both because we do not consent to this council, where this was pronounced, with the right of unity preserved, as Cyprian himself foretold; and because we judge that the wicked in Catholic unity do no harm to the good, until at the end the chaff is separated from the wheat. But these men, because they both bring forth this council as though on their own behalf, and contend that the good perish by communion with the wicked as though by contagion, find no way to say either that the earlier Christians, from whom they are propagated, were not traitors—because they are convicted by this council—or that the crimes of the earlier ones do not pertain to them—because they object to us our forebears.

## God does not hear a sinner.

**26.** 50. Likewise another Lucius, of Membresa, said[^79]: *It is written, " God does not hear a sinner "*[^80]. *One who is a sinner, how can he be heard in baptism?*

**26.** 51. We answer: How is the avaricious man heard, and the rapacious, and the usurer, and the murderer? Or are these not sinners?—whom nevertheless Cyprian rebukes and tolerates in the Catholic Church[^81].

## Custom is not to be set before the truth.

**27.** 52. Likewise another Felix, of Buslaceni, said[^82]: *In admitting heretics without the baptism of the Church, let no one set custom before reason and truth; for reason and truth always exclude custom.*

**27.** 53. To this the reply is: you do not show the truth, but you confess the custom. We would therefore rightly hold to the custom, confirmed by a later plenary council, even if the truth were still hidden—the truth that we believe has now been made manifest.

## Antichrists cannot rebaptize.

**28.** 54. Another, Saturninus of Abitinae, said[^83]: *If antichrist can give anyone the grace of Christ, then heretics too can baptize, for they have been called antichrists.*

**28.** 55. What if someone else were to say: if a murderer can give anyone the grace of Christ, then those too who hate their brothers can baptize, for they have been called murderers[^84]? Surely it would seem he was speaking the truth, and yet they can; so likewise can the heretics.

## Heretics do not have baptism.

**29.** 56. Quintus of Aggya said[^85]: *He can give something who has something; but what can the heretics give, who, as is agreed, have nothing?*

**29.** 57. To this the reply is: if, then, he can give something who has something, it is clear that heretics can give baptism, because when they withdraw from the Church they keep the sacrament of the washing that they had received there; for on returning they do not receive it again, since they had not lost it when they withdrew.

## No one can serve two masters.

**30.** 58. Another, Julian of Marcelliana, said[^86]: *If a man can serve two masters, God and mammon*[^87], *then baptism too can serve two, the Christian and the heretic.*

**30.** 59. On the contrary, if it can serve the self-controlled and the greedy, the sober and the drunkard, the devout and the murderer, why not also the Christian and the heretic? It does not in fact serve them; rather it is administered to them, or by them—to salvation for those who use it well, and to judgment for those who use it badly.

## Where the Church is not, there is no baptism.

**31.** 60. Tenax of Horrea Celiae said[^88]: *There is one baptism, but it belongs to the Church; where the Church is not, there baptism cannot be.*

**31.** 61. To this we reply: why, then, can it be where there is no rock but only sand, since the Church is on the rock and not on the sand?

## Heretics do not have God and Christ.

**32.** 62. Another, Victor of Assuras, said[^89]: *It is written that God is one, and Christ one, and the Church one, and baptism one*[^90]. *How can anyone be baptized there where there is no God and Christ and Church?*

**32.** 63. How can he be baptized even on that sand, where there is neither the Church, since it is on the rock, nor God and Christ, since the temple of God and Christ is not there?

## Outside the Church there is no salvation.

**33.** 64. Donatulus of Capsa said[^91]: *I too have always held this, that heretics, who have obtained nothing outside, should be baptized when they are converted to the Church.*

**33.** 65. To this the reply is: outside they have indeed obtained nothing toward salvation, but not nothing toward the sacrament. For salvation is proper to the good, whereas the sacraments are common to the good and the bad.

## A heretic cannot give what he does not have.

**34.** 66. Verulus of Rusicade said[^92]: *A heretical man cannot give what he does not have; much less can a schismatic, who has lost what he had.*

**34.** 67. We have already shown that they have it, because they do not lose it when they withdraw. For they do not receive it again when they return. Therefore, if they seemed unable to give precisely because they were thought not to have it, let them now be understood to be able to give, because they are understood also to have.

## Heretics can have nothing.

**35.** 68. Pudentianus of Cuicul said[^93]: *The newness of my episcopate, dearly beloved brothers, has made me wait to see what those of greater standing would judge. For it is clear that heresies have nothing and can do nothing; and so, if any come from them, it has most justly been decreed that they be baptized.*

**35.** 69. As the reply was given to those above, whom he was here waiting upon to see what they would judge, let it be understood that the same reply has been given to this man too.

## Heretics coming to the Church ought to be baptized.

**36.** 70. Peter of Hippo Diarrhytus said[^94]: *Since there is one baptism in the Catholic Church, it is clear that one cannot be baptized outside the Church; and therefore I judge that those dipped in heresy, or in schism, when they come to the Church, ought to be baptized.*

**36.** 71. The one baptism is so in the Catholic Church that, when some have gone out from it, in those who go out it does not become two, but the same one remains. What, then, is recognized in those who return is recognized also in those who received it from those who withdrew—the very thing that those who withdrew did not lose when they withdrew.

## Whatever heretics do ought to be annulled.

**37.** 72. Again, another, Lucius of Ausafa, said[^95]: *According to the prompting of my mind and of the Holy Spirit, since there is one God the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, and one Christ, and one hope, one Spirit, one Church, there ought also to be one baptism*[^96]. *And therefore I say that whatever has been set in motion or done among heretics ought to be annulled, and that those who come from there are to be baptized in the Church.*

**37.** 73. Let what they baptize, then, be annulled—those who hear the words of God and do not do them[^97]—when they begin to pass over from iniquity to righteousness, that is, from the sand to the rock. But if this is not done, because in them too what was Christ's was not violated by their iniquities, let this be understood of the heretics as well. For neither do they have the same hope, so long as they are on the sand, as those who are upon the rock; yet the baptism is the same in both, although, just as the hope is called one, so too the baptism is called one.

## The baptism of heretics does not give grace.

**38.** 74. Felix of Gurgites said[^98]: *I judge that, according to the precepts of the holy Scriptures, those unlawfully baptized by heretics outside the Church, if they wish to take refuge in the Church, should obtain the grace of baptism where it is lawfully given.*

**38.** 75. The reply is: rather, let them begin to have lawfully unto salvation what they had unlawfully unto ruin; for under the same baptism under which a person was being judged—when, in receiving it, he had renounced the world by words only and not by deeds[^99]—he is justified when he turns to God with a truthful heart.

## The baptism of heretics is not saving.

**39.** 76. Pusillus of Lamasba said[^100]: *I believe that baptism is not saving except in the Catholic Church. Whatever is outside the Catholic Church is a sham.*

**39.** 77. This indeed is true, that *baptism is saving only in the Catholic Church.* For it can exist even outside the Catholic Church, but there it is not saving, because there it does not work salvation—just as that good fragrance of Christ is certainly not saving in those who perish[^101], not through its own fault but through theirs. *Whatever is outside the Catholic Church is a sham,* but only insofar as it is not catholic. Yet something catholic can exist outside the Catholic Church, just as the name of Christ could exist outside the assembly of Christ—the name in which that man who did not follow along with the disciples cast out demons[^102]. For a sham can exist even within the Catholic Church, namely in those who renounce the world by words and not by deeds[^103], and yet the sham is not catholic. As, therefore, there is within the Catholic Church what is not catholic, so there can be outside the Catholic Church something that is catholic.

## Heretics have nothing.

**40.** 78. Salvianus of Gazaufala said[^104]: *It is agreed that heretics have nothing; and therefore they come to us, so that they may be able to receive what they did not have.*

**40.** 79. We reply: then those very men who founded the heresies are not heretics, because they withdrew from the Church and certainly had what they had received there. But if it is absurd to say that those are not heretics through whom the rest become heretics, then it can happen that a heretic has something which, by using it badly, he ruins.

## Truth, not custom, is to be followed.

**41.** 80. Honoratus of Tucca said[^105]: *Since Christ is the truth, we ought to follow the truth rather than custom; so that we may sanctify with the Church's baptism the heretics who come to us precisely because outside they could receive nothing.*

**41.** 81. This man too is a witness to the custom, where he helps us most, whatever else he may seem to say against us. But it is not because they received nothing outside that the heretics come to us; rather, they come so that what they received may begin to be of use to them—for outside it can be of use in no way.

## The counsel of a new bishop.

**42.** 82. Victor of Octavum said[^106]: *As you yourselves also know, I was not appointed bishop long ago, and so I was awaiting the counsel of my predecessors. I therefore judge thus: that whoever come from heresy should without doubt be baptized.*

**42.** 83. Let the reply that was given to those whom he was awaiting be taken as given to this man as well.

## Bishops do what the Apostles did.

**43.** 84. Clarus of Mascula said[^107]: *Plain is the sentence of our Lord Jesus Christ, who sent his Apostles and entrusted to them alone the power given to him by the Father—to whom we have succeeded, governing the Lord's Church with the same power and baptizing the faith of believers. And therefore heretics, who have neither power outside nor the Church of Christ, can baptize no one with his baptism.*

**43.** 85. Have ungodly murderers too succeeded the Apostles? Why, then, do they baptize? Is it because they are not outside? But they are outside the rock, to which the Lord gave the keys and where he said he would build his Church[^108].

## We ought not to deceive heretics.

**44.** 86. Secundianus of Thambei said[^109]: *We ought not by our presumption to deceive heretics, lest, not having been baptized in the Church of our Lord Jesus Christ and so not having obtained the forgiveness of sins, when the day of judgment comes they charge against us that through us they were not baptized and did not obtain the indulgence of divine grace. For this reason, since there is one Church and one baptism, when they are converted to us, let them obtain together with the Church both the Church and the baptism of the Church.*

**44.** 87. On the contrary, let them, transferred to the rock and joined to the dove, receive the forgiveness of sins, which they could not have outside the rock and outside the dove—whether openly outside, like the heretics, or as though within, like ruined Catholics; who nonetheless, as is clear, both have and give baptism without the forgiveness of sins, since even from such men those receive it who, not changed for the better, honor God with their lips while their heart is far from him[^110]. Yet the baptism is one in the same way that the dove is one, since those who are not in the dove in fellowship can still have baptism in common.

## We ought not to admit heretics into the house of God without baptism.

**45.** 88. Again, another, Aurelius of Cillae, said[^111]: *The apostle John set down in his Epistle: 'If anyone comes to you and does not have the doctrine of Christ, do not receive him into your house, and do not say to him, Greetings. For whoever says to him, Greetings, shares in his evil deeds'*[^112]. *How can such men be rashly admitted into the house of God, who are forbidden to be admitted into our own private house? Or how can we have communion with them without the baptism of the Church—men with whom, if we so much as say Greetings, we share in their evil deeds?*

**45.** 89. On this testimony of John there is no need to dwell long, since it does not pertain at all to the question of baptism that we are now treating. For he says: *If anyone comes to you and does not have the doctrine of Christ*[^113]. But heretics, leaving behind the doctrine of their error, are converted to the doctrine of Christ, so as to be incorporated into the Church and to begin to belong to that dove whose sacrament they had; and so what was not hers they are given—namely, peace, and love from a pure heart, and a good conscience, and faith unfeigned[^114]. But what was hers is recognized in them and received without any reproach—just as in the adulteress God recognizes his own gifts, even while she follows her lovers; for when her fornication is corrected, when she is converted to chastity, those gifts are not blamed, but she herself is set right[^115].

But just as Cyprian could defend himself, if this testimony of John were brought against him while he was in communion with such men, so let those defend themselves against whom it is alleged. For, as I have said, it does not pertain at all to this question. John says that those who hold an alien doctrine are not to be greeted; but the apostle Paul says still more forcefully: *If anyone who bears the name of brother among you is either greedy or a drunkard,* and so on, *not even to eat with such a one*[^116]—and yet with colleagues who were moneylenders, treacherous, defrauders, and robbers, Cyprian shared not a private table but the common altar of God. How this is to be defended has already been said sufficiently in other books.

## Heretics cannot give light.

**46.** 90. Litteus of Gemellae said[^117]: *If a blind man leads a blind man, both fall into the pit*[^118]. *Since, then, it is agreed that heretics cannot give light to anyone, being themselves blind, their baptism is of no force.*

**46.** 91. Nor do we say that it has force unto salvation so long as they are heretics, just as we do not say it of those murderers so long as they hate their brothers[^119]; for they too are in darkness, and if anyone follows them, they fall together into the pit. Yet not for that reason do they either fail to have baptism or fail to hand it on.

## Heretics cannot be in communion with us.

**47.** 92. Natalis of Oea said[^120]: *I, here present, as well as Pompeius of Sabrata and also Dioga of Leptis Magna, who have charged me to speak for them, absent in body but present in spirit*[^121], *judge what our colleagues also judge: that heretics cannot have communion with us unless they have been baptized with the baptism of the Church.*

**47.** 93. By communion, I believe, he means that which pertains to the fellowship of the dove; for in the sharing of the sacraments they were beyond doubt in communion with them, judging no one, nor removing anyone from the right of communion if he held a different view[^122]. But however he meant it, these words need not be greatly refuted. For certainly a heretic does not have communion unless baptized with the baptism of the Church. And it is agreed that the baptism is ecclesiastical even among heretics when it is consecrated with the words of the gospel—just as the Gospel itself is ecclesiastical and does not belong to their perversity, but certainly retains its own holiness.

## Heretics are to be baptized.

**48.** 94. Junius of Neapolis said[^123]: *From what I have once judged I do not withdraw, namely that we should baptize heretics coming to the Church.*

**48.** 95. Since here he brought forward no reasoning or testimony of the Scriptures, he need not detain us long.

## The opinion of holy Cyprian.

**44.** 96. Cyprian of Carthage said[^124]: *My view is most fully expressed by the letter that was written to our colleague Jubaianus—that heretics, called, according to the gospel and apostolic testimony, adversaries of Christ*[^125] *and antichrists*[^126], *when they come to the Church, are to be baptized with the one baptism of the Church, so that they may be made friends out of adversaries, and Christians out of antichrists.*

## The opinion of holy Cyprian, continued

**44.** 97. What else is to be argued here, when we have treated with all the diligence we could the very letter he mentioned, the one sent to Jubaianus? And let us remember that what he said here can be said of all the wicked—who, as he himself testifies, are even within the Catholic Church and both have and give baptism, as none of us doubts. For those come to the Church who cross over from the party of the devil to Christ, and build upon the rock, and are incorporated into the dove, and are kept safe in the enclosed garden and the sealed fountain[^127]; where there are not all those who live against the precepts of Christ, wherever they may seem to be. For in the letter he wrote to Magnus, when dealing with this very matter, he himself plainly enough warned us in what sort of fellowship the Church should be understood to consist. For he says, speaking of a certain man: *Let him become a stranger and profane to the Lord's peace, and an enemy of the Lord's unity, not dwelling in the house of God, that is, in the Church of Christ, in which none dwell but the harmonious and like-minded*[^128].

Let those, then, who would prescribe to us on the authority of Cyprian attend a little to what we are to say here. For if in the Church of Christ none dwell but the harmonious and like-minded, then beyond doubt those did not dwell in the Church of Christ—although they seemed to be within—who through envy and contention, without love, were proclaiming Christ; whom he himself understands[^129] to be not the heretics or schismatics mentioned by the apostle Paul, but false brothers living inwardly among them. These certainly ought not to baptize, since they did not dwell in the Church[^130], in which he himself says none dwell but the harmonious and like-minded—unless perhaps someone so shrinks from the truth as to say that the envious and ill-willed and the quarrelsome who lack love were harmonious and like-minded. And yet they baptized, nor did this detestable perversity of theirs in any way diminish or violate the sacrament of Christ, which was handled and dispensed through them.

**50.** 98. It is worth our while, indeed, to treat the whole passage set down in that same letter to Magnus, which he framed thus. *Not dwelling,* he says, *in the house of God, that is, in the Church of Christ, in which none dwell but the harmonious and like-minded—since the Holy Spirit speaks in the Psalms and says: 'God who makes the like-minded to dwell in a house'*[^131]. *Finally, the Lord's own sacrifices declare that Christians are bound to one another in like-mindedness by a firm and inseparable love. For when the Lord calls his body bread*[^132], *made up out of the gathering together of many grains, he indicates our people, whom he bore, made one; and when he calls his blood wine*[^133], *pressed out of many clusters and grapes and brought together into one, he likewise signifies our flock joined by the mingling of a multitude made one*[^134].

These words of blessed Cyprian show that he both understood and loved the beauty of the house of God[^135], which house he affirmed consists of the like-minded and harmonious, and which he taught both by prophetic testimony and by the signification of the sacraments. In that house certainly there were not those envious and loveless ill-willed men, who nonetheless baptized. From which it appears that the sacrament of Christ can be in them, and can be given by them, who are not in the Church of Christ, in which Cyprian himself testifies that none dwell but the like-minded and harmonious. For it cannot even be said that they could baptize so long as they were hidden, since those men were not hidden from the apostle Paul, whom in his Epistle that most truthful witness marks out, and says that he rejoices because they too were proclaiming Christ. For of them he says: *Whether in pretense or in truth, Christ is proclaimed; and in this I rejoice, yes, and I will rejoice*[^136].

## What the true Church is.

**51.** 99. Having considered all this, I think I may say without rashness that some are in the house of God in such a way that they themselves are also that same house of God, which is said to be built upon the rock[^137], which is called the one dove[^138], which is the beautiful bride without spot or wrinkle[^139], and the enclosed garden, the sealed fountain, the well of living water, the paradise with the fruit of its trees[^140]—the house that also received the keys, and the power of loosing and binding[^141]. If anyone despises this house when it rebukes and corrects him: *Let him be to you,* he says, *as a Gentile and a tax collector*[^142]. Of this house it is said: *O Lord, I have loved the beauty of your house, and the place where your glory dwells*[^143]; and: *Who makes the like-minded to dwell in a house*[^144]; and: *I was glad in those who said to me, We will go into the house of the Lord*[^145]; and: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; for ages of ages they will praise you*[^146]; and countless such passages.

This house is also called the wheat, bearing fruit—thirtyfold, or sixtyfold, or a hundredfold—with patient endurance[^147]. This house is in vessels of gold and silver[^148], and precious stones, and imperishable wood. To this house it is said: *Bearing with one another in love, eager to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace*[^149]; and: *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^150]. For this house exists in the good faithful and the holy servants of God dispersed everywhere and bound together in spiritual unity in the same communion of the sacraments, whether they know one another by sight or do not.

But others, I say, are in the house in such a way that they do not belong to the structure of the house, nor to the fellowship of its fruitful and peaceful righteousness[^151]; rather they are, as it were, the chaff among the grain. For we cannot deny that these too are in the house, since the Apostle says: *In a great house there are not only vessels of gold and silver, but also of wood and clay; and some are for honor, but others for dishonor*[^152]. Out of this innumerable number not only does a crowd within press upon the heart of the few who are holy by comparison with so great a multitude, but, by the breaking of the nets, heresies and schisms also arise among those who must now be said to be from the house rather than in the house, of whom it is said: *They went out from us, but they were not of us*[^153]. For those now even bodily separated are more separated than those who live inwardly in a carnal and animal way and are spiritually separated.

## Who belong to the Church.

**52.** 100. Now of all these kinds, those first ones who are in the house of God in such a way that they themselves are the house of God—whether they are already spiritual, or are still little ones nourished with milk[^154] but nonetheless advancing with intent heart toward the spiritual condition—no one doubts that they both have baptism profitably and hand it on profitably to those who imitate them;

whereas to the pretenders, whom the Holy Spirit flees[^155], even if they themselves, so far as in them lies, hand it on profitably, those nonetheless receive it unprofitably, since they do not imitate those through whom they receive it. But those who are in the great house as vessels for dishonor[^156] both have baptism unprofitably and hand it on unprofitably to those who imitate them; yet those receive it from them profitably who are joined not to them in heart and conduct but to the holy house.

But those who are more separated, being not so much in the house as from the house, neither have it profitably at all, nor is it received from them profitably—unless perhaps the necessity of receiving presses, and the mind of the one receiving does not withdraw from the bond of unity. Yet they both have it, though they have it unprofitably, and it is received from them, even when it is unprofitable to those receiving; and for this to become profitable, one must withdraw from the heresy or schism and cleave to that house. This is what not only heretics and schismatics ought to do, but also those who are in the house through the communion of the sacraments in such a way that they are outside the house through the difference of their conduct; for thus the sacrament begins to be profitable even to them, which is otherwise unprofitable.

## Certain difficulties.

**53.** 101. It is also commonly asked whether what is received from one who has not received it is to be approved, if perhaps he has learned out of some curiosity how it is to be given; and whether it makes no difference with what intention he receives to whom it is given, with pretense or without pretense:

if with pretense, whether deceiving, as in the Church, or in what is thought to be the Church, or in jest, as in a stage play; and which is the more wicked, to receive deceitfully in the Church, or in heresy or schism without deceit, that is, with an unfeigned mind; and whether to receive deceitfully in heresy, or in a stage play with faith, if anyone should in the course of the acting be moved by a sudden devotion.

And yet, if we compare such a person even with one who receives deceitfully within the Catholic Church itself, it would be strange to be in doubt which is to be preferred to the other; for I do not see what profit the mind of the one giving truthfully brings to the one receiving deceitfully. But let us suppose someone giving even deceitfully, where both the one handing it on and the one receiving act deceitfully within Catholic unity itself: is this baptism more to be accepted, or that which is given in a stage play, if there should be someone who, suddenly stirred, receives it faithfully? Or, so far as the men themselves are concerned, while there is a great difference between one believing in a stage play and one mocking in the Church, yet as to the integrity of the sacrament itself does it make no difference?

For if it makes no difference to the integrity of the sacrament within the Catholic Church itself whether some perform it deceitfully or truthfully, since both nonetheless perform this same thing, I do not see why it should make a difference outside—when the one who receives is not cloaked in pretense but changed in religion. Or do those who are truthful, among whom it is performed, avail more to confirm the sacrament than those who are deceitful, by whom and in whom it is performed, avail to frustrate it? And yet if it is afterward disclosed, no one repeats it, but that pretense is either punished by excommunication or healed by penance.

## A reply to the difficulties.

**53.** 102. But it is safe for us not to advance with any rashness of judgment in matters that have been begun in no catholic regional council and concluded in no plenary one; and yet to assert, with the confidence of a sure voice, what has been strengthened, under the governance of our Lord God and Savior Jesus Christ, by the consent of the universal Church. Nevertheless, if anyone should perhaps press me—set in a council where a question of such matters was being debated, with no such predecessors whose judgments I would rather follow—to say what I myself thought: if I were disposed as I was when I was dictating these things, I would by no means doubt that those have baptism who, wherever and from whomever, had received it consecrated with the words of the gospel, without pretense on their own part and with some faith—although it would not profit them unto spiritual salvation if they lacked the love by which they would be grafted into the Catholic Church. For *if I have faith,* he says, *so as to move mountains, but have not love, I am nothing*[^157].

As by the past decrees of those of greater standing, I do not doubt that those too have baptism who, though they receive it deceitfully, nonetheless receive it in the Church, or where it is thought to be the Church by those in whose fellowship it is received—of whom it has been said: *They went out from us*[^158]. But where there is neither any fellowship of those so believing, nor would the one who received it there himself so believe, but the whole thing were carried on in sport and mimicry and jest, as to whether the baptism so given should be approved, I would judge that the divine judgment must be implored through the oracle of some revelation, with harmonious prayer and with groanings of suppliant devotion lavishly poured out—yet in such a way that I would humbly await those who were to give their opinions after me, lest they bring forward something already examined and known. How much more, then, am I now to be taken as having said this without prejudice to a more diligent inquiry or a greater authority?

## The conclusion of the work.

**54.** 103. But now, as I think, it is time to bring even these books on the question of baptism to their due conclusion. In them our Lord God has shown us, through the peaceable bishop Cyprian and those who agreed with him, how greatly catholic unity is to be loved—so that in the matter on which they thought otherwise, until God should reveal that too[^159], they would rather bear with those who held a different view than separate themselves from them by a wicked schism. By this the mouths of the Donatists are utterly shut, even if we say nothing of the Maximianists.

For if the wicked contaminate the good within unity, then not even Cyprian himself found any Church to which he might be joined. But if the wicked do not stain the good within unity, then the sacrilegious Donatist puts forward no cause for separation. And as for baptism, if so many others have and hand it on who do the works of the flesh—such that those who do them will not possess the kingdom of God[^160]—then heretics too, who are numbered among those works, both have it and hand it on; for, since they did not lose it by withdrawing, they were able also to hand it on while remaining. But such men do so for such men as fruitlessly and unprofitably as do all the rest who are their equals, in this, that they will not possess the kingdom of God.

And just as, when they are corrected, baptism does not begin to be present where it was lacking, but to profit where it was already present, so it is also with heretics. Hence Cyprian, and those who held his view, could not prescribe to the Catholic Church, which they were unwilling to cut off. That they thought otherwise we do not dread, because together with them we venerate Peter as well; but that they did not withdraw from unity we rejoice, because together with them we are built upon the rock[^161].
