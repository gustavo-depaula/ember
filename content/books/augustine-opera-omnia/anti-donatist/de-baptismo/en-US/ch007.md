# Book VI

## What has already been said could suffice.

**1.** 1. It could perhaps already suffice that, by reasons so often repeated and turned over and handled in manifold disputation, with the documents of the divine Scriptures also added, and with so many testimonies of Cyprian himself supporting them, even those slower of heart, as I judge, now understand that the baptism of Christ can be violated by no perversity of man, whether of the giver or of the receiver. Nor for any other reason, in those times when this question was being discussed against a useful custom, with men disputing while love and unity were preserved, did it seem to certain distinguished men, prelates of Christ, among whom blessed Cyprian especially stood out, that the baptism of Christ could not exist among heretics or schismatics, except that the sacrament was not distinguished from the effect, or the use, of the sacrament. And because its effect and use, in liberation from sins and rectitude of heart, was not found among heretics, the sacrament itself too was thought not to be there.

But when men turned their eyes to the multitude of chaff within, since these too who are perverse and live in ruin in that very unity appear able neither to give nor to have remission of sins—because it was said not to the malicious but to the good sons, *If you forgive anyone their sins, they will be forgiven him; if you retain anyone, they will be retained*[^1]—yet that they have, and give, and receive the sacrament of baptism shone forth clearly enough to the pastors of the Catholic Church spread throughout the whole world, through whom afterward, by the authority of a plenary council, the original custom was confirmed;

and that even the sheep which was straying outside, and had received the Lord's mark from her deceitful plunderers outside, when she comes to the salvation of Christian unity, is corrected from error, freed from captivity, healed of her wound, while the Lord's mark in her is recognized rather than disapproved—since that very mark many wolves both have and stamp upon wolves, who indeed seem to be within, but are convicted, by the fruits of their morals, in which they persist to the very end, of not belonging to that sheep which even out of many is one; because, according to the foreknowledge of God, just as many sheep stray outside, so many wolves lie in wait inside; among whom nevertheless *the Lord knows those who are his*[^2], who hear no voice but the shepherd's, even when he cries out through men like the Pharisees, of whom it was said, *Do what they say*[^3].

## Cyprian was like a vine.

**1.** 2. For just as a spiritual man, having the end of the commandment, that is, love from a pure heart and a good conscience and faith unfeigned[^4], can perceive something less clearly because of the body which is still corrupted and weighs down the soul[^5], and hold a view otherwise—which God, when he wills, will reveal to him as he remains in that same love[^6]—so in a carnal and perverse man some good and useful thing can be found, which is from elsewhere, not from himself. For just as in a fruitful branch there is found something that must be pruned, so that it may bear more fruit[^7], so too on a barren and dry or bound-up reed a grape sometimes hangs.

And therefore, as it is foolish to love the prunings of a fruitful branch, while he acts fittingly who does not spurn sweet fruits wherever they hang,

so whoever, cut off from unity, rebaptizes for the reason that it seemed right to Cyprian that those coming from heretics should be baptized anew, turns away from what is praiseworthy in so great a man, and pursues what is to be amended, and does not even attain that very thing which he pursues.

For he, while by zeal for God he gravely detested those who had separated themselves from unity, thought them separated even from baptism itself; but these, thinking it a small crime that they themselves are separated from the unity of Christ, contend that his baptism too is not there, and that it went out with them. So far, then, are they from the fruitfulness of Cyprian that they are not equaled even to his prunings.

## In something Cyprian saw less, but he flourished in great love.

**2.** 3. Likewise, whoever, not having love and entering upon the ruined ways of the worst morals, seems to be within while he is outside, and does not repeat the baptism of Christ even in heretics, is in no way helped in his sterility by the fact that he is fruitful not by his own fruit but is laden with another's. But it can happen that someone flourishes in the root of love, and holds rightly the very view in which Cyprian thought otherwise, and yet in Cyprian more things are fruitful than in this man, and in this man more things are to be pruned than in Cyprian.

And so we not only in no way compare wicked Catholics, but we do not easily make even good ones equal to blessed Cyprian, whom devout mother Church numbers among the rare and few men of most excellent grace—although these recognize the baptism of Christ even among heretics, while to him it seemed otherwise; so that through him, seeing something less and yet remaining most firmly in unity, it might be shown to heretics more plainly by how sacrilegious a crime the bond of peace was broken. For the blind Pharisees, though sometimes saying what ought to be done, were not to be compared with the apostle Peter, though sometimes saying what ought not to be done. And not only is the dryness of these men not to be set beside his greenness, but neither is the fruit of others to be made equal to his abundance.

For no one now compels the Gentiles to judaize[^8], nor yet is anyone now in the Church, however much he has advanced, to be compared with the apostolate of Peter. Wherefore, paying due reverence and rendering worthy honor, as far as I am able, to the peaceful bishop and glorious martyr Cyprian, I nevertheless dare to say that he thought otherwise about the baptizing of schismatics or heretics than the truth afterward brought forth—not by my opinion, but by that of the whole Church, strengthened and confirmed by the authority of a plenary council: just as, venerating Peter, the first of the Apostles and most eminent of martyrs, according to his merit, I nevertheless dare to say that he did not act rightly in compelling the Gentiles to judaize; for this too I say not by my opinion, but by the saving doctrine of the apostle Paul, held and preserved throughout the whole Church.

## Everywhere baptism is holy in itself.

**2.** 4. Disputing, therefore, about Cyprian's opinion—set far below the merits of Cyprian—I say that both the good and the wicked can have the sacrament of baptism, can give it, can receive it: the good, indeed, profitably and wholesomely, but the wicked, perniciously and to their punishment; while nevertheless it is equally whole in both, and it makes no difference to its equal integrity in all how much worse the one is who has it among the wicked, just as it makes no difference how much better the one is who has it among the good.

And through this it also makes no difference how much worse the one is who hands it on, just as it makes no difference how much better;

and thus it makes no difference how much worse the one is who receives it, just as it makes no difference how much better. For it is in itself, both in those who are not equally righteous and in those who are not equally wicked, equally holy.

## The wicked have baptism, but are not in the modest, chaste dove, etc.

**3.** 5. But that the wicked, in no way changed for the better, have baptism, and hand it on, and receive it, we have, as I judge, sufficiently demonstrated both from the canonical Scriptures and from Cyprian's own letters; that they do not belong to the holy Church of God, although they seem to be within, appears most plainly from this, that they are avaricious, rapacious, usurers, envious, malevolent, and the rest of this kind;

but that one dove[^9], modest and chaste, the bride without spot and wrinkle[^10], the garden enclosed, the sealed fountain, the paradise with the fruit of apple trees[^11], and the other things that are likewise said of her:

this is not understood except of the good and the holy and the righteous, that is, not only of those good according to the workings of the gifts of God common to the good and the wicked, but also of those having the Holy Spirit according to that inmost and surpassing love[^12], to whom the Lord says, *If you forgive anyone their sins, they will be forgiven him; and if you retain anyone, they will be retained*[^13].

## The perversity of the one who hands on baptism does not hinder good fruits.

**4.** 6. And through this nothing fitting is said as to why a wicked man cannot also hand on baptism, who can have it; and as he has it perniciously, so too he can hand it on perniciously—not because he hands on something of such a kind, nor because such a man hands it on, but because he hands it on to such a man.

For when a wicked man hands it on to a good man, that is, to one changed by a true conversion in the bond of unity, between the good sacrament that is handed on and the good believer to whom it is handed on, the malice of the one who hands it on is overcome.

And when the sins of that man, truly converted to God, are forgiven, they are forgiven by those to whom he is joined by that true conversion. For it is the same Holy Spirit who forgives them, who is given to all the saints cleaving to one another in love, whether they know one another bodily or do not know one another. Likewise, when anyone's sins are retained, they are certainly retained by those from whom the one whose sins are retained separates himself by unlikeness of life and by the turning-away of a depraved heart, whether they know him bodily or do not know him.

## Even one separated can hand on and have baptism.

**5.** 7. Wherefore all the wicked are spiritually severed from the good; but if they are also bodily separated by open dissension, they become worse.

But, as has been said, it makes no difference to the holiness of baptism how much worse anyone is who has it, and to how much worse a man he hands it on: he can nonetheless hand it on though separated, just as he can have it though separated; but as perniciously as he has it, so perniciously does he hand it on.

But the one to whom he hands it on can receive it wholesomely, if he himself receives it not separated: as has happened to very many, that, with a Catholic mind and a heart not estranged from the unity of peace, by some necessity of pressing death they ran into some heretic, and from him received the baptism of Christ without his perversity, and, whether they died or were delivered, in no way remained among those to whom in heart they had never crossed over.

But if he himself also receives it separated, he receives it the more perniciously the more good is that which he receives not well; and it avails the more to the destruction of one separated, the more it could avail to the salvation of one joined. And therefore, if, corrected from that perversity and converted from separation, he comes to Catholic peace, under the same baptism which he had received his sins will be forgiven, for the sake of the bond of love, under which baptism his sins were retained, for the sake of the sacrilege of division: because that baptism is always holy, both in a righteous man and in an unrighteous man, and is neither increased by anyone's equity nor diminished by anyone's iniquity.

## What the bishops in the council of the year 256 thought about Cyprian's opinion.

**5.** 8. Since these things are so, what does it hinder this now evident truth that many fellow bishops agreed with Cyprian in that opinion and put forth their own opinions converging on the same thing, except that the love of that man toward the unity of Christ becomes more and more known? For if, thinking these things, he remained alone with no one agreeing, he might seem to have grown cold from the crime of schism only because he was not finding partners in error; but, with so many agreeing with him, the fact that he remained in unity with the rest who thought differently shows that he preserved the most holy bond of Catholic universality not from fear of solitude but from love of peace.

Wherefore it might indeed already seem superfluous to review the individual opinions of the other bishops of that council too; but since those slower of heart do not think an answer has been given if it is given to some passage of anyone's discourse not there but elsewhere, even when what is said there could be applied to this case, men are much better worn down by reading, so as to be sharpened, than they complain through understanding little, so as to be refuted.

## The statement put forth by Cyprian.

**6.** 9. First, then, we recall that Cyprian's own consultation, by which a peaceful soul, overflowing with the richness of love, is shown, and with which the council itself begins, must be considered again. *You have heard*, he says, *most beloved colleagues, what Jubaianus our fellow bishop has written to me, consulting our mediocrity about the unlawful and profane baptism of heretics; and what I have written back to him, judging, namely, what once and again and often we have judged, that heretics coming to the Church ought to be baptized and sanctified with the baptism of the Church. Likewise there were read to you other letters of Jubaianus, in which, according to his sincere and religious devotion, writing back to our letter, he not only agreed but also gave thanks that he had been instructed. It remains that we each put forth what we think about this very matter, judging no one, nor removing anyone from the right of communion if he should think differently. For none of us sets himself up as a bishop of bishops, or drives his colleagues by tyrannical terror to the necessity of obedience; since every bishop has, by the licence of his liberty and power, his own judgment, and can no more be judged by another than he himself can judge another; but let us all await the judgment of our Lord Jesus Christ, who alone and singly has the power both of setting us over the governance of his Church and of judging our acts*[^14].

## The bishops thought in different ways, but remained in unity.

**7.** 10. Now, as I judge, we have sufficiently disputed in the foregoing books, as far as we could, not only against the letter he wrote to Jubaianus, but also against that which he sent to Quintus, and against that which, together with certain colleagues, he sent to certain colleagues, and against that which he sent to Pompeius—on behalf of the universality of Catholic agreement and of the council, in whose unity these men remained as devout members. Wherefore it now seems opportune to consider what the others too judged individually, with that liberty which he himself did not take away even from us, saying, *Judging no one, nor removing anyone from the right of communion if he should think differently.*

That he said this not in order to catch, with such security, the silent thoughts of his colleagues, as though dug out from a hidden place, but because he truly loved peace and unity, is most easy to see from other similar passages, where he writes to individuals, as to Jubaianus himself. *These things*, he says, *we have written back to you very briefly, according to our mediocrity, dearest brother, prescribing to no one and prejudging no one, so as to keep each bishop from doing what he thinks right, having the free power of his own judgment*[^15].

And lest anyone, if by this same free power he thought differently, should seem to be expelled from the fellowship of the rest, he goes on and says, *We, as far as in us lies, do not contend with our colleagues and fellow bishops on account of heretics, with whom we hold divine concord and the Lord's peace*; and a little after, *Love of spirit, the honor of the college, the bond of faith, the concord of the priesthood are preserved by us patiently and gently*[^16].

So too in the letter he wrote to Magnus, when it was asked, concerning the baptism of those immersed and those merely sprinkled, whether there was any difference: *In this matter*, he says, *our modesty and reserve prejudges no one, so as to keep each from thinking what he shall have thought, and doing what he shall have thought*[^17].

By these words of his it appears clearly enough that these matters were handled by them at that time, when, not yet made clear, they were imbibed without ambiguity, but were still, being shut up, sought out with great effort. We, therefore, now holding the custom of the whole Church—concerning the simplicity of baptism to be recognized everywhere—strengthened also by universal councils, and having received also from Cyprian's words greater confidence, by which even then it might be permitted me, with the right of communion preserved, to think differently[^18],

with unity indeed set first and praised—such unity as blessed Cyprian and his colleagues, who held that council with him, maintained with those who thought differently—routing and overturning the seditious slanders of heretics and schismatics in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ, who, speaking through his Apostle, says, *Bearing with one another in love; striving to preserve the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace*[^19]; through whom he also says, *If you think anything otherwise, that too God will reveal to you*[^20]:

we undertake to consider and treat the opinions of the holy bishops, with the bond of unity and peace preserved with them—in retaining which we imitate them, as far as the Lord himself helps us.

## Heretics do not have one faith, one hope, and therefore not one baptism either.

**8.** 11. Caecilius of Bilta said: *I know one baptism in the Church alone, and outside the Church none. This will be one where there is true hope and certain faith. For so it is written: 'One faith, one hope, one baptism'*[^21]. *Not among heretics, where there is no hope and faith is false, where all things are done through falsehood, where the demoniac performs the exorcism, where one whose mouth and words give off a cancer questions about the sacrament, where the unbeliever gives faith, the criminal grants pardon for offenses, and the antichrist baptizes in the name of Christ, one cursed by God blesses, the dead man promises life, the unpeaceful gives peace, the blasphemer invokes God, the profane administers the priesthood, the sacrilegious sets up the altar.*

*To all these is added this evil also, that the prelate of the devil dares to make the Eucharist. Or let those who support them say that all these things are false concerning heretics. Behold, to what things the Church is compelled to consent, and is forced to communicate without baptism and without pardon for offenses. This thing, brothers, we ought to flee and avoid, and separate ourselves from so great a crime, and hold the one baptism which has been granted by God to the Church alone.*

## Many are baptized in the Church who say and do not.

**8.** 12. To these things I answer that whoever, even within, confesses that he knows God but denies it by his deeds—such as are the avaricious and the envious, and those who, on account of fraternal hatred, are called murderers, not by my testimony but by that of the holy apostle John[^22]; and they do not have hope, because they bear a bad conscience; and they are faithless, because they do not do what they vowed to God; and they are liars, because they profess false things; and they are demoniacs, because they give place in their heart to the devil and his angels; and their words work rottenness, since they corrupt good morals by evil conversations[^23];

and they are unbelievers, since they mock what God threatens against such; and they are criminals, because they live wickedly; and they are antichrists, because their morals are opposed to Christ; and they are cursed by God, because everywhere holy Scripture execrates such; and they are dead, because they lack the life of righteousness; and they are unpeaceful, because by contrary deeds they clash with the word of God; and they are blasphemers, because through their ruined acts the Christian name is disparaged; and they are profane, because they are shut out from that inner spiritual sanctuary of God; and they are sacrilegious, because in themselves they corrupt the temple of God by living wickedly; and they are prelates of the devil, because they serve fraud and avarice, which is idolatry.

But that there are some such people—indeed very many—even within, both the apostle Paul and the bishop Cyprian[^24] testify. Why, then, do these men baptize? Why are even some who renounce the world in words and not in deeds, and, unchanged from morals of this kind, baptized[^25], and, when they are changed, not rebaptized?

And as for the fact that he is indignant and says, *Behold, to what things the Church is compelled to consent, and is forced to communicate without baptism and without pardon for offenses*—if there were not other bishops who were compelling others to these things, he certainly would not say it.

Whence it is also shown that those then thought more truly who did not withdraw from the ancient custom, which afterward was confirmed by the universality of a council. But what is it that he adds and says, *This thing, brothers, we ought to flee and avoid, and separate ourselves from so great a crime*? For if he says this in such a way that he himself does not do these things nor approve them, it is one matter; but if it is so as to condemn and segregate, first, those who think differently, he resists the words of Cyprian, by which he says, *Judging no one, nor removing anyone from the right of communion if he should think differently*[^26].

## Whether baptism is bound up with the Church.

**9.** 14. To the things that Felix of Misgirpa said, we say this: If there were no one and true baptism except in the Church, it certainly would not be in those who depart from unity. But it is in them; for they do not receive it again when they return, for no other reason than that they had not lost it when they withdrew. And as for what he says, *For the things that are performed outside have no effect of salvation*[^27], I agree, and believe it to be wholly true. For it is one thing not to be there, and another to have no effect of salvation. For to those coming to Catholic peace the things that were within them outside, but did not profit, begin to profit.

## To baptize heretics is to make void the baptism of Christ.

**10.** 15. But to Polycarp of Hadrumetum[^28], since he said, *Those who approve the baptism of heretics make void our own*[^29], we answer: If that is the baptism of heretics which is given through heretics, then that is the baptism of the avaricious and of murderers which is given within by such men. But if the latter is not theirs, neither is the former theirs; and through this, among whomsoever it may be, it is Christ's.

## One must prove, not merely assert.

**11.** 17. Novatus of Thamugadi said what he did, but did not bring forward anything by which he might show that he ought to have done what he did. *For he named the testimony of the Scriptures and a decree of his colleagues*[^30], but brought forth nothing from these that we might consider.

## Some sinners are baptized within and baptize.

**12.** 19. But Nemesianus of Thubunae[^31] set down many testimonies of the Scriptures[^32]; yet he said much on behalf of the Catholic position, which we have undertaken to set forth and commend. Unless, perhaps, it is to be thought that he does not *trust in false things* who trusts in the hope of temporal things, as do all the avaricious and the rapacious, and those who renounce the world not in deeds but in words—such as nevertheless both baptize and are baptized within, of which Cyprian too is a witness[^33].

Such men *also chase flying birds*[^34], because they do not lay hold of the things they crave. *And he deserts the ways of his own vineyard, and strays from the path of his own little field; and he goes through trackless and dry places, and a land appointed to thirst, and he gathers with his hands what is unfruitful*—not only the heretic, but everyone who lives wickedly: for all righteousness is fruitful, and all iniquity unfruitful. And those who *drink another's water from a strange fountain*[^35] are not only heretics, but all who do not live according to what God teaches, and live according to what the devil teaches. For if he were speaking of baptism, he would not say, *Nor will you drink from a strange fountain*;

but, You will not wash yourself from a strange fountain. And now, as for what the Lord says, *Unless one is born again of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God*[^36], I do not at all see how it helps him toward what he thinks. For it is one thing to say: Everyone who will enter into the kingdom of heaven is first born again of water and the Spirit, because unless he is born again of water and the Spirit he will not enter into the kingdom of heaven—which the Lord said, and it is true; but it is another to say: Everyone who is born of water and the Spirit will enter into the kingdom of heaven—which is certainly false. For that Simon Magus too was born of water and the Spirit[^37], and yet did not enter into the kingdom of heaven. So it can happen to heretics too. Or if no one is born of the Spirit except one who is changed by a true conversion[^38], then all who renounce the world in words and not in deeds are certainly born not of the Spirit but of water alone—who nevertheless are within, with Cyprian as witness. For it is necessary that one of two things be granted: either those who falsely renounce the world are born of the Spirit, though unto destruction, not unto salvation, and thus heretics too can be; or, if what is written, *For the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee the feigned*[^39], avails also to this, that those falsely renouncing the world are not born of the Spirit, then one can be baptized with water and not be born of the Spirit; and in vain does Nemesianus say, *Neither can the Spirit work without the water, nor the water without the Spirit.* And now it has often been said elsewhere too how it can come about that those have one baptism in common who do not have one Church: just as it can come about, within the Church itself, that those holy by righteousness and those unclean by avarice do not have one Spirit, and yet have one baptism. For thus it was said, *one body*, that is, the Church, just as, *one Spirit*, and *one baptism*[^40].

And now the other things he said support our assertion rather. For he set down a testimony from the Gospel: *What is born of the flesh is flesh; and what is born of the spirit is spirit*[^41]; *because God is spirit, and he is born of God*[^42]; and he inferred: *Therefore all heretics and schismatics, in all things whatsoever they do, are carnal, as the Apostle says: 'For the works of the flesh are manifest, which are fornications, uncleannesses'*; and the rest that the Apostle says there, where he also recalled heresies, this man pursued: *Since whoever do these things will not inherit the kingdom of God*[^43]. Then he added and said: *And so the Apostle, together with all the wicked, condemns also those who make division, that is, schismatics and heretics.* Well; for in enumerating the works of the flesh, among which are also heresies, he found and said that the Apostle condemns all of them together. Let him, then, himself ask holy Cyprian, and hear from him how many even within live according to the wicked works of the flesh which the Apostle condemns along with heresies, and yet baptize and are baptized. Why, then, are heretics alone said not to be able to have the baptism which the partners of their condemnation have?

## With Scripture as witness, heretics are to be rebaptized.

**13.** 20. Januarius of Lambaesis said: *According to the authority of the holy Scriptures, I decree that all heretics are to be baptized, and so admitted into the holy Church*[^44].

## This man brings forward no testimonies.

**13.** 21. To him it is answered: According to the authority of the holy Scriptures, the Catholic council of the whole world decreed that the baptism of Christ, even when found among heretics, is not to be disapproved. But if he set down testimonies of the Scriptures, we would show that they are either not against us, or even on our side: as, behold, the one who follows him.

## What if the enemies of the Church baptize?

**14.** 23. Lucius of Castra Galbae[^45] set down a testimony from the Gospel, the Lord saying[^46]: *You are the salt of the earth; but if the salt has become tasteless, that which shall be salted with it will be good for nothing, but to be cast out and trodden underfoot by men*[^47]—as though we said that the men themselves who are cast out are good for anything, either for their own salvation or for that of others. But even those who seem to be such within are not only spiritually outside, but will also be bodily separated at the end. For all such are good for nothing; nor yet, on that account, is the sacrament of baptism that is in them nothing. For even in those very men who are cast out, if they come to their senses and return, the salvation which had departed returns to them; but baptism, because it had not departed, does not return.

And as for what the Lord says, *Go, therefore, and teach the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit*[^48], he did not permit any but the good to baptize; for to the wicked he would not say, *If you forgive anyone their sins, they will be forgiven him; if you retain anyone, they will be retained*[^49]. How, then, do the wicked baptize within, who cannot forgive sins? How do they also baptize the wicked who are unchanged, whose sins are still upon them, as John says, *He who hates his brother is in darkness even until now*[^50]?

But if they are then forgiven, when they join themselves, by an inmost love, to the good and the righteous, through whom in the Church sins are forgiven, although they were baptized by the wicked: so too those who come from outside, and approach the same framework of the body of Christ by the inner bond of peace;

yet the baptism of Christ is to be recognized in both, and to be disapproved in none, whether before they are converted, though it profits them nothing, or when they are converted, that it may profit. *Since they themselves, by withdrawing from the Church, which is one, have become tasteless and hostile, let it be done as it is written: 'The houses of those hostile to the law owe a cleansing'*[^51]. *And it is consequent*, he says, *that those who, baptized by the hostile, have been defiled, should first be purified, and then at last be baptized.*

What then? Are not the rapacious and murderers hostile to the law that says, *You shall not kill, you shall not steal*[^52]? They *owe*, therefore, *a cleansing*: who would deny this? And yet not only those baptized within by such men, but even those who, being such and unchanged, are baptized, although they still owe a purification that they may be changed, are nevertheless not baptized further when they have been changed. So great is the power in the sacrament of simple baptism that, although we confess that one baptized and still living wickedly ought to be cleansed, we nevertheless forbid that he be baptized further.

## On the authority of Cyprian in Letter 72.

**15.** 24. Crescens of Cirta[^53] said: *In so great an assembly of most holy fellow priests, the letters of our most beloved Cyprian to Jubaianus, and likewise to Stephen*[^54], having been read—which contain in themselves so many most holy testimonies descending from the divine Scriptures that, united through the grace of God, we ought deservedly all to agree—I judge, therefore, that all heretics or schismatics who shall wish to come to the Catholic Church should not enter unless they have first been exorcized and baptized; except, indeed, those who were previously baptized in the Catholic Church, who are nevertheless to be reconciled to the penance of the Church by the laying on of hands[^55].

## But it contains nothing about this question.

**15.** 25. Here we are prompted to ask again why he said, *except, indeed, those who were previously baptized in the Catholic Church.* Was it because they had not lost what they had received within? Then what they could have outside, why could they not also hand on? Or is it handed on unlawfully outside? But neither is it had lawfully outside, and yet it is had: so it is given unlawfully outside, but nevertheless it is given. And what is bestowed on one returning who had received it within is bestowed on one coming who had received it outside; that is, that he may have lawfully within what he had unlawfully outside.

But someone may perhaps ask what blessed Cyprian's letter to Stephen, mention of which was made in this opinion, has about this matter, since it was not mentioned at the opening of the council—because, I believe, it was not thought necessary. For he said that this letter too was read in that assembly of fellow priests: which I do not at all doubt was done, as I judge, as is customary, so that the bishops, now gathered, might also learn together something about that cause which is contained in that letter. For it does not at all pertain to the present question; and I wonder more why this man wished to mention it than why mention of it was omitted at the opening of the council. But if anyone thinks that I was unwilling to bring out into the open something set down in it that is necessary to the present cause, let him read it and know that what I say is true; or, if he finds anything else, let him refute me. For that letter has, in short, nothing about baptism given among heretics or schismatics, which is what we are now treating.

## Where remission of sins is not given, baptism is not given.

**16.** 26. Nicomedes of Segermes said: *This is my opinion, that heretics coming to the Church be baptized, in that they obtain no remission of sins outside, among sinners.*

## In the Church sinners do not remit sins.

**16.** 27. To him it is answered: This is the opinion of the whole Catholic Church, that heretics, although already baptized in heresy with the baptism of Christ, coming to the Church, are not baptized. For if there is no remission of sins among sinners, then sinners within do not remit sins either; and yet those baptized by them are not rebaptized.

## Whether the separated have the Trinity.

**17.** 28. Monnulus of Girba[^56] said: *The truth of the Catholic Church, our mother, has always remained and remains with us, brothers, and most of all in the Trinity of baptism, our Lord saying, 'Go, baptize the nations in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit'*[^57]. *Since, then,* he says, *we manifestly know that heretics have neither the Father, nor the Son, nor the Holy Spirit, those coming to the Church, our mother, ought to be truly born again and baptized, so that the cancer which they had, and the wrath of damnation, and the taint of error, may be sanctified by the holy and heavenly washing.*

## They have the Trinity at least in the sacrament.

**17.** 29. To him we answer that all who are baptized with a baptism consecrated by gospel words have the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit at least in the sacrament; but in heart and in life, not even those have them who within live in ruin and execrably.

## Antichrists cannot give the grace of baptism.

**18.** 30. Secundinus of Cedias[^58] said: *Since our Lord Christ says, 'He who is not with me is against me'*[^59], *and the apostle John calls those who go out from the Church antichrists*[^60]: *undoubtedly the enemies of Christ, who have been named antichrists, cannot administer the grace of saving baptism; and therefore I judge that those who flee from the snares of heretics to the Church are to be baptized by us, who have been called friends of God by his condescension*[^61]. There are also other enemies of Christ who give baptism.

**18.** 31. To him it is answered that all are adversaries of Christ to whom, as they say, *Lord, in your name we did many mighty works*[^62], and the other things that are said there, he will say at the end, *I never knew you; depart from me, all you who work iniquity*[^63]: which whole chaff, if it perseveres in malice to the very end, whether before its winnowing it flies off outside or seems to be within, is destined for the fire. If, then, heretics coming to the Church are for this reason to be baptized, that they may be baptized by friends of God, are then those avaricious, rapacious, murderous men friends of God, or are those whom they have baptized to be baptized anew?

## A heretic gives death.

**19.** 32. Felix of Bagai[^64] said: *As a blind man leading a blind man fall together into the pit*[^65], *so a heretic baptizing a heretic fall together into death.*

## It is true, but not on that account is one to be rebaptized.

**19.** 33. This is true; but what he adds is not therefore true: *And therefore*, he says, *the heretic is to be baptized and given life, lest we, the living, communicate with the dead.* Were not those dead who said, *Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die*[^66]? They did not believe the resurrection of the dead. Those, then, who were corrupted by their evil conversations and followed them—did they not fall into the pit together with them? And yet among them were those to whom the Apostle wrote as already baptized, and yet not for that reason, if they were corrected, would they be baptized anew. Does not the same Apostle say, *To be wise according to the flesh is death*[^67]? And certainly the avaricious, the cheats, the rapacious were wise according to the flesh, among whom Cyprian himself groaned[^68]. How, then, did the dead harm one living in unity? Or who would say that the baptism of Christ, which such men had or gave, was violated by their iniquities?

## It is just to rebaptize.

**20.** 34. Polianus of Mileu said: *It is just that a heretic be baptized in the holy Church*[^69].

## That baptism is not to be blown away.

**20.** 35. Nothing indeed could be said more briefly. But I think this too is brief: It is just that the baptism of Christ not be blown away in the Church of Christ.

## There is one baptism in the Church.

**21.** 36. Theogenes of Hippo Regius said[^70]: *According to the sacrament of the heavenly grace of God which we have received, we believe in one baptism which is in the holy Church.*

## Why did the water of Paradise flow outside Paradise?

**21.** 37. This could be my opinion too. For it is so balanced that it has nothing against the truth. For we too believe in one baptism which is in the holy Church. But if he had said, We believe in the baptism which is in the holy Church alone, an answer would have had to be given as to the others.

But now, because it was so said, *We believe in one baptism which is in the holy Church*, in such a way that it was said indeed to be in the holy Church, but it was not denied to be also elsewhere: whatever he thought, there is no need to dispute against these words. For if I were asked about each point—whether there was one baptism—I would answer that there is one.

Then, if I were asked whether this was in the holy Church, I would answer that it is. Thirdly, if it were asked whether I believed in this baptism, I would answer that I so believe; and through this I would answer that I believe in one baptism which is in the holy Church. But if it were asked whether it was in the holy Church alone, and was not among heretics or schismatics, I would answer, with the whole Church, that I do not so believe. But because this man did not set this down in his opinion, I think it dishonest that I should add to him words against which I might dispute, which I did not find there. For if he were so to say, There is one water of the river Euphrates, which is in Paradise[^71], he would certainly say what is true. But if he were asked whether this water was in Paradise alone, and were to say that it was so, he would say what is false. For it is also outside Paradise, in these lands into which it flows from that source. But who is so rash as to say that he would have answered what is false, when he could perhaps answer what is true? Wherefore the words of this opinion ought not to meet with contradiction, since they in no way hinder the truth.

## We do not communicate with a heretic.

**22.** 38. Dativus of Vada[^72] said: *We, as far as in us lies, do not communicate with a heretic, unless he has been baptized in the Church and has received remission of sins.*

## To baptize is not to communicate with a heretic.

**22.** 39. It is answered: If you wish him to be baptized for the reason that he did not receive remission of sins, then, if within you find someone who, holding hatred against his brother, was thus baptized—since the Lord cannot lie, who says, *If you do not forgive, it will not be forgiven you*[^73]—will you, when he is corrected, command him to be baptized anew? Certainly not; so, then, neither the heretic. But indeed, why this man did not say briefly, *We do not communicate with a heretic*, but added, *as far as in us lies*, is not to be passed over.

For he saw that many agreed with this opinion, from whose fellowship, nevertheless, lest unity be violated, these men could not separate themselves; and he added, *as far as in us lies*, certainly showing that he did not communicate willingly with those whom he thought not to have baptism, but that all things were nevertheless to be tolerated for the good of peace and unity;

which those also did who thought that these men did not act rightly, and held that which the truth, afterward more clearly declared, taught, and which the more ancient custom, confirmed by a later council, more strongly urged: yet they tolerated one another, with anxious devotion, while thinking differently with love preserved, striving to preserve the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace[^74], until to one of the two parties, if they thought anything otherwise, God would reveal that too.

Let these men hear this, by whom unity is assailed through this council, through which council it is declared how greatly unity is to be loved.

## Heretics do not have the Holy Spirit, therefore not even baptism.

**23.** 40. Successus of Abbir Germaniciana[^75] said: *To heretics either nothing is permitted, or everything is permitted. If they can baptize, they can also give the Holy Spirit; but if they cannot give the Holy Spirit, because they do not have the Holy Spirit, neither can they baptize spiritually. Therefore we judge that heretics are to be baptized.*

## Then not even other sinners.

**23.** 41. To him it can be answered in almost as many words thus: To murderers either nothing is permitted, or everything is permitted. If they can baptize, they can also give the Holy Spirit; but if they cannot give the Holy Spirit, because they do not have the Holy Spirit, neither can they baptize spiritually. Therefore we judge that those baptized by murderers, or murderers baptized and not changed for the better, when they have corrected themselves, are to be baptized. But this is not true. For *he who hates his brother is a murderer*[^76]. And Cyprian knew such men within, who certainly baptized. In vain, then, were such things said about baptizing heretics.

## Those who are outside the Church cannot baptize.

**24.** 42. Fortunatus of Tuccabori said[^77]: *Jesus Christ, our Lord and God, the Son of God the Father and Creator, built the Church upon Peter*[^78], *not upon heresy; and he gave the power of baptizing to bishops, not to heretics. Wherefore those who are outside the Church, and standing against Christ, scatter his sheep and flock, cannot baptize outside*[^79].

## Then not even the other sinners.

**24.** 43. To this he added *outside*, so that he could not be answered so briefly. For to him too it would be answered in almost as many words thus: Jesus Christ, our Lord and God, the Son of God the Father and Creator, founded the Church upon Peter, not upon iniquity; and he gave the power of baptizing to bishops, not to the iniquitous. Wherefore those who do not belong to the rock, upon which build those who hear the words of God and do them, but who, living against Christ by hearing his words and not doing them, and thereby building upon sand, corrupt his sheep and flock by the example of their ruined morals, cannot baptize[^80].

Could this not be said with verisimilitude? And yet it is false. For the iniquitous do baptize, since those rapacious men are iniquitous whom Cyprian reproved as set in unity with him[^81].

But, he says, for this reason he added *outside.* Why, then, can those men not baptize outside? Is it because they are worse by the very fact that they are outside? But it makes no difference to the integrity of baptism how much worse the one is who hands it on. For there is not as great a difference between the wicked man and the worse as there is between the good man and the wicked; and yet when the wicked man baptizes, he gives no other thing than the good man. Therefore, even when the worse man baptizes, he gives no other thing than the one who is less wicked. Or does it perhaps pertain not to the merit of the man, but to the sacrament of baptism itself, that it cannot be given outside? If this were so, it could not be had outside either, and it would be necessary to be baptized as often as anyone, withdrawing from the Church, returned again to the Church.

## The answer is explained.

**24.** 44. And now, if we ask more diligently what *outside* is—especially because he himself made mention of Peter, upon whom the Church is built[^82]—are not those in the Church who are in the rock, while those who are not in the rock are not in the Church either? Now, then, let us see whether those who hear Christ's words and do not do them set their building upon the rock. The Lord himself contradicts them, saying, *He who hears these words of mine and does them, I will liken him to a wise man who builds his house upon the rock*; and a little after, *He who hears these words of mine and does not do them, I will liken him to a foolish man who builds his house upon the sand*[^83]. If, then, the Church is on the rock, those who are on the sand, because they are outside the rock, are assuredly outside the Church. Let us recall, therefore, how many Cyprian mentions, as though set within, who build upon the sand, that is, who hear the words of Christ and do not do them[^84]. And therefore, because they are on the sand, they are convicted of being outside the rock, which is outside the Church; yet, both as long as they are so, and not yet, or never, changed for the better, they baptize and are baptized; and the baptism which they have remains whole, though they are destined for damnation.

## It is explained further.

**24.** 45. Nor can it be said in this place: For who does all the words of the Lord, which are written down in that very gospel discourse, in concluding which he said that the man builds upon the rock who heard those words and did them, but upon the sand the man who heard and did not do them? Because, even if all things are not fulfilled by some, in that same discourse he nevertheless set down the remedy, saying, *Forgive, and it will be forgiven you*[^85]. And after the Lord's Prayer, recalled and written down in that same discourse, he says, *For I say to you, if you forgive men their sins, your Father will also forgive you your sins; but if you do not forgive men, neither will your Father forgive you*[^86]. Hence Peter too says, *Because love covers a multitude of sins*[^87]: which those certainly did not have, and through this built upon the sand, of whom the same Cyprian says that within, in malevolent envy, even in apostolic times, they consorted without love[^88]; and therefore they seemed indeed to be within, but were outside, because they were not in that rock by which the Church is signified.

## A heretic's prayer does not sanctify the water.

**25.** 46. Sedatus of Tuburbo said[^89]: *In so far as water, sanctified by the priest's prayer in the Church, washes away offenses, in so far does it, infected by a heretic's speech as by a cancer, heap up sins. Wherefore one must strive with all peaceful strength that no one infected and dyed with heretical error refuse to receive the singular and true baptism of the Church, with which whoever has not been baptized will become a stranger to the kingdom of heaven.*

## Any invocation, even an imperfect one, cleanses the water.

**25.** 47. To him it is answered that, if the water is not sanctified when the one praying pours out, through unskillfulness, some words of error, then many—not only the wicked, but even good brothers—in the Church itself do not sanctify the water. For the prayers of many are corrected daily, if they are recited to the more learned, and many things are found in them against the Catholic faith. If it should be made known that some were baptized when those prayers were said over the water, will they be commanded to be baptized anew? Why so? Because for the most part the affection of the one praying overcomes the fault of the prayer; and because those fixed gospel words, without which baptism cannot be consecrated, avail so much that through them whatever is said in a faulty prayer against the rule of faith is made void, just as a demon is shut out by the name of Christ[^90].

For certainly, if a heretic brings forth a faulty prayer, he does not have the good affection of love by which that unskillfulness might be overcome; and therefore he is like anyone within the Catholic Church who is envious and malevolent, such as Cyprian reproves there[^91]: let such a one bring forth, as is wont to happen, some prayer in which he speaks against the rule of faith; for many rush into prayers composed not only by unskilled chatterers but even by heretics, and, through the simplicity of ignorance, not being able to discern them, use them, thinking them good; nor yet does what is perverse in them make void the things that are right there, but is rather made void by them: just as, in a man himself of good hope and probable faith, but a man nonetheless, if he holds any view otherwise, the things he holds rightly are not on that account made void, until God reveals to him also what he holds otherwise[^92].

But if he himself is wicked and perverse, even if he says a whole prayer, in no part opposed to the Catholic faith, he is not on that account upright himself, because the prayer is upright; and if in some parts he brings forth a perverse prayer, God is present to his own gospel words, without which the baptism of Christ cannot be consecrated, and he himself sanctifies his sacrament, so that to a man—whether before he is baptized, or when he is baptized, or afterward, whenever he is truly converted to him—that very thing may avail unto salvation which, unless he were converted, would avail unto destruction. For the rest, who does not know that it is not the baptism of Christ, if there are lacking there the gospel words of which the Creed consists? But heretics are more easily found who do not baptize at all than those who do not baptize with those words. And therefore we say that not every baptism (for in many sacrilegious rites of idols men are said to be baptized), but the baptism of Christ, that is, one consecrated with gospel words, is everywhere the same, and is violated by the perversity of no men whatever and of whatever kind.

## On the judgment of Solomon.

**25.** 48. Indeed, in this opinion too, what he inserted here and said is not carelessly to be omitted: *Wherefore one must strive with all peaceful strength that no one infected with heretical error*, and the rest[^93]. For he had regard to those words of blessed Cyprian who says, *Judging no one, nor removing anyone from the right of communion if he should think differently*[^94].

See how greatly the love of unity and peace avails in the good sons of the Church, that those whom they called sacrilegious and profane, admitted, as they thought, without baptism—if they could not, as much as they thought, correct them—they chose rather to tolerate than, on their account, to break that holy bond, lest, on account of the tares, the wheat be uprooted along with them[^95]; permitting, as far as in them lay, just as in that most noble judgment of Solomon, the infant body to be nourished by a false mother rather than to be cut in two[^96]. And this both those did who thought more truly about the sacrament of baptism, and these to whom, for the merit of so great a love, God was going to reveal it, if they thought anything otherwise[^97].

## Heresy does not come from God . . .

**26.** 49. Privatianus of Sufetula said: *Whoever says that heretics have the power of baptizing, let him first say who founded heresy. For if heresy is from God, it can also have the divine indulgence; but if it is not from God, how can it either have the grace of God or confer it on anyone?*[^98]

. . . but neither do other sins.

**26.** 50. To him it can be answered in as many words thus: Whoever says that the malevolent and the envious have the power of baptizing, let him first say who founded malevolence and envy. For if malevolence and envy are from God, they can also have the divine indulgence; but if they are not from God, how can they either have the grace of God or confer it on anyone?

But just as these things, said in the same way, are most manifestly false, so too are those things for the refuting of which these were said. For the malevolent and the envious do baptize, as Cyprian himself grants[^99], since he testifies that they too are within. So, then, heretics too can baptize: because baptism is the sacrament of Christ, while envy and heresy are works of the devil, which, whoever has them, does not thereby bring it about that, if he has the sacrament of Christ, that too should be numbered among the works of the devil.

## A difficulty.

**27.** 51. Privatus of Sufes said: *Those who approve the baptism of heretics, what else do they do but communicate with heretics?*[^100]

## It is not the baptism of heretics that is approved, but Christ's.

**27.** 52. To him it is answered: It is not the baptism of heretics that we approve in heretics, just as it is not the baptism of the avaricious, the treacherous, the cheats, the rapacious, the envious that we approve in them. For all these are iniquitous; but Christ is righteous, whose sacrament in them, as far as it pertains to him, they do not violate by their iniquity. Otherwise another could say: Those who approve the baptism of the iniquitous, what else do they do but communicate with the iniquitous? And if anyone should object this against the Catholic Church, it would be answered him as it has been answered to this man.

## There is one baptism in the Church alone.

**28.** 53. Hortensianus of Lares said[^101]: *Let the presumptuous and the supporters of heretics see to it; we vindicate to the Church the one baptism, which we know to be nowhere but in the Church. Or how can those baptize anyone in the name of Christ whom Christ himself declares to be his adversaries*[^102]? How, then, do other sinners baptize in the name of Christ?

**28.** 54. To him, in a similar tenor of words, we answer thus: Let the presumptuous and the supporters of the iniquitous see to it; we vindicate to the Church the one baptism, which we know to be the Church's, recalling it to the Church wherever it is found, when we can. Or how can those baptize anyone in the name of Christ whom Christ himself declares to be his adversaries? For to all the iniquitous he says, *I never knew you; depart from me, you who work iniquity*[^103]: and yet when they baptize, it is not they who baptize, but he of whom John says, *This is he who baptizes*[^104].

## There cannot be two baptisms; one who gives it to heretics takes it from himself.

**29.** 55. Cassius of Macomades said[^105]: *Since there cannot be two baptisms, whoever grants baptism to heretics takes it from himself. I judge, therefore, that the weeping and wasting heretics are to be baptized when they begin to come to the Church, so that, washed by the sacred and divine washing, and enlightened by the light of life, they may be received into the Church not as enemies but as peaceful, not as strangers but as members of the household of the Lord's faith*[^106], *not as adulterous but as sons of God, made not of error but of salvation: apart from those who, faithful in the Church, having been supplanted, had crossed over to the darkness of heresy; but these are to be restored by the laying on of hands.*

## This would hold of other sinners too.

**29.** 56. Another could say: Since there cannot be two baptisms of Christ, whoever grants baptism to the iniquitous takes it from himself. But these men too would resist us and say: We grant baptism to the iniquitous; which is not from themselves in them, like iniquity, but from Christ, whose is righteousness, and whose sacrament, even among the iniquitous, is not iniquitous. What, then, they would say to us about the iniquitous, let them say to themselves about heretics. And therefore what follows ought rather to have been said thus: I judge, therefore, that the weeping and wasting heretics are not to be baptized when they begin to come to the Church, if they already have the baptism of Christ, but are to be corrected from their perversity.

For it can be said of the iniquitous too, of whom heretics are a portion: I judge, therefore, that the weeping and wasting iniquitous, if they are already baptized, are not to be baptized when they begin to come to the Church, that is, to that rock outside which are all who hear the words of Christ and do not do them[^107]; but, already washed by the sacred and divine washing, and then also enlightened by the light of life, they are to be received into the Church not as enemies but as peaceful—for the iniquitous do not have peace; not as strangers but as members of the household of the Lord's faith—for to the iniquitous it was said, *How are you turned into the bitterness of a strange vine?*[^108]; not as adulterous but as sons of God—for the iniquitous are sons of the devil; made not of error but of salvation—for iniquity does not save: that is, into that rock, into that dove[^109], into that garden enclosed and sealed fountain[^110], which is recognized only in the wheat, and not in the chaff, whether the chaff be separated far off by the wind or seem to be mixed with the grain up to the final winnowing[^111]. In vain, then, did Cassius add, *apart from those who, faithful in the Church, having been supplanted, had crossed over to the darkness of heresy.* For if they too, by withdrawing from the Church, had lost baptism, let it be given back to them too; but if they had not lost it, let what was given through them be recognized.

## Bishops ought to be watchful lest heretics baptize.

**30.** 57. Another Januarius, of Vicus Caesaris, said: *If error does not yield to truth, much more does truth not consent to error; and therefore we stand by the Church, in which we preside, so that, vindicating its baptism to it alone, we may baptize those whom the Church has not baptized*[^112].

## It is the Rock that baptizes.

**30.** 58. It is answered: Those whom the Church baptizes, that rock certainly baptizes, outside which are all who hear the words of Christ and do not do them[^113]. Let all, then, be baptized who have been baptized by such men. But if this is not done, then, just as in these men, so in heretics the baptism of Christ, their iniquity and perversity being condemned or corrected, is to be recognized and approved.

## Heretics are not Christians, therefore . . .

**31.** 59. Another Secundinus, of Carpi, said[^114]: *Are heretics Christians or not? If they are Christians, why are they not in the Church of God? If they are not Christians, let them become such. Or to what will the word of the Lord pertain, who says, " He who is not with me is against me; and he who does not gather with me scatters "*[^115]?

*Whence it is established that upon the strange sons and the offspring of the antichrist the Holy Spirit cannot descend merely by the laying on of hands, since it is manifest that heretics do not have baptism.*

. . . are the iniquitous Christians? Why, then, do they baptize?

**31.** 60. To him it is answered: Are the iniquitous Christians or not? If they are Christians, why are they not in that rock on which the Church is built[^116]? For they hear the words of Christ and do not do them. If they are not Christians, let them become such. Or to what will the word of the Lord pertain, who says, *He who is not with me is against me; and he who does not gather with me scatters*[^117]? For they scatter his sheep who, by depraved imitation, lead them to the ruin of their own morals. Whence it is established that upon the strange sons (by which name all the iniquitous are called) and the offspring of the antichrist (which are all who are opposed to Christ) the Holy Spirit cannot descend merely by the laying on of hands, if a true conversion of heart is not present; since it is manifest that the iniquitous, as long as they are iniquitous, can indeed have baptism, but cannot have the salvation of which baptism is the sacrament.

For let us see whether heretics are described in that psalm where it is said thus of strange sons: *Lord, deliver me from the hand of strange sons, whose mouth has spoken vanity, and their right hand is a right hand of iniquity; whose sons are like new plants firmly set in their youth, their daughters adorned like the likeness of a temple. Their storehouses are full, overflowing from this into that; their sheep are fruitful, multiplying in their goings forth; their oxen are fat: there is no breach in the wall, nor passage, nor outcry in their streets. They have called the people blessed that has these things; blessed is the people whose God is the Lord himself*[^118]. If, then, these are the strange sons, who place their blessedness in temporal things and in the abundance of earthly happiness, and despise the divine precepts, let us see whether these are not the very same of whom Cyprian says thus, transferring them even into himself, so as to show that he speaks of those with whom he shared the sacraments: *While we do not hold the way of the Lord*, he says, *nor keep the heavenly commandments given to us for salvation. Our Lord did the will of the Father, and we do not do the will of the Lord, devoting ourselves to patrimony and gain, pursuing pride*, and the rest[^119]. But if these men could both have and hand on baptism, why is it denied that it can be among the strange sons? Whom, nevertheless, he exhorts, that by keeping the heavenly commandments sent to them through the only Son, they may deserve to be his brothers and the sons of God.

## Why defame heretics?

**32.** 61. Victoricus of Thabraca said[^120]: *If heretics are permitted to baptize and to give remission of sins, why do we defame them, so as to call them heretics?*

## But why defame the iniquitous?

**32.** 62. What if another were to say: If the iniquitous are permitted to baptize and to give remission of sins, why do we defame them, so as to call them iniquitous? What would be answered to this man about the iniquitous, let the same be answered to that man about heretics; that is, that the baptism with which they baptize is not theirs, and that it does not follow that whoever has the baptism of Christ is also secure about the remission of sins, if he has this in the sacrament only, and is not converted by a true confession of heart, so that it might be forgiven him who forgives[^121].

## By baptism heretics are reformed for the better, therefore . . .

**33.** 63. Another Felix, of Uthina, said: *It is in doubt to no one, most holy fellow priests, that human presumption cannot do as much as the adorable and venerable majesty of our Lord Christ. Mindful, therefore, of the peril, this too we ought not only to observe but also to confirm for all of us, that all heretics who run to the bosom of mother Church be baptized; so that the heretical mind, which has been polluted by a long-lasting decay, may, cleansed by the sanctification of the washing, be reformed for the better*[^122].

. . . this would hold of the other iniquitous too. Therefore . . .

**33.** 64. Perhaps this man, who placed the cause why heretics ought to be baptized in the cleansing of a long-lasting decay, would spare those who, rushing into some heresy, had been there for a brief time, and, quickly corrected, had migrated thence to the Catholic Church.

Then he too pays too little attention to the fact that it can be said thus: that all the iniquitous who run to that rock on which the Church is signified be baptized, so that the iniquitous mind, which outside the rock was building on the sand by hearing the words of Christ and not doing them, may, cleansed by the sanctification of the washing, be reformed for the better; and yet it is not done, if they are already baptized, even if it is proven that they were such when they were baptized—that is, that they renounced the world in words and not in deeds[^123].

. . . whether a dead man can baptize. Heretics are dead.

**34.** 65. Quietus of Buruch said: *We who live by faith ought, by a believing observance, to obey the things that have been foretold for our instruction. For it is written in Solomon: " He who is baptized by a dead man, what does his washing profit? "*[^124]. *Which certainly speaks of those who are dyed by heretics, and of those who dye them. For if those who are baptized among them obtain eternal life through the remission of sins, why do they come to the Church? But if nothing of salvation is received from a dead man, and therefore, their former error recognized, they return with penance to the truth, they will have to be sanctified by the one vital baptism which is in the Catholic Church*[^125].

## The other sinners too are dead.

**34.** 66. What it is to be baptized by a dead man, without prejudice to a more diligent consideration of that same Scripture, we have already said elsewhere[^126]. But I ask why they wish heretics alone to be understood as dead, since the apostle Paul said generally of sin, *The wages of sin is death*[^127]; and again, *To be wise according to the flesh is death*[^128]. And since he calls dead the widow who lives in pleasures[^129], how are those not dead who renounce the world in words and not in deeds[^130]? What, then, does the washing profit of him who is baptized by such men—except that he too, if he is such, has indeed the washing, but it does not profit him unto salvation? But if the one by whom he is baptized is such, while this man is converted to God with no false heart, then he is not baptized by that dead man, but by that living one of whom it was said, *This is he who baptizes*[^131], by whatever man baptizing bodily.

But as for what he says of heretics, *For if those who are baptized among them obtain eternal life through the remission of sins, why do they come to the Church?*—it is answered: They come for this reason, that, even if they have received the baptism of Christ as far as the celebration of the sacrament, they nevertheless do not obtain eternal life except through the love of unity: just as neither do those malevolent and envious men, to whom sins would not be remitted even if they did not hold hatred against those from whom they suffered injury; for the Truth said, *If you do not forgive, neither will your Father forgive you*[^132]: how much more when they hated those to whom they repaid evil for good[^133]? And yet these men, renouncing the world in words only and not in deeds, if afterward they were corrected, would not be baptized again, but would be sanctified by the one vital baptism. Which indeed is in the Catholic Church, but not in it alone; just as it is not in the saints alone, who are built upon the rock[^134], and out of whom that one dove is made perfect[^135].

## The truth is not to be despised for the sake of custom.

**35.** 67. Castus of Sicca said: *Whoever, despising the truth, presumes to follow custom is either envious and malicious toward the brethren, to whom the truth is revealed, or ungrateful toward God, by whose inspiration his Church is instructed*[^136].

## But if custom supports the truth, nothing is better.

**35.** 68. If this man could convict those who thought differently, and held that which the whole world too, afterward confirmed by a Christian council, maintained, of so following custom that they despised the truth, we would have to dread these words; but since that custom is found both to have been propagated from the truth and to be strengthened by the truth, we fear nothing in this opinion. And yet, if those men were *envious and malicious toward the brethren, or ungrateful toward God*[^137], behold with what sort of men they communicated, behold whom, thinking differently, they did not, as Cyprian foretold, remove from the right of communion, behold by whom they were not polluted in the preservation of unity, behold how greatly the bond of peace is to be loved; behold what they should consider who slander us about the council of our predecessor bishops, whose love they do not imitate, and by whose example, when it is considered, they are rightly condemned.

If there was a custom, as this opinion is witness, that heretics coming to the Church were received with the baptism they had, then either this was rightly done, or the wicked do not stain the good in unity. If it was rightly done, why do they accuse the whole world, because men are received in this way? But if the wicked do not stain the good in unity, how do they excuse themselves from the crime of sacrilegious separation?

## The rule of true faith is the doctrine of the Trinity.

**36.** 69. Eucratius of Theni said[^138]: *Our faith, and the grace of baptism, and the rule of ecclesiastical law, God and our Lord Jesus Christ, teaching the Apostles with his own mouth, fully completed, saying, " Go, teach the nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit "*[^139]. *The false and iniquitous baptism of heretics, therefore, is to be driven off by us and refuted with all attestation, from whose mouth is expressed venom, not life, and not heavenly grace, but blasphemy of the Trinity. And therefore it is manifest that heretics coming to the Church ought to be baptized with the whole and Catholic baptism, so that, purified from the blasphemy of their presumption, they may be reformed by the glory of the Holy Spirit.*

## Most certainly.

**36.** 70. Plainly, if it is not a baptism consecrated in the name of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, let it be reckoned the baptism of heretics, and as iniquitous let it be refuted by us with all attestation; but if we recognize this name in it, we rather distinguish the gospel words from the error of heretics, and approve what is sound in them, and amend what is faulty.

## Truth and custom are not to be set in opposition.

**37.** 71. Libosus of Vaga said: *In the Gospel the Lord says, " I am the truth "*[^140]: *he did not say, I am custom. And so, the truth having been made manifest, let custom yield to the truth: so that, even if in the past someone in the Church did not baptize heretics, now let him begin to baptize*[^141].

## He does not prove what the truth is.

**37.** 72. This man tried to show nothing of how it is the truth, to which he says custom ought to yield. Yet he helps us more against these men who have divided themselves from unity by confessing that that custom existed than by judging that it ought to yield to a truth which he does not show. For that is the custom which, if it admitted the sacrilegious to the altar of Christ without the cleansing of baptism, and polluted none of the good in unity, then whoever vainly separated themselves from that same unity, in which they could be polluted by the contagion of no evildoers, committed the manifest sacrilege of schism.

But if through that custom all perished, polluted, from what cavern do these men come forth, without original truth and with the craftiness of slander? But if the custom by which heretics were thus received had been right, let them abandon their fury, let them confess their error; let them come to the Catholic Church, not to be dyed again with the sacrament of baptism, but to be cured of the wound of their cutting-off.

## Heretics are to be execrated.

**38.** 73. Leucius of Theveste said: *Heretics, blasphemers, and the iniquitous, plucking apart with various words the holy and adorable words of the Scriptures, I judge are to be execrated; and therefore to be exorcized and baptized*[^142].

## But they are not to be rebaptized.

**38.** 74. I too judge that they are to be execrated; but not therefore to be exorcized and baptized. For it is their own fiction that I execrate, but the sacrament of Christ that I venerate.

## He thought this same thing.

**39.** 75. Eugenius of Ammedera said[^143]: *I too judge this same thing, that heretics are to be baptized.*

## But the Church does not think this.

**39.** 76. To him it is answered: But the Church does not judge this, to whom God has now, even by a plenary council, revealed what some of you then still held otherwise[^144]; and because in you love was preserved, you remained in unity.

## No water of baptism is given but ours.

**40.** 77. Likewise another Felix, of Bamaccora, said: *I too, following the authority of the divine Scriptures, judge that heretics are to be baptized; and also those who contend that they were baptized among schismatics. For if, according to the guarded saying of Christ, our fountain is private*[^145], let all the adversaries of our Church understand that it cannot be another's, nor can he who is the shepherd of one flock bestow the saving water on two peoples. And therefore it is manifest that neither heretics nor schismatics receive anything heavenly, who dare to receive from sinful men and from men estranged from the Church. When there is no place for the giver, certainly there is no profit for the receiver either[^146].

## But our fountain is like the fountain of Paradise.

**40.** 78. To him it is answered that the divine Scriptures have nowhere commanded that heretics baptized among heretics be baptized anew; but have shown in many places that all who are not in the rock, and do not belong to the members of that dove, are estranged from the Church, and yet baptize and are baptized, and have the sacrament of salvation without salvation. But that our fountain, which is like the fountain of Paradise, flows out even, like it, outside Paradise, has already been sufficiently said[^147]; and that *he who is the shepherd of one flock cannot bestow the saving water on two peoples*, that is, on his own and on the stranger, I grant and agree.

But because it is not saving to strangers, is it therefore not itself the same? For the water of the flood was saving to those set within the ark, but outside the ark it was death-dealing; yet it was the same water. And many strangers, that is, the envious, whom Cyprian says are on the devil's side and shows from the Scriptures[^148], seem to be, as it were, within, and yet, unless they were outside the ark, they would not die by the water. For baptism kills such men, when they use it wickedly; just as to those of whom the Apostle speaks, the good odor of Christ was unto death[^149]. Why, then, do schismatics or heretics not receive something heavenly—just as thorns or tares receive rain[^150], just as those very men who were outside the ark did indeed receive the water coming from the floodgates of heaven, but unto destruction, not unto salvation? And therefore that which he put at the end, *When there is no place for the giver, certainly there is no profit for the receiver either*, I do not labor to refute: because we too say that it does not profit the receivers, when they receive it in heresy, consenting to heretics; and therefore they come to Catholic peace and truth, not to receive baptism, but that what they had received may begin to profit them.

## Diverse merits do not destroy the unity of baptism.

**41.** 79. Likewise another Januarius, of Muzula, said[^151]: *I wonder that, although all confess that there is one baptism, not all understand the unity of that same baptism. For the Church and heresy are two and diverse things. If heretics have baptism, we do not have it; but if we do not have it, heretics cannot have it. But there is no doubt that the Church alone possesses the baptism of Christ, which alone possesses both the grace of Christ and the truth.*

## One must understand both the one baptism and the unity of baptism.

**41.** 80. Another could similarly say, and similarly say what is not true: I wonder that, although all confess that there is one baptism, not all understand the unity of that same baptism. For righteousness and iniquity are two and diverse things. If the iniquitous have baptism, the righteous do not have it; but if the righteous have it, the iniquitous cannot have it. But there is no doubt that the righteous alone possess the baptism of Christ, who alone possess both the grace of Christ and the truth. This is certainly false, as even they confess. For those envious men who are on the devil's side and set within, as Cyprian says[^152], and most well known to the apostle Paul[^153], had baptism; nor did they belong to the members of that dove which is safe in the rock.

## Heretics are not rebaptized but baptized.

**42.** 81. Adelphius of Tasualte said[^154]: *Without cause do certain men, by a false and invidious word, assail the truth, so as to say that we rebaptize, when the Church does not rebaptize heretics, but baptizes them.*

## The Church baptizes none but the unbaptized.

**42.** 82. On the contrary, indeed, now it does not rebaptize; because it baptizes none but those who are not baptized—which earlier custom the truth, more diligently examined, has confirmed even by a later council.

## Baptism is one, not two.

**43.** 83. Demetrius of Leptiminus said[^155]: *We who vindicate to the Catholic Church alone its own thing keep one baptism. But those who say that heretics truly and lawfully baptize are themselves the ones who make not two but many baptisms: for since there are many heresies, baptisms too will be counted according to their number.*

## Certainly, baptism is one.

**43.** 84. To him it is answered: If it is so, then let as many baptisms be counted as there are works of the flesh, of which the Apostle says, *Since those who do such things will not possess the kingdom of God*[^156]: among which heresies too are counted, and so many of these very works are tolerated within, as in chaff, and yet there is one baptism for all, which is violated by no work of iniquity.

## On the baptism of heretics and of pagans.

**44.** 85. Vincentius of Thibaris said[^157]: *We know that heretics are worse than pagans. If, converted, they should wish to come to God, they have, of course, the rule of truth which the Lord, by a divine precept, enjoined on the Apostles, saying, " Go, in my name lay on the hand, drive out demons "*[^158]. *And in another place: " Go, and teach the nations, dyeing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit "*[^159]. *Therefore, first by the laying on of hands in the exorcism, secondly by the regeneration of baptism, then they can come to the promise of Christ; but it ought not to be done otherwise.*

## Whether heretics are worse than pagans.

**44.** 86. By what rule he says that heretics are worse than pagans, I do not know; since the Lord says, *If he will not hear even the Church, let him be to you as a pagan and a tax collector*[^160]. Or is a heretic worse even than such a one? I in no way resist; yet not on that account, because the man himself is worse than a pagan, that is, than a Gentile and heathen, is the sacrament of Christ too, if he has any, mixed with his vices and morals, and corrupted and perishing by that same intermixture. For those who withdraw from the Church and become not only followers but even founders of heresies, if they withdraw already baptized, although according to this rule they are worse than pagans, nevertheless have baptism: for if, corrected, they return, they do not receive it; but if they had lost it, they would certainly receive it.

It can happen, therefore, that someone is worse than a pagan, and yet in him the sacrament of Christ not only is, but is even not worse than in a holy and righteous man. For although, as far as is in that man, he did not keep it but violated it in mind and will, nevertheless, as far as pertains to the sacrament itself, it has remained whole and inviolate even with its despiser and rejecter.

Were not the Sodomites pagans, that is, Gentiles? The Jews, then, were worse, to whom the Lord says, *It will be more tolerable for Sodom in the day of judgment than for you*[^161], and to whom the prophet says, *You have justified Sodom*[^162]; that is, in comparison with you, Sodom has been made righteous. Were the divine sacraments which were among the Jews therefore such as the Jews themselves were—the sacraments which the Lord himself also received, and to whose celebration he sent the lepers whom he had cleansed[^163], and at which, while Zechariah ministered, an angel stood by and announced that he had been heard while sacrificing in the temple[^164]? These same sacraments were both in good men of that time and in wicked men worse than pagans are—since they were set above the Sodomites in malice; and yet those sacraments were in both whole and divine.

## Even pagans have something upright and divine.

**44.** 87. For even the Gentiles themselves, if they could have anything divine and upright in their doctrines, our holy men did not disapprove, although those Gentiles, through their superstitions and idolatry and pride, and the rest of their ruined morals, were to be detested, and, unless they were corrected, to be punished by the divine judgment. For the apostle Paul too, among the Athenians, when he said certain things about God, bore witness that some of them had said something of the kind[^165]: which certainly, if they came to Christ, would be recognized in them, not disapproved. And holy Cyprian uses such men as witnesses against those same pagans. For when he was speaking of the magi: *Of whom, however,* he says, *the chief, Hostanes, both denies that the form of the true God can be seen, and says that true angels stand by his throne. With which Plato too agrees by like reasoning, and, keeping one God, calls the rest angels or daemons. Hermes Trismegistus also speaks of one God, and confesses him to be incomprehensible and inestimable*[^166]. If, then, these men had come to receive Christian salvation, it would certainly not be said to them: This evil you have, or, This false thing you have; but plainly it would deservedly be said: This, though whole and true in you, would nevertheless profit you nothing, unless you came to the grace of Christ. If, then, even in the pagans themselves something divine can be found and rightly approved, although salvation is still to be bestowed on them by Christ, we ought not to be so moved, even if heretics are worse, that we should wish to correct what is the evil that is their own in them, and be unwilling to recognize what is the good of Christ in them. But now we shall undertake the treatment of the remaining opinions of this council from another beginning.
