# Book IV

## The water of the rivers of Paradise and baptism.

**1. 1.** The Church, *compared to Paradise*[^1], indicates to us that men can indeed receive its baptism even outside it, but that no one can either obtain or hold the salvation of blessedness outside it. For the rivers too, from the spring of Paradise, as Scripture testifies, flowed forth abundantly even outside. For they are recalled by name, and through what lands they flow, and that they are established outside Paradise, is known to all[^2]; and yet in Mesopotamia or in Egypt, where those rivers reached, there is not the felicity of life that is recalled in Paradise. So it comes about that, though the water of Paradise is outside Paradise, the blessedness nevertheless is only within Paradise. So, then, the baptism of the Church can be outside the Church, but the gift of the blessed life is found only within the Church: which is also founded upon the rock, which received the keys of binding and loosing[^3]. *This is the one which holds and possesses all the power of her bridegroom and Lord:* by which conjugal power she can bear children even from handmaids; who, if they are not proud, are called into the lot of the inheritance; but if they are proud, they will remain outside[^4].

## Heretics cannot corrupt baptism.

**2. 2.** The more, then, because we fight for the honor and unity of the Church, let us not attribute to heretics whatever of hers we recognize among them: but, reproving them, let us teach that what they have from unity does not avail for salvation, unless they come to that same unity. For *the water of the Church is faithful and saving and holy*[^5] to those who use it well. But no one can use it well outside the Church. To those who use it ill, however, both outside and within, it is carried on for punishment, it does not avail for reward. And therefore baptism *cannot be corrupted and adulterated,* even if it is had by the corrupt and adulterous: *just as the Church herself too is incorrupt and chaste and modest;* and therefore the greedy, the rapacious, the usurers, do not belong to her—whom Cyprian himself testifies, in many places of his writings, to be not only outside but also within: and yet, with their hearts unchanged, they are baptized and baptize.

## In the Church of Cyprian feigners received baptism.

**2. 3.** For this too he says in a certain letter of his to the clergy about beseeching God, transferring to himself, like the holy Daniel, the sins of his people. For among the many evils he recalls, he also says this: *renouncing the world in words only and not in deeds*[^6]: as the Apostle says of certain men: *For they confess that they know God, but by their deeds they deny him*[^7].

These, then, the blessed Cyprian charges to dwell within the Church itself, who are baptized with their heart unchanged for the better, since they certainly renounce the world in words, not in deeds, whereas the apostle Peter says: *So too baptism now saves you by a like form; not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the appeal of a good conscience to God*[^8]; which certainly they did not have, of whom it is said: *renouncing the world in words only and not in deeds:* and yet he does this, rebuking and reproving them, that at some time they may walk in the way of Christ, and prefer to be his friends rather than the world's.

## Through conversion the sacrament profits.

**3. 4.** If they had obeyed him, and had begun now to live rightly, not as false Christians, but as true Christians, would he order them to be baptized again? Certainly not; rather, true conversion would bestow this upon them, that the sacrament which, while they were unchanged, availed for ruin, might, once they were changed, begin to avail for salvation. For these too are not *devoted to the Church*[^9], who seem to be within and live against Christ—that is, act against the commandments of Christ: nor are they to be judged to belong at all to that Church which he so cleanses with the washing of water in the word, that he may present to himself a glorious Church, not having spot or wrinkle or any such thing[^10]. But if they are not in that Church, to whose members they do not belong, they are not in the Church of which it is said: *One is my dove, the only one of her mother*[^11]: for that is the one without spot or wrinkle. Or let him assert, who can, that those who renounce the world in words, not in deeds, are members of this dove[^12].

Meanwhile, as for what we see—whence, I think, it was said: *He who minds the day, minds it to the Lord*[^13]: for God judges every day. But according to his foreknowledge, who knows whom he has predestined before the foundation of the world to be conformed to the image of his Son[^14], many even who are openly outside, and are called heretics, are better than many good Catholics. For what they are today, we see; what they will be tomorrow, we do not know. And to God indeed, with whom the things that are to come are present, even what they will be already is: but we, according to what each man is in the present, ask whether he is to be counted today among the members of that Church which is called the one dove, and the bride of Christ without spot or wrinkle,

of whom Cyprian says, in the letter which I have recalled, *that they did not hold the way of the Lord, nor keep the heavenly commandments given them for salvation, that they did not do the will of the Lord: zealous for patrimony and gain, pursuing pride, given over to rivalry and dissension, neglectful of simplicity and faith, renouncing the world in words only and not in deeds, each pleasing himself and displeasing all*[^15].

But if that dove does not recognize them among her members, and to such men, if they remain in the same perversity, the Lord is going to say: *I never knew you; depart from me, you who work iniquity*[^16]: they seem to be in the Church, but they are not; rather, they even *act against the Church. How, then, can they baptize with the baptism of the Church*[^17], which profits neither themselves nor those who receive it, unless they are inwardly changed by true conversion, so that the sacrament itself, which did not profit them when they received it, while they were renouncing the world in words and not in deeds, may begin to profit them when they have begun to renounce it in deeds too[^18]? So too with those whose separation is open; because neither these nor those are today among the members of that dove, but some of them perhaps will be.

## Not to baptize heretics is not to approve their baptism.

**4. 5.** We therefore do not *accept the baptism of heretics* when we do not baptize after them: but what we recognize to be Christ's, even in wicked men—whether openly outside, or secretly separated within—we receive with due veneration, those men being corrected in that wherein they went astray. But just as I seem to be pressed when it is said to me, *Does a heretic, then, forgive sins?*[^19], so I too press when I say: *Does, then, he who does not keep the heavenly commandments—the greedy, the rapacious, the usurer, the envious, renouncing the world in words and not in deeds—forgive sins?*[^20]

If through the power of the sacrament of God, then both the one and the other; if through his own merit, then neither the one nor the other. For that sacrament is recognized to be Christ's even in wicked men: but in the body of the one dove, incorrupt, holy, modest, not having spot or wrinkle[^21], neither the one nor the other is found. And just as baptism does not profit the one receiving it who renounces the world in words and not in deeds[^22], so it does not profit him who is baptized in heresy or in schism: but to each, when corrected, that begins to profit which before did not profit, but nevertheless was present.

## One baptized in heresy does not become a temple of God.

**4. 6.** *One baptized in heresy,* then, *does not become a temple of God*[^23]: is he, however, on that account not to be held as baptized? For neither does the greedy man baptized within become a temple of God, if he has not departed from greed; for those who become a temple of God certainly possess the kingdom of God. But the Apostle says, among many things: *Neither the greedy nor the rapacious will possess the kingdom of God*[^24]. For the same apostle in another place compares greed to the service of idols: *And greed,* he says, *which is the service of idols*[^25]. Which sense the same Cyprian so heightened in his letter to Antonian[^26] that he did not hesitate to compare greed to the sin of those who in time of persecution had professed by certificates that they would offer incense.

So, then, one baptized in heresy in the name of the holy Trinity nevertheless does not become a temple of God, if he has not departed from heresy, just as neither does one baptized in greed in the same name become a temple of God, if he does not depart from greed, which is the service of idols. For this too the same apostle says: *What agreement has the temple of God with idols?*[^27] Let it therefore not be asked of us whose temple of God he becomes whom we say does not become a temple of God[^28]. And yet he is not on that account not baptized, nor does his unclean error bring it about that the sacrament he received, consecrated by the words of the Gospel, is not holy: just as neither can the greed of that other man—which is the service of idols and a great uncleanness—bring it about that the baptism he receives is not holy, even if he is baptized by another greedy man likewise through the same words of the Gospel.

## The sin of one who knows is graver than that of one who does not know.

**5. 7.** *Accordingly,* he says, *in vain do certain men who are overcome by reason set custom against us, as though custom were greater than truth; or as though that were not to be followed in spiritual matters which has been revealed for the better by the Holy Spirit*[^29]. This is plainly true, that reason and truth are to be set before custom. But when truth supports custom, nothing ought to be held more firmly.

Then he continues and says: *For one who errs in simplicity can be pardoned,* he says, *as the apostle Paul says of himself, 'I who was first a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and insolent; but I obtained mercy, because I did it in ignorance'*[^30]. *But after inspiration and revelation have been made, one who perseveres in that wherein he had erred, knowingly and wittingly, sins without the pardon of ignorance. For he relies on a certain presumption and obstinacy, when he is overcome by reason.*

This is most true, that the sin of one who has sinned knowingly is far graver than that of one who has sinned unknowingly. And therefore the holy man Cyprian, not only learned but also teachable—who himself so understood what the Apostle says in praise of the bishop he describes[^31] that he said *this too is to be loved in a bishop, that he not only teach knowingly, but also learn patiently*[^32]—I do not doubt that, if he were treating this question, long and much turned over in the Church, with those most holy and most learned men through whom it afterward came about that that ancient custom was confirmed even by a plenary council, he would without hesitation show not only how learned he was in the things he had perceived with the firmest truth, but also how teachable in the things he had less attended to.

And yet, since it is most manifest that one who knows sins much more gravely than one who does not know, I would wish someone to tell me: if one falls into heresy not knowing how great an evil it is, and another does not depart from greed, knowing how great an evil it is, which of them is the worse. I can also put it thus: If one rushes into heresy unknowing, and another knowingly does not depart from idolatry—since the Apostle too says, *Greed, which is the service of idols*[^33]: and Cyprian himself understood the same sentence no otherwise, as I said, writing to Antonian, where he says[^34]: *Let not the new heretics flatter themselves in this, that they say they are not in communion with idolaters; since among them too there are adulterers and defrauders who are held by the crime of idolatry. For 'know this, understanding it, that every fornicator, or unclean person, or defrauder, which is to be an idolater, has no inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and of God'*[^35]: *and again, 'Mortify therefore your members that are upon the earth; fornication, uncleanness, and evil desire, and greed, which is the service of idols'*[^36].

I ask, then, who sins more gravely: he who unknowing has fallen into heresy, or he who knowing has not departed from greed, that is, from idolatry? According indeed to that rule by which the sins of those who know are set before the sins of ignorance, the greedy man with his knowledge wins in wickedness.

But lest perhaps the magnitude of the crime itself in heresy do what the knowing commission does in greed, let the unknowing heretic be made equal to the knowing greedy man: although the testimony which he himself set down from the Apostle does not seem to show this. For what do we detest in heretics except blasphemies? But, wishing to show that sins pertain to the ease of pardon of the ignorant, he set down from the Apostle where he says: *I who was first a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and insolent; but I obtained mercy, because I did it in ignorance*[^37].

But if, as I said, both sins can be weighed equally—the blasphemy of the ignorant and the idolatry of the knowing—and there be judged by the same sentence both he who, in seeking Christ, fell into a plausible discourse of falsehood, and he who, knowing, resists Christ speaking through the Apostle: *That every fornicator, or unclean person, or greedy man, which is the service of idols, has no inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and of God*[^38]: why, in the one, are baptism and the words of the Gospel disapproved, but in the other approved, when each is found alien from the members of that dove? Or is it because the one is an open litigant outside, that he may not be let in; but the other a crafty flatterer within, that he may not be cast out?

## The apostles said nothing about rebaptizing heretics.

**6. 8.** But as for what he says: *Nor let anyone say: What we received from the apostles, this we follow: since the apostles handed down only one Church, and one baptism, which is established only in that same Church*[^39]: this does not move me so much that I should dare to reject the baptism of Christ even when found among heretics (just as I must approve the Gospel itself when I find it among them, although I detest their error), as it admonishes me that there were certain men even in the times of the holy Cyprian who said that that custom—against which the African councils were acting, and about which he himself said a little before, *In vain do certain men who are overcome by reason set custom against us*[^40]—was handed down by the apostles.

Nor do I find why the same Cyprian found that custom—which after Cyprian was confirmed even by a plenary council of the whole world—so robust even before, that, when a man endowed with such great knowledge sought the authority he might follow for changing it, he found only the council of Agrippinus, held in Africa alone a few years before him. And since he perceived that this did not suffice for him against the practice of the whole world, he seized upon these reasonings, which we now, considering more diligently—confirmed by the antiquity of the custom itself, and afterward by the authority of a plenary council—find to be plausible rather than true: which seemed true to him as he labored in a most obscure question and wavered about the forgiveness of sins, whether it could fail to take place in the baptism of Christ, and whether it could take place among heretics:

and if something was less revealed to him there, that the magnitude of his love—by which he did not desert unity—might be proved, no one ought on that account to dare to set himself before such great firmnesses and merits of his virtues and such an abundance of his graces, merely because, admonished by the firmness of a universal council, he sees something that Cyprian did not see, because the Church did not yet have a plenary council on this matter.

Just as no one is so insane as to set himself before the merits of the apostle Peter, because, taught by the letters of the apostle Paul and strengthened now by the very custom of the Church, he does not compel the gentiles to judaize, which Peter once had compelled them to do[^41].

## We do not find anyone baptized by the apostles.

**6. 9.** We therefore do not *find that anyone, when he had been baptized among heretics, was admitted by the apostles with that same baptism and was in communion.* But neither do we find this, that anyone coming from heretics, who had been baptized among them, was baptized again by the apostles[^42]. But that custom, which even then men looking upward did not see to have been instituted by those who came later, is rightly believed to have been handed down by the apostles. And such things are many, which it would be long to recount. Wherefore, if those were saying something—those whom Cyprian, wishing to persuade them of what seemed good to him, addresses when he says, *Nor let anyone say: What we received from the apostles, this we follow*[^43]—how much more robustly do we now say: What the custom of the Church has always held, and what this disputation could not dissuade, and what a plenary council confirmed, this we follow? To this is added that, the reasonings and the testimonies of the Scriptures on both sides of the disputation being well perceived, it can also be said: What the truth has declared, this we follow.

## Philippians 1:18 is not to be set against this.

**7. 10.** For indeed that which certain men set against the disputations of Cyprian[^44]—that the Apostle says, *Nevertheless, in every way, whether by pretext or in truth, let Christ be announced*[^45]—Cyprian rightly refutes, showing that it does not pertain to the cause of heretics; since the Apostle was speaking of those who, with malevolent envy, seeking their own, were nevertheless within:

they were indeed announcing Christ, as the truth holds by which we believe in Christ, but not with that disposition with which he was announced by the good evangelists, the children of that dove. *For Paul,* he says, *was not speaking in his letter about heretics, or about their baptism, so that he should be shown to have set down anything pertaining to this matter.*

*He was speaking about brothers, whether walking disorderly and against ecclesiastical discipline, or keeping the evangelical truth in the fear of God. And some of them he set down as having spoken the word of God constantly and intrepidly, but some as caught up in envy and dissension; some as keeping benevolent love toward him, but others as having a malevolent dissension: but nevertheless that he patiently endured all things, provided that, whether in truth or by pretext, the name of Christ which Paul preached came to the notice of very many, and the still new and rough sowing of the word grew dense by the preaching of those who spoke. Now it is one thing for those who are within the Church to speak in the name of Christ; another for those who are outside, and act against the Church, to baptize in the name of Christ*[^46].

These words of Cyprian seem to admonish us to distinguish between those who are wicked outside and those who are wicked within. And those of whom the Apostle says that they announced the Gospel not chastely but through envy[^47], he says were within, and he says truly. Yet I think I do not say this rashly: If outside no one can have anything that is Christ's, neither can anyone within have anything that is the devil's. For if that enclosed garden[^48] could have the thorns of the devil, why could not the spring of Christ flow even outside the garden? But if it could not, whence, among those who were within even in the times of the apostle Paul himself, came so great an evil of envy and malevolent dissension? For these are the words of Cyprian. Or is envy and malevolent dissension perhaps a small evil? How, then, were these men in unity, who were not in peace? For the voice—not mine, nor any man's, but the Lord's own; nor through men, but through angels—sounded at Christ's birth: *Glory to God in the highest, and on earth peace to men of good will*[^49]. Which certainly would not have sounded by angelic mouth at Christ's birth on earth, unless God willed it to be understood that those are in the unity of the body of Christ who are in the peace of Christ; and that those are in the peace of Christ who are of good will. Now just as in benevolence there is good will, so in malevolence there is ill will.

## On the great evil of envy.

**8. 11.** But now envy itself, which cannot but be malevolent, how great an evil is it? Let us not seek other witnesses: Cyprian himself suffices for us, through whom the Lord thundered so many most true things about zeal and spite, and commanded wholesome things. Let us read, then, the letter of Cyprian on zeal and spite, and let us see how great an evil it is to envy those who are better—of which evil he teaches, from memory, that the origin came from the devil himself: *To be zealous over what you see to be good, and to envy those who are better, seems to certain men a light and slight fault, most beloved brothers.*

Then a little later, when he sought the head and origin of that evil: *Hence the devil,* he says, *at the very beginnings of the world, both perished first and destroyed.* And a little later: *What an evil it is,* he says, *most beloved brothers, by which an angel fell, by which that lofty and illustrious sublimity could be circumvented and overthrown, by which the very one who deceived was himself deceived? Thenceforth envy stalks on the earth, while the one destined to perish through spite obeys the master of perdition, while he imitates the devil who is zealous, as it is written, 'But by the envy of the devil death entered into the world; they therefore imitate him who are of his party'*[^50].

These words of Cyprian, in a letter known to the people, how true, how forceful we recognize them to be. Truly it befitted Cyprian both gravely to reprove and to admonish concerning zeal and spite—he whose heart, by such an abundance of love, he proved to be deeply alien from so deadly an evil: which love being most vigilantly kept, with his colleagues who thought differently about baptism not malevolently, while he himself thought otherwise not by malevolent contention but by human temptation—that which God would afterward reveal to him, persevering in love, when he willed[^51]—he remained in the unity of communion, openly saying: *judging no one, nor removing anyone from the right of communion if he should think differently. For none of us has set himself up to be a bishop of bishops, or by tyrannical terror drives his colleagues to the necessity of obeying.*

And at the end of this letter: *These things we have written back to you briefly,* he says, *according to our littleness, most dear brother, prescribing or prejudging for no one, so that each of the bishops may do what he thinks, having the free power of his own judgment. We, as far as in us lies, do not contend with our colleagues and fellow bishops on account of heretics, with whom we keep divine concord and the Lord's peace; especially since the Apostle too says: 'But if anyone seems to be contentious, we have no such custom, nor the Church of God'*[^52]. *By us there is kept, patiently and gently, the love of the spirit, the honor of the college, the bond of faith, the concord of the priesthood. For this reason we have also now written a little book,* On the Good of Patience, *as far as our littleness availed, with the Lord permitting and inspiring, which we have sent to you for our mutual love*[^53].

## The great patience of Cyprian.

**9. 12.** With this patience of love he tolerated not only, in an obscure question, good colleagues thinking otherwise not malevolently, just as he himself too was tolerated, until in process of time the most wholesome custom, when God willed, with the truth also declared, was confirmed by a plenary council; but also manifest wicked men, most well known to him, who thought otherwise not by the obscurity of the question, but, by the morals of a lost life, did contrary to what they preached, as the Apostle says of them: *You who preach that one should not steal, do you steal?*[^54]

For of such men, even those of his own time, his own colleagues, and remaining in communion with him, he says in his letter: *While their brothers in the Church are hungry, to wish to have silver abundantly, to seize estates by treacherous frauds, to increase their interest by multiplying usuries*[^55]. For here there is no obscurity of the question; Scripture openly proclaims: *Neither the greedy nor the rapacious will possess the kingdom of God*[^56]; and: *He who has not put out his money at usury*[^57]; and: *Every fornicator and unclean person and greedy man, which is the service of idols, has no inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and of God*[^58]. Such greedy men, then—so that they not only greedily treasured up their own, but also fraudulently snatched away the goods of others—such idolaters, as he himself both understands and convicts them, he would certainly not reprove unless he knew them.

For he would not bear false witness against his own fellow bishops. And yet, on account of Christ, who died for the weak[^59], lest, the weeds being rooted up before the time, the wheat too should be rooted up along with them[^60], he tolerated them with the bowels of fatherly and motherly love: imitating, of course, the apostle Paul, who endured those malevolent and envious toward him, with that same love toward the Church[^61].

## Even the envious have and give baptism.

**9. 13.** But nevertheless, because *by the envy of the devil death entered into the world,* and *they imitate him who are of his party*[^62]—not through that by which they were created by God, but through that by which they were made perverse by themselves; as Cyprian himself recalls, since the devil too, before he was the devil, was an angel and was good: whence are they in the unity of Christ who are of the party of the devil? Beyond doubt, as the Lord himself says: *An enemy man has done this,* who *sowed weeds over the field*[^63]. As, then, even within, what is the devil's is to be reproved, so too outside, what is Christ's is to be acknowledged. Or does Christ not have his own outside the unity of the Church, and does the devil have his own within the unity of the Church?

This perhaps can be said of men, that God has none of his own outside the communion of the Church, just as among the holy angels the devil has none of his own. But to this Church, which still bears the mortality of the flesh, as long as it is a pilgrim away from the Lord[^64], it has been permitted to the devil to mingle weeds, that is, wicked men; and he has been allowed to do so on account of the very pilgrimage of the Church, that the rest of that homeland which the holy angels fully enjoy might be more ardently desired: but about the sacraments this cannot be said. For just as those men can have and handle them, not for their salvation but for their ruin, in which case the inner weeds are destined for the fire: so too the outer weeds, which they received from the inner ones as they departed; because in departing they did not lose them. Which is without doubt made manifest when it is not restored to those returning, when perhaps some of those very ones who had departed come back. Nor let anyone say: But what wheat have the weeds? For if this is so, both within and outside the condition is, in this respect at least, equal. For grains of wheat are not found among the outer weeds and found among the inner. But when the question is about the sacrament, it is not asked whether the weeds have anything of wheat, but whether they have anything of heaven: for to both the outer and the inner weeds, together with the wheat itself, the rain is common, which is heavenly and sweet in itself, even if from it the weeds grow barrenly: so too the evangelical sacrament of Christ is divine and sweet, and not to be disapproved on account of the barrenness of those whom it also rains upon outside.

## Outside there can be wheat, and within weeds.

**10. 14.** Someone will say that the inner weeds are more easily turned into wheat. I grant it is so: but what does this pertain to the repeating of baptism? Is it that, if perhaps someone converted from heretics, by the time and ease of his conversion, anticipates one who is more slowly corrected and changed within from his malice, baptism is not to be repeated for him; while for the one who has been anticipated by the one who came from heretics, because he was more slowly corrected, it is to be repeated? Nothing, then, pertains to the matter now in question, who converts sooner or more slowly from his own perversity to the rectitude of faith, or of hope, or of love.

For even if the inner wicked can more easily become good, yet sometimes some of the number of the outer ones anticipate by conversion some inner ones, and, while those remain in barrenness, being reconciled to unity, they bear fruit with endurance, whether thirtyfold, or sixtyfold, or a hundredfold[^65]. Or if those alone are to be called weeds which persist to the end in malignant error, then both outside there are many grains of wheat, and within there are many weeds.

## Whether the outer wicked are worse than the inner.

**10. 15.** But are the outer wicked worse than the inner? It is indeed a great question, whether Nicolaus, already outer[^66], was worse than Simon, still inner[^67], because the one was a heretic, the other a magician. But if division itself, because it is a most certain sign of violated love, is held to be the graver evil, I grant it is so. But many, having lost love, do not on that account go outside, because they are held by worldly emoluments, and, seeking their own, not the things of Jesus Christ[^68], are unwilling to depart not from the unity of Christ, but from their own advantages. Whence it was said in praise of love: *It does not seek its own*[^69].

## The same discourse continues.

**10. 16.** Now, then, it is asked how men of the devil's party can belong to the Church, which has no spot or wrinkle or any such thing[^70], of which it was also said: *One is my dove*[^71]. But if they cannot, it is manifest that she groans among aliens, both those plotting within and those barking from without. Yet such men, even within, both receive baptism, and have it, and hand it on, holy in itself, and it is in no part violated by the malignity of those in which they persist to the very end.

Wherefore the same blessed Cyprian teaches us that baptism is to be considered in itself, consecrated by the words of the Gospel as the Church received it, with no perversity and malice, whether of those receiving or of those handing it on, joined or mingled with it: since he himself admonishes us of both—both that there were certain men within not keeping benevolent love, but caught up in envy and malevolent dissension, of whom the apostle Paul spoke; and that the envious are of the devil's party, as he testifies in the most open words in the letter he wrote on zeal and spite. Wherefore, since it is manifest that it can happen that, in those who are of the devil's party, the sacrament of Christ is nevertheless holy—not for their salvation, but for their judgment—not only if after receiving baptism they are perverted, but even if they receive it as such men, as the same Cyprian shows, renouncing the world in words only and not in deeds[^72]; and that, on that account, if they are afterward corrected, that which they had received while perverse is not to be repeated:

as far as I judge, it is now clear and plain that in this question about baptism we must not think who gives, but what he gives; or who receives, but what he receives; or who has, but what he has. For if men of the devil's party, and therefore in no way belonging to the one dove, can nevertheless both receive and have and give the holiness of baptism, in no way violated by their own perversity, as we are admonished from the letters of Cyprian himself: why do we attribute to heretics things that are not theirs? Why do we say that what is Christ's is theirs, and not rather recognize in them both the standards of our Emperor and amend the deeds of deserters? Wherefore it is indeed one thing, as the holy Cyprian says, *for those who are within the Church to speak in the name of Christ; another for those who are outside, and act against the Church, to baptize in the name of Christ*[^73].

But both many who are within act against the Church by living wickedly, and by drawing weak souls over to their own way of life; and some who are outside speak in the name of Christ, and are forbidden not to do the things that are Christ's, but to be outside, when, that they may be healed, we either rebuke, or reprove, or exhort them.

For that man too was outside, who did not follow along with the disciples, and was casting out demons in the name of Christ, whom the Lord admonished should not be forbidden[^74]: although certainly he was to be cured in that wherein he was wounded, by those words of the Lord where he says: *He who is not with me is against me; and he who does not gather with me scatters*[^75]. So then, some things are done outside in the name of Christ, not against the Church; and within, things are done by the devil's party against the Church.

## The perversity of men is to be corrected within and outside.

**11. 17.** What of this, which is even wonderful, which one who attends diligently finds can happen—that certain men, with love preserved, teach something useless, as Peter compelled the gentiles to judaize[^76], as Cyprian himself held that heretics should be baptized again: whence to such good members rooted in love, and in some respect not walking rightly, the Apostle says: *If you are of any other mind, this too God will reveal to you*[^77]: and that certain men, on the other hand, teach something wholesome without love, of whom the Lord says: *They sit in the chair of Moses. What they say, do; but what they do, do not do; for they say, and do not do*[^78]? Whence the Apostle too says of those envious and malevolent men, who nevertheless announced Christian salvation: *Whether by pretext or in truth, let Christ be announced*[^79].

Wherefore both within and outside the perversity of men is to be corrected, but the divine sacraments and words are not to be attributed to men. He therefore does not *act as patron to heretics* who does not attribute to them what he recognizes to be, though among them, yet not theirs[^80]. We do not *concede baptism to the heretic;* but the baptism of him of whom it was said, *This is he who baptizes*[^81], wherever we find it, we acknowledge. But the *faithless and the blasphemer,* if he persists in faithlessness and blasphemy, receives *the forgiveness of sins* neither *outside the Church* nor within the Church: or, if on account of the power of the sacrament he receives it for a point of time, both outside and within the same power works, just as the power of the name of Christ worked the casting out of demons even outside.

## If a perverse man receives baptism, it does not profit him for salvation, but the sacrament remains holy.

**12. 18.** But indeed *we find the apostles in all their letters execrating and detesting the sacrilegious depravity of heretics, so that they say that their talk creeps like a cancer*[^82]. What of it? Even those who said, *Let us eat and drink, for tomorrow we die*[^83], does not Paul make plain to be corrupters of good morals through evil conversations, at once adding: *Evil conversations corrupt good morals*[^84]; and yet he indicated that they were within, when he said: *How do some among you say that there is no resurrection of the dead?*[^85] But where does he not detest the greedy? Or indeed could anything be said more vehemently than that greed should be called the service of idols, as the same apostle set down[^86]: nor did Cyprian understand it otherwise[^87], and inserted it into his letters where it was needed; who nevertheless confesses that in his times there were in the Church not ordinary greedy men, but robbers and usurers; and not just any men, but bishops. And yet even those of whom the Apostle says, *Their talk creeps like a cancer*[^88], I would wish to understand as having been outside; but Cyprian himself does not allow me.

For when, writing to Antonian, he showed that before the time of the final separation of the just and the unjust one must in no way withdraw from the unity of the Church on account of the mingling of the wicked—where he declares how holy he is, and how most worthy of that glory of martyrdom which he deserved—he says: *How great is the swelling of arrogance, how great the forgetfulness of humility and gentleness, and how great the boasting of one's own arrogance, that anyone should dare, or believe he can do, what the Lord did not concede even to the apostles, that he should think he can distinguish the weeds from the wheat,*

*or, as though it were conceded to him to carry the winnowing fan and to cleanse the threshing floor, should try to separate the chaff from the wheat: and when the Apostle says, 'But in a great house there are not only gold and silver vessels, but also wooden and earthen'*[^89]*, he should seem to choose the gold and silver vessels, but to despise and cast off and condemn the wooden and earthen, when it is only on the day of the Lord that the wooden vessels are burned up by the fire of the divine blaze, and the earthen are broken by him to whom the iron rod was given*[^90]?

Thus, then, Cyprian, reproving those who, as though avoiding the fellowship of the wicked, had broken themselves away from unity, shows that by the great house which the Apostle spoke of, where there were not only gold and silver vessels, but also wooden and earthen, he understood nothing but the Church, where there are good and wicked, until in the end, like a threshing floor winnowed, it is cleansed[^91]. But if this is so, in the Church itself, that is, in that great house, there were vessels for dishonor, whose talk crept like a cancer. For when the Apostle was speaking of them, he taught this: *And their talk,* he says, *creeps like a cancer; among whom are Hymenaeus and Philetus, who have wandered from the truth, saying that the resurrection has already taken place, and they subvert the faith of some. But the firm foundation of God stands, having this seal: The Lord knows those who are his; and let everyone who names the name of the Lord depart from iniquity. But in a great house there are not only gold and silver vessels, but also wooden and earthen*[^92]. If, then, even those whose talk crept like a cancer were, as vessels for dishonor, in the great house—which great house Cyprian understands as the unity of the Church itself—did their cancer violate the baptism of Christ?

So then, neither outside, nor within, can anyone who is of the devil's party stain, either in himself or in anyone, the sacrament which is Christ's. The talk that *creeps like a cancer to the ears of hearers*[^93] does not, therefore, give the forgiveness of sins: but when baptism is given with the words of the Gospel, with whatever perversity he who gives it, or he to whom it is given, understands it, it is in itself holy on account of him whose it is. And if anyone, receiving it through a perverse man, nevertheless does not receive the perversity of the minister, but only the holiness of the mystery, joined to the unity of the Church in good faith and hope and love, receives the forgiveness of sins; not through words creeping like a cancer, but through the evangelical sacraments flowing from the heavenly spring. But if he himself who receives is perverse, what is given does not profit the perverse man unto salvation; and yet what is received remains holy in him, nor, if he is corrected, is it repeated for him.

## How one who is in darkness baptizes.

**13. 19.** There is therefore no *fellowship of justice with iniquity*[^94]; not only that which is outside, but also that which is within. For *the Lord knows those who are his; and let everyone who names the name of the Lord depart from iniquity*[^95]. There is also no *communion of light with darkness*[^96]; not only that which is outside, but also those which are within. For *he who hates his brother,* says John, *is in darkness even until now*[^97]. And certainly those hated Paul, who, announcing Christ in envy and malevolence and dissension, thought they were stirring up tribulation for his bonds[^98]; whom nevertheless the same Cyprian understands to have been within. *Since, then, darkness cannot enlighten, nor iniquity justify*[^99], as he himself says, I ask how these men can baptize within the Church itself?

I ask how those vessels, which the great house contains not in honor but in dishonor, can, within that same great house, minister to men to be sanctified what is holy—except because that holiness of the sacrament can neither be polluted by the unclean, whether when it is given through them, or when it is received by those who are not changed for the better in heart and life? Of whom, placed within, he himself says: *renouncing the world in words only and not in deeds*[^100].

## There are sinners in the Church, and yet they perform spiritual and divine things.

**13. 20.** There are, then, even within, *enemies of God, and men whose hearts the spirit of Antichrist has besieged:* and yet *they perform spiritual and divine things*[^101], which can neither profit themselves unto salvation as long as they are such, nor can they stain those things with their own uncleanness. As for what he says—that *nothing is permitted them unto ecclesiastical grace and salvation, who, scattering and attacking the Church of Christ, are by Christ himself called adversaries, and by his apostles antichrists*[^102];

it is to be taken thus, that such men are found both outside and within: yet the separation of those who are within, from the perfection and unity of that dove, not only does God know in certain men, but men too know in certain men; for, beholding their most wicked life and confirmed wickedness in the open, and comparing it with the rules of the divine precepts, they understand to how many weeds and chaff, placed whether outside or within, but nevertheless to be most openly separated in the end, the Lord is going to say: *Depart from me, you who work iniquity*[^103]; and: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^104].

## God calls sinners to repentance by scourges and rods.

**14. 21.** But of no one's conversion is one to despair, whether established outside or within, as long as the patience of God leads him to repentance[^105], and visits their crimes with the rod, and their sins with scourges. For in this way he does not scatter his mercy from them[^106], if they too at some time have mercy on their own soul, pleasing God[^107]. For just as the good man who has persevered to the end, he shall be saved[^108], so the wicked man, whether outside or within, who has persevered to the end, shall not be saved.

Nor do we say that *those baptized anywhere and in any way obtain the grace of baptism*[^109], if the grace of baptism is understood in salvation itself, which is conferred through the celebration of the sacrament: but this salvation many do not obtain even within, although it is manifest that they have the sacrament, which is holy in itself. Rightly, then: *The Lord admonishes us in the Gospel not to consent to those who give evil counsel*[^110], *but walk under the name of Christ*[^111]: but these are found both within and outside, because they do not go outside unless they have first been wicked within; and certainly of the vessels placed in the great house the Apostle was speaking: *But if anyone cleanses himself from such things, he will be a vessel for honor, sanctified, useful to the Lord, always prepared for every good work*[^112].

But how each one ought to cleanse himself from such things, he showed a little above, saying: *Let everyone who names the name of the Lord depart from iniquity*[^113]; lest he hear in the end, together with the chaff—whether that which flew before from the threshing floor, or that which is to be separated at the last: *Depart from me, you who work iniquity*[^114]. Whence it appears indeed, as Cyprian says, that *those things are not to be at once taken up and received which are boasted of in the name of Christ, but those which are done in the truth of Christ*[^115]. But it is certainly not done in the truth of Christ to seize estates by treacherous frauds, to increase interest by multiplying usuries[^116], to renounce the world in words only[^117]; all which things he himself declares, a sufficiently fit witness, to be done even within.

## Heretics come to the Catholic Church not that what is the gift of God may be repeated.

**15. 22.** But now, as for what he pursues in many words, that *those who blaspheme the Father of Christ cannot be baptized in Christ*[^118]—since it is manifest that they blaspheme by error (for one who approaches the baptism of Christ does not openly blaspheme the Father of Christ[^119], but is convicted of blaspheming by thinking otherwise about the Father of Christ than the truth teaches)—we have now sufficiently shown that to baptism, which is consecrated by the words of the Gospel, the error of anyone, whether giving or receiving, does not pertain, whether he think otherwise about the Father, or about the Son, or about the Holy Spirit, than the heavenly doctrine conveys. For many carnal and natural men are baptized even within, though the Apostle openly says: *But the natural man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God*[^120]; and, with baptism already received, he says they are still natural[^121].

But according to the carnal sense the soul given over to bodily senses cannot but think carnally about God. Whence many, making progress after baptism—and especially those baptized as infants or boys—the more their understanding is made serene and enlightened, while the interior man is renewed from day to day[^122], deriding and detesting and confessing, cast off their former opinions, which they held about God while they were deluded with their phantasms. Nor are they on that account thought not to have received baptism, or said to have received such a baptism as their error was: but in them both the integrity of the sacrament is honored, and the vanity of the mind is amended, even if it had grown hard, confirmed and perhaps defended with many contentions.

Wherefore even the heretic who is plainly outside received baptism there, the evangelical baptism—he certainly did not receive such a baptism as the error by which he is blinded. And therefore, if, coming to his senses, he sees that what he wrongly held is to be left behind, he is not at the same time to leave behind the good he had received: nor, because his error is to be disapproved, is the baptism of Christ on that account to be blown away in him.

For now, from those whom it happens are baptized within while thinking false things about God, it is sufficiently clear that the truth of the sacrament is to be distinguished from the error of the one who believes wrongly, although both can be found in one man; and therefore, when even one established in some error outside has nevertheless been baptized with a true sacrament, when he is restored to the unity of the Church, just as a true faith succeeds a false one, a true baptism cannot thus succeed even to a true baptism: because the very same thing cannot succeed itself; because neither can it depart. To this end, then, heretics come to the Catholic Church: that what is evil of theirs may be corrected, not that what is the good of God may be repeated.

## In heretics and schismatics the division is to be corrected, not the truth of the sacrament.

**16. 23.** Someone says: Is there, then, no difference, if two are established in equal error and malice, and, with life and heart unchanged, one of them is baptized outside, the other within? I confess there is a difference: for he is worse who is baptized even outside, not because he is baptized, but because he is outside; for of division itself the evil is neither none nor small: provided, however, that the one who is baptized within willed to be within not on account of some earthly or temporal advantage, but because he preferred the unity of the Church spread throughout the whole world to the cuttings-up of schisms; otherwise he too is to be counted among those who are outside.

Let us therefore set up two men in this way: one of them, for example, thinking about Christ what Photinus held, and being baptized in his heresy outside the communion of the Catholic Church; the other thinking this same thing, but being baptized in the Catholic Church, supposing it to be the Catholic faith. This latter I do not yet call a heretic, unless, the doctrine of the Catholic faith being made manifest to him, he has preferred to resist it, and has chosen what he held; which, before it happens, it is manifest that the one who is outside is the worse.

And so in this man only the false opinion is to be corrected, but in that man the division itself also; but in neither of them is the truth of the sacrament to be repeated. But if anyone thinks the same as they do, and knows that there is a heresy separated from Catholic unity where this is taught and learned, but for the sake of some worldly advantage has willed to be baptized in Catholic unity, or, having been baptized in it, has on this account been unwilling to go out from it; he is to be held not only as separated, but even so much the more wickedly, the more he adds to the error of heresy and the division of unity the deceit of pretense. Wherefore the depravity of any man, the more dangerous and tortuous it is, the more urgently and laboriously it is to be corrected: nor yet, on that account, if he has anything intact—especially not his own, but God's—is it, on account of his depravity, to be thought either nothing, or to be censured in the same way, or to be attributed to his depravity, and not to the bounty of him who, even to the soul fornicating away from him, and going after her lovers, gave his bread, and his wine, and his oil, and other things, whether nourishments or ornaments, which are hers neither from herself nor from her lovers, but from him who, pitying her, willed everywhere to admonish her to whom she should return[^123].

## Whatever schismatics have, outside the Church it does not avail for salvation.

**17. 24.** *Can the power of baptism,* he says, *be greater or more potent than confession? than passion? that a man should confess Christ before men, and be baptized in his own blood? And yet,* he says, *not even this baptism profits the heretic, if, though he confessed Christ, he was killed outside the Church*[^124]. This is most true: for one killed outside the Church is convicted of not having had love, of which the Apostle says: *And if I hand over my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^125]. But if for this reason—because love is lacking—the passion profits nothing, neither does it profit those whom Paul says, and Cyprian expounds, to live within in envy and malevolence without love: and yet they can both receive and hand on true baptism[^126].

*There is no salvation outside the Church*[^127]. Who denies it? And therefore whatever things are had of the Church herself do not avail for salvation outside the Church. But it is one thing not to have, another not to have profitably. He who does not have is to be baptized that he may have; but he who has it unprofitably is to be corrected that he may have it profitably. Nor is *the water adulterous in the baptism of heretics*[^128]: because neither is the creation itself which God founded evil, nor are the words of the Gospel, in whatever erring persons, to be censured, but their error, in which the soul is adulterous, even if it has from the lawful husband the ornament of the sacrament.

*Baptism, therefore, can be common to us and to heretics*[^129], with whom the Gospel too can be common, although their error differs from our faith: whether they think otherwise about the Father, or about the Son, or about the Holy Spirit, than the truth holds, or, cut off from unity, do not gather with Christ, but scatter[^130]: because the sacrament of baptism can be common to us, if we are the Lord's wheat, even within, with the greedy, with the rapacious, with the drunken, and the rest of pests of this kind, of whom it is said: *They will not possess the kingdom of God*[^131], and yet the vices by which they are separated from the kingdom of God are not common.

## Comparison between heretic sinners and Catholic sinners.

**18. 25.** For not of heresies alone does the Apostle say: *That those who do such things will not possess the kingdom of God*[^132]. But let it not be irksome to attend a little to what he enumerates together: *The works of the flesh are manifest,* he says, *which are fornications, uncleannesses, debaucheries, the service of idols, sorceries, enmities, contentions, rivalries, animosities, dissensions, heresies, envyings, drunkennesses, carousings, and the like; which I foretell you, as I have foretold, that those who do such things will not possess the kingdom of God*[^133]. Let us therefore set up a heretic who is chaste, continent, not greedy, not serving idols, hospitable, ministering to the needy, an enemy of no one, not contentious, patient, quiet, rivaling no one, envying no one, sober, frugal: there is certainly no doubt for anyone that, for this alone, that he is a heretic, he will not possess the kingdom of God. Let us set up another who is a fornicator, unclean, debauched, greedy, or even more openly given over to idols, a sorcerer, discordant, contentious, a rival, animose, seditious, envious, drunken, a carouser, but a Catholic: will he, for this alone, that he is a Catholic, possess the kingdom of God, doing such things, of which the Apostle thus concludes: *Which I foretell you, as I have foretold, that those who do such things will not possess the kingdom of God*[^134]? If we say this, we deceive ourselves[^135]. For the word of God does not deceive us, which neither is silent, nor spares, nor deceives us with any flattery. For on this account it says elsewhere too: *For know this, understanding it, that every fornicator, or unclean person, or greedy man, which is the service of idols, has no inheritance in the kingdom of Christ and of God: let no one deceive you with empty words*[^136]. There is therefore nothing for which we may complain of the word of God. It says altogether, and openly and freely says, that those who live wickedly do not belong to the kingdom of God.

## Scripture permits impunity to no Catholic sinner.

**19. 26.** Let us, then, not flatter a Catholic hedged about with all these vices, nor dare to promise him, because he is a Catholic Christian, the impunity which divine Scripture does not promise: nor, if he has any one of the things that have been mentioned, ought we to promise him the fellowship of that heavenly homeland. For to the Corinthians he enumerates them singly, in each of which singly it is understood that they will not possess the kingdom of God: *Do not err,* he says; *neither fornicators, nor those serving idols, nor adulterers, nor the effeminate, nor those who lie with men, nor thieves, nor the greedy, nor drunkards, nor revilers, nor the rapacious will possess the kingdom of God*[^137].

He did not say: Those who have all these vices together will not possess the kingdom of God: but: Neither these, nor those; so that in each you understand this, that none of them will possess the kingdom of God. As, then, heretics will not possess the kingdom of God, so the greedy will not possess the kingdom of God. Nor is it indeed to be doubted that the very punishments by which those will be tormented who will not possess the kingdom of God are diverse according to the diversity of crimes, and some sharper than others, so that in the eternal fire itself there may be unequal torments of punishments for the unequal weights of sins.

For not in vain does the Lord himself say: *It will be more tolerable for Sodom than for you in the day of judgment*[^138]. But nevertheless, for not possessing the kingdom of God, whichever of those vices you choose as the milder avails just as much as either several or one which you perceive as the graver. And because those will possess the kingdom of God whom that judge will set on his right hand, and to those who will not deserve to be set on the right hand nothing else will remain than to be on the left; there is no other voice which, as goats, they will hear from the mouth of the shepherd, except: *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^139]: although from that fire, as I said, diverse punishments can be distributed according to the diversity of crimes.

## Whether we ought to prefer a Catholic of the worst morals to a heretic only.

**20. 27.** But whether we ought to prefer a Catholic of the worst morals to some heretic in whose life, besides that he is a heretic, men find nothing to reprove, I do not dare to rush to a verdict. But if anyone says that, because he is a heretic, this cannot be the only thing, but that other things follow as well—for he is carnal and natural[^140], and on this account it is necessary that he be also a rival, and animose, and envious, and an enemy of the truth itself and dissenting from it—let him understand, of those evils too, that whichever one he has chosen as the milder cannot be the only one in anyone, namely for this reason, that he is carnal and natural; just as drunkenness, which they have now grown accustomed not only to name without horror, but even to proclaim with the people's merriment, can it be the only thing in him in whom it is? For what drunkard is not also contentious, and animose, and envious, and dissenting from the soundness of the precepts, and a grave enemy of those who reprove him? But now that he should not be also a fornicator and adulterer is difficult: yet he can fail to be a heretic; just as a heretic can fail to be a drunkard, nor an adulterer or fornicator, nor debauched or a lover of money or a sorcerer, and can at the same time fail to be all these. For not all the rest follow some one vice.

And so, two being set forth—one a Catholic with all these vices, the other a heretic without those which can fail to be in a heretic—although neither disputes against the faith, and yet each lives against the faith, and each is deceived by an empty hope, and each dissents from spiritual love, and on this account each is alien from the body of that one dove[^141]: why do we recognize in the one of them the sacrament of Christ, and are unwilling to in the other, as though it were the one's or the other's; when in each it is the same, and is none but God's, and, although in the worst men, is good? And if of the men who have this, one is worse than the other, not on that account is what they have worse in the one than in the other: because neither in two wicked Catholics, if one is worse than the other, does he bear a worse baptism; nor, if one of them is good, the other wicked, is the baptism wicked in the wicked man and good in the good; but in each it is good: just as the light of the sun, or even of a lamp, is certainly not worse in worse eyes than in better ones; but the same in both, although it differently, according to their diversity, either gladdens or torments them.

## What is to be said about catechumens in relation to the kingdom of God.

**21. 28.** But that which was objected to Cyprian about catechumens, *that, caught in martyrdom and killed for the name of Christ, they are crowned even without baptism*[^142], what it pertains to the matter I do not sufficiently understand: unless perhaps because they were saying that heretics with the baptism of Christ could much more be admitted to his kingdom, whereby catechumens were admitted; whereas he himself said: *Unless one is born again of water and the Spirit, he will not enter the kingdom of heaven*[^143].

In which matter neither do I doubt that a Catholic catechumen burning with divine love is to be preferred to a baptized heretic; but even within the Catholic Church itself we prefer a good catechumen to a wicked baptized person: nor yet on that account do we do injury to the sacrament of baptism, with which the latter is already, the former not yet, imbued; nor do we think the catechumen's sacrament to be preferred to the sacrament of baptism, when we recognize some catechumen to be more faithful and better than some baptized person. For the centurion Cornelius, not yet baptized, was better than Simon, baptized. For the former was filled with the Holy Spirit even before baptism[^144], the latter was puffed up with an unclean spirit even after baptism[^145].

Nevertheless, if Cornelius, even after the Holy Spirit was already received, had been unwilling to be baptized, he would become guilty of despising so great a sacrament. But when he was baptized, he certainly did not receive a better sacrament than Simon; but the diverse merits of men were distinguished under the equal holiness of the same sacrament: thus the good or evil merit of a man does not increase or diminish the holiness of baptism. But just as a good catechumen lacks baptism for attaining the kingdom of heaven, so a wicked baptized person lacks true conversion. For he who said: *Unless one is born again of water and the Spirit, he will not enter the kingdom of heaven*[^146], himself also said: *Unless your justice abounds more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven*[^147]. For, lest the justice of the catechumen be secure, it was said: *Unless one is born again of water and the Spirit, he will not enter the kingdom of heaven*: again, lest, baptism being received, iniquity be secure, it was said: *Unless your justice abounds more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven.*

The one without the other is too little: both make the heir of that possession. As, then, we ought not to disapprove the justice of a man, which began to exist before he was joined to the Church—as the justice of Cornelius had begun to exist before he himself was in the Christian people; which, if it were to be disapproved, the angel would not have said to him, *Your almsgivings have been accepted, and your prayers have been heard*[^148]; nor, if it were to suffice for attaining the kingdom of heaven, would he have been admonished to send to Peter: so the evangelical sacrament of baptism is not to be disapproved, even if it has been received outside the Church; which, however, because it does not profit unto salvation, unless he who has the integrity of baptism, his own depravity also being corrected, is incorporated into the Church, let us so correct the error of heretics that we acknowledge in them what is, not theirs, but Christ's.

## Suffering can sometimes take the place of baptism.

**22.** 29. The same blessed Cyprian draws no slight argument from the fact that suffering can indeed sometimes take the place of baptism, citing that thief to whom, though he had not been baptized, it was said, *Today you will be with me in paradise*[^149]. Pondering this again and again[^150], I find that it is not only suffering for the name of Christ that can supply what was lacking from baptism, but also faith and conversion of heart, if perhaps in straitened circumstances there is no opportunity to celebrate the mystery of baptism.

For that thief was not crucified for the name of Christ but for the deserts of his own crimes; nor did he suffer because he believed, but he believed while he was suffering. How much weight there is, then, even without the visible sacrament of baptism, in what the Apostle says, *Man believes with his heart and so is justified, and he confesses with his lips and so is saved*[^151], was made plain in that thief. But it is fulfilled invisibly only when it is not contempt for religion but the press of necessity that shuts out the ministry of baptism.

For in Cornelius and his friends it might far more readily seem superfluous that they should also be washed with water than in the case of that thief, since in them the gift of the Holy Spirit, which holy Scripture testifies that others received only after being baptized, had already shown itself by a sure sign suited to those times, when they spoke in tongues. Yet they were baptized, and in this deed there stands apostolic authority. So far is it that no one, in whatever advance of the inner man he has made, even if before baptism he has by a devout heart advanced to spiritual understanding, ought to despise the sacrament that is applied bodily by the work of the ministers; rather, through it God works the consecration of man spiritually.

Nor do I think the office of baptizing was assigned to John, so that it was called the baptism of John, for any other reason than that the Lord himself, who had given it, by not disdaining to receive the baptism of a servant[^152], might consecrate the way of humility and, by such a deed, declare most plainly how greatly his own baptism, with which he himself was to baptize, was to be esteemed. For as the most powerful physician of eternal salvation he foresaw that there would be no lack of a certain swelling of pride in some who, having so advanced in understanding of the truth and in probable conduct that they would not in the least hesitate to set themselves, in life and teaching, above many who had been baptized, would believe it superfluous for themselves to be baptized, once they felt they had reached that disposition of mind to which many already baptized were still striving to ascend.

## What the sanctification applied bodily avails for.

**23.** 30. But what the sanctification of the sacrament applied bodily avails and effects in a person (and yet that thief was not without it, since it was not the will to receive it that he lacked, but the necessity of not receiving it that was upon him) is difficult to say. Still, unless it availed very much, the Lord would not have received the baptism of a servant. But because it is to be considered in itself, apart from the salvation of the person for whose perfecting it is applied, it sufficiently shows—both in the wicked, and in those who renounce the world in words and not in deeds[^153]—that it is itself whole, even though such people cannot have salvation unless they are corrected.

But just as in the thief salvation was made complete because, though bodily it was lacking through necessity, spiritually it was present through devotion, so too when the sacrament itself is at hand, salvation is perfected if through necessity there is lacking what was present in the thief.

This is what the whole Church holds by tradition when little infants are baptized, who certainly cannot yet believe with the heart unto righteousness and confess with the lips unto salvation, as the thief could; indeed, by weeping and wailing while the mystery is celebrated over them, they drown out those mystical words with their own; and yet no Christian would say that they are baptized to no purpose.

## A comparison with circumcision.

**24.** 31. And if anyone should seek divine authority in this matter—although what the whole Church holds, and what was not established by councils but has always been retained, is most rightly believed to have been handed down by no other than apostolic authority—we can nevertheless truly infer what the sacrament of baptism avails in little ones from the circumcision of the flesh that the former people received. For before he received it, Abraham was justified[^154], just as Cornelius too was enriched with the gift of the Holy Spirit before he was baptized. Yet the Apostle says of Abraham himself that *he received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of faith*[^155]—he who had already believed with the heart, and it had been reckoned to him as righteousness[^156]. Why, then, was he commanded thereafter to circumcise every male infant on the eighth day[^157], who could not yet believe with the heart so that it might be reckoned to him as righteousness, unless because the sacrament also availed much in itself? This was made manifest by an angel in the case of Moses' son, who, while still uncircumcised, was being carried by his mother, and at a present and evident peril it was required that he be circumcised[^158]; and when it was done, the danger was driven off.

As, therefore, in Abraham the righteousness of faith came first, and circumcision, the seal of the righteousness of faith, was added, so in Cornelius spiritual sanctification came first in the gift of the Holy Spirit, and the sacrament of regeneration was added in the washing of baptism. And as in Isaac, who was circumcised on the eighth day of his birth, the seal of the righteousness of faith came first, and since he imitated his father's faith, there followed, as he grew, that very righteousness whose seal had come first in him as an infant, so in baptized infants the sacrament of regeneration comes first; and if they hold to Christian devotion, there will also follow in the heart the conversion whose mystery came first in the body.

And as in that thief the kindness of the Almighty supplied what was lacking from the sacrament of baptism, since it had been lacking not through pride or contempt but through necessity, so in infants who die baptized, the same grace of the Almighty is to be believed to supply the fact that, not from an impious will but from the want proper to their age, they can neither believe with the heart unto righteousness nor confess with the lips unto salvation. And so, when others answer on their behalf, that the celebration of the sacrament may be fulfilled for them, it avails for their consecration, because they themselves cannot answer. But if another should answer for one who is able to answer, it does not avail in the same way. From this rule comes that saying in the Gospel which, when it is read, moves everyone naturally: *He is of age; let him speak for himself*[^159].

## The sacrament of baptism is one thing, conversion of heart another.

**25.** 32. By all these things it is shown that the sacrament of baptism is one thing and conversion of heart another, but that a person's salvation is made complete by both; nor, if one of these is lacking, ought we to think it follows that the other is lacking too, since the one can exist without the other in an infant, and the other could exist without the one in the thief, God supplying in the one case and in the other what had been lacking through no fault of the will. But when one of the two is lacking through the will, a person is entangled in guilt. And baptism indeed can be present where conversion of heart is lacking; conversion of heart, on the other hand, can indeed be present where baptism has not been received, but not where baptism has been despised. For in no way is it to be called a conversion of heart to God when the sacrament of God is despised. Rightly, then, do we reprehend, anathematize, detest, and abominate the perversity of heart in heretics; yet they do not, on that account, fail to have the gospel sacrament, merely because they do not have that through which it is profitable[^160]. *Therefore, when they come to faith and truth and, doing penance, beg that their sins be forgiven them, we do not deceive or mislead them when, having corrected and reformed them* in the matter in which they had been depraved and perverted, *we so instruct them by heavenly disciplines for the kingdom of heaven* that what in them is whole we in no way violate, nor, on account of the fault of the man, do we say that whatever of God is in the man is either nothing or faulty.

## The baptism of John is examined.

**26.** 33. A few things now remain from the letter to Jubaianus; but because in them there is also discussion of the Church's past custom and of the baptism of John—which usually raises no small question for those who pay too little attention to a matter that is plain, since those who had received the baptism of John were commanded by the Apostle to be baptized in Christ[^161]—they are not to be treated carelessly, but to be put off to another volume, lest the size of this one be immoderate.
