## Marcellus’s Preface

*Marcellus, memorial secretary, to Severianus and Julian, his most beloved brothers, worthy of reverence in the name of Christ.*

*I gladly embrace the command that you lovingly and with confidence in the Lord have given me: that a measured abridgment of my own should distinguish and assemble the parts of the entire case and the successive stages of the proceedings, which the parties’ submissions have unfolded through lengthy disputes throughout this volume of records. I feel, and acknowledge, that love alone makes me equal to this work. For in carrying out every part of an assigned duty, someone who obeys instructions out of love for those who give them has greater strength than someone who obeys out of confidence in himself. Indeed, I have already contributed my share of service, as far as my modest ability allowed, to this undertaking, in which each person labored according to his strength in the Church’s cause. For the distinguished Marcellinus, tribune and notary, a man of outstanding integrity, enlisted me to share his responsibilities, less because of any merit of mine than through the kindness of the man who chose me, and I shared with the judge the labor of weighing the dispute. Yet I was also glad to undertake this additional work that you wished me to do. For in the same matter I take no less pleasure and pride in fulfilling my brothers’ desire now than in having provided counsel to the presiding judge then.*

*Accordingly, I have drawn together in a careful abridgment everything argued at greater length by the parties and everything entered in the record on either side through the judge’s interventions. I have also attached, in sequence, the familiar numerical signs used in reckoning, so that a reader’s attention, following this abridgment as an index, may be guided by the numbers directly to whatever he wishes to find in the pages of the proceedings, with the convenience of this shorter route. Perhaps we may seem to have misused our leisure by turning superfluous matters into a task. We would admit that we had indeed done this needlessly if people were willing to exert themselves in reading the entire sequence of volumes. But it is most unfair for those people to complain that this careful work is somehow pointless, when, if equally purposeful care is demanded of them in reading through the pages of this debate, they acknowledge that they regard it as a nuisance rather than careful study. Thus, in my judgment, we have not played at a useless task by removing the tedium of reading through the remedy of brevity. Certainly, even if the result of the work itself and its usefulness cannot establish that this labor was not futile, everything I have done here is nevertheless appropriate, because in doing it I obeyed your instructions. Now I would ask that you kindly pray for me in return for my service. In this way, what you do of your own accord will nevertheless count as my reward. May the omnipotence of the Trinity, that is, the omnipotence of our God, preserve your loving hearts in unfailing devotion and continual remembrance of me, my rightly beloved lords and brothers worthy of reverence in the name of Christ.*
