# Proceedings of the Conference of Carthage (3)

## Second Hearing

**1.** In the year after the consulship of the most distinguished Varanes, on the third day before the Nones of June, in the council chamber of the Baths of Gargilius at Carthage, there were present Sebastianus, Maximianus, and Petrus, most devoted domestic protectors; Ursus, Petronius, and Libosus, ducenarii; also Bonifacius and Evasius, attendants of the illustrious and eminent authorities; Filetus, attendant of the illustrious office of the count; Exitiosus, assistant to the chief clerk; Possidius, Quodvultdeus, and Colonicus, assistants to the record keepers of the staff of our lord the most distinguished and eminent proconsul; Navigius, assistant in the accounts office of our lord the most distinguished and eminent vicar; Nampius, clerk on the staff of the most distinguished legate of nurturing Carthage; Rufinianus, clerk of the most distinguished curator of lofty Carthage. Taking notes were Hilarus and Praetextatus, clerks of our lord the most distinguished and eminent proconsul; Fabius, clerk of our lord the most distinguished and eminent vicar; and Romulus, clerk of the most distinguished legate of nurturing Carthage; also taking notes were Januarius and Vitalis, notaries of the Catholic Church, and Victor and Cresconius, notaries of the Donatist church. Libosus, ducenarius of the illustrious authorities, said:

*Since the hearing was adjourned to today, and the bishops of both parties are present, they will be admitted if Your Highness orders it*.

**2.** Flavius Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let them come.* And there entered Aurelius, Alypius, Augustine, Vincentius, Fortunatus, Fortunatianus, Possidius, Novatus, Florentius, Maurentius, Priscus, Serenianus, Bonifacius, Scillacius, Deuterius, Leo, Asterius, and Restitutus, bishops of the Catholic Church. From the other side there also entered Primian, Petilian, Emeritus, Protasius, Montanus, Gaudentius, and Adeodatus, bishops of the party of Donatus.

**3.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*It is most certain that I often made the offer at the previous hearing, and now I ask you to consent to sit.*

When the Catholic bishops had sat down, Bishop Petilian said: *We do not dare to do what was not done at the previous hearing.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Although I remember making the offer at the previous hearing too, I nevertheless think it was not done then because, with so many bishops assembled, there could be no room to sit. Therefore, since I now see that a fixed number of persons has assembled, I ask that you consent to sit at least now.*

**4.** Bishop Petilian said:

*We do not sit in the absence of our fathers, especially since sitting together is forbidden by divine law, lest we wish to sit with opponents such as these*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

**5.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*If Your Holiness has declined to hear my request that you consent to sit, this cannot be wrested from me: that I conduct the hearing standing.*

When the Catholic bishops had risen, the judge's seat was removed at his own command, and our lord Marcellinus, the most distinguished and eminent tribune and notary, stood.

**6.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Let it be recorded that this is your wish, not ours*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

**7.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*With you standing, it is fitting for me to have such a wish, which concerns the respect owed to so many bishops*.

Bishop Petilian said*: You will have acted honorably*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

**8.** Martialis, the recording clerk, said:

*We have in our hands the written notice that the bishops presented to Your Nobility yesterday. If Your Nobility commands it, we will read it*.

**9.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let it be read, so that nothing may seem to have been concealed from the other party*.

**10.** Bishop Petilian said:

*We both say that we are bishops of the truth of Christ our Lord, and this has often been stated in public records. As for Donatus of holy memory, a man of a martyr's glory, our predecessor and the ornament of the church of this city, we honor him according to his place and merit*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

**11.** Bishop Possidius of the Catholic Church said:

*It is necessary to prove that you are bishops of the truth, not boast of it*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**12.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*The proceedings will record the statements of both parties*. And he added: *Let what was ordered be read*.

Martialis the clerk read: *In the year after the consulship of the most distinguished Varanes, on the fourth day before the Nones of June, to Flavius Marcellinus, the most distinguished and eminent tribune and notary: Primian, Petilian, Emeritus, Protasius, Montanus, Gaudentius, and Adeodatus, bishops and advocates of the Church of truth, say the following by written notice. Yesterday, on the Kalends of June, Your Eminence deigned to hear the matter between us and our opponents. And since, offering patience to the parties through almost the entire day, you deigned to hear certain matters that formed the preliminaries of the business, and it is necessary for the case that the mandate our opponents presented be made known to us, because the sequence of the proceedings cannot be transcribed owing to its length, lest we appear to come unprepared to court, command that copies of the mandate our opponents gave to be read be furnished to us today, the fourth day before the Nones of June, so that we may attend equipped for the business entrusted to us.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Primian, signed this notice on the fourth day before the Nones of June, at Carthage*.

Flavius Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Since it is clear that requests for the purpose of preparation should be granted, so that no obstacles of delay may suspend the coming hearing, the staff will hasten to deliver to you the mandate given by the Catholic bishops, transcribed under the supervision of the custodians appointed in person*.

And when he had read this,

**13.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let what has been read be entered in the proceedings*. *And he added: Since, among the many conditions settled at the previous hearing, one was clearly deferred—that, just as I would sign my rulings, each person would sign his statements—you must state Your Holiness's opinion on this*.

**14.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*We expressed our agreement to this in our letter*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**15.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let the will of the other party also be made known.*

**16.** Bishop Petilian said:

*It makes us very uneasy that we are asked, and commanded to do, something that customary practice has never observed*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

**17.** Bishop Fortunatianus of the Catholic Church said:

*Let it be attested that the opposing party has refused what was published in Your Nobility's edict*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**18.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Let it be attested that you have still not returned many churches or complied with his earlier edict*. And in another hand: *As written above.*

**19.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Then, for the complete trustworthiness of the proceedings, Your Holiness is satisfied with the custodians chosen by both sides and evidently assigned to the notaries and clerks*.

**20.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Let the proceedings first be issued to us; then we will answer*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked.*

**21.** Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*Then doubt is being cast either on the integrity of the staff or on the integrity of the custodians*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**22.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*The reliable record of the previous proceedings clearly shows that Your Holiness also agreed to this.*

**23.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Let us read and review them*. And in another hand: *As written above*.

**24.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let the staff read both what concerns the custodians chosen by each side and the point to which the hearing was adjourned.*

**25.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Let the proceedings first be issued to us. Let us read them again and review our words and our opponents' presentation. The laws have always allowed this. This is humane procedure*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

**26.** Bishop Fortunatianus of the Catholic Church said:

*Although the recent proceedings could not have escaped anyone, nevertheless we wish Your Nobility's understanding to be informed by this, so that it may be understood that they now refuse, through a certain delay, to come to the business of the case*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**27.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let what I ordered be read, so that I may know to what point the hearing was adjourned.*

**28.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*If you permit, it falls to me to address this.*

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Proceed.*

Bishop Emeritus said:

*Human minds are indeed sometimes so ready in their quickness that by sight or hearing they readily grasp everything brought to their notice. But perhaps that may be granted to learned or educated men according to the nature of the matter itself; I profess myself less suited to it. For who, noble sir, can retain in his understanding or grasp a fleeting voice and a swift speech as its very syllables pass by, when every discussion and dispute requires examination of the words, the meanings, and the inner details of the business? Therefore, when a sheet is read and the fleeting words are, as it were, scattered upon the air, our minds cannot grasp and retain them. Where, or to whom, has this been granted? If they enjoy such a gift of mind, let them not boast in the arrogance of their memory. For my part, I can only repeat things more often and learn through prolonged reading, so that I may [find] the assurance of truth, reread what I said, and observe what my opponent objected. You understand that this is the purpose of proceedings: written records are arranged in letters precisely so that memory alone, which cannot contain everything, may not be deceived by forgetfulness and lose knowledge of what it did. Indeed, foresight and wisdom provided for knowledge through writings and public records for no other reason than this: that what may be imperfectly grasped and understood in reading because of silence, the passage of a long time, or the continuous course of the proceedings themselves may be recognized through reading and constant study as what was debated in court. You see, then, noble sir, that our requests are just, and that the case should not be debated in passing; rather, once the proceedings have been issued and made known to us and everything has been examined, you should hear the case*. And in another hand: *As written above*.

**29.** Bishop Possidius of the Catholic Church said:

It is written: *In a multitude of words you will not escape sin. Since we keep this before our eyes, by God's inspiration, we do not wish to seem verbose. Therefore, if you agree, let us come to the case, which can soon be conducted and decided; but delaying statements*. And while he was speaking. (And in another hand: *I have checked*.)

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let what I ordered be read*.

**30.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*As he brought forward a passage from Scripture, it is written for us*: Hidden wisdom, an unseen treasure—*what use is there in either?* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked.*

**31.** Bishop Possidius of the Catholic Church said:

*There has never been wisdom in a multitude of words*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**32.** Martialis, the recording clerk, read from the codex:

*Your Nobility said: Since it is clear that almost the whole day has elapsed, let it be stated whether both parties agree to adjourn the business. Both parties said: We agree, since this day now seems to be spent. Your Nobility said: Let each party indicate to which day this adjournment should be made. Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said: As Your Nobility prescribed in the edict, with tomorrow allowed for transcribing the sheets, let us attend the day after tomorrow, if you command it. Bishop Adeodatus said: We too agree that the business be conducted the day after tomorrow. Your Nobility said:*

*As the parties have agreed, the business will resume the day after tomorrow.* And when he had read this,

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Your Holiness sees that you cannot go against your own agreements. Therefore let the case be pleaded.*

**33.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*We are not so forgetful as to go against our own statements. Nor do I condemn the staff's lack of care or the clerks' negligence here, where I understand that the length of the proceedings prevented them from being ready [by] the day appointed. But if it has been done and the business completed with everything transcribed, why is what was done not made known to us? For since the staff has dealt with what Your Nobility ordered to be reviewed, I must receive everything and consider and examine on what grounds and in what ways I ought to answer. From their mandate I have learned that the case is so confused that I must both be instructed about the past and understand, concerning the present, whom I ought to answer*. And in another hand: *I, Emeritus, have checked*.

**34.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Although Your Holiness's declaration alone suffices, nevertheless it is beyond doubt that yesterday you both requested and received whatever you thought could suffice for your fullest preparation. Therefore let us now come to the merits of the case*.

**35.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Your Highness observes that the clerk read from the notebooks what was promised or pronounced the day before yesterday. From this I recognize all the more that the records have not yet been written out. But if they have been written out and are ready, let the clerk himself answer. Let him produce the proceedings. Sign them; we will examine them and afterward answer.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

**36.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*I recognize a desire for delay and evidence of a changed intention. For, because of the length of the proceedings, Your Holiness could have requested at the previous hearing that the business be adjourned today. Moreover, yesterday, by submitting a written notice, you requested the delivery of what you thought sufficient for your fullest preparation*.

**37.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Let the clerks say whether in one day they could write out what appears to have been done in one day*. And in another hand: *As written above*.

**38.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let the clerks state for the record whether all the proceedings could be transcribed onto sheets yesterday*.

Martialis the clerk said: *We still have a portion in the notebooks. Indeed, the bishops assigned to us as custodians know this too.*

**39.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Your Highness has learned from the staff's answer that the records have not yet been set out on pages, and that there is nothing for either party to read and review, nor anything that can be given or briefly recalled to memory. Therefore, since an honorable request is being made, let Your Highness deign to grant what we ask: that the proceedings be issued in the customary way. Let us read so that we may answer*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

**40.** Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*The length of the proceedings lies more in the number and responses of the bishops of both parties than in any presentation of the business. Therefore there is no ground for complaint by the opposing party or for adjourning the hearing today, especially since they themselves declared that the business should be adjourned to today.* And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**41.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*So that it may again be clear to the parties, let the declaration of the venerable men be read once more.*

Bishop Petilian said: *From what will it be read?* And in another hand: *As written above*.

Bishop Montanus said: *Let it be read to us from a sheet*. And in another hand: *As written above.*

**42.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Then do they think that what is entered in the notebooks should be called into any doubt, when so many bishops were appointed as custodians?*

**43.** Bishop Petilian said:

*We do not know shorthand; nor is the nature of things, or of these characters themselves, if I may call them such, such that anyone can read another's shorthand. We cannot read the notebooks. Unless the proceedings are issued on pages, I have nothing to examine, nothing to read*. And in another hand: *As written above*.

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said: *As was said, let the venerable men's declaration be read again*.

**44.** Martialis, the recording clerk, again [read from the shorthand notebook:

*Your Nobility*] *said: Since it is clear that almost the whole day has elapsed, let it be stated whether both parties agree to adjourn the business. Both parties said: We agree, since this day now seems to be spent. Your Nobility said: Let each party indicate to which day this adjournment should be made. Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said: As Your Nobility prescribed in the edict, with tomorrow allowed for transcribing the sheets, let us attend the day after tomorrow, if you command it. Bishop Adeodatus said: We too agree that the business be conducted the day after tomorrow*. *Your Highness said: As the parties have agreed, the business will resume the day after tomorrow.* And when he had read this,

Bishop Petilian said: *From what did he read?*

**45.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*I think Your Holiness is aware of what the authority of the laws has determined on this point, since one may not go against one's own agreements. Therefore, lest they perhaps suspect any fraud on the part of the staff's clerks, let this same agreement be read from the tablets of the ecclesiastical notaries*.

**46.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*When we entered, Your Nobility deigned to interpose and ask whether we assented to the published edicts and whether we would affix our signatures to our own statements. I do not see which requirement I am to satisfy. For I am bound by Your Nobility's proclamation, in which you promised that I would put my signature to the proceedings and that everything done publicly would be publicly displayed. But now I am compelled to act and be rushed into the business before knowing the proceedings. What is this? I do not know which to choose first; I do not recognize which purpose of your judgments I am to follow. If the page must be signed, let the page be issued so that it can be signed. If this requirement is to be abandoned, your entire edict will totter; whatever you proclaimed to the public will be destroyed. For you said that everything done should be made publicly known through our signature and Your Highness's endorsement. Therefore what has been done must first be read, published, and known, so that, by Your Highness's judgment, we may proceed to the remaining matters of the business.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked.*

**47.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*As I ruled, let the ecclesiastical notaries read through what the staff's clerks have read.*

**48.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Keep to your course, noble sir; maintain the justice you have so often promised. For when our side objected the day before yesterday that the lawful day for the case appeared already to have passed, and that those who summoned us to court had forfeited their case, we were compelled to wish to abandon that condition and appear to agree to the present time. Let no one be ungrateful to me on this point, noble sir. For if I allowed so much time, subject to these reservations, provided they did not contradict their own undertakings, why is a short time not allowed me for the proceedings to be written and signed by Your Highness, so that I may see and read through what I ought to sign, grasp and recover my own meaning, and also my opponents' cunning and subtle ingenuity, so that, fully prepared, I may answer their claims? I think this humane and just. Do not, noble sir, give this matter and this most just petition any treatment that would send me away in anxiety. For you have promised to be most just, and we must believe it, provided just requests are in no way denied us.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked.*

**49.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*These things ought more properly and justly to have been requested at the previous hearing, when the hearing was adjourned to this day by the agreement of both parties. Therefore let the ecclesiastical notaries read to what point the hearing was adjourned.*

Bishop Petilian said: *Then we have been trapped.* And in another hand: *As written above*.

Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*What our brothers standing on the opposing side wish to blame on us was not done because of us*. And while he was speaking, (and in another hand: *I have checked*.)

Bishop Petilian said: *You are insulting us*. And in another hand: *As written above*.

**50.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*Your Nobility's edict states that the fourth month ends on the Kalends of June. We arrived earlier, and those who now seem to stir up resentment against us on this account gave their mandate on the eighth day before the Kalends of June, although, if they had arrived before the appointed day, they ought to have given their mandate before that day. Let Primian's own declaration also be read, stating on which day he promised to appear, and let this question be resolved, over which great hostility toward us has already been stirred up among the people.* And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**51.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*It is clear that these matters were already settled at the previous hearing, especially since the persons involved have evidently been confirmed on both sides. Nevertheless, if, when the case begins to be reconsidered, Your Holiness thinks these things should be read again, they will more appropriately be read then, although it is unnecessary to reconsider them after the persons have been confirmed. Therefore, as I ruled, let the ecclesiastical notaries read what was ordered*.

**52.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*Let it be recorded that we agreed to the day on the condition that these men maintained their own intention*. And in another hand: *As written above.*

**53.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let the ecclesiastical notaries read the tablets received*.

When a parchment roll of sheets, partly transcribed, and notebooks of tablets, likewise sealed, were brought before the judge's eyes within a cloth (that is, linen) wrapping,

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let the custodians state whether they recognize the seals*.

Bishop Leo of the Catholic Church said:

*I recognize my seal*. Bishop Marinianus said: *I recognize my seal*.

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Since it is clear that the impressions of the seals have been recognized by both parties, let the notaries read what I ordered*.

**54.** Bishop Petilian said: *It is wills, not proceedings, that are customarily opened in this manner*. And in another hand: *As written above*.

**55.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*What was done out of concern for both parties and by common agreement ought not to be criticized*.

Bishop Petilian said: *I do not criticize the precaution, but ask* [in accordance with] *customary procedure that the proceedings be lawfully written out, so that they can be read and examined. What urgency is there? Why the haste?* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

**56.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*Let it only be established that they themselves wish to postpone the matter. We ask Your Highness to grant it. It is humane: they wish to consider, to examine, and to come better prepared. Let us not, by refusing to postpone, make the proceedings longer, so that their very length brings us greater delays*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**57.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*It is clear that both parties agreed that the hearing be adjourned to today, an agreement which I see cannot be of use today. Meanwhile, as I ordered, let the ecclesiastical notaries read to what point the hearing was adjourned.*

**58.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Let it be recorded that this happens through no fault of ours, but solely because the notaries or clerks could not meet the deadline*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

**59.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Read*.

Januarius, notary of the church of Sitifis belonging to the party of Donatus, read from the notebook:

*Your Nobility said: Let the staff state how much of the day has now elapsed. Rufinianus, clerk of the most distinguished curator, said: Eleven hours of the day have elapsed. Your Nobility said: Since it is clear that almost the whole day has elapsed, let it be stated whether both parties agree to adjourn the business. Both parties said: We agree, since this day seems to be spent. Your Nobility said: Let each party indicate to which day this adjournment should be made. Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said: As Your Nobility prescribed in the edict, with tomorrow allowed for transcribing the sheets, we will attend the day after tomorrow, if you command it. Bishop Adeodatus said: We too agree that the business be conducted the day after tomorrow. Your Highness said: As the parties have agreed, the business will resume the day after tomorrow.*

And when he had read this.

**60.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*The declarations of both parties have shown that the hearing was adjourned to this day so that the business would be conducted today*.

**61.** Bishop Petilian said:

*If the clerks have completed the proceedings, let it be conducted*. And in another hand: *As written above.*

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Although the clearest declarations indicated to which day the hearing was adjourned, nevertheless, lest anything seem to have been denied the party requesting delay, the hearing will be adjourned, provided that it states to which day the trial should be postponed.*

Bishop Petilian said:

*Let the clerks say when they will issue the proceedings.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked*.

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let the clerks not delay in stating when the proceedings can be transcribed onto sheets and completed for issue*.

Hilarus, the recording clerk, said:

*As both parties agreed, only when they have signed their statements on the sheet can we state the day for issuing the proceedings.*

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Can the sheets be completed by the staff or signed by both parties today?*

Hilarus, the recording clerk, said:

We will complete the sheets today.

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Since the staff has reported that it can complete the sheets today, let Your Holiness state whether you are ready to sign today or tomorrow*.

**62.** Bishop Adeodatus said: *When the sheet has been presented to us, we will sign*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Adeodatus, have checked.*

**63.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let the staff now state how many days' delay after the signing of the sheets can suffice for issuing the proceedings, without any excuse*.

**64.** Hilarus, the recording clerk, said:

*If they sign tomorrow or today, we can, working vigilantly day and night, issue the proceedings after the third day, provided their notaries dictate to us from the signed sheet.*

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let the staff specify which day that is.*

Rufinianus, clerk to the most distinguished curator, said:

*Today is the third day before the Nones of June.*

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*I am asking for the day on which the proceedings have been promised for issue.*

Hilarus, the recording clerk, said:

*We can issue the proceedings on the seventh day before the Ides of June.*

**65.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Although great doubt now arises in me concerning the declarations, nevertheless let both parties indicate once more whether, on the sixth day before the Ides of June, with all evasions cut away, the principal business is to be conducted.*

**66.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*We wish it today too, and would have wished it were it possible. Nevertheless, since it was humane to grant our brothers what they seem to us to have requested not unjustly, we promise, with the help of the Lord our God, to be present on the sixth day before the Ides of June to conduct the principal business of the Church*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let the other party also deign to state its intention.*

**67.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*There is no ambiguity in our declarations. As for the preparation we demanded should take place in court, Your Nobility observes that our requests were just and lawful. We will attend on the appointed day, that is, the sixth day before the Ides of June, if God wills; provided, however, that after receiving the proceedings the most impartial judge grants us some time for examination.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked*.

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*The proceedings will be delivered to you in the morning on the seventh day before the Ides of June. State whether you will consent to be present on the next day, that is, the sixth day before the Ides of June, so that the principal business may be conducted*.

Bishop Emeritus said:

*We will attend the hearing if what Your Nobility has determined has been carried out.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked.*

**68.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Mindful of the risk and their own declaration, the clerks will hasten to issue the proceedings on the appointed day, in the morning, that is, on the seventh day before the Ides of June, with the notaries of both parties present to dictate. Let both parties know that they must give the staff a receipt for the proceedings, also specifying the hour. It befits bishops to cut away evasions and weave no obstacles of delay.*

**69.** Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*In accordance with Your Nobility's command, when we have received the proceedings, we will duly give assurance concerning the signature and the time.* And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**70.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*When the proceedings are presented to us, we will comply with the rulings*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked.*

**71.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*So that no further excuse may arise, the staff will hasten to issue to you the proceedings of this day as well on the same day.*

**72.** Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*Many falsehoods were circulated the day before yesterday. Lest the people be disturbed by deception of this kind, we ask that Your Nobility's edict make known that they themselves requested the delay*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**73.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*What the people say ought not to disturb Your Holiness. Nevertheless, as determined in my edict, the proceedings will be publicly displayed*.

And below:

*We, Hilarus and Martialis, clerks, have issued these proceedings, and have likewise corrected them as above.*

Flavius Marcellinus, the most distinguished and eminent tribune and notary, said:

*Everyone remembers that I promised some time ago, in a published edict, that whatever took place between the bishops of both parties would promptly become publicly known. Therefore I have taken care to show Your Holiness what has taken place thus far, so that you may judge with your own eyes this portion of the debate.* And in another hand: *Let it be posted*.

EDICT OF THE JUDGE

Flavius Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*I would indeed wish, as I now desire with the whole force of my prayers, that the joy arising from the demonstration of truth and the final exposure of error might be matched by rejoicing at a turning away from ancient superstition and a change of opinion for the better. For who could doubt the greatness of the joy when an unexpected cure comes to a long-standing illness? In such cases, restored health gives greater cause for exultation than health always retained.*

*But since the obstinacy of ruined minds, bound together by the bonds of a perverse conviction, either pretends not to return to the path of salvation that has been shown or, worse still, fears to do so through shamelessness, the judgment of the laws must now be brought forth and enforced. Those whom gentler medicines fail to restore to good health after the truth has been openly displayed before them must be restored by a more forceful course of treatment.*

*For to whom does the recent argument not clearly show, as the appended sequence of proceedings demonstrates and the inserted passages from the divine books also attest, that no one can be made guilty through the fault of another's crime, even if that crime could have been proved? Who does not see that the standing of the universal Church ought not to be overturned by an accusation against Caecilian, against whom nothing blameworthy could be proved, and that even the sentence passed against him by those who separated themselves from the body of the Church could not prejudice him, just as the condemnation recently pronounced by the Maximianists against the absent Primian was unable to harm him? And for whose correction would it not suffice in every respect that so many judgments disclosed and established that Donatus originated the schism, that Caecilian was absolved and vindicated, and that the final judgment of Constantine of triumphant memory even more clearly demonstrated both Caecilian's innocence and the malicious accusation of Donatus and his associates, while the vindication of Felix of Aptugna, his ordainer, was also shown by the documents of the proconsular judgment? Therefore, if these things, so clear and evident, cannot cure the chronic disease, the discharge of the festering wound must be restrained, so that, with its freedom curtailed, it may begin to harm only itself, since this is what it prefers. For through these measures it will come about that it may at last desire to be healed.*

*Let exposed falsehood therefore bow its neck to revealed truth. Accordingly, by the authority of this edict I admonish all members of the municipal order, owners of estates, managers, tenants of both the imperial household and private properties, and the elders of all localities: mindful of the laws and of their own rank, reputation, and safety, they must strive to prohibit Donatist assemblies in all cities and localities. They must hasten without delay to deliver to the Catholics the churches which, as is clear, I allowed them by my own humanity, without an imperial command, until the day of judgment, unless they prefer to become entangled in the snares of so many enactments. Those churches will certainly be theirs if they are willing to assent to Catholic unity. But with greater reason and diligence, after unity has been revealed in every way and superstition convicted, they must be driven from the pursuit of their own crime.*

*Those who, after the prohibitions of the laws, mingle in their assemblies must understand beyond doubt that they can no longer avoid the penalty prescribed by imperial decision, especially those who have clearly returned to their profane gatherings and communion after acknowledging the reverence due to the Catholic law. That this did not happen earlier I had deferred to some extent, solely from a regard for patience and the hope of correction. Let it suffice, then, that the enemies of the Christian faith have insulted the laws thus far, especially since their persons are certainly known. Why must they become subject to the bonds of so many enactments, when no amendment has followed?*

*Let the Donatists know that, even after the victory of truth, the Catholic bishops' decision remains the same: they may be received once the arrangements have been fulfilled which, as the public remembers, were offered to them before and are now certainly offered without doubt. It is certain that the pledge of the earlier edict is preserved in all respects. Therefore every bishop in the communion of Donatus must return to his own locality without any disturbance or harassment, so that, established in his own place, he may either return to the one true Church or, without evasion, no longer delay compliance with the laws. Those who know that they have crowds of Circumcellions on their estates must know that, unless they strive by every means to suppress and restrain their insolence, those properties in particular will soon be seized by the treasury. For in this matter provision is made both for the Catholic law and for public peace, so that their madness may subside.*

*A rereading of the proceedings, which discloses that the above-mentioned error of schism arose from the Donatists and that Caecilian and the others whom they wickedly thought fit to accuse were vindicated, will be able to instruct Your Holiness most fully.* (And in another hand): *Let it be posted*. (Given at Carthage) in the year after the consulship of the most distinguished Varanes, on the sixth day before the Kalends of July.
