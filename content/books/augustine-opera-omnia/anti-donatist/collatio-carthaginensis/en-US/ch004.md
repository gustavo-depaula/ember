# Proceedings of the Conference of Carthage (4)

## Third Hearing

**1.** In the year after the consulship of the most distinguished Varanes, on the sixth day before the Ides of June, at Carthage in the council chamber of the Baths of Gargilius, there were present Sebastianus, Maximianus, and Petrus, most devoted domestic protectors; also Vincentius and Taurillus, most devoted imperial agents; Ursus, Petronius, and Libosus, ducenarii of the illustrious authorities; Bonifacius and Evasius, attendants of the illustrious and eminent authorities; Filetus and Octavianus, attendants of the illustrious office of the count; Restitutus and Exitiosus, assistants to the chief clerk; Possidius, Quodvultdeus, and Colonicus, assistants to the record keepers of the staff of our lord the most distinguished and eminent proconsul; Navigius, assistant in the accounts office, and Peregrinus, assistant to the subordinate assistants on the staff of our lord the most distinguished and eminent vicar; Nampius, clerk on the staff of the most distinguished legate of nurturing Carthage; Rufinianus, clerk of the most distinguished curator of lofty Carthage. Taking notes were Hilarus and Praetextatus, clerks of our lord the most distinguished and eminent proconsul; Fabius, clerk of our lord the most distinguished and eminent vicar; Romulus, clerk of the most distinguished legate of nurturing Carthage; also taking notes were Januarius and Vitalis, notaries of the Catholic Church, and Victor and Cresconius, notaries of the Donatist church. Ursus, ducenarius of the illustrious authorities, said:

*At the previous hearing the bishops of the Donatist party requested a respite until today for the proceedings to be issued to them, so that, instructed by them, they might answer in Your Highness's court. Both parties are now outside the doors; if you command it, they will be admitted.*

**2.** Flavius Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let them come*. And there entered the Catholic bishops Aurelius, Alypius, Augustine, Vincentius, Fortunatus, Fortunatianus, and Possidius, with their fellow bishops Novatus, Florentius, Maurentius, Bonifacius, Priscus, Serenianus, and Scillacius, and Deuterius, Leo, Asterius, and Restitutus, likewise fellow bishops and custodians of the proceedings. From the opposing side there also entered the Donatist bishops Primian, Petilian, Emeritus, Montanus, Gaudentius, and Adeodatus, with their fellow bishops Peregrinus, Aptus, Clarentius, and Habetdeus.

**3.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*It is certain that the business was adjourned so that today, after the proceedings had been issued, the principal case would be conducted. Let the staff state whether this has been done*.

Martialis, recording clerk to our lord the most distinguished and eminent proconsul, said:

*It is established that, in accordance with Your Nobility's command, the proceedings were issued before the day our humble selves had promised. If Your Nobility orders it, we will demonstrate this fully from the receipts of both parties.*

**4.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let them be read*.

Martialis, the recording clerk, read:

*In the year after the consulship of the most distinguished Varanes, on the eighth day before the Ides of June: I, Fortunatianus, Catholic bishop of the city of Sicca, wrote that I received together the proceedings of the two hearings, the first held on the Kalends of June and the second on the third day before the Nones. I received them from Martialis, clerk of the proconsular office, assigned to Flavius Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, on the eighth day before the Ides of June, at the fifth hour of the day, in the restored Catholic church. Although the clerks promised to issue the proceedings on the seventh day before the Ides of June, their diligence hastened matters more effectively, so that the records of both hearings were delivered to us together before the promised day. Having received them at the place and time written above, we promise to come prepared to conduct the business on the day specified in the records*. When he had read this, he said: *I will read the other, if Your Nobility commands it.*

**5.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Read*.

Martialis the clerk read: *In the year after the consulship of the most distinguished Varanes, on the eighth day before the Ides of June: I, Montanus, bishop of the city of Zama Regia, wrote to you, Hilarus and Praetextatus, clerks, that I received from you the proceedings of the two hearings in which we contended with our betrayers and persecutors, the first held on the Kalends of June and the other on the third day before the Nones of the same month. You, clerks of the proconsular office mentioned above, delivered them to us before our fathers and fellow bishops on the eighth day before the Ides of June, at the third hour of the day, in the Theoprepia church. Having received them at the place and time written above, we declare that we will come to conduct the business on the day specified in the records, with the proper procedure preserved*.

**6.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*The receipts read will be attached to the present proceedings*. And he added: *Since it is clear that the proceedings were issued before the appointed day, it remains for the principal business to be presented.*

**7.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*We have long desired that the principal business be concluded. Therefore, if it is granted even at this late stage, since they desire to be on the opposing side, let them prove the charges so often brought against the holy Catholic Church spread throughout the world and never proved.* And while he was speaking, (and in another hand: *I have checked*.)

**8.** Bishop Adeodatus said:

*Let them state their case. Let us know what they are doing.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Adeodatus, have checked, reserving the right of appeal*.

**9.** Bishop Vincent of the Catholic Church said:

*Have you not learned their proposition from the mandate?* And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**10.** Bishop Possidius of the Catholic Church said:

*Let no one introduce snares of delay, if we have any confidence in defending our case*. And while he was speaking, (and in another hand: *I have checked*.)

**11.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*If a charge is being brought, let it be stated*.

**12.** Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*Let their mandate be read, and Your Nobility will learn their charge from it*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**13.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*With all delays removed, since it is clear that everything concerning the preliminaries of the business was settled at the previous hearing, let the case be pleaded*.

**14.** Bishop Fortunatus of the Catholic Church said:

*The opposing party is shown to have brought two charges in its mandate: betrayal and persecution. If, therefore, they wish us to conduct the Church's case, let it at last be concluded under your judgment. For it is wrong that the people should be led into error by waiting. If the Church spread throughout the whole world, as promised, is to be recognized, let it be demonstrated by the testimonies of Scripture. But if the opposing party's intention, through its usual tricks and evasions, is to prevent us from reaching the truth, let that be stated so that everyone may know it*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**15.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*What is the answer to these statements?*

Bishop Emeritus said:

*I never remember patience being condemned in court. Rather, it is always praiseworthy for the tongue to preserve silence and then, if it can, answer the objections clearly. Therefore, since all proceedings must have beginnings, and all business customarily takes its start from itself, why do we entangle the proceedings of past days and reopen them as though they had not been completed, when the presentation of the present day ought to assume and establish its own party? Therefore, if their proposition and our reply are being submitted to judgment, the standing of the persons must first be examined: who brought the matter into court, who caused the summons, who approached the rulers of the age, who sent envoys, who petitioned, who obtained the law, and who requested a hearing, so that, informed of their claims, we may be able to answer*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked, reserving the right of appeal.*

**16.** Bishop Fortunatianus of the Catholic Church said:

*It shows lack of confidence in the case to wish to examine the persons whom he evidently confirmed in the earlier debate. Therefore let us conduct the case of the Church which, as promised, is spread throughout the whole world*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**17.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Your Holiness, I think, has not paid careful attention to the proposition. For it is clear that something else was asked.*

**18.** Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*Then, in answer to what they asked, order the mandates of both parties to be read, and they will be informed on the matter they requested*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**19.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*The question was through whom this conference was requested, or who summoned to court the bishops standing on the opposing side. Your Holiness must therefore answer these questions*.

**20.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*If you judge it relevant, it is for Your Excellency to consider. The wording of the imperial rescript shows that the holding of a conference was granted to the Catholic bishops or the envoys of the Catholic bishops. We have both come; we are here. God has commanded us to be debaters and participants in a conference rather than litigants, and the most merciful emperor, fearing and serving God, desired nothing else. On the first day of our debate Your Highness judged that duly qualified persons were present and the mandates confirmed. Let nothing unnecessary to the matter be introduced. It is the Church that we uphold by the testimonies of divine Scripture, known to all, established, as it is written, on the highest mountain, to which all nations come. If there is anything to be said against this Church, let it now be said, and let no delays be introduced. If there is nothing to say, let the man who cannot substantiate, display, and prove his accusation to others yield to the truth. How long is this great expectation of the people to remain suspended? All are thinking about their souls, and we introduce delaying objections so that the goal of knowing the truth may never be reached.* And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**21.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*So that it may be understood in what manner the hearing was commissioned, let the text of the imperial command be read again.*

**22.** Bishop Petilian said:

*It is recorded in these proceedings that they called themselves Catholics without prejudice to us. Reserve the dispute over this matter*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, reserving the right of appeal*.

Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*Let what you ordered be read*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

Bishop Petilian said:

*For the true Catholic Church is among us: the Church that suffers persecution, not the one that inflicts it*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, preserving the effect of the appeal.*

Bishop Possidius of the Catholic Church said:

*This must be proved, not boasted of*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**23.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*The debate between both parties will determine this*. And he added: *Meanwhile let the imperial decree be read again.*

**24.** Martialis, the recording clerk, read:

*The emperors Caesars Flavius Honorius and Flavius Theodosius, pious, fortunate, victorious, and triumphant, ever Augusti, greet their Flavius Marcellinus.*

And while he was reading.

**25.** Bishop Petilian said:

*The emperor wished me to be frightened, not killed, persecuted, dragged away, or despoiled*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, reserving our right of appeal*.

**26.** Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*What is more, you were not even frightened*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**27.** Bishop Petilian said:

*You have praised the steadfastness of good faith and of true Catholic discipline*. And in another hand: (*I, Bishop Petilian,*) *have checked, reserving the right of appeal*.

**28.** Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*You were not frightened, as was said, nor were you afraid*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**29.** Martialis, the recording clerk, said:

*Among the greatest cares of our empire, reverence for the Catholic law is always either first or alone. For in the labors of war we pursue, and in the counsels of peace we arrange, nothing other than that the devoted people of our world preserve the true worship of God.*

*The Donatists ought long ago to have fulfilled this too, whether through fear or admonition; they disfigure Africa, that is, a very great part of our realm, which faithfully serves in secular duties, with empty error and needless dissension. Nevertheless, we do not tire of explaining repeatedly what the authority of all former emperors, devoted to God, determined, lest posterity justly blame us if anything injurious to the Catholic law should arise in our times. Nor are we unaware of the wording of the heavenly decree which the Donatists' perverse interpretation claims can advance their error. Although it invited corrupt minds more gently to correction, we nevertheless previously ordered it annulled, lest any opportunity be offered to superstitions. Now too, by like authority, we decree that fraud be excluded, and rightly declare that we willingly remove what had been enacted, lest anyone suppose that with our authorization he may sin against divine worship. And although the judgment of all is one and manifest—that the full truth of the Catholic law is approved by the right worship of human beings and by heavenly judgment—nevertheless, from a desire for peace and favor, we gladly admitted the embassy of the venerable bishops, which asks that the Donatist bishops be gathered in the assembly of the most celebrated city, so that, with bishops selected by each party, debates may be held and clear reasoning refute superstition.*

*We command that this matter be completed within four months, so that our Clemency too, as it desires, may sooner learn that the minds of the people have been converted. But if within the allotted time the Donatist bishops deliberately refuse to assemble, we wish the summons by three edicts to be observed, with periods of twenty days set for summoning the contumacious. Once these have elapsed and passed, if those summoned scorn to attend, let the people who have learned that their teachers have been defeated by silence yield together with their churches. Let them at last rejoice to have been conquered, and know that, if not our commands, at least the true commands of the Catholic law must be obeyed.*

*We wish you to preside as judge over this debate in the foremost place—you remember most fully whatever instructions you also received earlier—and we wish you to bear every responsibility both for assembling the bishops and for summoning them if they scorn to attend, so that with your proven diligence you may fulfill both the earlier instructions and the determinations now made known to you. Above all, you must observe this: setting aside the recent fraud, preserve intact and inviolate what antiquity long ago ordained concerning the Catholic law, what the religious authority of our ancestors established, and what our Serenity confirmed. So that appropriate assistance may not be lacking for your actions, our Serenity has admonished the eminent proconsul and vicar that, if they wish to retain their own dignities and their staff to avoid the ultimate penalties, they must ensure that abundant necessary personnel are assigned both from their own staffs and from those of all judges. It will then be your responsibility, if you learn that anything is being delayed by any artifice, to report it in dispatches, so that fitting correction may punish the negligent. You must report everything that a completed debate confirms when the bishops are assembled together, or that a sentence against the contumacious determines if they should withdraw, so that we may sooner know what our command has accomplished for strengthening the Catholic faith.*

And in the imperial hand: *Farewell, Marcellinus, most dear to us*. *Given at Ravenna on the day before the Ides of October*. [*In the consulship of the most beloved Varanes*.]

**30.** Bishop Petilian said:

*They think we should be called Donatists, although, if the question concerns the names of our fathers, I too can call them—indeed, I openly and plainly designate them—Mensurists and Caecilianists, those same men who are our betrayers and persecutors.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, without prejudice to our appeal*.

**31.** Bishop Vincent of the Catholic Church said:

*Do you now reject what you gladly embraced before the prefects' tribunal?* And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**32.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Even now I do not deny that Donatus, bishop of this city, of most blessed and holy memory, is and was my leader, and that his merits flourished so greatly that antiquity itself the glory of that time*. And while he was speaking, (and in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, preserving the effect of the appeal*.)

**33.** Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*Let them condemn the name of Donatus, and henceforth we will not call them Donatists*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**34.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Condemn the names of Mensurius and Caecilian, and you will not be called* (*a Mensurist or*) *a Caecilianist*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, without prejudice to our appeal*.

**35.** Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*Did I mention Mensurius?* And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**36.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*All these matters will remain reserved to both parties. And he added: It has clearly appeared that, at the bishops' request, the venerable emperor commissioned a debate under my adjudication, so that the cause of the original error might be examined. Let that now be brought forward*.

**37.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*The rescript has been read. Let the petitions also be read, so that the case may be heard*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked.*

**38.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*I think Your Holiness's expertise is not unaware that petitions are not customarily inserted in pragmatic rescripts. Indeed, it is not established that any were submitted by the bishops who requested the conference, as the text of the decree itself shows. Therefore let the business at last be presented*.

**39.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*If Your Excellency's ruling has established that petitions are not customarily inserted in a pragmatic rescript, let them show whether those whom they called envoys went by everyone's will and with a common mandate, so that, if their standing cannot rest on the petitions, it may at least rest on the envoys*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked, without prejudice to my appeal.*

**40.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*Let Your Nobility observe how much is being done so that nothing may be done*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

Bishop Montanus said: *The case must be conducted with proper procedure preserved*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Montanus, have checked, reserving the right of appeal.*

**41.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*They are present; both sides have assembled. This place is almost filled with bishops; the mandates have been read and confirmed. Our side does not deny requesting the conference so that in it they may finally either prove, or be shown unable to prove, the charges they habitually bring, not against us or each of us individually, but against the whole Christian world. That Christian world was promised and is being fulfilled by divine testimonies, not human opinion. What then? Are delaying devices, through I know not what evasiveness, still introduced by men who wish to be called bishops of Christ? The expectation not merely of this city, but of almost the whole human race, hangs in suspense; it longs to hear something about the Church, and we are still examining courtroom formulas and litigating most miserably. Let Your Nobility now observe what is happening. Deign at last, with God's help, to free us from this business by cutting away every little delay*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**42.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*I do not doubt that everyone's signatures, entered and arranged in the mandate at the previous hearing, can suffice for complete confirmation. Therefore, since everyone's consent has been expressed, let unnecessary matters be removed and the business at last presented*.

**43.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*Two things have been submitted to judgment, from which there can be no deviation: first, that they have shown in court that they act according to the imperial command; second, according to the wording of the mandate. If the two are connected, that must be demonstrated; but if they are separate and distinct, those who refuse to submit both to proof in court must abandon one. For the mandate must agree with the rescript, and the meaning of the instructions must be bound to the form of the imperial enactment. If Your Excellency understands neither that we wish to use delaying tactics nor that we hold back from pursuing the case, but that we wish first to establish the foundations and then settle the ecclesiastical business, why is delay raised as an objection against us? Why do they complain that we weave delays as though we were quibblers fleeing the hearing, when, if they would pay attention, we say that it is all the more incumbent and necessary for those presenting the case, with all delays cut away, to come to the documents and the heart of the business? Let it be shown on the opposing side who petitioned, how he petitioned, and what he petitioned for, so that, when I have grasped the agreement between mandate and rescript, both what is confirmed in the rescript and what was submitted in their petition may be established before the court*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked.*

**44.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*It is your part, noble sir. If he has answered what we said, we must reply. But if Your Excellency sees clearly that he has answered nothing of what we justly demand, but has added further needless delays to needless delays, judge as you see fit*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**45.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*It is clear that imperial authority has defined the precise scope of the hearing. For the wording of the decree that was read showed this: that, once the bishops have been summoned and representatives chosen, clear reasoning should, to use the law's own words, refute superstition. Therefore Your Holiness will kindly show more clearly on which side superstition lies*.

**46.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Your Highness observes their great lack of confidence and their great falsehood if they refuse to disclose what we request. For the man who fears to make his petition public has already judged his own conscience. Therefore we cause no delay; rather, delays are caused by them for Your Highness*. And while he was speaking, (and in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, preserving the merits of our appeal*.)

**47.** Bishop Vincent of the Catholic Church said:

*Is the judge's answer to you not sufficient?* And in another hand: *I have checked*.

Bishop Petilian said:

*Let them either read what we request or deny it*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, reserving our right of appeal.*

Bishop Fortunatianus of the Catholic Church said:

*How* *does what the opposing party has said concern the matter, except to cause delays?* And while he was speaking, (and in another hand: *I have checked*.)

Bishop Petilian of the party of Donatus [said:] *Judge*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, preserving the effect of our appeal.*

**48.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Your Holiness must indicate more clearly what you request.*

**49.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*At the previous hearing the opposing party produced a long roll purporting to contain the mandate. If their claim is supported by truth, they must publicly disclose in court that they follow only this, abandoning the imperial rescript for whose promulgation they have refused to show either the wording of the petitions or the envoys' mandate. Therefore, since our request in court is not irrelevant, we ask that they first either abandon the rescript or give up their mandate, so that we may come to the case*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked, without prejudice to the appeal* [*of ours*].

**50.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*Let our mandate be read, and they will understand how it contains everything*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

Bishop Petilian of the party of Donatus said:

*Be patient; grant the judge the patience he grants us*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, without prejudice to our appeal.*

**51.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*I have observed nothing principally established by the imperial decree except that the bishops should assemble and, through debate, discover the cause of the original error. He wished me to preside as judge over that debate. Your Holiness has heard the statement of the opposing party, in which it was clearly shown and promised that the Church spread throughout the world would be demonstrated by divine testimonies. You see that both the wording of the mandate and the present declaration have stated that everything would be established by examples and evidence from the law. For the most merciful emperor has not been shown to have prescribed the form of the conference; he merely determined that a conference should take place. Therefore, since their declaration has made clear that, in accordance with your desire, they will proceed by divine testimonies, I do not know what more you desire.*

**52.** Bishop Petilian said:

*It is most plainly understood that, through lack of confidence or belated shame, they are acting to avoid making their petition public to all, since it is established that they lied to the most merciful emperor*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, without prejudice to our appeal.*

**53.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*A conference was requested by our envoys. The law itself, addressed to Your Nobility, contains this*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**54.** Bishop Petilian said:

*We are not delaying action*. And in another hand: [*I, Bishop Petilian,*] *have checked, reserving our right of appeal*.

**55.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*It was granted. We all acknowledge that we requested a conference because you refused it, so that at least in this way we might finally come to this place and time for conference, debate, and demonstration of the truth. It has happened. We have come, and you wish our coming to achieve nothing. We acknowledge that we requested a conference. The emperor expressed nothing in his own words other than that we requested a conference. Let the conference itself now take place. Let us see what is said against the Church spread throughout the world, which we demonstrate by divine testimonies to have been promised and now displayed in such a way that it strikes the eyes of the blind and breaks into the ears of the deaf. Our brothers refuse to come to its demonstration, so that what cannot be hidden may be demonstrated. Let them state the crimes. Let us see through what crimes the promise made to Abraham perished: In your offspring all nations will be blessed. Let us hear, learn, know, and follow what crimes could have erased this promise of God sworn by an oath. But if nothing can be said against it, either pronounce judgment or remove every delay, and let the business at last be concluded*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**56.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*The truth is concealed by a great contrivance: in response to our inquiry, a small point is brought forward by the opposing side and the rest is kept silent. For he said that his envoys went, yet he refuses to disclose to the court their names, their standing, or their mandate. Therefore let him either abandon this claim because he cannot establish it, or, if he can prove it according to law—who the envoys were, when they were sent, or what they carried—the envoys must bring the mandate to the court's knowledge. Your Excellency sees that we ask nothing irrelevant, since they themselves are the reason we do not reach the substance of the business. For they stated that under their mandate they ought to stand against us on I know not what allegations. We too can overcome those very allegations by lawful testimony. First, therefore, with the beginning of the action set out, he must establish before the court his standing, whether as recipient or giver of a mandate; only then should we descend to the merits of the business*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked, reserving the right of appeal.*

**57.** Bishop Vincent of the Catholic Church said:

*What else is abandoning the case and investigating persons but a wish to escape? Why did you come?* And in another hand: *I have checked*.

Bishop Petilian said:

*Every case begins with the persons*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked, preserving the effect of our appeal.*

Bishop Vincent of the Catholic Church said:

*Has your own forum not shown you that matters passed over cannot be taken up again?* And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**58.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*The text of the heavenly decree has declared that a conference was requested and granted. I have not yet clearly understood why the persons of the envoys are now being brought forward*.

**59.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*If we denied requesting the conference, we would be refuted by the emperor's testimony. We have the emperor as witness; we acknowledge that we requested it. We have come so that what we requested and he granted may take place, and still I know not what doubts or delays are introduced*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**60.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*There, you have now acknowledged your role. Say when you petitioned; say through whom you petitioned.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked, reserving our right of appeal.*

**61.** Bishop Fortunatianus of the Catholic Church said:

*An investigation of persons is unnecessary for demonstrating the truth*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**62.** Bishop Montanus said:

*Since the opposing party had recourse to the most merciful emperor, it ought to produce both the mandate and the embassy, so that, when we have been able to consider them, we may know what we can answer in Your Nobility's court*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Montanus, have checked, reserving the right of appeal*.

Bishop Vincent of the Catholic Church said:

*It is far removed from the case, and outside it, to seek to reopen matters that the scope of this judgment did not at all include within its inquiry*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

Bishop Montanus said:

*What I demand belongs to your action*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Montanus, have checked, preserving the effect of the appeal*.

Bishop Vincent of the Catholic Church said:

*The presiding judge's sentence is definitive as to the purpose for which he adjourned us to today. There ought to be sufficient shame if we pass beyond what we ourselves judge concerning the case*.

And while he was speaking, (and in another hand: *I have checked*.)

Bishop Montanus said:

*What I demand belongs to your action. You laid the foundation of the case; you had recourse to the emperor. Therefore I must request both the mandate and the embassy, and the words you used, so that, when we have examined all these matters in Your Nobility's court, I may at last answer what serves our interest*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Montanus, have checked, preserving the effect of the appeal*.

Bishop Vincent of the Catholic Church said:

*You cannot overcome your own declaration. Recognize what you abandoned. You cannot reopen what you passed over*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*Why do you demand to know when I petitioned, when you see when I came? Why ask whether I petitioned, when the entire Catholic council present in this city gave the mandate, signed it, and, while present at your insistence, acknowledged the signatures? Nothing is sought here but delay, because nothing can be said against the universal Church commended by God's testimonies*. And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**63.** Bishop Montanus said:

*Let it be recorded that the opposing party is weaving delays, since they do not produce what our side demands. Therefore, if they wish that delay removed, let them produce what we demand: the mandate and the embassy. Then we will come to the merits of the business*. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Montanus, have checked, preserving the effect of the appeal.*

**64.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Therefore, despising and scorning these many great testimonies concerning the purity of the Church promised through the Spirit, they inappropriately affirm through the comparison of the weeds that the wicked will remain in it among the good, although the Lord himself interpreted that comparison to his apostles, saying:* He who sows the good seed is the Son of man. For neither can what has been interpreted by the Lord—rather, corrupt it. [The supplied source is corrupt here.] For if the apostles, the Lord's own companions, had learned that weeds sprouting in the Church, that is, the devil's sons, should be left in communion with the saints, they would never have expelled Simon, Erastus, Philetus, Alexander, Demas, Hermogenes, and others like them from the Church's threshold. Indeed, if that were so, the entire proclamation of divine Scripture would be void, which commands the polluted to be separated from among the saints through the bishops' diligence, as the Lord says through Moses:* An eternal statute*, he says,* throughout your generations, to distinguish between holy and profane, between clean and unclean (Lev 10). *Elsewhere the Spirit rebukes the priests for neglecting this, saying:* Her priests rejected my law and profaned my holy things; they did not distinguish between clean and unclean or separate holy from polluted (Ezek 22). *To this parable our opponents also add that chaff and grain ought to remain together in the Church. Jeremiah rebuts this, saying: What has chaff to do with wheat? And the apostle Paul:* What share has a believer with an unbeliever, or what fellowship has light with darkness (2 Cor 6; source: 1 Cor 6)? *And Solomon:* If a wolf ever shares fellowship with a lamb, then a sinner will with the righteous (Sir 13). *They also affirm that good and bad fish are contained and drawn together in one net until the shore, that is, the righteous and unrighteous until the end of the age, failing to see that this was said of hidden offenders. For the fishermen, that is, the bishops, do not know what a net placed in the sea contains until, drawn to shore for sorting, the good and bad are revealed. Thus those hidden within the Church and unknown to the bishops, when exposed at divine judgment, are separated from the fellowship of the saints like bad fish. So the Lord speaks figuratively in the Gospel about a hidden offender who had deceived the bishops by stealth, saying: The king, he says, entered to see the guests and saw there a man without a wedding garment, and said to him:* Friend, how did you come here? But he was speechless, and the king said to his servants: Take him away by the feet and hands and cast him into the outer darkness; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth (Matt 22; source: Matt 12). *According to this reasoning, therefore, they said in vain that the good are not abandoned because of the wicked through sacrilegious separation, but the wicked are tolerated because of the good in holy unity, since elsewhere the Lord indignantly shows that he is moved by this profane mixture and brings about a very great separation.* Because*, he says,* the whole house of Israel has become a mixture to me, all of bronze, silver, iron, tin, and lead mingled in a burning furnace, therefore he says: Thus says the Lord: Because you have all become one mixture, I therefore gather you into the midst of Jerusalem. As bronze, silver, iron, tin, and lead are gathered into a furnace to blow fire upon them and melt them, so I will gather you in my anger and burn you, and I will blow upon you the fiery blast of my anger. You will be melted in its midst, and you will know that I am the Lord who poured out my anger upon you (Ezek 22).

*Against these and similar passages, by which God's Church is defended from contamination by unclean persons mingled with it, it is clear with what conscience our opponents champion errors and refuse to distinguish the wicked from communion with the good. For they continue in most open blasphemy, saying that the Church cannot be stained by the offenses of the wicked, even manifest offenders. They adduce examples of prophets who, although they rebuked them for their offenses, nevertheless did not separate themselves bodily from them, whereas we show that the time was very different then, when they were permitted to repeat baptism as often as they sinned. Yet even so, these same prophets are found to have abstained from communion with the wicked. The prophet Zephaniah, sent to Jeroboam while he was in schism, was commanded to accept neither bread nor water; deceived into violating the command, he was destroyed by a lion. Elijah and Elisha too never shared communion with or consented to the schismatic altars of Samaria, but rather dwelt in solitude. The prophet Hosea is likewise shown not to have participated in the rites of those whose sacrifices he detests and condemns, saying:* Their sacrifices are like bread of mourning; whoever touches any of them will be defiled (Hos 9). *Finally, when could Amos have mingled in Samaria's rites, when the false prophet stirred up hostility against him before the king in these words:* Amos forms conspiracies against you in the midst of the house of Israel. The land cannot bear his words, and for this he is also expelled (Amos 7). *And in Jerusalem itself, how could the prophets share in the sacrifices of sinful men which they themselves, filled with the divine Spirit, condemned? Isaiah says:* What is the multitude of your sacrifices to me? says the Lord. I am full. I do not desire burnt offerings of rams, the fat of lambs, or the blood of bulls and goats; do not come thus into my presence. For who required these things from your hands? And you place polluted bread on my altar and said: How have we polluted it? By saying: The Lord's table is blessed, while you treated what was placed upon it as nothing (Mal 1). *You will find the same in all the prophets, for if they had shared communion with those they condemned so vehemently, they would have incurred the crime of transgression. Their presence among them, therefore, was a matter of dwelling together, not communion.*

*Then, in defense of their crime, they claim authority from the example of Judas the betrayer, saying that the Lord took him in despite foreknowing him and tolerated him despite knowing him. Let the enemies of the Lord's truth therefore go with their patron Judas, since in their usual manner they labor to defend the most manifest offenders.*

*Likewise, in defense of their polluted mixture, they bring forward the Apostle's words, in which he says:* Whether in pretense or in truth, I will have mercy, because they are children of fornication, for their mother committed fornication; she who bore them disgraced them (Phil 1; the source here combines wording from Philippians 1 and Hosea 2). *And in Isaiah:* As a garment spattered with blood will not be clean, so neither will you be clean, because you destroyed my land and killed my people. *But their assertion that Christ's baptism is defended just as the Catholic Church itself is defended as existing everywhere and among all people is disposed of, in a brief summary, by the decrees of our fathers, the most blessed martyrs. How rashly, forgetting themselves, the enemies of truth press their claim that baptism given among thieves, robbers, or any outsiders must be accepted, adducing for this an inappropriate testimony of the apostle Paul, where it is written:* Those who hold God's truth in unrighteousness (Rom 1). *Here they wished to defend theft with a stolen testimony. For the Apostle, speaking of the gentiles and rebuking unbelievers, spoke as follows:* For God's wrath will be revealed from heaven against all impiety and unrighteousness of those who hold God's truth in unrighteousness, because what can be known of God is manifest to them; God made it manifest to them. Therefore God gave them over, in the desire of their hearts, to uncleanness (Rom 1). *Thus, according to this understanding, they profess to accept even the defiled rites of the gentiles. They went on to heap the most evident blasphemies upon their error, saying that those who reject heretical baptism might reject Christ because demons confessed him. Here they not only insult the decrees of the holy martyrs, but are rather prepared to share communion with demons because they confessed Christ. As for their subtly concise statement that one baptized outside must be received, not so that what is lacking may be supplied, but so that what is present may benefit him, they are shown to have trapped themselves on both counts. **272.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*That is why those words were written down and these are being recorded. If anything escapes memory, I too can ask that it be read to me from the letter, and they can grant silence so that it may be read to them from the notebooks. Let us accomplish something. As I began to say, prompted by them, we find in the Scriptures the name world used in a bad sense and in a good sense. In a bad sense, as they themselves said:* And the world did not know him (John 1). *And:* If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him (1 John 2), *and* *other such passages. But the name world is used in a good sense:* That the world may believe that you sent me (John 17). *For believing does not belong to the wicked. The name world is used in a good sense:* He did not come to judge the world, but that the world might be saved through him (John **3**). *Only the Church in the world will be saved. Since world is therefore used both in a good sense and in a bad sense, where does this quarrel between the Scriptures come from? If an interpreter is lacking, bring the light of understanding. See the world in a bad sense as all lovers of temporal things throughout all nations; see the world in a good sense as all believers who bear the hope of eternal life throughout all nations.* God was in Christ*, he says,* reconciling the world to himself (2 Cor 5). *Let those speaking judge whether that detested world of which it was said that Christ's love is not in one who loves the world can be reconciled to God. Let Your Nobility therefore listen patiently. I see that holy Scripture foreshadowed the Church [through] good and bad mingled together, as they themselves have already admitted concerning the nets.*

And while he was speaking and a disturbance arose, Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*Let it be recorded that they are making a disturbance.*

**273.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Let the records contain that they themselves are making a disturbance.*

Bishop Alypius of the Catholic Church said:

*Where is the promised patience, and what courtesy is returned? Do they not indicate the manner of their dissension?* And in another hand: *I have checked*.

**274.** Bishop Petilian said:

*What the world is and what the Church is have been most plainly defined by the very author and maker of the world, through whom all things were made and without whom nothing was made. For the Lord himself said:* This world is the field (Matt 13). *He could have said: The world is the Church. What human being, then, dares to define what the world is, when the Lord himself, its maker and craftsman, has already deigned to define it?* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked.*

**275.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Show clearly what the world is which is said to be saved.*

**276.** Bishop Petilian said:

*Humanity was called the world where he said, reconciling the world to himself; for God wished to reconcile to himself not beasts, but human beings.* And in another hand: *I, Bishop Petilian, have checked.*

**277.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Do you say that the Church, afterward disclosed, can consist of all those designated by the name world, or do you deny it?*

**278.** Bishop Emeritus said:

*You draw us away from a clear preliminary objection. The Lord Christ says in the Gospel:* The field is the world. And while he was speaking. And in another hand: *I, Bishop Emeritus, have checked.*

Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*Then allow them to answer everything contained in the letter's text, and afterward I will judge everything most fully*.

**279.** Hilarus, the recording clerk, said:

*Since the parties have been conducting the case since daybreak and we have each filled two notebooks, if Your Excellency commands it, let other clerks replace us, so that we may go out to collate the records with custodians assigned to us.*

Vitalis, notary of the Catholic Church, said:

*I too submit what the clerk standing here submitted to Your Nobility*.

**280.** Marcellinus, the most distinguished tribune and notary, said:

*As was also done at the previous hearing, let those who report that they have filled their tablets depart with the custodians, with others appointed in their place.*

And while this was being done.

**281.** Bishop Augustine of the Catholic Church said:

*The world, then—for we too could understand it in no other way—is understood of human beings. Surely we are not so foolish as to say that beasts and all irrational animals belong to the salvation Christ promised. We say precisely this:* The world lies in the evil one*: these are human beings.* That the world may believe that you sent me*: these are human beings.* Reconciling the world to himself*: these are human beings; and the other things said in a bad sense mean those human beings, those human beings. For throughout the whole world there are both kinds, the bad mingled with the good. So too the Church, which they admitted was designated by the nets and contains the wicked, although they said the sinners are hidden, is now established as containing good and bad. The question concerns their being hidden or not hidden: whether it belonged to the piety of the good to tolerate even known wicked persons so as not to abandon the good, or whether those to whom anything evil appeared in the Church wished to abandon the whole because of the wicked. That is the question. This is what we prove. For we say that ecclesiastical discipline must certainly not be neglected, and wherever the wicked are exposed they must be restrained so that they may be corrected, not only by words of correction but also by excommunications and demotions, so that they may seek a humble place of salvation within the Church. This is done for their healing, not from hatred but from concern for a brother's salvation, as the Apostle himself also declares most clearly in one passage, saying:* If anyone does not obey our word through this letter, take note of that person and do not associate with him [so that they may be ashamed; and do not regard him as an enemy, but admonish him as a brother] . . .

N.B.
