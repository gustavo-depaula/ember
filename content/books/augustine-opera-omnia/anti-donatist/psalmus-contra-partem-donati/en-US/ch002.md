# Psalm

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

> It is a shameful thing to hear a case  
> and show favor to persons.

> No unjust man can  
> possess the kingdom of God.

> No one can bear it  
> that you should tear up his neighbor's garment:

> how much more does the man deserve death  
> who tears apart the peace of Christ.

> And who it is that has done this,  
> let us inquire without error.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## A

> An abundance of sins  
> tends to throw the brethren into confusion.

> For this reason our Lord  
> wished to forewarn us,

> comparing the kingdom of heaven  
> to a net let down into the sea[^1].

> It gathered many fish,  
> every kind from here and there,

> and when they had drawn them to the shore,  
> then they began to separate them:

> the good they put into vessels,  
> the rest, the bad, back into the sea.

> Whoever knows the Gospel,  
> let him recognize this with fear.

> He sees the net as the Church,  
> he sees this world as the sea[^2];

> and the mixed kinds of fish  
> are the just man together with the sinner;

> the shore is the end of the world:  
> then is the time to separate;

> those who have just now broken the nets  
> have loved the sea too much;

> the vessels are the dwelling places of the saints,  
> where these men cannot come.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## B

> A good listener perhaps  
> asks who it was that broke the net.

> Men full of pride,  
> who say that they are just.

> So they made the tear  
> and set up altar against altar.

> They handed themselves over to the devil,  
> when they wrangle about the handing over,

> and the crime they themselves committed  
> they want to shift onto others.

> They themselves handed over the books  
> and yet dare to accuse us,

> so that they commit a worse crime  
> than the one they committed before,

> since they could have excused the matter of the books  
> by their fear,

> the fear with which Peter denied Christ,  
> while he was terrified of death[^3].

> Now how will they excuse  
> the setting up of altar against altar?

> And having torn apart the peace of Christ,  
> so that they place their hope in man[^4],

> what persecution did not do,  
> they themselves did in peacetime.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## C

> Our keeper, great God,  
> you have power to set us free

> from these false prophets,  
> who seek to devour us.

> Their accursed wolfish heart  
> they cover with a sheep's skin[^5].

> The name of just man is the sheep's skin,  
> the schism is in the wolfish heart.

> Those who do not know the Scriptures  
> they tend to take in;

> for they hear "the traitors"  
> and do not know what was done before.

> And if I say to them, "Prove it,"  
> they have nothing to answer.

> They say they believed their own people:  
> I say that those people lied;

> for we too believed our own people,  
> who say that you handed the books over.

> Do you want to know who speak the truth?  
> Those who have remained in the root.

> Do you want to know who speak falsely?  
> Those who are not in unity.

> Long ago the case was already settled.  
> Why do you not stand in peace?

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## D

> Our elders spoke of this  
> and made books about it,

> the men who came to know the case then,  
> which they could prove while it was recent.

> There were certain men who handed over  
> the books of the holy law,

> bishops from Numidia,  
> and not just anyone from the common folk.

> When they had come to Carthage  
> to ordain a bishop,

> they found Caecilian already ordained  
> in his own see.

> They were angry because they themselves  
> had not been able to do the ordaining.

> There were other enemies too,  
> thoroughly unjust toward Caecilian,

> ungodly men, thieves, proud men,  
> of whom it would take long to tell.

> They all joined together  
> to trump up a charge against him:

> they say the man who ordained him  
> had handed over the holy books.

> Thus they broke the nets of peace,  
> and now they wander over the sea.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## E

> *Behold, how good and pleasant it is for brethren to dwell in unity*[^6].

> Hear the voice of the prophet,  
> that you may be in unity.

> Who has proved against us  
> the old charge about the handing over?

> Who brought it forward at a trial?  
> Who sat to judge it?

> By what witnesses did he convict?  
> Who dared to confirm this?

> But they gladly invented this,  
> the very thing they knew they had done themselves,

> because rumor was already speaking  
> of the handing over of the books.

> But those who had done it were lying hidden  
> in that turmoil.

> So they slandered others,  
> that they might be able to hide themselves.

> Through them the rest went astray,  
> the leaders of that party,

> because they thought it shameful  
> not to believe their colleagues.

> Now, brethren, let the error be ended  
> and let us be in unity.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## F

> They did what they wished  
> then, in that blindness.

> No judges took their seats,  
> so many priests, after the custom

> by which in great cases they are wont,  
> gathered together, to judge;

> no accuser and defendant  
> stood at the inquiry,

> no witness, no document  
> by which they could prove the charge,

> but only fury, deceit, and uproar,  
> which reign in falsehood.

> Either produce for us the records,  
> such as are usually kept in a council.

> Let us see what cause compelled  
> altar to be set up against altar.

> If the priest was bad,  
> he should have been deposed first;

> if he could not be deposed,  
> he should have been tolerated within the net,

> just as you now openly tolerate  
> so many bad men;

> you who put up with so many out of fury,  
> could have put up with one for the sake of peace.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## G

> Great joy it would be to us  
> if even then you were unwilling to go astray;

> but if then the truth was not seen,  
> at least now, taught by experience, look and see.

> For you now have many bad men

> and yet you do not separate them  
> from your communion.

> I am not speaking of those sins  
> which you can even deny:

> I mean cudgels, fires, killings,  
> which your people commit in broad daylight;

> and yet you put up with them,  
> whether out of error or out of fear.

> How small a thing it would have been for your fathers  
> to put up with one man for the sake of unity,

> if there was such an uproar  
> that they could not depose him.

> Add to this that he was innocent  
> and they could prove nothing,

> but, lest the charge be looked into  
> where they saw they themselves were caught,

> they pretended to be exceedingly just,  
> while they wished to throw everything into confusion.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## H

> Whoever seeks empty honors  
> does not wish to reign with Christ,

> like the prince of this evil,  
> after whose name they are called.

> For Donatus then wished  
> to take over all of Africa;

> then he asked the emperor  
> for judges from overseas.

> But this petition, however just,  
> did not spring from love.

> This the very truth proclaims,  
> which I now wish to relate.

> For the emperor consented,  
> and sent men to sit at Rome,

> priests who could then  
> hear Caecilian along with him.

> The case was argued, nothing was proved:  
> and he even dared to appeal

> and, after the tribunal of his colleagues,  
> to be heard by the emperor.

> From this his petition is shown  
> not to have sprung from love.

> Then, beaten everywhere, he began  
> to rebaptize Christians.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## I

> If you wish to follow justice,  
> consider the whole case.

> Why was what Donatus did afterward  
> not done before?

> The priests were at odds with one another  
> throughout the whole African region:

> priests from overseas  
> could thereupon pass judgment.

> Why did you rush off to schism  
> and altar against altar,

> so that what was judged afterward  
> you would no longer be willing to hear,

> and you would be forced to appeal  
> away from your own judges,

> while you wish to confirm  
> the reign of error by any means at all?

> And now you yourselves know it all,  
> but you pretend not to know,

> and when the truth presses you hard,  
> you say your fathers went astray,

> as though anyone were now forbidding you  
> to draw back from error.

> But pride has bound you  
> in the seat of pestilence[^7].

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## K

> Whoever has the love of Christ  
> cannot hate peace.

> Or now at least hear, you peoples,  
> and be reconciled with us,

> you who hold no episcopal seat  
> for which you might fight unjustly.

> If now your own bishops,  
> from some one region,

> had a dispute among themselves,  
> whom would you want to judge it

> but men of other regions  
> who had no part in that dispute?

> But when they had examined the case  
> and pronounced for one side,

> you would never hold communion  
> with those unwilling to agree with them.

> Why then did you hold communion  
> with these men who did this before?

> For they themselves did not agree  
> with the verdict of the overseas bishops,

> who judged in our favor;  
> for they are joined to us today.

> If Christ as judge says this,  
> what have you to answer?

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## L

> If the light of the heart is in you,  
> you can see the truth.

> There are the Petition of Donatus and the Acts  
> by which you may prove what has been said.

> And if you are unwilling to believe these,  
> then bring forward something of your own here.

> And if we too do not believe it,  
> there will be a quarrel without end.

> Let us embrace peace, then.  
> What is it to us, what was done before?

> You charge us with the handing over:  
> we answer that you did it.

> You cry out about Macarius  
> and we about the Circumcellions.

> That deed of ours is over and done;  
> your people do not cease even to this day.

> Our threshing floor has its chaff:  
> you want to be this chaff alone.

> For you do not want peace.  
> Those men threaten with the cudgel,

> and would that they only threatened  
> and did not strike day after day.

> If these men of yours drove them out,  
> they would have no one through whom to reign.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## M

> If Macarius exceeded the measure  
> laid down in the Christian law,

> he was at least carrying out the law of the king  
> when he fought for unity.

> I do not say this man committed no sin,  
> but that your people are worse.

> For who commanded them  
> to rage so through Africa?

> Neither Christ nor the emperor  
> is shown to have allowed this:

> cudgels and fires of their own  
> and madness without law.

> Although it is written, Put away the sword,  
> they think it no crime to use the cudgel,

> not so that a man should not die,  
> but so that he should be battered hard

> and die from it afterward,  
> already tormented in his weakness.

> And yet, if they show mercy,  
> they kill with a single blow of the cudgel.

> They call their cudgels "Israels,"  
> a name God uttered with honor[^8],

> so that they outrage that very name  
> more than the body they strike with it.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## N

> Do not now, brethren,  
> charge the time of Macarius against us.

> If those men were cruel,  
> they too greatly displease us;

> but if they say false things about them,  
> God can be the judge.

> Let us love the peace of Christ,  
> let us rejoice in unity.

> If there are bad men in the Church,  
> they cannot harm us.

> If they cannot remain among us,  
> let them be shut out, with peace preserved.

> If they cannot be shut out,  
> let them be shut out at least from the heart.

> Holy Ezekiel said  
> that certain men were marked,

> who groan over the sins of the brethren  
> and yet are not separated from them[^9].

> So let us, on account of bad brethren,  
> not separate ourselves from our mother,

> which is what the ungodly did then,  
> raising up an altar outside,

> so that they now have worse men  
> than those they pretend to have fled.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## O

> Everyone who knows the Scriptures  
> knows what I want to make plain.

> John the Baptist said  
> then to the Jews openly

> that Christ would winnow these  
> as his own threshing floor[^10].

> He sent laborers into the harvest,  
> the disciples, to preach[^11],

> through whom the threshing floor was gathered  
> and winnowed from the cross.

> Then the just, like grain,  
> filled the Church in purity,

> selling what they possessed  
> and bidding the world farewell.

> They were like the seed  
> that was scattered over the whole world[^12]

> so that another harvest might spring up,  
> which is to be winnowed at the end,

> and it grows among the weeds,  
> since there are heresies everywhere;

> the chaff of this harvest are the unjust,  
> who are not in unity,

> and if Macarius was one of these,  
> why do you want to rebaptize us?

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## P

> Set two threshing floors in your heart,  
> that you may see what I say.

> Surely the earlier one also had its saints,  
> as the Scriptures show.

> For God said that he had kept  
> for himself seven thousand men[^13],

> and priests and kings,  
> many just men, are in the law.

> There you have so many prophets,  
> you have many also among the people.

> Tell me: which of the just men then  
> set up an altar for himself apart?

> That wicked people committed  
> many crimes,

> sacrifice was offered to idols,  
> so many prophets were killed[^14],

> and yet none of the just men  
> withdrew from unity.

> The just put up with the unjust,  
> awaiting the winnower to come,

> they were mingled under one temple,  
> but, though mingled, were not so in heart,

> they said such things against them  
> and yet had one altar.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## Q

> What do you think of this?  
> The second harvest of the Church,

> which grows throughout the whole world,  
> must endure still more.

> It has the Lord's own example  
> in Judas the traitor too[^15].

> Him he bore among the good,  
> him he even sent out to preach.

> A bad servant was preaching,  
> but Christ was the object of faith,

> for those who believed the judge  
> did not concern themselves with the herald.

> When he gave the holy supper,  
> not even then did he shut him out[^16],

> and he could be betrayed by him  
> even if he had gone out beforehand.

> But an example was given us  
> to tolerate bad brethren,

> so that, when they cannot be shut out,  
> we should be separated from them in heart alone.

> But like chaff sticking to the ears of grain,  
> certain men are exceedingly proud,

> whom, before they are winnowed,  
> the storm snatches from the harvest.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## R

> I ask, answer us:  
> what do you wish to rebaptize?

> Your lapsed priests  
> you drive from communion,

> and yet no one, after them,  
> has dared to rebaptize,

> and all whom they baptized  
> are in communion with you today.

> What did they receive from them,  
> if they had nothing to give?

> Read how the adulterer  
> is punished in the holy law[^17];

> for he cannot say  
> that he sinned out of fear.

> If only the holy baptize,  
> then rebaptize after these men.

> Why do you bring false charges against us  
> who are in unity,

> who were not yet even born  
> at the time of that persecution?

> And it is written that the sins of the fathers  
> do not belong to the just[^18];

> but no one bears good fruit  
> if he has been cut off from the vine.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## S

> You know what the Catholic Church is  
> and what has been cut off from the vine.

> If any among you are wary,  
> let them come, let them live in the root;

> before they dry up too far,  
> let them now be freed from the fire.

> For this reason we do not rebaptize,  
> because there is one sign in the faith,

> not because we see you as holy,  
> but as holding only the outward form,

> for the branch that has been cut off from the vine  
> has that same form.

> But what good is the form to them,  
> if they do not live from the root?

> Come, brethren, if you wish  
> to be grafted into the vine.

> It is a grief to us when we see you  
> lying there cut off like this.

> Count the priests  
> down from the very seat of Peter,

> and in that line of fathers  
> see who succeeded whom:

> that is the rock which the proud gates of hell  
> do not overcome[^19].

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## T

> If such a man should come to you  
> full of catholic faith,

> such as those holy men  
> we are all accustomed to hear about,

> and should say to you, "O brother,  
> why do you want to rebaptize me?

> What was done before I do not know,  
> but now I am in the faith of Christ.

> If what I do not know defiles me,  
> show now what sort of man you are:

> see, I look upon your face,  
> but I do not know what is in your heart.

> If what I do not know defiles me,  
> perhaps you defile me,

> and if I believe you to be holy,  
> look at those with whom you are in communion.

> If what we do not know defiles,  
> then you can no longer be holy,

> you whom so many sins defile  
> that your people commit in secret.

> But if you do not trouble over what you do not know,  
> neither do I over what was done before."

> And yet such a Christian as this  
> you dare to rebaptize?

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

## V

> Woe to you who for the sake of your episcopal seats  
> contend thus unjustly.

> You cry out that you alone are holy,  
> but you say otherwise in your heart,

> because you too see that many  
> bad men abound everywhere.

> Surely you cannot say,  
> "We are mingled within the net"?

> For the answer is given you  
> that you have already broken that net.

> Nor can you say  
> that you are putting up with the chaff;

> for again we answer:  
> you would have done this before as well.

> For they were no worse  
> than that Judas the traitor[^20],

> with whom the apostles received  
> the first sacrament of the supper[^21],

> though they already knew him among them  
> to be guilty of so great a crime;

> and yet the filth in another's heart  
> did not defile them.

> And yet Christian brethren  
> you dare to rebaptize.

> You who rejoice in peace,  
> now judge what is true.

> Hear, brethren, what I say,  
> and do not be angry with me,

> for what you hear is not false;  
> you can consider it for yourselves.

> What if mother Church herself  
> were to address you in peace

> and say, "O my children,  
> why do you complain of your mother?

> Why have you deserted me?  
> I want now to hear it from you.

> You accuse your brethren  
> and I am torn cruelly.

> When the nations were pressing me hard,  
> I bore much with grief.

> Many deserted me,  
> but they did it out of fear;

> but you, no one compelled  
> to rebel against me like this.

> You say that you are with me,  
> but you see that it is false.

> I am called Catholic  
> and you are of the party of Donatus.

> The apostle Paul commanded me  
> to pray for the kings of the world[^22];

> you begrudge it that kings  
> are now in the Christian faith.

> If you are my children, why do you grieve  
> that my prayers have been heard?

> For when they sent gifts,  
> you were unwilling to accept them

> and you forgot the prophets  
> who foretold this beforehand,

> that the great kings of the nations  
> would send gifts to the Church[^23].

> And when you spurned these gifts,  
> you showed that you were not mine

> and you forced Macarius  
> to avenge his own grief.

> But what have I done to you,  
> I, your mother throughout the whole world?

> I drive out the bad whom I can;  
> those I cannot, I am forced to bear.

> I bear them until they are healed,  
> or are separated at the end.

> Why have you abandoned me  
> and I am tormented over your death?

> If you hate bad men so much,  
> look at the sort you have among you.

> If you too tolerate bad men,  
> why not within unity,

> where no one rebaptizes  
> and altar is not against altar?

> You tolerate so many bad men,  
> but for no good reward,

> because what you owe for Christ's sake  
> you choose to bear for Donatus's sake."

> We have sung to you, brethren,  
> if you are willing to hear of peace.

> Our judge is coming:  
> we give, and he requires.
