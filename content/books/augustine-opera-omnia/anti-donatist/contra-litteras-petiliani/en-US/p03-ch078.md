# The sacrament of God is always holy, even in evil men.

**48.** 112. Augustine replied: Show us the tribunal where you sat, that the whole world might stand before you; and with what eyes you inspected and examined—I do not say the consciences of all—but even the acts of all, so as to judge that it had lost innocence. He who was caught up to the third heaven[^1] says: *But neither do I judge myself*[^2]; and you dare to pass sentence about the whole earth, throughout which the inheritance of Christ is spread!

Next, if what you said seems to you sufficiently settled: *He who has not been innocent does not have holiness*; I ask, if Saul did not have the holiness of the sacrament, what did David venerate in him? But if he had innocence, why did he persecute the innocent? For on account of the sacrosanct anointing he both honored him living and avenged him slain: and because he cut off even a little piece from his garment, his heart, struck, trembled. Behold, Saul did not have innocence, and yet he had holiness—not of his own life (for no one can have this without innocence), but of the sacrament of God, which is holy even in evil men.

[^1]: 2 Cor 12:2.
[^2]: 1 Cor 4:3.
