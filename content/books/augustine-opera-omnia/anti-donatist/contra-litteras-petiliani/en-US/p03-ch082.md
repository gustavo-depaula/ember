# Petilian brings no proofs, but empty insults.

**51.** 118. Augustine replied: And do you not see that these are not any proofs, but empty insults? For this is what I said a little before: You speak the words of the Law, but you do not attend to whom you speak them against; just as the devil spoke the words of the Law, but did not recognize to whom he spoke them. He wished to cast down headlong our head, which was to ascend on high; but you wish to reduce the body of that same head, which is spread throughout the whole earth, to a tiny part.

Certainly you yourself said a little before that *we know the Law and speak lawfully, but blush in our acts.* You say these things, proving nothing; but even if you proved them about some, you would not thereby prejudge about all the rest. Nevertheless, even if all throughout the whole world were such as you most emptily charge, what has the Chair of the Roman Church done to you, in which Peter sat, and in which today Anastasius sits, or the Chair of the Church of Jerusalem, in which James sat, and in which today John sits, to whom we are joined in Catholic unity, and from whom you have separated yourselves by a wicked fury?

Why do you call the apostolic Chair the *seat of pestilence*? If on account of the men whom you think speak the Law and do not do it, did the Lord Jesus Christ, on account of the Pharisees, of whom he says: *For they say and do not do,* do any injury to the chair in which they sat? Did he not commend that chair of Moses, and rebuke them while the honor of the chair was preserved? For he says: *They sit in the chair of Moses. What they say, do; but what they do, do not do: for they say, and do not do*[^1]. If you considered these things, you would not, on account of the men you defame, blaspheme the apostolic Chair, with which you are not in communion. But what is this else than not to know what to say, and yet to be able to do nothing but curse?

[^1]: Matt 23:3.
