# How this psalm is understood allegorically.

**104.** 239. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: You speak truly. For that priesthood, in figure of the body of Christ, had an anointing, which is made safe by the joining of unity. For Christ himself is named from the chrism, that is, from the anointing. Him the Hebrews call Messiah, which word agrees with the Punic tongue, as do very many other Hebrew words, and almost all.

What, then, in that priesthood is the head, what the beard, what the edge of the garment?

As far as the Lord grants me to understand, the head is the Savior himself of the body, of whom the Apostle says: *And he himself is the head of the body, the Church*[^1]. By the beard, not incongruously, strength is understood. Therefore upon those who are strong in his Church, and cling to his mouth, that they may preach the truth without fear, the holy ointment runs down from Christ himself as from the head, that is, spiritual sanctification. The edge of the garment is given to be understood as that which is at the head of the garment, by which the head of the one putting it on enters; by this are signified the perfect faithful in the Church.

For in the edge is perfection; and surely you remember that it was said to a certain rich man: *If you wish to be perfect, go, sell all that you possess, and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me*[^2]*.* He indeed departed sad, a neglecter of perfection, a chooser of falling away; but were there therefore lacking those in whom the ointment of unity, by such a forsaking of earthly things made perfect, would run down from the head as upon the edge of the garment?

For, apart from the apostles, and those who with them were set over others and were teachers, whom we understand as the more eminent and stronger in the beard, read in the Acts of the Apostles and see who *laid the prices of their things sold at the feet of the apostles, and no one said anything was his own, but they had all things in common, and it was distributed to each as each had need, and they had one soul and one heart in God*[^3]. Surely you recognize that it is written thus.

Recognize, then, the good and pleasant thing, brothers dwelling as one. Recognize the beard of Aaron; recognize the edge of the spiritual garment. Ask Scripture itself where these things began to be done: you will find, in Jerusalem. From this edge of the garment the whole unity is woven throughout all the nations. By this the head entered into the garment, so that Christ might be clothed with the variety of the whole earth; for in this edge of the garment appeared that very variety of tongues.

Why, then, do you resist the head itself, from which that ointment of unity runs down, that is, the spiritual fragrance of love; why, I say, do you resist the head itself, testifying and saying: *Repentance and forgiveness of sins will be preached in his name to all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^4]? And in this ointment you wish to interpret the sacrament of the chrism; which indeed, in the class of visible signs, is most sacred, like baptism itself; but it can be also in most evil men, consuming their life in the works of the flesh, and not about to possess the kingdom of heaven; and therefore belonging neither to the beard of Aaron, nor to the edge of his garment, nor to any weaving of the priestly garment.

For where will you place the things the Apostle enumerates as *the manifest works of the flesh, which are,* he says, *fornications, uncleannesses, debaucheries, the service of idols, sorceries, enmities, rivalries, jealousies, animosities, dissensions, heresies, envyings, drunkennesses, carousings, and the like; which I foretell to you, as I have foretold, that those who do such things will not possess the kingdom of God*[^5]? I set aside fornications, which are committed secretly; interpret uncleannesses as you will, I set those aside too; let us place there sorceries also, because no one is openly a maker and giver of poisons; I set aside heresies too, because you wish this; the service of idols I do not know whether I ought to set aside, since the Apostle there places avarice, which rages publicly[^6]. These, then, being set aside, are there none among you debauched, none avaricious, none stubbornly practicing enmities, none contentious, jealous, animose, devoted to dissensions, envious, drunken, given to carousings? Are none such among you anointed, and do they not die known and manifest in these vices? If you say none; since out of zeal for contention you openly lie, see lest you yourself be of such.

But if you are removed from such men, not by separation of body, but by unlikeness of life, and grieving you behold such crowds around your altars; what shall we say, since they have been anointed with the holy oil, and, as the Apostle confirms with clear truth, will not possess the kingdom of God?

Is a sacrilegious injury to be done to the beard of Aaron and the edge of his garment, so that we should judge that they are to be placed there? Far be it. Distinguish, then, the visible holy sacrament, which can be both in the good and in the wicked—to the former for a reward, to the latter for a judgment—from the invisible anointing of love, which is proper to the good.

Distinguish these things, distinguish: may God distinguish you from the party of Donatus, and recall you to the catholic communion, from which they bound you—a catechumen snatched away to them—with the bond of a death-bearing honor.

Now the dew of Hermon upon the mountains of Zion[^7], however it be taken by you, you are not on the mountains of Zion; for you are not in the city set upon a mountain, which has this sure sign, that it cannot be hidden[^8]. It is therefore known to all the nations; but the party of Donatus is unknown to most nations; it is therefore not the one.

**105.** 240. PETILIAN SAID: *Woe, then, to you, who, by violating what is holy, tear apart unity, since the prophet says: If the people sins, the priest will pray for it; but if the priest sins, who will pray for him?*[^9].
