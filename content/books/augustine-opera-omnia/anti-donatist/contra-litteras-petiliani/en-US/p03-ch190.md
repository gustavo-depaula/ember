# The Church is not in the party of Donatus, but in the whole world.

**108.** 247. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: Against this error indeed we have already said much, both in this work and elsewhere. But because you judge that so great a buttress of your vanity is in this sentence, that you thought to end your letter with it, so that it might remain, as it were more freshly, in the minds of readers, I answer briefly. We approve baptism in heretics—not of heretics, but of Christ—just as in fornicators, the unclean, the debauched, idolaters, sorcerers, retainers of enmities, the contentious, the jealous, the animose, the devotees of dissensions, the envious, the drunken, carousers, and the like, we approve baptism, not theirs, but Christ's. For all these, among whom the heretics too are placed, as the Apostle says, will not possess the kingdom of God[^1]; and therefore they will belong to the left-hand part with the devil.

Nor are they therefore to be thought to be in the body of Christ, which is the Church, because they bodily become partakers of his sacraments. For those sacraments are holy even in such men, and to them, handling and taking them unworthily, they will avail for a greater judgment. But they themselves are not in that frame of the Church, which in the members of Christ, through connection and contact, grows into the increase of God. For that Church is on the rock, as the Lord says: *Upon this rock I will build my Church*[^2]. But they build on the sand, as the same Lord says: *Whoever hears my words and does not do them, I will liken him to a foolish man, who builds his house upon the sand*[^3]. But lest you think that the Church which is on the rock is in one part of the earth, and is not spread to the ends of the earth: hear its voice from the Psalm, groaning amid the evils of its pilgrimage. For it says: *From the ends of the earth I have cried to you; when my heart was in anguish, you exalted me upon a rock; you have led me, for you have become my hope, a tower of strength before the face of the enemy*[^4]. See how it cries from the ends of the earth.

It is not, then, in Africa alone, or in Africans alone, you who send a bishop to Rome to a few Mountain-folk, and to Spain to the house of one woman from Africa. See how it is exalted upon a rock. Not all, then, are to be reckoned in it who build on the sand, that is, who hear the words of Christ and do not do them; who nevertheless, both among us and among you, have and hand on the sacrament of baptism. See how its hope is God, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, not Peter, not Paul; how much less Donatus, or Petilian? It is not, then, yours that we fear to destroy, but Christ's: which even in the sacrilegious is in itself holy.

For we cannot receive those who come from you, unless we destroy what is yours. For we destroy the faithlessness of the deserter, we do not destroy the seal of the emperor. Accordingly, consider yourself, and destroy what you said: *I,* you say, *baptize their infected ones; they receive mine, which God forbid, as already baptized.* For you do not baptize the infected, but, in order to infect them by the fraud of your error, you rebaptize.

But we do not receive your baptized, but we destroy your error, whence they are yours, and we accept the baptism of Christ, whence they are baptized.

And so, opportunely, in the middle you put: *Which God forbid.* For you said: *They receive mine, which God forbid, as baptized.* For while you wished it to be understood, *which God forbid, they receive,* fearing that we receive your people; I take you to have said, not knowing it: *Mine, which God forbid.*

For truly God forbid that they should be yours, who now pass over to the catholic communion: nor do they so pass over that, baptized, they may be ours, but that they may be our partners; and that, baptized, they may be Christ's along with us.

[^1]: See Gal 5:19-21.
[^2]: Matt 16:18.
[^3]: Matt 7:26.
[^4]: Ps 61:2-3 (60:3-4).
