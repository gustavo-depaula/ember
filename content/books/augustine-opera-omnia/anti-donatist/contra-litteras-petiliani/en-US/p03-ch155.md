# The matter concerns kings who persecute Christians.

**92.** 202. PETILIAN SAID: *But what have you to do with the kings of this world, whom Christianity has never found anything but hostile? To teach this briefly, a king persecuted the Maccabee brothers*[^1]. *A king too, being himself sacrilegious, consigned three boys, in his ignorance, to religious flames*[^2]. *A king sought the life of the boy Savior*[^3]. *A king cast most righteous Daniel, as he supposed, to the bites of beasts*[^4]. *And the most wicked judge of a king killed the Lord Christ himself*[^5]. *Hence it is that the Apostle cries out: We speak wisdom among the perfect, a wisdom not of this age, nor of the rulers of this world who are passing away; but we speak the wisdom of God in a mystery, which was hidden, which God established before the ages for our glory, which none of the rulers of this age knew. For if they had known it, they would not have crucified the Lord of majesty*[^6].

*But let this be said of the former pagan kings. But you, since the emperors of this age desire to be Christians, do not permit them to be Christians, when by the deceit and fog of your lying you lead those very men, who believe with a good mind, straight to your iniquity, so that with the arms prepared for them against the enemies of the state they may rush upon Christians, and think that by your persuasions they do God a service, if they kill us whom you hate, since the Lord Christ says: The time will come, he says, when whoever kills you will think he does God a service*[^7].

*It makes no difference, then, to you who teach evil, whether the kings of the earth desire to be pagans—which God forbid—or Christians; since you do not cease to arm those same men against the family of Christ. But you do not know, or rather you have not read, that greater than the crime of one who kills a man is the crime of the one who persuades it? Jezebel had roused her husband the king to the death of a just or poor man, yet with equal punishment both the man and his wife perished*[^8].

*For you do not drive kings otherwise than as a subtle womanly persuasion has often driven kings into guilt. For through her daughter the wife of Herod obtained and earned that the head of John be brought on a platter to the table*[^9]. *The Jews so compelled Pontius Pilate that he fastened the Lord Christ to the cross, whose avenging blood they willed to remain upon themselves and their own forever*[^10]. *So, then, you overwhelm with our blood those who sin. For even if the judge strikes, it is not so much that the judge struck us as that your slanders did.*

*For the prophet David says, in the person of the Lord Christ: Why have the nations raged, and the peoples meditated vain things? The kings of the earth stood up, and the princes came together as one, against the Lord, and against his Christ. Let us break their bonds, and cast off from us their yoke. He who dwells in the heavens will laugh at them, and the Lord will mock them. Then he will speak to them in his anger, and in his fury he will confound them. But I have been established as king by him over Zion his holy mountain, proclaiming his decree. The Lord said to me: You are my Son, today I have begotten you. Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and the ends of the earth as your possession. You shall rule them with a rod of iron, and shatter them like a potter's vessel.*

*And those same kings, lest as though ignorant or unaware they should desire to persecute Christians, he admonished by these precepts, that they might not perish: which precepts, since they do not know them, would that we might teach them; or certainly you would without doubt show them to them, if you wished them to live; or certainly—which is a third possibility—your malice would have permitted them to read them for themselves: the first Davidic psalm would altogether persuade them to live and to reign as Christians; whom indeed you have wickedly deceived, while they commit themselves to you.*

*For you invent for them the things that are evil, and conceal the things that are good. Let them at last read, even if too late, what they ought to have read long ago. For what does it say? And now, kings, understand; be instructed, you who judge the earth. Serve the Lord in fear, and exult in him with trembling; lay hold of discipline, lest at any time the Lord be angry, and you perish from the righteous way. When his anger blazes up against you, how quickly, blessed are all who trust in him*[^11].

*You press the emperors, I say, with persuasions, as the Jews, whom we mentioned, pressed Pilate, although he himself cried out, washing his hands publicly: I am free from the blood of this just man*[^12]; *yet how can he who did it be clean of the crime? But, to be silent about ancient things, observe in your own examples how very many emperors and judges of yours perished by making persecution against us.*

*To leave aside Nero, who first persecuted the Christians; Domitian likewise, for the most part a Nero, Trajan, Geta, Decius, Valerian, Diocletian; Maximian too perished: who, when they were proclaiming incense, with the Lord's books burned, first Marcellinus, who was bishop of the Romans, but also Mensurius of Carthage, and Caecilian, remained out of those very sacrilegious flames as embers or ashes.*

*For the guilt of the incense implicated all of you who consented to Mensurius. Macarius perished, Ursacius perished, and all your Counts likewise perished by the vengeance of God. For Ursacius, laid low in a barbarian battle, the birds with savage talons and the greedy teeth of dogs tore apart with their bites. Is he not, at your impulse, the slayer, who, like king Ahab, whom we mentioned as persuaded by a woman, killed the just poor man*[^13]?

*So you do not cease to cut the throats of us too, the just and the poor (I would say poor as regards worldly resources, for the grace of God in us is not poor). For (even if you do not do it with your hand) you do cut a man's throat with the executioner tongue. For it is written: Death and life are in the hands of the tongue*[^14]. *All, therefore, who have been killed, you who are the persuader have killed. For the hand of the executioner grows hot only by your tongue, and that hard heat of the breast is kindled against another's blood by your words—just blood, the avenger of those who shed it.*
