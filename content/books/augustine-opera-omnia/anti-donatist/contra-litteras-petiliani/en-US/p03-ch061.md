# The baptism of John prefigured the baptism of Christ.

**37.** 87. But why the baptism of John was necessary at that time, which is now no longer necessary, we have both said elsewhere, and it does not now pertain to the matter at issue between us; except only that it may be seen that the baptism of John was one thing, but that of Christ another; just as that baptism was another in which the Apostle says our fathers were baptized in the cloud and in the sea, when they crossed the Red Sea through Moses[^1].

For the Law and the Prophets, until John the Baptist, had sacraments foretelling the thing to come[^2]; but the sacraments of our time attest that what those foretold as to come has come. John, therefore, of all who preceded the nearest, was the announcer of Christ. And because all the just and prophets of former times longed to see fulfilled what, by the revealing Spirit, they discerned would come to be; whence the Lord himself also says: *For many just men and prophets longed to see what you see, and did not see it; and to hear what you hear, and did not hear it*[^3]; therefore John was called more than a prophet, and among those born of women there was no greater[^4]; because to the former just men it was granted only to foretell Christ, but to him both to foretell him absent and to see him present, so that to him is found to have lain open what they longed for.

And therefore the sacrament of his baptism still pertains to the foretelling of Christ, but plainly the last foretelling: since up to him there were foretellings of the first coming of the Lord, of which coming there are now announcements, no longer foretellings. But the Lord, teaching the way of humility, deigned to receive the sacraments of his own foretelling which he found here, not as a help to his own purification, but as an example of our piety: that is, that he might show us with how great devotion we ought to receive those things which attest that he has now come, since he himself did not disdain to receive the things which were figures of him as about to come.

And John, therefore, although the nearest, and within one year a contemporary, nevertheless, when he baptized, went before Christ as he came; on this account it was said about him: *Behold, I send my angel before your face, who will prepare your way*[^5]. And he himself preached, saying: *One stronger than I comes after me*[^6]. So, then, the circumcision of the eighth day which was given to the fathers foretold our justification, in the stripping off of carnal desires[^7] through the resurrection of the Lord, which was done after the seventh, that is, the Sabbath day, on the eighth day, that is, the Lord's day, which is the third day after his burial: yet the infant Christ received that same circumcision of the flesh which foretold it.

And just as the Pasch which was celebrated by the Jews in the slaying of a lamb foretold the passion of the Lord and his passage from this world to the Father, yet the same Lord celebrated with his disciples that very Pasch which was in that same foretelling, when they suggested to him, saying: *Where do you wish us to prepare the Pasch for you?*[^8] so too he himself received the baptism of John, which was in his nearest foretelling.

But just as the circumcision of the flesh of the Jews is one thing, and another that which we celebrate of those baptized on the eighth day; and the Pasch which they still celebrate from a sheep is one thing, and another that which we receive in the body and blood of the Lord; so the baptism of John was one thing, and the baptism of Christ another. For to them these things were foretold as to come, to us those things are proclaimed as fulfilled.

And although Christ received those things; nevertheless they are not necessary for us, because we have received the very one who was announced through them. But at the recent coming of the Lord, whoever had received those things, it was needful for him to be imbued with these as well; but whoever had been imbued with these, it was not needful that he be forced back into those.
