# The holiness of baptism does not depend on the holiness of the minister.

**26.** 61. Augustine replied: Are you then not ashamed to call the baptism of Christ a falsehood, even if it should be in a most deceitful man? Far be it indeed that the Lord's grain, which was commanded to grow among the weeds throughout the whole field, that is, this world, until the harvest, that is, until the end of the age[^1], should be believed to have perished by your curses. Nevertheless, even in the weeds themselves, which were commanded not to be gathered but borne until the end, and in the chaff itself, which will not be wholly separated except by the final winnowing fan[^2]; does anyone dare to say that the baptism is false which is given and received in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit?

Those whom, convicted by the testimony of pregnant women, you depose from honor, whether your colleagues or your presbyters (since such examples are nowhere lacking), I ask: before they were convicted, were they deceitful or truthful? You will certainly reply: Deceitful. Why, then, did they both have and give true baptism? Why did human deceit not corrupt the divine truth in them? Is it not most truly written: *For the Holy Spirit of discipline will flee what is feigned*[^3]? Since, then, the Holy Spirit fled these feigners, why was the truth of baptism among them, unless because the Holy Spirit was fleeing the man's deceit, not the truth of the sacrament?

Moreover, if even deceitful men have true baptism, he who has this—although received from a most deceitful baptizer—has the same thing that the truthful have. Whence you ought to notice that your discourse is rather painted with childish pigments: and therefore one who, neglecting the living word, delights in such dyes, himself loves a painting in place of the truth.

[^1]: See Matt 13:24-30, 36-43
[^2]: See Matt 3:12
[^3]: Wis 1:5
