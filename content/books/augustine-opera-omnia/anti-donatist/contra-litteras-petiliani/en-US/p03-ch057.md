# The purity of baptism is distinct from the purity of receiver and giver.

**35.** 82. Augustine replied: In these few words of yours there are two errors. And the one indeed does not greatly pertain to the question being discussed between us, but convicts you of inexperience. For upon the hundred and twenty men the Holy Spirit came without anyone's laying on of hands, but upon Cornelius the centurion and those who were with him he came even before they were baptized[^1]. But the other error in these words of yours utterly destroys your whole case. For you say that the begetting water of a pure conscience must go before, that the Holy Spirit may follow. Accordingly, either all water consecrated in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit is of a pure conscience, not on account of those by whom it is ministered, or by whom it is received, but on account of him who, immaculate, instituted this baptism; or, if water of a pure conscience is made only by the pure conscience both of the man ministering and of the one receiving; what do you do about those whom you find to have been baptized by men who, not yet exposed, bore a stained conscience; especially if among the baptized themselves there exist someone who confesses that he had an evil conscience when he was baptized, in that by that occasion perhaps he had wished to arrive at some disgraceful act?

When, then, it has been made manifest to you that neither the one who ministered nor the one who received baptism had a pure conscience, will you judge that he must be baptized again? You will by no means say this, you will by no means do it. The purity of baptism, then, is altogether distinct from the purity or uncleanness of conscience, whether of the giver or of the receiver. Dare, then, now to say that the defrauder, the robber, the oppressor of orphans and widows, the separator of marriages, the betrayer, seller, and divider of others' patrimonies was of a pure conscience. Dare also to say that those were of a pure conscience whom, in such times, it is hard to think were lacking: if perhaps some, not for the sake of Christ, nor for the sake of eternal life, but for the sake of winning earthly friendships and satisfying earthly desires, courted being baptized by him. Moreover, if you do not dare to say that these were of a pure conscience; if you find any of that number baptized, give them the water of a pure conscience which they did not receive; but if you do not do this, cease to hurl against us what you do not know, lest you be compelled to answer against yourselves what you do know.

**36.** 83. Petilian said: *Which Holy Spirit certainly could not come into you, whom not even the baptism of penance washes; but the water of a betrayer, which is to be repented of, pollutes—which is true.*

[^1]: See Acts 1:15; 2:4; 10:44
