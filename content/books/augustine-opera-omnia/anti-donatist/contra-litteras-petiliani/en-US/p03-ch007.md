# The baptism of dead men does no good.

**7.** 14. Petilian said: *And again: 'He who is baptized by a dead man, his washing does him no good'*[^1]. *He did not assert the baptizer to be a dead man, a lifeless body, or a man's corpse stiffly laid out; but one not having the Spirit of God, who is compared to a dead man, as in another place he makes plain to a disciple, the Gospel attesting it. His disciple said: 'Lord, allow me to bury my father.' Jesus said to him: 'Follow me, and let the dead bury their own dead'*[^2].

*The disciple's father had not been baptized; he consigned the pagan to the pagans: unless he said this about unbelievers, A dead man cannot bury a dead man. A dead man, therefore, not slain by some death, but smitten by his own life. For one who lives in such a way as to be guilty is tormented by a dead life. To be baptized, then, by a dead man, is to have received death, not life. We must act and speak so as to show how a betrayer is held to be dead in his life.*

Dead is the one who has not deserved to be born by true baptism; dead likewise is the one who, begotten by just baptism, has been mingled with a betrayer: both lack the life of baptism, both he who never had it at all and he who had it and lost it. For the Lord Jesus Christ says: 'There shall come into him seven spirits more wicked, and that man's last state shall be worse than the first'[^3].

[^1]: Sir 34:25 (34:30)
[^2]: Matt 8:21-22
[^3]: Matt 12:45
