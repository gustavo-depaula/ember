# There is a great difference between the words of the Donatists and the words of Christ.

**84.** 187. He also says, as a most kindly teacher: *You who refuse to choose the good have condemned yourselves to be unwilling to live.* Yes indeed—if we believed your accusations, we would be living well; because we believe the promises of God, we condemn ourselves to be unwilling to live. You remember well, I suppose, what the apostles said to the Jews, when they were forbidden to preach Christ: this, then, we too say, that you may answer us whether we ought to obey God rather than men[^1].

Traitors, incense-burners, persecutors—these are the words of men against men. Christ has remained in the love of Donatus alone—these are the words of men exalting the glory of a man under the name of Christ, so that the glory of Christ himself may be lessened. For it is written: *In a broad nation is the glory of a king; but in the diminishing of the people is the ruin of the prince*[^2]; these, then, are the words of men.

But those words in the Gospel: *That Christ should suffer and rise on the third day, and that repentance and forgiveness of sins should be preached in his name to all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^3]; these are the words of Christ, commending the glory which he received from the Father in the breadth of his kingdom. Having heard both, we rather choose this communion of the Church, and we prefer the words of Christ to the words of men. I ask: who is there who would say that we have chosen evil, except one who says that Christ taught evil?

[^1]: See Acts 5:29.
[^2]: Prov 14:28.
[^3]: Luke 24:46-47.
