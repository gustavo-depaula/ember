# The examples brought forward by the Donatists do not truly bear on this question.

**92.** 207. Indeed you even dared to set forth terribly and to say: *But, to be silent about ancient things, observe in your own examples how very many emperors and judges of yours perished by making persecution against us.* When I had read this in your letter, I waited most intently for what you would say, and whom you would enumerate; when behold, as though by way of leaving them aside, you began to mention Nero, Domitian, Trajan, Geta, Decius, Valerian, Diocletian, Maximian. I admit there are more: but you have utterly forgotten against whom you are speaking. Were not all these pagans, and did they not universally persecute the Christian name for the sake of their idols? Wake up, then: for these were not of our communion; they persecuted the whole of unity itself, whether—as you suppose—the one from which we come, or—as Christ teaches—the one from which you have departed. But you had set out to show that our emperors and judges perished by making persecution against you.

Or do you not even require that we count these, because, leaving them aside, you mentioned them, saying: *To leave aside Nero*; and under that leaving-aside you ran through the rest? What need was there, then, that those who do not bear on the matter be mentioned? But what is that to me? I now leave these aside with you: let those at least now come forward whom you promised, the very many ones. Unless perhaps they are not found, for the reason that you said they perished.
