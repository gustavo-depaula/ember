# Poverty without love is nothing.

**99.** 228. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: This too bears on the matter, to point out how it is written. For where nothing hinders my purpose, if you deceive or are deceived about anything from the Scriptures, I do not mind. It is not, then, written thus: Whoever loses his substance; but: *Whoever loses his soul for my sake*[^1]. But about substance it is not written: Whoever loses; but: *Whoever leaves*[^2]; and not substance of money alone, but also many other things.

But you, meanwhile, have not lost your substance; whether you have left it, since you so boast of poverty, I do not know; if perhaps my colleague Fortunatus knows this, since you are in the same city, he has never told me, because I had never asked. Nevertheless, even if you have done this, you yourself, against yourself, in this very letter of yours mentioned the apostolic testimony: *If I distribute all my goods to the poor, and hand over my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^3]. For if you had love, you would not charge against the whole earth, unknown to you and unknown by you, even the proved crimes of the Africans, let alone the unproved. If you had love, you would not fabricate it in your slanders, but would recognize unity, most plainly expressed in the words of the Lord, *to the whole earth*[^4]. But if you have not done this, why do you boast as though you had done it? Do you, then, really dread riches, so that, having nothing, you may possess all things?

Say these things to your colleague Crispinus, who recently, near our Hippo, bought an estate, where he might plunge men into the deep. Hence I too know this all too well; you perhaps do not know it, and so, untroubled, you cry out: *We dread riches.* Hence I wonder that this voice of yours, in order to reach us, leaped over him. For between Constantina, where you are, and Hippo, where I am, Calama, where he is, although nearer to us, is nevertheless set between.

I wonder, then, how he did not first catch this voice, and throw it back so that it would not pass to us, and recite against you the praises of riches far more copiously. For he not only does not dread riches, but even loves them. And surely, for the rest, before you publish, recite such things to him; if he does not correct himself, we reply. But you, if it is true that you are poor, you have there my brother Fortunatus: you will more easily be able to please that colleague of mine from there, than this colleague of yours.

[^1]: Matt 16:25.
[^2]: Matt 19:29.
[^3]: 1 Cor 13:3.
[^4]: Acts 1:8.
