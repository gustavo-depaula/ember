# How Augustine deals with Petilian.

**1.** 1. Those who were able to read or hear them remember that we replied sufficiently to the first parts of Petilian's letter, which alone we had found.

But because afterward the whole of it was found and copied by the brothers, and sent to us so that we might reply to all of it, this duty of our pen was not to be shirked: not because he says anything new, to which a reply has not already been made in many ways and often; but on account of the slower brothers, who cannot relate what they have read somewhere to all like cases, I will so accommodate those who altogether compel me to reply point by point, that we may, as it were, discourse in alternating speeches in the present text.

I will set down the words from his letter under his name, and give my reply under mine, as though, while we were debating, they had been taken down by stenographers. Thus there will be no one to complain that I have passed over anything, or that, because the speakers were not distinguished, he could not understand: and at the same time, that those same Donatists, who are unwilling to debate with us face to face, may not, through the letters which they have issued to their own people, so escape the truth replying to them point by point, as if they were conversing with us face to face.
