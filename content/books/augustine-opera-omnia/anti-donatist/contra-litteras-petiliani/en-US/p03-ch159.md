# When the Donatists persecute the Catholics they think they render God a service.

**92.** 206. And where, you will say, was that fulfilled which the Lord says: *The time will come, when whoever kills you will think he does God a service*[^1]? For this could not be said of the pagans, who persecuted the Christians not for God's sake, but for the sake of their idols. Do you not see that, if this had been said of these emperors who rejoice in the Christian name, they would surely have commanded this above all, that you be killed?

Which they never at all commanded. But your people, resisting the laws in a hostile manner, pay the penalties they owe; and their voluntary deaths, while they think them invidious to us, they do not think ruinous to themselves. But if they judge that this was said by the Lord about kings who honor the name of Christ, let them ask what the Catholic Church suffered in the East, when Valens the Arian was ruling. See, I would have a way to understand that what the Lord says was fulfilled: *The time will come, when whoever kills you will think he does God a service*; lest heretics take it to themselves for the glory of their reputation, if Catholic emperors should command anything against their error.

Nevertheless we recall that that time was fulfilled after the Lord's ascension; holy Scripture, known to all, is the witness of this matter. The Jews thought they were doing God a service, when they killed the apostles. Among these who judged that they were rendering God a service was even our Saul, not yet ours; so that he counts this among his praises that are past and to be forgotten: *A Hebrew,* he says, *of Hebrews, according to the law a Pharisee, according to zeal persecuting the Church*[^2].

See the one who thought he was doing God a service, when he was doing what he himself soon endured. For forty Jews had conspired to kill him, when he made this known to the tribune, so that, surrounded by armed soldiers, he might escape their ambushes[^3]. But there was not yet one to say to him: What have you to do, not with kings, but with tribunes and royal arms? There was not one to say to him: Do you dare to seek protection through soldiers, when your Lord was led by them to the passion? These ravings did not yet exist, but against these future ones those examples were even then being prepared.

[^1]: John 16:2.
[^2]: Phil 3:5-6.
[^3]: See Acts 23:12-13.
