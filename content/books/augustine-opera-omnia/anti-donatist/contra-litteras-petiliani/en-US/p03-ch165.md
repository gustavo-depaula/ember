# If kings persecute the Donatists they are not persecutors.

**92.** 212. See, therefore, when Christian kings establish something against you for Catholic unity, lest perhaps with your lips you reproach them as ignorant of the divine Scriptures, while in your heart you grieve that they are sufficiently learned. For who could bear this sacrilegious and odious deceit of yours, to accuse kings in the case of Daniel alone, because he was cast into the lions' den; and not to praise kings, because he was loftily honored; when even at the time he was cast in, the king himself believed that he would rather be safe than die, and, anxious about him, did not dine? Then do you dare to say to Christians: *What have you to do with the kings of this world?* because Daniel suffered persecution from a king; and not to regard that same Daniel faithfully interpreting dreams for kings, calling the king lord, receiving gifts and honors?

And likewise, in the case of those same three boys, that, refusing to worship the statue, they were cast into the flames, to stir up the flames of resentment against kings[^1]; but that they were thus proclaimed and thus honored by the king, to keep silent and conceal it? See, the king was a persecutor, when he threw Daniel to the lions: what when, having received him safe, rejoicing and giving thanks, he sent his enemies to be torn apart and consumed by those same lions—was he a persecutor, or was he not?

I want you to answer. For if he was, why did Daniel himself not resist him, especially since by such great familiarity he could most easily have done so? And do you tell us to call kings back from the persecution of men? But if he was not a persecutor for the reason that he most justly avenged the crime committed against a holy man; how is it to be avenged by kings that the sacraments of Christ are blown away, if the members of the prophet, because they were put in danger, deserved to be so avenged?

Likewise I grant, and it is plain, that the king was a persecutor, when he cast the three boys into the flame, because they refused to worship the image: but I ask whether he was a persecutor, when he set forth that decree, that those who blasphemed the one true God should perish and their houses be laid waste? For if he was a persecutor, why do you answer Amen to the words of a persecutor[^2]? But if he was not, why do you call those persecutors who deter you from the fury of blasphemy? For if they compel you to make supplication to an idol, they are like the impious king, and you like the three boys; but if they prevent you from fighting against Christ, you are the impious ones, if you do this. But what they are, if they terribly forbid this, I do not say: you find something else to say, if you are unwilling to call them pious emperors.

[^1]: See Dan 3:1-24.
[^2]: See Dan 2-6.
