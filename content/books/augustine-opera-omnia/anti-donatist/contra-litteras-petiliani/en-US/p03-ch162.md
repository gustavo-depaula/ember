# Petilian's argument is rejected.

**92.** 209. Now how ridiculous this is, those who know how to speak in any way at all understand, I think: that when you had said: *Macarius perished, Ursacius perished, and all your Counts likewise perished by the vengeance of God;* as though we required you to prove it—when in reality neither hearer nor reader would require anything else—you at once attached a great proof, by which you might prove that all our Counts likewise perished by the vengeance of God. *For Ursacius,* you say, *laid low in a barbarian battle, the birds with savage talons and the greedy teeth of dogs tore apart with their bites.*

In this way another, likewise not knowing what he says, could say that all your bishops perished by the vengeance of God in prison: and when it is demanded of him whence he proves this, he could at once add, For Optatus, accused of being of the retinue of Gildo, was destroyed by such a kind of death. We are compelled to hear and discuss and refute these trifles: so greatly do we fear for the weak, lest, slower in understanding, they run quickly into your snares. But this Ursacius, if perhaps he lived well, and truly so died, will be consoled by the promise of God, who says: *I will require the blood of your souls from the hands of all beasts*[^1].

[^1]: Gen 9:5.
