# The example of Paul is against the Donatists, because they do not hold unity.

**81.** 180. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: You speak against yourself, but nevertheless, because you speak for the truth, if you love it, what you speak becomes for you. For I ask you, about whom did the apostle Paul say this? Let us recall it briefly, if you please. *Some,* he says, *proclaim Christ out of envy and rivalry, but some out of good will; some out of love, knowing that I am set for the defense of the Gospel; but some proclaim Christ even out of contentiousness, not purely, supposing to stir up affliction for my chains. What then? As long as Christ is proclaimed in every way, whether by pretext or in truth, in this I rejoice, and I will rejoice*[^1]. We see that these men proclaimed something indeed holy and pure and true, yet not purely, but out of envy and rivalry, without love, without purity.

Surely a little before you seemed to be speaking the praises of love against us from the Apostle's testimony, that where love is not, whatever there may be profits nothing: see, in these men love is not, and yet there was the preaching of Christ, about which the Apostle says he rejoices. For he rejoices not in their evil, but in the good of the name of Christ. For in him was love, which *does not rejoice over iniquity, but rejoices with the truth*[^2]. Envy, moreover, which was in them, is a diabolical evil; for by this the devil both fell and cast down. These men, then, so wicked, whom the Apostle thus reproves, in whom was so great a good in which the Apostle rejoices, where were they? Within, or without?

Choose whichever you wish. If within, see, Paul knew them, and yet they did not pollute him; so neither would those, about whom you say I know not what, whether you speak truths or fabricate falsehoods, pollute you in the unity of the whole earth. Why then did you separate yourselves? Why did you destroy by the sacrilege of a wicked schism?

But if they were without, see, even in those who are without, and who certainly cannot belong to eternal life, because they have not love and do not hold unity, there is nonetheless the holiness of the name of Christ, whose preaching the Apostle, on account of the holiness of the name itself, although he detests those men, confirms by rejoicing.

Rightly, then, we do no injury to the name itself, when those who were without come to us; but we correct them, and honor that name. See, therefore, how wickedly, in those whose deeds you reprove as it seems, you blow away even the sacrament of the name of Christ, which in them is holy. And you indeed, as your words indicate, judge that those men whom the Apostle mentioned were without, outside the Church. So, while you fear to suffer persecution from the Catholics, about which you stir up resentment against us by your speaking, you have confirmed the name of Christ in heretics, to which you do injury by rebaptizing.

[^1]: Phil 1:15-18.
[^2]: 1 Cor 13:6.
