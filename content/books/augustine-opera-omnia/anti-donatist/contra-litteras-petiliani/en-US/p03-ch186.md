# Prayer must be made even for priests.

**105.** 241. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: And I seemed, a little before, when we were discussing the oil of the sinner, to anoint your forehead, so that you might say, if you dared, whether you yourself were not a sinner. See, you have said it. O crime! O monstrosity! For because you assert that you are a priest, what else, by this prophetic testimony, did you say, except that you are altogether without sin? For if you have sin, who will pray for you according to your understanding? For you so sell yourselves to the wretched common people, mentioning from the prophet: *If the people sins, the priest will pray for it; but if the priest sins, who will pray for him?*[^1] that they may believe you to be without sin, and entrust their sins to be purged by your prayers.

Great men you, lofty, heavenly, divine, no longer men, but angels, who pray for the common people, and are unwilling that the common people pray for you! Are you more just than Paul, more perfect than so great an Apostle, who commended himself to the prayers of those he taught? *Persevere in prayer,* he says, *watching in it, in thanksgiving; praying at the same time for us also, that God may open to us a door of the word, to speak the mystery of Christ, for which I am also in chains, that I may make it manifest, as I ought to speak*[^2]. See, prayer is made for the Apostle, which you are unwilling should be done for a bishop. Do you see how diabolical this pride is?

Prayer is made for the Apostle, that he may make manifest the mystery of Christ, as he ought to speak. Accordingly, if you had pious common people, you ought to have exhorted them to pray for you, lest you should speak as you ought not. Are you more just than John the evangelist, who says: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^3]? Are you, finally, more just than Daniel, whom you yourself, in this very letter, mentioned, so as to say: *A king cast most just Daniel, as he supposed, to the bites of beasts*? Which he had not supposed; since with a most friendly disposition, as the context of the reading proves, he said to Daniel himself: *Your God, whom you serve continually, will himself deliver you*[^4].

But about this we have already said much; now, what is at issue—surely Daniel was most just; not by your testimony, although this would suffice me for the cause I am pleading with you; but by the testimony of the Spirit of God, speaking also through Ezekiel, where he named three of most eminent righteousness, Noah, Daniel, and Job, whom he said alone could be freed from a certain most vehement wrath of God, which was hanging over the rest[^5].

A most just man, then, one of three memorable ones, prays, and says: *While I was praying and confessing my sins and the sins of my people in the sight of the Lord my God*[^6]. And do you say that you are without sin; because, forsooth, you are a priest, and if the people sins, you pray for it; but if you sin, who will pray for you? Truly, because by the impiety of so great arrogance you show yourself unworthy that that priest should intercede for you, whom by these words, which you do not understand, the prophet wished to be understood.

For now, lest anyone inquire why this was said, I will explain it, as far as the Lord grants. God was preparing the minds of men through the prophet, that they might desire such a priest as no one could pray for. He himself was prefigured in the times of the first people and the first temple, where all things were figures of ours. Therefore the high priest alone entered into the holy of holies, to pray for the people, who did not enter with the priest into those inner holy things[^7]; just as that Priest entered into the secret places of the heavens, into those truer holy of holies, while we are still placed here, for whom he intercedes. Therefore the prophet said: *If the people sins, the priest will pray for it; but if the priest sins, who will pray for him?* that is: Desire such a priest, who cannot sin, that he may not need to be prayed for. And therefore prayer is made by the peoples for the apostles[^8]; but for that Priest, the Master and Lord of the apostles, prayer is not made. Hear John confessing and teaching this: *Brothers,* he says, *I write these things to you, that you may not sin; and if anyone sins, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the just; he is the propitiation for our sins*[^9]. *We have,* he said; *our sins,* he said. Learn humility, lest you fall; rather, that you may at last rise. For you would not have said these things, unless you had fallen.
