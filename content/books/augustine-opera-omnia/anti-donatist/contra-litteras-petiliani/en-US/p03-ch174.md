# Augustine exhorts the Donatists to save their souls and not to save their goods.

**98.** 226. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: This exhortation would be useful, I admit, if anyone used it in a good cause. You have altogether done well to deter men from preferring riches to their soul. But you who have heard these things, hear us too a little; for we too say this, but attend in what way.

If kings threaten to take away your riches, because you are not Jews according to the flesh, or because you do not worship idols or demons, or because you are not carried off into any heresies, but remain in Catholic unity; choose rather that your riches perish, lest you yourselves perish; but do not prefer anything else either, nor temporal life itself, to the eternal salvation which is in Christ. But if kings threaten you with losses or condemnation for this reason, because you are heretics; they terrify you not cruelly, but mercifully; while you fear them not bravely, but stubbornly. Hear, then, Peter saying: *But what glory is it, if, sinning, you suffer punishments?*[^1], so that you have neither here earthly consolations, nor in the age to come eternal life; but both here the straits of the unhappy, and there the hells of heretics?

You see, then, brother, with whom I now have to deal, that you ought first to show whether you hold the truth, and then to exhort men, that for the sake of holding it they may be ready to be without all the things they temporally hold; since you do not show this; because you cannot; not because talent is lacking, but because the cause is bad; why do you hasten, by your exhortations, to make men both beggars and ignorant, and needy and erring, and ragged and contentious, and famished and heretics, both losing temporal goods in this age and finding eternal evils in the judgment of Christ? But the cautious son, who, fearing the father's scourge, withdraws from the serpent's lair, is neither beaten nor perishes. But he who despises the pains of discipline before his own ruinous will, is both beaten and perishes. Do you not now understand, eloquent man, that he who, for the peace of Christ, is without all earthly things, has God; but he who, for the party of Donatus, loses even a few coins, has no heart?

[^1]: 1 Pet 2:20.
