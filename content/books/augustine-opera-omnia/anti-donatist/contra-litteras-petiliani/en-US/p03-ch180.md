# Augustine: those who applaud Petilian's words have not ears in the heart, but the heart in the ears.

**101.** 232. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: I would like to converse with those who, when these words were read or heard, applauded. For such men have not ears in the heart, but the heart in the ears. Let them, nevertheless, read again and again, and ponder, and feel them—not what they sound like, but what they mean.

First, to dispatch this last point: *So it comes about,* you say, *that you who had come innocent to baptism, return from baptism a parricide;* answer first, who comes innocent to baptism, except him who came to be baptized not in order that his iniquity might be washed away, but in order that the authority of humility might be set before us? For what will be forgiven the innocent? Or are you really so eloquent as to show us a certain sinful innocence? Do you not hear Scripture saying: *No one is clean from sin in your sight, not even the infant whose life on earth is of one day*[^1]? For why is recourse had to the remission of sins even with infants? Do you not hear another: *I was conceived in iniquity*[^2]?

Then, if he who had come without parricide returns a parricide for the reason that a parricide baptizes him; then as many as returned baptized by Optatus have been made Optatuses. Go now, and charge us with rousing the wrath of kings against you. Do you not fear lest as many of Gildo's henchmen be sought out from you as there are men who could have been baptized by Optatus? Do you see at last how this sentence of yours, like a bladder, has burst not only with an empty sound, but even upon your own head?

[^1]: Job 14:4-5.
[^2]: Ps 51:7 (50:7).
