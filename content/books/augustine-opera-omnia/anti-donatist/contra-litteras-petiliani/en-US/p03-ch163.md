# Some explanation of the second psalm.

**92.** 210. But as for the slander you bring against us, saying that the kings of this world are roused by us to anger against you, while we do not teach them the divine Scripture, but rather suggest our malice to them; I do not think they are so deaf to the very utterances of the holy books that you should not rather fear that they may know them. But, whether you will or not, they enter the Church, and even if we are silent, they lend an ear to the readers; and, to be silent about the rest, they take in most intently this very psalm which you mentioned.

For you said that we do not teach them, nor allow them, willing though they are, to know what is written: *And now, kings, understand; be instructed, you who judge the earth; serve the Lord in fear, and exult in him with trembling; lay hold of discipline, lest at any time the Lord be angry*[^1]*,* and so on. Believe that these things too are sung, and that they hear them; but surely they hear also that earlier passage in the same psalm, which you, unless I am mistaken, were unwilling to pass over for this reason, lest you be understood to have feared it. They hear, then, this too: *The Lord said to me: You are my Son, today I have begotten you; ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and the ends of the earth as your possession*[^2].

Hearing which, they surely wonder that there are some who contradict this inheritance of Christ, and try to reduce it to a scanty part of the earth; and wondering, they perhaps inquire, on account of what they hear in the words that follow: *Serve the Lord in fear;* in what way they can serve him, insofar as they are kings.

For all men ought to serve God: in one way by the common condition by which they are men; in another way by their various gifts, since one man does one thing in human affairs, another another. For no one as a private person could command idols to be taken away from the earth, which was foretold so long before to be in the future[^3]. Kings, then, have—apart from the fellowship of the human race, by the very fact that they are kings—a way to serve the Lord such as those who are not kings cannot.

[^1]: Ps 2:10-12.
[^2]: Ps 2:7-8.
[^3]: See Isa 2:18; Zech 13:2.
