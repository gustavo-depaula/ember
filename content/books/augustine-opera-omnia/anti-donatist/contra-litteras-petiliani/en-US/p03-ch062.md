# Therefore one had to be baptized after John.

**37.** 88. Therefore do not make a fog about the baptism of John, whose cause and reason, whether it be the one I have set forth, or some other better and more certain one be given, this nevertheless is manifest, that the baptism of John was one thing, that of Christ another; and that the former was called the baptism of John, as both the answer of those whom you mentioned declares, and the discourse of the Lord himself, where he says to the Jews: *Is the baptism of John from heaven, or from men?*[^1] But this baptism is not called the baptism of Caecilian, nor of Donatus, nor of Augustine, nor of Petilian, but the baptism of Christ. For if you think us shameless, because we wish no one to be baptized after us, when we see that there were men baptized after John, who is certainly incomparably greater than we; are John and Optatus perhaps equals? It seems ridiculous: and yet I think that you believe Optatus not equal, but greater.

For after John the Apostle baptized, but you dare to baptize no one after Optatus. Is it because Optatus was in your unity? Now with what feelings this can be endured I do not know, if the friend of the Companion, for whom God was a Companion, is said to have been in unity, and the friend of the bridegroom[^2] outside unity. But if John was most of all in unity, and was far more excellent and greater not only than Optatus, but than all of us and all of you, and yet after him Paul the apostle baptized; why do you not baptize after Optatus?

Unless perhaps your blindness presses you into these straits, that you say Optatus could give the Holy Spirit, John could not. But if you do not say this, lest your insanity be laughed at even by the insane themselves; what will you reply, why men had to be baptized after John, but no one is to be baptized after Optatus, except because those men had been baptized with the baptism of John; but whoever are baptized with the baptism of Christ, whether they be baptized through Paul or through Optatus, there is no difference between the baptism of the former and of the latter, although there is so great a difference between Paul and Optatus?

Return, then, transgressors, to your heart[^3], and do not weigh the divine sacraments by the morals and deeds of men. For those sacraments are holy through him whose they are: but, worthily handled, they bring a reward; unworthily handled, a judgment.

And although those who have handled the sacrament of God worthily and those who have handled it unworthily are not one[^4]: nevertheless it is one, whether handled worthily or unworthily; not so that it becomes better or worse, but unto the life or death of those who handle it. As for what you said, *that in those whom Paul baptized after John he washed what already was;* surely you would not say it, if you considered a little what you were saying.

For if the baptism of John had to be washed, beyond doubt it was filthy. Why, then, should I press you further? Recall or read, and see whence John received this: you will find against whom you have poured out this blasphemy; and when you have found it, let at least the breast be beaten, if the tongue is not bridled.

[^1]: Matt 21:25
[^2]: See John 3:29.
[^3]: See Isa 46:8.
[^4]: See 1 Cor 11:29.
