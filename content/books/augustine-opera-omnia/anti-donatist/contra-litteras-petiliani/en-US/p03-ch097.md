# How Augustine interprets the words of the Gospel cited by Petilian.

**61.** 138. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: Where perhaps you state the testimonies of the Scriptures otherwise than they stand, and it does not bear on the matter at issue between us, I do not much mind; but where, unless they are quoted truthfully, they hinder what we are treating, I do not think you ought to be angry if I remind you how it is written. See, what you have just put forward is not written so, but rather thus: *Whoever breaks one of these least commandments and so teaches shall be called least in the kingdom of heaven. But whoever does and so teaches shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven.* And he goes on at once to say: *For I tell you that unless your righteousness exceeds that of the scribes and Pharisees, you shall not enter the kingdom of heaven*[^1]. For elsewhere he points out and reproves the Pharisees, because they say and do not do.

These, then, he meant here too, when he said: *Whoever breaks and so teaches,* that is, teaches by words what he breaks by deeds. Above these he commanded our righteousness to abound, so that we may do and so teach. And yet, not even on account of those Pharisees, to whom you compare us not through good judgment but through ill will, did the Lord command that the chair of Moses be abandoned, in which indeed he was prefiguring his own; for though he said that they sit in the chair of Moses and say but do not do, he nevertheless admonishes the people to do what they say and not to do what they do[^2], lest the holiness of the chair be abandoned, and the unity of the flock be divided on account of bad shepherds.

[^1]: Matt 5:19-20.
[^2]: See Matt 23:2-3.
