# The example of the Maximianists.

**7.** 16. But how false is what you say: *Both lack the life of baptism, both he who never had it at all and he who had it and lost it:* you may notice this from the fact that those who, having been baptized, apostatize and return through penance, are not given baptism again, which, if they had lost it, would be given again.

But how do your dead men baptize, according to your view? Or are drunkards not dead (to be silent about the rest, and to say only what is known to all and is a daily matter), when the Apostle says about the widow: *But she who lives in pleasures is dead while she lives*[^1]? Then, in that council of yours in which you condemned Maximian together with his sponsors or his ministers, did it slip your mind how eloquently you said: *Very much after the example of the Egyptians, the shores are full of the corpses of the perishing; whose punishment in death itself is the greater, in that, after their soul has been wrenched out by the avenging waters, they do not even find burial?*

And yet whether one of them, Felicianus, has come back to life, you must see to that: nevertheless he has among you, inside, together with himself, those whom, while dead, he baptized outside.

As, therefore, one is baptized by a living man who is clothed with the baptism of the living Christ; so one is baptized by a dead man who is wrapped in the baptism of the dead Saturn, or of any other—so that we may say briefly, for the moment, how the words you set down can be understood without distress to any of us. For as they are taken by you, you are striving not to extricate yourselves, but to entangle us along with you.

[^1]: 1 Tim 5:6
