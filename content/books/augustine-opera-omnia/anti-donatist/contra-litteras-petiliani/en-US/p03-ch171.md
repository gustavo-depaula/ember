# There follows the refutation of Petilian.

**96.** 222. *But if you have innocence,* you say, *why do you pursue us with the sword?* Look for a moment at your bands, which are armed not, after the old manner of their parents, with clubs alone, but have added axes and lances and swords; and recognize whose voice it ought rather to be: *Why do you pursue us with the sword?* *Or if you call us guilty,* you say, *why do you seek us as innocent?* Here I answer briefly: You are sought as guilty by the innocent, so that you may cease to be guilty, and begin to be innocent. See, I have chosen both of ours, and answered to both of yours; now you choose one of two: Are you innocent, or guilty?

Nor can you say both; but say both, if this pleases you. For surely you cannot in the same cause be innocent, in which you are guilty. If, then, you are innocent, do not wonder that you are sought for peace by brothers: but if guilty, do not wonder that you are sought for punishment by kings. But because of these two you usurp one for yourselves, and hear the other from us; for you usurp innocence for yourselves, but you hear from us that you live impiously; receive again what I say about both. If you are innocent, why do you contradict the testimony of Christ? But if guilty, why do you not flee to his mercy? For both his testimony is about the unity of the world, and his mercy is in brotherly love.

**97.** 223. PETILIAN SAID: *Finally, as we have often already said, what kind of presumption is yours, that you presume upon kings, when David says: It is better to hope in the Lord than to hope in man: it is better to hope in the Lord than to hope in princes*[^1].

[^1]: Ps 118:8-9 (117:8-9).
