# If sinners cannot baptize, then not even your people could.

**33.** 78. Augustine replied: You had certainly proposed to discuss fully the baptism of the Trinity, and you had made us very attentive: but—what is, of course, easiest for you—how quickly you turned to your usual curses. This indeed you do copiously. For you set before yourself whomever you wish, against whom you may inveigh as much as you wish: in which great breadth of speech, by one most brief word that is said, Prove it, you are pressed into the narrowest straits. For this the seed of Abraham says to you, in which, while all the nations are blessed[^1], they do not care when they are cursed by you.

But nevertheless, since you deal with baptism, which you think to be true when it is in a just man, but false when it is in an unjust man; behold, I too, according to your rule, if I should discuss the baptism of the Trinity, can say, more copiously, I think, that he does not have God for a Father for whom God is a Companion; nor does he believe in his own Christ, except the one for whom he has suffered; nor does he have the Holy Spirit, who in a far different manner has burned wretched Africa with tongues of fire. How, then, does he have baptism, or how does he give it in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit?

Surely you now notice that there can be in an unjust man, or be given by an unjust man, a baptism that is not unjust, but just and true; not because it is his, but because it is God's. And I bring no calumny against you about this; which you do not cease to do about some men or other against the whole world, and—what is more intolerable—you do not even prove anything about those very men.

But this I do not know how it can be borne, that you bring calumny not only against holy men concerning unjust men; but you even procure a crime, from the contagion of sinful men, for holy baptism itself, which in any unjust man whatever must necessarily be holy, so that you say that baptism is such as is the one by whom it is had, or given, or received.

Moreover, if a man becomes such as the one with whom he approaches the holy things, and the sacraments themselves become such as the men in whom they are; it suffices for holy men, as a consolation, to hear from you a false charge in common with holy baptism: only see how you are condemned by your own mouth, if your sober men too are drunkards from the contagion of your drunkards, and your merciful men are robbers from the contagion of robbers, and whatever is found among you in evil men, this men who are not such are compelled to be; and baptism itself is unclean in all your unclean men, and, according to the diversity of that uncleanness, is diverse, if it is compelled to be such as is the one by whom it is had and given. These things are certainly false, and therefore they do us no harm when you say them against us and do not look to yourselves; but they harm you, because, when you say them falsely, they do not fall upon us, but, because you think them true, they recoil upon you.

**34.** 79. Petilian said: *For if those apostles were permitted to baptize whom John had washed with the baptism of penance, will it not be permitted to me to baptize you sacrilegious men?*

[^1]: See Gen 22:18
