# The matter concerns the three boys cast into the furnace of fire.

**92.** 211. When, then, they think of these things, they hear also that which you yourself mentioned about the three boys, and they hear it with a wonderful solemnity. For that same Scripture is sung in the Church most of all at the time when the very festival makes more fervent even those who throughout the rest of the year are more sluggish. What disposition, then, do you think Christian emperors have, when they hear that the three boys were sent into the furnace of burning fire, because they refused to consent to the king's iniquity of worshiping an image[^1]; except that they consider that the pious freedom of the saints can be overcome neither by royal power nor by the cruelty of punishment? And that they rejoice that they are not of the number of such kings, who punished as sacrilegious the despisers of idols?

But further, when in what follows they hear that the same king, terrified by so great a miracle—of not only the boys, but also the flames serving God—himself too began to serve God in fear, and to exult with trembling[^2], and to lay hold of discipline; do they not understand that these things were written down, and recited with such great solemnity, so that to the servants of God, that they may not yield to sacrilege for kings' sake, and to the kings themselves, that they may turn to religion and believe in God, examples might be set forth?

These men, then, wishing—from the admonition of that very psalm which you too inserted into your writings—to understand, and to be instructed, and to serve the Lord in fear, and to exult with trembling, and to lay hold of discipline, how intently do they hear what that king afterward said. For he said that he would set forth a decree to all the peoples over whom he ruled, that whoever spoke blasphemy against the God of Shadrach, Meshach, Abednego should perish, and their houses be torn down[^3]. And if they learn that he decreed this, lest the God who tempered the fires and freed the three boys be blasphemed; they surely consider what they ought to decree in their own kingdom, lest in their faithful that same God, the remitter of sins and now the liberator of the whole earth, be blown away.

[^1]: See Dan 3:1-24.
[^2]: See Ps 2:10-11.
[^3]: See Dan 3:96.
