# This accusation is not proved.

**20.** 46. And yet we ask of you, whom of your people you can prove to have been killed by ours. I recall no law given by the emperors that you should be killed. But those over whom you are wont to stir up the greatest odium, Marculus and Donatus—to put it more moderately—it is uncertain whether they threw themselves down, as your teaching does not cease to do by daily examples; or whether they were thrown down at the command of some authority.

For if it is incredible that the masters of the Circumcellions inflicted on themselves their accustomed deaths, how much more incredible that the Roman powers could have ordered unaccustomed punishments? And so about this matter, which you think too odious, if what you say is true, what does this pertain to the Lord's wheat? Let the chaff that flew outside accuse the chaff that remained inside: for the whole cannot be separated except by the final winnowing fan. But if it is false, what wonder is it if the chaff, carried off as by the light breath of dissension, even pursues the Lord's wheat with false accusations?

Therefore, about all such odious crimes, this grain of Christ, which was commanded to grow with the weeds throughout the field, that is, throughout the whole world, answers you with a free and secure voice: If you do not prove what you say, it pertains to no one; but if you prove it, it does not pertain to me. From which it comes about that whoever has separated himself from this unity of the grain on account of the crimes of the weeds or of the chaff, on account of that very evil of dissension and schism cannot defend himself even from the very crime of murder, since the Scripture says: *He who hates his brother is a murderer*[^1].

[^1]: 1 John 3:15
