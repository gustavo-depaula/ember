# This psalm speaks of the universal Church.

**47.** 110. Augustine replied: This psalm speaks of those who receive baptism well, and use the holy thing in a holy way. For these words do not pertain also to Simon Magus, who nevertheless received the same holy baptism; and because he was unwilling to use it in a holy way, he did not therefore defile it, nor teach that it must be repeated for such men.

But because you made mention of Goliath, attend to that psalm about Goliath himself, and see that he was conquered in a new song; for there it is said: *God, I will sing a new song to you; on the psaltery of ten strings I will play to you*[^1]. And see whether he belongs to this song who is not in communion with the world. For elsewhere it is said: *Sing to the Lord a new song; sing to the Lord, all the earth*[^2]. All the earth, then, in whose unity you are not, sings a new song. And of all the earth are these words too: *The Lord feeds me, and nothing shall be lacking to me*[^3], and the rest. These are not the words of the weeds, although they are borne in one harvest until the harvesting; they are not the words of the chaff, but of the wheat; although they are nourished by the same rain and at the same time, threshed on the same floor and at the same time, until by the final winnowing fan they are separated from one another—the two which certainly have in common one baptism, although they are not one. But even if your party were the Church of God, you would surely confess that this psalm there does not pertain to the frenzied bands of the Circumcellions.

Or if they too are led upon the paths of righteousness, why do you deny that they are your companions, when they are charged against you, although for the most part the fewness of your cutting-off is consoled not by the rod and staff of God, but by the clubs of those men, by whom you think yourselves safe even against the Roman laws; and to run upon these, what is it else than to walk in the midst of the shadow of death? But he with whom the Lord is fears no evils. Certainly, nevertheless, you will not dare to say that the words sung in this psalm are also the words of those frenzied men, and yet you not only confess but even profess that they have baptism.

They do not say these things, then, except those who are refreshed by the holy water, like all the just of God; not those who are overthrown by using it ill, like that magician baptized by Philip; and yet that water itself in both is one and holy. They do not say these things except those who will belong to the right hand; but nevertheless on the same pasture, under one shepherd, both sheep and goats are fed, until, that they may receive their due, they are separated.

They do not say these things except those who take from the table of the Lord life, like Peter; not judgment, like Judas; and yet it was one for both, but did not avail for both unto the one end, because they themselves were not one. They do not say these things except those who are blessed by the holy oil even in the spirit, like David; not those who are consecrated in the body alone, like Saul; and yet, when both had received one thing, the sacrament was not unequal for them, but the merit was unequal.

They do not say these things except those who, with mind changed, sigh for the cup of the Lord unto eternal life; not those who eat and drink judgment to themselves, as the Apostle says[^4]; and yet that one cup is one for both, not as belonging to them as one, inebriating the martyrs to lay hold of heavenly things[^5], not the Circumcellions to deal death by headlong leaps.

Remember, then, that the morals of evil men do no harm to the sacraments of God, so that these either are not at all, or are less holy; but they harm the evil men themselves, so that they have these unto the testimony of damnation, not unto the help of health.

For you ought at least to have considered the very last words of this psalm, and, on account of those who, having received baptism, apostatize, to understand that it cannot be said by all who receive holy baptism: *That I may dwell in the house of the Lord unto length of days*; and yet, whether for those who persevere or for those who have lapsed, although they themselves are not one, baptism is one; and although they are not both holy, baptism in both is holy; because apostates too, if they return, are not baptized as though they had lost it, but are humbled because they did an injury to what remained in them.

**48.** 111. Petilian said: *But lest you say that you are holy; first I assert this, that he who has not been innocent does not have holiness.*

[^2]: Ps 96:1 (95:1).
[^3]: Ps 23:1 (22:1).
[^4]: See 1 Cor 11:29.
[^5]: Ps 23:5 (22:5).
