# A traitor is not the same as a persecutor.

**103.** 237. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: In the person of the body of Christ, which is the Church of the living God, the pillar and ground of truth[^1], spread throughout the whole earth, on account of the Gospel which is preached, as the Apostle says, *in every creature which is under heaven*[^2]; in the person of the whole earth, of which the same David, whose words you do not understand, says: *He established the earth, which shall not be moved*[^3]; which you contend has not merely not been moved by the sins of others, but has altogether perished; in his person, then, I answer: I do not persecute the innocent. But David said *the oil of the sinner;* he did not say, of the traitor; not, of the incense-burner; not, of the persecutor; but, *of the sinner.* What, then, will you do according to your own understanding? See first whether you yourself are not a sinner. I do not want you to say to me: I am not a traitor, I am not an incense-burner, I am not a persecutor. Neither indeed am I, if it please the Lord, any of these; nor is the whole earth which shall not be moved. But say, if you dare: I am not a sinner. For he said, *the oil of the sinner.* For whatever even of light sin is found in you, how do you show that it does not belong to what was said, *the oil of the sinner*?

For I ask whether you pray with the Lord's Prayer? For if you do not pray with that prayer, with which the Lord taught the disciples to pray, whence did you learn another, exceeding the merits of the apostles, for your greater merits? But if you pray as that Master deigned to teach, how do you say: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^4]? For we do not ask this about those sins which were forgiven us in baptism. Therefore these words of the prayer either do not permit you to be one who entreats God, or they disclose you to be a sinner.

Let those go now, and kiss your head, who were baptized by you, whose heads perished by your oil. But you, see what you are, or what you think of yourself. Is it really so, that Optatus, whom pagans, Jews, Christians, ours, yours, throughout all Africa, cry out to be a thief, a plunderer, a betrayer, an oppressor, a separator, and the henchman—not the friend, not the client, but the henchman—of him whom a certain one of you called his Count God, was not even a sinner of some sort?

What, then, will they do, whose heads one guilty of a capital crime has anointed? Do not even they themselves kiss your heads, about whose heads, through your understanding, you judge so ill? At least betray them, and admonish them to be healed. Or is it rather your head that needs healing, raving as you are? What, then, you will say, did David say? Why do you ask me? Ask him; in the previous verse he will answer you: *The just man,* he says, *will correct me in mercy, and reprove me; but the oil of the sinner shall not anoint my head*[^5].

What plainer? What more open? I prefer, he says, to be healed by merciful chiding, than to be deceived and perverted by smooth flattery, as though my head were anointed. Under other words, the same sentence is elsewhere in the Scriptures: *Better are the wounds of a friend than the willing kisses of an enemy*[^6].

[^1]: See 1 Tim 3:15.
[^2]: Col 1:23.
[^3]: Ps 93:1 (92:1).
[^4]: Matt 6:12.
[^5]: Ps 141:5 (140:5).
[^6]: Prov 27:6.
