# The Donatists did not observe the beatitude of mercy.

**67.** 150. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: Neither do you prove yourselves righteous, nor that we inflict punishments even on the unrighteous. And yet, just as deceitful flattery is for the most part cruel, so just rebuke is always merciful. For from this comes that which you do not understand: *The righteous man shall correct me in mercy, and shall reprove me.* For when he had said this about the harshness of merciful rebuke, he at once added about the gentleness of harmful flattery, and said: *But let not the oil of the sinner anoint my head*[^1]. You, then, attend to where you are called, and from where you are called back. For how do you know with what disposition toward you he is whom you think savage?

But however he may be, each one bears his own burden[^2], both among us and among you; but cast off the burden of schism, which you all bear, so that you may bear your good burdens in unity; and those who bear bad ones, if you can, mercifully correct; if you cannot, peacefully bear with them.

[^1]: Ps 141:5 (140:5).
[^2]: See Gal 6:5.
