# The Donatists are chaff; what the name 'Macarius' signifies.

**39.** 94. But that you call the whole world, rejoicing in Christian fellowship, the party of Macarius; who in his right mind would say this? But because we say that you are of the party of Donatus, you look for a man of whose party you may say that we are; and in great straits you name someone or other, perhaps known in Africa, but unknown in the other parts of the world.

Therefore, behold, the whole seed of Abraham answers you from the whole earth: this Macarius, of whose party you say we are, we do not know at all. Answer in turn that you do not know Donatus. But even if we should say that you are of the party of Optatus, who of you can say that he does not know Optatus, except perhaps by face, as neither Donatus? But you rejoice, of course, in the name of Donatus; do you also rejoice in the name of Optatus? What good, then, does Donatus do you, when Optatus has defiled you all? What good does the sobriety of Donatus do you, when you are polluted by the drunkenness of the Circumcellions? What good does the innocence of Donatus, according to your opinion, do you, when you are stained by the rapacity of Optatus?

For this is your error, that you think the iniquity of a man avails more to contaminate another than the righteousness of a man to purify another. And so, if two alike share the divine sacraments, one just, the other unjust; but in such a way that neither does this one imitate that one's iniquity, nor that one this one's righteousness; you do not say that from this both become just, but both unjust, so that even that holy thing which they take alike becomes unclean, and loses its own holiness.

Where did iniquity find for itself such advocates, by whose raving it is thought thus victorious? Why, then, do you applaud yourselves over the name of Donatus, in so great an error of perversity, where Petilian does not deserve to be what Donatus was, but Donatus himself is compelled to be what Optatus was?

But let the house of Israel say: *The Lord is my part*[^1], let the seed of Abraham in all the nations say: *The Lord is the part of my inheritance*[^2]. For it knows how to say it, as Caiaphas in the Gospel of the glory of the blessed God[^3]. For you too, through the sacrament which is in you, like Caiaphas the persecutor of the Lord, prophesy unknowingly[^4]. For what in Greek is 'μακάριος' is in Latin 'blessed.' So we are plainly of the party of Macarius. For what is more blessed than Christ, whose we are, to whom all the ends of the earth are recalled and turn, and in whose sight all the families of the nations worship[^5]?

And so the party of this Macarius, that is, of this blessed one, does not fear your last malediction, perversely usurped from Solomon, that it should perish from the earth. For what was said about the impious, this you try to turn to the inheritance of Christ, and you contend with unspeakable impiety that this has come to pass. For when he was speaking about the impious, he said: *Let their part perish from the earth*[^6]. But when you say that what is written, *I will give you the nations for your inheritance*[^7]; and, *All the ends of the earth shall remember and turn to the Lord*[^8], has now perished from the earth; you wish to twist back to the inheritance of Christ what was foretold about the lot of the impious: but, the inheritance of Christ remaining and growing, when you say these things, you perish. For you do not everywhere prophesy through the sacrament of God, since here, by your insanity, you curse evilly. But the prediction of the prophets avails more than the malediction of false prophets.

**40.** 95. Petilian said: *Paul the apostle also cries out: 'Do not bear the yoke with unbelievers. For what participation has righteousness with iniquity? Or what fellowship has light with darkness? What agreement has Christ with Belial? Or what part has the believer with the unbeliever?'*[^9].
