# A true explanation of the words of Scripture.

**32.** 75. But now, as for your saying that there is indeed one baptism, but nevertheless consecrated in three degrees, and dividing these three among individual persons in such a way that you assigned the water to John, the Holy Spirit to the Lord Jesus Christ, and the third, the fire, to the Paraclete sent from above—consider for a moment how great the error is.

For you were led to think this because John says: *I indeed baptize in water: but he who comes after me is greater than I; he will baptize you in the Holy Spirit and fire*[^1]. And you were unwilling to consider that the three were not fitted singly to the three—the water to John, the Spirit to Christ, the fire to the Paraclete; but that these three pertain rather to two, one to John, and the other two to the Lord. For it was not said: I indeed baptize you in water; but he who comes after me is greater than I, whose shoes I am not worthy to carry, he will baptize you in the Holy Spirit; but the Paraclete who is to come after him, he will baptize you in fire; rather: *I indeed,* he says, *in water; but he who comes after me, in the Holy Spirit and fire.* He assigns one to himself, those two to the other. You see, then, how the number deceived you.

Attend further: you said that the one baptism is consecrated in three degrees, water, the Holy Spirit, and fire; and you set the three persons one to each, John to the water, Christ to the Spirit, the Paraclete to the fire.

If, then, John's water pertains to the same one whose unity is commended, those whom Paul the apostle had found baptized by John ought not to have been baptized at his command[^2]: for they already had the water pertaining, as you say, to the same baptism: it remained that they should receive the Spirit and the fire, because these had been lacking to John, that the baptism consecrated, as you assert, in three degrees, might be completed. But since by apostolic authority they were ordered to be baptized, it was sufficiently declared that that water of John does not pertain to the baptism of Christ, but belonged, for the necessary time, to another dispensation.

[^1]: Matt 3:11
[^2]: See Acts 19:1-6
