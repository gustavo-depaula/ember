# Petilian contradicts himself. (2)

**92.** 203. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: To this passage, so copiously amplified and arranged by you, where you speak invidiously against us about the kings of this world, if I were to reply as is due and fitting, I fear that you too would charge me with having wished to rouse the wrath of kings against you. Although, since in your manner you are carried by the force of this invective universally against all Catholics, you certainly do not pass me over. I will take care, nevertheless, to show, if I can, that you have done this rather by saying such things, than I by replying to them.

And first see how you yourself oppose yourself; surely you began this passage thus: *But what have you to do with the kings of this world, whom Christianity has never found anything but hostile?* By these words you certainly forbid us to approach the kings of this world.

And a little after you say: *And those same men, that is, the kings, lest as though ignorant or unaware they should desire to persecute Christians, he admonished by these precepts, that they might not perish; which precepts, since they do not know them, would that we might teach them; or certainly you would without doubt show them to them, if you wished them to live.*

How, then, do you want us to be teachers of kings? And our people indeed, if they have any friendship with Christian kings, if they use it well, do no wrong; but if any are puffed up by it, they sin much more lightly than you. For you, who reproach us thus, what had you to do with a pagan king—and, what is graver, an apostate and an enemy of the Christian name, Julian—from whom, when you begged that the basilicas be given back to you as though they were yours, you put this in his praise, *that with him justice alone had a place*?

By which words (for I believe you understand Latin) both the idolatry of Julian, and his apostasy, were called justice. The petition which your elders submitted is on record; the constitution which they obtained; the proceedings where they pleaded. Wake up, and attend: to an enemy of Christ, an apostate, an adversary of Christians, a slave of demons, with such words did he, he, that Pontius of yours, make supplication. Go now, and say to us: *What have you to do with the kings of this world?* so that, deaf, you may read to deaf peoples what you are unwilling to hear along with them: *You see the straw in your brother's eye, you do not see the beam in your own eye*[^1].

[^1]: Matt 7:3.
