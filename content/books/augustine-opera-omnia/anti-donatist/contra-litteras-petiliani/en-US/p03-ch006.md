# A reply to this assertion.

**6.** 13. Augustine replied: No man, even if he is not guilty by his own crimes, makes another innocent, because he is not God: otherwise, if the innocence of the baptized is expected to come from the innocence of the one baptizing, each person will be the more innocent the more innocent the one he has found to baptize him; and he himself will be the less innocent the less innocent is the one by whom he is baptized. And if perhaps the one who baptizes holds a hatred against someone, this too will be imputed to the one who is baptized. Why, then, does the wretch run to baptism?

That his own sins may be forgiven him, or that others' may be laid on him? Or, like a merchant's ship, does he set down some things and take up others? But by a good tree and its good fruit, and a bad tree and its bad fruit, we are accustomed to understand people, and their works, as is consequently shown in the words which you too cited: *A good man brings forth good things from the good treasure of his heart; and an evil man brings forth evil things from the evil treasure of his heart*[^1]. But when a man preaches the word of God, or ministers the sacrament of God, he does not, if he is evil, preach or minister from what is his own: rather, he will be counted among those of whom it was said: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do*[^2]: for they say the things that are God's, but do the things that are their own. For if it is as you say, that is, that the baptized themselves are reckoned the fruits of those who baptize them, you announce a great evil for Africa, if as many Optatuses have sprung up as Optatus baptized.

[^1]: Matt 12:35
[^2]: Matt 23:3
