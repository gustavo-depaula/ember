# The crimes of others do not make us guilty, neither us nor you.

**23.** 53. Or do you say: *Even if I am a sacrilegious man, I ought not to be killed by you*? It is one question about the atrocity of my deed, which you never truthfully demonstrate; another about the baptism of your blood, of which you lyingly boast. For I neither kill you, nor do you prove yourself to have been killed by anyone: nor, even if you proved it, does it pertain to me, whoever may have killed you—whether he acted justly according to a power lawfully given him by the Lord, or, like the chaff of the Lord's harvest, committed a crime out of some evil desire:

just as there does not pertain to you the man who in recent times, with an intolerable power and even with soldiers escorting him, not because he feared anyone, but that he might be feared by all, oppressed widows, overturned orphans, gave away the patrimonies of others, separated the marriages of others, procured the goods of the innocent to be sold, and divided the prices of what was sold with the owners lamenting.

I might seem to be saying these things on my own, if the one of whom I speak were not recognized though his name is unspoken. And if these things are true; just as these do not pertain to you, so neither does whatever you say pertain to us, even if you spoke the truth.

But if false rumor has lied about that just and innocent colleague of yours; in no way is even rumor to be believed, which has been sown about the innocent as though about betrayers of the books, or as though about killers of men.

To this is added that I recall a man who lived with you, whose anniversaries you frequented with so great a celebration, to whom you joined the kiss of peace amid the sacraments, into whose hands you placed the Eucharist, to whom in turn, as he gave it, you stretched out your hands, whose ears, amid such great groanings of Africa, you were afraid to offend with a free voice: a man who, because someone of you, even obliquely and most wittily, touched upon him, so as to say to him that he had God for his Companion, was extolled with enormous praise.

But you charge against us those men with whom we have not lived, whose faces we have not seen, in whose age we were either boys or perhaps not yet born. What is this so great injustice and perversity, to wish to lay on us the burdens of men unknown to us, when you are unwilling to bear those of your friends? The divine Scripture cries out: *You saw a thief, and you ran along with him*[^1]. If the man whom you saw did not contaminate you, why do you charge against me one whom I could not see? Or do you say: I did not run along with him, because his deeds displeased me? But surely you approached the altar with him.

Come now, that you may defend yourself, distinguish these things, and say that it is one thing to run together into sin, as the two elders ran together in lying in wait for the chastity of Susanna; another to receive the sacrament of the Lord with a thief, as the apostles received even that first supper with Judas. I favor the defense: but why do you not attend to how much more easily, by this defense of yours, the nations and the ends of the earth, throughout which the inheritance of Christ is spread, are absolved?

For if you could see a thief, and, with the thief seen, share the sacraments, yet not share the sin: how much less could the most remote nations, even if you spoke the truth and showed it, have misdeeds in common with African betrayers or persecutors, although they held the sacraments in common with them? Or do you say: I saw that bishop, but that thief I did not see.

Say what you wish, I favor this defense too, and by it the whole world is absolved from your accusations. For if it was permitted to you to be ignorant of the life of a man you knew, why is the whole world not allowed to be ignorant of men unknown to it? Unless perhaps it is permitted to the Donatists not to know what they are unwilling to know, but not permitted to the nations not to know what they cannot know.

...just as Optatus did not make you a thief.

**23.** 54. Or do you say: Theft is one thing, betrayal or persecution another? I grant that it is another; nor need I now labor to show what the difference is. Attend to the short cut. If that man did not make you a thief, because theft does not please you: who can make those men betrayers or murderers to whom either betrayal or murder is displeasing? First, then, confess that you are whatever evil Optatus was, whom you knew: and only so charge against me whatever evil those men were whom I did not know.

Do not say to me: But those are great things, these small. For first you must confess those small things about yourself—not that I too may confess, but that I may at least allow you to say whatever great things about me. Did Optatus, whom you knew, make you a thief, because he was your colleague, or did he not?

Answer one of two things. If you say: He did not; I ask why he did not: because he himself was not a thief? or because you do not know? or because it displeased you? If because he himself was not: then much more ought we not to believe that those men of whom you accused us were such as you say. For if about Optatus one must not believe what Christians and pagans and Jews, and finally both our people and yours, say; how much less must one believe what you say about anyone?

If because you do not know: all the nations answer you: Much more do we not know whatever you charge against us about those men. If because it displeased you: they answer you with the same voice: Although you have never proved it, nevertheless such things displease us. But if you say: Behold, that Optatus whom I knew made me a thief, because he was my colleague, and I used to approach the altar with him while he was committing these things; but I do not care, because the sin is slight: yet those men made you a betrayer and a murderer to him.

I answer that I do not grant that I too have been made a betrayer and murderer by the sins of others, just because you have confessed that you have been made a thief by another's sin: for you have been made a thief not by our judgment, but by your own mouth.

For we say that each one bears his own burden, as the Apostle attests[^2]; but you, not because you committed theft or consented to theft, but because you considered that what another did pertained to you, of your own accord put your shoulders under the burden of Optatus. For as the Apostle says, when he was speaking about foods: *I know and am confident in the Lord Jesus that nothing is common through him, but to him who considers anything to be common, to him it is common*[^3]; by the same rule it can be said that the sins of others do not pertain to those to whom they are displeasing; but if anyone considers that they pertain to him, they do pertain to him.

Therefore you do not hold us to be betrayers or murderers, even if you prove something of the sort about those who share the sacraments with us: but you, even if whatever Optatus did displeases you, we nevertheless hold to be a thief—not by our calumny, but by your own sentence. And lest you think this slight; read the Apostle saying: *Nor shall thieves possess the kingdom of God*[^4].

But those who shall not possess the kingdom of God will certainly not be at the right hand, among those to whom it will be said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^5]. If they will not be there, where will they be, except at the left? Among those, then, to whom it will be said: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^6].

In vain, then, do you make yourself secure, thinking slight a sin which separates from the kingdom of God and sends into the eternal fire. How much better will you flee to a true confession, and say: Each one of us will bear his own load, and the final winnowing fan will separate the chaff from the wheat[^7]?

**23.** 55. But of course you fear lest it be said to you at once: Why, then, while you try to lay others' burdens on others, did you dare, before the final winnowing fan, to separate yourselves from the Lord's harvest of the whole world? And so you, to whom the deeds of your own people are displeasing, while you take care lest the schism which you all made be charged against you, entangle yourselves even in sins which you did not commit: and while the eloquent Petilian fears lest I be allowed to say that I am not such as he thinks Caecilian was, he himself is compelled to say that he is such as he knows Optatus was.

Or are you not such as all Africa cries out that he was? Then neither are we such as those men whom you charge against us—men whom either your error suspects, or your fury defames, or the truth proves: much less are the Lord's grain throughout all the nations, who have not even heard the name of these men. There is, therefore, no cause why you should perish by so great a crime of separation and the sacrilege of schism. And yet, if you suffer anything by the divine judgment for this so great impiety, you say that you are even baptized in your own blood: so that it is not enough that, being divided, you are not pricked to remorse, unless, being punished, you even glory in it.

**24.** 56. Petilian said: *But you persist in the same: 'He who has once been washed has no need except to wash his feet'*[^8]. *That is once which has an author, that is once which the truth confirms.*

**24.** 57. Augustine replied: Baptism in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit has Christ as its author[^9], not any man whatever: and Christ is the truth[^10], not any man whatever.

**25.** 58. Petilian said: *For since, being guilty, you commit false things, I do not administer baptism twice, which you yourself do not administer even once.*

**25.** 59. Augustine replied: You do not prove us guilty; and if a guilty man baptizes with a false baptism, then all who are baptized by those who among you are not only manifestly but even secretly guilty do not have true baptism. For if he who gives baptism gives a thing of God; since he is already guilty before God, how does he give a thing of God, if a guilty man does not give true baptism? For you so expect that he be guilty even to you, as though what he is going to give were yours.
