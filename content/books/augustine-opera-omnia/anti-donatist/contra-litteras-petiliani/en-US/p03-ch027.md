# This warning does not pertain to heretics or schismatics.

**19.** 43. Augustine replied: To flee from city to city before the face of persecution was commanded or permitted not to heretics or schismatics, which you are, but to the preachers of the Gospel, which you resist. And, that we may easily prove it: you are now certainly in your cities, and no one persecutes you. You ought, then, to come forth, and render the account of your separation. For it is not the case that, just as the weakness of the flesh is excused when it yields to the violence of persecution, so too the truth ought to yield to falsehood.

Accordingly, if you suffer persecution, why do you not leave the cities in which you are, that you may fulfill what you recall from the Gospel? But if you do not suffer persecution, why are you unwilling to answer us? Or if you fear, perhaps, that when you have answered you will suffer persecution; how then do you imitate those preachers to whom it was said: *Behold, I send you as sheep in the midst of wolves*[^1]? To whom likewise it was said: *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^2]? And how do you not act against the command of the apostle Peter, who says: *Always ready to give an answer to everyone who asks you a reason for your faith and hope*[^3]?

Finally, why are you, through most violent mobs, wicked even of your own accord toward the Catholic churches, wherever you can, as the matter itself shows by innumerable examples? But you say that you defend your own places, and resist with clubs and slaughter, by whatever means you can. Why have you not there heard the voice of the Lord, saying: *But I say to you, do not resist evil*[^4]? Or if it can come about that sometimes the violent are rightly resisted by bodily force, and yet the command we hear from the Lord is not thereby violated, *But I say to you, do not resist evil*; why can this too not come about, that, through ordained and lawful powers, the godly should expel the ungodly, and the just the unjust, from seats which are unlawfully usurped or held to the injury of God? For the false prophets did not suffer persecution from Elijah in the same way that Elijah himself suffered it from a most wicked king[^5]. Or, because the Lord was scourged by persecutors, are those therefore to be compared to his sufferings whom he himself scourged and drove out of the temple.

It remains, then, that you confess that nothing else need be inquired into, except whether you separated yourselves justly or impiously from the communion of the whole world. For if it is found that you did this impiously, do not wonder if there are not lacking ministers of God by whom you are scourged: because you suffer persecution not from us, but, as it is written, from your own deeds[^6].

[^1]: Matt 10:16
[^2]: Matt 10:28
[^3]: 1 Pet 3:15
[^4]: Matt 5:39
[^5]: See 1 Kgs 18
[^6]: See Wis 11:20
