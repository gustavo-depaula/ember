# The truth of baptism does not depend on the truth of the priest.

**30.** 69. Augustine replied: You say this as though we were now inquiring who is a true priest, and not what is true baptism. For that anyone may be a true priest, he must be clothed not with the sacrament alone, but with righteousness as well, as it is written: *Let your priests be clothed with righteousness*[^1]. But one who is a priest by the sacrament alone, as was the high priest Caiaphas, the persecutor of the one and most true priest; although he himself is not truthful, nevertheless what he gives is true, if he gives not his own but God's: as it was said of Caiaphas himself: *But this he said not of himself, but, being high priest, he prophesied*[^2].

And yet, to use also that comparison which you yourself set down, if you should hear from someone, even a profane man, a prayer of a priest formed according to the words and mysteries of the Gospel; can you say to him: It is not true, although he himself is not only not a true priest, but not even any priest at all? Since the apostle Paul said that a certain testimony of some Cretan prophet or other, who was not counted among the prophets of God, was true; for he says: *One of themselves, their own prophet, said, The Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, lazy bellies: this testimony is true*[^3]. If, then, the Apostle, by the testimony of some foreigner or other, because he found it true, even himself attested it; why, when we have found in anyone whatever what is Christ's, and is true even if the one in whom it is found is perverse and deceitful, do we not distinguish the fault which the man has, and the truth which he has not as his own but as God's; and say: This sacrament is true; as that man said: *This testimony is true*?

Do we therefore say: This man too is truthful; because we say: This sacrament is true? Just as the Apostle, did he therefore count that prophet among the prophets of God, because he confirmed that what he found true in him was true? Likewise the same Apostle, when he was at Athens among the altars of demons, noticed a certain altar on which was written: *To the unknown God.* And he took up this testimony for building them up in Christ, so that he recalled it in his discourse, and added: *Whom you, not knowing, worship, him we announce to you*[^4]. Was it that, because he found that altar either among the altars of idols or set up by sacrilegious men, he therefore condemned or spurned what was true on it; or that, on account of the truth which he had read on it, he persuaded that the sacrileges of the pagans too were to be followed?

But consequently, when he wished to commend to their knowledge as well—as he judged fitting—the very Lord, unknown to them but known to himself, he said among other things: *And indeed not placed far from each one of us; for in him we live, and move, and are, as also certain of your own have said*[^5]. Was it that here too, because he found among sacrilegious men testimonies of the truth, he either approved them on account of these things, or condemned these things on account of them? But you must always err, as long as on account of the vices of men you violate the sacraments of God, or suppose that we, on account of the sacraments of God, which we are unwilling to violate in you, must take up also the sacrilege of your schism.

[^1]: Ps 132:9 (131:9)
[^2]: John 11:51
[^3]: Titus 1:12-13
[^4]: Acts 17:23
[^5]: Acts 17:27-28
