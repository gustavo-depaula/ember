# The Donatists do not share in the inheritance of Christ.

**8.** 20. Among the many words of your invective, you said: *If you burned the testament of a dead man with flames, would you not be punished as a forger? What, then, will become of you, who have burned the most holy law of God the judge?* In saying this you did not attend to what surely ought to move you: how it could come about that we burned the testament, and yet stand fast in that inheritance which is written down in that testament; whereas it is a marvel that you preserved the testament and lost the inheritance. Is it not written in that testament: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for your possession*[^1]?

Share in this inheritance, and charge against me about the testament whatever you wish. For what madness is it, to have refused to hand the testament over to the flames, so that you may quarrel against the words of the testator?

But we, since we have in our hands the ecclesiastical and municipal records, in which we read that those who ordained another bishop against Caecilian were rather the betrayers of the divine books, do not, however, insult you, do not inveigh against you, nor bewail in your hands the ashes of the holy pages, nor set the blazing torments of the Maccabees against the sacrilege of your swelling, saying: You would rather hand over your own limbs to the fires than the oracles of God. For we do not wish to be empty, so as to stir up an idle din for you over the misdeeds of others, which you either do not know or disapprove.

But that we see you separated from the communion of the whole world (which is a crime both very great, and manifest, and belonging to all of you)—if I should wish to magnify it, time would fail me sooner than words. And if you should wish to defend this, you will charge against the whole world things which, if they are to be charged, warn you whence you may the more be accused; if they are not to be charged, you are not defended.

Why, then, are you puffed up against me about betrayal, which is neither mine nor yours, if that pact holds, that we not charge against one another the deeds of others; but if it does not hold, it is yours rather than mine? And yet, even with that pact holding, I think I most justly say that he is to be judged the associate of the one who betrayed Christ, who, together with the whole world, has not given himself over to Christ. *Therefore,* says the Apostle, *you are Abraham's seed, heirs according to the promise*[^2]. And again he says: *Heirs indeed of God, and joint heirs with Christ*[^3]. And he shows that the same seed of Abraham pertains to all the nations from that which was said to Abraham: *In your seed all the nations shall be blessed*[^4]. Therefore I think that what I ask is just: that we attend a little to the testament of God, which has long since been opened, and that whomever we do not find a joint heir of the betrayed one, him we judge the heir of the betrayer; let him belong to the seller of Christ, who denies that Christ is the buyer of the world.

Indeed, when after the resurrection he showed himself to the disciples, and offered his limbs to be touched by those who doubted, he said this to them: *Thus it is written, and thus it behooved Christ to suffer, and to rise on the third day, and that penance and the remission of sins should be preached in his name among all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^5].

Behold the inheritance from which you estrange yourselves: behold the heir whom you resist. Would he then really spare Christ walking on earth, who contradicts him seated in heaven? Do you not yet understand that whatever you charge against us, you charge against his word? The Christian world is promised, and is believed; it is fulfilled, and is contradicted.

Consider, I beseech you, what you ought to have suffered for so great an impiety; and yet if you have suffered anything, I do not know, I have not seen, I have not done it; you today, who do not suffer the force of my persecution, render me the account of your separation. But again and often you are going to say things which, if you do not prove them, pertain to no one: but if you do prove them, do not pertain to me.

**9.** 21. Petilian said: *Hedged about, then, by these crimes, you cannot be a true bishop.*

**9.** 22. Augustine replied: By what crimes? What have you taught? What have you shown? And even if you had shown the crimes of some people or other, what is this to the seed of Abraham, in which all the nations are blessed[^6]?

[^2]: Gal 3:29
[^3]: Rom 8:17
[^4]: Gen 22:18; see Gal 3:8
[^5]: Luke 24:46-47
