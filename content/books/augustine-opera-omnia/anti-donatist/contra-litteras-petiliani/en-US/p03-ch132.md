# Outside the Church baptism conferred in the name of the Trinity is valid.

**80.** 178. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: Why do you not say that you will bring forward many things more freely from your own invention, which are not found in the Scriptures? For if you wish to bring forward testimonies from the holy Scriptures, will you bring forward what you have not found there? But how many your falsehoods are is in your own power. For where is it written what you have set down, or when was that ever suggested to the Lord, or that answer given by the Lord? *Many lay on hands in your name, and are not with us,* no disciple ever said to the Son of God; hence neither could that be answered by him: *Let them be; if they are not against you, they are for you.* But there is something similar, which we really do read in the Gospel, suggested to the Lord about a certain man who was casting out demons in his name, and did not follow him with the disciples, where the Lord says: *Do not forbid him: he who is not against you is for you*[^1]. But this has nothing at all to do with demonstrating sects which the Lord might seem to have spared. But if some resemblance of the saying deceived you, it is not a lie, it is human error.

But if you wished to cast clouds of falsehood over those ignorant of the holy Scriptures, may you be pricked and confounded and corrected. There is, nevertheless, something for us to treat about this very man about whom it was suggested to the Lord. For just as then, apart from the communion of the disciples, the holiness of the name of Christ was nonetheless of great avail; so apart from the communion of the Church the holiness of the sacrament is of avail. For baptism is consecrated only in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.

But who would be so utterly insane as to say that even apart from the communion of the Church the name of the Son is of avail, but that of the Father and of the Holy Spirit is not? Or that it is of avail in healing a man, but not of avail in consecrating baptism? But plainly, apart from the communion of the Church and the most holy bond of unity and the most surpassing gift of love, neither the one from whom a demon is cast out, nor the one who is baptized, attains eternal life; just as neither do those who through the communion of the sacraments are seen within, and through the iniquity of their conduct are understood to be without. But that Christ even bodily persecuted those whom he drove from the temple by scourging, we have already said above.

**81.** 179. PETILIAN SAID: *But the holy apostle Paul says this: In whatever way, he says, Christ is proclaimed*[^2].

[^1]: Luke 9:50.
[^2]: Phil 1:18.
