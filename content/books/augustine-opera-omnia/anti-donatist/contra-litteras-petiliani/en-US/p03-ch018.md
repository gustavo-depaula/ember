# Augustine: it is the Donatists.

**14.** 32. Augustine replied: If I should say that these things were said about men such as you are, you will reply: Prove it. For what? Have you proved it? Or if you think it is proved because it is said, there is no need to repeat the same things. Read those very words back to yourself, from us against you.

Behold, I too have proved it, if this is to prove. And yet learn what it is to prove. For I do not seek documents from outside in order to prove you vipers.

Behold, this very thing is viperous: not to have in the mouth the firmness of truth, but the venom of cursing, as it is written: *The venom of asps is under their lips.* And because this can be said anywhere by anyone against anyone, as though it were asked, Whose? he immediately added: *Whose mouth is full of cursing and bitterness*[^1]. When, therefore, you say such things against men scattered throughout the whole world, and whom you altogether do not know, many of whom have not even heard the name of Caecilian or of Donatus—men who, not having heard you, reply in silence: Whatever you say does not pertain to us, we have not seen it, we have not done it, we are utterly ignorant of what you are talking about—you who wish for nothing else than to say what you altogether cannot prove, what else is your mouth than full of cursing and bitterness?

Now see whether you can show that you are not vipers, unless you show that all the Christians of all the nations are betrayers and murderers, and are not Christians. Nay rather, even if you can know and show the lives and deeds of individual men spread throughout the whole world: nevertheless, before you do that, when you rashly hurl these things about, your mouth is viperous, your mouth is full of cursing and bitterness.

Show now, if you can, what prophet, what wise man, what scribe we have killed and crucified and scourged in our synagogues. Consider how much labor must be expended—and you will in no way prove that Donatus and Marculus were prophets, or wise men, or scribes, because they were not. And even if you could, what would you do to show that men were killed by us whom not even we ourselves know? How much less the whole world, which you curse with your venomous mouth?

Or whence can you show that we have a mind like that of the killers of those whom you cannot even show to have been killed by anyone at all? Consider all these things, see whether you can prove any of them, whether of the world or against the world: yet because you do not cease to curse it, you show to be true in yourselves the things which you falsely hurl about concerning it.

[^1]: Ps 14:3 (13:3)
