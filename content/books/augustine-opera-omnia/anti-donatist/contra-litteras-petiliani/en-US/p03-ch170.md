# Augustine refuted Petilian's most acute dilemma.

**96.** 221. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: O most acute dilemma, or rather most inept loquacity! Is it not the custom to set forth that one of two things be chosen as the answer, where both cannot be chosen? For if you set before me to choose one of two things to say—whether we are innocent or guilty, or again, of two others, whether you are guilty or not guilty—I could choose only one thing to say; but now you set before me these two, whether we are innocent, or whether you are guilty, and you want me to choose one of these two to answer. But I refuse to say one; because I say both, both that we are innocent, and that you are guilty. We say that we are innocent of your false and slanderous accusations, whoever in the Catholic communion can say with a clear conscience that we have neither handed over the holy books, nor consented to the worship of idols, nor killed anyone; or whatever other evil you are accustomed to charge; and that those who perhaps—which indeed you have not proved even of them—did these things, have shut the kingdom of heaven not against us, but against themselves; because each one of us bears his own burden[^1].

See, you have one. And that all of you are guilty and wicked, not some by the crimes of others which are committed among you by some and reproved by some, but by the crime of schism, from which most monstrous sacrilege none of you can say he is free, so long as he does not communicate with the unity of all the nations, unless he is forced to say that, concerning the Church which is spread through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem, Christ lied[^2]. See, you have the other. See, I have rendered to you the two, of which you wished us to say one. You surely ought to have considered that both could be said by us; and at least, if you wished this, to ask us to say one, when you saw that we could say both.

[^1]: See Gal 6:5.
[^2]: See Luke 24:47.
