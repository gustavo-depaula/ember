# The true sense of the testimony of Jeremiah 15:18.

**102.** 235. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: One who, ignorant of the Scriptures, hears these things, and does not believe that you are either so erring that you do not know what you are saying, or so deceiving that the one whom you have deceived does not know what he is saying, supposes that the prophet Jeremiah, when he wished to be baptized, took care lest he be baptized by impious men, and so said these things. For to what does it bear that, before you set down this testimony, you said: *At least imitate the prophets, who feared that their sacred souls be deceived by a false baptism*?

As though in Jeremiah's times anyone was washed by the sacrament of baptism, except that the Pharisees dipped themselves, in those washings which the Lord reproved, at almost every moment, and their couches and cups and dishes, as is read in the Gospel[^1]. How, then, could Jeremiah, as though desiring baptism and avoiding being baptized by the impious, have said these things? He said this, then, when he was complaining about a faithless people, by whose most evil conduct he was afflicted, not mingling himself with their deeds; who nevertheless did not bodily separate himself from that people, nor seek other sacraments than those which that people, according to the order of the Law, suited to that time, received.

This people, then, living wickedly, he called a wound, by which the heart of the just man was grievously struck, whether he said this of himself, or figured in himself what he saw would come. For thus he says: *Lord, remember me, and visit me, and make me innocent of those who persecute me—not in long-suffering; know how I have received reproach for your sake from those who despise your words. Consume them: and your word will be to me a delight and the joy of my heart, for your name has been invoked upon me, Lord almighty. I did not sit in the council of those making sport, but I feared before the face of your hand; I sat alone, for I am filled with bitterness. Why do those who grieve me prevail against me? My wound is strong; whence shall I be healed? It has become to me as deceitful water, which has no faith*[^2]. In all these things it is seen what the prophet wished to be understood, but it is seen by those who do not wish to pervert, for their own perverse cause, what they read.

For Jeremiah called his wound, which had become to him deceitful water that has no faith; but he wished those to be understood as his wound, who grieved him by living wickedly. Hence the Apostle too says: *Fightings without, fears within*[^3]; and again: *Who is weak, and I am not weak? And who is made to stumble, and I am not set on fire?*[^4] And because he despaired of their being able to be corrected, therefore he said: *Whence shall I be healed?* as though he would always have grief, so long as they were such, among whom he was compelled to live. But that a people is wont to be signified by the name of water is made plain in the Apocalypse, where we learn that the many waters are many peoples, not by our conjecture, but by the interpretation unfolded there[^5].

Do not, then, by a wrong understanding, or rather error, blaspheme the sacrament of baptism, even if it be in a most lost man; for neither in Simon the liar was the baptism he had received deceitful water[^6], nor do your so many liars give deceitful water, when they baptize in the name of the Trinity. For they do not begin to be liars when, betrayed and convicted, they confess their crimes; but rather they were liars then, when, being adulterers and wicked, they pretended that they were chaste and innocent.

**103.** 236. PETILIAN SAID: *David also said: The oil of the sinner shall not anoint my head*[^7]*. Whom, then, does he assert to be the sinner? Me, who suffer your crimes, or you, who persecute the innocent?*
