# God left men free even in drawing them.

**84.** 186. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: If I propose to you the question, how God the Father draws to the Son men whom he has left in free choice, perhaps you would resolve it with difficulty. For how does he draw, if he leaves it so that each may choose what he wills? And yet both are true: but few are able to penetrate this by understanding. As, then, it can come about that those whom he has left in free choice the Father nonetheless draws to the Son; so it can come about that the things which are admonished by the coercions of laws do not take away free choice.

For whatever hard and troublesome thing a man suffers, he is admonished to consider why he suffers it; so that, if he perceives that he suffers for the sake of righteousness, he may choose that very good of enduring such things for the sake of righteousness; but if he sees that it is for iniquity that he suffers, considering that he labors and is tormented most fruitlessly, he may change his will for the better, and at the same time be rid both of fruitless trouble and of the iniquity itself, which would surely have done him much graver and more ruinous harm.

And you, when kings establish something against you, believe that you are being admonished to consider why you suffer these things; if for the sake of righteousness, then indeed they are your persecutors; but you are blessed, who having suffered persecution for the sake of righteousness, shall possess the kingdom of heaven[^1];

but if for the iniquity of your schism, what are they but your correctors; while you, like the rest who are guilty of various crimes and pay the penalties by the laws, are surely unhappy both in this age and in the age to come? No one, then, takes free choice from you; but attend diligently to what you would rather choose: whether, corrected, to live in peace, or, persevering in malice, to suffer true torments under the name of a false martyrdom.

But he addresses you as though you were suffering things unworthy, for the sake of righteousness. As though you were suffering anything worthy of your iniquity, when you do so many unworthy things, and reign in such great impunity; so frenzied that by the Praises of God you terrify more than a war trumpet; so slanderous that you charge even the voluntary self-destructions of your own people to our persecutions.

[^1]: See Matt 5:10; 1 Pet 2:20.
