# You are the false witnesses, because you resist the truth, and you have shown this in the trial against the Maximianists.

**58.** 132. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: If what you hold is not ours, neither was what you received from the Maximianists yours. But if it was yours because they made a sacrilegious schism by not communicating with the party of Donatus, then consider what places you hold, and whose inheritance you do not communicate with; and reflect on what answer you can give, not to the kings of this world, but to Christ the King. Of him it was said: *He shall have dominion from sea to sea, and from the river to the ends of the earth*[^1]. From what river indeed, except where he was baptized, and where the dove descended upon him, a great sign of love and unity? But you do not communicate with this unity, and you still hold the places of unity; and you stir up resentment against us over the kings of this world, when through the orders of proconsuls you have driven your own schismatics out of the places of the party of Donatus.

These words are not flying through empty air: people are living, cities bear witness, the proconsular and municipal records are read aloud. Let the slanderous voice at last fall silent, charging the kings of this world against the whole earth, through whose proconsuls the divided one could not spare the divided one. When, therefore, we say that you hold what is ours, we are not shown to be giving false witness, unless you show that we are not in the Church of Christ; which you indeed never cease to say, but will never be able to show. Rather, when you say this, you no longer charge us, but Christ himself, with the crime of false witness. For we are in the Church that was foretold by his own testimony, and to which he bore witness through his own witnesses, saying: *You shall be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the whole earth*[^2]*.*

But we show you to be false witnesses not only from this, that you resist this truth, but also in the very trial in which you clashed with the schism of Maximian. For if you were acting according to the law of Christ, how much more fittingly do Christian emperors establish something according to it, if pagan proconsuls can judge according to it? But if you thought that even the laws of the earthly empire were to be invoked in aid, we do not find fault with that. Paul did this when, against those who wronged him, he testified that he was a Roman citizen[^3].

But I ask by what earthly laws it was decreed that the Maximianists should be driven from their places. You will find none at all; rather, it was evidently by the laws that were enacted against heretics, and among them against you yourselves as well, that you wished to expel them: you prevailed against the weak as though you were the more powerful. Hence, because they could do nothing, they call themselves innocent, like the wolf under the lion. Yet by laws enacted against you, you would surely not use them against others except through false witness.

For if those laws are truthful, then you too yield the places that you hold; but if they are false, why did you use them to shut others out? What if they are both truthful, and yet you could not have expelled others by them except deceitfully? For in order that the judges might serve them, they wished to expel heretics; and so they ought to have expelled you first; but you said that you were Catholics, so that the laws by which you were pressing others might spare you.

You may see what you seem to be in your own eyes; yet by those laws you are not Catholics. Why then have you either falsely deceived those laws as truthful by false witness, or usurped them as false to oppress others?

[^1]: Ps 72:8 (71:8).
[^2]: Acts 1:8.
[^3]: See Acts 22:25.
