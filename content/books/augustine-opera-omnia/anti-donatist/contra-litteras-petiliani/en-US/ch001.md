# Revisions II

## Chapter 25 (52): Answer to the Writings of Petilian, in three books

Before I finished the books *On the Trinity* and the books *The Literal Meaning of Genesis*, there suddenly arose the occasion of answering the writings of the Donatist Petilian, which he had written against the Catholic Church; and I could not put this off.

And I wrote three volumes on this matter. In the first I answered, with as much speed and truth as I could, the first part of the letter that he had written to his own people, since it had not all come into our hands but only its first small part. That letter too is addressed to our people, but it is counted among the books because the other two books deal with the same matter.

For afterward we found it in full, and I answered it with as much care as I had Faustus the Manichee, namely setting down his words piece by piece under his own name and, under mine, my reply to each one. But what I had written first, before we found the whole of it, reached Petilian, and in anger he tried to reply, saying against me rather whatever he pleased, while utterly failing in the matter at issue.

Although this could very easily have been noticed by comparing the writings of us both, still, for the sake of slower readers, I took care to show it by my reply. Thus a third book was added to this same work of ours.

This work begins in the first book thus: *You know that we have often wished;*

and in the second thus: *To the first parts of Petilian's letter;*

and in the third thus: *I have read your writings, Petilian.*
