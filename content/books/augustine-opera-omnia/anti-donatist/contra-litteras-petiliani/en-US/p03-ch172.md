# The Donatists, by making supplication to Julian, placed their hope in man.

**97.** 224. AUGUSTINE REPLIED: We do not hope in man; but, as far as we can, we admonish men to hope in the Lord; nor do we hope in princes; but, as far as we can, we admonish princes to hope in the Lord, and if we ask anything of princes for the benefit of the Church, we do not nonetheless hope in them. For neither did the Apostle hope in that tribune as in a prince, from whom he obtained that armed escorts be given him; nor in those armed men as in men, by whose multitude, surrounded, he escaped the ambushes of the lost[^1].

But neither do we reproach you yourselves, because you asked the emperor that the basilicas be given back to you, as though you had hoped in Julian the prince; but we reproach you because you despaired of the testimony of Christ, from whose unity you separated those very basilicas. For you received them, at the command of the enemy of Christ, in order that in them you might despise the commands of Christ, while what Julian established seems to you valid and true, when he says: *This too, to those who make supplication—Rogatianus, Pontius, Cassianus, and the rest of the bishops, and also the clergy—is added to fill the measure: that, the things which were wrongly done against them without a rescript being abolished, all be recalled to their ancient state;* but what Christ established seems invalid and false, when he says: *You shall be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and to the whole earth*[^2].

We beseech you, be corrected: return to this most evident unity of the whole world, so that not by the words of the apostate Julian, but by the words of Christ the Savior, all may be recalled to their ancient state. Have mercy on your soul. We no longer compare Constantine and Julian, to show how unequal they are.

We do not say: If you did not hope in man and in a prince, when you said to a pagan and apostate emperor, a man, that *with him justice alone had a place,* with which prayers and rescript, as is there written, as the records of the pleading testify, the party of Donatus universally made use; then much less ought we to be charged by you, as though we hope in man and in a prince, if we have asked anything of Constantine or of the rest of the Christian emperors with no sacrilegious flattery; or if they themselves, without our asking, mindful of the account they will render to the Lord, under whose words they tremble when they hear what you yourself mentioned: *And now, kings, understand*[^3]*,* and so on, and many other things, of their own accord establish anything for the unity of the Catholic Church.

But I am silent about Constantine: we set Christ and Julian against you; I say too little, God and man, the Son of God and a child of hell, the Savior of our souls and the slayer of his own soul. Why, in possessing the basilicas, do you hold the rescript of Julian, and, in embracing the peace of the Church, do you not hold the Gospel of Christ? We too cry out: *Let the things which were wrongly done all be recalled to their ancient state.* More ancient is the Gospel of Christ than the rescript of Julian: more ancient is the unity of Christ than the party of Donatus: more ancient are the prayers of the Church to the Lord for the unity of Christ, than those of Rogatianus and Pontius and Cassianus to Julian for the party of Donatus. Is it wrongly done, when kings forbid division; and not wrongly done, when bishops divide unity? Is it wrongly done, when, to defend the Church, kings serve the testimony of Christ; and not wrongly done, when, to deny the Church, bishops contradict the testimony of Christ? We beseech, then, that not against the Gospel, but according to the Gospel, even the words of Julian himself, to whom you so made supplication, be heard, and that the things which were wrongly done all be recalled to their ancient state.

[^1]: See Acts 23:12-33.
[^2]: Acts 1:8.
[^3]: Ps 2:10.
