# Revisions II

## Chapter 17 (44): Answer to the Letter of Parmenian, in three books

In the three books *Answer to the Letter of Parmenian*, the bishop of the Donatists at Carthage and the successor of Donatus, a great question is treated and resolved: whether, in unity and in the common sharing of the same sacraments, the wicked contaminate the good; and it is discussed how they do not contaminate them, on behalf of the Church spread throughout the whole world, against which they made a schism by their slander.

In the third book of these works[^1], where I discussed how we should understand what the Apostle says, *Drive out the wicked person from among you*[^2]*,* that what I said—*that each person should drive out the wickedness from himself*—should not be understood in this way, but rather in this way: that a wicked man should be removed from among the good men, which is done through ecclesiastical discipline,

the Greek language indicates clearly enough, where it is written without ambiguity, so that it is understood to mean *this wicked person*, not: this evil thing—although I also replied to Parmenian according to that latter understanding.

This work begins thus: *Indeed I have elsewhere said much against the Donatists.*

[^1]: Contra ep. Parmen. 3.1.2
[^2]: 1 Cor 5:13
