## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-27: Address. Augustine writes to the deacon Deogratias in the singular: rendered with "tu" throughout, as `augustine/enchiridion/pt-BR` does for Laurentius. The two model catecheses (¶24–¶49, ¶52–¶55) speak to a single catechumen ("brother"), also "tu". Where the catechesis briefly includes the catechumen among others (¶55 "unite with each other") the plural "vós" verb is used.
- 2026-09-27: Pronouns for God lowercase ("ele", "dele"), per `augustine/creed/pt-BR`; divine nouns and titles keep the source's capitals (Cabeça, Verbo, Mediador, Juiz, Rei). "Church"/"church" follows the source's capitalization.
- 2026-09-27: Scripture quotations follow the source's wording clause by clause. The source gives no chapter-and-verse references, so none were added. Notable: ¶48 "in your name we have eaten and drunken" (the source's conflation of Mt 7:22 and Lk 13:26) kept as "em teu nome comemos e bebemos"; ¶37 1 Tm 2:1-2 keeps the source's "adorations" and "charity" ("súplicas, adorações, intercessões e ações de graças… com toda a piedade e caridade"); ¶4 1 Cor 13:12 "em enigma e por espelho".
- 2026-09-27: Footnotes. The New Advent source carries no footnotes; nothing to drop. The translator's parenthetical supplements "(of the history)", "(true)", "(peculiarly)" etc. are kept as parentheses, as `augustine/creed/pt-BR` keeps the source's brackets.
- 2026-09-27: Headings. `## Introductory Notice` → `## Nota introdutória`; `## Chapter N.— Title` → `## Capítulo N — Título` (sentence case), matching `augustine/faith-and-the-creed/pt-BR`. The editor's introductory notice is translated, not dropped, as that sibling does.
- 2026-09-27: Paragraph numbers written `**N.**` per `.claude/rules/books.md`. The en-US file uses `N.` list markers; the numbering is a gapless 1–55, so it renders correctly, but it should be converted in a later pass.
- 2026-09-27: The *Retractations* quotation in the intro notice and its source passage in ¶30 are rendered with identical wording ("nem o anjo que, em companhia de outros espíritos que eram seus satélites, abandonou por soberba a obediência a Deus… Deus sabe dispor das almas que o abandonam"), since the notice quotes Augustine correcting that exact sentence ("souls" → "spirits").
- 2026-09-27: Source slips mirrored or silently read correctly: ¶9 "gound" (read "ground"), ¶12 "catchetical", ¶15 "neverless" (read "nevertheless"), ¶30 "trangress". ¶27's quoted speech of God lacks its closing quotation mark in the source; closed after "comigo". ¶53 "of Him it was determined" (capital H referring to Abraham) rendered "dele" with no reverential signal. The en-US file was not edited.
- 2026-09-27: ¶49 and ¶55 open and close with double quotation marks in the source although no quotation opens the catechesis; mirrored. Nested quotations inside the model catecheses use single curly quotes where the source uses single quotes.
- 2026-09-27: ¶3 "*Iratus sum*" kept in Latin italics (Augustine's point is that the Latin phrase is understood only by Latins).

## Expressions & Idioms

- ¶8 "on hearing may believe, on believing may hope, on hoping may love" → "ouvindo, creia; crendo, espere; esperando, ame" — keeps the gradation.
- ¶8 "here is great misery, proud man! But there is greater mercy, a humble God!" → "aqui está uma grande miséria: o homem soberbo! Mas ali está uma misericórdia maior: um Deus humilde!"
- ¶7 "the former type of love is of misery, the latter of mercy" → "da miséria… da misericórdia", keeping the *miseria/misericordia* echo.
- ¶13 "good speech, but never gracious speech" → "boa fala, mas nunca fala graciosa", to keep the link to "a graça da palavra".
- ¶23 charity "travails with some" → "sofre as dores do parto com uns".

## Review Round 1 (2026-09-27)

Mechanical audit (paragraph numbering, headings, markdown, book.json fields) came back clean: 55 gapless `**N.**` markers, 27/27 matching headings, no stray `N. ` list markers, no blockquotes in either language (consistent), no frontmatter, even italics-marker count. `book.json` pt-BR title/author verified against `augustine/creed` and `faith-and-the-creed` precedent as the Key Terms table claims.

A clause-by-clause bilingual read of all 55 paragraphs (5 batches) found one item worth recording, otherwise clean:

- ¶47: en-US "we shall enjoy that Trinity now by sight, wherein at present we walk by faith" vs. pt-BR "gozaremos então pela visão daquela Trindade em que agora caminhamos pela fé." A reviewer flagged this as swapping the two time-markers ("now" onto the sight-clause, "at present" onto the faith-clause) versus the English's placement. Rejected: the English's "now" on the future-tense "we shall enjoy" is the classic then/now (sight/faith, 1 Cor 13:12) contrast expressed awkwardly — "now" there means "at that point, directly," not literally the chronological present, while "at present" plainly means our current earthly walk of faith. Pairing pt-BR's future-tense "gozaremos" with "então" (a future/narrative-point marker) and its present-tense "caminhamos" with "agora" (the literal present) preserves that same then-sight/now-faith relationship without producing the jarring "gozaremos agora" (future verb + literal "now") a transplanted rendering would create. Not a defect.
