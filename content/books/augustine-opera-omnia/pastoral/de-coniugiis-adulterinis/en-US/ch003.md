# Book Two: New objections of Pollentius are to be refuted.

### Why we add a second book.

**1. 1.** To the things you had written to me, devout brother Pollentius, I had already written back no small volume concerning those who, while their spouses are living, are joined to others. When this had come to the notice of Your Charity, you added some things to your little book, desiring that I respond to these as well.

But while I was arranging to do so, by adding I too to mine, so that there might be one book of my response as well, suddenly that which I had finished earlier was published, the brothers demanding it and not knowing that something was still to be added. Hence it came about that I was compelled to respond in a separate second little work to the things you added. But the things you added are not joined to the end of your little work; rather, where it seemed fit, they were inserted into its body.

## The opinion of Pollentius, that adultery is to be reckoned as death (2, 2–13, 13)

### The opinion of Pollentius: a wife departing from a husband committing fornication is not found to be a violator of the precept.

**2. 2.** The first of these is that to which I think I ought to respond: that in these words of the Apostle, where he says, *But to the rest I say, not the Lord, that the wife not depart from her husband; but if she does depart, let her remain unmarried or else be reconciled to her husband*[^1],

you do not think that *if she does depart* was said in such a way that she is understood to have departed from a husband committing fornication, the one cause for which it is lawful to depart;

but rather you suppose that she has departed from a chaste husband, and that she was therefore commanded to remain unmarried, so that she might be reconciled to him, if he had refused to be continent, lest, not being reconciled, she drive her husband to commit fornication, that is, to take another woman while she herself lives.

But if she has departed from a husband committing fornication, you think that it is not commanded her to remain unmarried, but that she does this if she wishes to be continent, not so as to be found a violator of the precept if she marries.

A rule which seems to you to be observed by the husband as well: that he not dismiss his wife, except for the cause of fornication; but if he has dismissed her, let him remain without marriage, so that he may be reconciled to a chaste wife, unless perhaps she has chosen continence,

lest, fleeing reconciliation with a chaste wife, he himself force her to commit adultery, if, not containing himself while she lives, he marries another;

but that, if he has been separated from a wife committing fornication, he is then held by no precept to be continent, and does not at all commit adultery if he marries another while she is living,

since you think that what the same Apostle says—*A woman is bound as long as her husband lives; but if her husband dies, she is freed; let her marry whom she wishes*[^2]—is to be understood in such a way that, if a husband has committed fornication, he is to be reckoned as dead, and the wife as dead, and therefore it is permitted to either of them, as though after death so after fornication, to be joined to another.

### . . . that fornication is to be held as death.

**3. 3.** Having considered these views of yours, I ask you whether whoever marries a woman who has ceased to be bound to her husband is to be held an adulterer. This, I think, does not seem so to you.

For a woman *while her husband lives will be called an adulteress if she is with another man*[^3], because *a woman is bound as long as her husband lives*[^4]; but if this bond did not exist for her while her husband lives, she would marry another without any charge of adultery.

Accordingly, if she is bound as long as her husband lives, she is to be said to be loosed from this bond in no way except when her husband is dead.

Further, if this bond between husband and wife is loosed by the death of either of them, and fornication too is to be held as death, as you say, then without doubt the woman will be loosed from this when she has committed fornication.

For she could not be said to be bound to her husband when her husband had been loosed from her. And on this account, after she has, by committing fornication, ceased to be bound to her husband, whoever marries her will not be an adulterer.

**4. 3.** And see how absurd it is, that he should not be an adulterer for this reason, that he has married an adulteress.

Indeed, what is more monstrous, neither will the woman herself be an adulteress, since she will not be another man's wife to the later husband, but his own.

For, the former marriage bond being loosed by adultery, whomever she now marries, having no spouse, she will be not an adulteress with an adulterer, but rather a wife with a husband. How, then, will it be true: *A woman is bound as long as her husband lives*[^5]? Behold, her husband lives, because he has neither departed from the body nor committed fornication, which you wish to reckon as death; and yet the woman is no longer bound to him.

Do you not notice how this is against the Apostle, who says, *A woman is bound as long as her husband lives*? Or will you perhaps say, "He lives indeed, but he is no longer her husband, since he ceased to be such when she, by adultery, loosed the marriage bond"? How then *will she, while her husband lives, be called an adulteress if she is with another man*[^6]? Since that man is no longer her husband, the marriage bond having already been loosed by the woman's adultery.

For while what husband lives, except her own, will she be called an adulteress if she is with another man? But if that man has now ceased to be her husband, then surely she will not, while her husband lives, be called an adulteress if she is with another man, but, having no husband, she will, by marrying, be with her own husband.

Whoever holds this, do you not see how he holds against the Apostle? This indeed you do not yourself hold, but it follows from the things you do hold. Change, then, the premises, if you wish to avoid the conclusions, and do not say that, in this place, a dead husband or a dead wife ought to be understood to mean also one committing fornication.

### A woman is loosed from marriage only if her husband dies.

**4. 4.** Wherefore, according to sound doctrine, *a woman is bound as long as her husband lives*[^7]—that is, as long as he has not yet departed from the body; for *a woman is bound by law to her husband while he is living as her husband*—that is, while he is set in the body.

But if *he dies*, that is, has departed from the body, *she is freed from the law of her husband.* Therefore, *while her husband lives, she will be called an adulteress if she is with another man. But if her husband dies, she is freed from the law, so that she is not an adulteress if she is with another man*[^8]*.*

These words of the Apostle, so often repeated, so often pressed home, are true, are living, are sound, are plain. A woman begins to be the wife of no later husband unless she has ceased to be the wife of the former. But she will cease to be the wife of the former if her husband dies, not if he commits fornication.

And so a spouse is lawfully dismissed on account of the cause of fornication; but the bond of modesty remains, on account of which he becomes guilty of adultery who marries a woman dismissed even on account of this cause of fornication.

### The Apostle speaks of that death by which one departs from the body.

**5. 5.** For just as, the sacrament of regeneration remaining in him, one guilty of some crime is excommunicated, yet does not lack that sacrament, even if he is never reconciled to God;

so, the bond of the marriage covenant remaining in her, a wife is dismissed on account of the cause of fornication, yet she will not lack that bond, even if she is never reconciled to her husband; but she will lack it if her husband dies.

But the excommunicated guilty man will for this reason never lack the sacrament of regeneration, even unreconciled, since God never dies.

It remains, therefore, that, if we wish to think according to the Apostle, we not say that an adulterous husband is to be reckoned as dead, and that therefore his wife is permitted to marry another.

For although adultery is a death—not of the body, but, what is worse, of the soul—the Apostle was nevertheless not speaking of that death when he said, *But if her husband dies, let her marry whom she wishes*[^9]; but of that death alone by which one departs from the body.

For if by a spouse's adultery the marriage bond is loosed, that perversity follows which I have shown must be avoided: that the woman too is loosed from this bond by her unchastity;

and if she is loosed, she will be free from the law of her husband; and therefore—what is said most foolishly—she will not be an adulteress if she is with another man, because by adultery she has been freed from the former husband.

But if this is so far astray from the truth that no sense—I do not say Christian, but human—admits it, then surely *a woman is bound as long as her husband lives*[^10]—which, to say it more plainly, means as long as her husband is in the body.

By an equal rule, then, the husband too is bound as long as his wife is in the body. Hence, if he wishes to dismiss an adulteress, let him not marry another, lest what he blames in her, he himself commit.

Likewise also a woman, if she dismisses an adulterer, let her not couple another to herself; for she is bound as long as her husband lives, and is not freed from the law of her husband except when he is dead, so that she is not an adulteress if she is with another man.

### After adultery let a spouse be reconciled to a spouse.

**6. 5.** But that which seems hard to you—that after adultery a spouse be reconciled to a spouse—will not be hard, if faith is present.

For why do we still reckon as adulterers those whom we believe to have been healed either by being washed in baptism or by penance? These crimes, which in the old law of God were cleansed by no sacrifices, are without doubt cleansed by the blood of the New Testament;

and therefore at that time it was in every way forbidden to take back a wife defiled by another man; although David, as a prefigurer of the New Testament, took back without hesitation the daughter of Saul, whom her father, having separated her from him, had given to another[^11].

But now, after Christ said to the adulteress, *Neither will I condemn you; go, and now sin no more*[^12];

who would not understand that a husband ought to forgive what he sees the Lord of them both has forgiven, and that he ought no longer to call her an adulteress whose crime, now that she is penitent, he believes to have been blotted out by divine mercy?

### The Lord forgave the adulteress.

**7. 6.** But this, of course, the sense of unbelievers shudders at, so that some of little faith, or rather enemies of the true faith—fearing, I believe, lest impunity in sinning be given to their wives—removed from their manuscripts that which the Lord did concerning his indulgence toward the adulteress,

as though he who said *now sin no more* had granted permission to sin, or as though the woman ought not to have been healed by God the physician through the remission of that sin, lest the insane take offense.

For those to whom that deed of the Lord is displeasing are not themselves chaste, nor does chastity make them severe; but rather they are of that number of men to whom the Lord says: *Let him among you who is without sin first cast a stone at her*[^13]*.*

Except that those men, terrified by conscience, had withdrawn and ceased to tempt Christ and to pursue the adulteress[^14];

but these men, themselves sick, reproach the physician, and, themselves adulterers, rage against adulteresses:

to whom, if it were said—not what those men heard, *Let him who is without sin* (for who is without sin?), but, "Let him who is without this sin first cast a stone at her"—then perhaps those who were indignant that they had not killed the adulteress would consider with how great a mercy of God they were spared, so that adulterers might live.

### Between husband and wife let an equal rule of chastity be kept.

**8. 7.** But when we say these things to them, not only do they wish to detract nothing from severity, but they are moreover angry at the truth, and they speak and answer: "But we are men. Or will the dignity of our sex endure this injury, that, when we commit anything with women other than our wives, we should be put on a level with women in paying the penalties?"

As though they ought not for this very reason the more to bridle unlawful desires manfully, because they are men? As though they ought not for this very reason the more to offer themselves to their wives as an example of this virtue, because they are men? As though they ought not for this very reason the less to be overcome by lust, because they are men? As though they ought not for this very reason the less to serve the wantoning flesh, because they are men?

And yet they are indignant if they hear that adulterous husbands pay penalties like those of adulterous women, when they ought to be punished so much the more grievously, the more it belongs to them both to conquer by virtue and to govern women by example.

I am speaking, to be sure, to Christians, who faithfully hear, *The husband is the head of the wife*[^15], where they recognize that they ought to be the leaders, and the women the companions.

And therefore the husband must beware of going, in his living, by that way along which he fears his wife may follow by imitating.

But these men, who are displeased that an equal rule of chastity be kept between husband and wife, and who rather choose, especially in this matter, to be subject to the laws of the world than to those of Christ—since the laws of the courts do not seem to bind men by the same bonds of chastity as women—

let them read what the emperor Antoninus, by no means a Christian, ordained on this matter, where a husband is not allowed to accuse his wife of the crime of adultery if he has not, by his own conduct, offered her an example of chastity, in such a way that both would be condemned, if the trial itself should convict both alike of unchastity.

For these are the words of the aforesaid emperor, which are read in the Gregorian Code. *Assuredly*, he says, *my letter shall in no part prejudge the case. For even if the fault lay with you, so that the marriage was dissolved and, according to the Julian law, your wife Eupasia married, she will not on that account, by my rescript, be condemned for adultery, unless it be established that it was committed.*

*They will have before their eyes to inquire whether, while you were living chastely, you were to her too an author of cultivating good morals. For it seems to me most unfair that a husband should exact from his wife a chastity which he does not himself exhibit. This matter can both condemn the husband and—not on account of a setting-off of mutual crime—either settle the matter between the two or remove the ground of the action*[^16].

If these things are to be observed for the honor of the earthly city, how much more chaste are those whom the heavenly fatherland and the fellowship of the angels seek? Since these things are so, is the unchastity of men therefore the less, and not rather the greater and the worse, the more there is in them a proud and licentious boasting?

Let men, then, not shudder that Christ forgave the adulteress; but let them rather recognize their own peril as well, and, laboring under a like disease, flee to the same Savior with suppliant devotion,

and let them confess that what they read was done in her is necessary for themselves as well; let them receive the medicine of their adulteries, let them now cease to commit adultery, let them praise in themselves the patience of God, let them do penance, let them take indulgence, let them change their verdict concerning the punishment of women and their own impunity.

### If reconciliation is refused, let continence be undertaken.

**9. 8.** When these things have been considered and treated, if the common condition, the common evil, the common peril, the common wound, the common salvation be faithfully and humbly weighed, the reconciliation of spouses will be neither shameful nor difficult, even after adulteries have been perpetrated and purged, where, through the keys of the kingdom of heaven, the remission of sins is not doubted to take place—not so that, after a divorce from her husband, an adulteress be called back, but so that, after fellowship with Christ, she be not called an adulteress.

But behold, let it not be done; no one compels it, because perhaps some law of this age forbids it, after the manner of the earthly city, where the abolition of crimes through the Holy Blood has not been thought of.

Let continence, then, be undertaken, which no law forbids; let one not go into other adulteries.

And what is it to us, if an adulteress, even cleansed by divine mercy, is not reconciled to her husband, provided that, the adulterers not being reconciled, there be not made other supposed marriages, which are proved to be adulteries?

For *a woman is bound as long as her husband lives*[^17]. Therefore, consequently, the husband too is bound as long as his wife lives. This binding makes it so that they cannot be joined to others without an adulterous coupling.

Hence it is necessary that, out of two spouses, four adulterers be made, if both she marries another and he marries an adulteress.

For although he commits adultery more wickedly who, his wife having been dismissed not for the cause of fornication, marries another—which kind of adultery Matthew recalled—yet not only does he himself commit adultery,

but, as it is in Mark, *Whoever dismisses his wife and marries another commits adultery against her; and if a wife dismisses her husband and marries another, she commits adultery*[^18]; and, as it is in Luke, *Everyone who dismisses his wife and marries another commits adultery; and he who marries a woman dismissed by her husband commits adultery*[^19]*.* Concerning which testimonies I have already discoursed sufficiently in the earlier book.

### If we admit the complaints of the incontinent, very many adulteries will have to be permitted.

**10. 9.** But you answer me: *To live continently belongs to few; and therefore those who have dismissed spouses committing fornication, since they cannot be reconciled, see themselves so endangered that they pronounce the Law of Christ not human, but savage*.

O brother, so far as the incontinent are concerned, they can have many complaints by which, as you say, they may pronounce the Law of Christ savage, not human.

And yet on their account we ought not to pervert or change the Gospel of Christ. For you would be moved only by the complaint of those who dismiss spouses on the intervening cause of fornication, if they were not allowed to marry others, since continence belongs to few, and they ought to be exhorted to it by praise, not compelled by Law.

And so, if, an adulteress having been dismissed, another is not married, the incontinence of men will have, as you think, a just complaint. But consider how many are the cases where, if we were willing to admit the complaints of the incontinent, it would be necessary for us to permit adulteries to be committed.

What then? For what if a spouse is held by some long-lasting and incurable disease of the body, by which intercourse is impeded?

What if captivity or some force separates them, in such a way that the husband knows that his wife, of whom the enjoyment is denied him, is living—do you judge that the murmurs of the incontinent are to be admitted and adulteries to be permitted?

What of that very matter about which the Lord was asked, and answered that it ought not to be done, but that on account of *the hardness of their heart*[^20] Moses permitted a bill of divorce to be given and a spouse to be dismissed for any cause whatever?

Does not the Law of Christ displease the incontinent, who wish to cast off, by interposing a divorce, quarrelsome, injurious, domineering, disdainful wives, most difficult about rendering the marriage debt, and to marry others[^21]?

Now, then, because the incontinence of these men has shuddered at the Law of Christ, is the Law of Christ to be changed at their discretion?

### A woman is bound as long as her husband lives, whether he is continent or an adulterer.

**10. 10.** But now, if a wife leaves her husband, or a husband his wife, not for the cause of fornication but rather for that of continence, and the one to whom the divorce is given on this account is incontinent, I ask whether he or she will not be an adulterer or an adulteress, if joined to another?

If it is said that he "will not" be, the Lord is contradicted, whose words are these: *It was said: 'Whoever dismisses his wife, let him give her a bill of divorce.' But I say to you that everyone who dismisses his wife, except for the cause of fornication, makes her commit adultery; and he who marries a dismissed woman commits adultery*[^22].

Behold, she was dismissed, she did not dismiss; and because continence belongs to few, she yielded to incontinence and married; and yet an adulterer married an adulteress.

Both are guilty, both are to be condemned: both she who married while her husband lived, and he who married her whose husband lives.

Do we here call the Law of Christ inhuman, by which she is established guilty of so great a crime and punished, whom her husband dismissed with no preceding fornication on her part and, because continence belongs to few, by dismissing her compelled to marry?

Why do we not here say that he is to be held as dead who, by wickedly dismissing her, first broke the marriage bond? For by what reasoning will you say that he broke the marriage bond who, although he is an adulterer, did not dismiss his wife, and that he did not break it who dismissed even a chaste wife?

But I say that in both this bond remains, since *a woman is bound as long as her husband lives*[^23], whether he is continent or an adulterer, and therefore that she commits adultery who, having been dismissed, marries, and that he commits adultery who marries a dismissed woman, whether she was dismissed by an adulterer or by a continent man, since *a woman is bound as long as her husband lives.*

But now we are disputing about the complaints of the incontinent. For what seems more just than the complaint of this woman who says, "I was dismissed, I did not dismiss; and since continence belongs to few, I did not contain myself; lest I commit fornication, I married; and I am called an adulteress because I married"?

Shall we, on account of her seemingly just complaint, judge that the divine Law is to be changed, so that we do not judge her an adulteress? Far be it. But you will answer that she ought not to have been dismissed, because no cause of fornication had preceded. You say what is true; for the Lord expressed the sin of her husband, where he says, *He who dismisses his wife, except for the cause of fornication, makes her commit adultery*[^24]*.* But did she therefore not sin by marrying afterward, because he first sinned by dismissing? What, then, does it profit her that the incontinent woman complains of the Law of Christ, except that, murmuring, she is punished?

### To lie with an adulteress, even for the sake of procreating children, is not permitted.

**11. 11.** Now let us also consider those things which, inserting them in another place, you added and were unwilling to respond to. There you are moved and pity the man who is compelled to lie with an adulteress, if not by incontinence, at least by the necessity of procreating children, if it is not permitted him so to dismiss her as to marry another while she lives.

Hence you would rightly be moved, if it were not adultery to marry another while the adulterous wife is living.

But if it is adultery, as the things that have been argued have shown, what is pleaded under the pretext of procreating children? For the license of disgraceful acts is not on that account to be permitted. Or is it as much to be avoided to die without offspring as it is to be chosen to live in the world to come? Which adulterers will not be allowed to do, who must, after the first death, be condemned to the eternity of the second death.

For this pleading of procreating children compels even non-adulterous, indeed most chaste women, if perchance they are barren, to be dismissed and others to be married: which, I think, does not please you.

### If incontinence does not excuse adulteries, so much the less does the procreation of children.

**12. 11.** Wherefore, if adulteries are not to be excused for the cause of incontinence, how much less are they excused for the cause of procreating children!

**12. 12.** For it was that weakness, namely incontinence, that the Apostle wished to be relieved by the honorableness of marriage. For he did not say, "If he does not have children, let him marry," but, *If he does not contain himself, let him marry*[^25]*.*

It is by the propagation of children that what is conceded to incontinence in marrying is set off. For surely incontinence is a fault, whereas marriage is not a fault; and therefore by this a good comes about, so that the former is a venial evil. Since, then, marriage was instituted for the sake of begetting, it was for that cause that the patriarchs did it, who were united to women only by the office of begetting, but not unlawfully.

For there was then a certain necessity of propagating, which now there is not, since there is *a time for embracing*, as it is written—which surely was then—*and a time for refraining from embracing*[^26], which is now.

Speaking of which time, the Apostle says: *For the rest, brothers, the time is short; it remains that even those who have wives be as though not having them*[^27]*.*

Hence now it is said most certainly: *Let him who can receive it, receive it*[^28]; but let him who *does not contain himself, marry*[^29]*.*

Then, therefore, even continence descended into marriage for the sake of propagating children, by way of office; but now the marriage bond relieves the fault of incontinence, so that by those who do not contain themselves the propagation of children may come about, not through the baseness of debaucheries, but through the honorableness of marriages.

Why, then, did the Apostle not say, "If he does not have children, let him marry"? Because, namely, in this time of refraining from embracing, it is not necessary to propagate children. And why did he say, *If he does not contain himself, let him marry*?

Surely for this reason, lest through incontinence he be compelled to commit adultery. If, then, he contains himself, let him neither marry nor beget.

But if he does not contain himself, let him marry lawfully, lest he beget basely or, more basely, by lying together not beget. Although this last thing I have mentioned, some do even while lawfully married.

For unlawfully and basely is intercourse had even with a lawful wife, where the conception of offspring is avoided.

This Onan, the son of Judah, was doing, and God killed him on account of it[^30]. And so the propagation of children is itself the first and natural and lawful cause of marriage.

And on this account those who are joined on account of incontinence ought not so to temper their evil that they do away with the good of marriage, that is, the propagation of children.

For the Apostle was speaking of the incontinent where he says: *I wish therefore that the younger marry, bear children, be mothers of families, give the adversary no occasion for reviling. For some have already turned back after Satan*[^31]*.*

And so, when he said, *I wish the younger to marry*, he was surely admonishing this for the sake of propping up the ruin of continence.

But lest perhaps only the weakness of carnal desire be thought of by them, which alone was to be served by the work of marriage, while the good of marriage was either despised or neglected, he immediately subjoined: *bear children, be mothers of families.*

But those who choose to be continent surely choose something better than the good of marriage, that is, the begetting of children.

Hence, if continence is chosen so that something better than the good of marriage may be laid hold of, how much more is it to be kept so that adultery may be avoided! For when the Apostle had said, *But if he does not contain himself, let him marry; for it is better*, he says, *to marry than to burn*[^32], he did not say, "It is better to commit adultery than to burn."

### Let those who fear to be reconciled keep continence.

**13. 13.** There is nothing, then, to which we may exhort those who fear to be reconciled to adulterous spouses healed by repenting, except to the keeping of continence, *since a woman is bound as long as her husband lives*[^33]—whether he is an adulterer or chaste—and she commits adultery if she marries another,

and a husband is bound as long as his wife lives—whether she is an adulteress or chaste—and he commits adultery if he marries another.

For since this binding is not loosed even if a spouse is separated by divorce from a chaste spouse, much less is it loosed if, not separated, the spouse commits adultery.

And on this account nothing loosens it except the death of the spouse—not of one falling into adultery, but of one departing from the body. Wherefore, if a woman has withdrawn from an adulterous husband and does not wish to be reconciled to him, let her remain unmarried;

and if a husband has dismissed an adulterous wife and does not wish to take her back, even after penance, let him keep continence—if not from the will to choose the better good, at least from the necessity of avoiding the pernicious evil.

To this I would exhort, even if a wife were in an incurable and long-lasting infirmity, even if she were somewhere separated in body, where the husband could not approach her;

finally, to this I would exhort, even if a woman, wishing to live continently—although against discipline, because not by consent—nevertheless, chaste herself, dismissed a chaste husband.

For I think that no Christian would object to being an adulterer who, while his wife is long sick, or long absent, or desiring to live continently, has been united to another woman.

Thus, then, even with an adulteress dismissed, a man is an adulterer with another, since it is not this man or that, but *everyone who dismisses his wife and marries another commits adultery*[^34]*.*

Wherefore, if the life of the saints, free from the marriage bond, is the less sought after, let the punishment of adulterers be shuddered at, and let desire be bridled at least by fear, if continence is not chosen out of love.

For if, where there is fear, toil is at work, where there was toil, there will also be love. For we must not trust in our own strength; but prayer must be joined to our efforts, so that he who deters from evils may fill us with good things.

### The most absurd objections that follow from the opinion of Pollentius are refuted (14, 14–17, 18)

### A new objection of Pollentius is refuted.

**14. 14.** Let us also respond to that point where you think that husbands are driven to punish adulteresses without any mercy—wishing them to die, if, while they live, it is not permitted to marry others. Wishing to magnify this cruelty, you said: *"*It does not seem to me, most loving father, that this is a divine sense, where kindness and devotion are excluded[^35]."

You say this as though husbands ought to spare adulterous wives for the reason that it is permitted them to marry others—so that, if it is not permitted, they should not spare them, in order that it may be permitted.

On the contrary, they ought to show mercy to sinful women for this reason: that they too may obtain mercy for their own sins. And much more must this be done by those who, having dismissed adulterous wives, desire to live continently.

For they ought to be the more merciful, the more they wish to be holier, so that they may be divinely helped in keeping chastity in themselves, while they do not themselves humanly avenge the chastity violated by their wives.

And above all that word of the Lord must be recalled to their memory: *Let him who is without sin first cast a stone at her*[^36]*.* Not, "Let him who is without this sin"—since we are speaking of chaste husbands—but, *let him who is without sin*; and if they say that they are such, they deceive themselves, and *the truth* is *not* in them[^37]*.*

Further, if they do not deceive themselves and the truth is in them, there will not be in them a bloody severity. For knowing that they are not without sin, they forgive, so that it may be forgiven them, and neither kindness nor devotion is excluded from them.

For these are rather excluded, if it is the license of lust, not the care of devotion, that obtains for them pardon for their spouses' sins; that is, if they spare for this reason, that it is permitted them to marry others, and not rather for this reason, that they wish the Lord to spare them too.

### Let husbands spare the blood of adulterous wives.

**14. 15.** How much better, then, and more honorable, and finally more worthy of the Christian profession, that they spare the blood of adulterous wives—we say to them what is written:

*Forgive the injustice of your neighbor, and then, when you pray, your sins will be loosed. A man keeps anger against a man, and does he seek healing from the Lord? He has no mercy on a man like himself, and does he entreat concerning his own sins? He himself, being flesh, keeps wrath, and who will make propitiation for his sins?*[^38]

and from the Gospel: *Forgive, and you will be forgiven*[^39], so that we may be able to say, *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^40]; and from the Apostle: *Repaying no one evil for evil*[^41]; and any such things there are in the Holy Scriptures, by which the human mind, when it is roused to take vengeance, is, because it is Christian, made gentle.

### It is not permitted to a Christian man to kill an adulterous wife, nor to marry another.

**15. 15.** How much better, I say, do we say these things than that we should say: "Only dismiss these adulteresses, and do not seek their blood; whatever grief you have from their disgraceful acts, others whom you marry will console you; for you would with reason wish to remove these from the number of the living, if their life were a hindrance to your marrying others.

But as it is, since, even while these live, it is permitted you to provide yourselves other marriages, why do you so greatly wish to kill them?" If we say these things, do you not notice how far our persuading is from the Christian character?

we who both say falsely that something is permitted them which is not permitted—that is, while these wives live, to be coupled to others—and, if they spare them on that account, they spare not out of devotion, but on account of the free power of other marriages.

Finally, I ask you whether it is permitted to a Christian husband, either according to the old Law of God or by the Roman laws, to kill an adulteress? If it is permitted, it is better that he refrain from both—that is, both from the lawful punishment of her sinning and from the unlawful marriage while she lives.

But if he persists in choosing one or the other, it is better for him to do what is permitted—that the adulteress be punished—than what is not permitted—that, while she lives, he commit adultery.

But if—what is more truly said—it is not permitted to a Christian man to kill an adulterous wife, but only to dismiss her, who is so insane as to say to him, "Do what is not permitted, so that what is not permitted may be permitted you"?

For since both are unlawful according to the Law of Christ—whether to kill an adulteress or, while she lives, to marry another—one must abstain from both; one must not commit the unlawful in place of the unlawful. For if he is going to do what is not permitted, let him rather commit adultery and not commit homicide, so that, while his wife lives, he marries another and does not shed human blood. But if both are wicked, he ought not to perpetrate the one in place of the other, but to avoid both.

### What follows from the objection of Pollentius.

**16. 16.** Here I see what can be said by the incontinent: "namely, that he who dismisses an adulteress and permits her to live, if he marries another, is, as long as that former wife lives, a perpetual adulterer, and does not do fruitful penance, since he does not withdraw from the disgraceful act;

nor, if he is a catechumen, is he admitted to baptism, since he is not changed from that which hinders;

nor can he, as a penitent, be reconciled while persevering in the same wickedness; but if, by accusing the adulteress, he has killed her, this sin, since it is past and one does not remain in it, and,

if it was done by a catechumen, is washed away by baptism; and, if by one baptized, is healed by penance and reconciliation."

But are we, on that account, going to say that adultery is not adultery, which is without doubt committed if, while an adulterous spouse lives, another is married?

But, this kind of adultery aside, you surely do not doubt that it is adultery if someone marries the wife of a living man, dismissed by her husband through a bill of divorce without any fornication of the woman.

What then? When such a man has seen that he is neither admitted to baptism, if he is a catechumen, nor does penance to any profit, if it was as a baptized man that he did this, by not correcting and abandoning what he did,

if he should wish and be able to kill the man whose wife he married, so that this crime might either be washed away by baptism or loosed by repenting,

and so that that adultery too might not remain—the woman being emptied of the law of her husband after her husband's death—but, as to the past deed that was done, satisfaction be made through penance, or it be blotted out by regeneration;

is the Law of Christ on that account to be accused, as though it had compelled homicide to be done, when it says that to marry a woman divorced without the crime of fornication is adultery?

### From the opinion of Pollentius much graver things can be said than he himself said.

**16. 17.** For here, if we attend to what we are saying even a little, much graver things can be said than you yourself said.

For while you do not wish there to be adulteries, if others are married once adulterers have been dismissed, you found this:

*that if we call these adulteries, husbands will be compelled to kill adulteresses, by whose life they are hindered from marrying others.*

And, to magnify this, you said: *It does not seem to me, most loving father, that this is a divine sense, where kindness and devotion are excluded.*

If, then, someone, unwilling to believe that it is adultery when a woman divorced by her husband without the crime of fornication is married by another, should find this too against you,

that by this reasoning men are urged to perpetrate homicides and to attack the husbands of those women whom they have married after such divorce—whether by such snares as they can, or by calumnies, or to accuse them of some true crimes and kill them,

so that, those husbands being dead, there might be marriages with the women who, while their husbands lived, had been adulteries,

will he not, magnifying it, say to you: *It does not seem to me, most loving father, that this is a divine sense, where not only kindness and devotion are excluded, but even an enormous malice and impiety is aroused*[^42]?

Since it is much lighter and more tolerable that husbands kill adulteresses than that adulterers kill husbands.

Does it please you that, on account of this most empty prejudice, we abandon the defense of the Lord's sentence, or even accuse it besides, saying that it ought not to be judged adultery, even if a woman divorced by her husband apart from the cause of fornication is joined to another, lest she be compelled to kill the husband by whom she was dismissed, while she desires to convert the adultery into marriage by the death of her former husband?

I know that it does not please you that, on account of this most empty prejudice, the Law of Christ, when it is found to be true and sound, be called hard and inhuman.

And so it ought not to seem to you that adultery is to be denied, when, while an adulterous wife is living, another is married,

because the husband can by this be compelled to kill the adulteress, while he desires that it be permitted him, once she is dead, to marry another, if, while she lives, it is not permitted to do so.

What then? For what if the disparagers of the Christian faith should say this too—that men are compelled to kill their wives by the crimes of snares, the wives whom they cannot bear as troublesome, whether laboring under a long-lasting infirmity and unable to endure intercourse, or poor, or barren, or deformed, in the hope of marrying others who are healthy, wealthy, fertile, most beautiful,

because it is not permitted to divorce, apart from the cause of fornication, those women whom they are unwilling to endure, and to marry others, lest, bound forever by adultery, they can neither be baptized nor healed by penance—are we, on that account, lest these crimes of homicide be perpetrated, going to say that it is not adultery to couple others to oneself once wives have been divorced apart from the cause of fornication?

### Another most absurd consequence of the opinion of Pollentius.

**17. 18.** Now indeed, from the fact that you hold it not to be adultery if a husband has cast off his wife for the cause of fornication and married another, do you not think it must be guarded against, lest husbands learn to compel their wives—whom on account of countless other causes they cannot bear—to commit adultery, so that, the marriage bond having been loosed by fornication,

as you suppose, it may be permitted them to marry others, and, from the fact that they compelled those wives to commit adultery, either to be washed by baptism or healed by repenting,

since both grace and medicine will be denied them as long as they live with adulteresses, if, their former wives having been divorced apart from the cause of fornication, they marry others?

Unless perhaps someone should say that no one can make his wife commit adultery, if she is chaste; and yet the Lord says, *Everyone who dismisses his wife apart from the cause of fornication makes her commit adultery*[^43]*.*

Surely for this reason: that, although she was chaste with her husband, nevertheless, having been dismissed, she is, through incontinence, compelled to be coupled to another while the former lives, and this is to commit adultery.

But if she does not do this, nevertheless he, so far as in him lies, has compelled her to do it; and God will impute this sin to him, even if she remains chaste.

But who does not know how exceedingly rare are those women who live so chastely with their husbands that, even if they are dismissed by them, they do not seek others?

For incomparably greater is the number of women who, although they cleave chastely to their husbands, nevertheless, if they have been dismissed by their husbands, do not delay to marry.

When, therefore, men have believed the Lord saying, *Everyone who dismisses his wife apart from the cause of fornication makes her commit adultery*[^44], if they have believed you too, saying that, the wife committing fornication, it is permitted her husband to marry another, then whoever wishes, on account of any other troubles whatever, to be rid of the wife to whom he is joined, will first make her commit adultery by dismissing her without fornication,

so as then to marry another when that one has become an adulteress by marrying; and thus, freed from the former sin, by which he made her commit adultery, whether through baptism or through penance, he may seem to himself to have, without his own adultery, the woman whom, after the former adultery, he married as another, the marriage bond having, as it were, been loosed thereby.

But indeed, if he has contrived this, he will both make his wife an adulteress and will himself be an adulterer, even though by marrying another after his spouse's adultery; and it will profit him nothing that he believed you and not rather him who says, with no exception, *Everyone who leaves his wife and marries another commits adultery*[^45]*.*

## When continence is necessary (18, 19–20, 22)

### There are those who undertake continence out of necessity, and those who undertake it for a greater reward.

**18. 19.** All these things having been considered and treated, it remains that there be said to us, by those who faithfully hear them, what was said to the Lord: *If such is the case with a wife, it is not expedient to marry.*

To whom what should we too answer, except what he himself answered? *Not all receive this word, but those to whom it has been given. For there are eunuchs who were so born from their mother's womb; and there are eunuchs who were made so by men; and there are eunuchs who have made themselves eunuchs for the sake of the kingdom of heaven. Let him who can receive it, receive it*[^46]*.*

Therefore let him who can, receive what not all receive. But those can receive it to whom this is granted by the mercy of God, hidden, but not unjust.

But among all these *who have made themselves eunuchs for the sake of the kingdom of heaven*, some there are, in either sex, who have no knowledge of intercourse; others who have experienced it and turned away from it—partly indeed having experienced it unlawfully, partly lawfully.

Further, among those who have experienced it lawfully, some there are who have experienced it only lawfully, some both lawfully and unlawfully.

For there are among them those who know only their own marriages; but there are those who know other women too, and debaucheries of any kind.

But those who, after intercourse with spouses, make themselves eunuchs for the sake of the kingdom of heaven—either they lose their spouses by death, or by consent profess continence with them, or out of the necessity of divorces,

lest, by coupling themselves to others while their spouses live, they perpetrate adulteries, make *themselves eunuchs for the sake of the kingdom of heaven*—not so that they may be more glorious there, but because otherwise they cannot be there:

for those who contain themselves not out of this necessity, but out of the desire for a better good, could be there even with marital chastity preserved—although among lesser rewards, yet within.

But those who contain themselves for this reason, that they fear, while their former spouses live, to be joined to others, ought to bear a greater care for salvation than did those by whom continence was chosen as a greater reward.

For they will be there, if they will not be adulterers; but if they do not contain themselves, they will be adulterers; because, while their former spouses live, they will cleave not to spouses but to adulterers. And if they will be absent from the kingdom of heaven, where will they be, except where they will not be saved?

### With God's help, what was begun out of necessity will be made perfect by love.

**19. 20.** These, therefore, I address, so that what they ought to do if they had spouses wasting away with a long-lasting infirmity, or absent in a place inaccessible to them, or continent out of an unlawful willfulness,

this they may do, if they have had spouses foul with adulterous defilement and on this account divorcing from their fellowship: let them not seek other marriages, because they will not be marriages but adulteries.

For since the rule is equal in this bond for husband and wife, just as a wife *while her husband lives will be called an adulteress if she is with another man*[^47];

so too a husband, while his wife lives, will be called an adulterer if he is with another woman.

For although he sins more grievously who does so apart from the cause of fornication, nevertheless *everyone who dismisses his wife and marries another commits adultery*[^48]*.*

Let not the burden of continence terrify them; it will be light, if it is Christ's; it will be Christ's, if faith is present, which obtains from him who commands what he has commanded.

Let it not break them that their continence seems to be of necessity, not of will; for those too who chose it by will have made it to be of necessity, since they can no longer turn aside from it without condemnation,

and those who have been thrust into it by necessity make it to be of will, if they trust not in themselves, but in him from whom comes every good.

The former ascended to it for the sake of greater glory, that they might find something more; the latter fled to it out of care for their last salvation, lest they perish;

let both persevere, let both walk in that to which they have come, all the way to the end, let them be fervent in their zeal, let them entreat with their vows,

because the former too must think of salvation, so that they fear to fall from that which the will laid hold of, and the latter must not despair of glory, if they love to persist in that which necessity brought upon them.

For it can come about that, with God terrifying and exhorting, converting and fulfilling, the human affection is changed for the better, and so they vow to live most perseveringly without marriages and without any intercourse and without the unclean handling of lust,

so that, even if the separated marriages should, by the death of the spouses, open the way to marrying others, what lies open through what is lawful may be closed by a vow, and what was begun out of necessity may be made perfect by love.

To such persons, assuredly, that will be repaid which is repaid to those who either by equal consent vowed this with their spouses, or, bound by no marriages, chose continence for the sake of a greater good.

But if they contain themselves in such a way that, if those women die by whose life they are hindered from marrying, they intend to marry others, then surely, even if they themselves first depart from the body in such continence, it is reckoned to them only as marital chastity, on account of which they do not do what they would do if it were permitted. For to live continently with this intention is too little for receiving the rewards of that continence which is more freely chosen, but it suffices for avoiding adulteries.

### Let men be equal to women in chastity.

**20. 21.** But remember that I say these things of both sexes, yet chiefly on account of men, who think themselves superior to women for this very reason, that they may not deign to be their equals in chastity—in which they ought even to have gone before, so that the women might follow them as their own heads.

But when the Law forbids adulteries, if, under the pretext of incontinence, the excuse of carnal weakness is admitted, an occasion of perishing is opened to many under the name of a false impunity. For women too, to whom men do not wish anything of this kind to be permitted—as though it were permitted to themselves because they are men—are not without flesh.

But far be it that there should be owed to the better sex, as though to its honor, what is detracted from modesty, since just honor is owed to virtue, not to vice. On the contrary, since they demand so great a chastity from women, who surely have flesh—that, when their husbands are abroad for very long, they wish them to pass the fervor of youth uncontaminated by adulterous intercourse

(and very many pass it most chastely, and especially the Syrian women, whose husbands, occupied with the gains of trading, leave them as young girls and scarcely sometimes return as old men to old women)—by this very fact they are more evidently convicted that what they plead they cannot do is not impossible. For if the weakness of men could not do this, much less could the weaker sex of women do it.

### The example of the continence of the clergy is set forth.

**20. 22.** Hence, when we terrify those men who think manly excellence to be nothing but license to sin, lest, by clinging to adulterous marriages, they perish forever, we are accustomed to set before them also the continence of the clergy,

who for the most part are seized unwilling to take up the same burden, and, having taken it up, carry it through to its due end, with the Lord's help.

We say to them, then: "What? If you too were seized for taking up this office by the violence of the people, would you not chastely keep the office once taken up, being suddenly turned to obtaining from the Lord the strength of which you had never before thought?"

"But those men," they say, "are greatly consoled by the honor." We answer: "And let fear regulate you much more."

For if many ministers of God have taken up this burden suddenly and unexpectedly imposed, hoping to shine more illustriously in the inheritance of Christ, how much more ought you, by avoiding adulteries, to live continently, fearing not to shine less in the kingdom of God, but to burn in the fire of Gehenna?

These things and the like we say to them, as we can, who wish to marry others when their spouses have in any way departed from them or been dismissed on account of adultery, and who, when they are forbidden, set against us the weakness of the flesh.

But now this book too must be closed, and God must be asked either not to allow them to be tempted by separations of spouses, or so to allow it that the fear of their imperiled salvation may become for them the occasion of a greater or a more proven chastity.
