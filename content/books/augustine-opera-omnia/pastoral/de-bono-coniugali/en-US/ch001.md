# Revisions II

## Chapter 22 (49): The Excellence of Marriage, one book

**1.** The heresy of Jovinian, by making the merit of holy virgins equal to conjugal chastity, had such force in the city of Rome that it was said to have cast down into marriage even some consecrated women about whose chastity no suspicion had previously arisen. He pressed them especially with this argument, saying: "Are you then better than Sarah, better than Susanna or Anna?" And he would call to mind other women highly commended by the testimony of holy Scripture, women to whom they could not imagine themselves superior or even equal. In the same way he also undermined the holy celibacy of holy men by recalling and comparing it to the married patriarchs.

The holy Church that is there resisted this monster most faithfully and most forcefully. But these arguments of his lingered on in the little talks and whispers of certain people, which no one dared to urge openly. Yet even these creeping poisons had to be met, in secret, by the means the Lord granted, especially since it was being boasted that Jovinian could not be answered with praise of marriage but only with its disparagement.

For this reason I published a book whose title is *The Excellence of Marriage.* There a great question was postponed: the question of the propagation of children before human beings deserved death by sinning, since intercourse seems to be a matter of mortal bodies; but it is sufficiently explained, in my judgment, later in other writings of mine[^1].

**2.** I also said in one place: *what food is to the health of a human being, intercourse is to the health of the race, and neither is without carnal pleasure, which nevertheless, when it is regulated and brought back to its natural use under the restraint of temperance, cannot be lust*[^2]*.* This was said because lust is not the good and right use of sexual desire. For just as it is bad to use good things badly, so it is good to use bad things well. On this matter I argued more carefully elsewhere, especially against the new Pelagian heretics[^3].

What I said about Abraham, that *out of this obedience that father Abraham, who was not without a wife, was prepared to be without his only son, and to be so by killing him himself*[^4]*,* I do not fully approve. For rather he is to be believed to have believed that his son, if he had been killed, would soon be restored to him by resurrection[^5], as we read in the *Letter to the Hebrews*[^6].

This book begins thus: *Since every human being is a part of the human race.*

[^1]: De Civ. Dei 14, 21-22.
[^2]: De bono coniug. 16, 18.
[^3]: De nupt. et concup. 2, 21, 36; Contra Iul. 3, 7, 16; 5, 16, 60.
[^4]: De bono coniug. 23, 31.
[^5]: See Serm. 2, 1, 1.
[^6]: Heb 11:19.
