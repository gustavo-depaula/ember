# On the Creed, to Catechumens

**1. 1.** Receive the rule of faith, which is called the Creed. When you have received it, write it in your heart and say it to yourselves every day. Before you sleep, before you go out, fortify yourselves with your Creed. No one writes down the Creed so that it can be read; rather, let your memory be your book for rehearsing it, lest forgetfulness erase what careful attention has handed on. What you are about to hear, you are to believe; and what you have believed, you are also to recite with your tongue. For the Apostle says: *With the heart one believes unto righteousness, and with the mouth one confesses unto salvation* [^1]. This is the Creed that you will rehearse and recite. These words you have heard are scattered through the divine Scriptures, but they have been gathered from there and brought together, so that the memory of slow learners should not struggle, and everyone may be able to say and retain what he believes. Is this the first time you have heard that God is almighty? But you begin to have him as your Father when you are born through the Church your mother.

**1. 2.** From that source, then, you have already received and reflected on it, and through reflection retained it, so that you may say: *I believe in God the Father almighty* [^2]. God is almighty; and although he is almighty, he cannot die, cannot be deceived, cannot lie, and, as the Apostle says, *cannot deny himself* [^3]. How many things he cannot do, and he is almighty! Indeed, he is almighty precisely because he cannot do these things. For if he could die, he would not be almighty; if he could lie, be deceived, deceive, or act unjustly, he would not be almighty, because if these things were in him, he would not have been worthy to be almighty. Certainly our almighty Father cannot sin. He does whatever he wills: that is almightiness. He does whatever he wills in goodness, whatever he wills in justice; but he does not will anything that is done wickedly. No one resists the Almighty so as to prevent him from doing what he wills. He made heaven and earth, the sea and everything in them [^4], invisible and visible things. The invisible things include the Thrones, Dominions, Principalities, Powers, Archangels, and Angels in heaven, our fellow citizens if we live well. He made the visible things in heaven: the sun, moon, and stars. He adorned the earth with its land animals, filled the air with flying creatures, the earth with walking and crawling creatures, and the sea with swimming creatures. He filled everything with its own proper creatures. He also made man in his image and likeness in the mind, for that is where God's image is. Hence the mind itself, where God's image is, cannot be comprehended even by itself. We were made to rule the other creatures; but through sin we fell in the first man, and all came into the inheritance of death. We became lowly mortals, filled with fears and errors: this was the punishment earned by sin, and every human being is born with that deserved punishment and guilt. Therefore, as you saw today and as you know, even little children receive the exsufflation and exorcism, so that the devil's hostile power may be driven from them—the power that deceived man in order to possess human beings. It is not, then, God's creation in infants that is exorcised or breathed against, but the one under whom all who are born with sin stand; for he is the prince of sinners. Thus, because of the one who fell and sent everyone into death, one without sin was sent to lead all who believe in him to life, freeing them from sin.

**2. 3.** Therefore we believe *also in his only Son*—that is, the only Son of God the Father almighty—*our Lord*. When you hear “God's only Son,” recognize God. For God's only Son could not be other than God. He begot what he is, although he is not the one he begot. If he is the true Son, he is what the Father is; if he is not what the Father is, he is not the true Son. Look at mortal, earthly creatures: each begets what it is. A man does not beget an ox; a sheep does not beget a dog, nor a dog a sheep. Whatever begets, begets what it is. Hold firmly, steadfastly, and faithfully, then, that God the Father begot what he himself is: almighty. These mortal creatures beget through corruption. Does God beget in this way? One born mortal begets what he is; the immortal begets what he is. The corruptible begets a corruptible offspring; the incorruptible, an incorruptible offspring; the corruptible begets corruptibly, the incorruptible incorruptibly. So completely does he beget what he is that the one begets one, hence an only Son. You know that when I announced the Creed to you beforehand, I said this, and this is how you must believe: *We believe in God the Father almighty, and in Jesus Christ his only Son* [^5]. Now, when you hear “only Son,” believe that he is almighty. It is not that God the Father does what he wills and God the Son does not do what he wills. The Father and the Son have one will because they have one nature. The Son's will cannot be separated in the slightest from the Father's will. God and God, both one God; almighty and almighty, both one Almighty.

**2. 4.** We do not introduce two gods, as some do when they say, “God the Father and God the Son, but God the Father is greater and God the Son is lesser.” Both are what? Two gods? You are ashamed to say it; be ashamed to believe it. You say, “The Lord God the Father, and the Lord God the Son”; and the Son himself says: *No one can serve two masters* [^6]. Shall we belong to his household in such a way that, as in a large house with a head of the household and his son, we too say, “The greater master, the lesser master”? Turn away from such a thought. If you fashion such things in your heart, you are setting up idols within a single soul. Reject it entirely. First believe, then understand. When God grants someone quick understanding after he has believed, that is God's gift, not the achievement of human weakness. Yet if you do not understand yet, believe: the Father is the one God; Christ, God's Son, is God. Both are what? One God. And how can both be called one God? How? Are you surprised? In the *Acts of the Apostles*, it says: *The believers were* *of one soul and one heart* [^7]. There were many souls; faith had made them one. There were so many thousands of souls; they loved one another, and the many are one. They loved God in the fire of charity and came from multiplicity to a beautiful unity. If charity made so many souls one soul, what must charity be in God, where there is no difference but complete equality? If there could be such great charity on earth and among human beings that it made one soul from so many souls, could the Father and the Son, where the Father has always been inseparable from the Son and the Son from the Father, be anything but one God? Yet those souls could be called both many souls and one soul; but God, in whom there is an ineffable and supreme union, can be called one God, not two gods.

**2. 5.** The Father does what he wills; the Son does what he wills. Do not think that the Father is almighty and the Son is not almighty. That is an error: erase it from yourselves, let it not cling to your memory or be drunk into your faith, and if any of you has drunk it, let him vomit it out. The Father is almighty; the Son is almighty. If the Almighty did not beget an almighty Son, he did not beget a true son. For what are we saying, brothers, if the greater Father begot a lesser Son? What does “begot” mean? A man who is greater begets a son who is lesser: that is true. But while the one grows old, the other grows up, and by growing at least reaches his father's stature. If God's Son does not grow, since God cannot grow old either, he was born perfect. Born perfect indeed, if he does not grow and has not remained lesser, he is equal. To know that the Almighty was born of the Almighty, listen to the one who is Truth. What Truth says of himself is true. What does Truth say? What does the Son, who is Truth, say [^8]? *Whatever the Father does, the Son likewise does* [^9]. The Son is almighty, doing everything he wills. For if the Father does some things that the Son does not do, the Son spoke falsely when he said: *Whatever the Father does, the Son likewise does*. But because the Son spoke truly, believe: *Whatever the Father does, the* *Son likewise does*, and you have believed in the almighty Son. Even though you did not say that word in the Creed, you nevertheless expressed this when you believed in the only Son himself as God. Does the Father have anything that the Son does not have? The blasphemous Arian heretics say this, not I. What do I say? If the Father has anything that the Son does not have, the Son lies when he says: *Everything the Father has is mine* [^10]. There are many, indeed countless, testimonies proving that the Son, true God, is the Father's Son, that God the Father begot the true Son, God, and that the Father and the Son are one God.

**3. 6.** But let us see what this only Son of God the Father almighty did for us and suffered for us: *Born of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary* [^11]. That great God, equal to the Father, *was born* *of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary* in humility, to heal the proud. Man exalted himself and fell; God humbled himself and raised him up. What is Christ's humility? God stretched out his hand to man lying fallen. We fell; he descended. We lay on the ground; he bent down. Let us take hold and rise, so that we may not fall into punishment. This, then, is his bending down: *Born of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin Mary*. And that human birth itself is humble and exalted. Why humble? Because a human being was born from human beings. Why exalted? Because he was born of a virgin. A virgin conceived, a virgin gave birth, and after giving birth she remained a virgin.

**3. 7.** What comes next? *Suffered under Pontius Pilate*. Pontius Pilate was serving as governor and judge when Christ suffered. The judge's name marks the time when he *suffered*, *under Pontius Pilate*; when he suffered, he was *crucified and* *buried* [^12]. Who? What? For whom? Who? God's only Son, our Lord. What? *Crucified and buried*. For whom? For the ungodly and sinners. What great condescension, what great grace! *What shall I render to the Lord for all he has rendered to me?* [^13].

**3. 8.** He was born before all times, born before all ages. Born before. Before what, where there is no “before”? Do not imagine any time before Christ's birth in which he was born of the Father. I am speaking of that birth by which he is the only Son of God almighty, our Lord; I am speaking of that one first. Do not imagine a beginning in time in this birth; do not imagine any stretch of eternity when the Father existed and the Son did not. From whenever the Father is, the Son is. And what does “from whenever” mean where there is no beginning? Therefore the Father is always, without beginning; the Son is always, without beginning. “And how,” you will ask, “was he born if he has no beginning?” From the eternal, coeternal. There was never a Father without the Son, and yet the Son was begotten by the Father. Where can we find even some sort of comparison? We are among earthly things, among visible creation. Let the earth give me a comparison: it gives none. Let the element of water give me some comparison: it has none to give. Let some animal give me a comparison: it cannot either. An animal indeed begets, and both the one begetting and the one begotten are animals; but the father is earlier, and the son is born later. Let us find something of the same age, and believe in what is coeternal. If we can find a father of the same age as his son, and a son of the same age as his father, let us believe that God the Father is of the same age as his Son and God the Son is coeternal with his Father. On earth we can find something of the same age, but we cannot find something coeternal. Let us attend to what is of the same age and believe in what is coeternal. This may make you attentive. Someone says, “When can a father be found who is of the same age as his son, or a son of the same age as his father? To beget, the father comes earlier in age; to be born, the son follows in age. But how can a father here be of the same age as his son, or a son as his father?” Think of fire as the father, radiance as the son: there, we have found things of the same age. From the moment fire begins to exist, it immediately begets radiance: neither fire before radiance nor radiance after fire. And if we ask which begets which, whether fire begets radiance or radiance fire, the answer immediately comes to you through natural perception, the understanding implanted in your minds. You all cry out: fire begets radiance, not radiance fire. Here is a father beginning, here is a son simultaneously, neither earlier nor later. Here, then, is a father beginning, here is a son beginning simultaneously. If I have shown you a father beginning and a son beginning simultaneously, believe in a Father who does not begin, and with him a Son who likewise does not begin: the one eternal, the other coeternal. If you advance, you will understand: make the effort to advance. You are to be born, but you must also grow, because no one begins perfect. God's Son could be born perfect because he was born outside time, coeternal with the Father, preceding everything not in age but in eternity. He, then, was born coeternal with the Father, of whose generation the prophet said: *Who shall recount his generation?* [^14] Born of the Father outside time, he was born of a virgin in the fullness of time [^15]. Times had preceded this birth. At the fitting time, when he willed, when he knew it was right, then he was born; for he was not born unwillingly. None of us is born because he wills it, and none of us dies when he wills it. He was born when he willed and died when he willed. He was born as he willed, of a virgin, and died as he willed, on a cross. He did everything he willed, because he was man in such a way that God lay hidden: God assuming, man assumed, one Christ, God and man.

**3. 9.** What shall I say about his Cross? What can I say? He chose the most extreme kind of death so that his martyrs would fear no kind of death. He displayed his teaching in his humanity; he showed an example of endurance on the Cross. There was the work, because he was crucified; the example of the work, the Cross; the reward of the work, the Resurrection. On the Cross he showed us what we should endure; in the Resurrection he showed us what we should hope for. Like the supreme director of the games, he said: “Do it, and take the prize; perform the work, and receive the reward; struggle in the contest, and you will be crowned.” What is the work? Obedience. What is the reward? Resurrection without death. Why did I add “without death”? Because Lazarus rose and died; Christ rose and *dies no more; death will no longer have dominion over him* [^16].

**3. 10.** Scripture says: *You have heard of Job's endurance and have seen the Lord's end* [^17]. When we read how much Job endured, we shudder, recoil, and tremble. And what did he receive? Twice what he had lost. So, lest someone want to be patient for temporal rewards and say to himself, “Let me bear the loss; God will give me twice as many children. Job received everything doubled, and he fathered as many children as he had buried. Was that not double?” Certainly it was double, because the others were also alive. Let no one say, “I will bear evils, and God will repay me as he repaid Job,” so that it would no longer be endurance but greed. For if that holy man had not possessed endurance, he would not have bravely borne what happened. How would he have received the testimony the Lord gave him? *Have you considered*, the Lord says, *my servant Job? There is no one like him on earth, a blameless man, a true worshiper of God* [^18]. What testimony, brothers, did this holy man deserve from the Lord! Yet a wicked woman tried to deceive him by her persuasion, herself representing that serpent who, just as he deceived the first man made by God in paradise [^19], now also thought he could deceive a man pleasing to God by suggesting blasphemy. How much he suffered, brothers! Who can suffer so much in his property, his house, his children, his flesh, and in his wife herself, left to tempt him? But the devil would long since have taken away even her who remained, had he not kept her as his helper, since he had defeated the first man through Eve. He had kept an Eve. How much, then, Job suffered! He lost everything he had; his house collapsed—if only the house alone!—and crushed his children too. Because endurance had taken such a strong hold in him, listen to his answer: *The Lord gave, the Lord took away; as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done. Blessed be the Lord's name* [^20]. He took away what he gave. Was the one who gave them the one who bore them? He took away what he gave, as though Job were saying, “He has taken everything; let him take everything, let him leave me naked, and let him preserve himself for me. What will I lack if I have God? Or what use are other things to me if I do not have God?” The attack reached his flesh: he was struck with sores from head to foot, oozing pus and swarming with worms, yet he showed himself immovable in his God and stood firm in him [^21]. That woman, the devil's helper rather than her husband's comforter, tried to persuade him to blaspheme. *How long*, she said, *will you suffer this and that? Say a word against the Lord, and die* [^22]. So, because he had been humbled, he was to be exalted. The Lord did this to show it to human beings, for he himself kept greater things in heaven for his servant. Thus he exalted the humbled Job and humbled the exalted devil, because *he humbles the one and exalts the other* [^23]. But let no one, dearest brothers, when he suffers troubles of this kind, expect his reward here. For example, if someone suffers losses, let him not say, *The Lord gave, the Lord took away; as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done. Blessed be the Lord's name*, with the intention of receiving double. Endurance praises God, not greed. If you seek to receive twice what you lost and praise God for that reason, you praise him out of desire, not love. Do not appeal to that holy man's example; you deceive yourself. When Job bore everything, he did not hope for double. What I am saying can be seen both in his first confession, when he suffered losses and buried his children, and in his second, when he was already enduring the torment of sores in his flesh. These are the words of his first confession: *The Lord gave, the Lord took away; as it pleased the Lord, so it has been done. Blessed be the Lord's name*. He could have said, “The Lord gave, the Lord took away; the one who took can give again, can restore more than he removed.” He did not say this, but, *As it pleased the Lord*, he said, *so it has been done*: “Because it pleases him, let it please me too; let what pleased the good Lord not displease the subject servant; let what pleased the physician not displease the sick man.” Hear his other confession: *You have spoken*, he told his wife, *like one of the foolish women. If we have received good things from the Lord's hand, why should we not bear evil things?* [^24] He did not add what would have been true if he had said it: “The Lord is able both to restore my flesh to its former state and to multiply what he has taken from us,” lest he seem to have endured those things in that hope. He did not say these things; he did not hope for them. But for our instruction the Lord granted them to one who did not hope for them, so that we might be taught that God had been with him; for if he had not restored those things too, we could never have seen Job's hidden crown. And so what does divine Scripture say when it encourages endurance and hope for future things, not a reward in present things? *You have heard of Job's endurance and have seen the Lord's end* [^25]. Why *Job's endurance*, and not “You have seen Job's own end”? You would open your mouth wide for double; you would say, “Thanks be to God! Let me endure, and I will receive double, like Job.” *Job's endurance, the Lord's end*. We know Job's endurance, and we know the Lord's end. *God, my God, why have you forsaken me?* [^26] These are the Lord's words as he hung on the wood. It is as though God abandons him as regards present happiness, but he does not abandon him as regards eternal immortality. There is the Lord's end. The Jews seize him, the Jews mock him, the Jews bind him, crown him with thorns, dishonor him with spit, scourge him, overwhelm him with insults, hang him on the wood, pierce him with a spear, and finally bury him: it is as though he has been abandoned. But what of it? They were mocking him. Therefore have endurance so that you may rise and not die—that is, never die—like Christ. For thus we read: *Christ, rising from the dead, dies no more* [^27].

**4. 11.** *He ascended into heaven*: believe it. *He sits at the Father's right hand* [^28]: believe it. Understand “sits” to mean “dwells,” as when we say of someone, “He stayed in that country for three years.” Scripture too says that someone sat in a city for a certain time. Did he sit and never stand up? That is why people's dwellings are called seats. They have their seats there: do they always sit? Do they not rise, walk, or lie down? Yet they are called seats. Believe, then, that Christ dwells at God the Father's right hand: he is there. Do not let your heart ask you, “What is he doing?” Do not seek to discover what you are not permitted to find: he is there; that is enough for you. He is blessed, and from that blessedness comes the expression “the Father's right hand”; the Father's right hand is a name for that blessedness itself. For if we understand physically that he sits at the Father's right hand, the Father will be on the left. Can it be right to arrange them like that, the Son on the right and the Father on the left? There everything is right, because there is no misery there.

**4. 12.** *From there he will come to judge the living and the dead* [^29]. The living: those who remain alive; the dead: those who have gone before. It can also be understood this way: the living are the righteous; the dead are the unrighteous. For he judges both, giving each his due. At the judgment he will say to the righteous: *Come, blessed of my Father; receive the kingdom prepared for you from the beginning of the world* [^30]. Prepare yourselves for this, hope for this, live for this and live in this way; believe for this, be baptized for this, so that you may be told: *Come, blessed of my Father; receive the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world*. What will he say to those on the left? *Go into the eternal fire prepared for the devil and his angels* [^31]. Thus the living and the dead will be judged by Christ. We have spoken of Christ's first birth outside time; we have spoken of his other birth from a virgin in the fullness of time; we have spoken of Christ's Passion and Christ's judgment. Everything that had to be said about Christ, God's only Son, our Lord, has been said; but the Trinity is not yet complete in our account.

**5. 13.** The Creed continues: *And in the Holy Spirit* [^32]. This Trinity is one God, one nature, one substance, one power, supreme equality, no division, no difference, everlasting charity. Do you want to know that the Holy Spirit is God? Be baptized, and you will be his temple. The Apostle says: *Do you not know that your bodies are the temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God?* [^33] God has a temple; indeed, King Solomon, also a prophet, was commanded to build a temple to God. If he had built a temple to the sun, the moon, some star, or some angel, would God not have condemned him? Thus, because he built a temple to God, he showed that he worshiped God. And what did he build it from? Wood and stones, because God deigned to have his servant make him a house on earth, where he would be entreated and would dwell. Hence blessed Stephen says: *Solomon built him a house, but the Most High does not dwell in temples made by hands* [^34]. If, then, our bodies are the Holy Spirit's temple, what sort of God is he who built a temple for the Holy Spirit? Yet he is God. For if our bodies are the Holy Spirit's temple, the one who built our bodies also built a temple for the Holy Spirit. Listen to the Apostle saying: *God has arranged the body, giving greater honor to the part that lacked it* [^35], when he was speaking of the different members, so that there should be no divisions in the body. God created our body. God created the grass; who created our body? How do we prove that God creates the grass? The one who clothes it also creates it. Read the Gospel: *If God so clothes the grass of the field*, he says, *which is here today and tomorrow is thrown into the oven* [^36]. Therefore the one who clothes it creates it. And the Apostle says: *Fool, what you sow does not come to life unless it dies; and what you sow is not the body that will be, but a bare grain, perhaps* *of wheat or of something else. But God gives it a body as he wills, and to each of the seeds its own body* [^37]. If, then, God builds our bodies, if God builds our members, and our bodies are the Holy Spirit's temple, do not doubt that the Holy Spirit is God. And do not add him as though he were a third God, because the Father, the Son, and the Holy Spirit are one God. Believe in this way.

**6. 14.** After the proclamation of the Trinity comes: *The holy Church* [^38]. God and his temple have been shown. *For God's temple is holy*, says the Apostle, *and you are that temple* [^39]. She is the holy Church, the one Church, the true Church, the Catholic Church, fighting against all heresies. She can fight, but she cannot be conquered. All heresies have gone out from her like useless branches cut off from the vine; she remains in her root, in her vine, in her charity. The gates of the underworld will not prevail against her [^40].

**7. 15.** *The forgiveness of sins* [^41]. You have the Creed fulfilled in you when you are baptized. Let no one say, “I did that; perhaps I will not be forgiven.” What have you done? How much have you done? Name some monstrous act you have committed, something grave and dreadful that horrifies you even to think about. Whatever you may have done, did you kill Christ? Nothing is worse than that deed, because nothing is better than Christ. How great a crime is it to kill Christ? Yet the Jews killed him, and many afterward believed in him and drank his blood: the sin they had committed was forgiven them. Once you have been baptized, hold to a good life in God's commandments, so that you may preserve your baptism to the end. I am not telling you that you will live here without sin; but there are venial sins, without which this life is not lived. Baptism was established for all sins; prayer was established for the slight ones without which we cannot live. What does the prayer contain? *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors* [^42]. We are washed once by baptism; we are washed daily by prayer. But do not commit those sins for which you must be separated from Christ's body—may that be far from you. For those you see doing penance have committed crimes, adulteries, or other monstrous deeds; that is why they do penance. If their sins had been slight, daily prayer would have sufficed to wipe them away.

**8. 16.** Sins, then, are forgiven in the Church in three ways: in baptism, in prayer, and in the deeper humility of penance. Yet God forgives sins only to the baptized. Even the sins he forgives at first, he forgives only to the baptized. When? When they are baptized. The sins afterward forgiven to those who pray and do penance, he forgives to the baptized. For how can those who have not yet been born say *Our Father* [^43]? As long as they are catechumens, all their sins remain upon them. If that is true of catechumens, how much more of pagans? How much more of heretics? Yet we do not replace heretics' baptism. Why? Because they possess baptism as a deserter possesses his military mark: that is how they too possess baptism. They have it, but for their condemnation, not their crowning. Yet if the deserter himself reforms and begins to serve, does anyone dare to change his mark?

**9. 17.** We also believe in *the resurrection of the flesh*, which has already taken place in Christ, so that the body too may hope for what has already taken place in its head. Christ is the Church's head; the Church is Christ's body. Our head rose and ascended into heaven: where the head is, there too are the members. What kind of resurrection of the flesh? Lest someone think it is like Lazarus's, the words *unto eternal life* [^44] have been added so that you may know it is not so. May God give you new birth; may God preserve and protect you; may God lead you to himself, who is eternal life itself. Amen.
