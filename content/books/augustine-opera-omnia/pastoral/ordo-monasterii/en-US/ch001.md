# The Order of the Monastery

**1.** Above all, dearest brothers, let God be loved, and then our neighbor, for these are the principal commandments given to us.

**2.** We set out how we ought to pray and chant the psalms. At morning prayer, three psalms are to be said: the sixty-second, the fifth, and the eighty-ninth. At the third hour, one responsorial psalm is to be said, then two antiphons, a reading, and the concluding prayer; likewise at the sixth and ninth hours. At the lighting of the lamps, one responsorial psalm, four antiphons, another responsorial psalm, a reading, and the concluding prayer. At an appropriate time after the lighting of the lamps, readings are to be read while everyone is seated; after this, the customary psalms are to be said before sleep. For prayers at night, in November, December, January, and February, there are to be twelve antiphons, six psalms, and three readings; in March, April, September, and October, ten antiphons, five psalms, and three readings; in May, June, July, and August, eight antiphons, four psalms, and two readings.

**3.** Let them work from morning until the sixth hour and devote themselves to reading from the sixth hour until the ninth; at the ninth hour let them return the books. After eating, let them work, whether in the garden or wherever necessary, until the hour of the lighting of the lamps.

**4.** Let no one claim anything as his own, whether clothing or anything else, for we desire to live the apostolic life.

**5.** Let no one do anything with grumbling, lest he perish under the same judgment as those who grumbled.

**6.** Let them obey faithfully, honor their father after God, and show their superior the respect befitting the saints.

**7.** While seated at table, let them keep silence and listen to the readings. If anything is needed, let their superior attend to it. On Saturday and Sunday, as has been established, those who wish may receive wine.

**8.** If it is necessary to send someone on an errand for the monastery, let two go. Let no one eat or drink outside the monastery without permission, for this does not accord with the monastery’s discipline. If brothers are sent to sell the monastery’s products, let them take careful heed not to act against the command, knowing that they provoke God in his servants. If they purchase anything the monastery needs, let them act carefully and faithfully as servants of God.

**9.** Let there be no idle talk among them. From morning let them sit at their work. After the prayers of the third hour, let them likewise go to their work. Let them not stand about spinning stories, unless perhaps there is something beneficial to the soul; instead, let them sit silently at their work, unless the requirements of the work oblige someone to speak.

**10.** If anyone, instead of striving with all his strength and with the help of the Lord’s mercy to fulfill these things, despises them with an obstinate spirit, let him be warned once and a second time; if he does not amend, let him know that he is subject to the monastery’s discipline as is fitting. If his age warrants it, let him also be beaten.

**11.** By faithfully and devoutly observing these things in Christ’s name, you will make progress, and we will have no small joy in your salvation. Amen.
