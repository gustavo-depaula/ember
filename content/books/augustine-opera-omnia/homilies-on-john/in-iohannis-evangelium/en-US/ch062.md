# Tractate 62

## *When, therefore, he had received the morsel, he went out at once. And it was night* (Jn 13:26-31).

## Judas betrayed Christ, Christ betrayed himself; the one was carrying on the business of his own selling, the other of our redemption. *What you do, do quickly,* not because you are able, but because he wills it who is able to do everything. — Both good things harm, and evil things profit.

**1.** I know, dearly beloved, that some can be moved, whether the devout to inquire, or the impious to find fault, that after the Lord gave the dipped bread to his betrayer, Satan entered into him. For so it is written: *And when he had dipped the bread, he gave it to Judas, the son of Simon Iscariot; and after the bread, then Satan entered into him.* For they say: Did then the bread of Christ, handed from the table of Christ, deserve this, that after it Satan should enter into his disciple? To whom we answer that from this we are rather taught how much it is to be guarded against to receive a good thing in an evil way. For it makes a great difference, not what one receives, but who receives; nor what is given, but what sort is the one to whom it is given. For both good things harm, and evil things profit, according to those to whom they are given. *Sin,* says the Apostle, *that it might appear sin, worked death in me through what is good*[^1]. Behold, through a good thing came about an evil, while a good thing is received in an evil way. And again he himself says: *Lest I be lifted up by the greatness of my revelations, there was given me a thorn in my flesh, a messenger of Satan to buffet me. For which I asked the Lord three times that he might take it from me, and he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you; for power is made perfect in weakness*[^2]. Behold, through an evil thing came about a good, while an evil thing is received well.

Why, then, do you wonder if the bread of Christ was given to Judas, by which he might be made over to the devil; when you see, on the contrary, that a messenger of the devil was given to Paul, by which he might be perfected in Christ? So both the good thing harmed the evil man, and the evil thing profited the good man. Remember whence it is written: *Whoever eats the bread or drinks the cup of the Lord unworthily, will be guilty of the body and blood of the Lord*[^3]. And the discourse was about these, when the Apostle said this, who took the body of the Lord indiscriminately and negligently, as any other food. If, then, he is here rebuked who does not discern, that is, does not distinguish the Lord's body from other foods; in what manner is he condemned who comes to his table as an enemy feigning to be a friend? If the negligence of a guest is touched with reproof, with what punishment is the seller of his host struck? But what was the bread given to the betrayer, but a demonstration to what grace he had been ungrateful?

**2.** Satan, then, entered after this bread into the betrayer of the Lord, that he might now more fully possess one already handed over to him, into whom he had first entered to deceive him. For he was not absent from him when he went to the Jews and bargained for the price of betraying the Lord, since the Evangelist Luke most plainly attests this and says: *Now Satan entered into Judas, who was surnamed Iscariot, one of the twelve; and he went and spoke with the chief priests*[^4]. Behold where it is shown that Satan had already entered into Judas. He had entered first, then, by sending into his heart the thought by which he would betray Christ: for such he had already come to the supper. But now after the bread he entered into him, not to tempt one still another's, but to possess one his own.

**3.** But it was not then, as some who read carelessly suppose, that Judas received the body of Christ. For it must be understood that the Lord had already distributed to all of them the sacrament of his body and blood, where Judas too was, as holy Luke most plainly narrates: and then it came to this point, where, according to John's narration, the Lord most plainly expresses his betrayer by the morsel dipped and handed; perhaps by the dipping of the bread signifying his feigning. For not all things that are dipped are washed; but some are dipped that they may be stained. But if the dipping here signifies something good, the condemnation that followed was not undeservedly the lot of one ungrateful to that good.

## Unless Christ betrayed himself, no one would betray Christ.

**4.** Yet, Judas being now possessed not by the Lord but by the devil, when the bread had entered into the belly of the ungrateful man, and the enemy into his mind: still, I say, the full effect of so great an evil, now conceived in the heart, remained to be carried out, the damnable purpose of which had already gone before. And so, when the Lord, the living bread, had handed bread to the dead man, and by handing the bread had pointed out the betrayer of the bread: *What you do,* he says, *do quickly*[^5]. He did not command a crime; but he foretold to Judas an evil, to us a good. For what was worse for Judas, and what better for us, than Christ betrayed, by him against him, for us apart from him? *What you do, do quickly.* O word more of one ready than of one angry! O word expressing not so much the punishment of the traitor, as signifying the reward of the Redeemer! For he said: *What you do, do quickly,* not so much raging to the destruction of the faithless, as hastening to the salvation of the faithful; because *he was betrayed for our offenses*[^6], and loved the Church, and gave himself up for it[^7]. Whence also the Apostle says of him: *Who loved me, and gave himself up for me*[^8]. Unless, then, Christ betrayed himself, no one would betray Christ. What has Judas but sin? For in betraying Christ he did not think of our salvation, for which Christ was betrayed, but he thought of the gain of money, and found the loss of his soul. He received the reward he wished, but to him, unwilling, was given the reward he deserved. Judas betrayed Christ, Christ betrayed himself: the one was carrying on the business of his own selling, the other of our redemption. *What you do, do quickly,* not because you are able, but because he wills it who is able to do everything.

**5.** *But none of those reclining at table knew for what purpose he said this to him. For some thought, because Judas had the moneybag, that Jesus said to him: Buy the things we need for the feast day; or that he should give something to the poor*[^9]. The Lord, then, had moneybags too, and, keeping the things offered by the faithful, distributed them to the needs of his own and to others in want. Then for the first time was established the model of the Church's money, that we might understand that what he commanded—that there should be no thought for the morrow—was not commanded for this end, that no money should be kept by the saints; but lest God should be served for the sake of it, and righteousness be forsaken for fear of want. For the Apostle too, providing for the future, says: *If any believer has widows, let him supply them sufficiently, that the Church may not be burdened, so that it may suffice for those who are widows indeed*[^10].

**6.** *When, therefore, he had received the morsel, he went out at once. And it was night*[^11]. And he himself who went out was night. *When, therefore,* night *had gone out, Jesus says: Now is the Son of man glorified*[^12]. Day, then, uttered the word to day, that is, Christ to the faithful disciples, that they might hear him, and love him by following; and night announced knowledge to night, that is, Judas to the unbelieving Jews, that they might come to him, and seize him by persecuting[^13].

But from here on, for the Lord's discourse, which was made to the devout before he was held by the impious, a more attentive hearer is demanded; and therefore the expositor ought not to rush, but rather to defer.
