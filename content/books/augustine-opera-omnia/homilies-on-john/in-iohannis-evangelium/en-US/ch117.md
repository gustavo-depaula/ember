# Tractate 117

## *What I have written, I have written* (Jn 19:17-22).

## O the ineffable force of the divine working, even in the hearts of the ignorant! Pilate wrote what he wrote because the Lord said what he said.

**1.** As Pilate judged and condemned from the judgment seat, they took the Lord Jesus Christ about the sixth hour and led him away. *And bearing his own cross, he went out to what is called the Place of a Skull, in Hebrew Golgotha, where they crucified him*[^1]. What, then, is the meaning of what Mark the evangelist says—*Now it was the third hour, and they crucified him*[^2]—except that the Lord was crucified at the third hour by the tongues of the Jews, at the sixth hour by the hands of the soldiers? So that we may understand that the fifth hour had already passed, and something of the sixth had begun, when Pilate sat on the judgment seat, which John called about the sixth hour; and that while he was led away, and fastened to the wood with the two robbers, and while the things that are related as done were done beside his cross, the sixth hour was completed in full; from which hour until the ninth, the sun being darkened, the authority of three evangelists—Matthew, Mark, and Luke—attests that darkness came. But because the Jews tried to transfer the crime of Christ's killing from themselves to the Romans, that is, to Pilate and his soldiers, therefore Mark, suppressing that hour at which Christ was crucified by the soldiers, which had already begun to be the sixth, recorded rather the third hour, at which they are understood to have been able to cry out before Pilate, *Crucify, crucify*[^3]: so that not only those—the soldiers who hung him on the wood at the sixth hour—but also the Jews, who at the third hour cried out that he should be crucified, may be found to have crucified Jesus.

**2.** There is also another solution to this question: that the sixth hour here is not taken as the sixth hour of the day, since John does not say, Now it was about the sixth hour of the day, or about the sixth hour; but he says, *Now it was the preparation of the Passover, about the sixth hour*[^4]. Now "parasceve" in Latin is "preparation"; but the Jews prefer to use this Greek word in observances of this kind, even those who speak Latin more than Greek.

It was, then, the preparation of the Passover. And *our Passover*, as the Apostle says, *is Christ, who was sacrificed*[^5]; and if we reckon the preparation of this Passover from the ninth hour of the night—for it was then that the chief priests seem to have pronounced the Lord's sacrifice, saying, *He is guilty of death*[^6], while he was still being heard in the house of the high priest, whence it is fittingly taken that the preparation of the true Passover began there, of which the Passover of the Jews was the shadow, that is, the preparation of the sacrificing of Christ, from the moment he was pronounced by the priests to be sacrificed—then surely from that hour of the night, which is conjectured to have been the ninth, up to the third hour of the day at which Mark the evangelist attests that Christ was crucified, there are six hours, three of the night and three of the day. Hence in this preparation of the Passover, that is, the preparation for the sacrificing of Christ, which had begun at the ninth hour of the night, the sixth hour was being kept—that is, the fifth being completed, the sixth had now begun to run, when Pilate ascended the judgment seat: for it was still that very preparation, which had begun at the ninth hour of the night, until there came to pass the sacrificing of Christ for which preparation was made; which took place at the third hour according to Mark—not of the preparation, but of the day; and the same was the sixth not of the day, but of the preparation, the six hours being reckoned from the ninth of the night to the third of the day.

Of these two solutions of this difficult question, let each choose which he will. But he will judge better which to choose who has read what has been most laboriously argued in *The Agreement among the Evangelists*. And if still other solutions can be found, the more abundantly will the constancy of evangelical truth be defended against the slanders of unbelieving and impious vanity.

Now let us return to the narrative of John the evangelist, after these things have been briefly treated.

## To impiety a spectacle, a mockery.

**3.** *So they took Jesus,* he says, *and led him away; and bearing his own cross, he went out to what is called the Place of a Skull, in Hebrew Golgotha, where they crucified him*[^7]. He was going, then, to the place where he was to be crucified, carrying his own cross, Jesus. A great spectacle: but if impiety looks on, a great mockery; if piety, a great mystery: if impiety looks on, a great proof of disgrace; if piety, a great bulwark of faith: if impiety looks on, it laughs at the king carrying, in place of the scepter of his kingdom, the wood of his own torment; if piety, it sees the king bearing the wood for fastening himself, which he was also to fasten upon the foreheads of kings: in that very thing despicable to the eyes of the impious, in which the hearts of the saints were to glory. For to Paul, who would say, *But far be it from me to glory except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^8], he was commending his very cross by carrying it on his own shoulder; and for the lamp that was to burn, which was not to be put under a bushel, he was carrying the lampstand.

*Bearing his own cross,* then, *he went out to what is called the Place of a Skull, in Hebrew Golgotha, where they crucified him, and with him two others, one on either side, and Jesus in the middle*[^9]. These two were robbers, as we have learned from the narrative of the other evangelists, with whom Christ was crucified and among whom he was fastened; concerning whom prophecy had said beforehand: *And he was reckoned among the wicked*[^10].

**4.** *Pilate also wrote a title and put it on the cross; and it was written, Jesus of Nazareth, the King of the Jews. Many of the Jews therefore read this title, for the place where Jesus was crucified was near the city. And it was written in Hebrew, Greek, and Latin: The King of the Jews*[^11]. For these three languages were prominent there above the rest: Hebrew, on account of the Jews glorying in the law of God; Greek, on account of the wise of the Gentiles; Latin, on account of the Romans, who at that time were ruling over many and almost all nations.

**5.** *So the chief priests of the Jews said to Pilate, Do not write, The King of the Jews, but that he said, I am the King of the Jews. Pilate answered, What I have written, I have written*[^12]. O the ineffable force of the divine working, even in the hearts of the ignorant! Was there not a certain hidden voice resounding within Pilate, in a kind of, if it may be said, clamorous silence, the thing that was prophesied so long before in the writings of the Psalms: *Do not corrupt the inscription of the title*[^13]? Behold, he does not corrupt the inscription of the title; what he wrote, he wrote. But the chief priests too, who wanted this corrupted—what did they say? *Do not write,* they say, *The King of the Jews, but that he said, I am the King of the Jews.* What are you saying, you madmen? Why do you oppose the doing of what you can in no way change? For will it therefore not be true, because Jesus said, *I am the King of the Jews*? If what Pilate wrote cannot be corrupted, can what the truth said be corrupted?

But is Christ king only of the Jews, or also of the Gentiles? Of the Gentiles too, indeed. For when he had said in the prophecy, *But I have been set up by him as king over Zion his holy mountain, proclaiming the precept of the Lord*[^14], lest, on account of Mount Zion, anyone should say he was set up as king of the Jews alone, he at once added: *The Lord said to me, You are my Son, today I have begotten you; ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your heritage, and the ends of the earth as your possession*[^15]. Hence he himself too, now speaking by his own mouth among the Jews, says: *I have other sheep that are not of this fold; I must bring them also, and they will hear my voice, and there will be one flock and one shepherd*[^16]. Why, then, do we wish a great mystery to be understood in this title, in which was written, *The King of the Jews*, if Christ is king also of the Gentiles? Because, namely, the wild olive was made a partaker of the richness of the olive tree; the olive tree was not made a partaker of the bitterness of the wild olive. For in that the title was truly written of Christ, *The King of the Jews*, who are to be understood as the Jews, except the seed of Abraham, the children of the promise, who are also children of God? Since *it is not the children of the flesh,* says the Apostle, *who are the children of God; but the children of the promise are reckoned as the offspring*[^17]. And the Gentiles were those to whom he said: *And if you are Christ's, then you are Abraham's offspring, heirs according to the promise*[^18]. King, then, of the Jews is Christ, but of Jews by circumcision of the heart, in the spirit, not in the letter; whose praise is not from men but from God; belonging to the free Jerusalem, our eternal mother in heaven, the spiritual Sarah, casting out the slave woman and her children from the house of freedom. For this reason Pilate wrote what he wrote, because the Lord said what he said.
