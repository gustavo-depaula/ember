# Tractate 76

## *We will come to him and make our abode with him* (Jn 14:22-24).

## *We will come to him.* God the Trinity, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, come to us, while we come to them: they come by succoring, we come by obeying; they come by enlightening, we come by beholding; they come by filling, we come by receiving.

**1.** The disciples questioning, and Jesus the master answering them, we too, as if with them, learn, when we either read or hear the holy Gospel.

Because, then, the Lord had said: *Yet a little while, and the world now sees me no more, but you will see me*; Judas asked him about this very thing—not that betrayer of his who was surnamed Iscariot, but the one whose Epistle is read among the canonical Scriptures: *Lord, what has happened, that you are going to manifest yourself to us, and not to the world*[^1]? Let us be with them as disciples questioning, and let us too hear the common master. For Judas the holy, not the unclean, not a persecutor of the Lord, but a follower, asked the cause why Jesus was going to manifest himself not to the world, but to his own; why yet a little while and the world would not see him, but they themselves would see him.

## Love distinguishes the holy from the world.

**2.** *Jesus answered and said to him: If anyone loves me, he will keep my word: and my Father will love him, and we will come to him, and make our abode with him. He who does not love me does not keep my words*[^2]. Behold, the cause is set forth why he is going to manifest himself to his own, not to strangers, whom he calls by the name of the world; and the cause is this, that these love, those do not love. This is the cause of which the sacred Psalm sounds: *Judge me, O God, and separate my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^3]. For those who love, because they love, are chosen: but those who do not love, though they speak with the tongues of men and of angels, become sounding brass and a tinkling cymbal; and though they have prophecy, and know all mysteries, and all knowledge, and have all faith so as to move mountains, they are nothing; and though they distribute all their substance, and hand over their body to be burned, it profits them nothing[^4].

Love distinguishes the holy from the world, which makes them dwell of one mind in a house[^5]. In which house the Father and the Son make their abode; who also give the very love to those to whom they will give at the end even their very manifestation: about which the disciple asked the master, that not only those who then heard through his mouth, but we too through his Gospel might be able to know this. For he had asked about the manifestation of Christ, and heard about love and abode. There is, then, a certain inner manifestation of God, which the ungodly altogether do not know, to whom there is no manifestation of God the Father and the Holy Spirit: but of the Son there could be, but in the flesh; which is neither such as that other, nor can it always be present to them, of whatever sort it be, but for a little time; and this unto judgment, not unto joy; unto punishment, not unto reward.

**3.** Now, then, it is the time that we understand, as much as he himself deigns to open it, how it was said: *Yet a little while, and the world now sees me no more, but you will see me.* It is true indeed that after a little he was going to withdraw from their eyes even his body in which the ungodly too could see him; since after the resurrection none of them saw him. But since it was said, with the angels attesting: *So will he come as you saw him going into heaven*[^6]; and we believe nothing else than that he will come in the same body to the judgment of the living and the dead: without doubt the world will then see him, by which name are signified those alien from his kingdom. And through this it is far better understood that he wished to signify that time, in that which he says: *Yet a little while, and the world now sees me no more,* when at the end of the age he will be taken from the eyes of the condemned, that those, among whom the Father and he himself make their abode, may henceforth see him as lovers of him. But he said *a little while,* because even what seems long to men is very brief before the eyes of God: for of this little while this same John the Evangelist says: *Little children, it is the last hour*[^7].

**4.** But lest anyone should suppose that the Father and the Son only, without the Holy Spirit, make their abode with their lovers, let him recall what was said above of the Holy Spirit: *Whom the world cannot receive, because it does not see him, nor know him: but you will know him, because he will abide with you, and will be in you.* Behold, the Holy Spirit too makes his abode in the saints with the Father and the Son; within, surely, as God in his temple. God the Trinity, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, come to us, while we come to them: they come by succoring, we come by obeying; they come by enlightening, we come by beholding; they come by filling, we come by receiving: that their vision may be to us not external, but internal; and their abode in us not transitory, but eternal. So the Son does not manifest himself to the world: for "the world" was meant in this place of those of whom he at once added: *He who does not love me does not keep my words.* These are they who never see the Father and the Holy Spirit: but the Son they see, not that they may be made blessed, but that they may be judged, for a little while; and not him in the form of God, where he is with the Father and the Holy Spirit equally invisible; but in the form of man, where he willed to be to the world contemptible by suffering, terrible by judging.

**5.** But as for what he added: *And the word that you have heard is not mine, but his who sent me, the Father's*[^8]; let us not wonder, let us not be alarmed: he is not less than the Father, but he is only of the Father; he is not unequal to him, but he is not of himself. For he did not lie by saying: *He who does not love me does not keep my words.* Behold, he said the words were his; is he then contrary to himself, where he again said: *And the word that you have heard is not mine*? And perhaps on account of some distinction, where he said they were his, he said it in the plural, that is, *words*; but where he said the *word,* that is, the Word, was not his, but the Father's, he wished himself to be understood. *For in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^9]. Surely not his own, but the Father's is the Word: as neither is the image his own, but the Father's; nor is the very same one his own Son, but the Father's. Rightly, then, he attributes to the author whatever the equal one does, from whom he has this very thing, that he is equal to him without difference.
