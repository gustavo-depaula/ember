# Homily 9

## You have kept the good wine until now[^1].

## That the Lord, invited, came to the marriage, even apart from the mystical signification, he wished to confirm that he himself made marriage. The Lord came to the marriage that conjugal chastity might be made firm, and that the sacred sign of marriage might be shown.

**1.** May the Lord our God be present, to grant us to render what we promised. For yesterday, if your Holiness remembers, when we were shut out by the narrowness of time from completing the sermon we had begun, we deferred to today, so that the things which in this deed of the Gospel reading were placed mystically in sacred signs might, with his help, be opened up. There is, then, no need now to dwell longer on commending God's miracle. For it is God himself who, throughout the whole of creation, does daily miracles, which have grown cheap to human beings not through their easiness but through their constancy: but the rare things that were done by the same Lord, that is, by the Word incarnate for our sake, brought greater astonishment to human beings: not because they were greater than the things he does daily in creation, but because these things that happen daily are accomplished, as it were, by a natural course; while those were shown to the eyes of human beings by the efficacy of a power, as it were, present.

We said, as you remember, one dead man rose again, and people were astonished; while no one marvels that daily those are born who were not. So who would not marvel at water turned into wine, when God does this every year in the vines? But because all the things that the Lord Jesus did avail not only to rouse our hearts by miracles, but also to build them up in the doctrine of faith; it behooves us to search out what all those things mean, that is, what they signify. For the significations of all these, as you recall, we deferred to today.

## Joining is from God, divorce from the devil.

**2.** That the Lord, invited, came to the marriage, even apart from the mystical signification, he wished to confirm that he himself made marriage. For there were going to be those, of whom the Apostle spoke, forbidding to marry[^2], and saying that marriage was an evil, and that the devil had made it: whereas the same Lord says in the Gospel, when asked whether it is lawful for a man to dismiss his wife for any cause whatever, that it is not lawful except for the cause of fornication. In which answer, if you remember, he said this: *What God has joined, let man not separate*[^3]. And those who are well instructed in the Catholic faith know that God made marriage, and that as the joining is from God, so divorce is from the devil. But for this reason it is lawful to dismiss a wife in the cause of fornication, because she herself first refused to be a wife, who did not keep conjugal faith to her husband.

Nor are those who vow their virginity to God, although they hold a higher grade of honor and holiness in the Church, without marriage: for they too belong to the marriage along with the whole Church, in which marriage the bridegroom is Christ.

And so, then, the Lord, invited, came to the marriage, that conjugal chastity might be made firm, and that the sacred sign of marriage might be shown: because the bridegroom of that marriage too figured the person of the Lord, to whom it was said: *You have kept the good wine until now*[^4]. For Christ has kept the good wine until now, that is, his Gospel.

**3.** Now let us begin to uncover the very coverings of the sacred signs, so far as he grants in whose name we made the promise to you. There was prophecy in ancient times, and no times have ceased from the dispensation of prophecy: but that prophecy, when Christ was not understood in it, was water. For in the water the wine in a way lies hidden. The Apostle says what we are to understand in this water: *Until this present day*, he says, *as long as Moses is read, the same veil is placed over their heart; which is not unveiled, because in Christ it is done away. And when you have passed over*, he says, *to the Lord, the veil will be taken away*[^5]. By the veil he means the covering of the prophecy, so that it was not understood.

The veil is taken away when you have passed over to the Lord: so foolishness is taken away when you have passed over to the Lord, and what was water becomes wine for you. Read all the prophetic books, with Christ not understood: what will you find so insipid and silly? Understand Christ there, and not only does what you read have savor, but it even makes you drunk; changing the mind from the body, *so that, forgetting what is behind, you may stretch out to the things that are before*[^6].

## The whole of prophecy was speaking of Christ.

**4.** Therefore prophecy, from ancient times, from the very point at which the order of those born runs in the human race, was not silent about Christ: but it was hidden there; for it was as yet water. By what do we prove that in all the earlier times, up to the age in which the Lord came, prophecy did not fail concerning him? By the Lord himself saying it. For when he had risen from the dead, he found his disciples doubting about the very one whom they had followed. For they had seen him dead, and did not hope that he would rise, and their whole hope had collapsed. Whence did that robber, praised, deserve to be in paradise that very day? Because, fixed on the cross, he then confessed Christ, when the disciples doubted about him.

He found them, then, wavering, and in a way reproaching themselves that they had hoped for redemption in him. Yet they grieved that he had been killed without fault, because they knew him to be innocent. And this they themselves said after the resurrection, when he found some of them sad on the road: *Are you the only stranger in Jerusalem, and do you not know what was done in it in these days? But he said to them: What things? And they said: About Jesus of Nazareth, who was a prophet, mighty in deeds and words in the sight of God and of all the people, how our priests and rulers handed him over to the condemnation of death, and fixed him on the cross. But we hoped that he was the one who would redeem Israel; and now this is the third day today since these things were done*[^7]. When one of the two whom he found on the road going to a nearby village had said these and other things; he answered and said: *O foolish and slow of heart to believe all that the Prophets have spoken! Was it not fitting that Christ should suffer all these things, and enter into his glory? And beginning from Moses and all the Prophets, he interpreted to them in all the Scriptures the things that were about himself*[^8].

Likewise in another place, when he willed even to be handled by the hands of his disciples, that they might believe he had risen in the body: *These are the words*, he says, *which I spoke to you, while I was still with you, that all things must be fulfilled which are written in the Law of Moses, and the Prophets, and the Psalms about me. Then he opened their understanding, that they might understand the Scriptures, and he said to them: Thus it is written, that Christ should suffer, and rise from the dead on the third day, and that repentance and forgiveness of sins should be preached in his name to all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^9].

## It has no savor, if Christ was not understood there.

**5.** These things from the Gospel, which are surely plain, being understood, all those mysteries will lie open which are hidden in this miracle of the Lord. See what he says, that it was fitting that the things written about him should be fulfilled in Christ. Where are they written? *In the Law*, he says, *and the Prophets, and the Psalms*. He passed over nothing of the old Scriptures. That was the water; and therefore those men were called foolish by the Lord, because to them as yet water had savor, not wine. But how did he make wine out of water? When he opened their understanding, and expounded to them the Scriptures, beginning from Moses through all the Prophets. Whence, now made drunk, they said: *Was not our heart burning on the road, when he opened the Scriptures to us?*[^10] For they understood Christ in those books in which they had not known him.

Our Lord Jesus Christ, then, changed the water into wine, and what had no savor has savor, what did not make drunk makes drunk. For if he had commanded the water to be poured out from there, and so himself sent in wine from the hidden recesses of creation, from which he made bread too when he fed so many thousands[^11]—for the five loaves did not hold the fill of five thousand men, nor even twelve baskets full, but the omnipotence of the Lord was, as it were, a fountain of bread; so he could also, the water being poured out, pour in wine: but if he had done this, he would have seemed to disapprove the old Scriptures. But since he turned the water itself into wine, he showed us that the old Scripture too is from him: for at his command the water jars were filled. From the Lord indeed is that Scripture too; but it has no savor, if Christ be not understood there.

**6.** But attend to what he himself says: *The things written in the Law, and the Prophets, and the Psalms about me*. Now we know from what times the Law narrates, that is, from the beginning of the world: *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^12]. From there up to this time which we now pass through is the sixth age, as you have often heard and know. For the first age is reckoned from Adam to Noah; the second, from Noah to Abraham; and, as the Evangelist Matthew follows and distinguishes in order, the third, from Abraham to David; the fourth, from David to the migration into Babylon; the fifth, from the migration into Babylon to John the Baptist; the sixth, from then to the end of the age.

For this reason also on the sixth day God made *man to his own image*[^13]: because in this sixth age there is manifested through the Gospel the reforming of our mind, according to the image of him who created us; and the water is turned into wine, so that we may now have a taste of Christ made manifest in the Law and the Prophets. Therefore *there were there six water jars*[^14], which he commanded to be filled with water. Those six water jars, then, signify the six ages, in which prophecy did not fail. Those six times, then, distributed and distinguished as it were by joints, would be like empty vessels, were they not filled by Christ. What did I say, times that would run emptily, unless in them the Lord Jesus were preached? The prophecies have been fulfilled, the water jars are full: but that the water may be turned into wine, let Christ be understood in all that prophecy.

## The mystery of the Trinity.

**7.** What, then, is: *They held two or three measures each*[^15]? This expression commends a mystery to us especially. By "measures" (metretas) he means certain measures, as if he said urns, amphorae, or something of the kind. The name of a measure is "metreta," and this measure took its name from measure. For the Greeks call a measure "metron": from this they are called "metretae." They held, then, two or three measures each.

What do we say, brothers? If it said three only, our mind would run to nothing but the mystery of the Trinity. But perhaps not even so ought we quickly to turn our understanding away from it, because it said two or three: because, the Father and the Son being named, consequently the Holy Spirit too is to be understood. For the Holy Spirit is not the Spirit of the Father only, or of the Son only; but the Spirit of the Father and the Son. For it is written: *If anyone loves the world, the Spirit of the Father is not in him*[^16]: likewise it is written: *But whoever does not have the Spirit of Christ, this one is not his*[^17]. But it is the same Spirit of the Father and the Son. And so, the Father and the Son being named, the Holy Spirit too is understood; because the Spirit is of the Father and of the Son. But when the Father and the Son are named, two measures, as it were, are named; but when the Holy Spirit is understood there, three measures. Therefore it was not said, holding measures, some two, others three: but the same six water jars held two or three measures each. As though he said: Even when I say two, I wish the Spirit of the Father and the Son to be understood along with them: and when I say three, I more plainly declare the Trinity itself.

**8.** Whoever, then, names the Father and the Son, ought there to understand, as it were, the mutual love of the Father and the Son, which is the Holy Spirit. For perhaps, the Scriptures being examined—which I do not say in such a way that I can teach it today, or as if something else could not be found: but nevertheless, perhaps, the Scriptures, when searched, indicate that the Holy Spirit is love. And do not think love to be a cheap thing. But how is it cheap, when all things that are said to be not cheap are said to be dear (cara)? If, then, the things that are not cheap are dear, what is dearer than love (caritas) itself?

But love is so commended by the Apostle that he says: *I show you a more excellent way. If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal: and if I know all mysteries and all knowledge, and have prophecy and all faith, so as to move mountains, but have not love, I am nothing: and if I distribute all my goods to the poor, and deliver up my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^18]. How great, then, is love, which, if it is lacking, the rest are had in vain; if it is present, all things are had rightly?

Yet the apostle Paul, praising love most copiously and abundantly, said less of it than what the apostle John, whose this Gospel is, said briefly. For he did not hesitate to say: *God is love*[^19]. It is also written: *Because the love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^20]. Who, then, would name the Father and the Son, and not there understand the love of the Father and the Son? When he has begun to have it, he will have the Holy Spirit: but if he does not have it, he will be without the Holy Spirit. And just as your body without the spirit, which is your soul, if it be such, is dead; so your soul without the Holy Spirit, that is, without love, if it be such, will be reckoned dead.

Therefore the water jars held two measures, because in the prophecy of all times the Father and the Son are preached: but there too is the Holy Spirit; and therefore it was added, or three. *I and the Father are one*[^21], he says: but far be it that the Holy Spirit be lacking, where we hear: *I and the Father are one*. Yet because he named the Father and the Son, let the water jars hold two measures; but hear, or three: *Go, baptize the nations in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit*[^22]. And so, in that they are said to be two, it is not expressed, but understood; but in that they are said to be or three, the Trinity too is expressed.

**9.** But there is also another understanding, not to be passed over, and I will say it too: let each choose what he pleases; we do not withhold what is offered. For it is the table of the Lord, and it does not behoove the minister to defraud the guests, especially such as are so hungry that your eagerness is apparent. The prophecy which is dispensed from ancient times pertains to the salvation of all the nations. To the people of Israel alone indeed was Moses sent, and to that people alone the Law was given through him, and the Prophets themselves were from that people, and the very distribution of the times was distinguished according to that same people; whence also the water jars are said to be *according to the purification of the Jews*[^23]:

but nevertheless it is manifest that that prophecy was announced to the other nations as well; since Christ was hidden in him in whom all the nations are blessed, as was promised to Abraham, the Lord saying: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^24]. But he was not yet understood, because the water had not yet been turned into wine. Therefore the prophecy was dispensed to all the nations. And that this may stand out more pleasantly, let us recall, as time allows, certain things about the individual ages, as about the individual water jars.

## The six ages of time.

**10.** At the very beginning Adam and Eve were the parents of all the nations, not of the Jews only; and whatever was figured in Adam concerning Christ pertained surely to all the nations, who have salvation in Christ. What, then, shall I best say about the water of the first jar, but what the Apostle says about Adam and Eve? For no one will say that I have understood it perversely, when I bring forth not my own understanding but the Apostle's. How great a mystery, then, concerning Christ does that one thing contain, which the Apostle recalls, saying: *And the two shall be in one flesh: this is a great sacred sign*[^25]? And lest anyone should understand this greatness of the sacred sign in every individual man having a wife: *But I*, he says, *speak in Christ and in the Church*[^26]. What is this great sacred sign: The *two shall be in one flesh*? When the Scripture of Genesis was speaking of Adam and Eve, whence it came to these words: *Therefore a man will leave father and mother, and will cleave to his wife; and the two shall be in one flesh*[^27].

If, then, Christ cleaved to the Church, so that the two might be in one flesh, how did he leave his Father? how his mother? He left his Father, because *though he was in the form of God, he did not count it robbery to be equal to God, but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^28]. For this is to have left the Father, not because he deserted and withdrew from the Father; but because he did not appear to human beings in that form in which he is equal to the Father. How did he leave his mother? By leaving the synagogue of the Jews, of which he was born according to the flesh; and by cleaving to the Church, which he gathered out of all the nations.

Therefore the first jar too held prophecy concerning Christ: but when the things I speak of were not preached among the peoples, it was as yet water, it had not yet been changed into wine. And because the Lord enlightened us through the Apostle, to show us what we should seek there in that one sentence: *The two shall be in one flesh; a great sacred sign in Christ and in the Church*; now it is permitted us to seek Christ everywhere, and to drink wine from all the jars. Adam sleeps that Eve may come to be: Christ dies that the Church may come to be. To Adam sleeping Eve is made from his side: to Christ dead the side is struck with a lance, that the sacraments may flow forth, by which the Church is formed.

To whom is it not apparent that in those deeds done then, things to come were figured, since the Apostle says that Adam himself is a form of the one to come? *Who is*, he says, *a form of the one to come*[^29]. All things were prefigured mystically. For surely God was not unable to draw out a rib from a man awake, and to form a woman. Or was it perhaps that his side might not hurt when the rib was taken away, that for this reason he had to sleep? Who is there who so sleeps that bones are torn out of him without his waking? Or, because God was tearing it out, did the man for that reason not feel it? He could, then, have torn it out even from a man awake without pain, who could do so from one asleep. But without doubt the first jar was being filled: the prophecy of that time was being dispensed concerning this present time.

**11.** Christ was figured also in Noah, and in that ark of the world[^30]. For why were all the animals enclosed in the ark, unless that all the nations might be signified? For God did not lack the power to create again every kind of animal. For when all things were not, did he not say: Let the earth bring forth[^31], and the earth brought forth? From what source, then, he made them then, from the same he could remake them; by a word he made them, by a word he could remake them: except that he was commending a mystery, and was filling the second jar of the prophetic dispensation, so that through wood the figure of the world might be set free; because on wood the life of the world was to be fixed?

**12.** Now in the third jar, to Abraham himself, as I have already recalled, it was said: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^32]. And who does not see whose figure his only son bore, who carried for himself the wood for the sacrifice by which he himself was being led to be offered? For the Lord carried his own cross, as the Gospel says. Let it suffice to have recalled this from the third jar.

**13.** But concerning David, what shall I say of the fact that his prophecy pertained to all the nations; when we have just now heard a Psalm, and it is hard for a psalm to be spoken in which this does not sound forth? But surely, as I said, we have just now sung: *Arise, O God, judge the earth; for you will inherit among all the nations*[^33]. And therefore the Donatists are, as it were, cast out from the marriage: just as that man who did not have a wedding garment was invited and came, but was cast out from the number of the called, because he did not have a garment for the bridegroom's glory; for whoever seeks his own glory, not Christ's, does not have a wedding garment: for they refuse to sound in harmony with the voice of him who was the friend of the bridegroom, and said: *This is he who baptizes*[^34].

And not undeservedly was it cast at that man who did not have a wedding garment, by way of rebuke, that he did not have it: *Friend, why have you come here?*[^35] And as that man was struck dumb, so too are these. For what does the noise of the mouth profit, when the heart is dumb? For within, with themselves, they know that they have nothing to say. Within they are struck dumb, without they make a noise. They hear, willing or unwilling, sung even among themselves: *Arise, O God, judge the earth; for you will inherit among all the nations*: and by not communing with all the nations, what else do they recognize than that they themselves are disinherited?

**14.** What I was saying, then, brothers, is that the prophecy pertains to all the nations: for I wish to show another sense in what was said: *Holding two or three measures each*: to all the nations, I say, the prophecy pertains; we have just recalled that this was shown in Adam, *who is a form of the one to come*. But who does not know that from him all the nations arose: and that in his name, in four letters, the four parts of the world are indicated by their Greek appellations? For if they are said in Greek, East, West, North, South, as Sacred Scripture recalls them in many places; in the first letters of the words you find Adam: for the four mentioned parts of the world are called in Greek ἀνατολὴ, δύσις, ἄρκτος, μεσημβρία. If you write these four names as four lines one beneath another, in their first letters Adam is read.

This was figured in Noah on account of the ark, in which were all the animals, which signified all the nations: this in Abraham, to whom it was said more openly: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*: this in David, of whose Psalms, to pass over the rest, we have just sung: *Arise, O God, judge the earth; for you will inherit among all the nations*. For to what God is it said: *Arise*, but to him who slept? *Arise, O God, judge the earth*. As though it were said: You slept, having been judged by the earth; arise, that you may judge the earth. And to what does that prophecy pertain: *For you will inherit among all the nations*?

**15.** But now in the fifth age, as it were in the fifth jar, Daniel saw a stone cut from a mountain without hands, and that it broke all the kingdoms of the earth; and that the stone grew, and became a great mountain, so that it filled the whole face of the earth[^36]. What is more open, my brothers? A stone is cut from a mountain: it is *the stone which the builders rejected, and which has become the head of the corner*[^37]. From what mountain is it cut, but from the kingdom of the Jews, whence our Lord Jesus Christ was born according to the flesh? And it is cut without hands, without human work; because without a husband's embrace he arose from a virgin. That mountain from which it was cut had not filled the whole face of the earth: for the kingdom of the Jews had not held all the nations. But the kingdom of Christ, we see, occupies the whole world.

**16.** Now to the sixth age belongs John the Baptist, *than whom no one greater has arisen among those born of women*: of whom it was said: *Greater than a prophet*[^38]. How did he too show that Christ was sent to all the nations? When the Jews came to him to be baptized, and lest they be proud of the name of Abraham: *Brood of vipers, who showed you to flee from the wrath to come? Therefore bring forth fruit worthy of repentance*[^39]; that is, be humble: for he was speaking to the proud. But from what were they proud? From their carnal descent, not from the fruit of imitating their father Abraham. What does he say to them? *Do not say: We have Abraham for our father: for God is able from these stones to raise up sons to Abraham*[^40].

By "stones" he meant all the nations, not on account of firmness, as the stone was called which the builders rejected; but on account of the stolidity and hardness of folly, because they had been made like to those whom they worshiped: for they worshiped insensate images, being equally insensate. Whence insensate? Because in the Psalm it is said: *Let those who make them become like them, and all who trust in them*[^41]. Therefore when human beings have begun to worship God, what do they hear? *That you may be sons of your Father who is in heaven, who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^42].

Therefore, if a man is made like to that which he worships; what is: *God is able from these stones to raise up sons to Abraham*? Let us question ourselves, and we see that it has been done. For we came from the nations; but from the nations we would not have come, unless God had raised up from stones sons to Abraham. We have been made sons of Abraham by imitating his faith, not by being born through the flesh. For just as they, by degenerating, were disinherited; so we, by imitating, were adopted. Therefore, brothers, even this prophecy of the sixth jar pertained to all the nations; and therefore it was said of all: *Holding two or three measures each*.

**17.** But how do we show that all the nations pertain to the two or three measures? For it was the part of one weighing it, as it were, that he should call those same ones two which he had called three, namely to commend the sacred sign. How are there two measures? *The circumcision and the uncircumcision*. These two peoples Scripture recalls, and it passes over no kind of human being, when it says: *The circumcision and the uncircumcision*; in these two names you have all the nations: they are two measures. Of these two walls coming from different directions, in order to make peace in himself, *Christ was made the cornerstone*[^43].

Let us show three measures too in those same all the nations. There were three sons of Noah, through whom the human race was restored[^44]. Whence the Lord says: *The kingdom of heaven is like leaven, which a woman took and hid in three measures of meal, until the whole was leavened*[^45]. Who is this woman, but the flesh of the Lord? What is the leaven, but the Gospel? What are the three measures, but all the nations, on account of the three sons of Noah? Therefore the six water jars, holding two or three measures each, are the six ages of time, holding the prophecy that pertains to all the nations, whether signified in the two kinds of human beings, that is, Jews and Greeks, as the Apostle often recalls; or in three, on account of Noah's three sons. For the prophecy was figured as reaching all the way to all the nations. For in that it reaches, it was called a measure (metreta), as the Apostle says: *We have received a measure of reaching all the way to you*[^46]. For evangelizing to the nations, he says this, a measure of reaching all the way to you.
