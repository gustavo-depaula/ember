# Tractate 53

## *Though he had done such great signs before them, they did not believe in him* (Jn 12:37-43).

Those who are so proud in their wisdom that they think so much is to be attributed to the powers of their own will that they deny that divine help is necessary to them for living well cannot believe in Christ. For the syllables of the name of Christ, and the sacraments of Christ, profit nothing where faith in Christ is resisted.

**1.** The Lord Christ, having foretold his passion, and his fruitful death in the lifting up of the cross, where he said that he would draw all things after himself, when the Jews had understood that he had spoken of his own death, and had set before him the question how he could say he was to die, since they had heard out of the Law that the Christ remains forever, exhorted them, while there was yet a little light in them, by which they had learned that Christ is eternal, to walk, that they might learn the whole, lest they be overtaken by the darkness. And when he had spoken these things, he hid himself from them. These things you have learned in the previous Sunday readings and words.

**2.** Then the Evangelist brought in that from which today's chapter was read, and said: *But though he had done such great signs before them, they did not believe in him: that the word of Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled which he spoke: Lord, who has believed our report? and to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed*[^1]? Here he shows clearly enough that the arm of the Lord is named as the very Son of God; not that God the Father is defined by the figure of a human body, and that the Son cleaves to him as a member of the body: but because all things were made through him, therefore he is called the arm of the Lord. For as your arm is that through which you work, so the arm of God is the name given to his Word, because through the Word he made the world. For why does a man stretch out his arm in order to do something, except because what he says does not at once come to pass? But if he prevailed with such great power that, without any motion of his body, what he said came to pass, his arm would be his word. But the Lord Jesus, the only-begotten Son of God the Father, as he is not a member of the Father's body, so he is not a word that is thinkable or pronounceable or transitory; because, since all things were made through him, he was the Word of God.

## The Son of God is the arm of the Father.

**3.** When, therefore, we hear that the arm of God the Father is the Son of God, let not carnal habit cry out against us: but, as far as by his gift we are able, let us think of the Power of God and the Wisdom, through whom all things were made. For such an arm is neither extended when stretched out nor drawn in when gathered up. For he is not the same who the Father is, but he and the Father are one; and equal to the Father he is everywhere wholly, as is the Father: lest any occasion lie open to the detestable error of those who say that the Father alone exists, but that according to the diversity of his works he himself is called the Son, he himself is called the Holy Spirit; and that in these words they dare to say: Behold, you see that the Father alone exists, if his arm is the Son; for a man and his arm are not two persons, but one. They do not understand or notice how words are transferred from some things to other things, on account of some likeness, even in everyday speech about things visible and most well known; how much more so that things ineffable, which cannot at all be said as they are, may somehow be said to us? For a man calls another man, through whom he is accustomed to do whatever he does, his arm: and if that man is taken from him, in grief he says: I have lost my arm; and to him who has taken him away, he says, You have taken my arm from me. Let them understand, then, how the Son is called the arm of the Father, through whom the Father made all things; lest, by not understanding this and remaining in the darkness of their own error, they be like the Jews themselves, of whom it was said: *And to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?*

**4.** Here there arises another question, about which, that something may be suitably argued, and all its most hidden recesses searched out and shaken out as is fitting, I judge to be neither within my powers, nor within the narrowness of the time, nor within your capacity. Yet because your expectation does not allow us to pass on to other things unless we say something of it, receive what we can: and where we fall short of your expectation, ask the increase from him who appointed us to plant and to water; because, as the Apostle says: *Neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters; but God who gives the increase*[^2].

Some, then, murmur among themselves, and where they can, sometimes cry out, and contend with turbulent disputation, saying: What did the Jews do, or what was their fault, if it was necessary *that the word of Isaiah the prophet might be fulfilled which he spoke: Lord, who has believed our report? and to whom has the arm of the Lord been revealed?* To whom we answer that the Lord, foreknowing things to come, foretold through the Prophet the unbelief of the Jews; foretold it, however, did not cause it. For God does not compel anyone to sin because he already knows men's future sins. For he foreknew their sins, not his own; not anyone else's, but their own. Wherefore, if the things he foreknew of them are not theirs, he did not truly foreknow: but since his foreknowledge cannot be deceived, without doubt it is not another, but they themselves who sin, whom God foreknew would sin. The Jews, then, committed the sin which he did not compel them to commit, he to whom sin is not pleasing; but he foretold that they would commit it, he from whom nothing is hidden. And therefore, if they had willed to do not evil, but good, they would not have been hindered; and this they would have been foreseen to do by him who knows what each one will do, and what he will render to him for his work.

**5.** But the words of the Gospel that follow press more, and make the question deeper: for he adds, and says: *Therefore they could not believe, because Isaiah said again: He has blinded their eyes, and hardened their heart, that they may not see with their eyes, and understand with their heart, and be converted, and I heal them*[^3]. For it is said to us: If they could not believe, what sin is it of a man who does not do what he cannot do? But if by not believing they sinned, then they could believe, and did not. If, then, they could, how does the Gospel say: *Therefore they could not believe, because Isaiah said again: He has blinded their eyes, and hardened their heart*; so that—what is more grave—the cause why they did not believe is referred to God, since he himself *blinded their eyes, and hardened their heart*? For this is not even said of the devil, but of God, as the prophetic Scripture itself attests. For if we should think this said of the devil, that *he blinded their eyes, and hardened their heart,* we must labor how we can show the fault of those who did not believe, of whom it is said, *they could not believe.* Then what shall we answer concerning another testimony of the same prophet, which the apostle Paul set down, saying: *What Israel sought, this it did not obtain; but the election obtained it: the rest, however, were blinded, as it is written: God gave them a spirit of stupor; eyes, that they may not see, and ears, that they may not hear, to this present day*[^4]?

## Let us, trembling, cry out with the Apostle: *O the depth...!*

**6.** You have heard the question proposed, brothers; you see indeed how deep it is: but we answer as we can. *They could not believe,* because Isaiah the prophet foretold this: and the Prophet foretold this, because God foreknew that it would be. But why they could not, if it be asked of me, I answer at once, because they would not: for God foresaw their evil will, and through the Prophet he foretold it, he from whom things to come cannot be hidden.

But, you say, the Prophet states another cause, not that of their will. What cause does the Prophet state? That *God gave them a spirit of stupor; eyes, that they may not see, and ears, that they may not hear, and he blinded their eyes, and hardened their heart.* I answer that this too their will deserved. For God thus blinds, thus hardens, by forsaking and not helping: which he can do by a hidden judgment, by an unjust one he cannot.

This altogether the piety of religious people ought to keep unshaken and inviolate; as the Apostle, when he was treating this same most difficult question: *What then shall we say,* he says? *Is there injustice with God? By no means*[^5]. If, then, far be it that there should be injustice with God; whether when he helps, he does it mercifully, or when he does not help, he does it justly: for he does all things not by rashness, but by judgment. Now if the judgments of holy men are just, how much more those of the God who sanctifies and justifies? They are just, then, but hidden.

Therefore, when questions of this kind come up in the midst, why one this way, another that way; why this one is blinded by God forsaking him, that one is enlightened by God helping him: let us not usurp for ourselves a judgment upon the judgment of so great a judge, but, trembling, let us cry out with the Apostle: *O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments, and his ways past finding out*[^6]! Whence it was said in the Psalm: *Your judgments are like a great deep*[^7].

**7.** Let not, then, brothers, the expectation of your Charity drive me to penetrate this depth, to examine this abyss, to search out the unsearchable. I acknowledge my own measure; I think I perceive your measure too. This is higher than my growth, and stronger than my powers; I think it is so for yours as well. Together, then, let us hear the Scripture admonishing and saying: *Seek not the things that are too high for you, and search not the things that are too strong for you*[^8]. Not that these things are denied us, since God our teacher says: *There is nothing hidden that shall not be revealed*[^9]: but if we walk in that which we have attained, as the Apostle says, not only what we do not know and ought to know, but also if in anything we are otherwise minded, this too God will reveal to us[^10].

And since we have come into the way of faith, let us hold to it most perseveringly: it will lead us to the king's chamber, *in whom are hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge*[^11]. For the Lord Jesus Christ himself did not envy those great and especially chosen disciples of his, when he said: *I have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now*[^12]. We must walk, we must advance, we must grow, that our hearts may be capable of those things which we cannot now grasp. And if the last day shall find us advancing, there we shall learn what here we could not.

## Free choice and divine help.

**8.** But if anyone knows that he can expound this question more clearly and better, and is confident he can do so, far be it from me to be less ready to learn than to teach. Only let no one dare so to defend free choice as to try to take from us the prayer in which we say: *Lead us not into temptation*; and again, let no one deny the choice of the will, and dare to excuse sin. But let us hear the Lord, both commanding and helping; both bidding us what we ought to do, and helping us that we may be able to fulfill it. For excessive confidence in their own will has lifted some up into pride; and excessive distrust of their own will has cast some down into negligence. The former say: Why do we ask God not to be overcome by temptation, which is in our own power? The latter say: Why do we try to live well, which is in God's power? O Lord, O Father who art in heaven, lead us not into either of these temptations, *but deliver us from evil*[^13]! Let us hear the Lord saying: *I have prayed for you, Peter, that your faith may not fail*[^14]; lest we think that our faith is so in free choice that it does not need divine help. Let us hear also the Evangelist saying: *He gave them power to become sons of God*[^15]; lest we think that what we believe is not at all in our own power: yet in both let us recognize his benefits. For thanks must be given, because power was given; and we must pray, lest our weakness give way. It is faith itself which works through love, as the Lord has apportioned to each one its measure[^16]; *that he who glories*, not in himself, but *in the Lord may glory*[^17].

## Those who cannot believe.

**9.** It is not, then, to be wondered at that those could not believe, whose will was so proud that, being ignorant of the righteousness of God, they wished to establish their own: as the Apostle says of them: *They are not subject to the righteousness of God*[^18]. For because they were swollen not by faith, but as it were by works, blinded by their very swelling, they stumbled against the stone of stumbling. But it was said *they could not* in such a way that we must understand that they would not; just as it was said of the Lord our God: *If we do not believe, he remains faithful, he cannot deny himself*[^19]. Of the omnipotent one it was said, *he cannot.* As, then, that the Lord *cannot deny himself* is the praise of the divine will, so that they *could not believe* is the fault of the human will.

**10.** Behold, I too say that those who are so proud in their wisdom that they think so much is to be attributed to the powers of their own will that they deny that divine help is necessary to them for living well cannot believe in Christ. For the syllables of the name of Christ, and the sacraments of Christ, profit nothing where faith in Christ is resisted. And faith in Christ is to believe in him who justifies the ungodly; to believe in the Mediator, without whose interposition we are not reconciled to God; to believe in the Savior, who came to seek and to save what was lost; to believe in him who said: *Without me you can do nothing*[^20].

Because, then, one who is ignorant of the righteousness of God, by which the ungodly is justified, wishes to establish his own, by which the proud man is convicted, he cannot believe in this one. Hence those too *could not believe*: not because men cannot be changed for the better; but so long as they are minded in such ways, they cannot believe. Hence they are blinded and hardened; because, by denying divine help, they are not helped. This God foreknew of the Jews who were blinded and hardened, and the Prophet foretold it in his Spirit.

**11.** But as for what he added: *And be converted and I heal them*: whether a "not" is to be understood, that is, that they be not converted, the sentence above being connected, where it was said, *that they may not see with their eyes and understand with their heart*; since here too it was surely said, that they may not understand? For conversion itself is of his grace, to whom it is said: *O God of hosts, convert us*[^21]. Or perhaps this too is to be understood as done by the mercy of the heavenly physician, so that, since they were of a proud and perverse will, and wished to establish their own righteousness, they were forsaken to this end, that they should be blinded; were blinded to this end, that they should stumble against the stone of stumbling, and their face be filled with shame; and so, humbled, they might seek the name of the Lord, and not their own, by which the proud man is puffed up, but the righteousness of God, by which the ungodly is justified? For this turned out to good for many of them, who, pricked by their own wickedness, afterward believed in Christ: for whom he himself too had prayed, saying: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^22]. Of which ignorance of theirs the Apostle too says: *I bear them witness that they have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge*: for then he added this too, and said: *For being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, they are not subject to the righteousness of God*[^23].

## Let us cleanse our hearts through faith, that we may be prepared for the ineffable vision.

**12.** *These things Isaiah said, when he saw his glory, and spoke of him*[^24]. What Isaiah saw, and how this pertains to the Lord Christ, must be read and understood in his book. For he saw, not as he is, but in a certain significative manner, as the vision of the Prophet had to be shaped. For Moses too saw, and yet he said to him whom he was seeing: *If I have found grace before you, show me yourself, that I may see you manifestly*[^25]; because he did not see him as he is. But when this will be for us, the same holy John the Evangelist says in his Epistle: *Beloved, we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be: we know that when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^26]. He could have said, *for we shall see him,* and not added, *as he is*: but because he knew that he was seen by certain fathers and prophets, but not as he is, therefore, when he had said, *we shall see him,* he added, *as he is.*

For let none of those deceive you, brothers, who say that the Father is invisible and the Son visible. For those assert this who think him to be a creature; and they do not understand in what sense it was said: *I and the Father are one*[^27]. Indeed, in the form of God in which he is equal to the Father, the Son too is invisible: but that he might be seen by men, he took the form of a servant, and being made in the likeness of men, was made visible[^28]. He showed himself, then, even before he took on flesh, to the eyes of men, as he willed, in the creature subject to him, not as he is. Let us cleanse our hearts through faith, that we may be prepared for that ineffable and, so to speak, invisible vision. *For blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^29].

**13.** *Yet even of the rulers many believed in him; but on account of the Pharisees they did not confess it, lest they be put out of the synagogue: for they loved the glory of men more than the glory of God*[^30]. See how the Evangelist marked and disapproved of certain ones whom nonetheless he said had believed in him: who, if they had advanced in this entrance into faith, would by advancing have also overcome the love of human glory, which the Apostle had overcome, saying: *But far be it from me to glory, save in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world is crucified to me, and I to the world*[^31]. For to this end the Lord himself too fixed his cross, where the madness of proud impiety mocked him, on the foreheads of those who would believe in him, where there is, as it were, the seat of shame, that the faith may not blush for his name, and may love the glory of God more than that of men.
