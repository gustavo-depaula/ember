# Tractate 121

## *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father* (Jn 20:10-29).

## *Do not touch me.* Do not believe in me as you still think; do not stretch your understanding only as far as that I was made for you, without passing on to that through which you were made.

**1.** That the Lord had been taken from the tomb, Mary Magdalene had announced to his disciples Peter and John; and coming there, they found only the linen cloths in which the body had been wrapped; and what else could they believe but what she had said, what she too had believed? *So the disciples went away again to their own homes*[^1]: that is, to where they were living, and from where they had run to the tomb. *But Mary stood weeping outside at the tomb*[^2]. For while the men returned, a stronger affection fixed the weaker sex in the same place. And the eyes that had sought the Lord and not found him now had leisure for tears, grieving more that he had been taken from the tomb than that he had been killed on the wood; since not even the memory remained of so great a master, whose life had been taken from them. And so that grief now held the woman at the tomb.

*And as she wept, she stooped down and looked into the tomb*[^3]. Why she did this I do not know. For she did not fail to know that he whom she sought was no longer there; since she herself had announced to the disciples that he had been taken from there, and they had come to the tomb and had sought the body of the Lord not only by looking but even by entering, and had not found it. What, then, does she mean, that, weeping, she again stooped and looked into the tomb? Was it that, grieving too much, she thought it should be easily believed neither by her own eyes nor by theirs? Or was it rather brought about in her mind by a divine prompting that she should look? For she did look. *And she saw two angels in white, sitting, one at the head and one at the feet, where the body of Jesus had lain*[^4]. What does it mean that one sat at the head and the other at the feet? Was it that, since those who in Greek are called angels are in Latin messengers, they were in this way signifying that the gospel of Christ was to be announced, as it were, from the head to the feet, from the beginning to the end? *They said to her, Woman, why are you weeping? She said to them, Because they have taken away my Lord, and I do not know where they have laid him*[^5]. The angels were forbidding her tears: in which what else were they announcing but, in a manner, a joy to come? For they said thus, *Why are you weeping?*, as if they said: Do not weep. But she, thinking they had asked in ignorance, discloses the causes of her tears. *Because they have taken away,* she says, *my Lord*: calling the lifeless body of her Lord her Lord, signifying the part from the whole; just as we all confess Jesus Christ the only Son of God to be our Lord, who surely is at once both Word and soul and flesh, yet was crucified and buried, when only his flesh was buried. *And I do not know,* she says, *where they have laid him.* This was the greater cause of grief, that she did not know where to go to console her grief. But the hour had now come when joy should succeed her weeping for that which had in a manner been announced by the angels who forbade her to weep.

## In that woman the Church of the Gentiles was figured.

**2.** Finally, *when she had said this, she turned back and saw Jesus standing, and did not know that it was Jesus. Jesus said to her, Woman, why are you weeping? Whom do you seek? She, supposing he was the gardener, said to him, Sir, if you have carried him away, tell me where you have laid him, and I will take him away. Jesus said to her, Mary. Turning, she said to him, Rabboni, which means Master*[^6]. Let no one find fault with the woman for calling him "lord" as the gardener, and Jesus "master." For there she was asking, here she was recognizing; there she honored a man from whom she was begging a kindness; here she recalled the teacher from whom she was learning to distinguish things human and divine. She called "lord" one whose servant she was not, that through him she might come to the Lord whose she was.

So in one way she said "Lord"—*They have taken away my Lord*[^7]; in another way—*Sir, if you have carried him away*[^8]. For the prophets too called those "lords" who were men; but in another way him of whom it is written: *The Lord is his name*[^9].

But this woman, who had already turned back to see Jesus, when she thought he was the gardener and was surely speaking with him—how is she said to have turned again, so as to say to him, *Rabboni*—except that then, turned in body, she thought him to be what he was not; now, turned in heart, she recognized what he was?

**3.** *Jesus said to her, Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father; but go to my brethren and say to them, I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God*[^10]. There is in these words something that we ought to treat briefly indeed, yet rather attentively. For Jesus was teaching faith to the woman who had recognized and called him master, when he answered her these things; and that gardener was sowing in her heart, as in his own garden, the grain of mustard seed. What, then, is meant by *Do not touch me*? And, as if the cause of this prohibition were sought, he added, *for I have not yet ascended to my Father.* What is this? If, standing on earth, he is not touched, how would he be touched by men, seated in heaven? He who surely, before he ascended, offered himself to be touched by the disciples, saying, as Luke the evangelist attests: *Handle me and see, for a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see that I have*[^11]; or when he said to the disciple Thomas: *Put your finger here and see my hands, and bring your hand and put it into my side*[^12]. But who would be so absurd as to say that he wished to be touched by the disciples before he had ascended to the Father, but by the women did not, except when he had ascended to the Father? But not even thus, however willing one might be, would he be allowed to be foolish. For women too are read to have touched Jesus after the resurrection, before he ascended to the Father, among whom was this very Mary Magdalene, Matthew relating that Jesus met them, saying: *Hail. And they came up,* he says, *and took hold of his feet and worshiped him*[^13]. This was omitted by John, but truly stated by Matthew.

It remains, then, that some mystery lies hidden in these words; which, whether we find it or are quite unable to find it, we ought nonetheless in no way to doubt is there. Either, then, it was said—*Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father*—so that in that woman the Church of the Gentiles might be figured, which did not believe in Christ until he had ascended to the Father; or Jesus wished to be believed in—that is, to be touched spiritually—in this way: that he and the Father are one. For he in a manner ascends to the Father in the inmost senses of one who has so advanced in him as to acknowledge him equal to the Father; otherwise he is not rightly touched, that is, otherwise he is not rightly believed in. Now Mary could believe in such a way as to think him unequal to the Father, which is surely forbidden when it is said to her: *Do not touch me*: that is: Do not believe in me as you still think; do not stretch your understanding only as far as that I was made for you, without passing on to that through which you were made. For how was she not still believing in him carnally, whom she was weeping for as a man? *For I have not yet ascended,* he says, *to my Father*: there you will touch me, when you have believed me to be God not unequal to the Father.

*But go to my brethren and say to them, I am ascending to my Father and your Father.* He does not say: Our Father. In one way, then, mine; in another, yours: by nature mine, by grace yours. *And to my God and your God.* Nor did he say here: Our God. Therefore here too in one way mine, in another yours: my God, under whom I too am a man; your God, between whom and you I am the mediator.

**4.** *Mary Magdalene came, announcing to the disciples, I have seen the Lord, and he said these things to me. So when it was evening on that day, the first day of the week, and the doors were shut where the disciples were gathered for fear of the Jews, Jesus came and stood in the midst and said to them, Peace be with you. And when he had said this, he showed them his hands and his side*[^14]. For the nails had pierced his hands, the lance had opened his side: there, to heal the hearts of the doubting, the marks of the wounds were preserved. But the shut doors were no obstacle to the mass of the body in which the Godhead was. For he could enter though they were not opened, by whose birth the virginity of his mother remained inviolate.

*The disciples therefore rejoiced when they saw the Lord. He said to them again, Peace be with you*[^15]. The repetition is a confirmation; for he himself gives, through the prophet, the promised peace upon peace. *As the Father has sent me,* he says, *even so I send you*[^16]. We know the Son to be equal to the Father, but here we recognize the words of the Mediator. For he shows himself to be in the middle by saying: He me, and I you. *When he had said this, he breathed on them and said to them, Receive the Holy Spirit*[^17]. By breathing he signified that the Holy Spirit is the Spirit not of the Father alone, but also his own. *Whose sins you forgive,* he says, *they are forgiven them; and whose sins you retain, they are retained*[^18]. The love of the Church, which is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, forgives the sins of those who share in it, but retains the sins of those who do not share in it. Therefore, after he had said, *Receive the Holy Spirit*, he at once added this concerning the forgiving and retaining of sins.

**5.** *Now Thomas, one of the twelve, called the Twin, was not with them when Jesus came. So the other disciples said to him, We have seen the Lord. But he said to them, Unless I see in his hands the print of the nails, and put my finger in the place of the nails, and put my hand into his side, I will not believe. And after eight days his disciples were again inside, and Thomas with them. Jesus came, the doors being shut, and stood in the midst and said, Peace be with you. Then he said to Thomas, Put your finger here and see my hands, and bring your hand and put it into my side; and do not be unbelieving, but believing. Thomas answered and said to him, My Lord and my God*[^19]. He saw and touched the man, and confessed the God whom he neither saw nor touched; but through what he saw and touched, he now, with doubt removed, believed that.

*Jesus said to him, Because you have seen me, you have believed*[^20]. He did not say, you have touched me, but, *you have seen me*: since sight is in a way the general sense. For it is wont to be named also of the other four senses: as when we say, Hear and see how well it sounds, smell and see how good it smells, taste and see how good it tastes, touch and see how warm it is. Everywhere it has sounded: See; though sight properly is not denied to belong to the eyes. Hence here too the Lord himself: *Put,* he says, *your finger here and see my hands*: what else does he say but, Touch and see? And yet he had no eyes in his finger. Therefore, whether by gazing, or also by touching: *Because you have seen me,* he says, *you have believed.* Although it can be said that the disciple did not dare to touch, when he offered himself to be touched; for it is not written: And Thomas touched. But whether he saw and believed only by looking, or also by touching, what follows commends and praises rather the faith of the Gentiles: *Blessed are those who have not seen and yet have believed*[^21]. He used words of past time, as one who knew that what was to be future was, in his predestination, already done.

But now this homily must be restrained from prolixity: the Lord will grant that we discuss what remains another time.
