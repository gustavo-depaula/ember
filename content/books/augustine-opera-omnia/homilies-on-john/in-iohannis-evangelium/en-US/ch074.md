# Tractate 74

## *I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Paraclete* (Jn 14:15-17).

## He who loves has the Holy Spirit, and by having him deserves to have more, and by having more loves more.

**1.** We have heard, brothers, when the Gospel was read, the Lord saying: *If you love me, keep my commandments: and I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Paraclete, that he may abide with you forever, the Spirit of truth, whom the world cannot receive; because it does not see him, nor know him. But you will know him, because he will abide with you, and will be in you*[^1]. Many are the things that may be sought in these few words of the Lord; but it is much for us either to seek all that are to be sought here, or to find all that we seek here. Yet, as far as the Lord deigns to grant us, according to our and your capacity, attending to what we ought to say, and what you ought to hear, take from us, dearly beloved, what we can give, and ask from him what we cannot.

Christ promised the Spirit the Paraclete to the Apostles; but in what manner he promised, let us observe. *If you love me,* he says, *keep my commandments: and I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Paraclete, that he may abide with you forever, the Spirit of truth.* This surely is, in the Trinity, the Holy Spirit, whom the catholic faith confesses to be consubstantial and coeternal with the Father and the Son: he it is of whom the Apostle says: *The love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who is given to us*[^2]. How, then, does the Lord say: *If you love me, keep my commandments: and I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Paraclete*; when he says this of the Holy Spirit, whom unless we have, we can neither love God nor keep his commandments? How do we love in order to receive him, whom unless we have, we are not able to love? Or how shall we keep the commandments in order to receive him, whom unless we have, we cannot keep the commandments? Or does the love precede in us by which we love Christ, so that by loving Christ and doing his commandments, we may deserve to receive the Holy Spirit, that the love, not of Christ, which had already preceded, but of God the Father may be poured out *in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who is given to us?*

This is a perverse opinion. For he who believes that he loves the Son, and does not love the Father; surely does not love even the Son, but something he has feigned for himself. Then it is the apostolic word: *No one says: The Lord Jesus, except in the Holy Spirit*[^3]: and who says, The Lord Jesus, but he who loves him, if he says it in the way the Apostle wished it to be understood? For many say it with the voice, but with the heart and deeds deny it: as he says of such: *For they confess that they know God, but by their deeds deny him*[^4]. If he is denied by deeds, without doubt he is also said by deeds. *No one,* then, *says: The Lord Jesus,* in mind, in word, in deed, in heart, in mouth, in work, *no one says: The Lord Jesus, except in the Holy Spirit*; and no one says it so, except one who loves. Already, then, the Apostles were saying: *The Lord Jesus*: and if they said it in such a way as not to say it feignedly, confessing with the mouth, but with heart and deeds denying; surely if they said it truly, without doubt they loved. How, then, did they love, except in the Holy Spirit? And yet they are first commanded to love him, and to keep his commandments, in order to receive the Holy Spirit: whom unless they had, they surely could not love and keep his commandments.

## The Holy Spirit is promised even to one who has him.

**2.** It remains, then, that we understand that he who loves has the Holy Spirit, and by having him deserves to have more, and by having more loves more. Already, then, the disciples had the Spirit whom the Lord was promising, without whom they did not say, The Lord; and yet they did not yet have him as the Lord was promising him. Both, then, they had and did not have, who did not yet have him as much as he was to be had. They had him, then, in less measure, and he was to be given them more abundantly. They had him secretly, they were to receive him openly; for this too pertained to the greater gift of the Holy Spirit, that what they had should become known to them. Speaking of which gift, the Apostle says: *But we have received not the spirit of this world, but the Spirit who is from God, that we may know the things that are given us by God*[^5].

For the Lord performed the very manifest imparting of the Holy Spirit not once, but twice in number. For as soon as he rose from the dead, breathing on them he said: *Receive the Holy Spirit*[^6]. Did he, then, because he gave him then, therefore not send afterward the one whom he promised? Or is he not the same Holy Spirit, who was both then breathed by him, and afterward sent by him from heaven? Wherefore, why that very giving of him which was done manifestly was done twice, is another question: for perhaps on account of the two commandments of love, that is, of neighbor and of God, that it might be commended that love pertains to the Holy Spirit, this twofold giving of him was done in the manifestation. And if another cause is to be sought, this discourse must not now be sent into a longer measure than it ought by inquiry into it: provided, however, it be established that without the Holy Spirit we cannot love Christ and keep his commandments; and that we are able to do and do this the less, the less we receive him; but the more, the more we receive him. Accordingly, it is promised not in vain not only to one who does not have him, but also to one who has him: to one who does not have him, indeed, that he may have him; but to one who has him, that he may have him more abundantly. For unless one had less, another more, holy Elisha would not have said to holy Elijah: *Let the spirit that is in you be doubled in me*[^7].

**3.** But when John the Baptist says: *For God does not give the Spirit by measure*[^8], he was speaking of the very Son of God, to whom the Spirit was not given by measure; because in him dwells all the fullness of divinity[^9]. For not without the grace of the Holy Spirit is the mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus: for he himself says that the prophetic word was fulfilled in him: *The Spirit of the Lord is upon me; on account of which he anointed me, he sent me to preach the gospel to the poor*[^10]. For that he is the Only-begotten equal to the Father is not of grace, but of nature: but that man was assumed into the unity of the person of the Only-begotten is of grace, not of nature, the Gospel confessing and saying: *But the child grew and was strengthened, full of wisdom, and the grace of God was in him*[^11]. But to the rest it is given by measure, and given it is increased, until to each one his own measure be filled according to the manner of his perfection. Whence the Apostle too admonishes: *Not to be wise more than one ought to be wise, but to be wise unto soberness; to each one as God has apportioned the measure of faith*[^12]. For the Spirit himself is not divided, but the gifts through the Spirit: for *there are divisions of gifts, but the same Spirit*[^13].

**4.** But as for what he says: *I will ask the Father, and he will give you another Paraclete,* he shows that he himself too is a paraclete. For "paraclete" in Latin is called "advocate": and it was said of Christ: *We have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous*[^14]. But he said that the world cannot receive the Holy Spirit, just as it was also said: *The prudence of the flesh is enmity against God: for it is not subject to the law of God; for it cannot be*[^15]; as if we should say: Injustice cannot be just. For he means here by "the world" the lovers of the world, which love is not of the Father. And therefore to the love of this world, which we labor enough to have lessened and consumed in us, is contrary the love of God, which is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who is given to us. *The world,* then, *cannot receive him, because it does not see him, nor know him.* For worldly love does not have the invisible eyes through which the Holy Spirit, who cannot be seen except invisibly, can be seen.

**5.** *But you,* he says, *will know him, because he will abide with you, and will be in you.* He will be in them in order to abide, he will not abide in order to be: for to be somewhere is prior to abiding. But lest they should think that what was said, *he will abide with you,* was said in the way a guest is wont visibly to abide with a man; he expounded what he meant by *he will abide with you,* when he added and said, *he will be in you.* Therefore he is seen invisibly: nor, if he is not in us, can the knowledge of him be in us. For so by us is seen in us our own conscience too: for we see another's face, our own we cannot see; but our own conscience we see, another's we do not see. But conscience never is, except in us: but the Holy Spirit can be even without us; for he is given that he may be also in us. But to be seen and known as he is to be seen and known, he cannot be by us, if he is not in us.
