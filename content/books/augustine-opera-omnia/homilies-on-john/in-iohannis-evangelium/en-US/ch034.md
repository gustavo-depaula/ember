# Tractate 34

## *He who follows me will have the light of life* (Jn 8:12).

##  — He who shines for you that you may see, the same flows for you that you may drink. Abiding with the Father, truth and life; clothing himself in flesh, he became the way. Rise! the way itself has come to you. Rise and walk!

**1.** What we have just now heard and attentively received, when the holy Gospel was read, I do not doubt that we all tried also to understand: and each of us, concerning so great a matter as was read, took according to his own measure what he could; and the bread of the word being set forth, there is no one who complains that he tasted nothing. But again I do not doubt that it is hard to find anyone who has understood the whole. Yet even if there is one who understands well enough all the words of our Lord Jesus Christ just now recited from the Gospel, let him bear with our ministry, until, if we can, with his help, by treating it we may bring it about that either all or many understand what few rejoice to have understood.

## Christ is the light of the world, and with him is the fountain of life.

**2.** What the Lord says: *I am the light of the world*[^1], I think is clear to those who have eyes whereby they may become partakers of this light: but those who have eyes only in the flesh marvel at what was said by the Lord Jesus Christ: I am the light of the world. And perhaps there is not lacking one who says within himself: Is the Lord Christ perhaps this sun, which by its rising and setting carries out the day? For there have not been lacking heretics who thought such things. The Manichees thought that this sun, visible to the eyes of the flesh, exposed and public for seeing not only to men but even to cattle, was Christ the Lord. But the right faith of the catholic Church disapproves such a fancy, and recognizes it to be a diabolical teaching: nor does it only recognize this by believing, but in those whom it can, it also convicts it by disputing.

Let us therefore disapprove an error of this kind, which the holy Church from the beginning anathematized. Let us not think that the Lord Jesus Christ is this sun which we see rise in the east, set in the west; whose course night follows, whose rays are overshadowed by a cloud, which migrates by a fixed motion from place to place: this is not the Lord Christ. The Lord Christ is not a sun that was made, but the one through whom the sun was made. For *all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^2].

**3.** There is, then, a light that made this light: let us love this light, let us desire to understand it, let us thirst for it; that to it, with itself as guide, we may at last come, and in it so live that we may never at all die. For this is the light of which the prophecy, sent forth of old, sang thus in the Psalm: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord; as your mercy has been multiplied, O God*[^3]. These are the words of the holy Psalm: notice what of such a light the ancient word of God's holy men sent forth beforehand. *Men*, it says, *and beasts you will save, O Lord; as your mercy has been multiplied, O God.*

For since you are God, and have a manifold mercy; the same manifoldness of your mercy reaches, not only to men whom you created in your image, but even to the cattle which you subjected to men. For from whom is the soundness of man, from him also the soundness of the beast. Do not be ashamed to think this of the Lord your God: rather presume and trust, and beware lest you think otherwise. He who makes you sound, the same makes your horse sound, the same your sheep; let us come to the very least—the same your hen: *soundness is the Lord's*[^4], and God saves these things.

It moves you, you ask; I wonder why you doubt. Will he disdain to save, who deigned to create? Soundness is the Lord's—of angels, of men, of beasts; soundness is the Lord's. Just as no one is from himself, so no one is sound from himself. Accordingly most truly and most excellently the Psalm says: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord.* Why? *As your mercy has been multiplied, O God.* For you are God, you created, you save: you gave being, you give well-being.

## Drink and live.

**4.** If, then, as the mercy of God has been multiplied, men and beasts are saved by him; do not men have something else that God the creator bestows on them, which he does not bestow on beasts? Is there no distinction between an animal made in the image of God, and an animal subject to the image of God? There is plainly: besides this soundness common to us with the dumb animals, there is something that God bestows on us, but does not bestow on them.

What is this?

Follow on in the same psalm: *But the sons of men will hope under the covering of your wings*[^5]. Having now a soundness common with their beasts: The sons of men will hope under the covering of your wings. They have one soundness in reality, another in hope. This soundness which is in the present is common to men and beasts: but there is another which men hope for; and those who hope receive it, those who despair do not receive it. For the sons of men, it says, will hope under the covering of your wings.

But those who hope perseveringly are protected by you, lest they be cast down from hope by the devil: they will hope under the covering of your wings. If, then, they will hope, what will they hope for, except what the beasts will not have? *They will be inebriated with the abundance of your house; and you will give them to drink of the torrent of your delight*[^6]. What sort of wine is it, by which to be inebriated is praiseworthy? What sort of wine is it, which does not disturb, but directs the mind? What sort of wine is it, which makes one perpetually sound, not by inebriating makes one insane? They will be inebriated.

With what?

*With the abundance of your house; and you will give them to drink of the torrent of your delight.*

Whence?

*For with you is the fountain of life*[^7]. The fountain of life itself was walking on earth, he himself was saying: *Whoever thirsts, let him come to me*[^8]. Behold the fountain.

But we had begun to speak of the light, and we were treating the question set forth from the Gospel about the light. For it was read to us, the Lord saying: I am the light of the world. Hence the question, lest anyone with carnal mind think that this sun was to be understood: we came thence to the Psalm, and considering it, we found meanwhile that the Lord is the fountain of life. Drink and live. With you, it says, is the fountain of life: therefore under the shadow of your wings the sons of men hope, seeking to be inebriated with this fountain. But we were speaking of the light. Follow on, then: for the Prophet, when he had said, With you is the fountain of life, went on and added: *In your light we shall see light*[^9]; God from God, light from light. Through this light the light of the sun was made: and the light that made the sun, under which it also made us, was made under the sun for our sake. It was made, I say, for our sake under the sun, the light that made the sun. Do not despise the cloud of the flesh: he is covered by a cloud, not that he may be darkened, but that he may be tempered.

**5.** Speaking, then, through the cloud of the flesh, the unfailing light, the light of wisdom, says to men: *I am the light of the world: he who follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life*[^10]. How he took you away from the eyes of the flesh, and called you back to the eyes of the heart! For it is not enough to say: He who follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have light; for he added, of life; as it was said there: For with you is the fountain of life. See therefore, my brothers, how the words of the Lord agree with the truth of that psalm: both there light was placed with the fountain of life, and by the Lord it was called the light of life.

But in these bodily uses, light is one thing, the fountain another: the throat seeks the fountain, the eyes the light: when we thirst we seek the fountain; when we are in darkness, we seek the light; and if perhaps we thirst at night, we light a lamp that we may come to the fountain. Not so with God: what is the light, this is the fountain; he who shines for you that you may see, the same flows for you that you may drink.

**6.** You see, then, my brothers, you see, if you see within, what this light is of which the Lord says: He who follows me will not walk in darkness. Follow this sun, let us see whether you will not walk in darkness. Behold, by rising it comes out to you: it goes by its course to the west; your journey perhaps is to the east: unless you go in the contrary direction, not where it tends, by following it you will surely go astray, and instead of the east you will hold to the west. If you follow it on land, you will go astray: a sailor, if he follows it on the sea, will go astray.

Finally, it seems to you that the sun is to be followed, and you yourself too tend to the west, where it also tends: let us see, when it has set, whether you will not walk in darkness. See how, even if you are unwilling to desert it, it will itself desert you, carrying out its day by the necessity of its servitude. But our Lord Jesus Christ, meanwhile, even when through the cloud of the flesh he did not appear to all, held all things through the power of wisdom. Your God is everywhere whole: if you do not make a fall from him, he himself never makes a setting from you.

## From whom do you ask God, except from God?

**7.** *He who follows me*, he says, *will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life.* What he promised, he put in a word of future time: for he did not say, he has; but, he will have, the light of life. Yet he did not say, he who will follow me; but, he who follows me. In what we ought to do, he put the present time: but what he promised to those who do it, he signified by a word of future time. He who follows will have. Now he follows, afterward he will have: now he follows by faith, afterward he will have by sight. For as long as we are in the body, says the Apostle, *we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord: for we walk by faith, not by sight*[^11].

When by sight?

When we shall have the light of life, when we shall have come to that vision, when this night shall have passed away.

For of that day which is to dawn, it was said: *In the morning I will stand before you, and I will contemplate*[^12].

What is, in the morning?

When the night of this world is past, when the terrors of temptations are past, when that lion is overcome who, roaring by night, goes about *seeking whom he may devour*[^13]. In the morning I will stand before you, and I will contemplate. But now what do we think, brothers, befits this time, except what again is said in the Psalm: *I will wash my bed every night, I will water my couch with my tears*[^14]? Every night, it says, I will weep: I will burn with desire of the light. The Lord sees my desire; for another psalm says to him: *Before you is all my desire, and my groaning is not hidden from you*[^15]. Do you desire gold? you can be seen; for seeking gold you will be manifest to men. Do you desire grain? you ask who has it; and to him, desiring to come to what you desire, you make it known. Do you desire God? who sees, except God? For from whom do you ask God, as bread, as water, as gold, as silver, as grain? From whom do you ask God, except from God? He himself is asked from himself, who promises himself.

Let the soul stretch out its desire; and with a more capacious bosom let it seek to comprehend *what the eye has not seen, nor the ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man*[^16]. It can be desired, it can be longed for, it can be sighed after: it cannot be worthily thought, nor explained in words.

**8.** Therefore, my brothers, since the Lord briefly says: I am the light of the world; he who follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life: in which words there is one thing he commanded, another he promised: let us do what he commanded, lest with shameless face we desire what he promised; lest he say to us in his judgment:

Have you then done what I commanded, that you should seek what I promised?

What, then, have you commanded, O Lord our God?

He says to you: That you should follow me.

You asked counsel of life. Of what life, except that of which it was said: With you is the fountain of life? A certain man heard: *Go, sell all that you have, and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me*[^17]. He went away sad, he did not follow: he sought a good master, he appealed to a teacher, and he despised the one teaching: he went away sad, bound by his desires; he went away sad, having a great burden of avarice on his shoulders. He toiled, he was hot; and the one who wished to take the burden from him was thought not to be followed, but to be forsaken.

But afterward, when the Lord cried out through the Gospel: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you; take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am meek and humble of heart*[^18]: how many, on hearing the Gospel, did what that rich man, hearing it from his own mouth, did not do? Let us, then, do it now, let us follow the Lord; let us loose the fetters by which we are hindered from following. And who is fit to loose such knots, unless he helps to whom it was said: *You have broken my bonds*[^19]? Of whom another psalm says: *The Lord looses the fettered, the Lord raises up the crushed*[^20].

**9.** And what do the loosed and raised up follow, except the light from whom they hear: I am the light of the world; he who follows me will not walk in darkness? Because the Lord enlightens the blind. We are enlightened, then, now, brothers, having the eye-salve of faith. For his saliva went before with earth, with which he who was born blind might be anointed[^21]. We too are born blind from Adam, and have need of him to enlighten us. He mingled saliva with earth: *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^22]. He mingled saliva with earth; therefore it was foretold: *Truth has arisen from the earth*[^23]: but he himself said: *I am the way, the truth, and the life*[^24].

We shall fully enjoy the truth, when we shall see face to face; for this too is promised us. For who would dare to hope what God did not deign either to promise or to give? We shall see face to face. The Apostle says: *Now I know in part, now in a riddle through a mirror, but then face to face*[^25]. And the apostle John in his Epistle: *Beloved, now we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be: we know that when he appears, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is*[^26]. This is a great promise; if you love, follow.

I love, you say; but by what way do I follow?

If the Lord your God had said to you: I am the truth and the life; desiring the truth, longing for life, you would surely seek the way by which you could come to these, and you would say to yourself: A great thing is truth, a great thing is life; if only there were a way by which my soul might come there! You ask by what way? Hear him saying first: *I am the way.* Before he said to you where, he set first the way: *I am*, he says, *the way.*

The way to where?

*And the truth and the life.* First he said by what way you are to come, afterward he said to where you are to come.

I am the way, I am the truth, I am the life. Abiding with the Father, truth and life: clothing himself in flesh, he became the way. It is not said to you: Labor by seeking the way, that you may come to the truth and the life; this is not said to you. Sluggard, rise; the way itself has come to you, and has roused you from sleep as you slept, if indeed it has roused you: rise, and walk. Perhaps you try to walk, and cannot, because your feet hurt. Why do your feet hurt? Have they, at the bidding of avarice, run over rough places? But the Word of God has healed the lame too.

Behold, you say, I have sound feet, but I do not see the way itself.

He has enlightened the blind too.

**10.** All this by faith, as long as we are on pilgrimage away from the Lord, abiding in the body: but when we shall have walked through the way, and come to the homeland itself, what will be more joyful for us? what will be more blessed for us? Because nothing will be more peaceful: for nothing will rebel against man.

But now, brothers, it is hard for us to be without quarrel. We are indeed called to concord, we are commanded to have peace among ourselves; toward this we must strive, and with all our strength struggle, that we may at last come to the most perfect peace: but now we for the most part quarrel with those for whom we wish to look out.

He goes astray, you wish to lead him to the way; he resists you, you quarrel: the pagan resists, you dispute against the errors of idols and demons: the heretic resists, you dispute against other teachings of demons: the bad catholic does not wish to live well, you rebuke even your inner brother; he stays with you in the house, and seeks ruinous ways; you are anxious how to correct him, that you may render a good account of him to the Lord of you both.

How many necessities of quarrels on every side! Often a man, afflicted with weariness, says within himself: What is it to me to bear with contradictors, to bear with those who return evil for good? I wish to look out for them, they wish to perish: I consume my life in quarreling; I have no peace: I moreover make enemies of those whom I ought to have as friends, if they would attend to the benevolence of one looking out for them: what is it to me to endure these things? Let me return to myself, I will be with myself, I will call upon my God.

Return to yourself, there you find a quarrel: if you have begun to follow God, there you find a quarrel.

What quarrel, you say, do I find?

*The flesh lusts against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh*[^27]. Behold, you are yourself, behold, you are alone, behold, you are with yourself, behold, you suffer no other man: but you see *another law in your members, fighting against the law of your mind, and making you captive in the law of sin, which is in your members*. Cry out, then, and from the inner quarrel cry to God, that he may pacify you to yourself: *Wretched man that I am, who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^28]. Because he says: He who follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life. When the whole quarrel is ended, immortality will follow, because *the last enemy, death, will be destroyed*[^29]. And what sort of peace will there be? *This corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality*[^30]. That we may come to this—since then it will be in reality—now let us follow in hope him who said: I am the light of the world: he who follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life.
