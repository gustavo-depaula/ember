# Tractate 39

## *He who sent me is true: I speak in the world the things that I have heard from him* (Jn 8:26).

## If the love of God, poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us, makes many souls one soul, and makes many hearts one heart, how much more the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God, one light, and one beginning?

**1.** The words of our Lord Jesus Christ which he had with the Jews, so tempering his speech that the blind might not see, and the faithful might open their eyes, which today were recited from the holy Gospel, are these: *The Jews then said: Who are you?*[^1] Because the Lord had said above: Unless you believe that I am, you will die in your sins[^2]. To this, then, they: Who are you? as though seeking to know in whom they ought to believe, lest they die in their sin. He answered those saying: Who are you? and said: The beginning, because I also speak to you[^3].

If the Lord said himself to be the beginning, it can be asked whether the Father too is a beginning. For if the Son is a beginning who has a Father, how much more easily is God the Father to be understood to be a beginning, who indeed has a Son to whom he is Father, but does not have one from whom he is? For the Son is the Son of the Father, and the Father surely is the Father of the Son: but the Son is called God from God, the Son is called light from light: the Father is called light, but not from light; the Father is called God, but not from God. If, then, God from God, light from light, is a beginning; how much more easily is understood to be a beginning the light from which is light, and the God from which is God? It seems, therefore, absurd, dearly beloved, that we should call the Son a beginning, and not call the Father a beginning.

**2.** But what shall we do? will there be two beginnings? This is to be guarded against saying. What then? if both the Father is a beginning and the Son is a beginning, how are there not two beginnings? In the way we say the Father is God and the Son is God, and yet do not say two gods. For it is forbidden to say two gods, forbidden to say three gods: and yet he who is the Father is not the Son; he who is the Son is not the Father: but the Holy Spirit, the Spirit of the Father and of the Son, is neither the Father nor the Son.

Although, then, as catholic ears have been instructed in the bosom of mother Church, neither is he who is the Father the Son, nor is he who is the Son the Father, nor is the Holy Spirit of the Father and of the Son either Son or Father, yet we do not say that there are three gods: although, if it be asked of the individuals, it is necessary that of whomever we are questioned we confess to be God.

## From the foundation of faith to the summit of perfection.

**3.** And these things seem absurd to men who draw the accustomed to the unaccustomed, the visible to the invisible, comparing the creature to the Creator. For sometimes unbelievers question us and say: The Father whom you speak of, do you call him God?

We answer: God.

The Son whom you speak of, do you call him God?

We answer: God.

The Holy Spirit whom you speak of, do you call him God?

We answer: God.

Therefore, they say, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are three gods?

We answer: No.

They are troubled, because they are not enlightened: they have a closed heart, because they do not have the key of faith. We, then, brothers, with faith going before, which heals the eye of our heart, let us grasp without obscurity what we understand; what we do not understand, let us believe without doubting: let us not withdraw from the foundation of faith, that we may come to the summit of perfection. God is the Father, God is the Son, God is the Holy Spirit: and yet he who is the Son is not the Father, nor is he who is the Father the Son, nor is the Holy Spirit of the Father and of the Son either Father or Son. The Trinity is one God: the Trinity, one eternity, one power, one majesty; three, but not gods.

Let not the calumniator answer me: What then, three? For if three, he says, you ought to say what three.

I answer: The Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit.

Behold, he says, you have said three; but express what three.

Rather you count: for I complete three, when I say, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. For that which the Father is in respect to himself, is God; what he is in respect to the Son, is Father: what the Son is in respect to himself, is God; what he is in respect to the Father, is Son.

**4.** These things that I say you can recognize from daily likenesses. A man and another man, if one is father, the other son; what is man, is in respect to himself; what is father, is in respect to the son: and the son, what is man, is in respect to himself; but what is son, is in respect to the father. For "father" is a name said in relation to something, and "son" in relation to something: but these two are men. But God the Father is Father in relation to something, that is, to the Son; and God the Son is Son in relation to something, that is, to the Father: but not as those two are men, are these two gods.

Why is this not so there?

For the one is one thing, and the other is another—because that nature is divinity. There is something ineffable there, which cannot be unfolded in words, so that it both is a number and is not a number. For see whether the number does not, as it were, appear: Father and Son and Holy Spirit, a Trinity. If three, three what? The number fails. So God neither departs from number nor is grasped by number. Because they are three, there is, as it were, a number; if you ask what the three are, there is no number. Hence it is said, *Great is our Lord and great is his power, and of his wisdom there is no number*[^4]. Where you begin to think, you begin to count; where you have counted, you cannot answer what you have counted. The Father is the Father; the Son is the Son; the Holy Spirit is the Holy Spirit:

what are these three, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit? Not three gods?

No.

Not three almighties?

No.

Not three creators of the world?

No.

Is the Father, then, almighty?

Almighty, plainly.

Then is the Son not almighty?

Plainly the Son too is almighty.

Then is the Holy Spirit not almighty?

He too is almighty.

Three almighties, then?

No; but one almighty. They suggest number only in this, that they are related to one another, not in what they are in themselves. For because God the Father, in himself, is God together with the Son and the Holy Spirit, they are not three gods; because in himself he is almighty together with the Son and the Holy Spirit, they are not three almighties. But because the Father is not in relation to himself but in relation to the Son, and the Son is not in relation to himself but in relation to the Father, and the Spirit is not in relation to himself, in that he is called the Spirit of the Father and the Son, there is no way I can speak of three, except as Father and Son and Holy Spirit, one God, one almighty. Therefore, one origin.

## From this acknowledge the mystery of the Trinity.

**5.** Take something from the holy Scriptures, by which you may somehow grasp what is being said. After our Lord Jesus Christ had risen, and when he willed, ascended into heaven, ten days having been completed there, he sent from there the Holy Spirit; and those who were present in one room, filled with him, began to speak in the tongues of all nations[^5]. Terrified by the miracle, the Lord's slayers were stung with grief; grieving, they were changed; changed, they believed. There were added to the body of the Lord, that is, to the number of the faithful, three thousand persons. Likewise, when another miracle had been worked, another five thousand were added; a single people, no small one, was formed; and in it all, having received the Holy Spirit, by whom spiritual love was kindled, brought together into one by that very love and by the fervor of the Spirit, began, in the very unity of their fellowship, to sell all that they had and to lay the proceeds at the feet of the Apostles, that it might be distributed to each as anyone had need. And of them Scripture says this, that they had *one soul and one heart toward God*[^6].

Attend, then, brothers, and from this acknowledge the mystery of the Trinity—how we say: the Father is, and the Son is, and the Holy Spirit is, and yet there is one God. See, those many thousands were, and there was one heart; see, so many thousands were, and there was one soul. But where? In God. How much more is God himself one? Do I err in speech when I say that two men have two souls, or three men three souls, or many men many souls? I speak rightly, surely. Let them draw near to God: the soul of them all is one. If, when drawing near to God, many souls become one soul through love, and many hearts one heart, what does the very fount of love itself accomplish in the Father and the Son? Is the Trinity not there all the more one God? For from there love comes to us, from the Holy Spirit himself, as the Apostle says: *the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^7]. If, therefore, the love of God poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us makes many souls one soul and makes many hearts one heart, how much more do the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, one God, one light, and one origin?

**6.** Let us hear, then, the origin that speaks to us. *I have many things,* he says, *to speak and to judge concerning you*[^8]. You remember that he said, *I judge no one*[^9]; see, now he says, *I have many things to speak and to judge concerning you*. But it is one thing to say, I judge not; another, I have to judge. I judge not, he said, for the present; for he had come to save the world, not to judge the world[^10]. But when he says, *I have many things to speak and to judge concerning you*, he speaks of the judgment to come. For he ascended for this, that he might come to judge the living and the dead. No one will judge more justly than he who was judged unjustly.

*I have many things,* he says, *to speak and to judge concerning you: but he who sent me is true*[^11]. See how the equal Son gives glory to the Father. For he sets us an example, and, as it were, speaks within our hearts: O faithful man, if you hear my Gospel, the Lord your God says to you: where I, in the beginning the Word, God with God, equal to the Father, coeternal with the one who begot me, give glory to him whose Son I am, how are you proud against him whose slave you are?

**7.** *I have many things,* he says, *to speak and to judge concerning you: but he who sent me is true*[^12]—as though he were saying: I judge truly for this reason, that I am the truth of the one who is true. The Father is true, the Son is truth; which do we think is the greater? Let us consider, if we can, which is greater, the true or the truth. Let us inquire about certain things. Is a devout man more, or devotion? But devotion itself is more; for the devout man comes from devotion, not devotion from the devout man. For devotion can exist even if the one who was devout has become undevout. He himself lost devotion; he took nothing away from devotion. Again, what of the beautiful man and beauty? Beauty is more than the beautiful man; for beauty makes a thing beautiful, the beautiful man does not make beauty. The chaste man and chastity? Chastity is plainly more than the chaste man. For if there were no chastity, this chaste man would not have where to draw his chastity from; but if he should be unwilling to be chaste, chastity remains whole and entire.

If, then, devotion is more than the devout man, beauty more than the beautiful man, chastity more than the chaste man, are we going to say that truth is more than the true one? If we say this, we will begin to say that the Son is greater than the Father. For the Lord himself says most plainly: *I am the way and the truth and the life*[^13]. If, then, the Son is truth, what is the Father, except what the truth itself says: *He who sent me is true*? The Son is truth, the Father is true; I ask which is greater, but I find equality. For the Father is true not from a truth of which he has received a part, but from the truth which he wholly begot.

## God is true not by participating in truth, but by begetting it.

**8.** I see that it must be put more plainly. And surely, that I may not keep you long, let it be treated this far today; when I have finished what I wish to say, with God's help, let the sermon close. I have said this in order to make you attentive. Every soul, since it is a changeable thing—and though a great creation, still a creation; though better than a body, still made—every soul, then, since it is changeable, that is, now believes, now does not believe; now wills, now does not will; now is adulterous, now chaste; now good, now evil—is changeable. But God is what he is; therefore he kept for himself the proper name: *I am who am*[^14]. This is what the Son is, when he says: *Unless you believe that I am*; to this also belongs: *Who are you?*[^15] The Origin. God, therefore, is unchangeable, the soul changeable.

When the soul takes from God that whereby it is good, it becomes good by participating, just as your eye sees by participating. For when the light is withdrawn it does not see, but, made a partaker of it, it sees. Because, then, the soul becomes good by participating, if, being changed, it begins to be evil, the goodness of which it was a partaker remains, since it was good. For it was made a partaker of a certain goodness when it was good; and when it has been changed for the worse, that goodness remains whole. If the soul falls away and becomes evil, the goodness is not diminished; if it returns and becomes good, the goodness is not increased.

Your eye was made a partaker of this light, and you see; is it closed? You have not diminished this light; is it open? You have not increased this light. By this comparison, brothers, understand that if the soul is devout, there is devotion with God, of which the soul becomes a partaker; if the soul is chaste, there is chastity with God, of which the soul is a partaker; if the soul is good, there is goodness with God, of which the soul is a partaker; if the soul is true, there is truth with God, of which the soul is a partaker. And if the soul has not been a partaker of it, *every man is a liar*[^16]; if every man is a liar, no man is true of his own.

But the Father is true, true of his own, because he begot truth. It is one thing to say: this man is true, because he has now received truth; another to say: God is true, because he begot truth. See how God is true not by participating, but by begetting truth. I see that you have understood, and I rejoice; let this suffice for you today; the rest, when it pleases the Lord, as he grants, we will expound.
