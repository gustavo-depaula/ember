# Tractate 91

## *They hated me without cause*[^1].

## He hates without cause who seeks no advantage from his hatred, nor flees any disadvantage; thus the impious hate the Lord, thus the righteous love him — that is, without cause, so that they look for no goods besides him, since he himself will be all in all.

**1.** The Lord had said: *He who hates me hates my Father also*[^2]. For surely whoever hates the truth must needs hate also him from whom the truth was born: on which we have already spoken, as far as was granted. Then he added that on which we must now speak: *If I had not done among them the works which no one else has done, they would not have sin*[^3]. That great sin, namely, of which he also said above: *If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not have sin*[^4]. This is the sin by which they did not believe in him while he spoke and worked. For they did not have no sin before he spoke to them and worked among them; but this sin, by which they did not believe in him, is recalled in this way because by this very sin the rest also are held. For if they did not have this, and believed in him, the rest also would be forgiven them.

**2.** But what is this, that when he had said, *If I had not done the works among them,* he at once added, *which no one else has done*[^5]? For among the works of Christ none seem greater than the raising of the dead; which we know the prophets of old also did. For Elijah did it, and Elisha did it, both when he lived in this flesh and when he lay buried in his own tomb. For certain men carrying a dead body, when they had fled to it as enemies rushed upon them, and had laid it there, the dead man at once rose again[^6]. Yet Christ did some things which no one else did: that he fed five thousand men with five loaves, and four thousand with seven; that he walked upon the waters, and granted to Peter to do this; that he changed water into wine; that he opened the eyes of one born blind, and many other things which it would be long to recount.

But it is answered us that others too did things which he did not do, and which no one else did. For who but Moses struck the Egyptians with so many and so great plagues, led the people through a divided sea, obtained manna from heaven for the hungry, poured out water from the rock for the thirsty? Who but Joshua son of Nun divided the streams of the Jordan for the people about to cross, and, by a prayer sent up to God, reined in and fixed the running sun? Who besides Samson, on account of his thirst, was satisfied by the overflowing jawbone of a dead ass? Who besides Elijah was carried up on high in a fiery chariot? Who besides Elisha — as I recalled a little before — when his own corpse was buried, restored another corpse to life? Who besides Daniel lived unharmed among the mouths of hungry lions shut up with him? Who besides the three men, Hananiah, Azariah, and Mishael, walked unhurt in burning flames that did not burn?

## He did it in them, not among them or before them.

**3.** I pass over the rest, since I judge these enough by which it is shown that some of the saints also did certain wondrous works which no one else did. But that anyone of the ancients healed with so great a power so many vices and bad states of health and torments of mortals — none at all is read of. For, to say nothing of those whom by commanding, as they came to him, he made well one by one, the evangelist Mark says in one place: *And when evening had come, when the sun had set, they brought to him all who were ill, and those who had demons; and the whole city was gathered at the door: and he healed many who were afflicted with various diseases, and cast out many demons*[^7]. And when Matthew had recalled this, he added also the prophetic testimony, saying: *That what was spoken through the prophet Isaiah might be fulfilled, saying, He took our infirmities and bore our sicknesses*[^8]. Likewise in another place Mark says: *And wherever he entered, into villages, or into farms, or into cities, they laid the sick in the streets, and besought him that they might touch even the fringe of his garment; and as many as touched him were made well*[^9]. These things no one else did in them. For so is it to be understood, his saying *in them,* not among them or before them, but plainly *in them,* because he healed them. For these things he wished to be understood which not only caused wonder, but also conferred manifest health; for which benefits, surely, they ought to have returned love, not hatred.

It surpasses, indeed, all the miracles of the others, that he was born of a virgin, and that he alone could, neither in being conceived nor in being born, violate his mother's integrity: but this was done neither before them nor in them. For to come to know the truth of this miracle, the Apostles arrived, not by a sight shared with them, but by a discipleship set apart from them. And as for that — that on the third day, in the flesh in which he had been slain, he gave himself back living from the tomb, and, never thereafter to die, ascended with it into heaven — it surpasses even all that he did: but neither was this done in the Jews, nor before them; and he had not yet done this when he was saying, *If I had not done among them the works which no one else has done.*

**4.** Without doubt, then, the works he means are those in which, in their states of health, he showed such great miracles of healing as no one before had granted among them: for these they saw, and reproaching them with this he adds and says: *But now they have both seen and hated both me and my Father: but that the word may be fulfilled which is written in their Law: They hated me without cause*[^10]. He calls it their Law, not as discovered by them, but as given to them: just as we say, *Our daily bread*, which yet we ask of God by adding, *give us*[^11]. But he hates without cause who seeks no advantage from his hatred, nor flees any disadvantage: thus the impious hate the Lord; thus the righteous love him — that is, without cause, so that they look for no goods besides him, since he himself will be all in all.

But whoever considers more deeply Christ saying, *If I had not done among them the works which no one else has done* (yet even if the Father or the Holy Spirit did these, no one else did them, because the substance of the whole Trinity is one), will find that he himself did them, if ever any man of God did such a thing. For he can do all things in himself through himself; but no one can do anything without him. For Christ, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, is not three gods, but one God, of whom it is written: *Blessed be the Lord God of Israel, who alone does wonders*[^12]. No one else, then, did whatever works he did in them; since whatever other man did any of them, he did it by his doing. But these he himself did, not by their doing.
