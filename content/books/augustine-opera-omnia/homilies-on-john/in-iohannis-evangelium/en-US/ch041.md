# Tractate 41

## *If the Son sets you free, then you will be truly free*[^1].

## This is our hope, that we may be set free by one who is free, and that in setting us free he may make us slaves; for we were slaves of greed, and, set free, we are made slaves of love.

**1.** What follows from the earlier reading, and was recited to us today from the holy Gospel, I then put off speaking of, since I had already said many things, and the freedom into which the grace of the Savior calls us was not to be treated in passing or negligently: about this, today, with the Lord's help, we have determined to speak to you.

For those to whom the Lord Jesus Christ was speaking were Jews—in great part, indeed, enemies, but also in some part already made friends, and yet to be made so; for he saw there certain ones, as we have already said, who after his passion were going to believe. Looking on these, he had said: *When you have exalted the Son of man, then you will know that I am*[^2]. There were there also those who, while he was saying these things, at once believed; to these he spoke what we have heard today: *Then Jesus was saying to the Jews who had believed him: If you remain in my word, you will truly be my disciples.* By remaining you will be: for since you are now believers, by remaining you will be seers. Therefore it follows: *And you will know the truth*[^3].

The truth is unchangeable. The truth is bread; it refreshes minds and does not fail: it changes the one who eats, it is not itself changed into the one who eats. The truth itself is the Word of God, God with God, the only-begotten Son. This Truth was clothed in flesh for our sake, to be born of the Virgin Mary, and that the prophecy might be fulfilled: *Truth has arisen from the earth*[^4]. This Truth, then, when it was speaking with the Jews, lay hidden in the flesh: it lay hidden, not that it might be denied, but that it might be deferred; deferred, that it might suffer in the flesh; and it suffered in the flesh, that the flesh of sin might be redeemed.

Standing thus conspicuous according to the weakness of the flesh, and hidden according to the majesty of his divinity, our Lord Jesus Christ said to those who, when he was speaking these things, had believed him: *If you remain in my word, you will truly be my disciples.* For *he who perseveres to the end, he will be saved*[^5]. *And you will know the truth*, which now lies hidden from you, and is speaking to you. *And the truth will set you free*[^6]. This word the Lord drew from freedom: *it will set you free.* For nothing else, properly, sets free except what makes free. As to save is nothing else than to make safe; as to heal is nothing else than to make whole; as to enrich is nothing else than to make rich—so to set free is nothing else than to make free. This is plainer in the Greek word. For in Latin usage we generally say that a man is set free, which does not pertain to freedom but only to health: as anyone is said to be freed from sickness; it is said in common usage, but not properly. But the Lord so set down this word, when he said, *And the truth will set you free*, that in the Greek tongue no one doubts that he spoke of freedom.

## The truth sets us free from the slavery of sin.

**2.** Indeed the Jews too understood it thus, and answered him—not those who had already believed, but those in the crowd who did not yet believe—answered him: *We are the seed of Abraham, and we have never been slaves to anyone; how do you say: You will be free?*[^7] But the Lord had not said, You will be free; rather, *The truth will set you free.* In which word, however, they—because, as I said, it is plain in the Greek—understood nothing but freedom; and they lifted themselves up that they were the seed of Abraham, and said: *We are the seed of Abraham, and we have never been slaves to anyone; how do you say: You will be free?*

O inflated skin! This is not greatness, but a swelling. And even according to the freedom of this present time, how did you speak truly: *We have never been slaves to anyone*? Was not Joseph sold[^8]? Were not the holy prophets led into captivity[^9]? Furthermore, is this not the very people that made bricks in Egypt, and served harsh kings—not even in gold and silver, but in mud[^10]? If you have never been slaves to anyone, O ungrateful ones, why is it that God constantly charges you with this, that he set you free from the house of slavery[^11]? Or did perhaps your fathers serve, but you who are speaking have never been slaves to anyone? How, then, were you already paying tribute to the Romans, whence you set even for Truth himself a snare, as of a trap, that you might say: *Is it lawful to pay tribute to Caesar?*—so that if he had said, It is lawful, you would hold him as having wished ill to the freedom of the seed of Abraham; but if he should say, It is not lawful, you would slander him before the kings of the earth, that he forbade tribute to be paid to kings? Deservedly, when a coin was produced, you were defeated, and you yourselves were compelled to answer your own trap. For there it was said to you: *Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's*; when you yourselves had answered that the coin bore the image of Caesar[^12]. For as Caesar seeks his own image in the coin, so God seeks his own in man. These things, then, he answered the Jews. For the vain pride of men moves me, brothers, who lied even about that freedom of theirs which they understood in a carnal way, saying: *We have never been slaves to anyone.*

**3.** But what the Lord answered, this let us rather and more intently hear, lest we ourselves too be found slaves. For Jesus answered them: *Amen, amen, I say to you, that everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin*[^13]. He is a slave—would that of a man and not of sin. Who would not tremble under these words? May the Lord our God grant us, that is, both to me and to you, that I may speak according to the meaning about this freedom to be sought, and about that slavery to be avoided. *Amen, amen, I say to you*, says Truth: and what is it for the Lord our God to say, *Amen, amen, I say to you*? He commends greatly what he pronounces in this way; in a manner, if it is permitted to say so, it is his oath: *Amen, amen, I say to you.* For "amen" is interpreted "truly"; and yet it has not been interpreted, though it could have been said, Truly I say to you. Neither the Greek translator dared to do this, nor the Latin: for this word which is "amen" is neither Greek nor Latin, but Hebrew. So it has remained untranslated, that it might have honor by the veil of secrecy: not that it should be denied, but that, laid bare, it should not become cheap. Yet not once, but twice, was it said by the Lord: *Amen, amen, I say to you.* Now from the very doubling recognize how greatly this is commended.

**4.** What, then, is commended? Truly, truly I say to you, Truth says; which, even if it did not say: I say truly, could not lie at all: yet it commends, it inculcates; it in a manner rouses the sleeping, makes them attentive, does not wish to be despised. Saying what? *Amen, amen, I say to you, that everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin.*

O wretched slavery! Often, when men endure evil masters, they seek to be put up for sale—not seeking to have no master, but at least to change him; what is the slave of sin to do? Whom shall he appeal to? Before whom shall he appeal? Before whom shall he seek to be put up for sale? Again, the slave of a man, sometimes wearied by the harsh commands of his master, finds rest by fleeing: the slave of sin—whither does he flee? He drags himself along with himself wherever he flees. An evil conscience does not flee from itself; there is nowhere for it to go, it follows itself; nay rather, it does not depart from itself: for the sin which it commits is within. He committed sin to take some bodily pleasure: the pleasure passes, the sin remains; what delighted has gone by, what stings has remained. An evil slavery!

Sometimes men flee to the Church, and generally we put up with them as undisciplined: wishing to be rid of masters, who do not wish to be rid of sins. But sometimes, too, those subjected to an unlawful and wicked yoke flee to the Church, because free men are held back into slavery, and the bishop is appealed to: and unless he takes care to expend effort, lest free birth be oppressed, he is reckoned merciless.

Let us all flee to Christ; against sin let us appeal to God the liberator: let us seek to be put up for sale, that we may be redeemed by his blood. For the Lord says: *You were sold for nothing, and you will be redeemed without silver*[^14]. Without a price, but yours; because with mine. This the Lord says: for he himself gave the price, not silver, but his own blood. For we had remained both slaves and destitute.

## He sets us free who was made a sacrifice for sin.

**5.** The Lord alone, therefore, sets us free from this slavery: he who did not have it, he himself sets free from it; for he alone came in this flesh without sin. For the little ones whom you see being carried in their mothers' hands—they do not yet walk, and already they are fettered; for they have drawn from Adam what is to be loosed by Christ. To them too, when they are baptized, belongs this grace which the Lord promises; because he alone can set free from sin who came without sin, and was made a sacrifice for sin.

For you heard, when the Apostle was read: *On Christ's behalf,* he says, *we serve as an embassy, as though God were exhorting through us; we beseech on Christ's behalf*; that is, as though Christ were beseeching you. What? *To be reconciled to God*[^15]. If the Apostle exhorts and beseeches that we be reconciled to God, we were enemies of God. For no one is reconciled except out of enmities. But it was not nature that made us enemies, but sins. Whence enemies of him, thence slaves of sin. God has no free enemies; necessarily they are slaves: and they will remain slaves unless they are set free by him to whom, by sinning, they willed to be enemies. *We beseech, then,* he says, *on Christ's behalf, to be reconciled to God.*

But how are we reconciled, unless what separates us from him is loosed? For he says through the prophet: *He has not made his ear heavy that it should not hear, but your sins separate between you and God*[^16]. Because, then, we are not reconciled unless what is in the middle is taken away, and what should be in the middle is set there. For there is a middle that separates, but against it there is a mediator that reconciles; the separating middle is sin, the reconciling mediator is the Lord Jesus Christ: for *there is one God, and one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^17]. So that the dividing wall which is sin might be taken away, that mediator came, and the priest himself was made a sacrifice. And because he was made a sacrifice for sin, offering himself as a holocaust on the cross of his passion, the Apostle goes on and says—having said, *We beseech on Christ's behalf, to be reconciled to God*—as though we were to say, how shall we be able to be reconciled? *Him,* he says—that is, Christ himself—*who knew no sin, he made sin for us, that we might become the righteousness of God in him*[^18]. Him, he says, Christ himself, God, who knew no sin. For he came in the flesh, that is, *in the likeness of the flesh of sin*[^19], yet not in the flesh of sin, having no sin at all whatever: and therefore he was made a true sacrifice for sin, because he himself had no sin.

**6.** But perhaps I said from my own understanding that sin is a sacrifice for sin. Let those who have read acknowledge it; let those who have not read not be lazy—let them not be lazy, I say, to read, that they may be truthful in judging. For when God was giving commands about the sacrifices to be offered for sin—in which sacrifices there was not an expiation of sins, but a shadow of things to come—those very sacrifices, those very offerings, those very victims, those very animals that were brought to be slaughtered for sins, in whose blood that blood was prefigured, the Law calls sins: so much so that in certain places it is written thus, that the priests who were about to immolate should lay their hands on the head of the sin, that is, on the head of the victim to be immolated for sin. Such a sin, then, that is, a sacrifice for sin, was our Lord Jesus Christ made, who knew no sin.

**7.** Deservedly he sets free from this slavery of sin who says in the Psalms: *I have become like a man without help, free among the dead*[^20]. For he alone is free, because he had no sin. For he himself says in the Gospel: *Behold, the prince of this world comes*—signifying that the devil would come in the persecuting Jews; *behold,* he says, *he comes, and in me he will find nothing.* Not as in those whom he killed, even the just, he found some sin or other; in me he will find nothing. And as though it were said to him, if he will find nothing in you, why will he kill you? He went on and said: *But that all may know that I do the will of my Father, arise, let us go from here*[^21]. I do not, he says, pay the death of my own sin by necessity; rather, in that I die, I do the will of my Father: and I do more there than I suffer, because if I were unwilling, I would not even have suffered. You have him saying in another place: *I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again*[^22]. Behold, truly free among the dead. You will be free, if you are a slave.

**8.** Since, then, everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin, hear what hope of freedom there is for us. *But the slave,* he says, *does not remain in the house forever*[^23]. The Church is the house, the sinner is the slave. Many sinners enter into the Church. He did not, then, say: The slave is not in the house; but, he does not remain in the house forever. If, then, no slave will be there, who will be there? For when the just king sits on his throne, as Scripture says, *who will boast that he has a chaste heart? Or who will boast that he is clean from sin?*[^24] He has greatly terrified us, O my brothers, by saying: The slave does not remain in the house forever. But he adds and says: *But the Son remains forever.* Will Christ, then, be alone in his house? Will no people cleave to him? Of whom will he be the head, if there will be no body? Or is the Son perhaps all this, head and body? For not without cause did he both terrify and give hope: he terrified, lest we love sin; he gave hope, lest we despair of being loosed from sin. *Everyone,* he says, *who commits sin is a slave of sin. But the slave does not remain in the house forever.* What hope, then, is there for us, who are not without sin? Hear your hope: *The Son remains forever. If, therefore, the Son sets you free, then you will be truly free*[^25]. This is our hope, brothers, that we may be set free by one who is free, and that in setting us free he may make us slaves: for we were slaves of greed, and, set free, we are made slaves of love. This too the Apostle says: *But you, brothers, were called to freedom; only do not give freedom as an occasion for the flesh, but through love be slaves to one another*[^26].

Let not a Christian, then, say: I am free, I have been called to freedom; I was a slave, but I have been redeemed, and by that very redemption I have been made free, I will do what I will; let no one forbid me from my will, if I am free. But if by that will you commit sin, you are a slave of sin. Do not, then, abuse freedom for sinning freely, but use it for not sinning. For your will will be free, if it is devout. You will be free, if you are a slave; free of sin, a slave of righteousness: as the Apostle says, *When you were slaves of sin, you were free with regard to righteousness; but now, set free from sin and made slaves to God, you have your fruit unto sanctification, and the end eternal life*[^27]. Let us strive at this, let us do this.

## The first freedom is to be free of crimes.

**9.** The first freedom is to be free of crimes. Attend, my brothers, attend; that perhaps I may be able to lead you to a sense both of what kind this freedom now is, and of what kind it is to be. Examine anyone you please who is very just in this life: though he may already be worthy of the name of just, he is nevertheless not without sin. Hear the holy John himself, whose this Gospel also is, saying in his Epistle: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^28]. He alone could say this, free among the dead; of him alone could it be said who knew no sin; of him alone could it be said: for indeed *he was tried in all things according to our likeness, without sin*[^29]. He alone could say: *Behold, the prince of the world comes, and in me he will find nothing*[^30]. Examine any other just man you please: he is not in every way without sin. Not even such as was Job, to whom the Lord bore such witness that the devil envied him, and demanded that he be tempted, that, tempting, he might be overcome, and Job be proved[^31]. But Job was proved, not because, destined to be crowned, he was hidden from God, but that, to be imitated, he might become known to men. What does Job himself even say? *For who is clean? Not even the infant whose life on earth is of one day*[^32].

But plainly many just men have been called without complaint, which is understood as without crime: for no complaint is just, in human affairs, about those who have no crime. But a crime is a grave sin, most worthy of accusation and condemnation. God, then, does not condemn some sins and justify and praise others: he praises none, he hates them all. As a physician hates the sickness of the sick man, and acts by curing so that the sickness may be driven out and the sick man relieved: so God by his grace acts in us, so that sin may be consumed and man set free.

But when is it consumed? you will say. If it is diminished, why is it not consumed? Now it is diminished in the life of those making progress, which is consumed in the life of the perfect.

**10.** The first freedom, then, is to be free of crimes. Therefore the apostle Paul too, when he chose those to be ordained, whether presbyters or deacons, and whoever is to be ordained to a charge in the Church, did not say: If anyone is without sin; for if he said this, every man would be rejected, none would be ordained: but he said, *If anyone is without crime*[^33]—such as is murder, adultery, some uncleanness of fornication, theft, fraud, sacrilege, and the rest of this kind. When a man has begun not to have these (and every Christian man ought not to have them), he begins to lift up his head toward freedom: but that freedom is begun, not perfected. Why, says someone, is the freedom not perfect? *Because I see another law in my members, fighting against the law of my mind: for I do not do what I will,* he says, *but what I hate, that I do*[^34]. *The flesh,* he says, *desires against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh, so that you do not do the things that you will*[^35]. In part freedom, in part slavery: not yet whole, not yet pure, not yet full freedom, because not yet eternity. For we have in part weakness, in part we have received freedom.

Whatever sin was committed by us was previously blotted out in Baptism. Is it the case that, because all iniquity has been blotted out, no weakness has remained? If it had not remained, we would live here without sin. But who would dare to say this except a proud man, except one unworthy of the liberator's mercy, except one who wishes to deceive himself, and in whom the truth is not[^36]? Therefore, from the fact that something of weakness has remained, I dare to say, in the part by which we serve God, we are free; in the part by which we serve the law of sin, we are still slaves.

Hence the Apostle says what we had begun to say: *I delight in the law of God according to the inner man*[^37]. Behold whence we are free, whence we delight in the law of God: for freedom delights. For as long as you do what is just out of fear, God does not delight you. As long as you still act as a slave, he does not delight you: let him delight you, and you are free. Do not fear punishment, but love righteousness. Cannot you yet love righteousness? Fear at least punishment, that you may arrive at loving righteousness.

**11.** So that man already felt himself to be free in his higher part, whence he said: *I delight in the law of God according to the inner man.* The law delights me, what the law commands delights me, righteousness itself delights me. *But I see another law in my members*—this is the weakness that has remained—*fighting against the law of my mind, and making me captive in the law of sin, which is in my members*[^38]. In this part he feels captivity, where righteousness has not been fulfilled: for where he delights in the law of God, he is not a captive but a friend of the law; and therefore free, because a friend.

What, then, of what remains? What, except that we look to him who said: *If the Son sets you free, then you will be truly free*[^39]? Indeed he himself who was speaking looked to him: *Unhappy man that I am, who will set me free,* he says, *from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^40]. Therefore, *if the Son sets you free, then you will be truly free.* Finally, he concluded thus: *So then, I myself with the mind serve the law of God, but with the flesh the law of sin*[^41]. I myself, he says: for we are not two contrary to each other, coming from different principles; rather, I myself with the mind serve the law of God, but with the flesh the law of sin, as long as the languor struggles against health.

## Remain in the slavery of God, in the freedom of Christ.

**12.** But if with the flesh you serve the law of sin, do what the Apostle himself says: *Let not sin, therefore, reign in your mortal body, so that you obey its desires, nor present your members as weapons of iniquity to sin*[^42]. He does not say: Let it not be; but: Let it not reign. As long as sin must be in your members, let at least its kingdom be taken from it; let not what it commands be done. Does anger arise? Do not give anger a tongue for cursing; do not give anger a hand or foot for striking. That irrational anger would not arise, were sin not in the members: but take away its kingdom, let it not have weapons with which to fight against you; it will even learn not to arise, when it has begun not to find weapons. *Do not present your members as weapons of iniquity to sin*; otherwise you will be wholly captive, and there will be no saying: *With the mind I serve the law of God*[^43]. For if the mind holds the weapons, the members are not moved into the service of raging sin. Let the inner commander hold the citadel, since he stands ready to be helped under a greater commander; let him bridle anger, let him restrain desire. Yet there is within what may be bridled, there is within what may be restrained, there is within what may be held in check.

But what did that just man, serving the law of God with his mind, wish, except that there should be nothing at all to be bridled? And this everyone who tends toward perfection ought to strive for: that the desire itself, to which the members are not given for obeying, may daily be diminished in the one making progress. *To will,* he says, *lies at hand for me, but to accomplish the good does not*[^44]. Did he say: To do the good does not lie at hand for me? If he had said this, there would be no hope. He did not say: To do does not lie at hand for me, but: To accomplish does not lie at hand for me. For what is the accomplishing of the good, except the consuming and end of evil? And what is the consuming of evil, except what the Law says: *You shall not covet*[^45]? To covet not at all is the accomplishment of the good, because it is the consuming of evil. This is what that man was saying: To accomplish the good does not lie at hand for me; because he could not bring it about that he did not covet: he brought it about only that he bridled covetousness, that he did not consent to covetousness, and did not present his members to covetousness as a retinue.

*To accomplish the good, then,* he says, *does not lie at hand for me*: I cannot fulfill what was said: *You shall not covet.* What, then, is needful? That you fulfill: *Do not go after your desires*[^46]. Do this in the meantime, as long as unlawful desires are within your flesh: *Do not go after your desires.* Remain in the slavery of God, in the freedom of Christ; with the mind serve the law of your God. Do not give yourself to your desires: by following them, you add strength to them; by giving them strength, as to vines, when do you nourish enemies against yourself with your own strength?

**13.** What freedom, then, full and perfect, is there in that Lord Jesus who said: *If the Son sets you free, then you will be truly free*; when will there be full and perfect freedom? When there are no enmities, when *the last enemy, death, will be destroyed*. For *this corruptible must put on incorruption, and this mortal must put on immortality: but when this mortal has put on immortality, then will come to pass the word that is written: Death is swallowed up in victory. Where, O death, is your striving?*[^47] What is: *Where, O death, is your striving?* The flesh desired against the spirit, and the spirit against the flesh[^48], but that was when the flesh of sin was vigorous. Where, O death, is your striving? Now we shall live, now we shall not die, in him who died for us and rose again: *so that those who live,* he says, *may no longer live for themselves, but for him who died for them and rose again*[^49].

Let us, wounded, pray to the physician; let us be carried into the inn to be cured. For he is the one who promises health, who had mercy on the man left half-alive by robbers on the road: he poured in oil and wine, healed the wounds, lifted him onto the beast of burden, brought him to the inn, commended him to the innkeeper. To which innkeeper? Perhaps to the one who said: *On Christ's behalf we serve as an embassy*[^50]. He also gave two coins, to be spent on curing the wounded man[^51]: perhaps they are those two precepts on which *the whole Law hangs, and the Prophets*[^52]. So, brothers, the Church too at this time, in which the wounded man is healed, is the traveler's inn: but for the Church herself, on high, is the inheritance of the owner.
