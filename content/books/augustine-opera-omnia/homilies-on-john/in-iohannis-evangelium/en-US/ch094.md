# Tractate 94

## *It is expedient for you that I go*[^1].

## It is expedient for you that this form of a servant be taken away from you; the Word, made flesh, I dwell among you, but I do not wish you still to love me in a fleshly way, and, content with this milk, to desire to be always infants.

**1.** When the Lord Jesus had foretold to his disciples the persecutions they would suffer after his departure, he added and said: *But these things I did not tell you from the beginning, because I was with you: but now I go to him who sent me*[^2]. Here first it is to be seen whether he did not foretell to them, before his passion, the things that were to come. But the other three evangelists show that he foretold these things enough before it had come to the supper, after which, according to John, he said these things, where he says, *But these things I did not tell you from the beginning, because I was with you.* Or is this question perhaps thus solved, because they too relate that he was near to his passion when he said these things? It was not, then, from the beginning when he was with them, since he said these things now about to depart, and now about to go to the Father: and therefore even according to those evangelists it is true what is said here, *But these things I did not tell you from the beginning.* But what do we do about the faith of the Gospel according to Matthew, who recalls that these things were announced to them by the Lord, not only when he was now about to eat the Pasch with the disciples, the passion impending, but even from the beginning, where the twelve Apostles are first expressed by name and sent to divine works? What, then, does he mean by saying here, *But these things I did not tell you from the beginning, because I was with you*: unless it be that the things he says here concerning the Holy Spirit — that he would come to them and bear witness when they would suffer those evils — these he did not tell them from the beginning, because he was with them?

**2.** That Comforter, then, or Advocate (for both are rendered by what is in Greek "Paraclete"), was needed when Christ was departing; and therefore he had not spoken of him from the beginning, when he was with them, because they were consoled by his presence: but, about to depart, it was fitting that he should say that he was coming, through whom it would come about that, with love poured out in their hearts, they should preach the word of God with confidence; and, while he within them bore witness concerning Christ, they too should bear witness; nor should they be scandalized when the hostile Jews put them out of the synagogues and killed them, thinking that they offered a service to God: since love bears all things, that love which was to be poured out in their hearts through the gift of the Holy Spirit. Hence, then, this whole sense is drawn: that he was going to make them his martyrs, that is, his witnesses, through the Holy Spirit; so that, with him working in them, they should endure whatever was harsh in the persecutions, and should not grow cold in the love of preaching, kindled by that divine fire. *These things, therefore,* he says, *I have spoken to you, that when their hour has come, you may remember that I told you*[^3]. These things, namely, I have spoken to you, not only because you are to suffer them; but because, when that Paraclete has come, he will bear witness concerning me, lest by fearing these things you keep silent — whence it will come about that you too bear witness. *But these things I did not tell you from the beginning, because I was with you,* and I was consoling you by my bodily presence, presented to your human senses, which, as little ones, you were able to grasp.

**3.** *But now I go to him who sent me: and no one,* he says, *of you asks me: Where are you going*[^4]? He signifies that he would so go that no one would ask, because they would discern it being done openly to the sight of the body: for above they had asked him where he was going, and he had answered them that he was going where they could not then come. But now he promises that he will so go that none of them asks where he is going. For a cloud received him when he ascended from them; and as he went into heaven, they did not seek him with words, but escorted him with their eyes.

## If you cleave to the flesh in a fleshly way, you will not be capable of the Spirit.

**4.** *But because I have spoken these things to you,* he says, *sorrow has filled your heart*[^5]. He saw, surely, what those words of his were doing in their hearts: for, not yet having inwardly the spiritual consolation which they were to have through the Holy Spirit, they feared to lose what they saw outwardly in Christ; and because they could not doubt that they would lose him, who was announcing true things, the human affection was saddened, because the fleshly sight was being left desolate. But he knew what was rather expedient for them, because the inner sight is surely the better, by which the Holy Spirit was going to console them — not about to thrust a human body upon the bodies of those who behold, but about to pour himself into the breasts of those who believe.

Finally he adds: *But I tell you the truth: it is expedient for you that I go. For if I do not go away, the Paraclete will not come to you: but if I go away, I will send him to you*[^6]: as though he said: It is expedient for you that this form of a servant be taken away from you: the Word, made flesh, I dwell among you; but I do not wish you still to love me in a fleshly way, and, content with this milk, to desire to be always infants. *It is expedient for you that I go. For if I do not go away, the Paraclete will not come to you.* If I do not withdraw the tender nourishment by which I have fed you, you will not hunger for solid food; if you cleave to the flesh in a fleshly way, you will not be capable of the Spirit. For what is, *If I do not go away, the Paraclete will not come to you: but if I go away, I will send him to you*? Could he not, placed here, send him? Who would say this? For he had not withdrawn from where the other was; and he had so come from the Father that he did not cease to abide in the Father. Finally, how could he not, even when established here, send the one whom we know to have come upon him at his baptism and to have remained — nay rather, from whom we know him never to have been separable? What is it, then: *If I do not go away, the Paraclete will not come to you*; unless it be, you cannot grasp the Spirit so long as you persist in knowing Christ according to the flesh? Whence he who had already received the Spirit says: *Even if we had known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know him so no longer*[^7]. For even the very flesh of Christ he does not know according to the flesh, who knows the Word made flesh spiritually. This, doubtless, the good master wished to signify by saying: *For if I do not go away, the Paraclete will not come to you: but if I go away, I will send him to you.*

**5.** But when Christ departed bodily, not only the Holy Spirit, but also the Father and the Son were present to them spiritually. For if Christ so departed from them that the Holy Spirit was in them in his place, not with him, where is his promise when he says: *Behold, I am with you until the consummation of the world*[^8]; and, *We will come to him,* I and the Father, *and will make our dwelling with him*[^9]: since he had also promised that he would so send the Holy Spirit that he would be with them forever? And through this, since from being fleshly or animal they were to become spiritual, they would surely have the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit more capaciously. But in no one is the Father to be believed to be without the Son and the Holy Spirit, or the Father and the Son without the Holy Spirit, or the Son without the Father and the Holy Spirit, or the Holy Spirit without the Father and the Son, or the Father and the Holy Spirit without the Son: but where any one of them is, there is the Trinity, one God. But it was fitting that the Trinity be so introduced that, although there was no diversity of substances, yet the distinction of persons should be commended individually; where, to those who understand rightly, there can never seem to be any separation of natures.

**6.** But what follows — *And when he has come, he will convict the world concerning sin, and concerning righteousness, and concerning judgment: concerning sin, indeed, because they do not believe in me; concerning righteousness, because I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me; concerning judgment, because the prince of this world has been judged*[^10] — as though sin alone were not to believe in Christ, and as though righteousness itself were not to see Christ, and as though judgment itself were that the prince of this world, that is, the devil, has been judged: is very obscure, and not to be confined within this discourse, lest it become more obscure by brevity; but rather to be explained in another, as far as the Lord will help.
