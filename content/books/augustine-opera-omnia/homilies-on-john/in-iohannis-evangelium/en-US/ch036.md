# Tractate 36

## *You do not know whence I come, or where I go* (Jn 8:15-18).

## If you say Christ is only God, you deny the medicine by which you were healed; if you say Christ is only man, you deny the power by which you were created. Made so humble that he came to the cross, he indeed deferred his power, but published his mercy.

**1.** In the four Gospels, or rather the four books of the one Gospel, the holy apostle John, not without reason compared, according to spiritual understanding, to an eagle, raised his preaching higher and far more sublimely than the other three; and in his lifting up he wished our hearts too to be lifted up. For the other three evangelists, as though walking with the Lord as man on earth, said few things of his divinity: but this one, as if it irked him to walk on earth, just as he thundered in the very beginning of his discourse, lifted himself up, not only above the earth and above all the compass of air and sky, but even above every host of angels, and every order of invisible powers, and reached him through whom all things were made, by saying: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^1].

To so great a sublimity of beginning he preached the rest in keeping, and spoke of the Lord's divinity as no other did. This he belched forth which he had drunk. For not without cause is it narrated of him in this very Gospel that at the supper he reclined upon the breast of the Lord[^2]. From that breast, then, he drank in secret: but what he drank in secret, he belched forth in the open, that there might reach all nations not only the incarnation of the Son of God, and the Passion, and the resurrection; but also what he was before the incarnation—the Only Son to the Father, the Word of the Father, coeternal with the one begetting, equal to him by whom he was sent; yet in that very sending made less, so that the Father might be greater.

## The medicine by which we were healed, the power by which we were created.

**2.** Whatever, then, you have heard humbly stated of the Lord Jesus Christ, think of the dispensation of the flesh he assumed; what he became for our sake, not what he was that he might make us: but whatever you hear in the Gospel placed of him, or read, that is sublime and lofty above all creatures and divine, and equal to the Father and coeternal, know that you are reading what pertains to the form of God, not what pertains to the form of a servant. For if you hold this rule, you who can grasp it; but not all can grasp it, yet all ought to believe it: if, then, you hold this rule, against the calumnies of the heretical darkness, as though walking in the light, you will fight secure.

For there have not been lacking those who, reading only the gospel testimonies, followed those that are stated of the humility of Christ, and who were deaf to those testimonies that spoke of his divinity: deaf for this reason, so as to be badly wordy. Likewise certain men, attending only to those things that are said of the Lord's sublimity, even though they read his mercy, by which he became man for our sake, did not believe it, and thought it introduced by men and false; contending that our Lord Christ was only God, not also man. Some this way, some that; both in error.

But the catholic faith, holding from both what is true, and preaching what it believes, both understood Christ as God, and believed him as man: for both are written, and both are true. If you say Christ is only God, you deny the medicine by which you were healed: if you say Christ is only man, you deny the power by which you were created. Hold both, then, O faithful soul and catholic heart, hold both, believe both, faithfully confess both. Both God is Christ, and man is Christ.

How is Christ God?

Equal to the Father, one with the Father.

How is Christ man?

Born of a virgin, drawing mortality from man, not drawing iniquity.

**3.** Those Jews, then, saw the man, and neither understood nor believed God: and among other things you have already heard how they said to him: You speak witness of yourself; your witness is not true[^3]. You have also heard what he answered, when it was read yesterday, and discussed according to our strength. Today these words of his were read: *You judge according to the flesh.* Therefore, he says, you say to me: You speak witness of yourself, your witness is not true, because you judge according to the flesh; because you do not understand God, and you see a man, and by persecuting the man, you offend the hidden God. Therefore *you judge according to the flesh*[^4]. For this reason I seem to you arrogant, because I bear witness of myself.

For every man, when he wishes to bear praiseworthy witness of himself, seems arrogant and proud. Therefore it is written: *Let not your own mouth praise you, but let the mouth of your neighbor praise you*[^5]. But this was said to man. For we are weak, and we speak among the weak. We can speak truth and lie: even though we ought to speak truth, yet we can lie when we wish. The light cannot lie: far be it that in the splendor of the divine light the darkness of a lie be found. He spoke as the light, he spoke as the truth; but the light shone in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it: therefore they judged according to the flesh. You, he says, judge according to the flesh.

## Christ came first to save, afterward to judge.

**4.** *I judge no one*[^6]. Does the Lord Jesus Christ then judge no one? Is he not the one whom we confess to have risen on the third day, ascended into heaven, to sit there at the right hand of the Father, thence to come to judge the living and the dead? Is this not our faith, of which the Apostle says: *With the heart one believes unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation*[^7]? When, then, we confess these things, do we speak against the Lord? We say that the judge of the living and the dead will come; but he himself says: I judge no one.

This question can be solved in two ways: that either we understand, I judge no one, that is, now: as he says in another place: *I came not to judge the world, but to save the world*[^8]; not by denying his judgment, but by deferring it. Or surely, because he had said: You judge according to the flesh; he so added: I judge no one, that you may understand, according to the flesh. Let no scruple of doubt, then, remain in our heart against the faith we hold and announce concerning Christ the judge.

Christ came, but first to save, afterward to judge: judging unto punishment those who refused to be saved; leading unto life those who by believing did not spurn salvation. The first dispensation, then, of our Lord Jesus Christ is medicinal, not judicial: for if he had first come to judge, he would have found no one to whom to render the rewards of justice. Because, then, he saw all sinners, and that absolutely no one was free from the death of sin; first his mercy was to be dispensed beforehand, and afterward judgment to be exercised: for of him the Psalm had sung: *Mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord*[^9]. For it does not say judgment and mercy; for if judgment were first, there would be no mercy: but first mercy, afterward judgment.

What is the first mercy? The Creator of man deigned to be man: he became what he had made, lest what he had made should perish. What can be added to this mercy? And yet he added. It was too little for him to become man; but also to be reproved by men: it was too little to be reproved; and to be dishonored: it was too little to be dishonored; and to be killed: but this too is too little; by the death of the cross. For even when the Apostle commended his obedience made unto death, it was too little for him to say: *Made obedient unto death*: for not just any death, but he added, *even the death of the cross*[^10]. Nothing was worse than that death among all kinds of deaths. Finally, where the sharpest pains torment, it is called *cruciatus*, named from the cross. For those crucified, hanging on the wood, fixed by nails to the wood in feet and hands, were put to death by a prolonged death. For to be crucified was not the same as to be killed: but long was one alive on the cross; not because a longer life was chosen, but because the death itself was protracted, lest the pain be too quickly ended.

He willed to die for us: we say too little, he deigned to be crucified, made obedient unto the death of the cross. He chose the most extreme and worst kind of death, who was to take away all death: by the worst death he killed all death. For it was the worst to the Jews, who did not understand; for it was chosen by the Lord. For he was to have his very cross as a sign, he was to place that very cross, as a trophy over the conquered devil, on the foreheads of the faithful, so that the Apostle would say: *But far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom the world is crucified to me, and I to the world*[^11]. Nothing was then more intolerable in the flesh, nothing is now more glorious on the forehead. What does he keep for his faithful one, who gave such honor to his own punishment? Finally, now among the Romans it is not used in the punishment of the guilty: for where the cross of the Lord was honored, it was thought that the guilty one too would be honored, if he were crucified.

He, then, who came for this, judged no one: and he suffered evil men. He endured an unjust judgment, that he might do a just one. But in that he endured the unjust one, it was of mercy. Finally, made so humble that he came to the cross, he indeed deferred his power, but published his mercy. Whence did he defer his power? Because he was unwilling to come down from the cross, who was able to rise from the tomb. Whence did he publish his mercy? Because, hanging on the cross, he said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^12].

Whether, then, on account of this, because he had not come to judge the world, but to save the world, he said: I judge no one: or, as I recalled, because he had said: You judge according to the flesh, he added: I judge no one, that we may understand that Christ does not judge according to the flesh, as he was judged by men.

**5.** For that you may now acknowledge Christ as judge too, hear what follows: *And if I judge, my judgment is true.* Behold, you have him as judge too, but acknowledge the savior, lest you feel the judge. But why did he say his judgment was true? *Because I am not alone*, he says, *but I and the Father who sent me*[^13]. I told you, brothers, that this holy evangelist John flies very high: it is hard to comprehend him with the mind. But it is needful that I recall to your Charity the mystery of the one flying higher. Both in the prophet Ezekiel, and in the Apocalypse of this same John, whose Gospel this is, there is recalled a fourfold living creature, having four faces: of a man, of a calf, of a lion, of an eagle[^14]. Those who before us treated the mysteries of the holy Scriptures, most of them understood in this living creature, or rather in these living creatures, the four Evangelists. The lion is set for the king, since the lion seems to be in a way the king of beasts, on account of its power and terrible strength. This face was assigned to Matthew, because in the generations of the Lord he followed out the royal line, how the Lord was through the royal stock from the seed of King David. But Luke, since he began from the priesthood of the priest Zechariah, making mention of the father of John the Baptist, was assigned to the calf; because the calf was a great victim in the sacrifice of priests. To Mark, Christ the man was deservedly assigned, because he neither said anything of the royal power, nor began from the priestly, but only set out from Christ the man.

All these almost did not withdraw from earthly things, that is, from those things which our Lord Jesus Christ did on earth: of his divinity they spoke very little, as though walking with him on earth. There remains the eagle: it is John, the preacher of sublime things, and the contemplator with fixed eyes of the inner and eternal light. For the young of eagles too are said to be tested by their parents thus: namely, to be hung from the father's talon, and set against the rays of the sun: the one that has gazed firmly is acknowledged a son; if its sight has wavered, it is dropped from the talon as spurious. See now how sublime things he ought to have spoken who is compared to an eagle: and yet even we, creeping on the ground, weak and of scarcely any moment among men, dare to treat these things, and to expound these things; and we think ourselves either able to grasp them when we think, or grasped when we speak.

**6.** Why have I said these things? For perhaps after these words someone may justly say to me: Put down the book, then. What exceeds your measure, why do you take into your hand? why do you commit your tongue to it?

To this I answer: Many heretics abound, and God permitted them to abound for this reason, that we may not always be nourished with milk, and remain in dull infancy. For because they did not understand how the divinity of Christ was commended, they had their notions as they wished: not, however, by having right notions, they brought most troublesome questions to the catholic faithful; the hearts of the faithful began to be agitated and to waver. Then a necessity was laid upon spiritual men, who had not only read in the Gospel, but also understood, something concerning the divinity of our Lord Jesus Christ, to bring forth the arms of Christ against the arms of the devil: and concerning the divinity of Christ to fight, against false and deceitful teachers, with all the strength they could, in the most open contest; lest, while they themselves were silent, others should perish.

For whoever thought our Lord Jesus Christ to be either of a different substance than the Father is, or to be Christ only in such a way that he himself is the Father, himself the Son, himself the Holy Spirit: whoever also wished to think that he was only man, not God made man, or God in such a way as to be changeable in his divinity, or God in such a way as not to be also man; they made shipwreck of the faith, and were thrown out of the harbor of the Church, lest by their restlessness they should break the ships placed beside them. This thing compelled even us, the least, and so far as concerns us altogether unworthy, but so far as concerns his mercy set in some number of his stewards, not to be silent toward you about what either you may understand, and rejoice with me; or, if you are not yet able to understand, may remain secure by believing in the harbor.

## Let faith cleanse you, that understanding may fill you.

**7.** I will speak, then; let him grasp who can, let him believe who cannot: yet I will say what the Lord said: You judge according to the flesh; I judge no one, either now, or according to the flesh. But even if I judge, my judgment is true.

Why is your judgment true?

Because I am not alone, he says, but I and the Father who sent me.

What then, Lord Jesus? If you were alone, your judgment would be false; and therefore you judge truly, because you are not alone, but you and the Father who sent you? What shall I answer? Let him himself answer: My judgment, he says, is true.

Why?

Because I am not alone, but I and the Father who sent me.

If he is with you, how did he send you? Both he sent you, and he is with you? So then, though sent, you did not depart? so then, both you came to us, and remained there? How is this believed? how is it grasped?

To these two I answer: How is it grasped, you say rightly; how is it believed, you do not say rightly. Rather it is well believed for this reason, because it is not quickly grasped: for if it were quickly grasped, there would be no need for it to be believed; because it would be seen. You believe for this reason, because you do not grasp; but by believing you become fit to grasp. For if you do not believe, you will never grasp; because you will remain less fit. Let faith, then, cleanse you, that understanding may fill you.

My judgment is true, he says; because I am not alone, but I and the Father who sent me. Therefore, O Lord our God Jesus Christ, your sending is your incarnation. So I see, so I understand: finally so I believe, lest it be of arrogance to say, So I understand. Surely the Lord our Jesus Christ is here too; indeed he was here according to the flesh, now he is here according to his divinity: and he was with the Father, and had not departed from the Father. What, then, is said to have come to us as sent, commends his incarnation, because the Father was not incarnate.

## Escape Scylla and Charybdis.

**8.** For certain heretics were called Sabellians, who are also called Patripassians, who say that the Father himself suffered. Do not you, O catholic: for if you are a Patripassian, you will not be sound. Therefore understand the sending of the Son to be called the incarnation of the Son: but do not believe the Father to have been incarnate, yet do not believe the Father to have departed from the incarnate Son. He bore the flesh, he was with the Son.

If the Father is in heaven, the Son on earth; how was the Father with the Son?

Because both the Father and the Son were everywhere: for God is not in heaven in such a way as not to be on earth. Hear him who wished to flee the judgment of God, and found no way: *Where shall I go*, he says, *from your spirit, and where shall I flee from your face? If I ascend into heaven, you are there.* The question was about the earth; hear what follows: *If I descend into hell, you are present*[^15]. If, then, even in hell he is said to be present, what of things remains where he is not? For it is the voice of God in the prophet: *I fill heaven and earth*[^16]. Everywhere, then, is he who is enclosed by no place.

Do not turn away from him, and he is with you. If you wish to come to him, do not be slow to love: for you run not by feet, but by affections. Abiding in one place you come, if you believe and love. Therefore he is everywhere: if he is everywhere, how is he not with the Son? Is he then not with the Son, who, if you believe, is with you too?

**9.** Whence, then, is his judgment true, except because the Son is true? For he said this: And if I judge, my judgment is true; because I am not alone, but I and the Father who sent me. As if he said: My judgment is true, because I am the Son of God.

Whence do you prove that you are the Son of God?

Because I am not alone, but I and the Father who sent me.

Blush, O Sabellian, you hear the Son, you hear the Father. The Father is the Father; the Son is the Son. He did not say: I am the Father, and I myself am the Son; but, I am not alone, he says.

Why are you not alone?

Because the Father is with me.

I am, and the Father who sent me: you lay bare. I am, and he who sent me. Do not lose the person, distinguish the persons. Distinguish by understanding, do not separate by faithlessness; lest again, as though fleeing Charybdis, you run into Scylla. For the whirlpool of the impiety of the Sabellians was devouring you, so that you said that he himself is the Father who is the Son: now you have learned: I am not alone, but I and the Father who sent me. You acknowledge that the Father is the Father; and the Son is the Son. You acknowledge them well: but do not say, the Father is greater, the Son is less; do not say, the Father is gold, the Son is silver. There is one substance, one divinity, one coeternity, perfect equality, no unlikeness. For if you believe Christ to be only another, not the one who is the Father, yet think him in some way distant according to nature; you have indeed escaped Charybdis, but you have made shipwreck on the Scyllaean rocks.

Sail in the middle, avoid each perilous side. The Father is the Father; the Son is the Son. Now you say, the Father is the Father; the Son is the Son: you have well escaped the peril of the engulfing whirlpool; why do you wish to go to the other side, so as to say: The Father is one thing, the Son another thing? He is another, you say rightly; another thing, not rightly. For the Son is another, because he is not the same who is the Father; and the Father is another, because not the same who is the Son: yet not another thing, but this very same thing are both the Father and the Son. What is, this very same thing? One God is. You have heard: Because I am not alone, but I and the Father who sent me; hear how you may believe the Father and the Son, hear the Son himself: *I and the Father are one*[^17]. He did not say: I am the Father; or: I and the Father is one: but when he says: I and the Father are one: hear both, both one, and are, and you will be freed from both Charybdis and Scylla. In these two words that he said, the word one frees you from Arius: the word are frees you from Sabellius. If one, then not diverse; if are, then both the Father and the Son. For he would not say are of one; but neither would he say one of diverse.

Therefore, he says—that you may hear briefly—my judgment is true, because I am the Son of God. But I so persuade you, he says, that I am the Son of God, that you may understand that the Father is with me: I am not the Son in such a way as to have deserted him; I am not here in such a way as not to be with him; he is not there in such a way as not to be with me: I took the form of a servant, but I did not lose the form of God[^18]. Therefore, he says, I am not alone, but I and the Father who sent me.

**10.** He had spoken of judgment; he wishes to speak of witness. *In your Law*, he says, *it is written that the witness of two men is true. I am one who bears witness of myself, and the Father who sent me bears witness of me*[^19]. He expounded the Law to them too, if they were not ungrateful. For it is a great question, my brothers, and the matter seems to me to be set very much in a mystery, where God said: *In the mouth of two or three witnesses every word shall stand*[^20]. Is truth sought through two witnesses? So indeed, such is the custom of the human race: but yet it can happen that two also lie. Chaste Susanna was pressed by two false witnesses[^21]: were they, because they were two, therefore not false witnesses? Do we speak of two or three? The whole people lied against Christ.

If, then, a people composed of a great multitude of men was found a false witness; how is it to be taken: In the mouth of two or three witnesses every word shall stand: except that in this way, through a mystery, the Trinity was commended, in which is the perpetual stability of truth? Do you wish to have a good cause? Have two or three witnesses, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. Finally, when Susanna, a chaste woman and faithful wife, was pressed by two false witnesses, the Trinity supported her in her conscience and in secret: that Trinity from secret roused one witness, Daniel, and convicted the two.

Therefore, because in your Law it is written that the witness of two men is true, receive our witness, lest you feel judgment. For I, he says, judge no one, but I bear witness of myself: I defer judgment, I do not defer witness. Let us choose for ourselves God as witness.

**11.** Let us choose for ourselves, brothers, against the tongues of men, against the weak suspicions of the human race, God as judge, God as witness. For he who is judge does not disdain to be a witness, nor is he promoted when he becomes judge; since he who is witness will himself be judge.

Why is he himself the witness?

Because he does not seek another from whom to learn who you are.

Why is he himself the judge?

Because he himself has the power of putting to death and of giving life, of condemning and of absolving, of casting down into the depths of hell and of raising up into the heavens, of joining to the devil and of crowning with the angels.

Since, then, he himself has this power, he is judge. But because, in order to know you, he does not seek another witness; he who will then judge you, now sees you: there is no way for you to deceive him, when he has begun to judge. For you do not bring to yourself any false witnesses who could circumvent that judge, when he has begun to judge you. God says this to you: When you despised, I was seeing; and when you did not believe, I was not frustrating my sentence: I was deferring, I was not taking it away. You refused to hear what I commanded, you will feel what I foretold. But if you hear what I commanded, you will not feel the evils I foretold, but you will receive the good things I promised.

**12.** Let it not, indeed, move anyone that he says: My judgment is true; because I am not alone, but I and the Father who sent me; whereas elsewhere he said: *The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*[^22]. We have already discussed these same words of the Gospel, and now we admonish you that this was not said because the Father will not be with the Son judging; but because to the good and the evil in the judgment the Son alone will appear, in that form in which he suffered, and rose, and ascended into heaven. For while the disciples then gazed at him ascending, the angelic voice sounded: *So will he come, as you have seen him going into heaven*[^23]: that is, in the form of man in which he was judged he will judge, that even that prophetic word may be fulfilled: *They will look on him whom they pierced*[^24]. But when, with the just going into eternal life, we shall see him as he is; that will not be a judgment of the living and the dead, but only the reward of the living.

**13.** Likewise, let it not move anyone that he says: In your Law it is written that the witness of two men is true, and so anyone suppose that that was not the Law of God, because it was not said: In the Law of God: let him know that it was so said: In your Law, as if he said, in the Law which was given to you; by whom, except by God? Just as we say: Our daily bread; and yet we say: *Give us this day*[^25].
