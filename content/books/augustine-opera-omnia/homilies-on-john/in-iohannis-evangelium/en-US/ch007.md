# Homily 7

## *You will see heaven opened*[^1].

## O people living by faith, before I called you through my apostles, when you were under the shadow of death and did not see me, I saw you.

**1.** We rejoice together at your numbers, because, beyond what we could hope, you have eagerly gathered. This is what gladdens us and consoles us in all the labors and dangers of this life: your love for God, your devout zeal, your sure hope, and the fervor of your spirit. You heard, when the Psalm was read, that the needy and poor man cries out to God in this world[^2]. For it is the voice, as you have often heard and ought to remember, not of one man, and yet of one man: not of one, because the faithful are many—many grains groaning among the chaff, scattered throughout the whole world; but of one, because all are members of Christ, and so are one body. This people, then, needy and poor, does not know how to rejoice in the world; and its sorrow is within, and its joy is within, where none sees but he who hears the one who groans and crowns the one who hopes.

The joy of the world is vanity. With great expectation it is hoped that it will come, and it cannot be held when it has come. For this day, which is joyful to those who are perishing today in this city, will surely not exist tomorrow; nor will the very same men be tomorrow what they are today. All things pass, and all things fly away, and like smoke they vanish—and woe to those who love such things! For every soul follows what it loves. *All flesh is grass, and all the glory of flesh is as the flower of grass; the grass has withered, the flower has fallen; but the word of the Lord abides forever*[^3]. Behold what you should love, if you wish to abide forever. But you were going to say: How can I lay hold of the Word of God? *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^4].

## Let us be fed at the banquet of God.

**2.** Therefore, dearly beloved, let it belong to our neediness and our poverty that we also grieve for those who seem to themselves to abound. For their joy is like that of madmen. And just as a madman often rejoices in his madness and laughs, and bewails the one who is healthy, so we too, dearly beloved, if we have received the medicine that comes from heaven—since we too were all mad—as though made well, because we no longer love the things we used to love, let us groan to God over those who are still raving. For he is able to make even them well. And they need to look at themselves and be displeased with themselves. They wish to watch a spectacle, yet they do not know how to watch themselves. For if they would turn their eyes a little upon themselves, they would see their own confusion. Until that happens, let our pursuits be other, let the diversions of our soul be other. Our sorrow is worth more than their joy.

As far as the number of the brothers is concerned, it is hard that any of the men should have been swept off by that festivity; but as for the number of the sisters, it saddens us, and this is the more to be grieved over, because they do not rather hurry to the Church, whom, if not fear, then surely modesty ought to have called back from the public spectacle. Let him see to this who sees, and his mercy will be present to heal all. But let us who are gathered here be fed at the banquet of God, and let his discourse be our joy. For he has invited us to his Gospel; and he himself is our food, than which nothing is sweeter—but only if one has a healthy palate in the heart.

## The whole fruit is love.

**3.** I think it well that your Charity remembers that this Gospel is being recited in order, with fitting readings; and I suppose you have not forgotten what has already been treated, especially the more recent matters concerning John and the dove. Concerning John, that is, what new thing he learned about the Lord through the dove, he who already knew the Lord. And this was found out by the inspiration of the Spirit of God: that John already knew the Lord indeed, but that the Lord himself would so baptize as to transfer the power of baptizing from himself to no one—this he learned through the dove, because it had been said: *Upon whomever you see the Spirit descending like a dove, and remaining upon him, this is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*[^5]. What does "this is he" mean? Not another, even though through another.

But why through a dove? Many things have been said, and I neither can nor need to weave them all again; chiefly, however, on account of peace; because even the wood that was baptized outside, since the dove found fruit in it, she brought to the ark: just as you remember the dove sent forth by Noah from the ark, which was floating in the flood and was washed by baptism, not submerged. So when it had been sent forth, it brought back an olive branch; but it had not leaves only, it had fruit as well. And so this is to be wished for our brothers who are baptized outside, that they may have fruit: the dove will not leave them outside, unless she has brought them back to the ark. And the fruit is wholly love, without which a person is nothing, whatever else he may have.

And we have recalled and reviewed this, said most abundantly by the Apostle. For he says: *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become as sounding brass or a tinkling cymbal; and if I have all knowledge, and know all mysteries, and have all prophecy, and have all faith* (but how did he say *all* faith?) *so as to move mountains, but have not love, I am nothing. And if I distribute all my goods to the poor, and if I deliver up my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^6]. But in no way can those say that they have love who divide unity. These things have been said; let us look at what follows.

**4.** John bore witness, because he saw. What witness did he bear? *That this is the Son of God*[^7]. It was fitting, then, that he should baptize who is the only-begotten Son of God, not the adopted. Adopted sons are ministers of the only-begotten; the only-begotten has the power, the adopted the ministry. Although a minister baptize who does not belong to the number of the sons, because he lives badly and acts badly, what consoles us? This is he who baptizes.

**5.** *The next day again John was standing, and two of his disciples; and looking upon Jesus as he walked, he says: Behold the Lamb of God*[^8]. This Lamb, surely, in a singular sense: for the disciples too were called lambs: *Behold, I send you as lambs in the midst of wolves*[^9]. They too were called light: *You are the light of the world*[^10]; but otherwise was he of whom it was said: He was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world[^11]. So too the Lamb in a singular sense, alone without stain, without sin; not one whose stains have been wiped away, but one in whom there was no stain at all. For what? Because John said of the Lord, *Behold the Lamb of God*[^12], was John himself not a lamb? Was he not a holy man? Was he not the friend of the bridegroom? Therefore he in a singular sense: This is the Lamb of God; because by the blood of this Lamb alone could men be redeemed, in a singular way.

## Behold the spectacles of Christians.

**6.** My brothers, if we recognize our price, that it is the blood of the Lamb, who are those who today celebrate the festival of the blood of some woman or other? And how ungrateful they are! Gold was snatched, they say, from a woman's ear, and blood ran, and the gold was placed on a balance or scale, and the blood far outweighed it. If the blood of a woman had weight enough to tip the scale of gold, what weight has the blood of the Lamb to tip the scale of the world, through whom the world was made?

And indeed that spirit, whoever he is, in order to press down the scale, was appeased with blood. The unclean spirits knew that Jesus Christ would come; they had heard it from the angels, they had heard it from the prophets, and they expected that he would come. For if they did not expect it, why did they cry out: *What have we to do with you? Have you come before the time to destroy us? We know who you are, the Holy One of God*[^13]? They knew that he would come, but they did not know the time.

But what did you hear in the Psalm concerning Jerusalem? *For your servants have taken pleasure in her stones, and they will have pity on her dust: you, rising up, he says, will have mercy on Zion, for the time has come to have mercy on her*[^14]. When the time came for God to have mercy, the Lamb came. What kind of Lamb is it that wolves fear? What kind of Lamb is it that, being slain, slew the lion? For the devil was called *a lion going about and roaring, seeking whom he may devour*[^15]: the lion was conquered by the blood of the Lamb. Behold the spectacles of Christians. And what is greater, they with the eyes of the flesh see vanity, we with the eyes of the heart see truth.

Do not suppose, brothers, that the Lord our God has dismissed us without spectacles: for if there are no spectacles, why have you gathered together today? Behold what we have said, you have seen, and you have cried out: you would not cry out, had you not seen. And it is a great thing to behold throughout the whole world the lion conquered by the blood of the Lamb, the members of Christ drawn out from the teeth of lions and joined to the body of Christ. Therefore some spirit imitated something of the sort, so as to wish his image to be bought with blood, because he knew that the human race was at some time to be redeemed by precious blood. For the evil spirits invent for themselves certain shadows of honor, so as in this way to deceive those who follow Christ.

So far, my brothers, that those very men who seduce by amulets, by incantations, by the contrivances of the enemy, mix into their incantations the name of Christ: because they can no longer seduce Christians into taking poison, they add a little honey, so that through what is sweet what is bitter may lie hidden, and may be drunk to one's ruin. So far that I have known at a certain time a priest of that Capped One in the habit of saying: Even the Capped One himself is a Christian. To what end is this, brothers, except that otherwise Christians cannot be seduced?

**7.** Do not, then, seek Christ anywhere else than where Christ willed himself to be preached to you; and as he willed to be preached to you, so hold him, so write him in your heart. He is a wall against all the assaults and against all the snares of the enemy. Do not fear; nor does he tempt unless he has been permitted; it is established that he does nothing unless permitted or sent. He is sent, like an evil angel, by the ruling power; he is permitted, when he asks for something: and this, brothers, does not happen except that the just may be tested and the unjust punished. Why, then, do you fear? Walk in the Lord your God, be assured: what he does not will you to suffer, you do not suffer; what he has permitted you to suffer is the lash of one correcting, not the punishment of one condemning.

We are being trained for an everlasting inheritance, and we disdain to be lashed! My brothers, if any boy refused to be struck with blows or lashes by his father, how would he be called proud, hopeless, ungrateful for his father's discipline? And to what end does a human father train his human son? That he may be able not to lose the temporal goods that he acquired for him, that he gathered for him, that he does not wish him to lose, which he himself who leaves them behind was unable to hold forever.

He does not teach the son to possess together with him, but to possess after him. My brothers, if a father teaches his son as his successor, and the one he teaches is himself likewise to pass through all those things through which the one who admonished him is also to pass, how do you wish our Father to train us—our Father whom we are not to succeed, but to whom we are to come, and with whom we are to abide forever in an inheritance that does not wither, nor die, nor know hail? He is himself both the inheritance and the Father. Him we shall possess; and ought we not to be trained?

Let us, then, bear with the training of the Father. When our head aches, let us not run to those who chant spells, to fortune-tellers, and to the remedies of vanity. My brothers, shall I not bewail you? I find these things every day; and what shall I do? Do I not yet persuade Christians that hope is to be placed in Christ? Behold, if some remedy has been applied to a man, and he dies (for how many have died with remedies? and how many have lived without remedies?), with what countenance does the soul go forth to God? It has lost the sign of Christ, it has taken on the sign of the devil.

But perhaps he may say: I have not lost the sign of Christ?

Then you have had the sign of Christ together with the sign of the devil. Christ does not want partnership, but wants to possess alone what he has bought. He bought it at so great a price that he might possess it alone: you make the devil his partner in it, the devil to whom you had sold yourself through sin. *Woe to the double-hearted*[^16], who in their heart make a part for God and a part for the devil. God, angered because a part is made there for the devil, departs, and the devil will possess the whole. Not without reason, then, does the Apostle say: *Neither give place to the devil*[^17]. Let us, then, know the Lamb, brothers; let us know our price.

**8.** *John was standing, and two of his disciples*[^18]. Behold, two of John's disciples: because John was such a friend of the bridegroom, he did not seek his own glory, but bore witness to the truth: did he wish his disciples to remain with him, so as not to follow the Lord? Rather, he himself showed his disciples whom they should follow. For they regarded him as the Lamb; and he said: Why do you attend to me? I am not the Lamb; *Behold the Lamb of God*[^19]—of whom also he had said above: *Behold the Lamb of God*[^20]. And what does the Lamb of God profit us? *Behold*, he says, *him who takes away the sin of the world*[^21]. The two who were with John, having heard this, followed him. Let him come, let him teach us, and converse with us.

**9.** Let us look at what follows. *Behold the Lamb of God*[^22]: this was John. *And the two disciples heard him speaking, and they followed Jesus. But Jesus, turning and seeing them following him, says to them: What do you seek? They said: Rabbi (which is interpreted Teacher), where do you dwell?*[^23] They did not follow him as though already to cleave to him: for it is plain when they cleaved to him, since he called them from the boat. For among these two was Andrew, as you have just heard; and Andrew was the brother of Peter; and we know in the Gospel that the Lord called Peter and Andrew from the boat, saying: *Come after me, and I will make you fishers of men*[^24]. And from that time they cleaved to him, so as not to withdraw.

For now, then, that these two follow him, they do not follow as though not going to withdraw; but they wished to see where he dwelt, and to do what is written: *Let your foot wear out the threshold of his doors; rise to come to him continually and to be trained in his precepts*[^25]. He showed them where he was staying: they came and were with him. What a blessed day they spent, what a blessed night! Who is there to tell us what they heard from the Lord? Let us also build in our heart, and make a house where he may come and teach us; let him converse with us.

## Offer the sacrifice of humility.

**10.** *What do you seek? They said to him: Rabbi (which is interpreted Teacher), where do you dwell? He says to them: Come and see. And they came and saw where he was staying, and they stayed with him that day; and it was about the tenth hour*[^26]. Do we think it was of no concern to the Evangelist to tell us what hour it was? Can it be that he wished us to notice nothing there, to seek nothing? It was the tenth hour. This number signifies the law, because the law was given in ten commandments. But the time had come for the law to be fulfilled through love, because by the Jews it could not be fulfilled through fear. Hence the Lord says: *I have not come to abolish the law, but to fulfill it*[^27]. With good reason, then, at the tenth hour these two followed him at the testimony of the friend of the bridegroom; and at the tenth hour he heard: Rabbi, which is interpreted Teacher. If at the tenth hour the Lord was called Rabbi, and the number ten pertains to the law, the teacher of the law is none other than the giver of the law. Let no one say that one gave the law and another teaches the law: he himself teaches it who gave it; he is the teacher of his own law, and he teaches it. And mercy is on his tongue; therefore he mercifully teaches the law, as it is said of wisdom: *She carries law and mercy on her tongue*[^28]. Do not fear that you cannot fulfill the law; flee to mercy. If it is too much for you to fulfill the law, use that covenant, use that bond, use the prayers that the heavenly jurist has appointed and composed for you.

**11.** For those who have a case and wish to petition the emperor seek out some learned jurist, by whom their petition may be composed; lest perhaps, if they ask otherwise than they ought, they not only fail to obtain what they ask, but even receive punishment instead of a benefit. So when the Apostles sought to make their petition, and did not find how to approach God the emperor, they said to Christ: *Lord, teach us to pray*[^29]—that is: Our jurist, our assessor, indeed our fellow-sitter with God, compose our petition for us. And the Lord taught them from the book of the heavenly law, taught them how to pray; and in the very thing he taught, he set a certain condition: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^30].

If you do not petition according to the law, you will be guilty. Do you tremble at the emperor when you have become guilty? Offer the sacrifice of humility, offer the sacrifice of mercy, say in your prayer: Forgive me, since I too forgive. But if you say it, do it. For what will you do, where will you go, if you have lied in your prayer? Not as is said in the law court—you will be deprived of the benefit of the rescript; rather, you will not even obtain the rescript. For it is a principle of forensic law that one who has lied in his petition does not profit by what he has obtained. But this is among human beings, because a human being can be deceived; the emperor could be deceived when you sent your petition: for you said what you wished, and the one to whom you said it does not know whether it is true; he handed you over to be refuted by your adversary, so that if before the judge you are convicted of lying—since the emperor could only grant it, not knowing whether you had lied—there you will be deprived of the very benefit of the rescript to which you brought the rescript. But God, who knows whether you lie or speak the truth, does not bring it about that in judgment it does not profit you; rather, he does not even permit you to obtain it, because you have dared to lie to the truth.

## See that you do not depart from the Lamb.

**12.** What, then, will you do? Tell me. To fulfill the law in every part, so that you offend in nothing, is difficult: guilt, then, is certain; and you refuse to use the remedy? Behold, my brothers, what a remedy the Lord has set against the diseases of the soul.

What, then?

When your head aches, we praise you if you place the Gospel at your head, and have not run to an amulet. For to this point has the weakness of human beings been brought, and people who run to amulets are so to be bewailed, that we rejoice when we see a man laid up in his bed, tossed by fevers and pains, who has placed his hope nowhere except in setting the Gospel at his head: not because it was made for this, but because the Gospel has been preferred to amulets.

If, then, it is placed at the head so that the pain of the head may cease, is it not placed at the heart so that the heart may be healed of sins? Let it be done, then.

Let what be done?

Let it be placed at the heart, let the heart be healed. It is good, good, that you not be anxious about the health of the body, except to ask it of God. If he knows it profits you, he will give it; if he does not give it to you, it would not have profited you to have it. How many lie sick in their beds who are innocent; and if they had been well, would go forth to commit crimes? To how many is health a hindrance? The robber who goes forth to the highway to kill a man—how much better for him to have been sick! The man who rises by night to break through another's wall—how much better for him to be tossed by fevers! He would be sick more innocently; he is well to his own wickedness.

God, then, knows what is good for us: let us only see to it that our heart be healthy of sins; and when perhaps we are lashed in the body, let us beseech him. Paul the Apostle asked him to take away the goad of the flesh, and he was unwilling to take it away. Was he troubled? Was he saddened and did he say he was forsaken? Rather he said he was not forsaken, because what he wished to be taken away was not taken away, so that the weakness might be healed. For this he found in the voice of the physician: *My grace is sufficient for you; for power is made perfect in weakness*[^31].

How, then, do you know that God does not want to heal you? Still it is good for you to be lashed. How do you know how rotten is what the physician cuts, as he drives the iron through the rotten flesh? Does he not know the measure—what he is doing, how far he is doing it? Does the howling of the one being cut draw back the hands of the physician who cuts with skill? The one cries out; the other cuts. Is he cruel who does not heed the one crying out, or rather merciful, who pursues the wound in order to heal the sick man? These things, my brothers, I have said so that no one may seek anything besides the help of God when perhaps we are under some chastisement of the Lord. See that you do not perish, see that you do not depart from the Lamb and be devoured by the lion.

**13.** We have said, then, why the tenth hour: let us look at what follows. *Andrew, the brother of Simon Peter, was one of the two who had heard from John and had followed him. He found his brother Simon, and says to him: We have found the Messiah, which is interpreted Christ*[^32]. "Messiah" in Hebrew is "Christ" in Greek, and "Anointed" in Latin. For "Christ" is named from anointing. "Anointing" is the Greek; therefore "Christ" means "anointed." He is anointed in a singular sense, anointed above all; whence all Christians are anointed, he above all. As it says in the Psalm, listen: *Therefore God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness above your fellows*[^33]. For his fellows are all the saints; but he in a singular sense is the Holy of holies, in a singular sense anointed, in a singular sense Christ.

## In the name of Peter the Church is figured.

**14.** *And he led him to Jesus. And Jesus, looking upon him, said: You are Simon, the son of John; you shall be called Cephas, which is interpreted Peter*[^34]. It is no great thing that the Lord said whose son this man was. What is great to the Lord? He knew all the names of his saints, whom he predestined before the foundation of the world; and do you marvel that he said to one man: You are the son of so-and-so, and you shall be called such-and-such? Is it great that he changed his name, and made Peter out of Simon? But "Peter" comes from "rock" (petra), and the rock is the Church: therefore in the name of Peter the Church is figured. And who is secure, except the one who builds upon the rock?

And what does the Lord himself say? *Whoever hears these words of mine and does them, I will liken him to a prudent man building upon the rock* (he does not yield to temptations): *the rain came down, the floods came, the winds blew and beat upon that house, and it did not fall; for it was founded upon the rock. Whoever hears my words and does not do them* (now let each one of us fear and beware), *I will liken him to a foolish man who built his house upon the sand: the rain came down, the floods came, the winds blew and beat upon that house, and it fell; and great was its ruin*[^35]. What does it profit one to enter the Church who wishes to build upon the sand? For by hearing and not doing, he builds indeed, but upon the sand. For if he hears nothing, he builds nothing; but if he hears, he builds. But we ask, where. For if he hears and does, upon the rock; if he hears and does not do, upon the sand. There are two kinds of builders: either upon the rock, or upon the sand.

What, then, of those who do not hear? Are they secure? Does he call them secure, because they build nothing? They are naked under the rain, before the winds, before the floods: when these come, they sweep them away before they cast down houses. Therefore there is one security: both to build, and to build upon the rock. If you wish to hear and not to do, you build; but you build a ruin: and when temptation comes, it casts down the house, and with its very fall it sweeps you away. But if you do not hear, you are naked, and you yourself are swept off by those temptations.

Hear, then, and do; this is the one remedy. How many perhaps today, by hearing and not doing, have been swept off by the flood of this festivity? For by hearing and not doing, this annual festivity comes as a flood, the torrent is filled, and it will pass and dry up; but woe to the one whom it has carried off! Let your Charity, then, know this: that unless one both hears and does, he does not build upon the rock; and he does not belong to that great name which the Lord so commended. For he made you attentive. For if Peter had been so called before, you would not so see the mystery of the rock; and you would think he was so called by chance, not by the providence of God: therefore the Lord willed him first to be called otherwise, so that by the very change of name the vital force of the sacred sign might be commended.

**15.** *And on the next day he wished to go forth into Galilee, and he found Philip. He says to him: Follow me. Now he was from the city of Andrew and Peter. And Philip found Nathanael*[^36]: Philip already called by the Lord. *And he said to him: Him of whom Moses in the Law and the Prophets wrote, we have found, Jesus the son of Joseph*[^37]. He was called the son of him to whom his mother was betrothed. For that he was conceived and born of her while she remained untouched, all Christians well know from the Gospel. This Philip said to Nathanael; he added also the place, *from Nazareth*[^38]. *And Nathanael said to him: From Nazareth can anything good come*[^39]. How is it to be understood, brothers? Not as some pronounce it: for it is also wont to be pronounced thus: *From Nazareth can anything good come?* For Philip's voice follows, and says: *Come and see*[^40]. But this reply can follow both pronunciations, whether you pronounce it as affirming: *From Nazareth something good can come*; and the other: *Come and see*; or as doubting, and asking the whole thing: *From Nazareth can anything good come? Come and see*. Since, then, whether it be pronounced in the one way or the other, the words that follow do not conflict; it is for us to seek what we should rather understand in these words.

**16.** What kind of man this Nathanael was, we prove in what follows. Hear what kind of man he was: the Lord himself bears witness. Great is the Lord, made known by the testimony of John; blessed is Nathanael, made known by the testimony of the truth. For although the Lord might not be commended by the testimony of John, he bore witness to himself; for the truth is sufficient for its own testimony. But because human beings could not grasp the truth, they sought the truth by a lamp: and therefore John, through whom the Lord might be shown, was sent.

Hear the Lord bearing witness to Nathanael: *And Nathanael said to him: From Nazareth can anything good come? Philip says to him: Come and see. And Jesus saw Nathanael coming to him, and says of him: Behold a true Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^41]. A great testimony! This was not said to Andrew, nor said to Peter, nor to Philip, which was said of Nathanael: *Behold a true Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^42].

## From a fisherman an emperor was won.

**17.** What, then, do we make of this, brothers? Ought this man to be first among the Apostles? Not only is he not found first among the Apostles, but Nathanael is neither in the middle nor last among the twelve—he to whom the Son of God bore so great a testimony, saying: *Behold a true Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^43]. Is the reason sought? So far as the Lord intimates, we find it with probability. For we ought to understand that this Nathanael was learned and skilled in the Law: for that reason the Lord was unwilling to place him among the disciples; because he chose the unlearned, that by them he might confound the world.

Hear the Apostle saying these things: *For consider your calling, brothers, that not many are powerful, not many are noble: but God chose the weak things of the world, to confound the strong; and the lowly things of the world and the contemptible God chose, and the things that are not, as though they were, that the things that are might be brought to nothing*[^44]. If a learned man were chosen, perhaps he would say he was chosen for this reason, that his learning deserved to be chosen. Our Lord Jesus Christ, wishing to break the necks of the proud, did not seek a fisherman through an orator; but from a fisherman he won an emperor. Cyprian was a great orator, but Peter the fisherman came first, through whom afterward there would believe not only the orator, but also the emperor. No noble was first chosen, no learned man; because God chose the weak things of the world, to confound the strong.

This man, then, was great and without guile: for this reason alone he was not chosen, lest the Lord should seem to anyone to have chosen the learned. And it came from his very learning in the Law that, when he had heard *from Nazareth*—for he had searched the Scriptures, and knew that from there the Savior was to be expected, which other scribes and Pharisees did not easily know—this man, then, most learned in the Law, when he had heard Philip saying: *We have found Jesus, of whom Moses in the Law and the Prophets wrote, from Nazareth, the son of Joseph*; that man who knew the Scriptures excellently, on hearing the name of Nazareth, was raised up in hope, and said: *From Nazareth something good can come*.

**18.** Now let us look at the rest concerning him: *Behold a true Israelite, in whom there is no guile*[^45]. What is, *in whom there is no guile*? Did he perhaps have no sin? Was he perhaps not sick? Was the physician perhaps not necessary to him? Far from it. No one here is born such that he does not need that physician. What, then, does it mean, *in whom there is no guile*? Let us seek somewhat more attentively; it will appear presently in the name of the Lord.

The Lord says "guile" (dolus); and everyone who understands Latin words knows that guile is when one thing is done and another pretended. Let your Charity attend. "Guile" (dolus) is not "grief" (dolor): I say this because many brothers less skilled in Latin speak so as to say "guile torments him" for what is "grief." Guile is fraud, it is pretense. When someone covers something in his heart and speaks another thing, it is guile, and he has, as it were, two hearts: he has one fold of the heart, as it were, where he sees the truth; and another fold, where he conceives a lie. And that you may know this to be guile, it is said in the Psalms: *Deceitful lips*[^46]. What is, *deceitful lips*? It follows: *With a heart and a heart they have spoken evil things*[^47]. What is, *with a heart and a heart*, but with a double heart?

If, then, there was no guile in this man, the physician judged him curable, not healthy. For one thing is healthy, another curable, another incurable: he who is sick with hope is called curable; he who is sick with despair, incurable; but he who is already healthy needs no physician. The physician, then, who had come to heal, saw this man curable, because there was no guile in him. How was there no guile in him? If he is a sinner, he confesses himself a sinner. For if he is a sinner and calls himself just, there is guile in his mouth. Therefore in Nathanael he praised the confession of sin; he did not judge that he was not a sinner.

**19.** Therefore, when the Pharisees, who seemed to themselves just, reproached the Lord because the physician mingled with the sick, and said: *Behold with whom he eats, with publicans and sinners*[^48]; the physician answered the madmen: *The healthy have no need of a physician, but those who are ill: I have not come to call the just, but sinners*[^49]. This is to say: Because you call yourselves just, while you are sinners, you judge yourselves healthy, while you languish; you repel the medicine, you keep no health.

Hence that Pharisee who had invited the Lord to dinner seemed to himself healthy: but that sick woman burst into the house, where she had not been invited, and made shameless by her desire for health, drew near, not to the Lord's head, not to his hands, but to his feet; she washed them with tears, wiped them with her hair, kissed them, anointed them with ointment—a sinful woman who made peace with the footprints of the Lord. That man, as though healthy, reproached the physician—that Pharisee who was reclining there; and he said within himself: *If this man were a prophet, he would know what woman had touched his feet*[^50]. He had suspected that the Lord did not know, because he did not repel her, as though to avoid being touched by unclean hands: but the Lord knew, and permitted himself to be touched, so that by the touch itself he might heal.

The Lord, seeing the heart of the Pharisee, put forth a likeness: *Two debtors owed a certain moneylender; one owed him fifty denarii, the other five hundred: when they had nothing to repay, he forgave them both; which of them loved him more? And he said: I suppose, Lord, the one to whom he forgave more. And turning to the woman, he said to Simon: Do you see this woman? I entered into your house, you gave me no water for my feet; but she washed my feet with tears, and wiped them with her hair: you gave me no kiss; she has not ceased to kiss my feet: you gave me no oil; she has anointed my feet with ointment. Therefore I say to you: her many sins are forgiven her, because she has loved much: but to whom little is forgiven, he loves little*[^51]. This is to say: You are more sick, but you think yourself healthy; you think little is forgiven you, when you are the greater debtor.

Rightly did she, because there was no guile in her, deserve the medicine. What is, there was no guile in her? She confessed her sins. This too he praises in Nathanael, that there was no guile in him: for many Pharisees who abounded in sins called themselves just, and brought guile, through which they could not be healed.

**20.** He saw, then, this man in whom there was no guile, and said: Behold a true Israelite, in whom there is no guile. *Nathanael says to him: Whence do you know me? Jesus answered and said: Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you; that is, under the fig tree. Nathanael answered him and said: Rabbi, you are the Son of God, you are the king of Israel*[^52]. Something great this Nathanael could understand, in what was said: *When you were under the fig tree, I saw you, before Philip called you*. For he uttered such a word: *You are the Son of God, you are the king of Israel*; such as Peter uttered so long after, when the Lord said to him: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood has not revealed it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^53]. And there he named the rock, and praised the firm foundation of the Church in that faith. Here this man already says: *You are the Son of God, you are the king of Israel*. Whence? Because it was said to him: *Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you*.

## We seek, because we have been sought.

**21.** It is to be asked whether this fig tree signifies something. For hear, my brothers: we find a fig tree cursed, because it had leaves only and had no fruit. At the origin of the human race, when Adam and Eve had sinned, they made themselves girdles of fig leaves: therefore fig leaves are understood as sins. Now Nathanael was under the fig tree, as it were under the shadow of death. The Lord saw him, of whom it was said: *For those who sat under the shadow of death, a light has risen for them*[^54]. What, then, was said to Nathanael? You say to me, O Nathanael: *Whence do you know me?* You now already speak with me, because Philip called you. The one whom he had already called through the apostle, he saw to belong to his Church.

O you Church, O you Israel, in whom there is no guile; if you are the people of Israel in whom there is no guile, you have now already known Christ through the Apostles, as Nathanael knew Christ through Philip. But in his mercy he saw you before you knew him, while you lay under sin. For did we first seek Christ, and not he us? Did we come, sick, to the physician, and not the physician to the sick? Had not that sheep been lost, and the shepherd, leaving the ninety-nine, sought it and found it, which he joyfully carried back on his shoulders? Had not that drachma been lost, and the woman lit a lamp, and sought through her whole house until she found it? And when she had found it: *Rejoice with me, she says to her neighbors, for I have found the drachma which I had lost*[^55].

So we too had been lost like the sheep, and had been lost like the drachma: and our shepherd found the sheep, but he sought the sheep; the woman found the drachma, but she sought the drachma. Who is the woman? The flesh of Christ. What is the lamp? *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ*[^56]. Therefore we were sought, that we might be found; having been found, we speak. Let us not be proud, because before we were found, we were lost, had we not been sought. Therefore let not those whom we love, and wish to win for the peace of the Catholic Church, say to us:

What do you want with us?

why do you seek us, if we are sinners? We seek you for this reason, that you may not perish: we seek, because we have been sought; we wish to find you, because we have been found.

**22.** And so, when Nathanael had said: *Whence do you know me?* the Lord said to him: *Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you*[^57]. O you Israel without guile, whoever you are, O people living by faith, before I called you through my apostles, when you were under the shadow of death and did not see me, I saw you. The Lord then says to him: *Because I said to you: I saw you under the fig tree, you believe: you will see greater things than these*[^58]. What is this, you will see greater things than these? And he says to him: *Amen, amen, I say to you, you will see heaven opened, and the angels ascending and descending upon the Son of man*[^59].

Brothers, I have said something greater than is *I saw you under the fig tree*. For it is more that the Lord justified us when we were called, than that he saw us lying under the shadow of death. For what would it have profited us if we had remained there, where he saw us? Would we not still be lying down? What is this greater thing? When did we see the angels ascending and descending upon the Son of man?

**23.** I had already at some time spoken of these ascending and descending angels; but lest you have forgotten, I say it briefly, as one recalling: for I would say more if I were not recalling, but only now introducing it.

Jacob saw a ladder in a dream, and on that ladder he saw angels ascending and descending; and the stone which he had placed at his head, he anointed[^60]. You have heard that "Messiah" is "Christ," you have heard that "Christ" is "anointed." For he did not so place the anointed stone, that he would come and adore it: otherwise it would be idolatry, not a signifying of Christ. The signifying, then, was made, as far as the signifying ought to be made, and Christ was signified. The stone was anointed, but not as an idol. The stone was anointed: why a stone? *Behold, I lay in Zion a chosen stone, precious, and whoever believes in him will not be confounded*[^61]. Why anointed? Because "Christ" comes from "chrism." But what did he then see on the ladder? Angels ascending and descending. So it is also with the Church, brothers: the angels of God, the good preachers, preaching Christ; that is, they ascend and descend upon the Son of man. How do they ascend, and how do they descend? We have an example from one man: hear the apostle Paul; what we have found in him, let us believe of the other preachers of the truth.

See Paul ascending: *I know a man in Christ, fourteen years ago, caught up to the third heaven, whether in the body or out of the body I do not know, God knows: and that he heard ineffable words which it is not permitted a man to utter*[^62]. You have heard him ascending; hear him descending. *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to carnal: as to little ones in Christ I gave you milk to drink, not solid food*[^63]. Behold, he who had ascended descends. Ask where he had ascended. To the third heaven. Ask where he descended. To giving milk to the little ones. Hear that he descends: *I became a little one*, he says, *in your midst, as if a nurse should cherish her children*[^64].

For we see both nurses and mothers descend to the little ones: and if they know how to say Latin words, they cut them short, and shake their tongue, as it were, so that from a fluent tongue they may make childish endearments; because if they spoke otherwise, the infant would not hear, nor would the infant make progress. And some fluent father, if he is so great an orator that the law courts resound with his tongue and the tribunals are shaken; if he has a little son, when he returns home he sets aside the forensic eloquence to which he had ascended, and with a childish tongue descends to the little one. Hear in one place the Apostle himself ascending and descending, in one sentence: For whether, he says, *we are beside ourselves, it is for God; or whether we are sober, it is for you*[^65]. What is, *we are beside ourselves, it is for God*? That we may see those things which it is not permitted a man to utter. What is, *we are sober, it is for you*? *For I judged that I knew nothing among you, except Jesus Christ, and him crucified*[^66]. If the Lord himself ascends and descends; it is manifest that his preachers too ascend by imitation, descend by preaching.

## The joys of truth.

**24.** And if we have held you somewhat longer, it was by design, that the unsuitable hours might pass: we suppose that those people have by now finished their vanity. But let us, brothers, now that we have been fed at the saving banquet, do what remains, so that we may solemnly fill the Lord's day with spiritual joys, and compare the joys of truth with the joys of vanity: and if we shudder, let us grieve; if we grieve, let us pray; if we pray, let us be heard; if we are heard, we win them too.
