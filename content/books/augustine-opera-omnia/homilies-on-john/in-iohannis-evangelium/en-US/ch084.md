# Tractate 84

## *Greater love than this no one has, than that a man lay down his life for his friends*[^1].

## We who approach the table of the Lord, where the body and blood of him who laid down his life for us is taken, ought also ourselves to lay down our lives for our brothers.

**1.** The fullness of the love by which we ought to love one another, dearly beloved brothers, the Lord defined when he said: *Greater love than this no one has, than that a man lay down his life for his friends*[^2]. Therefore, since he had said above, *This is my commandment, that you love one another, as I have loved you*[^3], to which words he added what you have now heard, *Greater love than this no one has, than that a man lay down his life for his friends,* there follows from this what this same evangelist John says in his epistle: *That as Christ laid down his life for us, so we also* ought *to lay down our lives for the brothers*[^4]; loving one another, surely, as he loved us, who laid down his life for us.

This, doubtless, is what is read in the Proverbs of Solomon: *If you sit down to dine at the table of a ruler, observe and understand what is set before you; and so put forth your hand, knowing that you ought to prepare such things*[^5]. For what is the table of a ruler, but that from which is taken the body and blood of him who laid down his life for us? And what is it to sit down at it, but to approach humbly? And what is it to observe and understand what is set before you, but worthily to consider so great a grace? And what is it so to put forth your hand, that you know you ought to prepare such things, but what I have already said — that as Christ laid down his life for us, so we also ought to lay down our lives for the brothers? For as the apostle Peter also says: *Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, that we should follow in his steps*[^6]. This is to prepare such things. This the blessed martyrs did with burning love; and if we do not celebrate their memorials in vain, and at the banquet where they too were filled approach the table of the Lord, we ought, as they did, to prepare such things ourselves. For this reason indeed we do not commemorate them at that table in the same way as the others who rest in peace, namely so as to pray for them, but rather so that they may pray for us, that we may cleave to their footsteps; because they fulfilled that love than which the Lord said there could be none greater. For they showed to their brothers such things as they had equally received from the Lord's table.

## Let us imitate Christ with devout obedience.

**2.** And let not this be said as though for that reason we could be equal to the Lord Christ, if for his sake we should carry martyrdom even to blood. He had the power of laying down his life and of taking it up again; but we neither live as long as we will, and we die even if we are unwilling: he, by dying, at once slew death in himself; we, in his death, are freed from death: his flesh did not see corruption; ours, after corruption, will at the end of the world be clothed through him with incorruption: he had no need of us in order to save us; we without him can do nothing: he offered himself to us branches as the vine; we cannot have life apart from him. Finally, even if brothers die for brothers, yet no martyr's blood is shed for the remission of the brothers' sins, which he did for us; nor in this did he give us something to imitate, but something for which to be thankful. So far, then, as the martyrs shed their blood for their brothers, so far did they show such things as they had received from the Lord's table. For in the other things I have mentioned — though I could not mention them all — the martyr of Christ is far from equal to Christ. But if anyone should compare himself, I do not say to the power of Christ, but to his innocence — I will not say by thinking that he heals another, but at least by having no sin of his own — even so he is more greedy than the account of salvation demands; it is far above him, he cannot hold so much. And it is well that he is warned by that saying of Proverbs, which at once adds and says: *But if you are more greedy, do not covet his foods; for it is better that you take nothing from there than that you take to yourself more than you ought. For these,* it says, *have a deceitful life*[^7] — that is, hypocrisy. For by saying that he is without sin, he cannot show himself righteous, but only feign it. Therefore it was said: *For these have a deceitful life.* There is one alone who could both have the flesh of a man and not have sin.

Rightly is that which follows enjoined upon us, and by such a word and proverb human weakness is addressed, and it is told: *Do not stretch yourself out, poor as you are, against the rich man*[^8]. For the rich man is he who, never liable to any debt either inherited or his own, is himself righteous and justifies others — Christ. Do not stretch yourself out against him, you who are so poor that you appear in prayer a daily beggar for the remission of sins. *But from your own counsel,* it says, *abstain.* From what, if not from deceitful presumption? For he, because he is not man only but also God, is therefore never guilty. *For if you direct your eye toward him, he will nowhere appear*[^9]. *Your eye,* that is, the human eye by which you discern human things, *if you direct it toward him, he will nowhere appear*; because he cannot be seen as you are able to see. *For he will prepare for himself wings like an eagle's, and will go into the house of his governor*: from where, indeed, he came to us, and did not find us such as he came.

Let us, then, love one another, as Christ also loved us and handed himself over for us. *For greater love than this no one has, than that a man lay down his life for his friends*[^10]. And let us imitate him with devout obedience in such a way that we presume by no boldness to compare ourselves to him.
