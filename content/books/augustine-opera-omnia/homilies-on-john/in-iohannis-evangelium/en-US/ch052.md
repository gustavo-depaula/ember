# Tractate 52

## *Now is my soul troubled* (Jn 12:27-36).

## He transferred us into himself, our head took us up into himself, he took on the affection of his members; and therefore *he troubled himself.* For it was fitting that the one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus, as he roused us to the highest things, should also suffer with us the lowest.

**1.** After the Lord Jesus Christ, in the words of yesterday's reading, exhorted his servants to follow him as he followed, having thus foretold his passion, that *unless a grain of wheat falling into the ground dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it bears much fruit*—where he roused those who would follow him to the kingdom of heaven to hate their life in this world, if they thought to keep it unto life eternal—he tempered his affection again to our weakness, and said, whence today's reading began: *Now is my soul troubled*[^1].

Why is your soul troubled, Lord? Surely a little before you said: *He who hates his life in this world keeps it unto life eternal*. Is your soul, then, loved in this world, so that it is troubled when the hour comes for it to go forth from this world? Who would dare to affirm this of the Lord's soul? But he transferred us into himself, our head took us up into himself, he took on the affection of his members: and therefore it was not troubled by anything, but, as was said of him when he raised Lazarus, *he troubled himself*[^2]. For it was fitting that the one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus, as he roused us to the highest things, should also suffer with us the lowest.

## O Lord mediator, God above us, man for our sake!

**2.** I hear him saying above: *The hour has come that the Son of man should be glorified: if the grain be put to death, it bears much fruit.* I hear: *He who hates his life in this world keeps it unto life eternal.* And I am not only permitted to marvel, but commanded to imitate. Then, in the following words: *If anyone serves me, let him follow me; and where I am, there will my servant be also*, I am set on fire to despise the world, and in my sight the whole of this life, however long it may be, is nothing but a vapor; before the love of eternal things all temporal things grow cheap to me: and again I hear this same Lord of mine, who by those words snatched me up from my weakness to his firmness, saying: *Now is my soul troubled.* What is this? How do you bid my soul to follow, if I see your soul troubled? how shall I bear what such great firmness feels as heavy? what foundation shall I seek, if the rock gives way? But I seem to hear in my thought the Lord answering me, and in a certain way saying: You will follow all the more, because I interpose myself thus that you may bear it: you heard the voice of my strength addressed to you; hear in me the voice of your weakness: I supply strength that you may run, and I do not check your hastening; but I transfer into myself what you tremble at, and I lay myself beneath you as the path by which you may pass.

O Lord mediator, God above us, man for our sake, I acknowledge your mercy! For in that you, so great, are troubled by the will of your love, you console many in your body who are troubled by the necessity of their weakness, lest they perish by despairing.

## God glorifies Christ even in his members.

**3.** Finally, let the man who wishes to follow hear by what way he is to follow. Perhaps a terrible hour has come upon him; the choice is set before him either of committing iniquity or of undergoing suffering; the weak soul is troubled, for whose sake the unconquered soul was troubled of its own accord: set the will of God before your own will. For attend to what your creator and your teacher then adds, who made you, and that he might teach you, himself also became what he made: for he became man, who made man; but he remained God unchangeable, and he changed man for the better. Hear, then, what he adds, when he had said: *Now is my soul troubled. And what shall I say,* he says? *Father, save me from this hour: but for this cause I came to this hour. Father, glorify your name*[^3]. He has taught you what to think, taught you what to say, whom to call upon, in whom to hope, whose sure and divine will to set before your own human and weak will. Do not, therefore, think that he fails from on high, because he wishes you to advance from below. For he deigned even to be tempted by the devil, by whom surely, if he had not willed it, he would not have been tempted, just as, if he had not willed it, he would not have suffered: and he answered the devil those things which you ought to answer in temptations. And he indeed was tempted, but was not endangered; that he might teach you, when you are endangered in temptation, to answer the tempter, and not to go after the tempter, but to come out from the danger of temptation.

And as here he said: *Now is my soul troubled,* so also where he says: *My soul is sorrowful even unto death*[^4]; and: *Father, if it can be, let this cup pass from me*[^5]: he took on the weakness of man, that he might teach one thus made sorrowful and troubled to say what follows: *Yet not what I will, but what you will, Father*[^6]. For thus a man is directed from human things to divine, when the divine will is set before the human will. But what is *glorify your name,* but, in his passion and resurrection? What else, then, is it, but that the Father should glorify the Son, who glorifies his name even in the like sufferings of his servants? Hence it is written of Peter that for this reason he said of him: *Another will gird you, and carry you where you do not wish*; because he wished to signify *by what death he was to glorify God*[^7]. Therefore in him too God glorified his name, because thus he glorifies Christ even in his members.

**4.** *There came, therefore, a voice from heaven: I have both glorified it, and I will glorify it again*[^8]. *I have glorified it,* before I made the world: *and I will glorify it again,* when he rises from the dead and ascends into heaven. And it can be understood otherwise. *I have glorified it,* when he was born of the Virgin, when he worked deeds of power, when, the heaven pointing him out by a star, he was worshiped by the Magi, when he was recognized by holy men full of the Holy Spirit, when he was declared by the Spirit descending in the form of a dove, when he was shown by the voice sounding from heaven, when he was transfigured on the mountain, when he did many miracles, when he healed and cleansed many, when from a very few loaves he fed so great a multitude, when he commanded the winds and the waves, when he raised the dead: *and I will glorify it again,* when he rises from the dead, when death shall no more have dominion over him, when God shall be exalted above the heavens, and his glory above all the earth.

**5.** *The crowd, therefore, that stood by and had heard it, said that it had thundered; others said: An angel spoke to him. Jesus answered and said: This voice came not for my sake, but for yours*[^9]. Here he shows that by that voice there was indicated, not to himself what he already knew, but to those to whom it had to be indicated. And as that voice came to pass by his divinity not for his own sake but for others', so his soul was troubled by his will not for his own sake but for others.

**6.** Attend to the rest. *Now,* he says, *is the judgment of the world*[^10]. What, then, is to be expected at the end of the age? But the judgment that is expected at the end will be the judgment of the living and the dead to be judged, the judgment of eternal rewards and punishments. What kind of judgment, then, is now? Already in the previous readings I admonished your Charity as much as I could, that a judgment also is spoken of not of condemnation but of separation: whence it is written: *Judge me, O God, and separate my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^11]. Now there are many judgments of God: whence it is said in the Psalm: *Your judgments are a great deep*[^12]. The Apostle also says: *O the depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge of God! how unsearchable are his judgments*[^13]! Among these judgments is also this that the Lord here says: *Now is the judgment of the world*; that judgment at the end being reserved, where last of all the living and the dead are to be judged.

The devil, then, possessed the human race, and held the guilty bound to punishment by the bond of their sins; he held dominion in the hearts of unbelievers, and dragged them, deceived and captive, to worship the creature, forsaking the Creator: but through faith in Christ, which was confirmed by his death and resurrection, through his blood, which was shed for the remission of sins, thousands of believers are freed from the dominion of the devil, are joined to the body of Christ, and under so great a head the faithful members are quickened by his one Spirit. This he called judgment, this separation, this expulsion of the devil from those he has redeemed.

## How is it said: *now he will be cast out*?

**7.** Finally, attend to what he says. As though we were asking what it was that he said, *Now is the judgment of the world,* he went on to explain it: for he says: *Now the prince of this world will be cast out*[^14]. We have heard what kind of judgment he said it was. Not, then, that which is to come at the end, where the living and the dead are to be judged, some separated to the right, others to the left; but the judgment by which *the prince of this world will be cast out.* How, then, was he within, and to where did he say he was to be cast out? Was he perhaps in the world, and sent outside the world? For if he were speaking of that judgment which is to come at the end, someone might suppose that the eternal fire, into which the devil with his angels and all who are of his party are to be sent—not by nature, but by fault; not because he created or begot them, but because he persuaded and held them—someone might, then, suppose that eternal fire to be outside the world, and that this is what was said, *he will be cast out.* But since he says: *Now is the judgment of the world*; and, explaining what he said: *Now,* he says, *the prince of this world will be cast out*: this is to be understood as what happens now, not what is to be so long afterward on the last day. The Lord, then, was foretelling what he knew, that after his passion and glorification many peoples throughout the whole world would believe, in whose hearts the devil was within; and when from faith they renounce him, he is cast out.

**8.** But someone says: Was he not cast out from the hearts of the Patriarchs and Prophets and the righteous of old? He was indeed cast out. How, then, was it said, *now he will be cast out*? How do we think, except that what then was done in very few men was now foretold as soon to be done in many and great peoples? So also that which was said: *But the Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*[^15], can raise a similar question, and admits a similar solution. For the Prophets did not foretell things to come without the Holy Spirit; or did not the aged Simeon and the widow Anna recognize the Lord as an infant in the Holy Spirit; and Zechariah and Elizabeth, who foretold so many things through the Holy Spirit about him, not yet born but already conceived? But *the Spirit was not yet given*; that is, that abundance of spiritual grace by which those gathered together would speak in the tongues of all, and so the Church to come would be foretold in the tongues of all nations: by which spiritual grace the peoples would be gathered, by which sins would be forgiven far and wide, and thousands of thousands reconciled to God.

**9.** What, then, someone says, because the devil is cast out of the hearts of believers, does he no longer tempt any of the faithful? On the contrary, he does not cease to tempt. But it is one thing to reign within, another to assault from without: for sometimes an enemy assaults even the most fortified city, and does not take it. And if some of his missiles, when thrown, reach us, the Apostle advises whence they may not harm us; he recalls the breastplate and the shield of faith[^16]. And if at times he wounds, there is one present who heals. For as it was said to those who fight: *These things I write to you, that you may not sin*: so those who are wounded hear what follows: *And if anyone has sinned, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous; he is the propitiation for our sins*[^17]. For what do we pray when we say: *Forgive us our debts,* but that our wounds may be healed? And what else do we ask when we say: *Lead us not into temptation*[^18], but that he who lies in wait, or contends from without, may break in on no side, may overcome us by no deceit, by no power? Yet however great the engines of war he raises against us, when he does not hold the place of the heart where faith dwells, he has been cast out. But *unless the Lord guard the city, he who guards it will keep watch in vain*[^19]. Do not, then, presume upon yourselves, if you do not wish to call the devil, who has been cast out, back inside again.

**10.** But far be it from us to suppose that the devil is called the prince of the world in such a way that we believe him able to have dominion over heaven and earth. But "the world" is the name for the wicked men who are spread throughout the whole earth: as "house" is the name for those by whom it is inhabited, according as we say, It is a good house, or, It is a bad house, not when we blame or praise the structure of walls and roofs, but the character either of good men or of wicked ones. So, then, it was said: *The prince of this world*: that is, the prince of all the wicked who dwell in the world. "The world" is also the name for the good, who likewise are spread throughout the whole earth: hence the Apostle says: *God was in Christ, reconciling the world to himself*[^20]. These are they from whose hearts the prince of this world is cast out.

## *All things,* he says, *I will draw after me*, that he may be their head.

**11.** When, therefore, he had said: *Now the prince of this world will be cast out; And I,* he says, *if I be lifted up from the earth, will draw all things after me*[^21]. What *all things,* but those from whom he is cast out? Now he did not say, all men, but, *all things*: *for faith is not of all*[^22]. So he did not refer this to the totality of men, but to the wholeness of the creature; that is, spirit, and soul, and body; both that by which we understand, and that by which we live, and that by which we are visible and able to be touched. For he who said: *A hair of your head will not perish*[^23], draws all things after himself. Or, if by *all things* men themselves are to be understood, we can speak of all who are predestined to salvation: of whom he says that none will perish, when he was speaking above of his sheep[^24]. Or surely all kinds of men, whether in all tongues, or in all ages, or in all ranks of honors, or in all varieties of natural gifts, or in all the professions of lawful and useful arts, and whatever else can be named according to the innumerable differences by which men differ among themselves, apart from sins alone, from the highest to the lowliest, from the king to the beggar; *all things,* he says, *I will draw after me*: that he may be their head, and they his members.

But *if I,* he says, *be lifted up from the earth*; that is, when I shall be lifted up: for he does not doubt that what he came to fulfill will be. This refers to what he said above: *But if the grain dies, it bears much fruit*. For what else did he call his being lifted up, but his suffering on the cross? Which the Evangelist himself too did not pass over in silence: for he added, and said: *Now he said this, signifying by what death he was to die*[^25]?

**12.** *The crowd answered him: We have heard out of the Law that the Christ remains forever; and how do you say: The Son of man must be lifted up? And who is this Son of man?*[^26] They kept in memory that the Lord constantly said he was the Son of man. For in this place he does not say: If the Son of man be lifted up from the earth; but he had said above—which was read and treated yesterday, when those Gentiles were announced who desired to see him—*The hour has come that the Son of man should be glorified*. So, holding this in mind, and understanding what he now says, *When I shall be lifted up from the earth,* of the death of the cross, they asked him, and said: *We have heard out of the Law that the Christ remains forever; and how do you say: The Son of man must be lifted up? For who is this Son of man?* For if he is the Christ, they say, he remains forever: if he remains forever, how will he be lifted up from the earth, that is, how will he die by the suffering of the cross? For they understood that he had said this, which they were planning to do. It was not, then, infused wisdom that opened to them the obscurity of these words, but a goaded conscience.

## Believe in the truth, that you may be reborn in the truth.

**13.** *Jesus therefore said to them: Yet a little while the light is in you.* Hence it is that you understand that the Christ remains forever. *Walk, therefore, while you have the light, that the darkness may not overtake you*[^27]. Walk, draw near, understand the whole: both that Christ will die, and that he will live forever, and that he will shed his blood to redeem, and that he will ascend to the heights to lead you. But the darkness will overtake you, if you so believe Christ's eternity as to deny in him the lowliness of his death.

*And he who walks in darkness does not know where he is going*[^28]. So he can stumble against the stone of stumbling and the rock of offense, which the Lord was to the blind Jews: just as to those who believe the stone which the builders rejected became the head of the corner[^29]. For this reason they disdained to believe in Christ, because their impiety despised him as dead, laughed at him as killed: and this very thing was the death of the grain to be multiplied, and the lifting up of him who draws all things after himself. *While you have the light,* he says, *believe in the light, that you may be sons of the light*[^30]. When you have heard something of the truth, believe in the truth, that you may be reborn in the truth.

**14.** *These things Jesus spoke, and he went away, and hid himself from them*[^31]. Not from those who had begun to believe and to love, not from those who had come to meet him with branches of palms and with praises: but from those who saw and envied; because they did not even see, but stumbled in blindness against that stone. But when Jesus hid himself from those who desired to kill him (of which you must often be reminded, lest you forget), he took counsel for our weakness; he did not detract from his own power.
