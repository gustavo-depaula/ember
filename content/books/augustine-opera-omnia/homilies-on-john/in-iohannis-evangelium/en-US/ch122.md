# Tractate 122

## *Afterward Jesus revealed himself again at the sea of Tiberias* (Jn 20:30-31).

## This chapter indicates, as it were, the end of this book; but there is then related here how the Lord revealed himself at the sea of Tiberias, and in the catch of fish commended to the Church the sacrament of what the final resurrection of the dead will be.

**1.** After the narrative of the event in which the disciple Thomas, the places of the wounds in Christ's flesh being offered him to touch, saw what he was unwilling to believe and believed, John the evangelist inserts these things and says: *Now Jesus did many other signs also in the presence of his disciples, which are not written in this book. But these are written, that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God; and that believing, you may have life in his name*[^1]. This chapter indicates, as it were, the end of this book; but there is then related here how the Lord revealed himself at the sea of Tiberias, and in the catch of fish commended what the final resurrection of the dead will be. To commend this, then, I think it serves that what was interposed as, so to speak, the end of the book should be a kind of preface to the narrative that was to follow, so as in a manner to give it a more eminent place—the narrative that begins thus: *Afterward Jesus revealed himself again at the sea of Tiberias; and he revealed himself thus. There were together Simon Peter and Thomas called the Twin, and Nathanael who was from Cana of Galilee, and the sons of Zebedee, and two others of his disciples. Simon Peter said to them, I am going fishing. They said to him, We are coming with you*[^2].

**2.** It is wont to be asked concerning this fishing of the disciples, why Peter and the sons of Zebedee returned to what they had been before they were called by the Lord; for they were fishermen when he said to them: *Come after me, and I will make you fishers of men*[^3]. For then they followed him so as to cleave to his teaching, leaving all things; so much so that, when that rich man departed from him sorrowful, to whom he had said, *Go, sell what you have and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven; and come, follow me*[^4], Peter said to him: *Behold, we have left all and followed you*[^5]. What, then, does it mean that now, as though the apostolate were abandoned, they become what they were, and take up again what they had left, as though forgetting that they had heard: *No one putting his hand to the plow and looking back is fit for the kingdom of heaven*[^6]? Now if they had done this while Jesus was dead, before he had risen from the dead—which indeed they could not, since the day on which he was crucified held them all attentive until his burial, which was done before evening; and the next day was the sabbath, when, observing their ancestral custom, they were surely not permitted to work; and on the third day the Lord rose and called them back to the hope concerning him which they had now begun not to have—yet if they had done it then, we should suppose they had done so out of the despair that had taken hold of their minds. But now, after he had been restored to them alive from the tomb, after the most evident truth of his living-again flesh had been offered to their eyes and hands, not only to be seen but even to be touched and handled; after the places of the wounds had been examined, down to the confession of the apostle Thomas, who had declared he would not otherwise believe; after they had received the Holy Spirit by his breathing; after the words uttered by his mouth into their ears: *As the Father has sent me, so I send you; whose sins you forgive, they are forgiven them, and whose sins you retain, they are retained*[^7]: suddenly they become as they had been, fishers not of men but of fish.

**3.** To those, then, who are troubled by this it must be answered that they were not forbidden by their craft—lawful, that is, and permitted—to seek the necessary means of life, the integrity of their apostolate being preserved, if at any time they had nothing else from which to live.

Unless perhaps someone will dare to think or to say that the apostle Paul did not belong to the perfection of those who, leaving all things, followed Christ, because, lest he should burden any of those to whom he preached the gospel, he earned his own living with his own hands: where rather was fulfilled what he says: *I labored more than all of them*[^8]; and he added: *Yet not I, but the grace of God with me*[^9]: so that this too may appear to be attributed to the grace of God, that he could, both in mind and in body, labor so much more than all of them, that he neither ceased from preaching the gospel, nor yet, like them, supported this life from the gospel; while he sowed it, among so many nations in which the name of Christ had not been prophesied, far more widely and fruitfully. Here he shows that to live from the gospel, that is, to have one's living, was not a necessity imposed on the apostles, but a power given. This power the same apostle mentions, saying: *If we have sown spiritual things among you, is it a great matter if we reap your carnal things? If others share in this power over you, do not we more? But we,* he says, *have not used this power*[^10]. And a little later: *Those who serve the altar,* he says, *share with the altar; so also the Lord ordained that those who proclaim the gospel should live by the gospel: but I have used none of these things*[^11]. It is, then, sufficiently plain that it was not commanded, but placed within the apostles' power, that they should live from no other source than the gospel, and from those among whom, by preaching the gospel, they sowed spiritual things, should reap carnal things—that is, take the support of this flesh, and as soldiers of Christ receive their due pay, as from the provincials of Christ. Hence the same excellent soldier had said a little above on this matter: *Who ever serves as a soldier at his own expense?*[^12] Which, however, he himself did, because he labored more than all of them. If, then, blessed Paul, in order that he might not use that power which he surely had together with the other preachers of the gospel, but might serve as a soldier at his own pay, lest by his teaching, as though for sale, he should offend nations wholly estranged from the name of Christ, learned, having been brought up otherwise, a craft he did not know, so that, while the teacher supported himself with his own hands, no hearer might be burdened—how much more blessed Peter, who had already been a fisherman, did what he knew, if for that present time he found nothing else from which to live?

**4.** But someone will answer: And why did he not find it, since the Lord promised, saying: *Seek first the kingdom and the justice of God, and all these things shall be added to you*[^13]? Surely even thus the Lord fulfilled what he promised. For who else furnished the fish that were caught? He who is to be believed to have brought upon them the lack by which they were driven to go fishing, for no other reason than that, willing to display an arranged miracle, he might both feed the preachers of his gospel and at the same time increase that very gospel by the great sacrament that was in the number of the fish, which he was about to commend. Of which matter we too must now say what he himself shall furnish.

**5.** *So Simon Peter says, I am going fishing. They say to him* who were with him: *We are coming with you too. And they went out and got into the boat; and that night they caught nothing. But when morning had now come, Jesus stood on the shore; yet the disciples did not know that it was Jesus. So Jesus says to them, Children, have you any fish? They answered him, No. He says to them, Cast the net on the right side of the boat, and you will find. So they cast it, and now they could not haul it in for the multitude of fish. So that disciple whom Jesus loved says to Peter, It is the Lord. When Simon Peter heard that it was the Lord, he girded his tunic about him, for he was naked, and threw himself into the sea. But the other disciples came in the boat (for they were not far from the land, but about two hundred cubits off), dragging the net full of fish. When they had gone ashore, they saw a charcoal fire laid, with a fish placed on it, and bread. Jesus says to them, Bring some of the fish that you have now caught. Simon Peter went up and hauled the net ashore, full of large fish, a hundred and fifty-three; and although there were so many, the net was not torn*[^14].

**6.** This is a great sacrament in the great Gospel of John; and, that it might be the more emphatically commended, it was written in the last place. That, then, seven disciples were in this fishing—Peter, and Thomas, and Nathanael, and the two sons of Zebedee, and two others whose names are not given—these, by their sevenfold number, signify the end of time. For the whole of time revolves in seven days. To this belongs the fact that, when morning had come, Jesus stood on the shore; for the shore too is the end of the sea, and therefore signifies the end of the age. The same end of the age is shown also by the fact that Peter drew the net to land, that is, to the shore. This the Lord himself made plain, where in another place he gave the comparison of a dragnet cast into the sea: *And they draw it,* he says, *to the shore*[^15]. Explaining what the shore was, he says: *So it will be at the consummation of the age*[^16].

## The sacrament of the Church in the two catches of fish.

**7.** But that was a parable in word, not in deed; whereas by a deed, just as in this place he signified what the Church will be like at the end of the age, so by another catch of fish the Lord signified what the Church is like now. For the fact that he did the one at the beginning of his preaching, but this after his resurrection, shows hereby that that catch of fish signifies the good and the bad, whom the Church now has; but this signifies only the good, whom she will have for eternity, the resurrection of the dead being completed at the end of this age. Finally, there Jesus did not stand on the shore as here, when he ordered the fish to be caught; but, *going up into one boat, which was Simon's, he asked him to put out a little from the land; and sitting in it he taught the crowds. And when he had ceased speaking, he said to Simon, Put out into the deep, and let down your nets for a catch*[^17]. And there what was caught of fish was in the boats, not, as here, where they drew the net to land. By these signs, and any others that may be found, there the Church was figured in this age, but here at the end of the age: therefore that was done before, but this after, the Lord's resurrection; for there Christ signified us as called, here as raised up. There the nets are not cast on the right, lest they signify only the good, nor on the left, lest only the bad; but indifferently: *Let down,* he says, *your nets for a catch*, that we may understand the good and the bad mingled: but here he says: *Cast the net on the right side of the boat*, that he might signify those who stood on the right, the good alone. There the net was breaking, to signify schisms: but here, since then, in that supreme peace of the saints, there will be no schisms, it belonged to the evangelist to say: *And although there were so many*, that is, so large, *the net was not torn*; as if he were looking back to where it was torn, and by comparison with that evil were commending this good. There so great a multitude of fish was caught that the two boats, being filled, were sinking, that is, were pressed down toward submersion: for they were not sunk, yet they were endangered. For from where do there arise in the Church so many things that we lament, except when so great a multitude cannot be withstood, which, with its ways utterly estranged from the path of the saints, enters almost to the sinking of discipline? But here they cast the net on the right side, *and now they could not haul it in for the multitude of fish.* What does it mean, *now they could not haul it in*, except that those who belong to the resurrection of life, that is, to the right, and end their days within the nets of the Christian name, will appear only on the shore, that is, at the end of the age, when they have risen? Therefore they could not so haul in the nets as to pour back into the boat the fish they had caught; as was done with all those for whom the net was broken and the boats were pressed down. But the Church holds these on the right, after the end of this life, in the sleep of peace, lying hidden as it were in the deep, until the net comes to the shore to which it was being drawn, as though from two hundred cubits. As for what there was figured by the two boats, on account of circumcision and uncircumcision; this, in this place, I think was figured by the two hundred cubits, on account of the elect of both kinds, both of circumcision and of uncircumcision, as though a hundred and a hundred; because in sum the number one hundred passes to the right. Finally, in that fishing the number of the fish is not stated, as though there happened that which was foretold through the prophet: *I have declared and spoken; they are multiplied beyond number*[^18]: but here there are not any beyond number, but the number is certain—a hundred and fifty-three; the meaning of which number must be rendered, the Lord helping.

## What the number one hundred and fifty-three signifies.

**8.** For if we set up a number that signifies the Law, what will it be but ten? For it is most certain to us that the Decalogue of the Law—that is, those ten most well-known precepts—was first written by the finger of God on two stone tablets. But the Law, when grace does not help, makes transgressors, and is only in the letter; for on this account especially the Apostle says: *The letter kills, but the spirit gives life*[^19]. Let the spirit, then, be added to the letter, lest the letter kill him whom the spirit does not give life; but that we may carry out the precepts of the Law, not by our own strength, but by the gift of the Savior. Now when grace is added to the Law, that is, the spirit to the letter, the number seven is in a manner added to the number ten. For that by this number, namely seven, the Holy Spirit is signified, the documents of the sacred Scriptures, which are to be heeded, attest. For surely holiness, or sanctification, belongs properly to the Holy Spirit; whence, although the Father too is spirit and the Son is spirit, since God is spirit, and the Father is holy and the Son is holy, yet by his proper name the Spirit of both is called the Holy Spirit. Where, then, did sanctification first sound in the Law, except on the seventh day? For God did not sanctify the first day, on which he made light; or the second, on which he made the firmament; or the third, on which he separated the sea from the land, and the land brought forth grass and trees; or the fourth, on which the stars were created; or the fifth, on which were made the animals that live in the waters and fly in the air; or the sixth, on which the living soul of the land and man himself; but he sanctified the seventh day, on which he rested from his works. Fittingly, then, by the number seven the Holy Spirit is signified. Isaiah the prophet too says: *The Spirit of God shall rest upon him*; and then commending him by a sevenfold work or gift: *the Spirit of wisdom and understanding, the Spirit of counsel and fortitude, the Spirit of knowledge and piety; and the Spirit of the fear of God shall fill him*[^20]. And what in the Apocalypse? Are not seven Spirits of God spoken of, though it is one and the same Spirit, dividing his gifts to each as he wills? But the sevenfold operation of the one Spirit was so named by the same Spirit, who was present to the writer, that they were called seven Spirits.

And so when the Holy Spirit, through the number seven, is added to the ten of the Law, they become seventeen; which number, when all the numbers it contains from one up to itself are added together, grows to a hundred and fifty-three. For if to one you add two, they become three; if to these you add three and four, they all become ten; then if you add all the numbers that follow up to seventeen, the sum is brought to the above-mentioned number. That is, if to ten, at which you had arrived from one up to four, you add five, they become fifteen; add to these six, and they become twenty-one; add to these seven, and they become twenty-eight; add to these eight and nine and ten, and they become fifty-five; add to these eleven and twelve and thirteen, and they become ninety-one; add again to these fourteen and fifteen and sixteen, and they become a hundred and thirty-six: to this number add that which remains, of which we are speaking, that is seventeen, and that number of the fish will be completed. Not, then, are a hundred and fifty-three saints only signified as about to rise to eternal life, but thousands of saints belonging to the grace of the Spirit; by which grace there is concord with the Law of God as with an adversary; so that, with the Spirit giving life, the letter may not kill, but what is commanded by the letter may be fulfilled with the help of the Spirit, and whatever falls short may be forgiven. All, then, who belong to this grace are figured by this number, that is, are figuratively signified.

This number also contains the number fifty taken three times, and, in addition, the three themselves on account of the mystery of the Trinity: now the number fifty is completed by multiplying seven by seven, with the addition of one; for seven times seven is forty-nine. And one is added, so that by it may be signified that he is one who, through the seven, is shown by reason of the sevenfold operation: and we know that the Holy Spirit was sent on the fiftieth day after the Lord's ascension, whom the disciples were ordered to await as promised.

**9.** Not in vain, then, are these fish said to be both so many and so great—that is, both a hundred and fifty-three and large. For so it is written: *And he drew the net to land, full of large fish, a hundred and fifty-three.* For when the Lord had said, *I have not come to abolish the Law, but to fulfill it*[^21]—being surely about to give the Spirit through whom the Law could be fulfilled, as though about to add seven to ten—after a very few words interposed he says: *Whoever, then, breaks one of these least commandments and teaches men so, shall be called least in the kingdom of heaven; but whoever does and teaches them, shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven*[^22]. He, then, will be able to belong to the number of the large fish. But that least one, who breaks by his deeds what he teaches by his words, can be in such a Church as is signified by that first catch of fish, having good and bad, since it too is called the kingdom of heaven; on which account he says: *The kingdom of heaven is like a net cast into the sea, and gathering of every kind*[^23]. Where he wishes to be understood also the good and the bad, whom he says are to be separated on the shore, that is, at the end of the age. Finally, to show that these least are the reprobate, who teach good things by speaking, which they break by living wickedly, and are not going to be even as least in eternal life, but altogether not to be there; when he had said, *shall be called least in the kingdom of heaven*, he at once added: *For I tell you that unless your justice abounds more than that of the scribes and Pharisees, you will not enter the kingdom of heaven*[^24]. They are surely the scribes and Pharisees who sit on the chair of Moses, and of whom he says: *Do what they say; but what they do, do not do; for they say and do not do*[^25]: they teach by their words what they break by their ways. It follows, then, that he who is least in the kingdom of heaven, such as the Church now is, does not enter into the kingdom of heaven, such as the Church will then be; since, by teaching what he breaks, he will not belong to the fellowship of those who do what they teach: and therefore he will not be in the number of the large fish; since *whoever does and teaches, shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven.* And because he will be great here, therefore he will be there, where that least one will not be. For so great will they be there, that he who is least there is greater than the one than whom no one here is greater. But yet those who are great here, that is, who in the kingdom of heaven, where the dragnet gathers good and bad, do the good things they teach, they will be the greater in that eternity of the kingdom of heaven—the very ones whom these fish on the right, belonging to the resurrection of life, indicate.

There follows the account of the Lord's meal with these seven disciples, and of the things he said after the meal, and of the very end of the Gospel, that we may discuss what God shall grant: but this is not to be confined within this homily.
