# Tractate 31

## *Where is this man about to go? Surely not into the dispersion of the gentiles?* (Jn 7:25-36).

## The Jews, not knowing, foretold our salvation. For we did not learn Christ in the flesh, and yet we have been granted to eat his flesh, and to be members in his flesh. Because he sent to us his servants, his brothers, his members, himself; for he sent to us his members, and made us his members.

**1.** Your Charity remembers that in the earlier sermons it was both read in the Gospel, and discussed by us as we could, that the Lord Jesus went up as if secretly to the feast, not because he feared to be seized—he whose power it was not to be seized—but to signify that even on that feast day which was celebrated by the Jews, he was hidden, and that he was a mystery: in today's reading, then, the power appeared which had been thought timidity; for he spoke openly on the feast day, so that the crowds marveled, and said what we heard when the reading was read: *Is not this the man whom they sought to kill? And behold, he speaks openly, and they say nothing to him: have the rulers truly recognized that this is the Christ?*[^1]

Those who knew with what savagery he was sought, marveled with what power he was not seized. Then, not fully understanding his power, they thought it was the rulers' knowledge that they themselves had recognized that he was the Christ: and therefore they spared him whom they had so greatly sought to kill.

## Who will declare his generation.

**2.** Then those same men, among themselves, who had said, *Have the rulers recognized that this is the Christ?*, made for themselves a question by which it might seem to them that he was not the Christ: for they added and said, *But this man we know whence he is; but Christ, when he comes, no one knows whence he is*[^2]. Whence this opinion arose among the Jews, that when Christ comes no one knows whence he is (for it did not arise emptily); if we consider the Scriptures, we find, brothers, that the holy Scriptures said of Christ that *he will be called a Nazarene*[^3]. Therefore they foretold whence he is.

Again, if we ask for the place of his nativity, as though he were from where he was born; this too was not hidden from the Jews, because of the Scriptures that had foretold it. For when the magi, having seen the star, sought to adore him, they came to Herod, and said what they were seeking and what they wanted: but he, having called together those who knew the Law, asked them where Christ was to be born: they said, *In Bethlehem of Judah*[^4]; and they also brought forward the prophetic testimony[^5].

If, then, the Prophets had foretold both the place whence was the origin of his flesh, and the place where his mother bore him; whence arose this opinion among the Jews, which we just now heard, *Christ, when he comes, no one knows whence he is*, unless because the Scriptures had proclaimed and announced beforehand both things? According to the man, the Scriptures had foretold whence he was: according to God, he was hidden from the impious, and sought by the pious. For to this end these men too said, *Christ, when he comes, no one knows whence he is*; because this opinion had been generated in them by what was said through Isaiah: *But who will declare his generation?*[^6] Finally, the Lord himself answered both—that they knew him whence he was, and that they did not know him; that he might bear witness to the holy prophecy that was foretold of him, both according to the humanity of his weakness, and according to the divinity of his majesty.

**3.** Hear, then, the Word of the Lord, brothers, see how he confirmed for them both what they said, *This man we know whence he is*, and what they said, *Christ, when he comes, no one knows whence he is*. Jesus, then, cried out, teaching in the temple: *You both know me, and you know whence I am; and I have not come from myself, but he who sent me is true, whom you do not know*[^7]. This is to say: You both know me, and you do not know me: this is to say: You both know whence I am, and you do not know whence I am.

You know whence I am, Jesus of Nazareth, whose parents too you know. For in this matter alone the virgin's childbearing was hidden, for which her husband nevertheless was a witness: for he could faithfully attest to this, who could also as a husband be jealous. This excepted, then—the virgin's childbearing—they knew all in Jesus that pertains to a man: his face was known, his homeland was known, his lineage was known, where he was born was known. Rightly, then, he said: You both know me, and you know whence I am, according to the flesh and the human form he bore: but according to his divinity: *And I have not come from myself, but he who sent me is true, whom you do not know*; but that you may know him, believe in him whom he sent, and you will know. For *no one has ever seen God, except the only-begotten Son who is in the bosom of the Father; he has declared him*[^8]: and, *No one knows the Father except the Son, and the one to whom the Son wills to reveal him*[^9].

**4.** Finally, when he had said, *But he who sent me is true, whom you do not know*; to show them whence they could know what they did not know, he added: *I know him.* Therefore seek from me, that you may know him. But why do I know him? *Because I am from him, and he sent me*[^10]. Magnificently he showed both. I am from him, he says; because the Son is from the Father, and whatever the Son is, is from the one whose Son he is. Therefore we call the Lord Jesus God from God; we do not call the Father God from God, but only God: and we call the Lord Jesus Light from Light; we do not call the Father Light from Light, but only Light. To this, then, pertains what he said: I am from him. But that you see me in the flesh, he sent me. Where you hear, he sent me, do not understand an unlikeness of nature, but the authority of the one begetting.

## The Lord was not born under fate.

**5.** They sought, then, to seize him, *and no one laid hands on him, because his hour had not yet come*[^11]: that is, because he did not will it. For what is it, his hour had not yet come? For the Lord was not born under fate. This is not to be believed even of you, much less of him through whom you were made. If your hour is his will; what is his hour, except his own will? He did not, then, speak of an hour by which he would be compelled to die, but by which he would deign to be killed.

For he awaited the time in which he would die, because he also awaited the time in which he would be born. Speaking of this time, the Apostle says: *But when the fullness of time came, God sent his Son*[^12]. Therefore many say: Why did Christ not come earlier? To whom it must be answered, because the fullness of time had not yet come, he ordering it through whom times were made: for he knew when he ought to come. First, through a long series of times and years he had to be foretold; for it was no small thing that was to come: long was he to be foretold, always to be held. The greater the judge who was coming, the longer the series of heralds that went before.

Finally, when the fullness of time came, there came also the one who would free us from time. For freed from time, we are to come to that eternity where there is no time: nor is it there said, When will the hour come; for it is an everlasting day, which is neither preceded by yesterday nor shut out by tomorrow. But in this world the days roll on, and some pass and others come; none abides: and the moments in which we speak drive one another out in turn, nor does the first syllable stand still, that the second may be able to sound. From the time we have been speaking, we have aged a little, and without any doubt I am now older than in the morning: so nothing stands still, nothing remains fixed in time.

We ought therefore to love him through whom times were made, that we may be freed from time, and fixed in eternity, where there is now no changeableness of times. Great, therefore, is the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ, that he was made for our sake in time, through whom times were made; that he was made among all things, through whom all things were made[^13]: that he was made what he himself made. For he was made what he had made; for he was made man, he who had made man, lest what he had made should perish. According to this dispensation, the hour of his nativity had already come, and he was born: but the hour of his Passion had not yet come, and therefore he had not yet suffered.

## He departed by power, who had not come by necessity.

**6.** Finally, that you may know not the necessity, but the power of the one dying: I say this on account of some who, when they hear, his hour had not yet come, are built up toward believing in fate, and their hearts become foolish: that you may know, then, the power of the one dying, recall the Passion itself, gaze upon the crucified. He said, hanging on the wood: *I thirst.* They, hearing this, on a reed by means of a sponge offered him vinegar on the cross: he received it, and said: *It is finished*; and bowing his head, he gave up his spirit[^14]. You see the power of the one dying, because he was waiting for this, until all things should be fulfilled that had been foretold of him as to come before his death. For the prophet had said: *They gave me gall for my food, and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink*[^15]. He waited that all these things might be fulfilled: after they were fulfilled, he said: *It is finished*; and he departed by power, because he had not come by necessity.

Therefore some marveled more at this power of the one dying than at the power of the one working miracles. For they came to the cross, that the bodies might be taken down from the wood, since the Sabbath was dawning; and the robbers were found living. For the punishment of the cross was harsher for this reason, because it tortured longer, and all the crucified were put to death by a long death. But those men, lest they remain on the wood, with their legs broken, were forced to die, that they might be taken down from there. The Lord, however, was found dead[^16], and men marveled: and those who despised him living, so marveled at him dead, that some said: *Truly this man was the Son of God*[^17].

Whence is that also, brothers, where he says to those seeking him: *I am he*; and they, going backward, all fell down[^18]. There was, then, in him the highest power. Nor was he compelled by an hour to die; but he awaited the hour in which his will might opportunely be done, not in which an unwilling necessity might be fulfilled.

**7.** *But of the crowd many believed in him.* The lowly and the poor the Lord made sound. The rulers were raging; and therefore they not only did not acknowledge the physician, but even desired to kill him. There was a certain crowd that quickly saw its sickness, and recognized his medicine without delay. See what that crowd, moved by the miracles, said to itself: *When Christ comes, will he do more signs than this man does?*[^19] Surely, if there will not be two, this is he. They believed in him, then, saying these things.

**8.** But those rulers, having heard the faith of the multitude, and that murmur by which Christ was glorified, sent attendants to seize him. Whom would they seize? One still unwilling? Because, then, they could not seize one unwilling, they were sent so that they might hear one teaching. Teaching what? Jesus, then, says: *Yet a little time I am with you.* What you wish to do now, you will do, but not now; because now I will it not. Why do I not will it yet now? Because *yet a little time I am with you: and then I go to him who sent me*[^20]. I must fulfill my dispensation, and so come to my Passion.

## Visible in the flesh, invisible in his majesty.

**9.** *You will seek me, and you will not find me; and where I am, you cannot come*[^21]. Here he foretold his resurrection: for they were unwilling to acknowledge him present, and afterward sought him, when they saw the multitude already believing in him. For great signs were done, even when the Lord rose, and ascended into heaven. Then through the disciples great things were done; but he through them, who also through himself: for he himself had said to them: *Without me you can do nothing*[^22].

When that lame man who sat at the gate rose at the voice of Peter, and walked on his own feet, so that men marveled, Peter so addressed them, that he did not do these things in his own power, but in the strength of the one whom they had killed[^23]. Many, pricked to the heart, said: *What shall we do?*[^24] For they saw themselves bound by the immense crime of impiety, when they killed him whom they ought to have venerated and adored: and they thought this was inexpiable. For it was a great crime, the consideration of which made them despair: but they ought not to despair, for whom the Lord, hanging on the cross, deigned to pray. For he had said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^25]. He saw some of his own among many strangers; for them he was already asking pardon, from whom he was still receiving injury. For he attended not to the fact that he was dying by them, but that he was dying for them.

It is much that was granted to them, both by them and for them; so that no one may despair of the forgiveness of his own sin, when those who killed Christ merited pardon. Christ died for us; but surely by us? But those men saw Christ dying by their crime; and they believed in Christ forgiving their crimes. While they drank the blood they had shed, they despaired of their own salvation. Therefore he said this: *You will seek me, and you will not find me; and where I am, you cannot come*: because they were going to seek him after the resurrection, pricked to the heart.

He did not say, where I will be; but, where I am.

For Christ was always there, whither he was to return: for he so came that he did not depart. Whence in another place he says: *No one has ascended into heaven, except the one who descended from heaven, the Son of man, who is in heaven*[^26]: he did not say, who was in heaven. On earth he was speaking, and he said he was in heaven. He so came that he did not depart from there: he so returned that he did not forsake us. Why do you marvel? God does this. For man, according to the body, is in a place, and migrates from a place; and when he has come to another place, he will not be in that place from which he came: but God fills all things, and is everywhere whole; he is not held by places according to spaces. Yet the Lord Christ was, according to his visible flesh, on earth, according to his invisible majesty, in heaven and on earth: therefore he says: *Where I am, you cannot come*.

He did not say, you will not be able; but, you cannot: for such were they then that they could not. For that you may know this was not said toward despair, he said something of the kind even to his disciples: *Where I go, you cannot come*[^27]; though praying for them he said: *Father, I will that where I am, they too may be with me*[^28]. Finally, he explained this to Peter, and said to him: *Where I go, you cannot follow me now; but you will follow afterward*[^29].

**10.** The Jews, then, said—not to him, but to themselves: *Where is this man about to go, that we shall not find him? Surely he is not about to go into the dispersion of the gentiles, and to teach the gentiles?*[^30] For they did not know what they said; but because he willed it, they prophesied. For the Lord was about to go to the gentiles, not by the presence of his body, but nevertheless by his own feet.

Who were his feet?

The feet that Saul wished to trample by persecuting, when the head cried out to him: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^31]

What is this saying that he said: *You will seek me, and you will not find me; and where I am, you cannot come?*[^32] Whence the Lord said this they did not know, and yet, not knowing, they announced beforehand something that was to be. For the Lord said this because the place—if a place is even to be spoken of, that is, the bosom of the Father from which the only-begotten Son never departs—they did not know; nor were they fit to think where Christ was, whence Christ did not withdraw; whither Christ was to return, where Christ remained. How is this to be thought by the human heart, much less explained by the tongue?

This, then, they in no way understood; and yet on this occasion they foretold our salvation: that the Lord was about to go to the dispersion of the gentiles, and to fulfill what they read and did not understand: *A people whom I did not know has served me; in the hearing of the ear it has obeyed me*[^33]. They did not hear, in whose eyes he was present; they heard, in whose ears he sounded.

## The cross is the seed from which another harvest rises.

**11.** For of that Church to come from the gentiles, the woman who suffered an issue of blood bore the type: she touched, and was not seen; she was unknown, and was healed. For it was a figure, that the Lord asked: *Who touched me?*[^34] As if not knowing, he healed the unknown woman: so he did also with the gentiles. We did not learn him in the flesh, and yet we have been granted to eat his flesh, and to be members in his flesh.

Why?

Because he sent to us.

Whom?

His heralds, his disciples, his servants, his redeemed whom he created, but whom he also redeemed as his brothers: I have said too little of the whole—his members, himself; for he sent to us his members, and made us his members.

Yet according to the form of body which the Jews saw and despised, Christ was not among us: because this too had been said of him, as the Apostle also says: *For I say that Christ was a minister of the circumcision for the sake of God's truth, to confirm the promises made to the fathers*[^35]. To them he ought to come, from whose fathers and to whose fathers he was promised: therefore he himself also says: *I was not sent except to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel*[^36]. But what does the Apostle say in what follows? *And the gentiles glorify God for his mercy*[^37]. What does the Lord himself say? *I have other sheep that are not of this fold*[^38]. He who had said, I was not sent except to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel, how does he have other sheep to whom he was not sent, unless because he signified that he showed his bodily presence as sent only to the Jews, who saw and killed him? And yet many from there both before and after believed. The first harvest was winnowed from the cross, that there might be seed from which another harvest would rise.

But now, when, roused by the fame of the Gospel and its good fragrance, his faithful believe throughout all nations, there will be *the expectation of the gentiles*[^39], when he comes who has already come; when he is seen by all, who then was not seen by some and was seen by others; when he comes to judge, who came to be judged; when he comes to separate, who came that he might not be distinguished. For Christ was not distinguished from the impious, but judged with the impious: for of him it was said: *He was reckoned among the wicked*[^40]. The robber escaped: Christ was condemned. The criminal received indulgence; he was condemned who released the crimes of all who confess.

Yet the cross itself, if you attend, was a tribunal: for with the judge set in the middle, the one robber who believed was freed[^41], the other who insulted was condemned. Already it signified what he will do with the living and the dead; setting some at the right hand, others at the left: that robber like those who will be at the left, the other like those who will be at the right. He was being judged, and he was threatening judgment.
