# Homily 25

I came not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me (Jn 6:15-40).

The teacher of humility came not to do his own will, but the will of him who sent him. Let us come to him, let us enter to him, let us be incorporated into him, that we too may not do our own will, but the will of God; and he will not cast us out, because we are his members, because he willed to be our head by teaching humility.

**1.** Yesterday's reading was from the Gospel; this is today's which follows it, from which today's sermon is owed. When that miracle had been done, where Jesus fed five thousand men from five loaves, and the crowds had marveled and called him a great Prophet who had come into the world, this follows: *Jesus, therefore, when he knew that they had come to seize him and make him king, fled again into the mountain, himself alone*[^1]. It is given us to understand, then, that the Lord, when he was sitting on the mountain with his disciples and saw the crowds coming to him, had come down from the mountain and fed the crowds in the lower places. For how can it be that he should flee there again, unless he had first come down from the mountain? It signifies something, then, that the Lord came down from on high to feed the crowds. He fed them, and went up.

**2.** But why did he go up, when he knew that they wished to seize him and make him king? What then? Was he not a king, who feared to be made king? He certainly was: and not such a king as would be made by men, but such a one as would give a kingdom to men. Does Jesus perhaps here too signify something to us, whose deeds are words? Then, in this—that they wished to seize him and make him king, and on this account he fled into the mountain, himself alone—does he keep silent about what was done in him, say nothing, signify nothing?

Or was this perhaps to seize him—to wish to anticipate the time of his kingdom? For he had now come, not yet to reign in the way in which he is to reign in that which we say, *Your kingdom come*[^2]. Always indeed he reigns with the Father inasmuch as he is the Son of God, the Word of God, the Word through which all things were made. But the Prophets foretold his kingdom, even inasmuch as Christ was made man, and made his faithful ones Christians. There will be, then, a kingdom of Christians which is now being gathered, now being made ready, now being bought with the blood of Christ: there will at last be his manifest kingdom, when the glory of his saints will be made open after the judgment carried out by him; which judgment he himself said above the Son of man would carry out[^3].

Of which kingdom the Apostle too said, *When he will have handed over the kingdom to God the Father*[^4]. For which reason he himself says, *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the beginning of the world*[^5]. But the disciples and the crowds believing in him thought that he had so come as to reign already: this is to wish to seize him and make him king—to wish to anticipate his time, which he himself concealed within himself, that he might bring it forth at the opportune moment, and at the opportune moment declare it at the end of the age.

**3.** For that you may know that when they wanted to make him king—that is, to anticipate, and to have already the manifest kingdom of Christ, who first had to be judged, and then to judge: when he was crucified, and those who hoped in him had lost the hope of his resurrection, rising from the dead he found there two men conversing with despair, and with groaning discussing the things that had happened; and appearing to them as if unknown, since their eyes were held that he might not be recognized by them, he joined in their conversation as they treated of these things: but they, telling him what they were conversing about, said that that great prophet in deeds and words had been killed by the chief priests; *And we*, they said, *were hoping that it was he who was to redeem Israel*[^6].

Rightly you hoped, truly you hoped: in him is the redemption of Israel. But why do you hasten? You wish to seize. This too indicates this sense to us, that when the disciples asked him about the end, they said to him: If you are to present yourself at this time, then when is the kingdom of Israel? For now they desired it to be, now they wanted it: this is to wish to seize, and to make king. But he said to the disciples, since he was still to ascend alone: *It is not for you to know the times or the moments which the Father has set in his own power: but you will receive power from on high, the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and you will be witnesses to me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea and Samaria, and even to the ends of the earth*[^7]. You wish that I should now exhibit the kingdom; first let me gather what I may exhibit: you love the height, and you will attain the height; but follow me through humility.

So too it was foretold of him: *And a congregation of peoples will surround you, and for the sake of this return on high*[^8]; that is, that a congregation of peoples may surround you, that you may gather many, return on high. So he did; he fed them, and went up.

**4.** But why was it said, *he fled*? For truly, if he did not wish, he would not be seized; if he did not wish, he would not be carried off—he who, if he did not wish, would not even be recognized. For that you may know that this was done mystically, not out of necessity, but out of a signifying arrangement, you will see presently in what follows; for he appeared to those same crowds that were seeking him, and speaking with them he said many things to them, disputed much about the heavenly bread: was he not disputing about bread with the very ones from whom he had fled lest he be seized? Could he not, then, even then have brought it about that he should not be apprehended by them, just as afterward when he was speaking with them? He signified something, then, by fleeing. What is, *he fled*? His height could not be understood. For whatever you do not understand, you say, It flees me. Therefore *he fled again into the mountain, himself alone*[^9]. The firstborn from the dead, ascending above all the heavens, and interceding for us[^10].

## The darkness grows, and Jesus has not yet come.

**5.** Meanwhile, with him placed above as the one great priest (who entered within the veil, with the people set outside; for that priest signified this in the Old Law, who did this once a year[^11]): with him, then, placed above, what were the disciples suffering in the little boat? For with him set on high, that little boat prefigured the Church. If we do not first understand in the Church that which that little boat was suffering, then those things were not signifying, but simply passing by: but if we see expressed in the Church the truth of those significations, it is manifest that the deeds of Christ are kinds of utterances.

*But when it had become late*, he says, *his disciples went down to the sea: and when they had boarded the little boat, they came across the sea to Capernaum*. Quickly he stated as finished what was done afterward. *They came across the sea to Capernaum*. And he returns, to expound how they came, because sailing across the lake they crossed over. And while they were sailing to that place to which he has already said they came, by recapitulating he expounds what happened: *Darkness had now come, and Jesus had not come to them*[^12]. Rightly darkness, because the light had not come: *Darkness had now come, and Jesus had not come to them*.

The more the end of the world approaches, the more errors grow, terrors multiply, iniquity grows, unbelief grows: in short, the light, which is love—as is shown plainly enough by John the evangelist himself, so that he said, *He who hates his brother is in the darkness*[^13]—is most frequently extinguished: this darkness of fraternal hatreds grows, grows daily; and Jesus has not yet come. From where does it appear that they grow? Because *iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold*[^14]. The darkness grows, and Jesus has not yet come. As the darkness grows, as love grows cold, as iniquity abounds, these are the very waves troubling the ship: the storms and winds are the shouts of the slanderers. Hence love grows cold, hence the waves swell, and the ship is troubled.

**6.** As a great wind blew, the sea was rising up. The darkness was growing, understanding was diminishing, iniquity was increasing. *When they had therefore rowed about twenty-five or thirty stadia*[^15]. Meanwhile they were going on, making progress; nor did those winds and storms and waves and darkness bring it about either that the ship should not make progress, or, broken up, should sink: but amid all those evils it went on. For because iniquity has abounded, and the love of many grows cold, the waves grow, the darkness increases, the wind rages: but yet the ship goes on. For *he who has persevered to the end, he will be saved*[^16].

Nor is the number of stadia itself to be despised. For truly it could not signify nothing, what was said: *When they had rowed twenty-five or thirty stadia, then Jesus came to them*. Twenty-five would suffice, thirty would suffice, especially because it was an estimate, not an assertion. Would the truth be endangered in one estimating, if he said about thirty stadia, or about twenty-five stadia? But from twenty-five he made thirty.

Let us inquire into the number twenty-five. Of what does it consist, from what is it made? Of five. That number five belongs to the Law. The five books of Moses are these, those five porticoes holding the sick are these, the five loaves feeding five thousand men are these. Therefore the number twenty-five signifies the Law: since five by five, that is, five times five, makes twenty-five, the square of five. But to this Law, before the Gospel came, perfection was lacking. But perfection is comprised in the number six.

For this reason God perfected the world in six days[^17], and those very five are multiplied by six, that the Law may be fulfilled through the Gospel, so that six times five become thirty. To those, then, who fulfill the Law, Jesus comes. And he comes how? Treading on the waves; having all the swellings of the world under his feet, pressing down all the loftinesses of the age. This happens in proportion as time is added, and as the age of the world advances. In this world tribulations increase, evils increase, afflictions increase, all these are heaped up: Jesus passes by, treading on the waves.

**7.** And yet so great are the tribulations that even those who have believed in Jesus, and who strive to persevere to the end, are afraid of failing: with Christ treading on the waves, pressing down the ambitions and loftinesses of the age, the Christian is afraid. Were not these things foretold to him? Rightly, when Jesus too was walking on the waves, they were afraid[^18]: just as Christians, although having hope in the world to come, when they see the loftiness of this world pressed down, are very often disturbed by the affliction of human affairs.

They open the Gospel, they open the Scriptures; and they find there all these things foretold; that the Lord does this. He presses down the loftinesses of the age, that he may be glorified by the humble. Of whose loftiness it was foretold: *You will destroy the strongest cities*; and, *The enemy's swords have failed unto the end, and you have destroyed their cities*[^19]. Why, then, are you afraid, Christians? Christ speaks: *It is I, do not be afraid*[^20]. Why are you afraid of these things? Why do you fear? I foretold these things, I do them, it is necessary that they come about. *It is I, do not be afraid*. *They wished, then, to receive him into the ship*, recognizing him and rejoicing, made secure. *And immediately the ship was at the land to which they were going*[^21]. The end was made, at the land: from the wet to the solid, from the troubled to the firm, from the journey to the end.

**8.** *On the next day the crowd that stood across the sea, from where they had come, saw that there was only one little boat there, and that Jesus had not gone into the ship with his disciples, but that his disciples alone had departed: but other ships came up from Tiberias, near the place where they had eaten the bread, giving thanks to the Lord. When, therefore, the crowds saw that Jesus was not there, nor his disciples, they boarded the little boats, and came to Capernaum seeking Jesus*[^22].

Yet so great a miracle was made known to them. For they saw that the disciples alone had boarded the ship, and that another ship was not there. But ships came from there near that place where they had eaten the bread, in which the crowds followed him. With the disciples, then, he had not boarded, another ship was not there: from where, then, was Jesus suddenly across the sea, unless because he walked upon the sea, to show a miracle?

**9.** *And when the crowds had found him.* See, he presents himself to the crowds, by whom he had feared to be seized, and had fled into the mountain. He altogether confirms and makes known to us that all those things were said in mystery; and done in a great sacrament, that they might signify something. See, it is he who had fled from the crowds into the mountain: does he not speak with those same crowds? Now let them seize him, now let them make him king. *And when they had found him across the sea, they said to him: Rabbi, when did you come here?*[^23]

## Jesus is scarcely sought for the sake of Jesus.

**10.** After the sacrament of the miracle, he brings in a sermon too, that, if it can be done, those who have been fed may be fed, and that he whose bellies he satisfied with loaves may satisfy their minds too with sermons; but only if they grasp them. And if they do not grasp them, let what they do not grasp be taken up, lest the fragments perish. Let him speak, then, and let us hear. *Jesus answered and said: Amen, amen, I say to you, you seek me, not because you have seen signs, but because you have eaten of my loaves*[^24]. You seek me for the sake of the flesh, not for the sake of the spirit. How many do not seek Jesus except that he may do them good according to the present time! One has a lawsuit, he seeks the intercession of clergy; another is oppressed by a more powerful man, he flees to the church; another wants intercession made for him with one with whom he has little influence: this one in this way, that one in that way; the church is filled daily with such people. Jesus is scarcely sought for the sake of Jesus.

*You seek me, not because you have seen signs, but because you have eaten of my loaves. Work not for the food that perishes, but for that which abides unto eternal life*[^25]. You seek me for the sake of something else; seek me for my own sake. For he makes known that this food is himself, which is brought to light in what follows. *Which the Son of man will give you*[^26]. You were expecting, I believe, to eat loaves again, to recline again, to be fattened again. But he had spoken of the food, not that which perishes, but that which abides unto eternal life: just as it had been said to that Samaritan woman: *If you knew who it is that asks you for a drink, you perhaps would have asked of him, and he would give you living water*: when she said: From where do you have it, since you have no bucket, and the well is deep? he answered the Samaritan woman: *If you knew who asks you for a drink, you would have asked of him, and he would give you water, so that whoever drinks of it will thirst no more: for whoever drinks of this water will thirst again*[^27]. And she rejoiced, and wished to receive it, as though she would not suffer thirst of the body, she who was wearied by the labor of drawing; and thus amid conversations of this kind she came to the spiritual drink: altogether in this way here too.

The Word of God commends his own humility.

**11.** This food, then, not that which perishes, but that which abides unto eternal life, *which the Son of man will give you: for this one God the Father has sealed*[^28]. Do not understand this Son of man as you would other sons of men, of whom it was said, *But the sons of men will hope in the protection of your wings*[^29]. This Son of man, set apart by a certain grace of the Spirit, and according to the flesh the Son of man, excepted from the number of men; he is the Son of man. This Son of man is also the Son of God, this man is also God. In another place, questioning the disciples, he says: *Whom do men say that I, the Son of man, am? And they: Some John, others Elijah, others Jeremiah, or one of the Prophets. And he: But whom do you say that I am? Peter answered: You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^30].

He called himself the Son of man, and Peter called him the Son of the living God. Excellently the one recalled what he had mercifully exhibited; the other recalled what abided in glory. The Word of God commends his own humility, the man recognizes the glory of his Lord. And in truth, brothers, I think it is just; he humbled himself for our sake, let us glorify him: for the Son of man is not for his own sake, but for ours. He was, then, the Son of man in that way, when *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^31].

For this is why God the Father sealed him. What is it to seal, except to set something proper to a thing? For this is to seal—to impose something which is not confused with the rest. To seal is to set the sign of a thing. On whatever thing you set a sign, you set the sign for this reason, that, not being confused with others, it may not fail to be recognized by you. The Father, then, sealed him.

What is, he sealed him?

He gave him something proper, that he might not be compared with other men. Therefore it was said of him: *God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness above your fellows*[^32]. What, then, is it to seal? To have him excepted: this is, above your fellows.

And so, he says, do not despise me, because I am the Son of man; and seek from me the food, not that which perishes, but that which abides unto eternal life. For I am the Son of man in such a way that I am not one of you: I am the Son of man in such a way that God the Father sealed me.

What is, he sealed me?

He gave me something proper, by which I might not be confused with the human race, but through me the human race might be freed.

**12.** They said, therefore, to him: *What shall we do, that we may work the works of God?* For he had said to them: *Work for the food, not that which perishes, but that which abides unto eternal life*. What shall we do, they say? By observing what will we be able to fulfill this precept? *Jesus answered and said to them: This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent*[^33]. This, then, is to eat the food, not that which perishes, but that which abides unto eternal life. Why do you prepare teeth and belly? Believe, and you have eaten.

Faith is indeed distinguished from works, as the Apostle says, that a man is justified by faith without works[^34]: and there are works which seem good, without faith in Christ; and they are not good, because they are not referred to that end from which they are good: *For the end of the Law is Christ, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^35]. Therefore he did not wish to distinguish faith from work, but said that faith itself is the work. For it is the very faith *that works through love*[^36]. And he did not say: This is your work; but: This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he has sent: that *he who glories may glory in the Lord*[^37].

Because, then, he was inviting them to faith, they were still seeking signs by which they might believe. See whether the Jews do not seek signs. They said, therefore, to him: *What sign, then, do you do, that we may see and believe you? What do you work?*[^38] Was it a small thing that they had been fed from five loaves? They knew this indeed, but to this food they preferred the manna from heaven. But the Lord Jesus declared himself to be such as to set himself above Moses. For Moses did not dare to say of himself that he gave food, not that which perishes, but that which abides unto eternal life. This one promised something more than Moses. For through Moses a kingdom was promised, and a land flowing with milk and honey, temporal peace, an abundance of children, the health of the body, and all the rest—temporal things indeed, yet in figure spiritual: because they were promised to the old man in the Old Testament.

They were attending, then, to the things promised through Moses, and attending to the things promised through Christ. The one promised a full belly in the land, but with food that perishes: this one promised food, not that which perishes, but that which abides forever. They were attending to him as promising more, and as it were did not yet see him doing greater things. And so they were attending to what sort of things Moses had done, and they still wanted some greater things to be done by him who was promising such great things. *What*, they say, *do you do, that we may believe you?* And that you may know that they were comparing those miracles to this miracle, and therefore judging as it were lesser these things that Jesus did: *Our fathers*, they say, *ate manna in the desert*[^39].

But what is manna? Perhaps you despise it.

*As it is written: He gave them manna to eat*[^40]. Through Moses our fathers received bread from heaven, and it was not said to them by Moses: Work for the food, not that which perishes. You promise food, not that which perishes, but that which abides unto eternal life, and you do not work such works as Moses. He did not give barley loaves, but gave manna from heaven.

**13.** Jesus said, therefore, to them: *Amen, amen, I say to you, Moses did not give you bread from heaven, but my Father gives you bread from heaven. For the true bread is that which comes down from heaven, and gives life to the world*[^41]. The true bread, then, is that which gives life to the world; and it is the food of which I spoke a little before: Work for the food, not that which perishes, but that which abides unto eternal life. Therefore that manna too signified this, and all those signs were mine. You have loved my signs; do you despise him who was signified?

Moses, then, did not give bread from heaven: God gives bread. But what bread? Perhaps manna? No, but the bread which manna signified, namely the Lord Jesus himself. My Father gives you the true bread. For the bread of God is that which comes down from heaven, and gives life to the world. They said, therefore, to him: *Lord, give us always this bread*[^42]. Just as that Samaritan woman, to whom it was said, He who drinks of this water will never thirst; she at once, taking it according to the body, but yet wishing to be free of need: *Give me*, she said, *Lord, of this water*[^43]: so these too: Lord, give us this bread, which may refresh us, and not fail.

## O secret without weariness!

**14.** But Jesus said to them: *I am the bread of life: he who comes to me will not hunger; and he who believes in me will never thirst*[^44]. *He who comes to me*, this is the same as what he says, *and he who believes in me*; and what he said, *will not hunger*, is to be understood as, *will never thirst*. For by both is signified that eternal fullness, where there is no want. You desire bread from heaven; you have it before you, and you do not eat. *But I said to you that you have both seen me and have not believed*[^45]. But not for that reason have I lost the people. For has your unbelief made void the faith of God[^46]?

For see what follows: *All that the Father gives me will come to me; and him who comes to me I will not cast out*[^47]. What is that within, from which one does not go out? A great inner chamber, and a sweet secret. O secret without weariness, without bitterness of evil thoughts, without the interruption of temptations and pains! Is it not that secret into which he will enter to whom the Lord will say, to the well-deserving servant, *Enter into the joy of your Lord*[^48]?

**15.** *And him who comes to me I will not cast out. Because I came down from heaven, not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me*[^49]. For this reason, then, will you not cast out him who comes to you, because you came down from heaven, not to do your own will, but the will of him who sent you? A great sacrament! I beg you, let us knock together; let something come out to us that may feed us, according to what has delighted us. That great and sweet secret: *He who comes to me*. Attend, attend, and weigh: *He who comes to me I will not cast out*.

Therefore, he who comes, he says, I will not cast out.

Why?

Because I came down from heaven, not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me.

Is this, then, the cause why you do not cast out him who comes to you—that you came down from heaven not to do your own will, but the will of him who sent you?

It is.

Why do we ask whether it is? It is, he himself says so. For it is not lawful for us to suppose anything other than what he says: *He who comes to me I will not cast out*. And as though you were asking, Why? *Because I came not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me*.

I fear that the soul went out from God for this reason, because it was proud; indeed, I do not doubt it. For it is written: *The beginning of all sin is pride*; and, *The beginning of man's pride is to apostatize from God*[^50]. It is written, it is firm, it is true. Then what is said of the proud mortal, girded with rags of flesh, weighed down by the burden of a corruptible body, and yet exalting himself, and forgetting with what skin he is clothed—what does Scripture say to him? *Why is earth and ashes proud?* Why is it proud? Let it say why it is proud. *Because in his life he cast away his inmost parts*[^51]. What is, he cast away, except he threw them far off? This is to go outside. For indeed to enter within is to seek the inmost things; to cast away the inmost things is to go outside. The proud man casts away the inmost things, the humble man seeks the inmost things. If by pride we are cast out, by humility we return.

## Pride is the head of all diseases.

**16.** Pride is the head of all diseases, because pride is the head of all sins. When a physician treats a sickness, if he cures what has been caused through some cause, and does not cure the very cause by which it was caused, he seems to heal for a time, but, the cause remaining, the disease returns. For example—I will say this more expressly: a humor in the body breeds scab or sores; in the body a great fever arises, and no small pain: certain medicines are applied which restrain the scab and quiet that heat of the sore; and they are applied and profit: you see a man who was full of sores and scab healed; but because that humor was not cast out, the sore is returned to again. Recognizing this, the physician purges the humor, removes the cause, and there will be no sores.

From where does iniquity abound? Through pride. Cure pride, and there will be no iniquity. That the cause of all diseases might be cured, then, that is, pride, the Son of God came down and was made humble. Why are you proud, O man? God for your sake was made humble. You would perhaps be ashamed to imitate a humble man; at least imitate a humble God. The Son of God came in man, and was made humble: you are commanded to be humble, you are not commanded to become a beast from a man: he, God, was made man; you, man, recognize that you are a man: your whole humility is to recognize yourself.

Therefore, because God teaches humility, he said: I came not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me. For this is a commendation of humility. For pride does its own will; humility does the will of God. Therefore, he who comes to me, I will not cast out.

Why?

Because I came not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me. I came humble, I came to teach humility, I came as the teacher of humility: he who comes to me is incorporated into me; he who comes to me is made humble; he who clings to me will be humble; because he does not do his own will, but God's; and therefore he will not be cast out, because when he was proud, he was thrown out.

**17.** See those inner things commended in the Psalm: *But the sons of men will hope in the protection of your wings*. See what it is to go within, see what it is to flee to his protection, see what it is even to run under the lashes of the father: for *he scourges every son whom he receives*[^52]. *But the sons of men will hope under the covering of your wings*[^53].

And what is within?

*They will be inebriated from the abundance of your house*. When you have sent them within, entering into the joy of their Lord; *they will be inebriated from the abundance of your house, and you will give them to drink from the torrent of your delight. For with you is the fountain of life*. Not outside, away from you, but within, with you, there is the fountain of life. *And in your light we shall see light. Extend your mercy to those who know you, and your righteousness to those who are upright in heart*[^54].

Those who follow the will of their Lord, not seeking their own things, but the things of the Lord Jesus Christ, they are upright in heart; their feet are not moved. *For God is good to Israel, to the upright in heart.* *But my feet*, that man says, *were almost moved*.

Why?

*Because I was zealous over sinners, beholding the peace of sinners*[^55]. To whom, then, is God good, except to the upright in heart? For to me, crooked in heart, God was displeasing. Why was he displeasing? Because he gave happiness to the wicked: and therefore my feet wavered, as though I had served God without cause. For this reason, then, my feet were almost moved, because I was not upright in heart.

What, then, is one upright in heart?

One who follows the will of God. That man is happy, this one labors: that one lives badly and is happy, this one lives justly and labors. Let the one who lives justly and labors not be indignant; within he has what that happy man does not have: let him not, then, be saddened, not be wasted away, not fail. That happy man has gold in his chest, this one has God in his conscience. Compare now gold and God, the chest and the conscience. That man has that which perishes, and there he has that whereby he perishes; this one has God, who cannot perish, and there he has that which cannot be taken away: but only if he is upright in heart; for then he enters, and does not go out.

For this reason what did that man say? *For with you is the fountain of life*: not with us. Therefore we ought to enter that we may live, not as though it sufficed us to perish, not as though we wished to be satisfied from our own and so to wither: but to set our mouth to the very fountain, where the water does not fail. Because Adam wished to live by his own counsel, and fell through him who had earlier fallen through pride, who handed him the cup of that same pride. Because, then, with you is the fountain of life, and in your light we shall see light; let us drink within, let us see within.

For why was there a going out from there?

Hear why: *Let not the foot of pride come to me*. Therefore that one went out, to whom the foot of pride came.

Show that he went out for this reason.

*And let not the hands of sinners move me*: on account of the foot of pride.

Why do you say this?

*There fell all who work iniquity*.

Where did they fall?

In that very pride. *They were driven out, and could not stand*[^56].

If, then, pride drove out those who could not stand, humility lets in those who can stand forever. For this reason indeed he who said, *The humbled bones will exult*, foretold: *To my hearing you will give exultation and gladness*[^57].

What is, to my hearing?

By hearing you I am happy, from your voice I am happy; by drinking within I am happy. Therefore I do not fall, therefore the humbled bones will exult: therefore the friend of the bridegroom stands, and hears him[^58]; therefore he stands, because he hears. He drinks from the inner fountain, therefore he stands. Those who refused to drink from within fell there; they were driven out, and could not stand.

## Christ, the teacher of humility.

**18.** And so the teacher of humility came not to do his own will, but the will of him who sent him. Let us come to him, let us enter to him, let us be incorporated into him, that we too may not do our own will, but the will of God: and he will not cast us out, because we are his members, because he willed to be our head by teaching humility. Lastly, hear him himself proclaiming: *Come to me, you who labor and are burdened: take my yoke upon you, and learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart; and when you have learned this, you will find rest for your souls*[^59], whence you may not be cast out: *because I came down from heaven, not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me*[^60]; I teach humility, none but the humble can come to me. Nothing casts one out but pride: how does he go out who keeps humility, and does not slip from the truth?

As much as could be said of the hidden sense has been said, brothers: for the sense lies sufficiently hidden here, and I do not know whether it has been brought out and carved by me in fitting words—why he does not cast out him who comes to him, because he came not to do his own will, but the will of him who sent him.

**19.** *But this*, he says, *is the will of the Father who sent me, that of all that he has given me I should lose nothing*[^61]. He himself is given to him who keeps humility; this one he receives: he who does not keep humility is far from the teacher of humility. *That of all that he has given me I should lose nothing.* So it is not the will in the sight of your Father that one of these little ones should perish. Of the puffed up one may perish, of the little ones nothing perishes: because *unless you become like this little one, you will not enter into the kingdom of heaven*[^62].

*Of all that the Father has given me I should lose nothing; but I will raise it up on the last day*[^63]. See how here too he delineates that twofold resurrection. He who comes to me now rises again, made humble in my members: but I will also raise him up on the last day, according to the flesh. *For this is the will of my Father who sent me, that everyone who sees the Son and believes in him may have eternal life; and I will raise him up on the last day*[^64]. Above he said: *He who hears my word, and believes him who sent me*[^65]: but now: *He who sees the Son, and believes in him*. He did not say: He sees the Son, and believes in the Father: for to believe in the Son is the same as to believe in the Father. Because *as the Father has life in himself, so he has given to the Son also to have life in himself*[^66]. *That everyone who sees the Son and believes in him may have eternal life*: by believing and passing over to life, as it were by that first resurrection. And because it is not the only one, *and I will raise him up*, he says, *on the last day*.
