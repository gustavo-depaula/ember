# Tractate 108

## *Sanctify them in the truth*[^1].

## The heirs of the New Testament are sanctified in the truth, of which truth the sanctifications of the Old Testament were shadows; and when they are sanctified in the truth, they are surely sanctified in Christ, who truly said, *I am the way, and the truth, and the life.*

**1.** The Lord, still speaking to the Father and praying for his disciples, says: *I have given them your word, and the world has hated them*[^2]. They had not yet experienced this in their own sufferings, which afterward followed them; but in his manner he says these things, foretelling future things by words of past time. Then, adding the reason why the world hated them: *Because they are not,* he says, *of the world, as I also am not of the world.* This was conferred on them by regeneration: for by generation they were of the world, on account of which he had already said to them, *I chose you out of the world*[^3]. It was granted them, then, that as he, so they too should not be of the world, he freeing them from the world. But he himself was never of the world: because even according to the form of a servant he himself was born of the Holy Spirit, of whom they were reborn. For if they are now not of the world, because they are reborn of the Holy Spirit; for that reason he was never of the world, because he was born of the Holy Spirit.

**2.** *I do not ask,* he says, *that you take them out of the world, but that you keep them from evil*[^4]. For they still had it necessary, although they were now not of the world, yet to be in the world. He repeats the same thought: *They are not of the world,* he says, *as I also am not of the world. Sanctify them in the truth*[^5]. For thus they are kept from evil, which he prayed above might be done. But it can be asked how they were already not of the world, if they were not yet sanctified in the truth; or, if they already were, why he asks that they may be. Is it because even those who are sanctified advance in the same sanctity, and become more holy; and this not without the help of the grace of God, but he furthering their advancement who sanctified its beginning? Whence the Apostle too says: *He who began a good work in you will perfect it unto the day of Christ Jesus*[^6]. And so the heirs of the New Testament are sanctified in the truth, of which truth the sanctifications of the Old Testament were shadows: and when they are sanctified in the truth, they are surely sanctified in Christ, who truly said, *I am the way, and the truth, and the life*[^7]. Likewise, when he says, *The truth will free you,* a little after, explaining what he had said: *If the Son,* he says, *frees you, then you will be truly free*[^8]; that he might show that he had first called himself the truth, which he afterward called the Son. What else, then, did he say in this place too, *Sanctify them in the truth,* but, sanctify them in me?

**3.** Finally, he follows, and does not cease to intimate this more openly: *Your word,* he says, *is truth*[^9]. What else did he say, but, *I am the truth*? For the Greek Gospel has "logos," which is read there too, where it was said, *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^10]. And surely we know the Word himself to be the only-begotten Son of God, which *was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^11]. Whence here too it could be put, and in certain copies it is put, *Your Word is truth*; just as in certain copies there too it is written, *In the beginning was the Word* (sermo). But in the Greek, without any variation, both there and here it is "logos." The Father, then, sanctifies in the truth, that is, in his Word, in his Only-begotten, his heirs and his co-heirs.

**4.** But now he still speaks of the Apostles; for following he adds: *As you sent me into the world, I also have sent them into the world*[^12]. Whom did he send, but his Apostles? For the very name of Apostles, since it is Greek, signifies nothing in Latin but those sent. God sent his Son, then, not in the flesh of sin, but in the likeness of the flesh of sin; and his Son sent those whom, born in the flesh of sin, he sanctified from the stain of sin.

## Christ sanctified himself in himself, the man in the Word himself.

**5.** But since, through this, that the mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus, was made head of the Church, they are his members; therefore he says what follows: *And for them I sanctify myself*[^13]. For what is, *And for them I sanctify myself,* but, I sanctify them in myself, since they too are I? Since those of whom he says this, as I said, are his members, and Christ is one, head and body: the Apostle teaching and saying of the seed of Abraham, *But if you are Christ's, then you are the seed of Abraham*[^14]; when he had said above, *It does not say: And to seeds, as in many; but as in one: And to your seed, which is Christ*[^15]. If, then, the seed of Abraham is Christ, what else was said to those to whom it was said, *Then you are the seed of Abraham,* but, then you are Christ? Hence it is that in another place the same apostle says: *Now I rejoice in my sufferings for you, and fill up in my flesh what is lacking of the tribulations of Christ*[^16]. He did not say, of my tribulations, but, *of Christ*: because he was a member of Christ; and in his persecutions, such as Christ ought to suffer in his whole body, he too was filling up Christ's tribulations for his portion.

That this too may be certain in this place, attend to what follows. For when he had said, *And for them I sanctify myself,* that we might understand that he said this — that he sanctified them in himself — he soon added: *That they too may be sanctified in the truth*[^17]. Which is what else than, in me, according to that whereby the truth is that Word which in the beginning was God? In whom even the son of man himself was sanctified from the beginning of his creation, when the Word was made flesh; because the Word and the man were made one person. Then, therefore, he sanctified himself in himself, that is, the man in himself the Word; because one Christ, the Word and the man, sanctifies the man in the Word. But for the sake of his members: *And for them,* he says, *I* — that is, so that it may profit them too, because they too are I; as it profited me in myself, because I am a man without them: *And I sanctify myself,* that is, I sanctify them in me as myself, since in me they too are I. *That they too may be sanctified in the truth.* What is *they too,* but, as I; *in the truth,* which is what I myself am? Then he begins to speak no longer of the Apostles alone, but also of his other members: which, he himself granting it, must be treated in another sermon.
