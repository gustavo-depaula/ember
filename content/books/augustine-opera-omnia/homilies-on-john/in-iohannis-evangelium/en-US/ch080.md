# Tractate 80

## *You are already clean, because of the word that I have spoken to you*[^1].

This word of faith has such power in the Church of God that through the one who believes, offers, blesses, and dips, it cleanses even a tiny infant, though not yet able to believe with the heart unto righteousness and to confess with the mouth unto salvation. All this is brought about by the word that the Lord has spoken to us.

**1.** This passage of the Gospel, brothers, where the Lord calls himself the vine and his disciples the branches, says this according to that which is the head of the Church, and we its members — the mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus. For the vine and the branches are of one nature; and on that account, since he was God, of whose nature we are not, he became man, so that in him human nature might be the vine, of which we men also could be the branches. What, then, is *I am the true vine*[^2]? Did he, in order to add *true,* refer this to that vine from which this comparison is drawn? For it is called a vine by comparison, not by property — just as he is called a sheep, a lamb, a lion, a rock, a cornerstone, and other such things, which are themselves rather the true things from which these comparisons, not properties, are drawn. But when he says, *I am the true vine,* he distinguishes himself from that one to which it is said: *How are you turned into bitterness, you alien vine*[^3]? For how is that a true vine which was expected to bear grapes but bore thorns?

**2.** *I am,* he says, *the true vine, and my Father is the vinedresser. Every branch in me that does not bear fruit, he will take away; and every one that bears fruit, he will prune, so that it may bear more fruit*[^4]. Are the vinedresser and the vine one? Christ is the vine, then, according to that which he says, *The Father is greater than I*[^5]; but according to that which he says, *I and the Father are one*[^6], he himself is also the vinedresser. And not such as those are who exhibit their ministry by working from outside; but such that he gives the increase even from within. *For neither is he who plants anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase*[^7]. But Christ is certainly God, because *the Word was God*[^8]; whence he and the Father are one; and even if *the Word was made flesh*[^9], which it was not, it remains what it was. Finally, after he had said of the Father, as of the vinedresser, that he takes away the unfruitful branches but prunes the fruitful ones so that they may bear more fruit, he at once showed himself also to be the cleanser of the branches: *You are already clean,* he says, *because of the word that I have spoken to you*[^10]. See, he himself too is the cleanser of the branches, which is the office of the vinedresser, not of the vine — he who also made the branches his workers.

For although they do not give the increase, yet they contribute some help; but not of their own: *Because without me,* he says, *you can do nothing*[^11]. Hear them also confessing: *What then is Apollos? What then is Paul? Ministers through whom you believed, and to each as the Lord gave. I planted, Apollos watered*[^12]. This too, then, was *as the Lord gave to each one*; so not of their own. But now what follows, *but God gave the increase*[^13], he does not do through them, but through himself: this exceeds human humility, exceeds angelic sublimity, and belongs altogether to none but the vinedresser, the Trinity. *You are already clean* — clean, that is, and yet to be cleansed. For unless they were clean, they could not bear fruit; and yet every one that bears fruit the vinedresser prunes, so that it may bear more fruit. It bears fruit because it is clean; and that it may bear more, it is pruned still further. For who is in this life so clean that he is not to be more and more cleansed? Here, *if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us; but if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all iniquity*[^14] — he cleanses, surely, those who are clean, that is, fruitful, so that they may be all the more fruitful the cleaner they are.

## A sacrament is, as it were, a visible word.

**3.** *You are already clean, because of the word that I have spoken to you*[^15]. Why does he not say, you are clean because of the baptism by which you have been washed, but says, *because of the word that I have spoken to you*, unless it be that even in the water it is the word that cleanses? Take away the word, and what is the water but water? The word is added to the element, and there comes to be a sacrament, itself also a kind of visible word. For he had said this very thing when he washed the disciples' feet: *He who has been washed has no need except to wash his feet, but is wholly clean*[^16]. Whence does the water have such great power that it touches the body and washes the heart, if not by the working of the word — not because it is spoken, but because it is believed? For even in the word itself, the passing sound is one thing, the abiding power another. *This is the word of faith which we preach,* says the Apostle, *that if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is the Lord, and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved. For with the heart it is believed unto righteousness, but with the mouth confession is made unto salvation*[^17].

Whence it is read in the Acts of the Apostles: *Cleansing their hearts by faith*[^18]; and in his own epistle blessed Peter says: *So baptism also saves you; not the putting away of the filth of the flesh, but the appeal of a good conscience*[^19]. *This is the word of faith which we preach*[^20]; and by it, without doubt, baptism too is consecrated, so that it can cleanse. For Christ, the vine together with us, with the Father the vinedresser, *loved the Church and gave himself for her.* Read the Apostle, and see what he adds: *That he might sanctify her,* he says, *cleansing her by the washing of water in the word*[^21]. The cleansing, therefore, would in no way be attributed to a fluid and perishable element, unless there were added, *in the word.* This word of faith has such power in the Church of God that through the one who believes, offers, blesses, and dips, it cleanses even a tiny infant, though not yet able to believe with the heart unto righteousness and to confess with the mouth unto salvation. All this is brought about through the word, of which the Lord says: *You are already clean, because of the word that I have spoken to you*[^22].
