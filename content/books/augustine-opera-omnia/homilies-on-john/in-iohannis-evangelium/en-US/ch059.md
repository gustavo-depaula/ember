# Tractate 59

## *He who receives me receives him who sent me* (Jn 13:16-20).

## So, then, let each one receive him who is sent in such a way that in him he attends to the one who sent him. — A master of humility both in word and in example.

**1.** We have heard in the holy Gospel the Lord speaking and saying: *Amen, amen I say to you; the servant is not greater than his lord, nor the apostle greater than he who sent him: if you know these things, you will be blessed if you do them*[^1]. This he said for the reason that he had washed the disciples' feet, a master of humility both in word and in example: but we shall be able, with his help, to discuss the things that must be more laboriously discussed, if we do not linger on those that are plain. When, therefore, the Lord had said these things beforehand, he added: *I do not speak of all of you: I know whom I have chosen: but that the Scripture may be fulfilled: He who eats bread with me will lift up his heel against me*[^2]. What is this but, he will trample me? It is known of whom he speaks; Judas, that betrayer of his, is touched on. Therefore he had not chosen him, whence by this discourse he separates him from those whom he chose. So that what I say, he says: *You will be blessed if you do them, I do not speak of all of you*: there is among you one who will not be blessed, nor will do them. *I know whom I have chosen.* Whom, but those who will be blessed by doing what he commanded, and showed must be done, who can make them blessed? The betrayer Judas, he says, is not chosen.

What, then, is it that he says in another place: *Did I not choose you twelve, and one of you is a devil*[^3]? Or was he too chosen for something, for which surely he was necessary; but not for the blessedness of which he now says: *You will be blessed if you do them*? This he does not say of all: for he knows whom he has chosen for the fellowship of this blessedness. Not of these is this one, who ate his bread in such a way as to lift up his heel against him. They ate the bread the Lord, he the bread of the Lord against the Lord: they ate life, he punishment. *For he who eats unworthily,* says the Apostle, *eats judgment to himself*[^4]. *From now on,* he says, *I tell you, before it comes to pass; that when it has come to pass, you may believe that I am he*[^5]; that is, I am he of whom that Scripture went before, where it was said: *He who eats bread with me will lift up his heel against me.*

**2.** Then there follows, and he says: *Amen, amen I say to you; he who receives anyone I shall send receives me; and he who receives me receives him who sent me*[^6]. Did he wish it to be understood that there is as great a distance between him whom he sends and himself, as between himself and God the Father? If we should take it in this way, we shall make I know not what gradations—far be it—after the manner of the Arians. For when they hear or read these words of the Gospel, they at once run to those gradations of their dogma, by which they do not ascend to life, but are cast down into death. For at once they say: As far as the apostle of the Son is distant from the Son, although he said: *He who receives anyone I shall send receives me*; so far also is the Son distant from the Father, although he said: *And he who receives me receives him who sent me.*

But if you say this, you have forgotten, heretic, your gradations. For if, on account of these words of the Lord, you distinguish the Son from the Father by as great an interval as you distinguish the apostle from the Son, where are you going to place the Holy Spirit? Has it slipped from you that you are accustomed to place him after the Son? He will therefore be himself between the apostle and the Son; and the Son will be far more distant from the apostle than the Father from the Son. Or perhaps, that this distinction may remain at equal intervals, between the Son and the apostle, and between the Father and the Son, will the Holy Spirit be equal to the Son? But you do not want this either. Where, then, are you going to place him, if you place the apostle below the Son by as great a distinction as the Son below the Father?

Restrain, then, the boldness of your presumption; and in these words do not seek as great a distance of the Father and the Son as of the Son and the apostle. Hear rather the Son himself saying: *I and the Father are one*[^7]. Where the truth has left you no suspicion of distance between the Begetter and the Only-begotten, where Christ has dashed your gradations to pieces, where the rock has broken your ladders.

## The Father and the Son are of one nature.

**3.** But the heretics' calumny refuted, in what way are we to take these words of the Lord: *He who receives anyone I shall send receives me; and he who receives me receives him who sent me?* For if we should wish to understand that it was said, *He who receives me receives him who sent me,* because the Father and the Son are of one nature; it will seem to follow, by the rule of the same words by which it was said, *He who receives anyone I shall send receives me,* that the Son and the apostle are of one nature. This too, indeed, could be understood not inconveniently, since he is the giant of twofold substance who exulted to run the way: *for the Word was made flesh*[^8], that is, God was made man. Accordingly, he might seem to have said thus: *He who receives anyone I shall send receives me* according to the man: *but he who receives me* according to God *receives him who sent me.* But when he said these things, it was not the unity of nature that was commended by him; but in him who is sent, the authority of the sender. So, then, let each one receive him who is sent in such a way that in him he attends to the one who sent him. If, then, you attend to Christ in Peter, you will find the teacher of the disciple; but if you attend to the Father in the Son, you will find the Begetter of the Only-begotten: and so in him who is sent you receive the sender without any error.

The things that follow in the Gospel are not to be cramped by the brevity of time. And therefore let this discourse, dearly beloved, as the food of holy sheep, if it suffices, be taken wholesomely; if it is scant, be ruminated upon with longing.
