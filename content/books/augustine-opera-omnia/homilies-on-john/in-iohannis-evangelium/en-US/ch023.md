# Homily 23

## You will not come to me, that you may have life (Jn 5:19-40).

He who was God became man, by taking up what he was not, not by losing what he was; thus God became man. There you have something for your weakness, there you have something else for your perfection. Let Christ raise you up by that whereby he is man, lead you by that whereby God is man, and bring you through to that which is God. Let us dig deeper that we may arrive, until we arrive at the rock.

**1.** In a certain place in the Gospel the Lord says that the prudent hearer of his word ought to be like a man who, wishing to build, digs deeper until he arrives at the foundation of the stable rock, and there safely sets what he constructs against the assault of the river: so that when it comes, it is rather beaten back by the firmness of the building than, by its impact, makes a ruin of that house[^1]. Let us think of God's Scripture as it were a field, where we wish to build something. Let us not be lazy, nor content with the surface; let us dig deeper, until we arrive at the rock: *and the rock was Christ*[^2].

**2.** Today's reading has spoken to us of the Lord's testimony, that he does not have testimony necessary from men, but has a greater testimony than men are; and he said what that testimony is: *The works*, he says, *which I do bear witness of me*. Then he added: *And the Father who sent me bears witness of me*[^3]. The very works which he does, he says he has received from the Father. The works, then, bear witness; the Father bears witness.

Did John bear no testimony? He bore it indeed, but as a lamp, not to satisfy friends, but to confound enemies: for it had already been foretold beforehand in the person of the Father, *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ; his enemies I will clothe with confusion; but upon him my sanctification will flourish*[^4]. Suppose you are placed as it were in the night; you looked at a lamp, and you marveled at the lamp, and you exulted at the light of the lamp: but that lamp says that there is a sun, in which you ought to exult; and although it burns in the night, it bids you to await the day.

Not, then, that there was no need of that man's testimony. For why would he be sent, if there were no need? But lest a man remain in the lamp and think that the light of the lamp suffices for him; therefore the Lord neither says that that lamp was superfluous, nor yet says that you ought to remain in the lamp.

God's Scripture speaks of another testimony: there indeed God bore witness to his Son, and in that Scripture the Jews had placed their hope, namely in the Law of God, ministered to them through Moses, the servant of God. *Search*, he says, *the Scripture, in which you think you have eternal life; it bears witness of me: and you will not come to me, that you may have life*[^5].

Why do you think you have eternal life in Scripture? Question it, to whom it bears witness; and understand what eternal life is. And because, on account of Moses, they wanted to repudiate Christ, as though an adversary to the institutions and precepts of Moses, again he himself convicts them as it were from another lamp.

**3.** For all men are lamps, because they can both be lit and be extinguished. And the lamps indeed, when they are wise, shine and burn with the Spirit; for even if they were burning and have been extinguished, they also stink. For the servants of God have remained good lamps from the oil of his mercy, not from their own strength. For God's gift, freely given, is the oil of the lamps. *I have labored more than all of them*, says a certain lamp; and lest he should seem to burn by his own strength, he added, *yet not I, but the grace of God with me*[^6].

All prophecy, then, before the Lord's coming is a lamp; of which the apostle Peter says: *We have the more sure prophetic word, to which you do well to attend, as to a lamp shining in a dark place, until the day dawns and the morning star rises in your hearts*[^7]. The Prophets, therefore, are lamps, and all prophecy is one great lamp. What of the Apostles? Are they themselves not lamps too? Plainly they are lamps. For he alone is not a lamp. For he is not lit and extinguished, because *as the Father has life in himself, so he has given to the Son to have life in himself*[^8].

The Apostles, then, are lamps too; and they give thanks, because they have both been lit by the light of Truth, and burn with the Spirit of love, and the oil of God's grace is supplied to them. If they were not lamps, the Lord would not say to them, *You are the light of the world*. For after he said, *You are the light of the world*, he showed that they should not think themselves such a light as was spoken of in *The true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^9]. Now this was said of the Lord, when he was distinguished from John. For of John the Baptist it had been said: *He was not the light, but that he might bear witness to the light*[^10]. And lest you say: How was he not the light, of whom Christ says that he was a lamp[^11]? In comparison with the other light he was not the light. For there was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world.

Therefore when he also said to the disciples, *You are the light of the world*, lest they should think something attributed to themselves which was to be understood of Christ alone, and so the lamps be extinguished by the wind of pride, when he had said, *You are the light of the world*, he at once added: *A city set on a mountain cannot be hidden, nor do they light a lamp and put it under a measuring-bowl, but on a lampstand, that it may shine for all who are in the house*[^12].

But what if he did not call the Apostles a lamp, but the lighters of the lamp which they were to set on the lampstand? Hear that he called them a lamp. *So let your light shine before men*, he says, *that seeing your good works they may glorify*—not you, but *your Father who is in heaven*[^13].

## The soul is made blessed by participation in God.

**4.** Therefore both Moses bore witness to Christ, and John bore witness to Christ, and the other Prophets and Apostles bore witness to Christ. Above all these testimonies he sets the testimony of his own works. Because through them too none but God bore witness to his Son. But God bears witness to his Son in another way too: through his own Son God points out the Son, points out himself through the Son. If a man can come to this, he will not need lamps either, and truly by digging deeper he will bring his building through to the rock.

**5.** Today's reading, then, brothers, is easy: but on account of yesterday's debt (for I know what I deferred, not took away, and the Lord has deigned to grant me to speak to you today as well), recall what you ought to require back, if perhaps in some way, with piety preserved and a wholesome humility, we may stretch ourselves not against God, but toward God; and lift up our soul to him, pouring it out over ourselves, like that man in the Psalm to whom it was said, *Where is your God?* *These things*, he says, *I meditated, and poured out my soul over myself*[^14]. Let us lift up the soul to God, then, not against God; for this too was said: *To you, O Lord, I have lifted up my soul*[^15]. And let us lift it up with his help; for it is heavy.

But why is it heavy? Because *the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind that ponders many things*[^16]. Perhaps, then, we may be able to gather our mind from the many to the one, and, plucked away from the many, to lift it up again to the one (which indeed we shall not be able to do, as I said, unless he helps who wishes our souls to be lifted up to him), and may touch in some part how the Word of God, the only Son of the Father, coeternal and equal with the Father, does nothing but what he has seen the Father doing, while yet the Father himself does nothing except through the seeing Son. It seems to me that the Lord Jesus, in this place wishing to suggest something great to the attentive and to pour it into the capable, but to rouse the incapable to zeal, that those not yet understanding may by living well become capable,

has suggested to us that the human soul and the rational mind, which is in man, is not in a beast—that it is not given growth, not made blessed, not enlightened, except by the very substance of God; and that this soul does something through the body and concerning the body, and has the body as its subject, and that the senses of the body can be soothed or hurt through corporeal things, and on this account—that is, on account of a certain partnership and embrace of soul and body in this life—the soul is delighted when the senses of the body are soothed, or saddened when they are hurt; yet that its blessedness, by which the soul itself is made blessed, comes about only by participation in that life which is ever living, unchangeable, and eternal in substance, which is God: so that, just as the soul, which is lower than God, makes that which is itself lower, namely the body, to live, so it does not make that same soul to live blessedly, except what is higher than the soul itself. For the soul is higher than the body, and God is higher than the soul. It bestows something on the lower; something is bestowed on it by the higher. Let it serve its Lord, lest it be trampled by its own servant.

This, my brothers, is the Christian religion, which is preached throughout the whole world, while its enemies shudder; and where they are conquered, murmur; where they prevail, rage. This is the Christian religion, that one God be worshiped, not many gods; because none but the one God makes the soul blessed. By participation in God it is made blessed. The weak soul is not made blessed by participation in a holy soul, nor is the holy soul made blessed by participation in an angel: but if the weak soul seeks to be blessed, let it seek that from which the holy soul is blessed. For you are not made blessed by an angel; but from where the angel is, from there you too are.

## Souls rise again, bodies rise again.

**6.** These things being laid down beforehand and most firmly established—that the rational soul is not made blessed except by God, that the body is not given life except through the soul, and that the soul is a certain middle thing between God and the body—attend and recollect with me, not today's reading, of which we have spoken sufficiently, but yesterday's reading, which, see, for three days now we have been turning over and treating, and digging according to our strength, until we arrive at the rock.

Christ is the Word, Christ is the Word of God with God, Christ is the Word and the Word is God, Christ and God and Word is one God. Go there, O soul, despising the rest, or even transcending it; go there. Nothing is more powerful than this creature which is called the rational mind, nothing is loftier than this creature: whatever is above it is now the Creator. But I was saying that Christ is the Word, and Christ is the Word of God, and Christ is the Word, God; but Christ is not only the Word, because *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^17]: therefore Christ is both Word and flesh. For *though he was in the form of God, he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*. And what of us in the depths, who, weak and creeping on the ground, could not reach God—were we to be abandoned? Far be it. *He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^18]. Not, then, losing the form of God. He who was God became man, then, by taking up what he was not, not by losing what he was: thus God became man. There you have something for your weakness, there you have something else for your perfection. Let Christ raise you up by that whereby he is man, lead you by that whereby God is man, and bring you through to that which is God.

And the whole preaching and dispensation through Christ is this, brothers, and there is no other: that souls may rise again and bodies may rise again. For both were dead; the body from weakness, the soul from wickedness. Because both were dead, let both rise again. What both? Soul and body. Through what, then, the soul, except through Christ as God? Through what the body, except through Christ as man?

For there was in Christ too a human soul, a whole soul: not only the irrational part of the soul, but also the rational part which is called mind. For there were certain heretics, and they were driven out of the Church, who thought that the body of Christ did not have a rational mind, but as it were the soul of a brute beast: for, the rational mind excepted, the life is that of a brute beast. But because they have been expelled, and expelled by the truth, receive the whole Christ—Word, rational mind, and flesh. This whole is Christ. Let your soul rise again from wickedness through that which is God, let your body rise again from corruption through that which is man.

Accordingly, dearly beloved, hear the great depth, as it seems to me, of this reading; and see how Christ speaks here of nothing else than why Christ came: that souls may rise again from wickedness, that bodies may rise again from corruption. I have already said through what souls rise again—through the very substance of God; through what bodies rise again—through the human dispensation of our Lord Jesus Christ.

**7.** *Amen, amen, I say to you, the Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing: for whatever he does, this the Son also does likewise*[^19]. Heaven, earth, sea, the things in heaven, the things on earth, the things in the sea, visible things, invisible things, animals on the earth, shrubs in the fields, things swimming in the waters, things flying in the air, things shining in heaven; besides all these, Angels, Virtues, Thrones, Dominations, Principalities, Powers: *all things were made through him*[^20]. Did God make all these things and show them, once made, to the Son, so that he too might make another world full of all these things? Surely not. But what then? *For whatever he does, this*—not other things, but this—*the Son also* does, and not unlike, but *likewise*.

*For the Father loves the Son, and shows him all that he himself does*[^21]. The Father shows the Son that souls may be raised, because through the Father and the Son souls are raised: nor can souls live unless their life is God. If, then, souls cannot live unless their life is God, just as they are themselves the life of bodies, what the Father shows the Son—that is, what he does—he does through the Son. For he does not show the Son by doing, but by showing he does through the Son. For the Son sees the Father showing before anything is made, and from the Father's showing and the Son's seeing comes about what comes about, by the Father through the Son. So souls are raised, if they can see this conjunction of unity—the Father showing, the Son seeing, and that through the Father's showing and the Son's seeing a creature comes to be; and that there comes about through the Father's showing and the Son's seeing that which is neither the Father nor the Son, but below the Father and the Son, whatever is made by the Father through the Son. Who sees this?

## Remove all corporeal things.

**8.** See, again to carnal perceptions, see, once more we humble ourselves and come down to you—if indeed we had at any time ascended at all from you. You wish to show something to your son, that he may do what you do: you are going to do it, and so to show it. What, then, you are going to do in order to show your son, you surely do not do through your son: but you alone do what he may see done, and do another such thing likewise. It is not so there: why do you go to your own likeness, and blot out in yourself the likeness of God? There it is not at all so. Find something whereby you may show your son what you do, before you do it; so that, when you have shown it, you may do through your son what you do.

Now perhaps it as it were occurs to you: See, you say, I am thinking of making a house, and I want it to be built through my son: before I build it myself, I show my son what I want to make; and he makes it, and I through him, to whom I have shown my will. You have indeed withdrawn from the former likeness, but you still lie in great unlikeness. For see, before you make the house, you indicate to your son and show him what you want to make; so that, while you show it before you make it, he may make what you have shown, and you through him: but you are going to speak words to your son, words will run between you and him; and between the one showing and the one seeing, or the one speaking and the one hearing, an articulate sound flies, which is not what you are, not what he is. For that sound which goes out of your mouth, and, the air being struck, touches your son's ear, and, having filled the sense of hearing, brings your thought through to his heart; that sound, then, is not yourself, is not your son himself. A sign was given from your mind to your son's mind, which sign is neither your mind nor your son's mind, but something else.

Do we then think that the Father spoke with the Son in this way? Were there words between God and the Word? How can this be? Or, whatever the Father wanted to say to the Son, if he wanted to say it by a word—the Son himself is the Word of the Father; would he then speak by a word to the Word? Or because the Son is the great Word, were lesser words going to run between the Father and the Son? Was some sound, and as it were a temporal and fleeting creation, going to come out of the Father's mouth and strike the Son's ear? Does God have a body, so that this should proceed as it were from his lips; and does the Word have bodily ears, into which a sound may come?

Remove all corporeal things, see simplicity, if you are simple. But how will you be simple? If you do not entangle yourself with the world, but disentangle yourself from the world: for by disentangling yourself you will be simple. And see whether you can grasp what I say, or, if you cannot, believe what you do not see. You speak to your son, you speak by a word; the word that sounds is neither you nor your son.

**9.** I have, you say, another way to show it: for my son is so well taught that he does not hear me even when I speak, but I show him by a nod what he is to do. See, show by a nod what you wish; surely your mind wishes to show what it has in itself. From what do you make the nod? From the body, of course—lips, face, eyebrows, eyes, hands. All these are not what your mind is; these too are middle things: something was understood through these signs, which are not what your mind is, nor what your son's mind is: but all this which you do by the body is below your mind, and below your son's mind; nor can your son know your mind unless you give him signs from the body. What, then, do I do? It is not so there; simplicity is there. The Father shows the Son what he does, and by showing he begets the Son.

I see what I have said: but because I see also to whom I have said it, let this understanding come about in you someday. Now, if you cannot comprehend what God is, comprehend at least what God is not: you will have advanced much, if you think nothing of God other than what he is. You cannot yet arrive at what he is; arrive at what he is not. God is not a body, not earth, not heaven, not moon, not sun, not stars, not these corporeal things. For if not the heavenly things, how much less the earthly? Take away every body.

Hear yet another thing: God is not a changeable spirit. For I confess, and it must be confessed, because the Gospel says, *God is spirit*[^22]. But pass beyond every changeable spirit, pass beyond the spirit that now knows, now does not know, now remembers and forgets; that wills what it did not will, does not will what it willed; whether it already undergoes these changes, or can undergo them: pass beyond all these. You will not find in God anything of changeableness, nothing that is now one way and a little before was another. For where you find now one way and now another, a certain death has occurred there: for it is death, not to be what one was. The soul is called immortal: it is indeed, because the soul always lives, and there is in it a certain abiding life, but a changeable life. According to the changeableness of this life it can be called mortal too: because if it was living wisely and grows foolish, it has died for the worse; if it was living foolishly and grows wise, it has died for the better.

For Scripture teaches us that there is a death for the worse and a death for the better. Surely those were dead for the worse of whom it is said, *Let the dead bury their dead*[^23]; and, *Rise, you who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ will enlighten you*[^24]; and, from this reading, *When the dead will hear, and those who hear will live*[^25]. They were dead for the worse, therefore they come to life again. By coming to life again they die for the better, because in coming to life again too they will not be what they were: and not to be what one was is death.

But perhaps, if it is for the better, it is not called death? The Apostle called it death: *If you have died with Christ to the elements of this world, why do you still, as though living in this world, make decrees?*[^26] and again: *For you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^27]. He wishes us to die that we may live, because we lived that we might die.

Whatever, then, dies both from the better to the worse and from the worse to the better, this is not God: because the highest goodness can neither go to the better, nor true eternity to the worse. For true eternity is where there is nothing of time. But was it now this, and now that? Then time has already been admitted, it is not eternal. For that you may know that God is not as the soul is: surely the soul is immortal; what then is it that the Apostle says of God, *Who alone has immortality*[^28], unless because he plainly said this: He alone has unchangeableness, because he alone has true eternity? There, then, is no changeableness.

## Let us recognize the Creator in his image.

**10.** Recognize in yourself something that I want to speak of, within, within in you; not in you as in your body, for there too it can be said to be in you. For in you there is health, in you any age you please, but according to the body; in you is your hand, your foot: but one thing is in you within, another in you as in your garment. But leave outside both your garment and your flesh, descend into yourself, go to your inner chamber, your mind, and there see what I want to say, if you can. For if you yourself are far from yourself, from where can you draw near to God? I was speaking of God, and you thought you were going to understand: I speak of the soul, I speak of you; understand, there I will prove it to you.

For I do not go very far afield for examples, when from your mind I want to give some likeness for your God; because surely not in the body, but in the mind itself, was man made to the image of God. In his likeness let us seek God, in his image let us recognize the Creator. There within, if we can, let us find this which we are speaking of: how the Father shows the Son, and the Son sees what the Father shows, before anything is made by the Father through the Son. But when I have spoken and you have understood, do not even so think that that thing is now anything of such a sort; rather keep there the piety which I want kept by you, and which I especially urge: that is, that if you cannot comprehend what God is, you should not think it a small thing to know what he is not.

**11.** See, in your mind I see two things, your memory and your thought—that is, as it were a certain edge and gaze of your soul. You see something, and perceive it through the eyes, and commit it to memory: there within is what you committed to memory, hidden away in a secret place as in a storehouse, as in a treasury, as in a certain inner chamber and recess. You are thinking of something else, your attention is elsewhere; that which you saw is in your memory, and is not seen by you, because your thought is directed to something else. Now I prove it; I speak to those who know: I name Carthage, and all of you who know it now within yourselves have seen Carthage. Are there as many Carthages as there are souls of yours? You have all seen it through this name: through these four syllables known to you, bursting from my mouth, your ears were touched, the sense of the soul was touched through the body, and the mind was turned back from another attention to that which was there, and saw Carthage. Was Carthage then made there? It was already there, but it lay hidden. Why did it lie hidden there? Because your mind was attending to something else: but when your thought was turned back to that which was in the memory, it was formed from it, and a certain vision of the mind came about. Before there was not vision, but there was memory: when thought was turned back to memory, vision came about.

Your memory, then, showed Carthage to your thought, and what was in it before you directed your attention, it showed to the attention of thought turned toward it. See, a showing was made by the memory, a vision came about in the thought; and no words ran in between, no sign was given from the body; you neither nodded, nor wrote, nor made a sound; and yet thought saw what memory showed. But both that which showed and that to which it showed are of the same substance. But that your memory might have Carthage, this image was drawn in through the eyes: for you saw what you would store in the memory. So the tree which you remember you saw, so the mountain, so the river, so a friend's face, so an enemy's, so a father's, mother's, brother's, sister's, son's, neighbor's; so of the letters written in a book, so of the book itself, so of this basilica: all these you have seen, and, once seen, because they already existed, you committed to memory; and as it were you put there what you might see by thinking when you willed, even when they were absent from these bodily eyes.

For you saw Carthage when you were at Carthage; your soul received its form through the eyes; this form was stored in your memory, and you, a man set at Carthage, kept something within which you could see within yourself even when you were not there. All these things you received from without. What the Father shows the Son, he does not receive from without: the whole is done within; because there would be nothing of created things without, unless the Father had made this through the Son. Every creature was made by God; before it was made it did not exist. Therefore it was not, once made, seen and retained in memory, that the Father might show it to the Son as memory to thought: but the Father showed it to be made, the Son saw it to be made, and the Father made it by showing it, because he made it through the Son seeing it.

And therefore it ought not to disturb you that it was said, *except what he sees the Father doing*: it was not said, *showing*. For by this it was signified that for the Father to do is the same as to show; so that from this it may be understood that he makes all things through the Son seeing. Neither that showing nor that vision is temporal. For because through the Son all times are made, surely the things to be made could not be shown to him at any time. But the Father's showing begets the Son's vision in the same way as the Father begets the Son. For the showing generates the vision, not the vision the showing. And if we were able to gaze more purely and perfectly, perhaps we would find that the Father is not one thing and his showing another, nor the Son one thing and his vision another.

But if we have scarcely grasped this, scarcely been able to explain how the memory shows to thought what it received from without, how much less shall we be able to grasp or explain how God the Father shows the Son what he does not have from elsewhere, or what is not other than himself? We are little ones; I tell you what God is not, I do not show what he is: therefore, that we may grasp what he is, what shall we do? Will you be able by me, by my means? I will say this to little ones, both to you and to me: there is one through whom we are able; just now we sang, just now we heard, *Cast your care upon the Lord, and he will nourish you*[^29]. For this is why you cannot, O man, because you are a little one: if you are a little one, you must be nourished; once nourished, you will be grown; and what as a little one you could not do, grown you will see: but that you may be nourished, *Cast your care upon the Lord, and he will nourish you*  
12.

Now, then, let us run through briefly what remains, and see here what I have commended, how the Lord suggests it. *The Father loves the Son, and shows him all that he himself does*[^30]. He himself raises souls, but through the Son, that the souls raised may enjoy the substance of God, that is, of the Father and the Son. *And greater works than these will he show him*[^31]. Greater than what? Than the healings of bodies. We have already treated this before, and ought not to linger. For the resurrection of the body for eternity is greater than this healing of the body in that sick man, which was done for a time. *And greater works than these will he show him, that you may marvel*. He will show it as it were temporally: therefore as it were to the man made in time, because the Word, God, was not made, through whom all times were made: but Christ was made man in time. It is apparent under what Consul, on what day, the Virgin Mary, having conceived by the Holy Spirit, bore Christ: therefore he was made man in time, he through whom, as God, times were made. Therefore as it were in time he will show him greater works, that is, the resurrection of bodies, that you may marvel at the resurrection of bodies brought about through the Son.

**13.** Then he returns to that resurrection of souls: *For as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*[^32]—but according to the spirit. The Father gives life, the Son gives life; whom the Father will, whom the Son will: but the Father the same ones whom the Son does; because *all things were made through him*. *For as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*. This was said of the resurrection of souls: what of the resurrection of bodies? He returns, and says: *For the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*[^33]. The resurrection of souls comes about through the eternal and unchangeable substance of the Father and the Son: but the resurrection of bodies comes about through the temporal dispensation of the Son's humanity, not coeternal with the Father. Therefore, when he mentioned the judgment, where the resurrection of bodies would take place, *For the Father*, he said, *judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*: but of the resurrection of souls, *As the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*.

That, then, the Father and the Son do together: but this, of the resurrection of bodies: *The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son. That all may honor the Son, as they honor the Father*[^34]. This was assigned to the resurrection of souls. *That all may honor the Son*. How? *As they honor the Father*. For the resurrection of souls the Son works as the Father does: so the Son gives life as the Father does. Therefore in the resurrection of souls let all honor the Son, as they honor the Father. What of the honoring on account of the resurrection of the body? *He who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him*[^35]. He did not say, *as*; but, *honors*, and *honors*. For the man Christ is honored, but not as the Father, God. Why? Because in this sense he said, *The Father is greater than I*[^36]. But when is the Son honored as the Father is honored? When *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and all things were made through him*[^37]. And therefore in this second honoring what does he say? *He who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him*. The Son was not sent except because he was made man.

## See, the word of God lingers with us.

**14.** *Amen, amen, I say to you*. Again he returns to the resurrection of souls, that we may grasp him by his repeating it: because we could not follow the word as it were flying; see, the word of God lingers with us, see, it as it were dwells with our weaknesses: it returns again to the commendation of the resurrection of souls. *Amen, amen, I say to you, he who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life*—but as it were from the Father. Because he who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life from the Father, by believing in him who sent him. *And will not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life*:

but he is given life from the Father, in whom he believes. What, do you not give life? See that *the Son too gives life to whom he will*. *Amen, amen, I say to you, the hour comes when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live*[^38]. Here he did not say: They will believe him who sent me, and therefore will live; but by hearing the voice of the Son of God, those who hear—that is, obey the Son of God—will live. Therefore they will both live from the Father, when they believe the Father; and live from the Son, when they hear the voice of the Son of God. Why will they both live from the Father and live from the Son? *For as the Father has life in himself, so he has given to the Son to have life in himself*[^39].

**15.** He has finished with the resurrection of souls; it remains to speak more plainly of the resurrection of bodies. *And he gave him power also to do judgment*[^40]: not only to raise souls through faith and wisdom, but also to do judgment. But why this? *Because he is the Son of man*. The Father, then, does something through the Son of man which he does not do out of his own substance, to which the Son is equal; such as being born itself, being crucified itself, dying itself, rising again itself: for none of these happened to the Father. So too the raising of bodies. For the raising of souls the Father does out of his own substance through the substance of the Son, by which he is equal to him; for souls are made partakers of that unchangeable light, not bodies: but the raising of bodies the Father does through the Son of man. *For he gave him power also to do judgment, because he is the Son of man*: according to what he said above, *For the Father judges no one*[^41].

And to show that he said this of the resurrection of bodies: *Do not marvel at this, for the hour comes*. It is not now, but *the hour comes, in which all who are in the tombs* (this too you heard yesterday most fully) *will hear his voice and will come forth*. And to where? Into judgment: *Those who have done good, to the resurrection of life; those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment*[^42]. And do you do this alone, since the Father has given all judgment to the Son, and judges no one? *I*, he says, *do it*. But how do you do it? *I can do nothing of myself: as I hear, I judge, and my judgment is just*[^43].

When the resurrection of souls was being treated, he did not say, *I hear*, but, *I see*. For *I hear* is as it were the command of the Father giving an order. Now, then, as man, as the one than whom the Father is greater; now from the form of a servant, not from the form of God, *as I hear, I judge; and my judgment is just*. From where comes the just judgment of a man? My brothers, attend: *Because I do not seek my own will, but the will of him who sent me*[^44].
