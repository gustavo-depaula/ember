# Tractate 111

## *That they too may be one in us*[^1].

## We too are the place of God, since we are his temple; even as he prays for us who died for us, and lives for us, that in them we may be one. — Hear, and be rejoicing in hope.

**1.** The Lord Jesus raises his own to a great hope, than which there can be none greater at all. Hear, and be rejoicing in hope, for the sake of which this life is not to be loved, but to be endured, that you may be patient in its tribulation. Hear, I say, and attend to that by which our hope is lifted up. Christ Jesus says: the only-begotten Son of God, who is coeternal and equal to the Father, says; who for our sake was made man, but not made, as *every man is a liar*[^2], says; the way, the life, the truth, says; he who overcame the world, says to those by whom he overcame it. Hear, believe, hope, desire what he says: *Father,* he says, *those whom you gave me, I will that where I am, they too may be with me*[^3]. Who are these whom he says were given him by the Father? Are they not those of whom he says elsewhere, *No one comes to me, unless the Father who sent me draw him*[^4]? Now how those things which he says are done by the Father, he himself too does with the Father, we know, if we have profited anything in this Gospel. They are, then, those whom he received from the Father, whom he himself too chose out of the world, and chose that they should now not be of the world, as he himself too is not of the world: that they may yet be themselves too the world believing and knowing that Christ was sent by God the Father, that the world might be freed out of the world, lest the world to be reconciled to God should be condemned with the most hostile world. For thus he says at the beginning of this prayer: *You gave him power over all flesh,* that is, over every man, *that to all whom you gave him he may give eternal life*[^5]. Where he shows that he received power indeed over every man, that he might free whom he willed, condemn whom he willed, he who will judge the living and the dead; but that those were given him to all of whom he should give eternal life. For thus he says: *That to all whom you gave him he may give eternal life.* Accordingly, those are not given him to whom he will not give eternal life: although their power too is given to him, to whom is given power over all flesh, that is, over every man. So the reconciled world will be freed out of the hostile world, when he exerts his power upon it, to send it into eternal death: but he makes that one his own, to whom he gives eternal life.

Wherefore to absolutely all his sheep the good shepherd, to all his members the great head, promised this reward, that where he himself is, we too with him may be: nor will it be possible but that this be done which the almighty Son said he willed to the almighty Father. For there too is the Holy Spirit, equally eternal, equally God, the one Spirit of the two, and the substance of the will of both. For that which he is read to have said, the passion drawing near, *But not what I will, but what you will, Father*[^6], as though there were or had been one will of the Father, another of the Son, is the sound of our weakness, faithful though it be, which our head transfigured into himself, when he also bore our sins. But that the will of the Father and of the Son is one, of whom the Spirit too is one, by joining whom we recognize the Trinity, even if weakness does not yet permit us to understand, let piety believe.

**2.** But since we have already said, for the brevity of the sermon, to whom he promised, and how firm that promise is; let us see, as far as we are able, this very thing — what it is that he deigned to promise. *Those whom you gave me,* he says, *I will that where I am, they too may be with me.* As far as pertains to the creature in which he *was made of the seed of David according to the flesh*[^7], he himself too was not yet where he was going to be: but he could say, *where I am,* in that way in which we may understand that he was soon going to ascend into heaven, so that he might say that he was already there where he was soon going to be. He could also in that way in which he had already said before, speaking to Nicodemus: *No one has ascended into heaven, except him who descended from heaven, the Son of man, who is in heaven*[^8]. For there too he did not say, will be; but, *is,* on account of the unity of the person, in which both God is man, and man is God.

He promised, then, that we shall be in heaven: for there the form of a servant was lifted up, which he took from the Virgin, and placed at the right hand of the Father. On account of the hope of so great a good as this, the Apostle too says: *But God, who is rich in mercy, on account of the great love with which he loved us, even when we were dead in sins, made us alive together with Christ, by whose grace we have been saved; and raised us up together, and made us sit together in the heavenly places in Christ Jesus*[^9]. This, then, can be understood as what the Lord said: *That where I am, they too may be with me.* And he indeed said of himself that he was already there; but of us he said that he willed that we should be there with him, he did not show that we already were. But the Apostle spoke of that which the Lord said he willed to be done, as though it had been done. For he does not say: He is going to raise up, and to make us sit in the heavenly places; but, *he raised us up, and made us sit in the heavenly places*: because not vainly but faithfully he already reckons as done what he does not doubt will be.

But as far as pertains to the form of God in which he is equal to the Father, if we wish to understand according to it what was said, *I will that where I am, they too may be with me*; let all thought of bodily images depart from the mind: whatever occurs to the mind as long, broad, dense, colored with any bodily light, diffused through any spaces of place whether finite or infinite, from all these let it, as far as it can, turn away the keen edge of its contemplation or intention. And let it not be asked where the Son, equal to the Father, is, since no one finds where he is not. But let him who wishes to seek, rather seek to be with him; not everywhere as he is, but wherever he can be. For he who said to the man hanging in punishment and confessing in a salutary way, *Today you will be with me in paradise*[^10]; according to that which he was as man, his soul that very day was going to be in hell, his flesh in the tomb; but according to that which he was as God, he was certainly in paradise too. And therefore the soul of the robber, absolved from its former crimes, and now blessed by his gift, although it could not be everywhere as he, yet even that very day could be with him in paradise, whence he, who is everywhere always, had not withdrawn.

Accordingly, doubtless, it was not enough for him to say, *I will that where I am, they too may be*; but he added, *with me.* For to be with him is a great good. For even the wretched can be where he is, since whoever they are, wherever they are, he too is there: but the blessed alone are with him, because they cannot be blessed except from him. Or was it not truly said to God, *If I ascend into heaven, you are there; and if I descend into hell, you are present*[^11]? Or is Christ not the Wisdom of God, which *reaches everywhere on account of its purity*[^12]? But the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness does not comprehend it. And through this — that we may take some example, however far unlike, from a visible thing — just as a blind man, even if he be where the light is, is yet not himself with the light, but is absent from what is present; so the unbeliever and impious man, or even the faithful and devout, yet not fit to behold the light of wisdom, even if he can be nowhere where Christ too is not, is yet not himself with Christ, at least by sight. For that a devoutly faithful man is with Christ by faith is not to be doubted: on account of which he says, *He who is not with me is against me*[^13]. But when he was saying to God the Father, *Those whom you gave me, I will that where I am, they too may be with me,* he was speaking altogether of that sight, in which we shall see him as he is.

**3.** Let no one disturb the most serene sense by a clouded contradiction; let the things that follow bear witness to the preceding words. For when he had said, *I will that where I am, they too may be with me,* he at once followed and added: *That they may see my glory which you have given me, because you loved me before the foundation of the world*[^14]. *That they may see,* he said; not, that they may believe. This is the reward of faith, not faith. For if faith in the Epistle to the Hebrews was rightly defined, *The conviction of things that are not seen*[^15]; why should not the reward of faith be defined: The vision of the things which, believed, were hoped for? For when we shall see the glory which the Father gave the Son — even if we understand it to be spoken of in this place, not the glory which the Father gave the Son in begetting him equal to himself, but that which he gave him, made son of man, after the death of the cross: when, therefore, we shall see that glory of the Son, then surely will be the judgment of the living and the dead, then the impious man will be taken away, that he may not see the glory of the Lord; which, but that whereby he is God? For blessed are the pure in heart, since they shall see God: nor are the impious pure in heart, therefore they shall not see. Then they themselves will go into eternal punishment; for thus the impious man will be taken away, that he may not see the glory of the Lord: *but the righteous* will go *into eternal life*[^16].

And what is eternal life? *That they may know you,* he says, *the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*[^17]: not, surely, as those knew him who, although not pure in heart, were yet able to see him judging in the glorified form of a servant; but as the one true God is to be known by the pure in heart — the Son, with the Father and the Holy Spirit, because the Trinity itself is the one true God.

If, then, according to that whereby the Son of God is God equal and coeternal to the Father, we take this saying, *I will that where I am, they too may be with me,* we shall be in the Father with Christ; but he as he, we as we, wherever in body we shall be. For if places must be spoken of even for those by which bodies are not contained, and there is a place for each thing where it is; the eternal place of Christ where he always is, is the Father himself, and the place of the Father is the Son; because: *I,* he says, *am in the Father, and the Father is in me*[^18]; and in this prayer: *As you, Father,* he says, *in me, and I in you*[^19]: and our place is they themselves, because there follows: *That they too may be one in us*: and we too are the place of God, since we are his temple; even as he prays for us who died for us, and lives for us, that in them we may be one; because *his place was made in peace, and his dwelling in Zion*[^20], which we are. But who is fit to think these places, or what are in these places, without spacious capacities and without bodily masses? Yet not a little is gained, if at least whatever such thing occurs to the eye of the heart is denied, rejected, disapproved: and a certain light, in which these things are to be denied, rejected, disapproved, is thought of, as it can be; and is known how certain it is, and is loved, that one may rise from there, and tend to the inner things: which, when the mind, weak and less pure than they are, cannot penetrate; it is not without a groan of love and tears of desire driven back from there; and let it patiently bear it, so long as it is cleansed by faith, and that it may be able to dwell there, is prepared by holy morals.

**4.** How, then, shall we not be with Christ where he is, when we shall be in the Father in whom he is? Nor was the Apostle here silent to us — although we do not yet hold the thing, but yet bear the hope. For he says: *If you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God; savor the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth. For you are dead,* he says, *and your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^21]. Behold, meanwhile, by faith and hope our life, where Christ is, is with him; because it is with Christ in God. Behold, as it were already done is that which he prayed should be done, saying, *I will that where I am, they too may be with me*: but now by faith. But when will it be by sight? *When Christ,* he says, *your life, shall appear, then you too will appear with him in glory*[^22]. Then we shall appear what we then shall be; because then it will appear that not vainly we believed and hoped this before we were. This will be done by him to whom the Son, when he had said, *That they may see my glory which you have given me,* at once subjoined: *Because you loved me before the foundation of the world.* For in him he loved us too before the foundation of the world, and then predestined what at the end of the world he is going to do.

**5.** *Father,* he says, *righteous, the world has not known you*[^23]. Because you are righteous, therefore it has not known you. For that world predestined to condemnation deservedly did not know you: but the world which through Christ he reconciled to himself, knew you not by merit, but by grace. For what is it to know him, but eternal life? which to the condemned world he certainly did not give, but to the reconciled he gave.

Therefore, then, the world has not known you, because you are righteous, and to its merits you granted that it should not know: and therefore the reconciled world has known, because you are merciful, and that it might know you came to its aid not by merit, but by grace.

Finally there follows: *But I have known you.* He himself is the fountain of grace, God by nature, but man of the Holy Spirit and the Virgin by ineffable grace: finally, on account of him, because the grace of God is through Jesus Christ our Lord: *And these have known,* he says, *that you sent me.* They are the reconciled world. But because you sent me, therefore they have known: therefore they have known by grace.

## The prayer of the Savior is ended, the passion begins.

**6.** *And I have made known to them,* he says, *your name, and will make it known*[^24]. I have made it known by faith, I will make it known by sight: I have made it known to those who are on pilgrimage with an end, I will make it known to those who reign without end. *That the love,* he says, *with which you loved me may be in them, and I in them.* It is not a usual mode of speech, *the love with which you loved me may be in them, and I in them*: for usually it would be said, the love by which you loved me. This indeed was translated from the Greek: but there are similar Latin ones too; as we say, He served a faithful service, he soldiered a strenuous soldiering; when it would seem it ought to be said, He served with faithful service, he soldiered with strenuous soldiering. But of what sort this mode of speech is, *the love with which you loved me*; such the Apostle too used: *I have fought the good fight*[^25]: he does not say, with a good fight, which would be said more usually and as it were more rightly. But how is the love with which the Father loved the Son also in us, except because we are his members, and in him we are loved, since he whole is loved, that is, head and body? Therefore he subjoined, *and I in them*; as if he said, since I too am in them. For in one way he is in us as in his temple; but in another way because we ourselves too are he, since, according to that whereby he was made man that he might be our head, we are his body.

The prayer of the Savior is ended, the passion begins: therefore let this sermon too be ended, that of the passion, what he himself shall grant, may be discussed in other sermons.
