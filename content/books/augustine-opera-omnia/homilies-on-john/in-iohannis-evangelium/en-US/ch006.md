# Homily 6

## *This is he who baptizes* [^1]

## Christ is the one who baptizes in the Holy Spirit. Let Peter baptize, this is he who baptizes; let Paul baptize, this is he who baptizes; let Judas baptize, this is he who baptizes.

**1.** I confess to your Holiness, I had feared that this cold weather would make you cold about gathering: but because by this celebration and your large attendance you show that you are fervent in spirit, I do not doubt that you have also prayed for me, that I may discharge my debt to you. For I had promised, in the name of Christ, to discourse today, since the narrowness of time then prevented us from being able to explain by treatment why God wished to show the Holy Spirit through the form of a dove. That this might be explained, today's day has dawned for us; and I perceive that, by a desire to hear and by devout devotion, you have gathered more numerously. May God fulfill your expectation out of our mouth. For you loved, that you might come: but what did you love? If us, this too is well; for we wish to be loved by you, but we do not wish hope to be placed in us. Because, then, we love you in Christ, love us in return in Christ, and let our love for one another groan to God: for that very groan is the groan of the dove.

## The groan of the dove.

**2.** If, then, it is the groan of the dove, which we all know—but doves groan in love; hear what the Apostle says, and do not marvel that the Holy Spirit willed to be shown in the form of a dove: *For what we should pray for as we ought*, he says, *we do not know: but the Spirit himself intercedes for us with groans that cannot be uttered* [^2]. What then, my brothers, are we going to say this, that the Spirit groans, where there is perfect and eternal blessedness for him with the Father and the Son? For the Holy Spirit is God, just as the Son of God is God, and the Father is God. Three times I said God, but I did not say three gods: for it is rather God three times than three gods; because the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one God: this you know very well. Therefore the Holy Spirit does not groan in himself, with himself, in that Trinity, in that blessedness, in that eternity of substance: but he groans in us, because he makes us groan.

And it is no small thing, that the Holy Spirit teaches us to groan: for he intimates to us that we are on pilgrimage, and teaches us to sigh for the homeland, and by that very desire we groan. He for whom it is well in this world—rather, he who thinks that it is well with him, who exults in the joy of carnal things, and the abundance of temporal things, and a vain happiness—has the voice of the raven: for the voice of the raven is clamorous, not given to groaning. But he who knows himself to be in the pressure of this mortality, and to be on pilgrimage away from the Lord, not yet to hold that perpetual blessedness that has been promised us, but to have it in hope, going to have it in reality, when the Lord comes splendid in his manifestation, who first came hidden in humility; he who knows this, groans. And as long as he groans for this, he groans well: the Spirit taught him to groan, from the dove he learned to groan.

For many groan in earthly unhappiness, whether shaken by losses, or weighed down by sickness of body, or shut up in prisons, or bound with chains, or tossed by the waves of the sea, or hedged about by some snares of enemies, they groan: but they do not groan with the groan of the dove, they do not groan with the love of God, they do not groan in the spirit. Therefore such people, when they have been freed from those very pressures, exult in loud voices: where it appears that they are ravens, not doves. Rightly was the raven sent from the ark, and did not return; the dove was sent, and returned: those two birds Noah sent. He had there a raven, he had also a dove; both these kinds that ark contained: and if the ark prefigured the Church, you surely see that it is necessary that in this flood of the world the Church should contain both kinds, both the raven and the dove. Who are the ravens? Those who seek their own. Who are the doves? Those who seek the things that are Christ's [^3].

**3.** For this reason, then, when he sent the Holy Spirit, he showed him visibly in two ways: through a dove, and through fire: through a dove, over the Lord baptized; through fire, over the disciples gathered together. For when the Lord had ascended into heaven after the resurrection, having spent forty days with his disciples, when the day of Pentecost was completed, he sent them the Holy Spirit, as he had promised. The Spirit, then, coming at that time, filled that place, and after a first sound was made from heaven as of a violent blast being borne along, as we read in the Acts of the Apostles: *There appeared to them*, it says, *divided tongues as of fire, which also settled upon each one of them; and they began to speak in tongues, as the Spirit gave them to utter* [^4]. By the one we saw a dove over the Lord, by the other divided tongues over the disciples gathered together: there simplicity, here fervor is shown.

For there are those who are called simple, and they are lazy: they are called simple, but they are sluggish. Not such was Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit: he was simple, because he harmed no one; he was fervent, because he reproved the impious. For he did not keep silent to the Jews: his are those flaming words: *Stiff-necked, and uncircumcised in heart and ears, you have always resisted the Holy Spirit* [^5]. A great onslaught: but the dove rages without gall. For that you may know that he raged without gall, those who were ravens, when they heard these words, ran at once to stones against the dove; Stephen began to be stoned: and he who a little before, raging and fervent in spirit, had as it were made an onslaught against enemies, and as if violently had inveighed against them in words fiery and so flaming, as you have heard: *Stiff-necked, and uncircumcised in heart and ears*—so that one who heard those words would think that Stephen, if he were allowed, would at once wish them to be consumed: when the stones came against him from their hands, on bended knee he said: *Lord, do not lay this offense to their charge* [^6]. He had clung to the unity of the dove. For his master, over whom the dove descended, had done that first; who, hanging on the cross, said: *Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they do* [^7]. Therefore, that those sanctified by the Spirit should have no deceit, it was shown in the dove: that simplicity should not remain cold, it was shown in the fire. And let it not disturb you that the tongues were divided. For tongues differ, and therefore he appeared in divided tongues. *Tongues*, it says, *divided as of fire, which also settled upon each one of them*. Tongues differ among themselves, but the diversity of tongues is not schism. In the divided tongues do not fear scattering, recognize unity in the dove.

**4.** So then, so it was fitting that the Holy Spirit should be shown coming over the Lord, that each one might understand that, if he has the Holy Spirit, *he ought to be simple as a dove*; to have with his brothers true peace, which the kisses of doves signify. For ravens too have kisses, but in ravens the peace is false, in the dove the peace is true. Therefore not everyone who says, Peace be with you, is to be heard as a dove.

From where, then, are the kisses of ravens distinguished from the kisses of doves? Ravens kiss, but they tear; the nature of doves is innocent of tearing: where, then, there is tearing, there is no true peace in the kisses; those have true peace who have not torn the Church. For ravens feed on death, the dove does not have this; it lives on the fruits of the earth, its food is innocent: which truly, brothers, is to be marveled at in the dove. There are very small sparrows that even kill flies: none of these things does the dove; for it does not feed on death. Those who tore the Church feed on deaths.

God is able; let us pray that those revive who are devoured by them and do not feel it. Many acknowledge it, for they revive; for at their coming we daily give thanks in the name of Christ. Only be you so simple that you may be fervent; and let your fervor be in your tongues. Do not keep silent; speaking with burning tongues, kindle the cold.

**5.** For what, my brothers? Who does not see what they do not see? And no wonder; because they are like the raven that was sent out from the ark and was unwilling to return from there. For who does not see what they do not see? And they are even ungrateful to the Holy Spirit himself. See, the dove descended over the Lord, and over the Lord baptized; and there appeared that holy and true Trinity, which to us is one God. For the Lord ascended from the water, as we read in the Gospel: *And behold, the heavens were opened to him, and he saw the Spirit descending like a dove, and it remained upon him: and at once a voice followed: You are my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased* [^8]. The Trinity appears most manifestly: the Father in the voice, the Son in the man, the Spirit in the dove. In this Trinity, to which the Apostles were sent, let us see what we see, and what is a marvel that they do not see: for they do not truly fail to see, but to that which strikes their face they shut their eyes. To what were the disciples sent, *in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit* [^9], by him of whom it was said: ***This is he who baptizes***? For it was said to the ministers by him who kept this power for himself.

## My dove is one.

**6.** For this is what John saw in him, and recognized what he did not know: not that he did not know him to be the Son of God, or that he did not know him to be the Lord, or that he did not know him to be the Christ, or indeed that he did not know this either, that he himself was going to baptize in water and the Holy Spirit; for this too he knew: but that in such a way as to keep that very power for himself, and transfer it to none of the ministers—this is what he learned in the dove.

For through this power, which Christ alone kept for himself, and poured out into none of the ministers, although he deigned to baptize through his ministers, through this stands the unity of the Church, which is signified in the dove, of which it was said: *My dove is one, she is the only one of her mother* [^10]. For if, as I have already said, my brothers, the power were transferred from the Lord to a minister, there would be as many baptisms as there were ministers, and the unity of Baptism would no longer stand.

**7.** Attend, brothers: before our Lord Jesus Christ came to baptism (for after baptism the dove descended, in which John recognized something proper, when it had been said to him: ***He upon whom you see the Spirit descending like a dove, and remaining upon him, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit***), he knew that he himself baptizes in the Holy Spirit; but that it was with such a property, that the power should not pass from him into another, although by his giving—this he learned there.

And from where do we prove that John already knew this too, that the Lord was going to baptize in the Holy Spirit; so that this may be understood, that he learned in the dove that the Lord was going to baptize in the Holy Spirit in such a way that that power should pass into no other man? From where do we prove it? The dove descended when the Lord was already baptized: but before the Lord came to be baptized by John in the Jordan, we said that he knew him, by those words where he says: *Do you come to me, to be baptized? I ought to be baptized by you* [^11].

But see, he knew the Lord, he knew the Son of God: from where do we prove that he already knew that he himself baptizes in the Holy Spirit?

Before he came to the river, when many ran to John to be baptized, he said to them: *I indeed baptize you in water: but he who comes after me is greater than I, the strap of whose sandal I am not worthy to loosen; he will baptize you in the Holy Spirit and fire* [^12]: this too he already knew. What, then, did he learn through the dove, lest he be afterward found a liar (which may God turn from us to suppose); except that there was going to be a certain property in Christ such that, although many ministers were going to baptize, whether righteous or unrighteous, the holiness of Baptism would not be attributed except to him over whom the dove descended, of whom it was said: ***This is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit***? Let Peter baptize, this is he who baptizes: let Paul baptize, this is he who baptizes; let Judas baptize, this is he who baptizes.

**8.** For if Baptism is holy according to the diversity of merits, then, because the merits are diverse, the baptisms will be diverse; and each one is thought to receive something the better, the better the one from whom he is seen to have received it. The saints themselves—understand, brothers—the good who belong to the dove, who belong to the lot of that city Jerusalem, the good themselves in the Church, of whom the Apostle says: *The Lord knows who are his* [^13]; are of diverse graces, they do not all have equal merits: some are holier than others, some are better than others. Why then, if one is baptized, for example, by that righteous holy man, and another by another of inferior merit before God, of inferior rank, of inferior continence, of inferior life, is what they received nevertheless one and equal and the same, except because ***this is he who baptizes***?

Just as, then, when a good man and a better man baptize, this one did not for that reason receive a good thing, and that one a better; but although the ministers were a good man and a better, what they received is one and equal, it is not better in the one, and inferior in the other: so too when a bad man baptizes, out of some either ignorance of the Church, or toleration (for the bad are either unknown, or tolerated; the chaff is tolerated, until at the last the threshing floor is winnowed), that which was given is one, and not unequal on account of unequal ministers; but one and equal, on account of: ***This is he who baptizes***.

**9.** Therefore, dearly beloved, let us see what they are unwilling to see; not that they do not see it, but that they grieve to see it: as though it were shut against them. To what were the disciples sent, in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, to baptize as ministers? To what were they sent? *Go*, he said, *baptize the nations* [^14]. You have heard, brothers, how that inheritance came: *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and the ends of the earth as your possession* [^15]. You have heard how the law went forth from Zion, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem [^16]: for there the disciples heard: *Go, baptize the nations in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit* [^17].

We were made attentive, when we heard: *Go, baptize the nations*. In whose name? *In the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit*. This is one God, because it is not, in the names of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit; but, *in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit*. Where you hear one name, there is one God: just as it was said of the seed of Abraham, and the apostle Paul expounds it: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed: he did not say: In your seeds, as in many; but as in one: And in your seed, which is Christ* [^18]. Just as, then, because there he does not say, in your seeds, the Apostle wished to teach you that Christ is one: so here too, when it was said, *in the name*, not, in the names; just as there, *in the seed*, not, in seeds; one God, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, is proved.

**10.** But: See, say the disciples to the Lord, we have heard in what name we should baptize, you have made us ministers, and you said to us: *Go, baptize in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit*: where shall we go?

Where? Did you not hear? To my inheritance. You ask: Where shall we go? To that which I bought with my blood.

Where, then? To the nations, he says.

I thought he said: Go, baptize the Africans in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.

Thanks be to God: the Lord has solved the question, the dove has taught. Thanks be to God: the Apostles were sent to the nations; if to the nations, to all tongues. This the Holy Spirit signified, divided in the tongues, united in the dove. The tongues are divided here, the dove couples here. Have the tongues of the nations agreed, and the one tongue of Africa disagreed? What more evident, my brothers? In the dove, unity; in the tongues of the nations, fellowship.

For at one time the tongues also disagreed through pride, and then out of one were made many tongues. For after the flood certain proud men, as though trying to fortify themselves against God, as if anything were lofty against God, or anything safe for pride, raised up a tower; as though, lest a flood, if one should come afterward, should destroy them. For they had heard and reckoned that all iniquity had been destroyed by the flood: they were unwilling to refrain from iniquity; they sought the height of a tower against a flood; they built a lofty tower. God saw their pride, and caused this error to be sent upon them, that they should not understand one another speaking; and through pride diverse tongues were made.

If pride made the diversities of tongues, the humility of Christ gathered the diversities of tongues. Now what that tower had separated, the Church gathers. Out of one tongue many were made; do not marvel, pride did this: out of many tongues one is made; do not marvel, love did this. For although the sounds of the tongues are diverse, in the heart one God is invoked, one peace is kept. From where, then, dearly beloved, ought the Holy Spirit to be shown, designating a certain unity, except through a dove, so that to the Church at peace it might be said: *My dove is one*? From where ought humility to be shown, except through a simple and groaning bird, not through a proud bird that exalts itself like the raven?

## Apart from the dove there is no baptism.

**11.** And perhaps they will say: Because, then, there is a dove, and one dove, apart from the one dove there can be no Baptism: therefore if the dove is with you, or you are the dove, when I come to you, give me what I do not have. You know that this is theirs: now it will appear to you that it is not from the voice of the dove, but from the cry of the raven.

For let your Charity attend a little, and fear the snares: rather, beware, and take the words of those who contradict as to be spat out, not swallowed down and given to the bowels. Do with them what the Lord did, when they offered him a bitter drink; he tasted, and spat it out [^19]: so you too, hear, and cast it away. For what do they say? Let us see.

See, he says, you are the dove, O Catholic Church, it was said to you: *My dove is one, she is the only one of her mother*: to you surely it was said.

Wait, do not question me: if it was said to me, prove it first; if it was said to me, I wish to hear it quickly.

He says: It was said to you.

I answer with the voice of the Catholic Church: To me.

But this, brothers, which sounded from my mouth alone, sounded, I think, also from your hearts, and we all said together, it was said to the Catholic Church: *My dove is one, she is the only one of her mother*.

Apart from the dove herself, he says: There is no Baptism; I was baptized apart from the dove herself; therefore I do not have Baptism: if I do not have Baptism, why do you not give it to me when I come to you?

**12.** And I question: for now let us set aside to whom it was said: *My dove is one, she is the only one of her mother*; we are still seeking: either it was said to me, or it was said to you; let us set aside to whom it was said. This, then, I ask: if the dove is simple, innocent, without gall, peaceable in kisses, not savage in claws; I ask whether to the members of this dove belong the greedy, plunderers, the deceitful, drunkards, the disgraceful: are they members of this dove?

Far be it, he says.

And indeed, brothers, who would say this? To say nothing else, if I name plunderers alone, they can be members of the hawk, not members of the dove: kites plunder, hawks plunder, ravens plunder; doves do not plunder, do not tear: therefore plunderers are not members of the dove.

Was there not even one plunderer among you? Why does the Baptism remain that a hawk gave, not a dove? Why do you not baptize among your own selves after plunderers and adulterers and drunkards, after the greedy among your own selves? Or are all these members of the dove? Thus you dishonor your dove, that you make for her vulture's members.

What then, brothers, what do we say? The bad and the good are in the Catholic Church: but there only the bad are. But perhaps I say this with a hostile mind: and let this afterward be inquired into. And there too they certainly say that there are good and bad: for if they say that they have only the good; let their own people believe them, and I subscribe. There are among us, let them say, none but the holy, the righteous, the chaste, the sober; no adulterers, no usurers, no defrauders, no perjurers, no wine-bibbers: let them say it; for I do not attend to their tongues, but I touch their hearts. But since they are known to us and to you and to their own, just as you too are known to your own in the Catholic Church and to them; let neither us reproach them, nor them flatter themselves.

We confess that in the Church there are both good and bad, but like the grain and the chaff. Sometimes the one baptized by the grain is chaff; and the one baptized by the chaff is grain. Otherwise, if the one baptized by the grain avails, and the one baptized by the chaff does not avail: it is false, ***This is he who baptizes***. But if it is true, ***This is he who baptizes***: then what is given by him avails; and, as a dove, he baptizes. For that bad man is not a dove, nor does he belong to the members of the dove: nor can it be said, in the Catholic Church, nor among them, if they say their Church is the dove, that he is the dove. What, then, do we understand, brothers? Since it is manifest, and known to all, and even if they are unwilling they are convicted: that there too, when the bad give it, there is no baptizing after them; and here, when the bad give it, there is no baptizing after them. The dove does not baptize after the raven: why does the raven wish to baptize after the dove?

**13.** Let your Charity attend: and why was something designated through the dove, so that when the Lord was baptized the dove came—that is, the Holy Spirit in the form of a dove—and remained upon him, since at the coming of the dove John recognized this, a certain proper power in the Lord to baptize? Because through this proper power, as I have said, the peace of the Church was made firm. And it can happen that someone has Baptism apart from the dove: but that Baptism should profit him apart from the dove, cannot be.

Let your Charity attend, and understand what I say: for by this circumvention too they often seduce those of our brothers who are lazy and cold. Let us be more simple and more fervent.

See, they say, did I receive it, or did I not receive it?

I answer: You received it.

If, then, I received it, there is nothing for you to give me; I am secure, even by your own testimony: for I say that I received it, and you confess that I received it; the tongue of both makes me secure: what, then, do you promise me? Why do you wish to make me a Catholic, when you are not going to give me anything more, and you confess that I have already received what you say you have? But when I say: Come to me, I say it because you do not have it, you who confess that I have it: why do you say: Come to me?

## Baptism without love profits nothing.

**14.** The dove teaches us. For she answers from the head of the Lord, and says: You have Baptism, but you do not have the love by which I groan.

What is this, he says: I have Baptism, I do not have love? I have the sacraments, and not love?

Do not cry out; show me how the one who divides unity has love.

I, he says, have Baptism.

You have it, but that Baptism without love profits you nothing; because without love you are nothing. For that Baptism, even in him who is nothing, is not nothing: that Baptism indeed is something, and a great something; on account of him of whom it was said: ***This is he who baptizes***. But lest you should think that what is great could profit you anything if you were not in unity, the dove descended over the one baptized, as though saying: If you have Baptism, be in the dove, lest what you have not profit you. Come, then, to the dove, we say: not that you may begin to have what you did not have, but that what you had may begin to profit you. For outside you had Baptism to your ruin: if you have it within, it begins to profit you to salvation.

**15.** For Baptism not only did not profit you, but it even did you harm.

Even holy things can do harm: for in the good, holy things are present unto salvation; in the bad, unto judgment. For certainly, brothers, we know what we receive, and surely what we receive is holy, and no one says it is not holy: and what does the Apostle say? *But he who eats and drinks unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself* [^20]. He does not say that the thing is bad: but that the bad man, by receiving badly, receives unto judgment the good thing he receives. Was the morsel that was handed to Judas by the Lord bad? Far be it. The physician would not give poison: the physician gave health; but by receiving unworthily, the one who received it without peace received it to his ruin.

So then it is also with the one who is baptized.

I have it, he says, for myself.

I confess, you have it: observe what you have; by the very fact that you have it, you will be condemned.

Why?

Because you have the thing of the dove apart from the dove. If you have the thing of the dove in the dove, you have it securely. Suppose you are a soldier: if you have your emperor's mark within, you serve securely; if you have it outside, that mark not only does not profit you for service, but you will even be punished as a deserter. Come, then, come, and do not say: I already have it, it already suffices me. Come; the dove calls you, by groaning she calls you.

My brothers, I say to you; call by groaning, not by quarreling: call by praying, call by inviting, call by fasting; from your love let them understand that you grieve for them. I do not doubt, my brothers, that if they see your grief, they will be confounded, and will revive.

Come, then, come: do not fear; fear if you do not come: rather, do not fear, but mourn. Come, you will rejoice if you come: you will indeed groan in the tribulations of the pilgrimage; but you will rejoice in hope. Come where the dove is, to whom it was said: *My dove is one, she is the only one of her mother*. Do you see one dove over the head of Christ, do you not see tongues in the whole world? The same Spirit through the dove, the same also through the tongues: if through the dove the same Spirit, and through the tongues the same Spirit, the Holy Spirit was given to the whole world, from which you cut yourself off, so that you cry out with the raven, not groan with the dove. Come, then.

**16.** But perhaps you are anxious, and say: Having been baptized outside, I fear lest I be guilty from that, because I received it outside. You have now begun to recognize what is to be groaned over: you say truly, that you are guilty; not because you received it, but because you received it outside. Hold, then, what you received, amend the fact that you received it outside: you received the thing of the dove, apart from the dove: there are two things you hear; You received it, and: You received it apart from the dove: that you received it, I approve; that you received it outside, I disapprove. Hold, then, what you received; it is not changed, but acknowledged: it is the mark of my King, I will not be sacrilegious; I correct the deserter, I do not alter the mark.

**17.** Do not boast of Baptism, for I say, it is the same; see, I say, it is the same; the whole Catholic Church says, it is the same: the dove notices, and acknowledges, and groans, because you have it outside; she sees there what she acknowledges, she sees also what she corrects. It is the same, come. Do you boast that it is the same, and are unwilling to come?

What, then, of the bad, who do not belong to the dove? The dove says to you: The bad too, among whom I groan, who do not belong to my members, and among whom I must groan—do they not have what you boast of having? Do not many drunkards have Baptism? Do not many of the greedy? Do not many idolaters, and what is worse, secretly? Did not the pagans go to idols, or used to go publicly? Now in secret Christians seek out fortune-tellers, consult astrologers. And these have Baptism, but the dove groans among the ravens. Why, then, do you rejoice that you have it? You have this, which a bad man too has. Have humility, love, peace: have the good that you do not yet have, that the good you have may profit you.

**18.** For what you have, Simon Magus too had: the Acts of the Apostles are witnesses, that canonical book to be recited every year in the Church. At the anniversary solemnity after the passion of the Lord you know that book is recited, where it is written how the Apostle was converted, and from a persecutor made a preacher: where also on the day of Pentecost the Holy Spirit was sent in divided tongues as of fire. There we read that many believed in Samaria through the preaching of Philip: but it is understood to be either one of the Apostles, or one of the deacons; because we read that seven deacons were ordained there, among whose names is also that of Philip. Through the preaching of Philip, then, the Samaritans believed; Samaria began to abound with the faithful: there was that Simon Magus; through his magic arts he had driven the people mad, so that they thought him the power of God: yet, moved by the signs that were done by Philip, he too believed; but how he believed, the things that followed afterward showed: Simon too was baptized.

The Apostles who were at Jerusalem heard this; Peter and John were sent to them, and found many baptized: and because none of them had yet received the Holy Spirit, as he then descended—to show the signification of the nations that were going to believe, that those upon whom the Holy Spirit descended should speak in tongues—they laid hands on them, praying for them, and they received the Holy Spirit. That Simon, who was not in the Church a dove, but a raven, because he sought the things that were his own, not the things that were Jesus Christ's; whence among Christians he had loved power rather than righteousness, saw that through the laying on of the Apostles' hands the Holy Spirit was given (not because they gave it, but because at their prayer it was given), and said to the Apostles: *What will you take from me in money, that through the laying on of my hands the Holy Spirit may also be given?* And Peter said to him: *Your money be with you unto perdition; since you thought the gift of God was to be purchased with money* [^21].

To whom does he say: *Your money be with you unto perdition?* Surely to one baptized. He already had Baptism: but he did not cling to the bowels of the dove. Hear that he did not cling; note the very words of the apostle Peter: for it follows: *You have no part nor lot in this faith; for I see you to be in the gall of bitterness* [^22]. The dove has no gall: Simon had it; therefore he was separated from the bowels of the dove. What did Baptism profit him? Do not, then, boast of Baptism, as though salvation from it alone sufficed you: do not be angry, lay aside the gall, come to the dove; here what outside not only did not profit you, but even did you harm, will profit you.

**19.** Nor say: I do not come, because I was baptized outside. See, begin to have love, begin to have fruit, let fruit be found in you, and the dove will send you inside. We find this in Scripture. The ark had been built of imperishable wood: the imperishable wood is the saints, the faithful belonging to Christ. For just as in the temple the living stones of which the temple is built were called the faithful men; so the imperishable wood is the men who persevere in faith. In that ark, then, was imperishable wood; for the ark is the Church: there the dove baptizes; for that ark was borne upon the water: the imperishable wood was baptized within. We find a certain wood baptized outside, all the trees that were in the world. Yet the water itself was the same, it was not another: all of it had come from heaven, and from the depths of the springs: it was the same water in which the imperishable wood that was in the ark was baptized, and in which the wood outside was baptized.

The dove was sent out, and at first found no rest for her feet: she returned to the ark; for all things were full of waters, and she preferred to return rather than to be rebaptized. But that raven was sent out before the water dried up: rebaptized, he was unwilling to return; he died in those waters. May God turn from us the death of that raven. For why did he not return, except that he was cut off by the waters? But the dove, finding no rest for her feet, when the water cried out to her from every side: Come, come, dip here; just as these heretics cry out: Come, come, here you have it: she, finding no rest for her feet, returned to the ark [^23].

And Noah sent her again, just as the ark sends you, that you may speak to them: and what did the dove do afterward? Because there was wood baptized outside, she brought back to the ark a branch of olive. That branch had both leaves and fruit: let there not be in you words alone, let there not be in you leaves alone; let there be fruit, and you return to the ark, not by yourself, the dove calls you back. Groan outside, that you may call them back inside.

**20.** For indeed, if this fruit of the olive be examined, you will find what it was. The fruit of the olive signifies love. From where do we prove this? Just as oil is pressed down by no liquid, but, bursting through all, springs up and rises above: so too love cannot be pressed into the depths; it must necessarily rise to the heights. Therefore the Apostle says of it: *I show you a yet more excellent way* [^24]. Because we said of oil that it rises above, lest perhaps the Apostle did not say of love: *I show you a more excellent way*, let us hear what follows: *If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become like sounding brass, or a clanging cymbal* [^25].

Go now: Grant it, and cry out: I am eloquent: go now, and cry out: I am learned. How eloquent? How learned? Have you spoken with the tongues of angels? And yet if you spoke with the tongues of angels, not having love, I would hear sounding brass and clanging cymbals. I seek some solid thing, I would find fruit among the leaves: let there not be words alone, let them have the olive, let them return to the ark.

**21.** But, you will say, I have the sacrament. You say truly: the sacrament is divine; you have Baptism, and I confess it. But what does the same Apostle say? *If I should know all the sacraments, and have prophecy and all faith, so as to move mountains*: lest perhaps you should also say this: I have believed, it suffices me. But what does James say? *The demons too believe, and tremble* [^26]. Faith is great, but it profits nothing if it does not have love. The demons too confessed Christ. Therefore, believing but not loving, they said: *What have we to do with you?* [^27] They had faith, they did not have love: therefore they were demons. Do not boast of faith; you are still to be compared with the demons. Do not say to Christ: What have I to do with you? For the unity of Christ speaks to you. Come, recognize peace, return to the bowels of the dove. You were baptized outside; have fruit, and you return to the ark.

**22.** And you:

Why do you seek us, if we are bad?

That you may be good. For this reason we seek you, because you are bad: for if you were not bad, we would have found you, we would not be seeking you. He who is good has already been found: he who is bad is still being sought. For this reason we seek you; return to the ark.

But I already have Baptism.

*If I should know all the sacraments, and have prophecy and all faith, so as to move mountains, but have not love, I am nothing* [^28]. Let me see fruit there, let me see the olive there, and you are called back to the ark.

## You are not in the dove if you are for Donatus.

**23.** But what do you say?

See, we suffer many evils.

These—if you suffered them for Christ, not for your own honors.

Hear what follows: for they sometimes boast that they give many alms, that they give to the poor; that they suffer troubles: but for Donatus, not for Christ.

See for what you suffer: for if you suffer for Donatus, you suffer for a proud man; you are not in the dove, if you suffer for Donatus. He was not the friend of the bridegroom: for if he were the friend of the bridegroom, he would seek the glory of the bridegroom, not his own. See the friend of the bridegroom saying: *This is he who baptizes*. He for whom you suffer was not the friend of the bridegroom. You do not have the wedding garment; and if you have come to the banquet, you are to be cast out [^29]; rather, because you have been cast out, therefore you are wretched: return at some point, and do not boast. Hear what the Apostle says: *If I distribute all my goods to the poor, and hand over my body to be burned, but have not love* [^30]. See what you do not have. *If I hand over*, he says, *my body to be burned*; and surely for the name of Christ: but because there are many who do that boastfully, not with love, therefore: *If I hand over my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing* [^31]. With love those martyrs acted who suffered in the time of persecution; with love they acted: but these act out of swelling and out of pride; for, since a persecutor is lacking, they hurl themselves down. Come, then, that you may have love.

But we have martyrs.

What martyrs? They are not doves, and so they tried to fly, and fell from the rock.

If we are the dove, let us groan, let us endure, let us hope.

**24.** You see, then, my brothers, that all the divine pages cry out against them, all prophecy, the whole Gospel, all the apostolic letters, every groan of the dove; and they do not yet awaken, they do not yet rouse themselves. But if we are the dove, let us groan, let us endure, let us hope; the mercy of God will be present, that the fire of the Holy Spirit may blaze up in your simplicity; and they will come. We must not despair; pray, preach, love; the Lord is fully able. They have already begun to recognize their own face: many have recognized it, many have blushed; Christ will be present, that the rest too may recognize it. And surely, my brothers, even if the chaff alone remain there, let all the grains be gathered: whatever has borne fruit there, let it return to the ark through the dove.

**25.** Now, failing everywhere, what do they set before us, finding nothing to say?

They have taken our country houses, they have taken our estates. They produce the testaments of men. See where Gaius Seius donated an estate to the Church over which Faustinus presided.

Of what Church was Faustinus bishop? What is the Church?

the Church, he said, over which Faustinus presided:

but Faustinus did not preside over the Church; he presided over a faction. The dove, however, is the Church. Why do you shout? We have not devoured estates—let the dove have them; let it be asked which is the dove, and let her have them. For you know, my brothers, that these estates do not belong to Augustine; and if you do not know it, and suppose that I rejoice in the possession of estates, God knows—he himself knows what I feel about those estates, or what I endure on their account; he knows my groans, if he has deigned to grant me any share in the dove.

Here, then, are the estates: by what right do you defend the estates? By divine right or human?

Let them answer: We have divine right in the Scriptures, human right in the laws of kings.

By what does anyone possess what he possesses? Is it not by human right? For by divine right, *the earth is the Lord's, and the fullness thereof*[^32]. God made the poor and the rich from one clay, and one earth supports both poor and rich. Yet by human right one says: This estate is mine, this house is mine, this slave is mine. By human right, then, by the right of emperors. Why? Because it is through the emperors and kings of this age that God distributed these very human rights to the human race. Do you wish that we read the laws of the emperors and deal with the estates according to them? If you wish to possess by human right, let us recite the laws of the emperors; let us see whether they were willing that anything be possessed by heretics.

But what is the emperor to me?

It is by his right that you possess the land. Either do away with the rights of emperors, and then who dares to say: That estate is mine, or that slave is mine, or this house is mine? But if, in order that these things may be held by human beings, they have received the rights of kings, do you wish that we recite the laws, so that you may rejoice that you have even a single garden, and attribute it to nothing but the gentleness of the dove, because you are permitted to remain even there? For laws are read out, plain laws, in which the emperors decreed that those who outside the communion of the Catholic Church usurp for themselves the name of Christian, and refuse to worship in peace the author of peace, should dare to possess nothing in the name of the Church.

**26.** But what have we to do with the emperor?

But I have already said: it is a matter of human right. And yet the Apostle wished kings to be served, wished kings to be honored, and said: *Reverence the king*[^33]. Do not say: What have I to do with the king? What, then, have you to do with your possession? It is through the rights of kings that possessions are possessed. You have said: What have I to do with the king? Then do not call your possessions your own; for you have renounced the very human rights by which possessions are possessed.

But I am dealing with divine right, he says.

Then let us recite the Gospel; let us see how far the Catholic Church belongs to Christ, upon whom the dove came, which taught: ***This is he who baptizes***[^34]. How, then, can he possess by divine right who says: I baptize, when the dove says, ***This is he who baptizes***[^35], when Scripture says, *My dove is one, she is the only one of her mother*[^36]? Why have you torn the dove apart? Rather, you have torn your own bowels apart: for you tear yourselves apart, while the dove remains whole.

Therefore, my brothers, if they have nowhere anything to say, I will say what they should do: let them come to the Catholic Church, and with us they will have not only the earth, but also him who made heaven and earth.
