# Tractate 72

## *Greater works he will do; because I go to the Father* (Jn 14:12-14).

## To do greater works through one who believes in him than apart from him is not a falling away, but a condescension.

**1.** What it means, and how it is to be taken, that the Lord says: *He who believes in me, the works that I do he too will do,* is not easy to comprehend: and since this is most difficult to understand, he added another more difficult: *And greater than these he will do.* What is this? We were not finding one who would do the works that Christ did; are we going to find one who will even do greater? But we had said in the former discourse that it was greater, by the passing of his shadow, that the disciples did, than by the touch of his fringe, that the Lord himself did, to heal the sick; and that more believed through the Apostles than through the Lord himself preaching by his own mouth: so that these seemed to be the works to be understood as greater: not that the disciple was greater than the master, or the servant than the Lord, or the adopted than the Only-begotten, or man than God; but that through them he himself deigned to do the same greater works, who says to them in another place: *Without me you can do nothing*[^1]. For he himself—to pass over other things, which are innumerable—made them without them, made this world without them; and because he deigned even himself to become man, he made himself too without them. But what do they without him, except sin?

Finally, here too what could move us concerning this matter, he soon took away: for when he had said: *He who believes in me, the works that I do he too will do, and greater than these he will do*; he at once went on to add: *Because I go to the Father, and whatever you ask in my name, this I will do*. He who had said *he will do,* afterward says *I will do*; as if he said: Let this not seem impossible to you: for he who believes in me will not be able to be greater than I, but I am going to do, even then, greater things than now; greater through him who believes in me, than apart from him through me: yet I myself apart from him, I myself through him: but when apart from him, he will not do it himself; when through him, although not through himself, he too will do it. But to do greater works through him than apart from him is not a falling away, but a condescension. For what shall the servants render to the Lord, for all that he has rendered to them? Since among other goods he deigned to bestow this too on them, that he should do greater works through them than apart from them. Did not that sad rich man depart from his mouth, when he sought counsel for eternal life? He heard, he cast it away: and yet afterward what one man did not do that he heard from him, many did, when the good master spoke through the disciples; contemptible to the one whom he admonished, rich, through himself, lovable to those whom out of rich he made poor through the poor. Behold, he did greater things preached by those who believed, than spoken to those who heard.

## To believe in Christ is itself the work of Christ.

**2.** But this still moves us, that he did these greater things through the Apostles: yet, signifying not them alone, he did not say: The works that I do you too will do, and greater than these you will do: but, wishing all who belong to his household to be understood: *He who believes in me,* he says, *the works that I do he too will do, and greater than these he will do.* If, then, he who believes will do them, surely he who will not do them does not believe: just as: *He who loves me keeps my commandments*[^2]; whence surely he who does not keep them does not love. Likewise in another place: *He who hears,* he says, *these words of mine and does them, I will liken him to a wise man who builds his house upon the rock*[^3]: he, then, who is not like this wise man, without doubt either hears these words and does not do them, or does not hear them at all. *He who believes in me,* he says, *even though he die, will live*[^4]: he, then, who will not live, surely does not believe. Such too is this: *He who believes in me will do*: surely he who will not do does not believe.

What is this, brothers? Is he not to be counted among those who believe in Christ, who has not done greater works than Christ? It is hard, it is absurd, it cannot be borne: it is not tolerated unless it is understood. Let us hear the Apostle, then: *To one who believes,* he says, *in him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is reckoned unto righteousness*[^5]. In this work let us do the works of Christ, since to believe in Christ is itself the work of Christ. This he works in us, surely not without us. Hear, then, now, and understand: *He who believes in me, the works that I do he too will do*: first I do, then he too will do; because I do that he may do. What works, but that out of ungodly he be made righteous?

**3.** *And greater than these he will do.* Than which, I beseech you? Does he do greater than all the works of Christ, who works out his own salvation with fear and trembling[^6]? Which surely Christ works in him, but not without him. I would say outright that this is greater than heaven and earth, and whatever is seen in heaven and on earth. For heaven and earth will pass away; but the salvation and justification of the predestined, that is, of those whom he foreknows, will remain. In the former are only the works of God, but in the latter is also the image of God.

But in the heavens too the Thrones, Dominions, Principalities, Powers, Archangels, Angels are works of Christ; does he, then, do greater works than these, who, Christ working in him, cooperates toward his own eternal salvation and justification? Here I do not dare to rush to a sentence: let him understand who can, let him judge who can, whether it is greater to create the righteous than to justify the ungodly. Certainly, if each is of equal power, the latter is of greater mercy. *For this is the great sacrament of godliness, which was manifested in the flesh, justified in the Spirit, appeared to the angels, preached among the nations, believed in the world, taken up in glory*[^7]. But no necessity compels us to understand all the works of Christ where he says: *Greater than these he will do.* For *these* perhaps he meant which he was doing in that hour: but then he was doing the words of faith, and of these works he had spoken before, saying: *The words that I speak to you I do not speak of myself: but the Father, abiding in me, he does the works.* Then, therefore, his words were his works. And surely it is less to preach the words of righteousness, which he did apart from us, than to justify the ungodly, which he does in us in such a way that we too do it.

It remains to inquire how it is to be taken: *Whatever you ask in my name, this I will do.* For on account of many things which his faithful ask, and do not receive, no small question arises from this: but since this discourse must now be closed, let at least a little delay be granted for considering and treating it.
