# Tractate 85

## *I will no longer call you servants*[^1].

## It is a great condescension that the Lord deigns to call those whom he knows to be his servants his friends. One can therefore be at once a servant and a friend, who is a good servant.

**1.** When the Lord Jesus had commended the love that he showed us by dying for us, and had said, *Greater love than this no one has, than that a man lay down his life for his friends,* he says: *You are my friends, if you do what I command you*[^2]. Great condescension! Since a servant cannot be good unless he does the commands of his master, from this very thing by which good servants can be proved, he wished them to be understood as his friends. But, as I said, this is condescension, that the master deigns to call those whom he knows to be his servants his friends.

For that you may know that it belongs to the office of servants to do the commands of their master, in another place he certainly rebukes servants, saying: *But why do you call me, Lord, Lord, and do not do what I say*[^3]? When, therefore, you say, Lord, he says, show by doing what is bidden what it is you say. Will he not say to the obedient servant: *Well done, good servant; because you have been faithful over a few things, I will set you over many; enter into the joy of your Lord*[^4]? One can therefore be at once a servant and a friend, who is a good servant.

**2.** But let us attend to what follows. *I will no longer call you servants; because the servant does not know what his master does*[^5]. How, then, are we to understand that a good servant is both a servant and a friend, when he says, *I will no longer call you servants, because the servant does not know what his master does*? He so establishes the name of friend that he takes away that of servant; not so that both remain in one, but so that the one succeeds the other as it withdraws.

What is this? Will we then, when we have done the Lord's commands, not be servants? Will we then not be servants, when we have been good servants? And who can contradict the truth which says, *I will no longer call you servants*? And why he said this, he teaches: *Because the servant,* he says, *does not know what his master does.* Does not a master entrust even his secrets to a good and proven servant? What, then, does he mean by saying, *The servant does not know what his master does*? Well, granted, *he does not know what he does*; but does he not know even what he commands? For if he does not know this either, how does he serve? Or how is he a servant who does not serve? And yet the Lord says: *You are my friends, if you do what I command you. I will no longer call you servants.* O wonderful thing! Since we cannot serve unless we do the Lord's commands, how is it that by doing the commands we shall not be servants? If I shall not be a servant by doing the commands, and unless I do the commands I cannot serve, then by serving I shall not be a servant.

## As there are two fears, so there are two servitudes.

**3.** Let us understand, brothers, let us understand; and may the Lord bring it about in us that we understand, and may he also bring it about that we do what we have understood. But if we know this, then assuredly we know what the Lord does, because none but the Lord makes us such, and through this we belong to his friendship.

For as there are two fears, which make two kinds of those who fear, so there are two servitudes, which make two kinds of servants. There is a fear which perfect love casts out, and there is another, chaste fear remaining forever and ever. That fear which is not in love the Apostle had in view when he said: *For you have not received the spirit of servitude again in fear*[^6]. But that chaste fear he had in view when he said: *Do not be high-minded, but fear*[^7]. In that fear which love casts out, there is also a servitude to be cast out together with the fear itself; for the Apostle joined both, that is, servitude and fear, by saying: *For you have not received the spirit of servitude again in fear.* It was the servant belonging to this servitude that the Lord too had in view when he said: *I will no longer call you servants; because the servant does not know what his master does*[^8]. Not, surely, that servant belonging to chaste fear, to whom it is said, *Well done, good servant, enter into the joy of your Lord*[^9]; but that servant belonging to the fear to be cast out by love, of whom he says elsewhere: *The servant does not abide in the house forever; but the son abides forever*[^10]. Since, therefore, he has given us power to become sons of God, let us be not servants but sons: so that in a certain wonderful and ineffable, yet true, manner we may be able to be servants who are not servants; servants, namely, by chaste fear, to which belongs the servant entering into the joy of his Lord; but not servants by the fear to be cast out, to which belongs the servant who does not abide in the house forever.

But that we may be such servants who are not servants, let us know that it is the Lord who does it. But that servant does not know this, who does not know what his Lord does; and when he does anything good, he is so lifted up as though he himself did it, not his Lord; and he glories in himself, not in the Lord, while he has deceived himself, because he glories as though he had not received. But let us, dearly beloved, that we may be able to be the Lord's friends, know what our Lord does. For it is he himself who makes us not only men but also righteous, and not we ourselves. And that we may know this, who but he himself brings it about? *For we have not received the spirit of this world, but the Spirit who is from God, that we may know the things that have been given us by God*[^11]. From him whatever is good is given. Therefore, because this too is good, it is surely given by him that it may be known by whom every good is given; so that altogether, concerning all goods, *he who glories may glory in the Lord*[^12]. But what follows — *But I have called you friends; because all things whatsoever I have heard from my Father I have made known to you*[^13] — is so profound that it is in no way to be confined within this discourse, but to be deferred to another.
