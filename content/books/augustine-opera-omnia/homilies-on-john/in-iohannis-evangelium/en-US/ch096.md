# Tractate 96

## *When that Spirit of truth has come, he will teach you all truth*[^1].

## In this way the Holy Spirit will teach you all truth, when he will pour out love in your hearts more and more.

**1.** In this chapter of the holy Gospel, where the Lord says to his disciples, *I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now*[^2], there first arises this to be inquired: how he said above, *All things that I heard from my Father I have made known to you*[^3]; and here says, *I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now.* But how he said the former — that he had, as it were, done what he had not yet done, just as the prophet testifies that God did the things that are to come, saying, *He who made the things that are to be*[^4] — we have already explained, as we were able, when we treated those very words.

Now, then, you perhaps wish to know what these things are which the Apostles could not then bear. But who of us would dare to say that he is already capable of those things which they were not able to grasp? And through this, neither are those things to be expected from me to be told, which I perhaps would not grasp if they were told me by another; nor could you bear them, even if I were so great that you should hear from me these things which are higher than you. And it can indeed be that there are among you some already fit to grasp those things which others are not yet able to grasp; and if not all of those things concerning which that divine master said, *I still have many things to say to you,* yet perhaps some of them: but to wish to presume and say what those things are which he himself did not say is rash. For the Apostles were not yet fit even to die for Christ, to whom he said, *You cannot follow me now*[^5]; whence the first of them, Peter, who had already presumed that he could do this, experienced something other than he thought: and yet afterward both men and women, boys and girls, young men and virgins, the old together with the young, were crowned in countless numbers with martyrdom; and the sheep were found able to do what then, when the Lord was saying these things, the shepherds were not yet able to bear. Ought it, then, to have been said to those sheep, in that crisis of temptation in which it was necessary to contend even unto death for the truth, and to shed their blood for the name or doctrine of Christ — ought it, I say, to have been said to them: Which of you would dare to think himself fit for martyrdom, for which Peter was not yet fit, when the Lord himself instructed him mouth to mouth? Thus, then, someone might say that it ought not to be said to Christian peoples, who desire to hear what those things are concerning which the Lord then said, *I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now*: If the Apostles could not yet, much less can you: because perhaps in this way many can hear what then Peter could not yet, just as many can be crowned with martyrdom, which then Peter could not yet — especially now that the Holy Spirit has been sent, who was not then yet sent, of whom he at once added, saying: *But when that Spirit of truth has come, he will teach you all truth*[^6]; thus certainly showing that they could not bear the things he had to say for this reason, because the Holy Spirit had not yet come to them.

**2.** Behold, let us grant that it is so, that many can now bear, the Holy Spirit having been sent, those things which then, he being not yet sent, the disciples could not bear: do we therefore know what those things are which he was unwilling to say, which we would then know if we read or heard them spoken by him? For it is one thing to know whether they can be borne by us or by you; another to know what they are, whether they can be borne or not. Since he himself was silent about them, who of us would say: These or those are they? Or if he dared to say, by what does he prove it? For who is so vain or rash that, when he has said even true things to whom he wishes and what he wishes, he affirms without any divine testimony that these are the things which the Lord then was unwilling to say? Who of us would do this, and not incur the greatest fault of rashness, in a matter in which neither prophetic nor apostolic authority excels? For surely, if we had read any of those things in the Books confirmed by canonical authority, which were written after the Lord's ascension, it would have been too little to have read this, unless it were also read there that this is among the things which the Lord then was unwilling to say to the disciples, because they could not bear them. As if, for example, I should say that what we read at the head of this Gospel — *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; this was in the beginning with God*[^7], and the other things that follow — since they were written afterward, and it is not narrated that the Lord Jesus said them when he was here in the flesh, but one of his Apostles wrote them down, his Spirit revealing it to him, is among the things which the Lord then was unwilling to say, because the disciples could not bear them: who would hear me saying these things so rashly? But if, where we read this, we also read this, who would not believe so great an apostle?

**3.** But this too seems to me most absurdly said, that the disciples could not then bear those things concerning invisible and most lofty matters which we find in the apostolic Writings, which were written afterward, and which it is not narrated that the Lord said when he was visibly with them. For why could they not then bear those things which now, in their Books, who does not read, who does not bear, even if he does not understand? Some things, indeed, unbelieving men both do not understand when they read or hear in the holy Scriptures, and, read or heard, cannot bear: as the pagans, that the world was made through him who was crucified; as the Jews, that he is the Son of God, who dissolved the sabbath in the way they themselves keep it; as the Sabellians, that the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are a Trinity; as the Arians, that the Son is equal to the Father, and the Holy Spirit to the Father and the Son; as the Photinians, that Christ is not a man only like us, but also God equal to God the Father; as the Manicheans, that Christ Jesus, through whom we are to be set free, deigned to be born in the flesh and of the flesh: and all the rest of the men of perverse and diverse sects certainly cannot bear whatever is found in the holy Scriptures and in the catholic faith that is brought forth against their errors; just as we cannot bear their sacrilegious vanities and lying madnesses. For what is it to be unable to bear, but not to have with an even mind? But all the things which after the Lord's ascension were written down with canonical truth and authority — what believer, or even catechumen, before he, baptized, receives the Holy Spirit, does not read and hear with an even mind, even if he does not yet understand as he ought? How, then, could the disciples not bear any of those things which were written after the Lord's ascension, even the Holy Spirit not yet being sent to them, when now the catechumens bear all things, the Holy Spirit not yet received? For even if the sacraments of the faithful are not disclosed to them, it is not on that account because they cannot bear them; but that they may be desired by them all the more ardently, the more honorably they are concealed from them.

## Advance in the love which the Holy Spirit pours out in your hearts.

**4.** Wherefore, dearly beloved, do not expect to hear from us what the Lord then was unwilling to say to the disciples, because they could not yet bear it: but rather advance in the love which is poured out in your hearts through the Holy Spirit who is given to you; that, fervent in spirit and loving spiritual things, you may be able to know the spiritual light and the spiritual voice, which fleshly men cannot bear — not by any sign appearing to the bodily eyes, nor by any sound resounding in the bodily ears, but by an inner gaze and hearing. For what is utterly unknown is not loved. But when what is known, however little, is loved, it comes about by that very love that it is known better and more fully. If, then, you advance in the love which the Holy Spirit pours out in your hearts, *he will teach you all truth*; or, as other copies have it, *he will lead you into all truth*: whence it is said, *Lead me, O Lord, in your way, and I will walk in your truth*[^8]. So it will come about that you learn those things which the Lord was then unwilling to say, not from outward teachers, but that you may all be taught by God; so that those very things which you have learned and believed, through readings and sermons applied from outside, concerning the nature of God — not corporeal, nor enclosed in any place, nor stretched out as it were by mass through infinite spaces of place, but everywhere whole and perfect and infinite, without the brilliance of colors, without the figures of outlines, without the marks of letters, without the sequence of syllables — you may be able to behold with the mind itself.

Behold, I have said something which is perhaps among those things, and yet you have received it; and not only have you been able to bear it, but you have even gladly heard it. But that inner master, who, while he still spoke outwardly to the disciples, said, *I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now,* if he wished to say inwardly to us that which I have said concerning the incorporeal nature of God, as he says it to the holy angels, who always see the face of the Father, we could not yet bear it. Accordingly, what he says, *He will teach you all truth,* or, *He will lead you into all truth,* I do not think can be fulfilled in anyone's mind in this life (for who, living in this body which is corrupted and weighs down the soul, could know all truth, when the Apostle says, *We know in part*[^9]?); but because it is brought about through the Holy Spirit, of whom we have now received the pledge, that we may come also to that very fullness: of which the same apostle says, *But then face to face*; and, *Now I know in part, but then I shall know as I also have been known*[^10]: not that in this life he knows the whole, which the Lord has promised will be ours, even unto that perfection, through the love of the Spirit, saying, *He will teach you all truth*; or, *He will lead you into all truth.*

**5.** Since these things are so, dearly beloved, I admonish you in the love of Christ, that you beware of impure seducers and the sects of obscene baseness, of whom the Apostle says: *For the things that are done by them in secret, it is shameful even to speak of*[^11]: lest, when they begin to teach horrible filths, which human ears, of whatever sort they be, cannot bear, they say that these are the very things which the Lord meant, *I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now*; and assert that it comes about through the Holy Spirit that those unclean and nefarious things can be borne. There are some evils which any human modesty cannot bear, and there are some goods which the small human sense cannot bear: these are done in unchaste bodies, those are removed from all bodies; this is committed by impure flesh, that is scarcely discerned by a pure mind. *Be renewed, then, in the spirit of your mind*[^12], *and understand what is the will of God, what is good and well-pleasing and perfect*[^13]: *that, rooted and grounded in love, you may be able to comprehend with all the saints what is the length, the breadth, the height, and the depth; to know also the love of Christ that surpasses knowledge, that you may be filled unto all the fullness of God*[^14]. For in this way the Holy Spirit will teach you all truth, when he will pour out love in your hearts more and more.
