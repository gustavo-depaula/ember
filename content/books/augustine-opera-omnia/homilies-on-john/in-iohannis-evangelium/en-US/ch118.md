# Tractate 118

## *They took his garments and made four parts, and the tunic* (Jn 19:23-24).

## The Lord's fourfold garment figured his fourfold Church, spread, that is, throughout the whole world, which consists of four parts. But that tunic, allotted by lot, signifies the unity of all the parts, which is held together by the bond of love.

**1.** Let us treat, as far as it helps, in this homily the things that were done beside the Lord's cross when he had now been crucified. *The soldiers, then, when they had crucified him, took his garments and made four parts, a part for each soldier, and also the tunic. Now the tunic was without seam, woven from the top in one piece; so they said to one another, Let us not tear it, but cast lots for it, whose it shall be. This was to fulfill the Scripture which says, They divided my garments among them, and for my clothing they cast lots*[^1]. What the Jews willed was done: not they themselves, but the soldiers who obeyed Pilate, with Pilate himself judging, crucified Jesus; and yet if we consider their wills, their plots, their effort, their betrayal, and finally their clamors that extorted it, it was surely the Jews rather who crucified Jesus.

**2.** But of the dividing and the casting of lots for his garments we must not speak in passing. For although all four evangelists mention this matter, the others nonetheless more briefly than John: they obscurely, but he most plainly. For Matthew says: *And after they had crucified him, they divided his garments, casting lots*[^2]. Mark: *And crucifying him, they divided his garments, casting lots for them, who should take what*[^3]. Luke: *And dividing his garments, they cast lots*[^4]. But John said also how many parts they made of his garments—namely, four, so that they might take them singly. From which it appears that there were four soldiers who obeyed the governor in crucifying him. For he plainly says: *The soldiers, then, when they had crucified him, took his garments and made four parts, a part for each soldier, and also the tunic*; "took" is to be supplied, so that this is the sense: They took his garments and made four parts, a part for each soldier; they took also the tunic. And he spoke in such a way that we see no lot was cast over the rest of the garments, but over the tunic, which they took along with the rest but did not divide in the same way. For concerning this he goes on, explaining: *Now the tunic was without seam, woven from the top in one piece.* And why they cast lots over it he relates, saying: *They said, therefore, to one another, Let us not tear it, but cast lots for it, whose it shall be.* So it appears that in the other garments they had equal parts, so that it was not necessary to cast lots; but in that one they could not have had individual parts unless it were torn, so that they would take its pieces uselessly; and that they might not do this, they preferred that it should come to one by casting lots. With this evangelist's narrative the prophetic testimony also agrees, which he himself at once subjoins: *That the Scripture,* he says, *might be fulfilled which says, They divided my garments among them, and over my clothing they cast lots*[^5]. For he does not say, they allotted, but, *they divided*; nor does he say, casting lots they divided; but, naming no lot at all over the rest of the garments, he afterward said, *and over my clothing they cast lots*, on account of that one remaining tunic. Of this matter I will say what he himself shall grant, when I have first repelled the slander that can arise, as though the evangelists disagreed among themselves, showing that the words of none of the others are contrary to the narrative of John.

**3.** For Matthew, by saying *They divided his garments, casting lots*[^6], wished it to be understood that the tunic too belonged to the whole division of the garments, over which they cast lots; because, in dividing all the garments, among which it too was included, they cast lots over it. Such also is what Luke says: *Dividing his garments, they cast lots*[^7]; for in dividing they came to the tunic, over which the casting of lots took place, so that among them the whole division of his garments was completed. And what difference does it make whether it is said, *Dividing they cast lots*, as Luke says, or, *They divided, casting lots*, as Matthew says—except that Luke, by saying *lots*, used the plural for the singular, an idiom not unusual in the holy Scriptures—although some manuscripts are found to have *lot*, not *lots*? Mark alone, then, seems to have raised some question; for by saying *Casting lots for them, who should take what*[^8], he seems to speak as though the lot was cast over all the garments, not over the tunic alone. But here too brevity makes obscurity; for it was said, *Casting lots for them*, as if it were said, Casting lots while they were being divided; which is just what happened. For the division of all his garments would not have been complete unless it had been made clear by lot who was also to take that tunic, so that the dispute of those dividing might thus be ended, or rather not arise at all. So that what he says—*who should take what*—since this is assigned to the lot, is not to be referred to all the garments that were divided; for the lot was cast as to who should take that tunic; and since he omitted to relate what kind it was, and how, when equal parts had been made, it alone remained, which, lest it be torn apart, should come to the lot, therefore it was put thus: *who should take what*—that is, who should take it: as if the whole were said thus: They divided his garments, casting lots over them, as to who should take the tunic that remained over and above the equal parts.

## One for all, because there is unity in all.

**4.** Someone may perhaps ask what is signified by the dividing of the garments into so many parts, and the casting of lots over that tunic. The fourfold garment of the Lord Jesus Christ figured his fourfold Church, spread, that is, throughout the whole world, which consists of four parts, and distributed to all those same parts equally, that is, in concord. On account of which he says elsewhere that he will send his angels to gather his elect from the four winds; which is what, except from the four parts of the world—east, west, north, and south?

But that tunic, allotted by lot, signifies the unity of all the parts, which is held together by the bond of love. And when the Apostle was about to speak of love, he says: *I show you a still more excellent way*[^9]; and in another place he says: *To know the love of Christ which surpasses knowledge*[^10]; and again elsewhere: *But above all these things, love, which is the bond of perfection*[^11]. If, then, love both has a more excellent way, and surpasses knowledge, and is above all the precepts, with good reason the garment by which it is signified is said to be woven from the top. And it is without seam, that it may never be unstitched; and it comes to one, because it gathers all into one. Just as among the apostles, when their very number was twelve, that is, fourfold by threes, and all were questioned, Peter alone answered: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*[^12]; and to him it is said: *I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven*[^13], as though he alone received the power of binding and loosing: since he said that one thing for all, and received this with all, as bearing the person of unity itself; therefore one for all, because there is unity in all. Hence here too, when he had said *woven from the top*, he added, *throughout*. And if we refer this to what it signifies, no one has no share in it who is found to belong to the whole; from which whole the Church is called catholic, as the Greek language indicates.

But in the lot, what is commended except the grace of God? For thus it came to all in one, since the lot pleased all, because the grace of God too in unity comes to all; and when the lot is cast, it yields not to anyone's person or merits, but to the hidden judgment of God.

**5.** Nor should anyone say that these things signified nothing good because they were done through evil men—namely, not through those who followed Christ, but through those who persecuted him. For what shall we say of the cross itself, which was likewise made and fastened to Christ by his enemies and the impious?

And yet it is rightly understood that by it is signified what the Apostle says: *What is the breadth, and length, and height, and depth*[^14]. For it is broad in the transverse wood, on which the hands of the one hanging are stretched out; and it signifies good works in the breadth of love: it is long from the transverse wood down to the ground, where the back and feet are fastened; and it signifies perseverance through the length of time to the end: it is high in the top, by which the transverse wood is surmounted upward; and it signifies the supreme goal, to which all works are referred, since all that is done well and perseveringly in breadth and length is to be done for the sake of the height of the divine rewards: it is deep in the part by which it is fixed in the earth; for there it is also hidden and cannot be seen, but from there all its visible and prominent parts rise up; just as all our good things proceed from the depth of the grace of God, which can be neither comprehended nor judged.

But even if the cross of Christ signified only what the Apostle says—*But those who belong to Jesus Christ have crucified their flesh with its passions and desires*[^15]—how great a good is it? Yet the good spirit does this only by lusting against the flesh, while it was the enemy—that is, the evil spirit—who made that cross of Christ. Finally, what is that which all know—the sign of Christ—except the cross of Christ? And unless that sign is applied, whether to the foreheads of believers, or to the very water from which they are reborn, or to the oil with which they are anointed in chrism, or to the sacrifice by which they are nourished, none of these is duly accomplished. How, then, is nothing good signified by what evil men do, when through the cross of Christ, which evil men made, every good is signed upon us in the celebration of his sacraments?

But so much for these things; what follows we shall see, God helping us, by discussing it another time.
