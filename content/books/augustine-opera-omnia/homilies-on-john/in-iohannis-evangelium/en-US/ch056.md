# Tractate 56

## *He began to wash the feet of the disciples; and he came to Simon Peter* (Jn 13:6-10).

## Daily he washes our feet, he who intercedes for us, and daily we have need to wash our feet, that is, to direct the ways of our spiritual steps.

**1.** When the Lord was washing the feet of the disciples: *He came to Simon Peter; and Peter says to him: Lord, do you wash my feet*[^1]? For who would not be terrified to have his feet washed by the Son of God? And so, although it would have been great boldness for a servant to contradict the Lord, a man to contradict God: yet Peter chose to do this rather than to endure that his feet should be washed by the Lord and God. Nor ought we to think that Peter shrank from this and refused it among the rest, when others before him had willingly or with equanimity allowed it to be done to them. For these words of the Gospel are more easily taken thus, since, when it had been said: *He began to wash the feet of the disciples, and to wipe them with the linen cloth with which he was girded*; then it was added: *He came, therefore, to Simon Peter,* as though, having now washed some, he had come after them to the first. For who does not know that the most blessed Peter is the first of the Apostles?

But it is not to be understood that he came to him after some others; but that he began with him. When, therefore, he began to wash the feet of the disciples, he came to the one with whom he began, that is, to Peter: and then Peter, what any of them too would have shuddered at, shuddered, and said: *Lord, do you wash my feet?* What is *you*? what is *my*? These are to be pondered rather than spoken; lest perhaps what the soul somewhat worthily conceives from these words, the tongue should not unfold.

## Christ at the feet of Peter.

**2.** But *Jesus answered and said to him: What I am doing you do not know now, but you will know hereafter*[^2]. Yet he, terrified by the loftiness of the Lord's deed, does not permit to be done what he did not know why it was done; but he is still unwilling to see Christ humble even to his feet, he cannot bear it. *You will not wash my feet,* he says, *forever*[^3]. What is *forever*? I will never bear this, I will never endure it, I will never allow it: for that is not done forever which is never done.

Then the Savior, terrifying the sick man who resisted with the peril of his own salvation: *If I do not wash you,* he says, *you will have no part with me*[^4]. It was said thus, *If I do not wash you,* when it was a matter of the feet alone; as it is usual to say, You are treading on me, when only the sole is trodden on. But he, disturbed by love and fear, and more terrified that Christ should be denied him than that he should be humbled even to his feet: *Lord,* he says, *not only my feet, but also my hands and my head*[^5]. Since you so threaten that my members are to be washed by you, not only do I not withdraw the lowest, but I even lay beneath you the first. Lest you deny me the part to be received with you, I deny you no part of my body to be washed.

**3.** *Jesus says to him: He who is washed has no need but to wash his feet, but is clean all over*[^6]. Here perhaps someone may be moved, and say: On the contrary, if he is clean all over, what need has he even to wash his feet? But the Lord knew what he was saying, even if our weakness does not penetrate his secret things. Yet, so far as he deigns to instruct us and to teach us out of his law, according to my grasp, according to my measure, let me too, with his help, answer something concerning the depth of this question: and first, I shall most easily show that the expression itself is not self-contradictory. For who could not most correctly speak thus: He is clean all over, except his feet? But it is more elegant if one says: He is clean all over, but his feet; which means the same. This, then, the Lord says: *He has no need but to wash his feet, but is clean all over.* All over, surely, except his feet, or, but his feet, which he has need to wash.

**4.** But what is this? what does it mean? what need is there that we inquire into it? The Lord says, the truth speaks, that even he who is washed has need to wash his feet. What, my brothers, what do you think? except that a man is indeed in holy Baptism washed all over, not except the feet, but altogether all over: yet when afterward he lives among human affairs, surely he treads the earth. The very human affections, then, without which one does not live in this mortality, are as it were the feet, where we are affected by human things; and we are so affected that, if we say we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us[^7].

Daily, then, he washes our feet, he who intercedes for us: and that daily we have need to wash our feet, that is, to direct the ways of our spiritual steps, we confess in the Lord's prayer itself, when we say: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^8]. For *if we confess,* as it is written, *our sins,* surely he who washed the feet of his disciples *is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all iniquity*[^9], that is, even to the feet by which we keep company on the earth.

## Christ cleanses his Church.

**5.** Accordingly, the Church, which Christ cleanses by the washing of water in the word, is without spot or wrinkle[^10] not only in those who, after the washing of regeneration, are at once taken from the contagion of this life, and do not tread the earth so as to have need to wash their feet; but also in those to whom the Lord, granting this mercy, made them depart from this world with their feet too washed. But in those who tarry here, even if it be clean, since they live justly, yet they have need to wash their feet, since they are surely not without sin. On account of this it says in the Song of Songs: *I have washed my feet; how shall I soil them*[^11]? For it says this when it is constrained to come to Christ, and to tread the earth when it comes.

Another question again arises. Is not Christ above? did he not ascend into heaven, and sit at the right hand of the Father? does not the Apostle cry out, and say: *If, then, you have risen with Christ, mind the things that are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God; seek the things that are above, not the things that are on the earth*[^12]? How, then, are we, in order to go to Christ, compelled to tread the earth; when rather we ought to have the heart lifted up to the Lord, that we may be able to be with him?

You see, brothers, that the constraints of today's time confine this question. And even if you perhaps see this less, I in any case see how great a discussion it needs. Whence I ask that you allow it rather to be held over than to be handled either too negligently or too narrowly, your expectation not defrauded, but deferred. For the Lord will be present, who makes us debtors, that he may also make us payers.
