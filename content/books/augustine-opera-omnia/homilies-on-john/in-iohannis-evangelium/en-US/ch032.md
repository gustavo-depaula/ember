# Tractate 32

## *But this he said of the Spirit, whom those believing in him were to receive* (Jn 7:37-39).

## We too receive the Holy Spirit, if we love the Church, if we are joined together by love, if we rejoice in the catholic name and faith. Let us believe, brothers: as much as each one loves the Church of Christ, so much does he have the Holy Spirit. — If we thirst, let us come; let us come by loving.

**1.** Amid the dissensions and doubts of the Jews about the Lord Jesus Christ, among the other things he said, by which some were confounded, others taught, on the last day of that festival[^1] (for these things were then taking place), which is called Scenopegia, that is, the construction of tabernacles—about which festival your Charity already remembers that there was a discussion earlier—the Lord Jesus Christ calls, and this not in any ordinary way of speaking, but crying out, that whoever thirsts may come to him.

If we thirst, let us come; and let us come not by feet, but by affections; not by migrating, but by loving. Although according to the inner man, one who loves also migrates. And it is one thing to migrate by body, another by heart: he migrates by body who by motion of the body changes place; he migrates by heart who by motion of the heart changes affection. If you love one thing, when you loved another, you are not where you were.

**2.** The Lord, then, cries out to us—for he stood, and cried out: *If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink. He who believes in me, as the Scripture says, out of his belly will flow rivers of living water.* What this might be, since the Evangelist explained it, we ought not to delay over. For in what sense the Lord said, *If anyone thirsts, let him come to me and drink*, and, *He who believes in me, out of his belly will flow rivers of living water*, the Evangelist consequently explained, saying: *But this he said of the Spirit whom those believing in him were to receive.*

*For the Spirit had not yet been given, because Jesus had not yet been glorified*[^2]. There is, then, an inner thirst and an inner belly, because there is an inner man. And the inner man indeed is invisible, the outer visible: but the inner is better than the outer. And what is not seen is more loved: for it is established that the inner man is more loved than the outer. Whence is this established? Let each one prove it in himself. For although those who live badly subject their minds to the body, yet they wish to live, which is nothing but the mind's, and they declare themselves more to be those who rule than those things which are ruled. For minds rule, bodies are ruled. Each one rejoices in pleasure, and takes pleasure from the body: but separate the mind, and nothing remains in the body that may rejoice; and if it rejoices from the body, the mind rejoices. If it rejoices in its own house, ought it not to rejoice in itself? And if the mind has something whereby it may be delighted from without, does it remain without delights within? It is altogether established that a man loves his soul more than his body.

But in another man too a man loves the soul more than the body. For what is loved in a friend, where love is more sincere and chaste? What is loved in a friend—the mind, or the body? If fidelity is loved, the mind is loved: if benevolence is loved, the seat of benevolence is the mind: if you love this in another, because he too loves you, you love the mind; because not the flesh, but the mind loves. For you love because he loves you: ask whence he loves you, and see what you love. Therefore the devout man is loved, and is not seen.

**3.** I also wish to say something in which it may appear more to your Love how much the mind is loved, and how it is set before the body. Those very wanton lovers, who are delighted by the beauty of bodies, and are inflamed by the form of limbs, then love more when they are loved. For if one loves and perceives that he is held in hatred, he is angry rather than loves.

Why is he angry rather than loves?

Because what he spends is not returned to him.

If, then, those lovers of bodies wish to be loved in return, and this delights them more if they are loved; what sort are the lovers of minds? And if those are great who are lovers of minds, what sort are the lovers of God, who makes minds beautiful? For just as the mind makes comeliness in the body, so God in the mind. For nothing makes the body something to be loved except the mind: when it has migrated, you shudder at the corpse; and however much you loved those beautiful limbs, you hasten to bury it. The comeliness of the body, then, is the mind: the comeliness of the mind is God.

## The fountain does not forsake us, if we do not forsake the fountain.

**4.** The Lord, then, cries out that we may come and drink, if we thirst within; and he says that when we have drunk, rivers of living water will flow from our belly. The belly of the inner man is the conscience of the heart. When this liquor has been drunk, the cleansed conscience comes to life; and drawing, it will have a fountain; indeed it will itself be a fountain.

What is the fountain, and what is the river that flows from the belly of the inner man? Benevolence, by which one wishes to look out for his neighbor. For if he thinks that what he drinks ought to suffice for himself alone, living water does not flow from his belly: but if he hastens to look out for his neighbor; then it does not dry up, because it flows. We shall now see what it is that they drink who believe in the Lord; for surely we are Christians, and if we believe, we drink. And let each one acknowledge in himself whether he drinks, and whether he lives from what he drinks: for the fountain does not forsake us, if we do not forsake the fountain.

**5.** The Evangelist explained, as I said, whence the Lord cried out, to what kind of drink he invited, what he served to those who drink, saying: *But this he said of the Spirit whom those believing in him were to receive. For the Spirit had not yet been given, because Jesus had not yet been glorified.* What Spirit does he mean, except the Holy Spirit? For every man has in himself his own spirit, of which I was speaking when I was commending the mind. For the mind of each one is his own spirit: of which the apostle Paul says: *For who among men knows the things of a man, except the spirit of the man, which is in him?* Then he added: *So too the things of God no one knows, except the Spirit of God*[^3]. Our things no one knows, except our spirit. For I do not know what you think, nor you what I think: for these are our own, which we think within; and of the thoughts of each man, his own spirit is the witness.

So too the things of God no one knows, except the Spirit of God. We with our spirit, God with his: yet in such a way that God with his Spirit knows also what goes on in us; while we without his Spirit cannot know what goes on in God. But God knows in us even what we ourselves do not know in ourselves. For Peter did not know his own weakness, when he heard from the Lord that he would deny him three times[^4], and being sick was ignorant of himself; the physician knew the sick man. There are, then, certain things that God knows in us, while we do not know them. Yet so far as pertains to men, no one knows himself as the man himself does: another does not know what goes on in him, but his spirit knows.

But having received the Spirit of God, we learn also what goes on in God: not the whole, because we have not received the whole. From the pledge we know many things: for we have received a pledge, and the fullness of this pledge will be given afterward. Meanwhile in this pilgrimage let the pledge console us, because he who deigned to give us a pledge is prepared to give much. If such is the earnest, what is that of which it is the earnest?

**6.** But what is it that he says: *For the Spirit had not yet been given, because Jesus had not yet been glorified?* The meaning is evident. For it was not that the Spirit of God did not exist, who was with God; but he was not yet in those who had believed in Jesus. For the Lord Jesus so disposed not to give them this Spirit of whom we are speaking, except after his resurrection; and this not without cause. And perhaps if we ask, he will grant that we find; and if we knock, he will open that we may enter. Devotion knocks, not the hand: although the hand too knocks, if the hand does not cease from works of mercy. What, then, is the cause why the Lord Jesus Christ determined to give the Holy Spirit only when he was glorified?

Before we say this as we can, we must first inquire, lest perhaps it move someone, how the Spirit was not yet in holy men, when of the Lord himself, newly born, it is read in the Gospel that Simeon recognized him in the Holy Spirit, and Anna the widow, the prophetess, also recognized him[^5]; John himself, who baptized him, recognized him[^6]: Zechariah, filled with the Holy Spirit, said many things[^7]; Mary herself received the Holy Spirit, that she might conceive the Lord[^8]. We have, then, many preceding indications of the Holy Spirit, before the Lord was glorified by the resurrection of his flesh. For the Prophets too, who announced beforehand that Christ would come, did not have another spirit.

But there was to be a certain manner of this giving, which had altogether not appeared before: of this it is here spoken. For nowhere do we read before that men, gathered together, having received the Holy Spirit, spoke in the tongues of all nations. But after his resurrection, first when he appeared to his disciples, he said to them: *Receive the Holy Spirit.* Of this, then, it was said: The Spirit had not been given, because Jesus had not yet been glorified. And *he breathed into their face*[^9], he who by his breath gave life to the first man, and raised him from the mud[^10], by which breath he gave the soul to its members; signifying that he is the one who breathed into their face, that they might rise from the clay, and renounce clayey works.

Then first, after his resurrection, which the Evangelist calls glorification, the Lord gave his disciples the Holy Spirit. Then, having stayed with them forty days, as the book of the Acts of the Apostles shows, in their sight, and as they watched and accompanied him with their eyes, he ascended into heaven[^11]. There, when ten days were past, on the day of Pentecost he sent the Holy Spirit from above. By whom, as I said, those who had been gathered in one place, having received him, were filled, and spoke in the tongues of all nations[^12].

**7.** What then, brothers, because now one who is baptized in Christ, and believes in Christ, does not speak in the tongues of all nations, is he not to be believed to have received the Holy Spirit? Far be it that such faithlessness should tempt our heart. We are certain that every man receives him: but as much as the vessel of faith brings to the fountain, so much it fills.

Since, then, he is received even now, someone may say, why does no one speak in the tongues of all nations? Because the Church itself now speaks in the tongues of all nations. Formerly the Church was in one nation, where it spoke in the tongues of all. By speaking in the tongues of all, it signified that it would come to pass that, by growing through the nations, it would speak in the tongues of all.

Whoever is not in this Church does not now receive the Holy Spirit. For cut off and divided from the unity of the members, which unity speaks in the tongues of all, let him renounce his claim; he does not have it. For if he has it, let him give the sign that was then given.

What is it, let him give the sign that was then given?

Let him speak in all tongues.

He answers me: What then, do you speak in all tongues?

I do indeed speak, because every tongue is mine, that is, of that body of which I am a member.

The Church, spread through the nations, speaks in all tongues; the Church is the body of Christ, in this body you are a member: since, then, you are a member of that body which speaks in all tongues, believe that you speak in all tongues. For the unity of the members is in concord through love; and that unity itself speaks as then one man was speaking.

## If you love unity, whoever has anything in it has it for you too.

**8.** We too, then, receive the Holy Spirit, if we love the Church, if we are joined together by love, if we rejoice in the catholic name and faith. Let us believe, brothers: as much as each one loves the Church of Christ, so much does he have the Holy Spirit. For the Spirit is given, as the Apostle says, for manifestation. What manifestation? As he himself says: *To one is given through the Spirit the utterance of wisdom, to another the utterance of knowledge according to the same Spirit, to another faith in the same Spirit, to another the gift of healings in the one Spirit, to another the working of mighty deeds in the same Spirit*[^13]. For many things are given for manifestation, but you perhaps have nothing of all those that I have mentioned. If you love, you have not nothing: for if you love unity, whoever has anything in it has it for you too. Take away envy, and what I have is yours: let me take away envy, and what you have is mine.

Spite separates, health joins. The eye alone sees in the body: but does the eye see for itself alone? It sees for the hand, it sees for the foot, it sees for the other members: for if some blow comes against the foot, the eye does not turn itself away from there so as not to take precaution. Again, the hand alone works in the body; but does it work for itself alone? It works for the eye too; for if a blow comes that goes not to the hand, but only to the face, does the hand say: I do not move, because it does not aim at me? So the foot by walking does service for all the members: the other members are silent, and the tongue speaks for all.

We have, then, the Holy Spirit, if we love the Church: and we love it, if we stand fast in its framework and love. For the Apostle himself, when he had said that diverse gifts are given to diverse men, as it were the offices of the several members: *Still*, he says, *I show you a more excellent way*[^14], and he began to speak of love. He set it before the tongues of men and angels, he set it before the faith of miracles, he set it before knowledge and prophecy, he set it even before that great work of mercy by which each one distributes to the poor what he possesses; and finally he set it even before the suffering of the body: before all these so great things he set love. Have love itself, and you will have all things: because without it, whatever you may be able to have will profit nothing.

But that the love of which we speak pertains to the Holy Spirit (for the question now being treated in the Gospel is about the Holy Spirit), hear the Apostle saying: *The love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^15].

**9.** Why, then, did the Lord wish to give, after his resurrection, the Spirit whose greatest benefits are in us, because the love of God has been poured out through him in our hearts? What did he signify? That in our resurrection our love may blaze, and separate us from the love of the world, that it may run wholly into God. For here we are born and die; let us not love this: let us migrate by love, let us dwell above by love, by that love with which we love God. Let us meditate on nothing else in this pilgrimage of our life, except that here too we shall not always be, and that there we shall prepare for ourselves a place by living well, whence we shall never migrate. For our Lord Jesus Christ, after he rose, dies no more; *death*, as the Apostle says, *shall no longer have dominion over him*[^16].

Behold what we are to love. If we live, if we believe in him who rose; he will give us, not what those men love here who do not love God, or love it so much the more, the less they love him: but they love this so much the less, the more they love him. But let us see what he promised us: not earthly and temporal riches, not honors and powers in this world; for you see that all these are given even to evil men, lest they be greatly prized by the good. Not, finally, the very soundness of the body: not because he himself does not give it, but because, as you see, he gives it even to cattle. Not a long life. For what is long that some day ends? He did not promise as something great to us who believe long life, or decrepit old age; which all wish for before it comes, and all murmur about when it has come. Not beauty of the body, which either disease of the body or that very old age which is wished for destroys. A man wishes to be beautiful, and wishes to be old: these two desires cannot agree with each other: if you will be old, you will not be beautiful: when old age has come, beauty will flee; and the vigor of beauty and the groaning of old age cannot dwell in one.

All these things, then, he did not promise us, who said: *He who believes in me, let him come and drink; and rivers of living water will flow from his belly.* He promised eternal life, where we may fear nothing, where we may not be disturbed, whence we may not migrate, where we may not die; where neither is a predecessor mourned, nor a successor hoped for. Because, then, such is what he promised us who love and are fervent with the love of the Holy Spirit; therefore he did not wish to give the Spirit himself, except when he was glorified: that in his own body he might show the life which we do not have now, but hope for in the resurrection.
