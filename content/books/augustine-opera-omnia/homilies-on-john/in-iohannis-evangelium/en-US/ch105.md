# Tractate 105

## *This is eternal life, that they may know you*[^1].

## Further, if the knowledge of God is eternal life, we tend the more to live, the more we advance in this knowledge.

**1.** That the Son was glorified by the Father according to the form of a servant, which the Father raised from the dead and placed at his right hand, the matter itself indicates, and no Christian doubts. But since he said not only, *Father, glorify your Son,* but also added, *that your Son may glorify you,* it is rightly asked how the Son glorified the Father, when the everlasting glory of the Father was neither diminished in the human form, nor could be increased in its divine perfection. But in itself the glory of the Father can neither be lessened nor increased; among men, however, it was without doubt less, when God was known only in Judea: not yet did children praise the name of the Lord *from the rising of the sun to its setting*. But because this was brought about through the Gospel of Christ, that through the Son the Father became known to the nations; surely the Son glorified the Father. But if the Son had only died, and had not risen, without doubt he would neither have been glorified by the Father, nor have glorified the Father: but now, glorified by the Father in the resurrection, by the preaching of his resurrection he glorifies the Father. For the very order of words discloses this: *Glorify,* he says, *your Son, that your Son may glorify you*; as if he said: Raise me up, that you may become known to the whole world through me.

**2.** Then, opening up more and more how the Son glorifies the Father: *As you have given him,* he says, *power over all flesh, that to all whom you have given him he may give eternal life*[^2]. By all flesh he meant every man, signifying the whole by a part; just as, again, the whole man is signified by the higher part, where the Apostle says: *Let every soul be subject to the higher powers*[^3]. For what did he say, *Every soul,* but, every man? And this too, that power over all flesh was given to Christ by the Father, is to be understood according to the man: for according to that which is God, *all things were made through him*, and in him were created all things in heaven and on earth, visible and invisible. *As,* then, *you have given him power,* he says, *over all flesh,* so let your Son glorify you, that is, make you known to all flesh which you have given him. For you have so given, *that to all whom you have given him he may give eternal life.*

## Where there is full knowledge, there is the highest glorification.

**3.** *But this is eternal life,* he says, *that they may know you, the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*[^4]. The order of the words is, *that they may know you and Jesus Christ whom you have sent as the one true God.* For the Holy Spirit too is consequently understood, because he is the Spirit of the Father and the Son, as the substantial and consubstantial love of both. Since the Father and the Son are not two gods, nor are the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit three gods; but the Trinity itself is the one only true God. Nor yet is the Father the same as the Son, nor the Son the same as the Father, nor the Holy Spirit the same as the Father and the Son; since the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are three; but the Trinity itself is one God. If, then, the Son glorifies you in that way *as you have given him power over all flesh,* and you have so given, *that to all whom you have given him he may give eternal life,* and, *this is eternal life, that they may know you*; the Son therefore glorifies you in this way, that to all whom you have given him he may make you known.

Further, if the knowledge of God is eternal life, we tend the more to live, the more we advance in this knowledge. But we shall not die in eternal life: then, therefore, the knowledge of God will be perfect, when there will be no death. Then will be the highest glorification of God; because the highest glory, which in Greek is called "doxa." Whence is said "doxasei," which certain Latins have rendered, glorify; others, make glorious. And by the ancients glory, by which men are called glorious, was thus defined: Glory is frequent fame concerning someone with praise. But if a man is praised when his fame is believed, how will God be praised when he himself will be seen? On account of which it is written: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you*[^5]. There will be the endless praise of God, where there will be the full knowledge of God; and because there is full knowledge, therefore the highest glorification.

**4.** But first God is glorified here, while, being announced to men, he becomes known, and is preached through the faith of believers. On account of which he says: *I have glorified you upon the earth, I have finished the work which you gave me to do*[^6]. He does not say, you commanded; but, *you gave*: where evident grace is commended. For what does even the human nature in the Only-begotten have which it did not receive? Did it not receive that it should do no evil, but all good things, when it was taken up into the unity of the person by the Word, through whom all things were made? But how did he finish the work which he received to do, when there still remains the experience of the passion, where he most of all set for his martyrs the example which they should follow — whence the apostle Peter says: *Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, that we should follow in his steps*[^7] — unless because he says he has finished what he most certainly knew he would finish? Just as, long before, in prophecy he used words of past time, when after very many years what he said was going to be: *They dug,* he says, *my hands and feet, they numbered all my bones*[^8]; he does not say: They will dig and will number. And in this very Gospel: *All things,* he says, *that I heard from my Father I have made known to you*[^9]; to whom he says afterward: *I still have many things to say to you; but you cannot bear them now*[^10]. For he who by certain and unchangeable causes predestined all future things, did whatever he was going to do: for it is also said of him through the prophet, *He who made the things that are to be*[^11].

**5.** According to this he also says what follows: *And now glorify me, Father, with yourself, with the glory which I had with you before the world was*[^12]. For above he had said: *Father, the hour has come, glorify your Son, that your Son may glorify you*: in which order of words he had shown that the Son was first to be glorified by the Father, that the Son might glorify the Father. But now he said: *I have glorified you upon the earth, I have finished the work which you gave me to do: and now glorify me*; as though he himself first glorified the Father, from whom he then asks that he be glorified. Therefore it is to be understood that above he used both words according to that which was going to be, and in the order in which it was going to be: *Glorify the Son, that the Son may glorify you*: but now he used a word of past time of a future thing, where he says, *I have glorified you upon the earth, I have finished the work which you gave me to do.* Then, by saying, *And now glorify me, Father, with yourself,* as though he were to be glorified afterward by the Father, whom he himself had first glorified; what does he show, but that above, where he says, *I have glorified you upon the earth,* he so spoke as though he had done what he was going to do; but here he asked that the Father might do that through which the Son was going to do the former, that is, that the Father might glorify the Son, by which glorification of the Son the Son also was going to glorify the Father? Finally, if of the thing which was future we put even a word of future time, where he himself put the past for the future time, no obscurity of the meaning will remain: as if he had said: I will glorify you upon the earth, I will finish the work which you have given me to do: and now glorify me, Father, with yourself. Surely it is thus plain, as that where he says, *Glorify your Son, that your Son may glorify you*: and it is altogether the same meaning, except that here too the manner of that glorification is stated, but there it is left unsaid; as though the former were explained by this latter to those whom it could disturb, how the Father glorifies the Son, and especially how the Son also glorifies the Father. For by saying that the Father is glorified by him upon the earth, but he himself by the Father with the same Father, he surely shows the manner of each glorification. For he himself glorified the Father upon the earth by preaching him to the nations; but the Father glorified him with himself by placing him at his right hand. But for this reason afterward, concerning the glorifying of the Father, where he says, *I have glorified you,* he preferred to put a word of past time, that he might show it already done in predestination, and that what most certainly was going to be should be held as already done; that is, that the Son too, glorified by the Father with the Father, might glorify the Father upon the earth.

**6.** But this predestination he opened more manifestly in his own glorification, by which the Father glorified him, in that he added: *With the glory which I had with you before the world was.* The order of the words is, *which I had with you before the world was.* To this belongs what he says, *And now glorify me*; that is, as then, so now also; as then by predestination, so now also by perfection: do in the world what was already with you before the world; do in its own time what before all times you appointed. This some have thought to be so understood, as though the human nature which was taken up by the Word were turned into the Word, and the man were changed into God; nay rather, if we consider more carefully what they supposed, the man would perish in God. For no one, from this change of the man, will say that the Word of God is either doubled or increased, so that either there are two of what was one, or there is more of what was less. Further, if the human nature, changed and turned into the Word, the Word of God is to be as great as it was and what it was, where is the man, if he does not perish?

**7.** But nothing compels us to this opinion, which I do not at all see to agree with the truth, if, when the Son says, *And now glorify me, Father, with yourself, with the glory which I had with you before the world was,* we understand the predestination of the glory of the human nature which is in him, that nature which from mortal was to be immortal with the Father; and that this was already done by predestining before the world was, which in the world too in its own time should be done. For if of us the Apostle said, *As he chose us in him before the foundation of the world*[^13], why is it thought to shrink from the truth, if the Father then glorified our head, when he chose us in him, that we might be his members? For we were so chosen as he was glorified; because before the world was, neither were we, nor was he himself the mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus. But he who through him, insofar as he is his Word, also *made the things that are to be,* and *calls the things that are not, as though they were*[^14]; surely, according to that which he is, as mediator of God and men, a man, before the foundation of the world God the Father glorified him, if he then chose us too in him. For what does the Apostle say? *But we know that to those who love God all things work together for good, to those who according to his purpose are called. For those whom he foreknew, he also predestined to become conformed to the image of his Son, that he himself might be the firstborn among many brothers: but those whom he predestined, them he also called*[^15].

**8.** Unless perhaps we shall fear to say that he himself was predestined, because the Apostle seems to have said this only of us, that we should be made conformed to his image. As if, indeed, anyone faithfully regarding the rule of faith would deny that the Son of God was predestined, who cannot deny that he was a man. For rightly is he said not predestined according to that which he is the Word of God, God with God. For why should he be predestined, when he already was what he was, without beginning, without end, everlasting? But that was to be predestined which was not yet, so that it should come to be in its own time, just as before all times it had been predestined to come to be. Whoever, then, denies that the Son of God was predestined, denies this same son of man. But, on account of the contentious, let us hear the Apostle even here, at the beginning of his Writings. For in the first of his Epistles, which is to the Romans, and at the very beginning of that Epistle, it is read: *Paul, a servant of Jesus Christ, called an Apostle, set apart for the Gospel of God, which he had promised before through his Prophets in the holy Scriptures concerning his Son, who was made for him of the seed of David according to the flesh, who was predestined Son of God in power according to the Spirit of sanctification by the resurrection from the dead*[^16].

According to this predestination, then, he was also glorified before the world was, that his glory might be from the resurrection of the dead with the Father, at whose right hand he sits. When, therefore, he saw that the time of that predestined glorification of his had now come, that now too there should be done in the rendering what had already been done in the predestination, he prayed, saying: *And now glorify me, Father, with yourself, with the glory which I had with you before the world was*: as if he said: The glory which I had with you, that is, that glory which I had with you in your predestination, it is time that I have it with you, living also at your right hand. But since the discussion of this question has long held us, what follows must be treated in another sermon.
