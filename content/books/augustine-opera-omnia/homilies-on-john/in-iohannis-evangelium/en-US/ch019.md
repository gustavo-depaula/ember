# Homily 19

## *The hour is coming, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God*[^1].

Do not be silent, Lord, about the resurrection of the flesh, lest men not believe it, and we remain arguers, not preachers. Let those who hear understand, let them believe that they may understand, let them obey that they may live. God raises souls, through Christ the Son of God; God raises bodies, through the same Christ the Son of man.

**1.** In the previous sermon, as far as our affection and poverty of understanding moved us, we spoke, on occasion of the Gospel words where it is written, *The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*, of what the seeing of the Son is—that is, the seeing of the Word, because the Son is the Word: and since all things were made through the Word, how it can be understood that the Son first sees the Father doing, and only then he himself does what he has seen done; whereas the Father has done nothing except through the Son. For *all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^2]. Yet we said nothing unfolded; but because we understood nothing unfolded either. For sometimes speech fails where even understanding advances; how much more does speech suffer failure when the understanding has no perfection?

Now, therefore, as far as the Lord grants, let us briefly run through the reading, and at least today complete the appointed task. If perhaps any time or strength remains, we will go back over, if we can (as far as it can be done both by us and among you), what the seeing of the Word is, what it is for something to be shown to the Word. For all things have been said here, which, if they are understood according to a human and carnal sense, the soul full of phantasms makes for us nothing other than certain images, as it were, of two men, the Father and the Son—the one showing, the other seeing; the one speaking, the other hearing: all of which are idols of the heart; which, if they have already been cast down from their temples, how much more are they to be cast down from Christian breasts?

**2.** *The Son cannot*, he says, *do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*. This is true, hold this: provided, however, that you do not lose what you held at the very opening of this Gospel, that *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*; and especially that *all things were made through him*. For what you have now heard, join to that hearing, and let both agree in your hearts. So, then, the Son cannot do anything of himself except what he sees the Father doing, in such a way that yet the Father does the things he does only through the Son, because the Son is his Word: and *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God, and all things were made through him*. *For whatever things he does, these the Son likewise does*; not other things, but these; nor unlike, but likewise.

**3.** *For the Father loves the Son, and shows him everything that he himself does*[^3]. To what he said above, *except what he sees the Father doing*, this too seems to pertain, that *he shows him everything that he himself does*. But if the Father shows what he does, and the Son cannot do unless the Father has shown, and the Father cannot show unless he has done; the consequence will be that the Father does not do all things through the Son: but further, if we hold it fixed and unshaken that the Father does all things through the Son, then before he does, he shows the Son. For if the Father, when he has done, shows the Son, so that the Son may do what is shown—things which, being shown, are already done—then without doubt the Father does something without the Son. But the Father does not do anything without the Son, because the Son of God is the Word of God, and all things were made through him.

It remains, then, perhaps, that the things the Father is going to do, he shows to be done, so that they may be done through the Son. For if the Son does the things which the Father shows as done, the things which the Father shows as done he surely did not do through the Son. For they could not be shown to the Son unless done; the Son could not do them unless shown: therefore done without the Son. But it is true, *All things were made through him*: therefore before they were done, they were shown. But this we said must be deferred, to which we are to return when the reading has been run through, if, as we said, any time or strength remains to us, to go back over the things we deferred.

## A difficult question arises.

**4.** Hear something further and more difficult: *And greater works than these*, he says, *will he show him, so that you may marvel*[^4]. *Greater than these*: than what greater? It readily occurs—than those you have just heard, the cures of bodily sicknesses. For from that man who had been thirty-eight years in his infirmity and was healed by the word of Christ, the whole occasion of this sermon arose: and for this reason the Lord could say, *Greater works than these will he show him, so that you may marvel*. For there are works greater than these, and the Father will show them to the Son. He did not say "he showed," as of the past, but "he will show," of the future—that is, he is going to show.

Again a difficult question arises. For is there something with the Father which has not yet been shown to the Son? Is there something with the Father which still lay hidden from the Son, when the Son was speaking these things? For if he *will show*—that is, is going to show—he has not yet shown; and he is going to show the Son then, when he also shows these very persons: for it follows, *so that you may marvel*. And this is difficult to see, how the eternal Father shows, as it were temporally, some things to the coeternal Son, who knows all things that are with the Father.

**5.** What, however, are those greater works? For this perhaps is easy to understand. *For as the Father*, he says, *raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*[^5]. Greater works, then, are to raise the dead than to heal the sick. But as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will. Some, then, the Father, others the Son? But all things are through him: therefore the Son gives life to the same ones to whom the Father does; because not other things, nor otherwise, but these things the Son too likewise does. So it is plainly to be understood, and so to be held: but remember that the Son gives life to whom he will. Hold here, then, not only the power of the Son, but also the will. Both the Son gives life to whom he will, and the Father gives life to whom he will; and the Son gives life to the same ones as the Father: and by this the power and the will of the Father and the Son are the same.

What is it, then, that follows? *For the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son, that all may honor the Son as they honor the Father*[^6]: which he so added, as if giving a reason for the previous statement. It moves much; be attentive.

The Son gives life to whom he will, the Father gives life to whom he will: the Son raises the dead, as the Father raises the dead. *For the Father judges no one.* If at the judgment the dead are to be raised, how does the Father raise the dead, if he judges no one? For all judgment he has given to the Son. But in that judgment the dead are raised; and they rise again, some to life, others to punishment:

and if the Son does all this, but the Father does not do it for the reason that the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son, it will seem contrary to what was said, *As the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*. They raise together, then. If they raise together, they give life together. They judge together, then: how, then, is it true, *For the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*?

Let the questions proposed move you for the time; the Lord will grant that, solved, they may delight. So it is, brothers; every question, unless when proposed it makes one attentive, will not delight when set forth. Let the Lord himself, then, go on, lest perhaps in the things he subjoins he open himself somewhat. For he weaves his light beneath a cloud; and it is hard, after the manner of the eagle, to fly above all the mist by which the whole earth is covered, and to see in the words of the Lord the purest light[^7]. Lest, then, perhaps by the heat of his rays he should scatter our gloom, and deign somewhat to open himself in what follows, let us, these things deferred, see what comes next.

## How the Father is to be honored.

**6.** *He who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him*[^8]. This is true and plain. For all judgment he has given to the Son, as he said above, *that all may honor the Son as they honor the Father*.

What if there are found those who honor the Father and do not honor the Son?

It cannot be, he says: *He who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him*.

Therefore no one can say: I was honoring the Father, because I did not know the Son.

If you were not yet honoring the Son, neither were you honoring the Father. For what is it to honor the Father, except that he has a Son? For it is one thing when God is commended to you because he is God, and another thing when God is commended to you because he is Father. When he is commended to you because he is God, the creator is commended to you, the almighty is commended to you, a certain supreme, eternal, invisible, unchangeable spirit is commended to you: but when he is commended to you because he is Father, nothing else is commended to you than that he also has a Son; because he cannot be called Father if he has no Son, just as neither can he be called Son if he has no Father.

But lest perhaps you should honor the Father indeed as greater, but the Son as less, so that you say to me: I honor the Father, for I know that he has a Son; and I do not err in the name of the Father, for I do not understand the Father without the Son, yet I honor the Son too as less: the Son himself corrects you, and calls you back, saying, *that all may honor the Son*—not as inferior, but *as they honor the Father*. He, then, who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him.

I, you say, wish to give greater honor to the Father, less to the Son. There you take away honor from the Father, where you give less to the Son. For what else seems to you, thinking so, but that the Father either was unwilling or unable to beget a Son equal to himself? If unwilling, he envied; if unable, he failed. Do you not see, then, that by thinking so—where you wish to give greater honor to the Father—there you are insulting to the Father? Accordingly, honor the Son as you honor the Father, if you wish to honor both the Father and the Son.

## Understand the harmony of the Scriptures.

**7.** *Amen, amen I say to you, that he who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life; and does not come into judgment, but has passed*—not, now passes, but has already passed—*from death into life*[^9]. And attend to this: *He who hears my word*: and he did not say, believes me; but, *believes him who sent me*. Let him hear, then, the word of the Son, that he may believe the Father. Why does he hear your word, and believe another? Is it not so, that when we hear someone's word, we believe that same one bringing forth the word, and we lend faith to the one speaking to us?

What, then, did he wish to say: *He who hears my word, and believes him who sent me*; except that his word is in me? And what is, *he hears my word*, except, he hears me? But *he believes him who sent me*: because when he believes him, he believes his word; and when he believes his word, he believes me; because I am the Word of the Father.

There is peace, then, in the Scriptures, and all things are ordered, in no way quarreling. Do you cast away the strife of your heart, understand the harmony of the Scriptures. Would the truth say things contrary to itself?

**8.** *He who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life; and does not come into judgment, but has passed from death into life*. You remember what we had set down above, that *as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*. He now begins to open himself, and to speak of the resurrection of the dead, and behold, now the dead rise again.

For *he who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life, and will not come into judgment*.

Prove that he has risen again.

*But he has passed*, he says, *from death into life*. He who has passed from death to life, without doubt has surely risen again. For he would not pass from death to life unless he were first in death and not in life: but when he has passed, he will be in life and will not be in death. He was dead, then, and lived again; he had perished, and was found.

Accordingly there now takes place a certain resurrection, and men pass from a certain death to a certain life: from the death of unbelief to the life of faith; from the death of falsehood to the life of truth; from the death of iniquity to the life of righteousness. This too, then, is a certain resurrection of the dead.

## A resurrection before the resurrection.

**9.** Let him open it more fully, and let it grow clear to us, as he has begun. *Amen, amen I say to you, that an hour is coming, and now is*[^10]. We were awaiting at the end the resurrection of the dead; for so we have believed: or rather, we were not awaiting it, but plainly we ought to await it; for it is no false thing we believe, that at the end the dead will rise again. When, then, the Lord Jesus wished to suggest to us a certain resurrection of the dead before the resurrection of the dead—not like that of Lazarus, or of that widow's son, or of the ruler of the synagogue's daughter, who rose to die again (for of those dead too a certain resurrection took place before the resurrection of the dead): but as here he says, *he has*, he says, *eternal life; and does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life*. To what life? To eternal life. Not, then, like the body of Lazarus: for he too passed from the death of the tomb to the life of men, but not eternal, to die again; whereas the dead who will rise at the end of the world will pass into eternal life.

When, then, our Lord Jesus Christ, the heavenly Master, the Word of the Father and the Truth, wished to show us a certain resurrection of the dead to eternal life, before the resurrection of the dead to eternal life: *An hour is coming*, he says. You, without doubt, imbued with the faith of the resurrection of the flesh, were awaiting that hour of the end of the world, the day of judgment; and lest in this place you should await that, he added, *and now is*. What he says, then, *An hour is coming*, he does not say of that last hour, where, at the command and the voice of the archangel, and at the trumpet of God, the Lord himself will descend from heaven, and the dead in Christ will rise first: then we who are alive, who remain, will be caught up together with them in the clouds to meet Christ in the air, and so we shall always be with the Lord[^11].

That hour will come, but it is not now. But what this hour is, take note: *An hour is coming, and now is*. What takes place in it? What, except the resurrection of the dead? And what kind of resurrection? That those who rise may live forever. This will be also in the last hour.

**10.** What, then? How do we understand these two resurrections? Is it perhaps that those who rise now will not rise then; so that there is now a resurrection of some, then of others? It is not so. For by this resurrection, if we have rightly believed, we have risen; and we ourselves who have already risen await another resurrection at the end. But both now we have risen to eternal life, if we abide perseveringly in that faith; and then we shall rise to eternal life, when we shall be made equal to the angels.

Let him himself, then, distinguish, let him himself open what we have dared to speak: how there takes place a resurrection before the resurrection, not of some and others, but of the same; nor such as that of Lazarus, but to eternal life. He will plainly open it. Hear the Master growing clear, and our Sun stealing into our hearts; not the Sun that the eyes of the flesh desire, but that to which the eyes of the heart burn to be opened.

Let us, then, hear him: *Amen, amen I say to you, that an hour is coming, and now is, when the dead*—see the resurrection expressed—*when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God; and those who have heard will live*[^12]. Why did he add, *those who have heard will live*? For could they hear unless they were alive? It would suffice, then: An hour is coming, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God. We would already understand them to be living; since, unless they were living, they could not hear. Not, he says, because they live do they hear, but by hearing they come to life again: they will hear, and those who have heard will live.

What, then, is *they will hear*, except, they will hearken? For as far as concerns the hearing of the ear, not all who will hear will live: for many hear and do not believe; by hearing and not believing they do not hearken: by not hearkening they do not live.

And so here, *those who will hear* is nothing other than those who will hearken. Those, then, who have hearkened will live: let them be certain, let them be secure, they will live. Christ is preached, the Word of God, the Son of God, through whom all things were made, surely by the grace of the dispensation, having taken flesh, born of a Virgin, an infant in the flesh, a young man in the flesh, suffering in the flesh, dying in the flesh, rising in the flesh, ascending in the flesh, promising resurrection to the flesh, promising resurrection to the mind—to the mind before the flesh, to the flesh after the mind. He who hears and hearkens will live; he who hears and does not hearken, that is, hears and despises, hears and does not believe, will not live.

Why will he not live?

Because he does not hear.

What is, he does not hear?

He does not hearken. Therefore those who have heard will live.

**11.** Attend now to what we had said should be deferred, that it may now, if it can be done, be opened. He at once subjoined, concerning this very resurrection: *For as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself*[^13]. What is, *the Father has life in himself*? He does not have life elsewhere, but in himself. For his living is in him; it is not from elsewhere, not another's: he does not as it were borrow life, nor as it were become a partaker of a life which is not what he himself is; but he has life in himself, so that that very life is himself to himself.

If I shall be able to say yet a little about it, with examples set forth for the forming of your understanding, with the Lord's help I shall be able, and through the devotion of your attention. God lives, and the soul also lives: but the life of God is unchangeable, the life of the soul is changeable. God neither advances nor fails; but he himself is always in himself, is just as he is: not otherwise now, otherwise afterward, otherwise before. But the life of the soul is greatly now in one way, now in another: it lived foolish, it lives wise; it lived unjust, it lives just: now it remembers, now it forgets; now it learns, now it cannot learn; now it loses what it had learned, now it grasps what it had lost: changeable is the life of the soul.

And when the soul lives in iniquity, that is its death: but when it becomes just, it is made a partaker of another life, which is not what it itself is; for by raising itself to God and clinging to God, it is justified by him. For it is said, *To the one who believes in him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is reckoned for righteousness*[^14]. By falling away from him it becomes unjust, by advancing toward him it becomes just. Does it not seem to you like something cold that, brought near to fire, grows warm, and removed from the fire grows numb? Does it not seem to you like something dark that, brought near to light, grows bright, and removed from the light grows black? Such a thing is the soul, not such a thing is God.

A man too can say that he has light now in his eyes. Let your eyes, then, say, as it were in a voice of their own, if they can: We have light in ourselves. Against this it is said: You do not properly say that you have light in yourselves: you have light, but in heaven; you have light, if perhaps it is night, but in the moon, in lamps, not in yourselves: for closed you lose what open you perceive. You do not have light in yourselves: when the sun sets, keep the light, if you can: it is night, and you enjoy the nocturnal light; the lamp removed, keep the light: but when, the lamp taken away, you remain in darkness, you do not have light in yourselves.

This, then, is to have light in oneself, not to be in need of light from another. See where, if anyone understands, he shows the Son equal to the Father, when he says, *As the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself*: so that this only is the difference between the Father and the Son—that the Father has life in himself, which no one gave him; but the Son has life in himself, which the Father gave.

## How the Father gave life to the Son.

**12.** But here too a certain cloud arises that must be scattered. Let us not fail, let us be attentive: these are the pastures of the mind, let us not be disdainful, that we may live.

See, you say, you yourself confess that the Father gave life to the Son, so that he has it indeed in himself, as the Father has life in himself—the one not in need, let the other too not be in need; that the one be life, let the other also be; and both joined in one life, not two; because there is one God, not two gods: and this very thing is to be life. How, then, did the Father give life to the Son? Not as though the Son was previously without life, and, in order to live, received life from the Father: for if this were so, he would not have life in himself.

See, I was speaking of the soul. The soul exists: even if it is not wise, even if it is not just, it is a soul; even if it is not devout, it is a soul. It is, then, one thing for it to be a soul, another to be wise, to be just, to be devout. There is, then, something by which it is not yet wise, not yet just, not yet devout; yet it is not nothing, yet there is not no life: for by certain of its works it shows itself to be life, even if it does not show itself wise, devout, just. For unless it lived, it would not move the body; it would not command the feet to step, the hands to work, the eyes to gaze, the ears to hear; it would not open the mouth for the voice, nor move the tongue for the distinguishing of voices. By these works, then, it shows that it lives, and that there is something better than the body: but does it by these works show itself wise, devout, just? Do not the foolish too, and the ungodly, and the unjust, walk, work, see, hear, speak?

But when it raises itself to something which it itself is not, and which is above it, and from which it itself is, it receives wisdom, righteousness, devotion: without which, when it was, it was dead, and did not have the life by which it itself lived, but the life by which it gave life to the body. For it is one thing in the soul by which the body is given life, another by which the soul itself is given life. For it is better than the body; but better than the soul itself is God. The soul itself, then, even if it be unwise, unjust, ungodly, is the life of the body. But because its own life is God, just as, when it is in the body, it furnishes the body with vigor, beauty, mobility, the offices of the members; so when its life, God, is in it, he furnishes it with wisdom, devotion, righteousness, love. It is, then, one thing that is furnished to the body from the soul, another that is furnished to the soul from God: it gives life, and is given life; dead, it gives life, if it is not itself given life.

And so, when the word comes and is poured into those who hear, and they are made not only hearers but also obeyers, the soul rises from its death to its life; that is, from iniquity, from foolishness, from ungodliness, to its God, who is to it wisdom, righteousness, brightness. Let it rise to him, let it be enlightened by him. *Draw near*, he says, *to him*.

And what will it be for us?

*And be enlightened*[^15]. If, then, by drawing near you are enlightened, and by withdrawing you are darkened, your light was not in you, but in your God. Draw near, that you may rise; if you withdraw, you will die. If, then, by drawing near you live, by withdrawing you die, your life was not in you. For it is your very life that is your light. *For with you is the spring of life, and in your light we shall see light*[^16].

**13.** Not, then, as the soul is something other before it is enlightened, and becomes better when it is enlightened by participation in something better; not in this way is the Word of God, the Son of God, something other before it received life, so that by participating it has life: but it has life in itself; and through this it is itself the very life.

What, then, does he say: *He granted the Son to have life in himself*?

I would say briefly: He begot the Son. For he was not without life, and received life; but by being born he is life. The Father is life by not being born; the Son is life by being born. The Father from no father, the Son from God the Father. The Father, what he is, is from no one: but what he is as Father, is because of the Son. But the Son both what he is as Son is because of the Father; and what he is, is from the Father. This, then, he said: He gave life to the Son, that he might have it in himself; as if he said: The Father, who is life in himself, begot the Son, who would be life in himself. For by saying "he begot," he wished "he gave" to be understood.

As if we said to anyone: God gave you to be. To whom did he give it? If he gave being to someone already existing, he did not give being to him; because he was, before being given to one who could receive. When, then, you hear, He gave you to be, you were not one who could receive, and by existing you received that you should be. The builder gave to this house to be. But what did he give it? That it should be a house. To whom did he give it? To this house. What did he give it? That it should be a house. How could he give to a house to be a house? For if it was a house, to whom would he give it to be a house, when it was already a house? What is it, then: He gave it to be a house? He made it to be a house.

What, then, did he give the Son? He gave him to be the Son, he begot him to be life: this is, He granted him to have life in himself, so that he should be life not in need of life, lest he be understood to have life by participation. For if he had life by participation, he could also, by losing it, be without life: do not take this in the Son, do not think it, do not believe it. The Father, then, abides life, and the Son too abides life: the Father life in himself, not from the Son; the Son life in himself, but from the Father. Begotten by the Father so as to be life in himself: but the Father, unbegotten, life in himself. Nor did he beget a lesser Son, who by growing should become equal. For he was not aided by time toward his own perfection, through whom times were created perfect. Before all times he is coeternal with the Father. For the Father was never without the Son: but the Father is eternal; therefore the Son too is coeternal.

What of you, soul? You were dead, you had lost life; hear the Father through the Son. Rise, receive life; that in him you may receive the life which you do not have in yourself—in him who has life in himself. The Father, then, and the Son give you life; and the first resurrection takes place, when you rise to participate in the life which you yourself are not, and by participating are made living. Rise from your death to your life, which is your God; and pass from death to eternal life. For the Father has eternal life in himself; and unless he begot such a Son as would have life in himself, then not *as the Father raises the dead and gives them life*, would *the Son too give life to whom he willed*.

## On the resurrection of the flesh.

**14.** What, then, of that resurrection of the body? For these who hear and live, whence do they live, except by hearing? For *the friend of the bridegroom stands and hears him, and rejoices with joy because of the bridegroom's voice*[^17], not because of his own voice; that is, by participating, not by existing, they hear and live: and all who hear, live; because all who obey, live. Say something, Lord, also about the resurrection of the flesh. For there have been those who denied it, and said that this is the only resurrection, which takes place through faith. Of which resurrection the Lord just now made mention, and inflamed us, that certain dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and will live. Not that of those who hear some will die and others will live; but all who hear will live, because all who obey will live. See, we see the resurrection of the mind: let us not, then, lose faith about the resurrection of the flesh. And unless you, Lord Jesus, speak of it, whom shall we set against the contradictors?

For all the sects which have presumed to insinuate some religion into men have not denied this resurrection of minds: lest it be said to them, If the soul does not rise, why do you speak to me? what do you wish to do in me? if you do not make from worse a better, why do you speak? if you do not make from the unjust a just man, why do you speak? but if you make from the unjust a just man, from the ungodly a devout one, from the foolish a wise one, you confess that my soul rises, if I obey you, if I believe you.

Therefore, wishing to be believed, all who established a sect even of some false religion could not deny this resurrection of minds; all agreed about it: but many denied the resurrection of the flesh; they said the resurrection had already taken place in faith. Such men the Apostle resists, saying: *Among whom are Hymenaeus and Philetus, who have strayed concerning the truth, saying that the resurrection has already taken place, and they overturn the faith of some*[^18]. They said the resurrection had already taken place, but in such a way that no other was to be hoped for: and they reproved men who hoped for the resurrection of the flesh, as though the resurrection that had been promised were already, by believing, fulfilled in the mind.

The Apostle reproves them.

Why does he reprove them? Were they not saying what the Lord just now was speaking, *An hour is coming, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God; and those who have heard will live*?

But I am still speaking about the life of minds, Jesus says to you; I am not yet speaking about the life of bodies; but I am speaking about the life of the life of bodies—that is, of souls, in which is the life of bodies: for I know there are bodies lying in tombs, I know too that your bodies will be in tombs; I am not yet speaking of that resurrection: I am speaking of this one; in this one rise, lest in that one you rise to punishment. But that you may know that I am speaking of that one, what do I add? *For as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself*[^19]. This life, which the Father is, which the Son is, to what does it pertain? to the soul, or to the body? For the body does not perceive that life of wisdom, but the rational mind. For not every soul can perceive wisdom. For the beast too has a soul; but the soul of the beast cannot perceive wisdom. The human soul, then, can perceive this life which the Father has in himself, and granted the Son to have life in himself; because that is *the true light which enlightens*—not every soul, but *every man coming into this world*[^20]. When, then, I speak to the mind itself, let it hear, that is, obey, and live.

**15.** Do not, then, Lord, be silent about the resurrection of the flesh; lest men not believe it, and we remain arguers, not preachers. Therefore, *as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself*. Let those who hear understand, let them believe that they may understand, let them obey that they may live. Let them hear yet another thing, lest they suppose the resurrection to be finished here. *And he has given him power also to execute judgment*[^21].

Who?

The Father.

To whom did he give it?

To the Son. For to him to whom he granted to have life in himself, he gave power also to execute judgment. *Because he is the Son of man*[^22]. For this Christ is both the Son of God and the Son of man. *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; this was in the beginning with God*[^23]. See how he granted him to have life in himself. But because *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^24], made man of the Virgin Mary, he is the Son of man.

Accordingly, because he is the Son of man, what did he receive?

Power to execute judgment.

What judgment?

At the end of the world: and there will be the resurrection of the dead, but of bodies. Souls, then, God raises, through Christ the Son of God; bodies God raises, through the same Christ the Son of man. *He gave him power.* This power he would not have unless he received it, and were a man without power.

But he himself is the Son of man, who is also the Son of God. For by clinging to the unity of person, the Son of man to the Son of God, one person was made, and the same one is the Son of God who is also the Son of man. But what he has on account of what, must be distinguished. The Son of man has a soul, has a body. The Son of God, who is the Word of God, has a man, as the soul has a body. As the soul having a body does not make two persons, but one man; so the Word having a man does not make two persons, but one Christ. What is a man? A rational soul having a body. What is Christ? The Word of God having a man. I see of what things I speak, and who speaks, and to whom I speak.

## On the judgment.

**16.** Now hear about the resurrection of bodies—not me, but the Lord about to speak, for the sake of those who have risen by rising from death, by clinging to life.

To what life?

To one that does not know death.

Why does it not know death?

Because it does not know changeableness.

Why does it not know changeableness?

Because it is life in itself. *And he gave him power also to execute judgment, because he is the Son of man.*

What judgment, what kind of judgment?

*Do not marvel at this*, because I said, He gave him power also to execute judgment.

*Because an hour is coming*[^25]. He did not add, *and now is*: therefore he wishes to suggest a certain hour at the end of the world. There is an hour now, that the dead may rise; there will be an hour at the end of the world, that the dead may rise: but let them rise now in mind, then in flesh; let them rise now in mind through the Word of God the Son of God, let them rise then in flesh through the Word of God made flesh, the Son of man. For not even to the judgment of the living and the dead will the Father himself come: nor yet does the Father withdraw from the Son.

How, then, will he himself not come?

Because he himself will not be seen in the judgment. *They will look on him whom they have pierced*[^26]. That form will be the judge, which stood beneath the judge; that will judge which was judged: for it was judged unjustly, it will judge justly.

The form of a servant, then, will come, and it itself will appear. For how would the form of God appear to the just and the unjust? For if the judgment took place only among the just alone, the form of God would appear as to the just: but because the judgment is to be of the just and the unjust, and it is not permitted that the unjust should see God—*for blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God*[^27]—such a judge will appear as can be seen both by those whom he will crown and by those whom he will condemn. The form of a servant, then, will be seen; the form of God will be hidden. The Son of God will be hidden in the servant, and the Son of man will appear; because he gave him power also to execute judgment, because he is the Son of man. And because he alone will appear in the form of a servant, but the Father will not appear, because he is not clothed with the form of a servant; therefore he said above, *The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*[^28].

It was well deferred, then, that he himself should be the expositor who was the proposer. For above it was hidden; now, I think, it is manifest that he gave him power also to execute judgment, because the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son: because the judgment is to be through that form which the Father does not have. And what kind of judgment? *Do not marvel at this*; because *an hour is coming*: not the one that now is, that souls may rise; but the one that is to come, that bodies may rise.

**17.** Let him say this more expressly, that the heretic who denies the resurrection of the body may find no slander—although the understanding now grows clear. When above it had been said, *An hour is coming*, he added, *and now is*: but now, *An hour is coming*; he did not add, *and now is*. Yet let him break, by the open truth, all the handles, all the little keys of slanders, all the knots of the snares. *Do not marvel at this; because an hour is coming, in which all who are in the tombs*[^29]. What is more evident? what more express? Bodies are in tombs: souls are not in tombs, neither of the just nor of the unjust. The soul of the just man was in Abraham's bosom, the soul of the unjust man was tormented in hell[^30]: in the tomb, neither the one nor the other. Above, when he said, *An hour is coming, and now is*; I beg you, attend. You know, brothers, that to the bread of the belly one comes with labor; how much more to the bread of the mind? With labor you stand and hear; but we with greater labor stand and speak. If we labor for your sake, ought you not to labor together for the sake of those same selves of yours?

Above, then, when he said, *An hour is coming*, and added, *and now is*, what did he subjoin? *When the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who have heard will live*. He did not say, all the dead will hear, and those who have heard will live: for he wished the unjust dead to be understood. And do all the unjust hearken to the Gospel? The Apostle says plainly: *But not all hearken to the Gospel*[^31]. Yet those who hear will live; because all who hearken to the Gospel will pass to eternal life through faith: yet not all hearken, and this is now. But at the end, all who are in the tombs—that is, the just and the unjust—will hear his voice, and will come forth[^32]. How is it that he was unwilling to say, and will live? For all will come forth, but not all will live. For in what he said above, *And those who have heard will live*; in that very hearkening he wished eternal and blessed life to be understood, which not all will have who come forth from the tombs. Now, then, both by the mention of tombs and by the express coming forth from the tombs, we plainly understand the resurrection of bodies.

**18.** *All will hear his voice, and will come forth.*

And where is the judgment, if all will hear, and all will come forth? It is as if all is confused; I see nothing distinguished. Surely you received power to judge, because you are the Son of man; behold, you will be present at the judgment, bodies will rise: say something about the judgment itself, that is, about the separation of the evil and the good.

And hear this too: *Those who have done good, to the resurrection of life; but those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment*[^33].

Above, when he was speaking of the resurrection of minds and souls, did he make a distinction? But all who hear will live; because by hearkening they will live. But in rising and coming forth from the tombs, not all will go to eternal life, but those who have done well: but those who have done ill, to judgment. For here he put "judgment" for "punishment." And there will be a separation, and not such as it is now. For even now we are separated, not in places, but in conduct, in affections, in desires, in faith, in hope, in love. For we live together with the unjust; but the life of all is not one: in hiddenness we are separated, in hiddenness we are set apart; like grains on the threshing floor, not like grains in the barn. And the grains are both separated on the threshing floor and mixed: they are separated, when they are stripped from the chaff; they are mixed, because they are not yet winnowed.

Then the separation will be open, as of conduct, so also of life; as of wisdom, so also of bodies. Those who have done well will go to live with the angels of God; those who have done ill, to be tormented with the devil and his angels. And the form of a servant will pass away. For to this it had presented itself, that it might execute judgment: after the judgment it will go on from here, will lead with itself the body whose head it is, and will offer the kingdom to God. Then plainly will be seen that form of God, which could not be seen by the unjust, to whose sight the form of a servant was to be exhibited.

He says elsewhere thus: *These will go into eternal burning* (of certain ones on the left); *but the just into eternal life*[^34]: of which he says in another place, *But this is eternal life, that they may know you the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*[^35]. Then there will appear the one who, *though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God*[^36]: then he will show himself as he promised he would show himself to those who love him. *For he who loves me*, he says, *keeps my commandments; and he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him, and will show myself to him*[^37]. To those to whom he was speaking, he was present: but they saw the form of a servant; the form of God they did not see. By the beast they were being led to the inn to be cured, but when healed they will see; for *I will show*, he says, *myself to him*. How is he shown equal to the Father? When he says to Philip, *He who sees me sees also my Father*[^38].

**19.** *I can do nothing of myself: as I hear, I judge, and my judgment is just*[^39]. Because we were going to say to him: You will judge, and the Father will not judge, because he has given all judgment to the Son; therefore you will not judge according to the Father: he added, *I can do nothing of myself: as I hear, I judge, and my judgment is just; because I do not seek my own will, but the will of him who sent me*[^40]. Surely the Son gives life to whom he will. He does not seek his own will, but the will of him who sent him. Not my own, not my private will; not mine, not the Son of man's; not mine, which resists God.

For men do their own will, not God's, when they do what they will, not what God commands: but when they so do what they will that they nevertheless follow the will of God, they do not do their own will, although they do what they will. Do willingly what you are commanded; and so you will both do this which you will, and you will not do your own will, but that of the one who commands.

**20.** What then? *As I hear, so I judge.* The Son hears, and the Father shows him, and the Son sees the Father doing. And these things we had deferred to treat a little more thoroughly, as far as our strength allows, if time after the reading was finished and strength had remained to us. If I were to say that I can still speak, perhaps you can no longer hear. Again, perhaps out of eagerness for hearing you say: We can. It is better, then, that I confess my weakness, that, now wearied, I cannot speak, than that, you being now well satisfied, I should still pour in what you may not digest well. Accordingly, of this promise, which I had deferred to today's time, if there had been time to spare, hold me, with the Lord's help, a debtor for tomorrow.
