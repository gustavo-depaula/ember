# Tractate 66

## *Peter says to him: Why can I not follow you now? I will lay down my life for you* (Jn 13:36-38).

## Before the death and resurrection of the Lord, Peter both died by denying, and came to life again by weeping; but he died because he himself proudly presumed, and came to life again because the Lord kindly looked upon him.

**1.** When the Lord Jesus was commending to the disciples the holy love by which they should love one another: *Simon Peter says to him: Lord, where are you going?*[^1] The disciple surely said this to his Master, the servant to his Lord, as though ready to follow. And therefore the Lord, who saw his mind, why he asked this, answered him thus: *Where I go, you cannot now follow me*: as if he said: Because of what you ask, you cannot now. He does not say, you cannot; but *you cannot now*: he brought a delay, he did not take away hope; and the same hope which he did not take away, but rather gave, he confirmed by the next words, adding and saying: *but you will follow afterward.* Why do you hasten, Peter? The rock has not yet made you firm with its spirit. Do not be lifted up by presuming, *you cannot now*: do not be cast down by despairing, *you will follow afterward.*

But what does he still say? *Why can I not follow you now? I will lay down my life for you*[^2]. He saw what desire was in his mind; what strength, he did not see. The weak man was boasting of his will, but the physician was looking at his condition; the one was promising, the other foreknowing: the one who did not know was daring, the one who foreknew was teaching. How much had Peter taken upon himself, looking at what he willed, ignorant of what he could do? How much had he taken upon himself, that when the Lord had come to lay down his life for his friends, and through this for him too, he should be confident to offer this to the Lord: and, Christ's life not yet laid down for him, he should promise that he would lay down his own life for Christ? *Jesus answered:* *Will you lay down your life for me?* Will you do for me what I have not yet done for you? *Will you lay down your life for me?* Can you go before, who cannot follow? Why do you presume so much? what do you think of yourself? what do you believe yourself to be? Hear what you are: *Amen, amen I say to you; the cock will not crow, until you deny me three times*[^3]. Behold how quickly you will appear to yourself, you who speak great things, and do not know yourself to be a little one. You who promise me your death will deny your life three times. You who already think you can die for me, first live for yourself: for by fearing the death of your flesh, you will bring death upon your soul. For as great a life as it is to confess Christ, so great a death is it to deny Christ.

## Peter died by denying, and came to life again by weeping.

**2.** Or did the apostle Peter, as some by a perverse favor strive to excuse him, not deny Christ, because, when asked by the maidservant, he answered that he did not know the man, as the other evangelists testify more expressly? As though one who denies the man Christ does not deny Christ; and denies in him that which he became for our sake, lest what had made us should perish. He, then, who so confesses Christ as God that he denies the man, for him Christ did not die; because according to the man Christ died. He who denies the man Christ is not reconciled to God through the mediator. *For there is one God, and one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^4]. He who denies the man Christ is not justified; *because as by the disobedience of one man many were made sinners; so also by the obedience of one man many will be made righteous*[^5]. He who denies the man Christ will not rise in the resurrection of life; *because by a man came death, and by a man the resurrection of the dead: for as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive*[^6]. But by what is he the head of the Church, except by the man, that the Word was made flesh? that is, the only-begotten God of God the Father was made man. How, then, is he in the body of Christ who denies the man Christ? For he who denies the head, how is he a member? But why should I linger over many things, when the Lord himself has taken away all the windings of human argumentation? For he did not say: The cock will not crow until you deny the man; or, as he was wont to speak with men by a more familiar condescension: The cock will not crow until you deny the Son of man three times: but he said, *until you deny me three times.* What is *me,* but what he was? and what was he but Christ?

Whatever of him, then, he denied, he denied him himself, he denied Christ, he denied his Lord God. For his fellow disciple Thomas too, when he exclaimed: *My Lord and my God,* touched not the Word, but the flesh; he handled with curious hands not the incorporeal nature of God, but the body of the man. He touched, then, the man, and yet recognized God. If, then, what the latter touched, that Peter denied; what the latter exclaimed, against that Peter offended. *The cock will not crow, until you deny me three times.* Though you say: *I do not know the man*; though you say: *Man, I do not know what you say*; though you say: *I am not of his disciples*: you will deny me. If—which it is wrong to doubt—Christ said this, and truly foretold it, beyond doubt Peter denied Christ. Let us not accuse Christ when we defend Peter. Let weakness acknowledge its sin; for the truth does not lie. Peter's weakness indeed acknowledged its sin, fully acknowledged it; and how much evil it committed by denying Christ, it showed by weeping. He himself refutes his defenders, and, that he may convict them, he produces his tears as witnesses.

Nor, when we say these things, does it delight us to accuse the first of the Apostles: but by looking at him we ought to be admonished that no man should trust in human strength. For what else pertained to our teacher and Savior, but that he should show, in the very first of the Apostles, by example, that no one ought in any way to presume of himself? In Peter's soul, then, came to pass what he was offering in his body. Yet he did not go before for the Lord, as he rashly presumed, but otherwise than he thought. For before the death and resurrection of the Lord, he both died by denying, and came to life again by weeping: but he died because he himself proudly presumed; and came to life again because the Lord kindly looked upon him.
