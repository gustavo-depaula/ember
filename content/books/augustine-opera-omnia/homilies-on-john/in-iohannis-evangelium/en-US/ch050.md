# Tractate 50

## *Jesus, six days before the Passover, came to Bethany. They made him a supper there* (Jn 11:55-56 and 12:1-11).

## What did our Lord Jesus Christ wish to admonish his church, when he willed to have one lost man among the twelve, except that we should bear with the wicked and not divide the body of Christ?

**1.** Today's reading of the holy Gospel, about which we are to speak what the Lord will give, follows yesterday's, about which we spoke what the Lord gave. Some things in the Scriptures are so plain that they call for a hearer rather than an expositor; on these we ought not to linger, so that there may be time enough for the necessary things on which we must linger.

## The Lord came that we might have the true Passover.

**2.** *The Passover of the Jews, then, was near*[^1]. The Jews willed to have that feast day bloodstained with the Lord's blood. On that feast day was slain the Lamb who consecrated that same feast day for us by his blood. There was a plan among the Jews about killing Jesus: he who had come from heaven to suffer willed to draw near to the place of his passion, because the hour of his passion was at hand.

*Many therefore went up to Jerusalem from the region before the Passover, to sanctify themselves.* The Jews did this according to the Lord's command, given through holy Moses in the Law, that on the feast day which was the Passover all should gather from every side, and be sanctified by the celebration of that day. But that celebration was a shadow of what was to come. How was it a shadow of what was to come? It was a prophecy of Christ who was to come, a prophecy of one who would suffer for us on that day—so that the shadow might pass and the light come, so that the foreshadowing might pass and the truth be held fast. The Jews, then, had the Passover in shadow, we have it in light. For what need was there that the Lord should command them to slay a sheep on that very feast day, except that he was the one of whom it was prophesied: *Like a sheep he was led to the slaughter*[^2]? With the blood of the slain animal the doorposts of the Jews were marked; with the blood of Christ our foreheads are marked. And that marking, because it was a foreshadowing, was said to keep the destroyer from the houses that were marked: the sign of Christ keeps the destroyer from us, if our heart receives the Savior. Why have I said this? Because many have their doorposts marked, and within no dweller abides: they easily have the sign of Christ on their forehead, and in their heart they do not receive the word of Christ. Therefore I have said, brothers, what I repeat: the sign of Christ keeps the destroyer from us, if our heart has Christ as its indweller.

I have said these things lest anyone should perhaps wonder what these feasts of the Jews meant. The Lord came, then, as it were to be a victim, that we might have the true Passover, when we celebrate his passion as the immolation of the sheep.

**3.** *They sought Jesus, therefore*[^3]: but in an evil way. For blessed are those who seek Jesus, but in a good way. Those men sought Jesus so that neither they themselves nor we should have him; but we received him as he departed from them. Those who seek are blamed, and those who seek are praised: for the mind of the seeker finds either praise or condemnation. For you have it also in the Psalms: *Let them be confounded and put to shame who seek my soul*[^4]: these are they who were seeking in an evil way. But in another place he says: *Flight has perished from me, and there is no one who seeks my soul*[^5]. Those who sought are faulted, and those who did not seek are faulted.

Therefore let us seek Christ that we may have him; let us seek that we may hold him, but not that we may kill him: for those men too sought that they might hold him, but so as soon not to have him. *They sought him, therefore, and spoke among themselves: What do you think, that he is not coming to the feast?*[^6]

## Let them come to the Church and hear where Christ is.

**4.** *Now the chief priests and the Pharisees had given a commandment, that if anyone knew where he was, he should disclose it, so that they might arrest him*[^7]. Let us now disclose to the Jews where Christ is. Would that they were willing to hear and to arrest him, whoever they are of the seed of those who had given the commandment that it should be disclosed to them where Christ was. Let them come to the Church, let them hear where Christ is, and arrest him. Let them hear from us, let them hear from the Gospel. He was slain by their forefathers, he was buried, he rose again, he was recognized by his disciples, before their eyes he ascended into heaven, there he sits at the right hand of the Father; he who was judged is to come as judge: let them hear, and hold him fast. They answer: How shall I hold one who is absent? how shall I stretch out my hand into heaven, to hold one who sits there? Send your faith, and you have held him. Your forefathers held him in the flesh; you, hold him in the heart: for Christ, though absent, is also present. Unless he were present, he could not be held by us ourselves. But since it is true, what he says: *Behold, I am with you always, to the consummation of the age*[^8]; both he has gone and he is here; both he has returned and he does not forsake us: for he carried his body into heaven, he did not take his majesty from the world.

**5.** *Jesus therefore, six days before the Passover, came to Bethany, where Lazarus had been dead, whom Jesus raised up. And they made him a supper there, and Martha served; but Lazarus was one of those who reclined at table*[^9]. Lest men should think that what had happened was an apparition, because a dead man had risen, he was one of those reclining at table; he was living, he was speaking, he was feasting: the truth was being shown, the unbelief of the Jews was being confounded. The Lord, then, reclined at table with Lazarus and the others, and Martha, one of the sisters of Lazarus, served.

## We have heard the deed; let us seek out the mystery.

**6.** But *Mary,* the other sister of Lazarus, *took a pound of costly ointment of pure nard, anointed the feet of Jesus, and wiped his feet with her hair, and the house was filled with the fragrance of the ointment*[^10]. We have heard the deed; let us seek out the mystery. Whatever faithful soul you wish to be, anoint with Mary the feet of the Lord with the costly ointment. That ointment was righteousness, and therefore it was a pound; and it was ointment of costly pure nard. As for his saying *pure* (pisticus), we ought to believe it to be some place from which this costly ointment came; yet this too is not without meaning, and accords very well with a mystery. In Greek, "pistis" means faith. You were seeking to work righteousness: the righteous one lives by faith.

Anoint the feet of Jesus: by living well follow in the Lord's footsteps. Wipe them with your hair: if you have things to spare, give to the poor, and you have wiped the feet of the Lord; for the hairs seem to be the body's superfluity. You have something to do with your superfluities: to you they are superfluities, but to the Lord's feet they are necessities. Perhaps on earth the Lord's feet are in need. For of whom but of his members is he going to say in the end: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^11]? You spent your superfluities, but you served my feet.

**7.** Now *the house was filled with the fragrance*; the world was filled with good repute: for a good fragrance is a good reputation. Those who live badly and are called Christians do injury to Christ: of such it is said that through them *the name of the Lord is blasphemed*[^12]. If through such men the name of God is blasphemed, through good men the name of the Lord is praised. Hear the Apostle: *We are the good fragrance of Christ,* he says, *in every place.* It is said also in the Song of Songs: *Your name is ointment poured out*[^13]. Call your attention back to the Apostle: *We are,* he says, *the good fragrance of Christ in every place, both among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing; to some we are the fragrance of life unto life, to others the fragrance of death unto death: and who is sufficient for these things*[^14]?

The present reading of the holy Gospel gives us occasion to speak of this fragrance in such a way that it may both be sufficiently said by us and diligently heard by you, since the Apostle himself says: *And who is sufficient for these things?* So, that we may try to speak of it, are we sufficient, or are you sufficient to hear these things? We indeed are not sufficient; but he is sufficient who deigns to say through us what it will profit you to hear.

Behold, the Apostle is a *good fragrance,* as he himself says: but this same good fragrance *to some* is *the fragrance of life unto life, but to others the fragrance of death unto death*; yet it is a good fragrance. For does he say: To some we are a good fragrance unto life, to others a bad fragrance unto death? He called himself a good fragrance, not a bad one; and the same good fragrance he said was to some unto life, to others unto death. Happy are those who live by the good fragrance: but what is more unhappy than those who die by the good fragrance?

**8.** And who is it, someone says, whom the good fragrance kills? This is what the Apostle says: *And who is sufficient for these things?* How God does these things in wondrous ways, so that by the good fragrance the good live and the wicked die; how it may be—so far as the Lord deigns to inspire (for perhaps there lies hidden there a deeper understanding that cannot be penetrated by me)—yet so far as I have been able to penetrate, it ought not to be denied you.

The apostle Paul, who acted well, lived well, preached righteousness in word and demonstrated it in deed, a wondrous teacher, a faithful steward—repute spread him abroad everywhere: some loved him, some envied him. For he himself says in a certain place of some that they preached Christ not chastely but out of envy; *supposing,* he says, *that they were stirring up affliction for my chains.* But what does he say? *Whether in pretense or in truth, let Christ be proclaimed*[^15]. They proclaim who love me, they proclaim who envy me; those live by the good fragrance, and those die by the good fragrance; yet, with both preaching, let the name of Christ be proclaimed, let the world be filled with the best fragrance.

You loved one who acted well, you lived by the good fragrance: you envied one who acted well, you died by the good fragrance. Because you willed to die, have you therefore made that fragrance to be bad? Do not envy, and the good fragrance will not kill you.

**9.** Finally, hear here too, from this ointment, how to some it was a good fragrance unto life, to others a good fragrance unto death. After devout Mary had done this in homage to the Lord, at once *one of his disciples, Judas Iscariot, who was about to betray him, said: Why was this ointment not sold for three hundred denarii, and given to the poor*[^16]? Woe to you, wretch! the good fragrance has killed you. Why he said this, the holy Evangelist disclosed. For we ourselves would have supposed, if his mind had not been laid open to us through the Gospel, that he could have said this out of care for the poor. It is not so. But what? Hear the truthful witness: *Now he said this, not because he cared about the poor, but because he was a thief, and having the moneybag, carried what was put into it*[^17]. Did he carry it, or carry it off? He carried it as part of his office, he carried it off as theft.

## The wicked are to be borne with, lest the body of Christ be divided.

**10.** Behold, hear how this Judas was not made perverse only then, when, corrupted by the Jews, he betrayed the Lord. For very many who are careless about the Gospel suppose that Judas perished only then, when he received money from the Jews to betray the Lord. He did not perish then; he was already a thief, and was following the Lord as one already lost; for he was following not with the heart but with the body. He was filling up the number twelve of the Apostles, he did not have the apostolic blessedness; he had been the twelfth in appearance. When he withdrew, and another succeeded, the apostolic truth was filled up, and the integrity of the number remained.

What then did our Lord Jesus Christ wish, my brothers, to admonish his Church, when he willed to have one lost man among the twelve, except that we should bear with the wicked and not divide the body of Christ? Behold, Judas is among the holy ones; behold, Judas is a thief, and—that you may not make light of it—a thief and a sacrilegious one, not just any sort of thief: a thief of the moneybags, but the Lord's moneybags; of the moneybags, but holy ones. If crimes are distinguished in a court of law, what sort of theft and embezzlement—for embezzlement is the name for theft from public property, and theft of private property is not judged the same as theft of public property—how much more severely must the sacrilegious thief be judged, who has dared to take not from just anywhere, but from the Church? Whoever steals anything from the Church is to be compared with the lost Judas.

Such was this Judas, and yet he went in and out with the eleven holy disciples. He came alike to the Lord's very supper: he could keep company with them, he could not defile them. Of one bread both Peter and Judas received, and yet what part has the faithful with the unbeliever? For Peter received unto life, Judas unto death. For as that fragrance was good, so was that food good. As, therefore, the good fragrance, so also the good food gives life to the good and brings death to the wicked. *For he who eats unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself*[^18]: judgment to himself, not to you. If judgment to himself, not to you, then bear with the wicked, you who are good, that you may come to the rewards of the good, and not be cast into the punishment of the wicked.

**11.** Receive the example of the Lord keeping company on earth. Why did he have moneybags, he whom the angels served, except because his Church was going to have its own moneybags? Why did he admit a thief, except that his Church might bear patiently with thieves? But that man, who had been accustomed to take money from the moneybags, did not hesitate, when he had received money, to sell the Lord himself. Let us see what the Lord answers to this. See, brothers: he does not say to him, You say these things because of your thefts. He knew the thief, and did not expose him; but rather bore with him, and was showing us an example of patience for putting up with the wicked in the Church. *Jesus therefore said to him: Let her alone, that she may keep it for the day of my burial*[^19]. He announced that he was going to die.

## When he received the keys, Peter signified the Church.

**12.** But what is it that follows? *For the poor you will always have with you, but me you will not always have*[^20]. We understand indeed: *The poor you will always have*: what he said is true. When is the Church without the poor? But *me you will not always have,* what does that mean? How is it to be understood: *me you will not always have*? Do not be alarmed; it was said to Judas. Why, then, did he not say, you (singular) will have, but *you (plural) will have*? Because Judas is not one alone. The one wicked man signifies the body of the wicked, just as Peter signifies the body of the good—rather, the body of the Church, but in its good members. For if there were not in Peter the mystery of the Church, the Lord would not say to him: *To you I will give the keys of the kingdom of heaven: whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven also; and whatever you bind on earth shall be bound in heaven also*[^21]. If this was said to Peter alone, then the Church does not do this. But if it is done in the Church as well, so that what are bound on earth are bound in heaven, and what are loosed on earth are loosed in heaven—because when the Church excommunicates, the excommunicated person is bound in heaven; when one is reconciled by the Church, the reconciled person is loosed in heaven—if, then, this is done in the Church, then when Peter received the keys he signified the holy Church.

If in the person of Peter the good in the Church were signified, in the person of Judas the wicked in the Church were signified; to them it was said: *me you will not always have.* For what is *not always*? And what is "always"? If you are good, if you belong to the body that Peter signifies, you have Christ both in the present and in the future: in the present through faith, in the present through the sign, in the present through the sacrament of Baptism, in the present through the food and drink of the altar. You have Christ in the present, but you will have him always; because when you depart from here, you will come to him who said to the thief: *Today you will be with me in paradise*[^22].

But if you conduct yourself badly, you seem to have Christ in the present, because you enter the Church, you mark yourself with the sign of Christ, you are baptized with the baptism of Christ, you mingle yourself with the members of Christ, you approach the altar of Christ: in the present you have Christ, but by living badly you will not have him always.

**13.** It can also be understood thus: *The poor you will always have with you, but me you will not always have.* Let the good too take this, but let them not be anxious: for he was speaking of the presence of his body. For according to his majesty, according to his providence, according to his ineffable and invisible grace, that is fulfilled which was said by him: *Behold, I am with you always, to the consummation of the age*[^23]. But according to the flesh which the Word assumed, according to the fact that he was born of the Virgin, that he was seized by the Jews, that he was fastened to the wood, that he was taken down from the cross, that he was wrapped in linen cloths, that he was laid in the tomb, that he was made manifest in the resurrection—*me you will not always have with you.* Why? Because according to the presence of his body he kept company with his disciples forty days, and as they escorted him by seeing, not by following, he ascended into heaven, and is not here. For he is there, he sits at the right hand of the Father; and he is here, for he has not withdrawn the presence of his majesty. Or otherwise: according to the presence of his majesty we always have Christ; according to the presence of the flesh it was rightly said to the disciples: *me you will not always have.* For the Church had him according to the presence of the flesh for a few days; now it holds him by faith, it does not see him with the eyes. Therefore, whether it was said in this way: *me you will not always have,* there is now, as I think, no question at all, for it has been resolved in two ways.

**14.** Let us hear the few things that remain: *A great crowd of the Jews therefore learned that he was there; and they came, not for the sake of Jesus only, but that they might see Lazarus, whom he had raised from the dead*[^24]. Curiosity brought them, not love: they came, and they saw. Hear the foolish plan of empty vanity. When they had seen Lazarus raised up—because so great a miracle of the Lord was spread abroad by such evidence, declared by such manifestation, that they could neither hide what had been done nor deny it—see what they devised. *Now the chief priests took counsel to put Lazarus also to death; because on account of him many of the Jews were going away and believing in Jesus*[^25]. O foolish thought, and blind savagery! Could the Lord Christ, who was able to raise up a dead man, not raise up one who had been killed? When you were bringing death upon Lazarus, were you taking away the Lord's power? If a dead man seems to you one thing and a man killed another, behold, the Lord did both, and raised up both Lazarus who was dead, and himself who was killed.
