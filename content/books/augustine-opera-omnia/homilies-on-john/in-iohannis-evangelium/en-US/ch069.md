# Tractate 69

## *Thomas says to him: Lord, we do not know where you are going; and how can we know the way?* (Jn 14:3-6).

## The apostle Thomas, that he might question you, had you before him, and yet would not understand you unless he had you within him; I question you because I know you to be above me; but I question, as far as I can pour out my soul above me, where I may hear you not sounding and yet teaching.

**1.** Now it is the time, dearly beloved, that, as far as we are able, we should understand from the Lord's later words his earlier ones, and from those that follow those that precede, in that which you heard answered to the apostle Thomas questioning. For the Lord had said above, when he spoke of the mansions, which he both said are in his Father's house, and that he goes to prepare them; where we understood both that those mansions already are in predestination, and that they are being prepared while the hearts of those who will dwell there are cleansed through faith, since the very house of God is they themselves; and what else is it to abide in the house of God, but to be in the people of God, since the same people is in God, and God in it?

In order to prepare this, the Lord went away; that, by believing in him who is not seen, the mansion, which in the form will be always to come, may now through faith be prepared: on account of this, then, he had said: *And if I go, and prepare a place for you, I come again, and will receive you to myself, that where I am you too may be. And where I go you know, and the way you know.* To this *Thomas says to him: Lord, we do not know where you are going; and how can we know the way*? The Lord had said they knew both, this one says they know neither, both the place to which one goes, and the way by which one goes. But he does not know how to lie: therefore they did know, and did not know that they knew. Let him convince them that they already know what they think they still do not know. *Jesus says to him: I am the way, and the truth, and the life*[^1]. What is it, brothers? Behold, we have heard the disciple questioning, we have heard also the master teaching, and we do not yet grasp, even after the voice that sounds, the meaning that lies hidden. But what can we not grasp? Could his Apostles, with whom he was speaking, say to him: We do not know you? Accordingly, if they knew him, and he himself is the way, they knew the way: if they knew him, and he himself is the truth, they knew the truth: if they knew him, and he himself is the life, they knew the life. Behold, they were convicted of knowing what they did not know that they knew.

## He went through himself and to himself and to the Father.

**2.** What, then, did we too not grasp in this discourse? What do you think, my brothers, except that he said: *And where I go you know, and the way you know*? And behold, we have learned that they knew the way, because they knew him who is the way: but the way is that by which one goes; is the way also that to which one goes? Now he had said that they knew both, both where he goes and the way. There was need, then, that he say: *I am the way,* in order to show that those who knew him knew the way which they had thought they did not know: but what need was there that he say: *I am the way, and the truth, and the life,* since, the way by which one goes being known, it remained to know where one goes; except that he was going to the truth, going to the life? He was going, then, to himself, through himself. And we, where do we go, but to him? and by what way do we go, but through him? He himself, then, to himself through himself; we to him through him: rather, both to the Father, both he and we. For of himself too he says elsewhere: *I go to the Father*: and in this place, for our sake: *No one,* he says, *comes to the Father except through me*[^2]. And through this both he himself through himself both to himself and to the Father, and we through him both to him and to the Father. Who grasps these things, but he who is spiritually wise? And how little is it that he grasps here, even if he is spiritually wise? Brothers, why do you want these things expounded to you by me? Consider how lofty they are. You see what I am, I see what you are: in all of us *the corruptible body weighs down the soul, and the earthly dwelling presses down the mind that ponders many things*[^3]. Do we think we can say: *To you I have lifted up my soul, you who dwell in heaven*[^4]? But under so great a weight, where we groan being burdened, how shall I lift up my soul, unless he lift it with me who laid down his own for me? Let me say, then, what I can, let him of you grasp it who can. By whose gift I speak, by his gift he grasps who grasps, and by his gift he believes who does not yet grasp. *For unless you believe,* says the prophet, *you will not understand*[^5].

**3.** Tell me, my Lord, what shall I say to your servants, my fellow servants? The apostle Thomas, that he might question you, had you before him; and yet would not understand you, unless he had you within him: I question you, because I know you to be above me; but I question, as far as I can pour out my soul above me, where I may hear you not sounding and yet teaching.

Tell me, I beseech you, how you go to yourself? Did you, in order to come to us, leave yourself: especially since you did not come from yourself, but the Father sent you? I know indeed that you emptied yourself; but because you took the form of a servant, not because you either laid down the form of God, to which you might return, or lost the form which you might receive again: and yet you came, and reached not only to carnal eyes, but even to the hands of men. How, but in the flesh? Through this you came, remaining where you were, through this you returned, not leaving where you had come. If, then, through this you came and returned; through this without doubt you were a way not only for us, by which we might come to you, but also for yourself, by which you might come and return. But when you went to the life, which you yourself are, you surely led that same flesh of yours from death to life. For the Word of God is one thing, the man another: but the Word was made flesh, that is, man. So the person of the Word is not one, that of the man another, since both are Christ, one person: and through this, as, when the flesh died, Christ died; and when the flesh was buried, Christ was buried (for so we believe in the heart unto righteousness, so we make confession with the mouth unto salvation[^6]); so, when the flesh came from death to life, Christ came to life. And because the Word of God is Christ, Christ is life. So in a certain wondrous and ineffable way, he who never laid down or lost himself, came to himself. He had come, as has been said, through the flesh, God to men, the truth to liars: *for God is truthful, but every man a liar*[^7]. And so, when he took away from men, and lifted up his flesh to that place where no one lies; he himself, because the Word was made flesh, through himself, that is, through the flesh, returned to the truth, which he himself is. Which truth indeed, although among liars and in death, he kept: for at some time Christ was dead, but he was never false.

**4.** Receive an example, although different and far unequal, yet somehow tending to the understanding of God, from those things which are placed nearer to God. Behold, I myself, as far as pertains to my mind, since I am this which you too are, if I am silent, I am with myself: but if I speak to you what you may understand, in a certain way I go forth to you, and I do not leave myself, but I both come to you, and do not withdraw from where I go forth. But when I shall have been silent, in a certain way I return to myself; and in a certain way I remain with you, if you hold what you heard in the discourse which I speak. If the image which God made can do this, what can the image of God, God, not made by God, but born of God, do—whose body, by which he went forth to us, and in which he returned from us, did not, like my sound, slip away, but remains there where *he now dies no more, and death will no more have dominion over him*[^8]?

Many things of these words of the Gospel could perhaps still be said and ought to be said: but your hearts are not to be burdened with spiritual foods, however sweet; especially because *the spirit is willing, but the flesh is weak*[^9].
