# Tractate 81

## *Without me you can do nothing*[^1].

## He does not say: without me you can do little; but: *you can do nothing.* Whether little, then, or much, it cannot be done without him, without whom nothing can be done.

**1.** Jesus said that he was the vine, and his disciples the branches, and the Father the vinedresser; on which we have already discussed as we were able. But in this passage, when he was still speaking of himself, who is the vine, and of his branches, that is, the disciples, he says: *Abide in me, and I in you*[^2]. They are not in him in the same way as he is in them. But both are of profit, not to him, but to them. For the branches are in the vine in such a way that they confer nothing on the vine, but receive from it that by which they live; whereas the vine is in the branches in such a way that it furnishes them vital nourishment, and does not take it from them. And through this, both to have Christ abiding in oneself and to abide in Christ is of profit to the disciples in both respects, not to Christ. For when a branch is cut off, another can sprout from the living root; but the one that has been cut off cannot live without the root.

## He who is not in Christ is not a Christian.

**2.** Finally he adds and says: *As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself unless it abides in the vine, so neither can you, unless you abide in me*[^3]. A great commendation of grace, my brothers: it instructs the hearts of the humble, it stops the mouths of the proud. See whom — if they dare — those may answer who, *being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, are not subject to the righteousness of God*[^4]. See whom they may answer who are pleased with themselves and think God not necessary to them for doing good works. Do they not *resist this truth, men corrupt in mind, reprobate concerning the faith*[^5], who answer and speak iniquity, saying: From God we have it that we are men, but from ourselves that we are righteous?

What do you say, you who deceive yourselves — not asserters but headlong wreckers of free choice — from the height of pride, through the emptiness of presumption, into the depths of submersion? For surely it is your voice that says man does righteousness of himself: this is the height of your pride. But the truth contradicts and says: *The branch cannot bear fruit of itself unless it abides in the vine*[^6]. Go now over the precipices, and, having nothing to fix yourselves to, be tossed about by windy talkativeness. These are the emptinesses of your presumption. But see what follows you, and, if there is any sense in you, shudder. For he who thinks he bears fruit of himself is not in the vine; he who is not in the vine is not in Christ; he who is not in Christ is not a Christian. These are the depths of your submersion.

**3.** Again and again consider what the truth further adds and says: *I am,* he says, *the vine, you the branches. He who abides in me, and I in him, he bears much fruit; for without me you can do nothing*[^7]. Lest anyone should think that a branch could bear at least some small fruit of itself, after he had said, *he bears much fruit,* he did not say, because without me you can do little, but, *you can do nothing.* Whether little, then, or much, it cannot be done without him, without whom nothing can be done. For even if a branch bears little, the vinedresser prunes it so that it may bear more; yet unless it abides in the vine and lives from the root, it cannot of itself bear any fruit, however little. But although Christ would not be the vine unless he were man, yet he would not bestow this grace on the branches unless he were also God. But since without this grace there can be no living, just as death too is in the power of free choice: *If anyone does not abide in me,* he says, *he will be cast out like a branch, and will wither; and they will gather him and cast him into the fire, and he burns*[^8]. The wood of the vine, therefore, is the more contemptible if it does not abide in the vine, as it is the more glorious if it abides; finally, as the Lord says of these branches also through the prophet Ezekiel, when cut off they are of use for no purposes of vinedressers, and are assigned to no works of carpenters[^9]. One of two things suits the branch, either the vine or the fire; if it is not in the vine, it will be in the fire: so that it may not be in the fire, let it be in the vine.

**4.** *If you abide in me,* he says, *and my words abide in you, you will ask whatever you will, and it will be done for you*[^10]. For by abiding in Christ, what can they will but what befits Christ? What can they will, abiding in the Savior, but what is not foreign to salvation? For we will one thing because we are in Christ, and another thing because we are still in this world. For from this dwelling in the world it sometimes creeps upon us to ask for that which, without our knowing it, is not expedient for us. But far be it that it should be done for us, if we abide in Christ, who does not do, when we ask, anything but what is expedient for us.

Abiding in him, then, when his words abide in us, we will ask whatever we will, and it will be done for us. For if we ask and it is not done, we are not asking what abiding in him has, nor what his words that abide in us have, but what the desire and weakness of the flesh has — which is not in him, and in which his words do not abide. For surely to his words belongs that prayer which he taught, where we say: *Our Father, who are in heaven*[^11]. Let us not depart in our petitions from the words and the senses of this prayer, and whatever we ask will be done for us. For his words are then to be said to abide in us when we do what he has commanded and love what he has promised; but when his words abide in the memory and are not found in the life, the branch is not reckoned in the vine, because it does not draw life from the root. To this distinction applies what is written: *And to those who keep his commandments in memory, that they may do them*[^12]. For many keep them in memory in order to despise them, or even to deride and assail them. In these the words of Christ do not abide; they touch them in a manner, but do not cohere with them: and therefore they will be to them not for a benefit, but for a testimony. And because they are present in them in such a way as not to abide in them, they are held by them for this end, that by them they may be judged.
