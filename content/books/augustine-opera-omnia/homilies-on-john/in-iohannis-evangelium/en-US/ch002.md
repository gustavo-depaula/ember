# Homily 2

## *There was a man sent from God . . .* [^1]

## If you wish to live devoutly and as Christians, cling to Christ according to that which he was made for us, so that you may arrive at him according to that which he is, and according to that which he was.

**1.** It is good, brothers, that, passing over no passage, we should go through the text of the divine Scriptures, and especially of the holy Gospel, as we are able; and that, according to our capacity, we should be fed, and minister to you that from which we too are fed. We remember that on the past Lord's Day the first chapter was treated, that is: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God. All things were made through him, and without him was nothing made. What was made, in him is life; and the life was the light of men; and the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it* [^2]. Up to this point, I believe, it was treated: let all who were present recall it; and those who were not present, believe us and those who wished to be present. Now then, since we cannot always repeat everything, for the sake of those who wish to hear what follows, and to whom it is a burden if the earlier matters are repeated to the defrauding of the later; let those who were not present deign not to demand the things passed over, but together with those who were present to hear now the things at hand.

## The wood by which we cross the sea.

**2.** There follows: *There was a man sent from God, whose name was John* [^3]. For the things that were said above, dearest brothers, were said of the ineffable divinity of Christ, and almost ineffably. For who will grasp: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God* [^4]? And lest the name of Word become cheap to you, through the habit of daily words: *And the Word was God* [^5]. This Word is the selfsame about which we spoke much yesterday: and may the Lord have granted that, even merely by speaking, we brought something to your hearts. *In the beginning was the Word*. It is the selfsame, it is in the same manner; as it is, so it always is; it cannot be changed: this is 'is.' This is the name he told to his servant Moses: *I am who I am*; and: *He who is sent me* [^6].

Who, then, will grasp this, when you see all mortal things changeable; when you see not only that bodies vary through their qualities—being born, growing, declining, dying—but also that the souls themselves are stretched and torn apart by the affection of diverse wills: when you see that men can both attain wisdom, if they bring themselves near to its light and warmth, and can lose wisdom, if by evil affection they withdraw from it? When, therefore, you see that all these things are changeable, what is that which 'is,' except that which transcends all the things that so exist that they do not truly exist? Who, then, will grasp this? Or who, however he may strain the powers of his mind to attain, as he can, that which 'is,' will be able to arrive at that which he has somehow attained with his mind?

For it is just as if someone were to see his homeland from afar, and a sea lay between; he sees where he is to go, but he has no way to go. So we wish to arrive at that stability of ours, where what is, is, because this alone always is in such a way as it is; the sea of this world lies between, along which we go, even though we now see where we are going: for many do not even see where they are going. Therefore, that there might also be a way by which we might go, he came from there, the one to whom we wished to go. And what did he do? He set up the wood by which we might cross the sea. For no one can cross the sea of this world unless carried by the cross of Christ. This cross even one weak of eyes sometimes embraces: and he who does not see from afar where he is going, let him not depart from it, and it will carry him through.

**3.** And so, my brothers, let me impress this upon your hearts: if you wish to live devoutly and as Christians, cling to Christ according to that which he was made for us, so that you may arrive at him according to that which he is, and according to that which he was. He drew near, that this might be done for us; because for us he was made this, by which the weak might be carried, and might cross the sea of the world, and arrive at the homeland; where there will no longer be need of a ship, because no sea is crossed. It is better, then, not to see with the mind that which is, and yet not to depart from the cross of Christ, than to see it with the mind and despise the cross of Christ. It is good, and best beyond this, if it can be done, both that one should see where he must go, and should hold to that by which he is carried as he goes.

This the great minds of the mountains, who were called mountains, were able to do—those whom the light of righteousness especially illuminates: they were able, and they saw that which is. For John, seeing, said: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^7]. They saw this, and in order to arrive at that which they saw from afar, they did not depart from the cross of Christ, and they did not despise the humility of Christ. But the little ones, who cannot understand this, not departing from the cross and passion and resurrection of Christ, are carried in that same ship to that which they do not see—the ship in which those too who see arrive.

## O proud wisdom!

**4.** But indeed certain philosophers of this world arose and sought the Creator through the creature: for he can be found through the creature, as the Apostle plainly says: *For his invisible things, from the foundation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood through the things that have been made; his eternal power also and divinity, so that they are without excuse* [^8]. And there follows: *Because, when they knew God*: he did not say, Because they did not know him; but, *Because, when they knew God, they did not glorify him as God, or give thanks; but they became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened* [^9]. Whence was it darkened? It follows, and he says more openly: *For saying that they were wise, they became fools* [^10].

They saw where they were to come; but, ungrateful to the one who granted them what they saw, they wished to attribute to themselves what they saw; and, made proud, they lost what they were seeing, and turned from there to idols and images and the worship of demons, to adore the creature and to despise the Creator. But it was already as men cast down that they did these things: yet in order that they might be cast down, they became proud; and when they were proud, they said that they were wise. These, then, of whom he said, *When they knew God*, saw this that John says, that all things were made through the Word of God. For these things too are found in the books of the philosophers: and that God has an only-begotten Son, through whom all things are. They were able to see that which is, but they saw it from afar: they were unwilling to hold to the humility of Christ, in which ship they would have safely arrived at that which they were able to see from afar; and the cross of Christ became cheap to them.

The sea must be crossed, and you despise the wood? O proud wisdom! you mock the crucified Christ; he is the very one you saw from afar: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God* [^11]. But why was he crucified? Because the wood of his humility was necessary for you. For you had swelled with pride, and had been cast far from that homeland; and the way is cut off by the waves of this world, and there is no way to cross to the homeland unless you are carried by the wood. Ungrateful one, you mock him who came to you that you might return! He himself was made the way, and that through the sea: therefore he walked on the sea, to show that there is a way in the sea. But you, who cannot walk on the sea as he did, let yourself be carried by the ship, be carried by the wood: believe in the crucified, and you will be able to arrive.

For your sake he was crucified, to teach humility; and because, if he had come thus as God, he would not have been recognized. For if he had come thus as God, he would not have come to those who could not see God. For according to that which he is as God, he neither comes nor departs; since he is everywhere present and is contained in no place. But according to what did he come? In that he appeared as man.

**5.** Because, then, he was a man in such a way that God lay hidden in him, a great man was sent before him, by whose testimony he might be found to be more than a man. And who is this? *There was a man* [^12]. And how could this man speak the truth about God? *Sent from God* [^13]. What was he called? *Whose name was John* [^14]. Why did he come? *This man came for a testimony, to bear witness concerning the light, that all might believe through him* [^15]. What sort of man was this who would bear witness concerning the light? This John was something great, of immense merit, great grace, great loftiness! Marvel, indeed marvel; but as at a mountain. A mountain, however, is in darkness unless it is clothed with light. Therefore marvel at John only so far as to hear what follows: *He was not the light* [^16]; lest, when you think the mountain is the light, you make shipwreck on the mountain instead of finding comfort. But what ought you to marvel at? The mountain as a mountain. But raise yourself to him who illuminates the mountain, who was raised up for this, that he might first receive the rays and announce them to your eyes. Therefore, *he was not the light*.

**6.** Why, then, did he come? *But that he might bear witness concerning the light* [^17]. Why this? *That all might believe through him* [^18]. And concerning what light would he bear witness? *He was the true light* [^19]. Why is *true* added? Because an enlightened man too is called light; but the true light is that which enlightens. For our eyes too are called lights; and yet, unless either a lamp is lit by night or the sun comes out by day, these lights are open for no purpose. The enlightened man.

So then John too was light, but not the true light: because, unenlightened, he was darkness; but by enlightenment he was made light. But unless he were enlightened, he would have been darkness, like all the impious, to whom, now that they believed, the Apostle said: *You were once darkness*. But now, because they had believed, what? *But now you are light*, he says, *in the Lord* [^20]. Unless he added *in the Lord*, we would not understand. *Light*, he says, *in the Lord*: darkness not in the Lord were you. *For you were once darkness*—there he did not add, in the Lord. Therefore darkness in yourselves, light in the Lord. So too *he was not the light, but that he might bear witness concerning the light* [^21].

**7.** But where is the light itself? *He was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world* [^22]. If every man coming, then John himself too. He himself, then, enlightened the one by whom he wished to be pointed out. Let your Charity understand: for he was coming to weak minds, to wounded hearts, to the bleary eyesight of the soul. For this he had come. And from where could the soul see that which perfectly is? Just as it often happens that from some illuminated body it is recognized that the sun has risen, which we cannot see with our eyes. For even those who have wounded eyes are able to see a wall lit and illumined by the sun, or a mountain, or a tree, or something of this kind they are able to see; and in something else that is illumined, that rising is shown to them which they still bear too weak an eyesight to see.

So then all those to whom Christ had come were too weak to see him: he shone upon John; and through him, confessing that he was shone upon and enlightened, not the one who shone and enlightened, was recognized the one who enlightens, recognized the one who illumines, recognized the one who fills. And who is he? *Who enlightens*, he says, *every man coming into the world* [^23]. For if man had not departed from there, he would not need to be enlightened: but for this reason he is to be enlightened here, because he departed from there, where man could always have been enlightened.

## Through a lamp we seek the day.

**8.** What then? If he came here, where was he? *He was in this world* [^24]. He both was here, and he came here: he was here through divinity; he came here through the flesh: because, when he was here through divinity, he could not be seen by the foolish and the blind and the wicked. The wicked themselves are the darkness of which it was said: *The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it* [^25]. See, he is here even now, and he was here, and he is always here; and he never departs, he departs nowhere. You need to have the means by which you may see that which never departs from you; you need not to depart from him who departs nowhere; you need not to forsake him, and you will not be forsaken. Do not fall, and he will not set for you. If you fall, he sets for you; but if you stand, he is present to you.

But you did not stand: recall from where you fell, from where the one who fell before you cast you down. For he cast you down not by force, not by a shove, but by your own will. For if you had not consented to evil, you would have stood, you would have remained enlightened. But now, because you have already fallen and become wounded in heart—the means by which that light could be seen—he came to you in such a form as you could see; and he showed himself such a man that he sought testimony from a man. God seeks testimony from a man, and God has a man as witness; God has a man as witness, but for man's sake: so weak are we! Through a lamp we seek the day: for John himself was called a lamp, the Lord saying: *He was a burning and shining lamp, and you were willing to exult for an hour in his light; but I have a testimony greater than John's* [^26].

**9.** Therefore he shows that for the sake of men he willed to be pointed out through a lamp, for the faith of believers, so that through that same lamp his enemies might be confounded. For those enemies who tempted him and said: *Tell us: By what authority do you do these things? I too will ask you*, he said, *one question: tell me, the baptism of John, from where is it? from heaven, or from men? And they were troubled, and said among themselves: If we say from heaven, he will say to us: Why then did you not believe him?* (because he had borne witness to Christ, and had said: *I am not the Christ, but he is*). *But if we say it is from men, we fear the people, lest they stone us; for they held John as a prophet* [^27]. Fearing stoning, but more fearing the confession of the truth, they answered the truth with a lie; *and iniquity lied to itself* [^28]. For they said: *We do not know* [^29].

And the Lord, because they had shut themselves off against him by denying that they knew what they did know, did not himself open to them, because they did not knock. For it was said: *Knock, and it will be opened to you* [^30]. But not only did they not knock, that it might be opened; rather, by denying, they barred the very door against themselves. And the Lord said to them: *Neither do I tell you by what authority I do these things* [^31]. And they were confounded through John; and there was fulfilled in them: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ; his enemies I will clothe with confusion* [^32].

**10.** *He was in the world, and the world was made through him* [^33]. Do not think that he was in the world in the way that earth is in the world, that heaven is in the world, that the sun, moon, and stars are in the world, that trees, cattle, and men are in the world. He was not in the world in that way. But how was he? As a craftsman, governing what he made. For he did not make in the way that a carpenter makes. The chest he makes is outside him, and it is placed in another spot while it is being fashioned; and although it is nearby, the one who fashions it sits in another spot, and is outside the thing he fashions: but God, poured into the world, fashions it; placed everywhere, he fashions it, and does not withdraw anywhere, nor does he, as it were, turn from outside the mass he fashions. By the presence of his majesty he makes what he makes; by his presence he governs what he made. So then he was in the world as the one through whom the world was made. For *the world was made through him, and the world did not know him* [^34].

**11.** What does *the world was made through him* mean? Heaven, earth, sea, and all things that are in them are called the world. Again, in another sense, the lovers of the world are called the world. *The world was made through him, and the world did not know him* [^35]. For did the heavens not know their Creator, or did the angels not know their Creator, or did the stars not know their Creator, whom the demons confess [^36]? All things everywhere bore witness. But who did not know him? Those who, by loving the world, were called the world.

For by loving we dwell with the heart: and by loving, they deserved to be called by the name of that in which they dwelt. Just as we say: That house is bad; or: That house is good: in the one we call bad we do not accuse the walls; or in the one we call good we do not praise the walls: but a bad house means bad inhabitants, and a good house means good inhabitants. So too the world means those who inhabit the world by loving it. Who are they? Those who love the world: for they dwell with their heart in the world. For those who do not love the world dwell in the world in the flesh; but with the heart they inhabit heaven, as the Apostle says: *But our citizenship is in heaven* [^37]. Therefore *the world was made through him, and the world did not know him* [^38].

**12.** *He came to his own*: because all these things were made through him. *And his own did not receive him* [^39]. Who are his own? The men he made. The Jews, whom he first made to be over all the nations. Because the other nations adored idols and served demons; but that people had been born of the seed of Abraham: and they especially are his own; because, through the flesh which he deigned to take on, they are also his kindred. *He came to his own, and his own did not receive him*. Did they not receive him at all, did no one receive him? Was no one, then, made safe? For no one will be made safe except him who has received Christ when he came.

## The only one would not remain alone.

**13.** But he added: *But as many as received him* [^40]. What did he grant them? Great kindness! Great mercy! He was born the only one, and he would not remain alone. Many men, when they have had no children, after their prime is past adopt children for themselves; and they do by their will what they could not do by nature: this men do. But if someone has an only son, he rejoices in him the more; because he alone will possess everything, and there will be no one to divide the inheritance with him, so that he should remain the poorer. Not so with God: the only-begotten, the same one whom he had begotten and through whom he had created all things, he sent into this world, so that he would not be one alone, but would have adopted brothers. For we were not born of God as that Only-begotten was, but adopted through his grace. For he, the Only-begotten, came to loose the sins in which we were entangled, lest, because of the hindrance of those sins, he should not adopt us: those whom he willed to make his brothers, he himself loosed, and made coheirs.

For the Apostle says thus: *But if a son, then an heir through God* [^41]; and again: *Heirs indeed of God, but coheirs of Christ* [^42]. He was not afraid to have coheirs; because his inheritance does not become cramped if many possess it. Those very ones, indeed, while he possesses them, become his inheritance, and he in turn becomes their inheritance. Hear how they become his inheritance: *The Lord said to me: You are my Son; this day I have begotten you. Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance* [^43]. How does he become their inheritance? He says in the Psalm: *The Lord is the portion of my inheritance and of my cup* [^44]. Let us possess him, and let him possess us: let him possess us as Lord; let us possess him as salvation, let us possess him as light. What, then, did he give to those who received him? ***He gave them power to become sons of God, to those who believe in his name*** [^45]: that they might hold to the wood, and cross the sea.

**14.** And how are they born? These, because they become sons of God and brothers of Christ, are surely born. For if they are not born, how can they be sons? But the sons of men are born of flesh and blood, and of the will of a man, and of the embrace of marriage. But how are these born to him? ***Who are born not of bloods***—as it were, of male and female. 'Bloods' is not Latin: but because in Greek it was put in the plural, the one who was translating preferred to put it thus, and as it were to speak less correctly according to the grammarians, and yet to set forth the truth for the hearing of the weak. For if he had said 'blood' in the singular, he would not have set forth what he meant: for men are born of the bloods of male and female. Let us say it, then, and not fear the rods of the grammarians, provided we arrive at the solid and surer truth. Let the one who understands rebuke me, ungrateful that he understood.

*Not of bloods, nor of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of a man* [^46]. He put 'flesh' for the woman: because, when she had been made from the rib, Adam said: *This now is bone of my bones, and flesh of my flesh* [^47]; and the Apostle says: *He who loves his wife loves himself; for no one ever hated his own flesh* [^48]. 'Flesh,' then, is put for the wife, just as sometimes 'spirit' is put for the husband. Why? Because he rules, she is ruled: he ought to command, she to serve. For where the flesh commands and the spirit serves, the household is perverse. What is worse than a household where the woman has command over the man? But the household is rightly ordered where the man commands, the woman obeys. So a man himself is rightly ordered where the spirit commands, the flesh serves.

**15.** These, then, ***were born not of the will of the flesh, nor of the will of a man, but of God*** [^49]. But that men might be born of God, first God was born of them. For Christ is God, and Christ was born of men. He sought, indeed, only a mother on earth, because he already had a Father in heaven: born of God, through whom we might be created; and born of a woman, through whom we might be recreated. Do not marvel, then, O man, that you are made a son by grace, that you are born of God according to his Word. First the Word himself willed to be born of man, so that you might be born of God without anxiety, and might say to yourself: Not without cause did God will to be born of man, except that he reckoned me to be of some worth, so that he might make me immortal, and was born mortally for my sake. Therefore, when he had said, ***they were born of God***; as if lest we should marvel and shudder at so great a grace, so that it should seem incredible to us that men are born of God, as if making you free of anxiety, he says: ***And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*** [^50]. Why, then, do you marvel that men are born of God? Consider God himself born of men: ***And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us***.

## The flesh had blinded you, the flesh heals you.

**16.** But because ***the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us***, by that very birth he made an eye-salve, by which the eyes of our heart might be wiped clean, and we might be able to see his majesty through his humility. Therefore ***the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us***; he healed our eyes: and what follows? ***And we saw his glory*** [^51]. No one could see his glory unless he were healed by the humility of the flesh. From what cause could we not see? Let your Charity attend, then, and see what I say. There had rushed upon man, as it were, dust into the eye, earth had rushed in, it had wounded the eye, he could not see the light: this wounded eye is anointed; he had been wounded by earth, and earth is applied there that he may be healed. For all eye-salves and medicines are nothing but from the earth. You were blinded by dust, you are healed by dust: therefore the flesh had blinded you, the flesh heals you. For the soul had become carnal by consenting to carnal affections; from this the eye of the heart had been blinded.

***The Word was made flesh***: this physician made you an eye-salve. And since he came in such a way as to extinguish the vices of the flesh by means of the flesh, and to slay death by means of death; therefore it was brought about in you that, since ***the Word was made flesh***, you might be able to say: ***And we saw his glory***. What sort of glory? Such as the Son of man was made? That is his humility, not his glory. But to what was the eyesight of man brought, healed through the flesh? ***We saw***, he says, ***his glory, glory as of the Only-begotten from the Father, full of grace and truth*** [^52]. Concerning grace and truth we will treat more fully in another place in this same Gospel, if the Lord deigns to grant it. Let this now suffice, and be built up in Christ, and be strengthened in faith, and be watchful in good works: and do not depart from the wood, by which you may be able to cross the sea.
