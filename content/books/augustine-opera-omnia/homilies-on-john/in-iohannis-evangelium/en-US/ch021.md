# Homily 21

## *And greater works than these will he show him, that you may marvel*[^1].

Let us be glad and give thanks that we have been made not only Christians, but Christ. Do you understand, brothers, do you grasp the grace of God upon us? Marvel, rejoice, we have been made Christ. For if he is the head, we are the members; the whole man is he and we. The fullness of Christ, the head and the members. What is the head and the members? Christ and the Church.  
Growing by loving.

**1.** Yesterday, as far as the Lord deigned to grant, we treated, with what ability we could and understood with what capacity we could, how the works of the Father and the Son are inseparable; and that the Father does not do some things and the Son others, but the Father does all things through the Son, as through his Word, of whom it is written: *All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^2]. Today let us see the following words, and let us both entreat and hope for his mercy from the same Lord, that first, if he himself judges it worthy, we may understand what is true; but if we cannot do this, let us not go into what is false. For it is better not to know than to err: but to know is better than not to know. And so before all things we ought to strive to know: if we can, thanks be to God; but if we cannot for the time arrive at the truth, let us not go to falsehood.

For we ought to consider what we are, and what we are treating. We are men carrying flesh, walking in this life: and even if we are now reborn from the seed of the word of God, yet we are so renewed in Christ that we are not yet wholly stripped of Adam. For what is mortal and corruptible in us *weighs down the soul*[^3]—that it is from Adam appears, and is manifest: but what is spiritual in us lifts up the soul, from the gift of God and from his mercy, who sent his Only-begotten to share with us our death, and to lead us to his immortality. Him we have as Master, that we may not sin; and as defender, if we have sinned and have confessed and turned; and as intercessor for us, if we have desired any good thing from the Lord; and as giver with the Father, because the Father and the Son are one God.

But a man was speaking these things to men; God hidden, man manifest, that he might make manifest men gods; and the Son of God, made the son of man, that he might make the sons of men sons of God. By what art of his wisdom he does this, we recognize in his words. For he who is little speaks to little ones: but he himself is so little that he is also great; we, however, are little, but in him great: he speaks, then, as one cherishing and nourishing sucklings, and growing by loving.

**2.** He had said, *The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*. But we understood that the Father does not do something separately, which, when the Son has seen it, he too may do something of the kind, his Father's work being beheld; but that what he said, *The Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*, means that the whole Son is from the Father, and his whole substance and power is from him who begot him. But now, when he had said that he does these same things likewise which the Father does, that we may not understand the Father to do some things and the Son others, but the Son by a like power to do the very same things which the Father does, while the Father does them through the Son; he went on to say what we heard read today: *For the Father loves the Son, and shows him everything that he himself does*[^4].

Again mortal thought is disturbed. The Father shows the Son what he himself does: therefore, says someone, the Father does something separately, that the Son may be able to see what he does. Again there occur to human thought as it were two craftsmen, as if a smith should teach his son his craft, and show him whatever he does, that he too may be able to do it: *He shows him everything*, he says, *that he himself does*. When, then, the Father does it, does the Son not do it, that the Son may be able to see what the Father does? Surely all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing. How do we see that the Father shows the Son what he does; when the Father does nothing except what he does through the Son? What did the Father make? The world.

Did he so show the made world to the Son, that he too might make something of the kind? Let there be given us, then, a world which the Son also made: but *all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*, and *the world was made through him*[^5]. If the world was made through him, and all things were made through him, and the Father does nothing that he does not do through the Son; where does the Father show the Son what he does, except in the very Son through whom he does it?

For what place is there where the work of the Father may be shown to the Son, as though he made it outside and sat outside, and the Son attended to the hand of the Father, how he made it? Where is that inseparable Trinity? where is the Word of whom it was said that he himself is the Power and Wisdom of God[^6]? where is what Scripture says of that Wisdom: *For it is the brightness of the eternal light*[^7]? where is what is said again of it: *It reaches mightily from end to end, and orders all things sweetly*[^8]? If the Father does anything, he does it through the Son; if through his Wisdom and his Power he does it: he does not show him outside what he may see, but in himself he shows him what he does.

**3.** What does the Father see, or rather what does the Son see in the Father that he too may do it? Perhaps I could say it; but grant me one who can grasp it: or perhaps I could think it, and not say it; or perhaps not even think it. For that divinity exceeds us as God exceeds men, as the immortal the mortal, as the eternal those in time. Let him breathe upon us and grant, from that spring of life, that he now deign to bedew and distill something into our thirst, lest in this wilderness we wither. Let us say to him, Lord, to whom we have learned to say, Father. For we dare this, because he himself willed that we should dare: if only we so live that he does not say to us, *If I am Father, where is my honor? if I am Lord, where is my fear?*[^9]

Let us, then, say to him, Our Father. To whom do we say, Our Father? To the Father of Christ. He, then, who says to the Father of Christ, Our Father, what does he say to Christ, except, Our Brother? Yet not as Christ's Father, so also is he our Father: for never did Christ so join us together as to make no distinction between us and himself. For he is the Son equal to the Father, he is eternal with the Father, and coeternal with the Father: but we are made through the Son, adopted through the Only-begotten. Accordingly, it was never heard from the mouth of our Lord Jesus Christ, when he spoke to the disciples, that he said of God his most high Father, Our Father: but he said either, My Father, or, Your Father. He did not say Our Father, so much so that in a certain place he set down these two: *I go*, he says, *to my God and your God*. Why did he not say, Our God? And he said, *My Father and your Father*[^10]; he did not say, Our Father. So he joins as to distinguish, so he distinguishes as not to disjoin. He wishes us to be one in himself, but the Father and himself one.

**4.** However much, then, we understand, and however much we see, even when we have been made equal to the angels, we shall not see as the Son sees. For we, even when we do not see, are something. And what else are we when we do not see, except not seeing? Yet we are, even if not seeing; and in order to see, we turn ourselves to him whom we may see; and there comes to be in us a seeing which was not, while we ourselves nevertheless were. For there is a man not seeing, and the same man, when he has seen, is called a man seeing. To see, then, is not for him the same as to be a man: for if to see were for him the same as to be a man, a man would never be except seeing. But since there is a man not seeing, and he seeks to see what he does not see; there is one who seeks, and one who turns himself to see: and when he has well turned himself and has seen, there comes to be a man seeing, who before was a man not seeing. Seeing, then, comes to him, and departs from him: it comes to him when he turns himself, it departs from him when he turns himself away.

Is the Son so? Far be it. The Son was never not seeing, and afterward became seeing: but to see the Father is for him the same as to be the Son. For we, by turning away to sin, lose illumination; and by turning ourselves to God, receive illumination. For the light by which we are illumined is one thing, and we who are illumined another. But the light itself by which we are illumined neither turns away from itself, nor loses its light, because it is light.

So, then, the Father shows the thing which he does to the Son, in such a way that the Son sees all things in the Father, and is all things in the Father. For by seeing he was born, and by being born he sees. But it was not that at one time he was not born, and afterward was born; just as it was not that at one time he did not see, and afterward saw: but in that which is for him to see, in that is for him to be, in that is for him not to be changed, in that is for him to persist without beginning and without end.

Let us not, then, take it carnally—that the Father sits, and does a work, and shows the Son; and the Son sees the work which the Father does, and does it in another place, or out of other material. For all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing. The Son is the Word of the Father, God said nothing that he did not say in the Son. For by saying in the Son what he was going to make through the Son, he begot the very Son through whom he would make all things.

**5.** *And greater works than these will he show him, that you may marvel*[^11]. Again here he troubles us. And who is there who can worthily search out so great a secret? But now, since he has deigned to speak to us, he himself opens it. For he would not wish to say what he was unwilling to be understood: since he deigned to speak, without doubt he stirred up an audience; does he forsake, when stirred up, the one whom he stirred up to hear?

We have said, as we could, that the Son does not know temporally, nor is the Son's knowledge one thing and the Son himself another; nor is the Son's seeing one thing and the Son himself another: but that the seeing itself is the Son, and the knowledge or wisdom itself of the Father is the Son; and that this wisdom and this seeing is eternal from the eternal, and coeternal with him from whom it is; and that there nothing is varied through time; nor is anything born which was not; nor does anything perish which was. We have said it, as we could.

What, then, does time do here now, that he should say, *greater works than these will he show him*? that is, he is going to show, this is, he will show. *He has shown* is one thing, *he will show* another: *he has shown*, we say of the past; *he will show*, we say of the future. What, then, do we do here, brothers? Behold him whom we had said to be coeternal with the Father, in whom nothing is varied through time, nothing is moved through spaces either of moments or of places, who abides always with the Father seeing, seeing the Father and by seeing existing—again, naming times to us, *he will show him*, he says, *greater works than these*. Is he, then, going to show the Son yet something which the Son does not know? What, then, do we do? how do we understand this?

Behold, our Lord Jesus Christ was above, he is below. When was he above? When he said, *Whatever the Father does, these same things the Son also does likewise*. Whence now below? *Greater works than these will he show him*.

O Lord Jesus Christ, our Savior, Word of God through whom all things were made, what is the Father going to show you that you do not yet know? what of the Father is hidden from you? what is hidden from you in the Father, from whom the Father is not hidden? what greater works is he going to show you? or than what works are those greater which he is going to show? For when he said, *greater than these*, we ought first to understand than what they are greater.

**6.** Let us recall whence this discourse arose. When he was cured who had been thirty-eight years in his infirmity, and he commanded him, healed, to take up his pallet and go to his house. For from this the Jews were stirred up, with whom he was speaking: he was speaking in words, and was silent in understanding; in a manner he nodded to those who understood, he concealed from those who were angry: from this, then, when the Jews were stirred up, because the Lord did this on the Sabbath, they gave occasion for this discourse[^12]. Let us not, then, so hear these things as though forgetful of what was said above, but let us look back at that sick man of thirty-eight years suddenly made well, the Jews marveling and being angry. They sought darkness about the Sabbath rather than light about the miracle.

Speaking, then, to these indignant ones, he said this: *Greater works than these will he show him*. *Greater than these*: than what? Because you have seen a man made well, whose sickness had lasted up to thirty-eight years, greater than these is the Father going to show the Son. What are the greater works? It follows, and he says: *For as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*[^13]. Plainly these are greater. For it is far more that a dead man should rise again, than that a sick man should grow well: these are greater. But when is the Father going to show them to the Son? For does the Son not know them? and did he who was speaking not know how to raise the dead? did he through whom all things were made[^14] still have to learn to raise the dead? did he who brought it about that we should live, who were not, still have to learn that we should be raised again? What is it, then, that he wishes to say?

## When one of my least ones learns, I learn.

**7.** For he descended to us, and he who a little before was speaking as God, began to speak as man. Yet he himself is man who is God, because God was made man: but made what he was not, not losing what he was. Therefore man came to God, so that he who was God might be man; not that he might now be man and not be God. Let us, then, hear him also as a brother, who were hearing him as the founder: as founder, because the Word in the beginning; as brother, because born of the Virgin Mary: founder before Abraham, before Adam, before the earth, before heaven, before all things corporeal and spiritual; but brother from the seed of Abraham, from the tribe of Judah, from an Israelite Virgin. If, then, we know this one who speaks to us to be both God and man, let us understand the words of God and man: for sometimes he says to us such things as pertain to majesty, sometimes such as pertain to lowliness. For he himself is the exalted one, who is lowly that he may make us lowly ones exalted.

What, then, does he say? *The Father will show me greater than these, that you may marvel*. Therefore he is going to show us, not him. Since, then, the Father is going to show us; therefore he said, *that you may marvel*. For he explained what he wished to say, *The Father will show me*.

Why did he not say, The Father will show you; but, he will show the Son?

Because we too are members of the Son; and we members, as it were what we learn, he himself learns in a manner in his members.

How does he learn in us?

As he suffers in us.

How do we prove that he suffers in us?

From that voice from heaven: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^15]

Is it not he himself who as judge will sit at the end of the world, and, setting the just at the right hand, the unjust at the left, will say: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom: for I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*? and when they answer, *Lord, when did we see you hungry?* he will say to them, *When you gave it to one of my least ones, you gave it to me*[^16]? He, then, who said, *When you gave it to one of my least ones, you gave it to me*; let him now too be questioned by us, and let us say to him: Lord, when will you be one who learns, since you teach all things? For at once he answers us in our faith: When one of my least ones learns, I learn.

**8.** Therefore let us be glad and give thanks that we have been made not only Christians, but Christ. Do you understand, brothers, do you grasp the grace of God upon us? Marvel, rejoice, we have been made Christ. For if he is the head, we are the members; the whole man is he and we. This is what the apostle Paul says: *That we may no longer be little ones, tossed about and carried around by every wind of doctrine*. But above he had said: *Until we all come to the unity of the faith, and to the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the age of the fullness of Christ*[^17]. The fullness of Christ, then, is the head and the members.

What is, the head and the members?

Christ and the Church.

For we would arrogate this to ourselves in pride, unless he himself deigned to promise this, who through the same apostle says: *But you are the body of Christ and members*[^18].

**9.** When, then, the Father shows to the members of Christ, he shows to Christ. A certain great miracle takes place, but yet a true one: it is shown to Christ what Christ knew, and it is shown to Christ through Christ. It is a wonderful and great thing, but Scripture so speaks. Are we going to contradict the divine oracles, and not rather to understand, and out of his gift give thanks to him who gave it? What is it that I said, it is shown to Christ through Christ?

It is shown to the members through the head. Behold, see this in yourself: suppose yourself, with eyes closed, to wish to lift something; the hand does not know where to go, and surely your hand is your member, for it is not separated from your body; open the eyes, the hand now sees where to go, the head showing it, the member followed. If, then, in you something of the kind could be found, that your body should show to your body, and through your body something should be shown to your body; do not marvel that it was said, it is shown to Christ through Christ.

For the head shows that the members may see, and the head teaches that the members may learn: yet one man, the head and the members. He was unwilling to separate himself, but deigned to be glued together with us. He was far from us, and very far: what so far as the made and the maker? what so far as God and man? what so far as righteousness and iniquity? what so far as eternity and mortality? Behold how far the Word was, in the beginning God with God, through whom all things were made. How, then, did he become near, so that he should be what we are, and we in him? *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^19].

## As man he calls upon the Father; as God he acts together with the Father.

**10.** This, then, is what he is going to show us; this he showed to his disciples, who saw him in the flesh. What is it? *As the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*[^20]. Some by the Father, others by the Son? Surely *all things were made through him*[^21]. What do we say, my brothers? Christ raised Lazarus: did the Father raise some other dead man, so that Christ might see how he should raise Lazarus? Or when Christ raised Lazarus, did the Father not raise him too, and did the Son alone act without the Father? Read that very passage and see that there he calls upon the Father, that Lazarus may rise[^22]. As man, he calls upon the Father; as God, he acts together with the Father. Therefore Lazarus, who rose again, was raised both by the Father and by the Son in the gift and grace of the Holy Spirit; and that wonderful work the Trinity performed. So let us not understand it in this way—*As the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*—as though we should suppose that some are raised and given life by the Father and others by the Son; rather, the very ones whom the Father raises and gives life, those same the Son also raises and gives life, since *all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^23].

And to show that he has a power equal, though given him by the Father, he therefore says, *So also the Son gives life to whom he will*, in order to show his own will there; and lest anyone should say that the Father raises the dead through the Son, but that he does so as one who is powerful and has power, while the Son acts as out of another's power, doing something as a minister, like an angel, he indicated his power when he said, *So also the Son gives life to whom he will*. For the Father does not will anything other than the Son; but as in them there is one substance, so also there is one will.

**11.** And who are these dead whom the Father and the Son give life? Are they the ones of whom we spoke—Lazarus, or that widow's son[^24], or the ruler of the synagogue's daughter[^25]? For we know that these were raised by Christ the Lord. He wants to suggest to us something else, namely the resurrection of the dead, which we all await—not the kind that some had so that the rest might believe. For Lazarus rose again to die, but we shall rise again to live forever. Does the Father bring about such a resurrection, or the Son? Rather, the Father in the Son.

Therefore the Son, and the Father in the Son. How do we prove that he is speaking of this resurrection? When he had said, *For as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*, lest we should understand that resurrection of the dead which he brings about for a miracle, not for eternal life, he went on to say, *For the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*[^26]. What is this? He was speaking of the resurrection of the dead, that *as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*; whereupon he at once added, as it were a reason, something about judgment, saying, *For the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*—why, unless because he had spoken of that resurrection of the dead which is to be at the judgment?

## And if we cannot yet reach the spring, let us drink from the stream.

**12.** *For the Father*, he says, *judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*[^27]. A little earlier we were supposing that the Father does something which the Son does not do, when he said, *For the Father loves the Son and shows him all that he himself does*[^28]—as though the Father were doing and the Son were watching. So a carnal understanding was creeping into our mind, as though the Father were doing what the Son was not doing, while the Son watched the Father showing what was being done by the Father. So it was as though the Father were doing what the Son was not doing; now we already see the Son doing something which the Father does not do. How he turns us about and handles our mind, leads it here and there, does not let it remain in one place of the flesh, that by turning us he may exercise us, by exercising us may cleanse us, by cleansing us may make us capable, and having made us capable may fill us!

What do these words do to us? What was he saying? What is he saying? A little earlier he was saying that the Father shows the Son whatever he does; I saw, as it were, the Father doing and the Son waiting; now again I see the Son doing and the Father idle: *For the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*. When, then, the Son is to judge, will the Father be idle and not judge? What is this? What am I to understand?

Lord, what do you say? You are the Word, God; I am a man. Do you say that the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son? I read in another place that you say, *I judge no one; there is one who seeks and judges*[^29]. Of whom do you say, *There is one who seeks and judges*, if not of the Father? He seeks redress for the wrongs done to you, he judges concerning the wrongs done to you. How is it here that the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son? Let us also question Peter; let us hear him speaking in his Letter: *Christ*, he says, *suffered for us, leaving us an example, that we should follow in his steps; he committed no sin, nor was guile found in his mouth; when he was reviled, he did not revile in return; when he suffered, he did not threaten, but committed himself to him who judges justly*[^30]. How is it true that the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son?

Here we are troubled; troubled, let us sweat; sweating, let us be cleansed. Let us try, however we can, by his gift, to penetrate the deep secrets of these words. Perhaps we are being rash, because we want to examine and scrutinize the words of God. And why were they spoken, unless that they might be known? Why did they sound forth, unless that they might be heard? Why were they heard, unless that they might be understood? May he, then, strengthen us and grant us something, as much as he himself sees fit; and if we cannot yet reach the spring, let us drink from the stream. See, John himself has flowed out to us as it were a stream; he has brought down to us the Word from on high, has humbled it and in a manner laid it low, so that we should not shrink from the height but draw near to the lowly one.

**13.** There is altogether a certain true and strong understanding, if somehow we can hold to it, of why *the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*. For this was said because at the judgment none will appear to men but the Son. The Father will be hidden, the Son manifest. In what will the Son be manifest? In the form in which he ascended. For in the form of God he is hidden with the Father; in the form of a servant he is manifest to men. Therefore the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son—but a manifest judgment; in which manifest judgment the Son will judge, because he himself will appear to those who are to be judged. More plainly does Scripture show us that he himself will appear. On the fortieth day after his resurrection he ascended into heaven while his disciples watched[^31]; and the angelic voice came to them: *Men of Galilee*, it said, *why do you stand looking into heaven? This one who has been taken up from you into heaven will come in the same way as you saw him going into heaven*[^32]. How did they see him going? In the flesh, which they touched, which they handled, whose very scars they verified by touching, in that body in which he went in and out among them for forty days, showing himself to them in truth, not in any falsehood—not a phantom, not a shadow, not a spirit, but, as he himself said, not deceiving, *Handle me and see, for a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see that I have*[^33]. That body is now indeed worthy of a heavenly dwelling, not subject to death, not changeable through the ages. For just as he had grown from infancy to that age, he does not so decline from the age that was youth into old age: he remains as he ascended, about to come to those to whom, before he comes, he willed his word to be preached.

So, then, he will come in human form: this both the wicked will see, and those placed at the right hand will see, and those separated to the left will see; as it is written, *They shall look on him whom they have pierced*[^34]. If they shall look on him whom they have pierced, they will see that very body which they struck with the lance: the Word is not struck with a lance; this, then, the wicked will be able to see, which they were able even to wound. They will not see the God hidden in the body: after the judgment he will be seen by those who will be at the right hand. This, then, is what he says: *The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*—because the Son will come manifest to judgment, appearing to men in a human body, saying to those on his right, *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*; saying to those on his left, *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^35].

## The form of God will be kept for the sons.

**14.** See, the form of a man will be seen by the godly and the ungodly, by the just and the unjust, by the faithful and the unbelieving, by those who rejoice and those who mourn, by the confident and the confounded: see, it will be seen. When that form has been seen at the judgment, and the judgment has been carried out—where it was said that the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son, for this reason, that the Son will appear at the judgment in the form which he received from us—what is to be afterward? When will the form of God be seen, for which all the faithful thirst? When will that be seen which *in the beginning was the Word, God with God*, through whom all things were made? When will that form of God be seen, of which the Apostle says, *Though he was in the form of God, he did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*[^36]? For great is that form, where the equality of Father and Son is even now known: ineffable, incomprehensible, especially to little ones. When will it be seen?

See, at the right hand are the just, at the left are the unjust; all alike see a man, see the Son of man, see him who was pierced, see him who was crucified, see him humbled, see him born of the Virgin, see the Lamb of the tribe of Judah: when will they see the Word, God with God? He will be there then too, but the form of a servant will appear. The form of a servant will be shown to servants; the form of God will be kept for the sons. Let the servants, then, become sons; let those at the right hand go into the eternal inheritance long ago promised, which the martyrs believed in without seeing, for the promise of which they poured out their blood without hesitation: let them go there and see it there. When will they go there? Let the Lord himself say: *So these will go into eternal burning, but the just into eternal life*[^37].

## I will not cheat your love.

**15.** See, he has named eternal life. Did he tell us this because there we shall see and know the Father and the Son? What if we live forever, but do not see that Father and Son?

Hear another place where he named eternal life and expressed what eternal life is. Do not be afraid, I am not deceiving you; not without cause did I promise to those who love me, saying, *He who has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me; and he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him, and will show myself to him*[^38].

Let us answer the Lord and say: What great thing, O Lord our God? What great thing? Will you show us yourself? Why, did you not show yourself even to the Jews? Did not even those who crucified you see you? But you will show yourself at the judgment, when we shall stand at your right hand: will not even those who will stand at the left see you? What is it that you will show us yourself? For do we not now see you when you speak?

He answers: I will show myself in the form of God; you now see the form of a servant. I will not cheat you, O faithful man; believe that you will see. You love, and do not see: will not love itself bring you through to seeing? Love, persevere in loving: I will not cheat your love, he says, I who have cleansed your heart. For why have I cleansed your heart, unless that God may be seen by you? *For blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^39].

But this, says the servant, as it were disputing with his Lord, you did not express when you said, *The just will go into eternal life*; you did not say, They will go that they may see me in the form of God, may see the Father to whom I am equal. Notice what he said elsewhere: *And this is eternal life, that they know you the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*[^40].

**16.** And so now, after the mention of the judgment, which the Father, judging no one, has given wholly to the Son, what is to be? What follows? *That all may honor the Son, as they honor the Father*[^41]. By the Jews the Father is honored, the Son is despised. For the Son was seen as a servant, the Father was honored as God. The Son too will appear equal to the Father, *that all may honor the Son, as they honor the Father*. Now, then, we hold this in faith. And let not the Jew say: I honor the Father; what have I to do with the Son? Let one answer him: *He who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father*[^42]. You lie altogether, you blaspheme the Son and do wrong to the Father. For the Father sent the Son; you despise the one he sent: how do you honor the sender, you who blaspheme the one sent?

**17.** See, says someone, the Son was sent; and the Father is greater, because he sent. Withdraw from the flesh. The old man suggests oldness; do you, in the new, recognize newness. Let the new—to you, ancient from the beginning, perpetual, eternal—call your understanding back to this. Is the Son less because the Son was said to be sent? I hear a sending, not a separation.

But this, he says, we see in human affairs, that the one who sends is greater than the one who is sent.

But human affairs deceive a man; divine affairs purify him. Do not attend to human affairs, where the one who sends seems greater and the one who is sent less—although human affairs themselves give testimony against you. For instance, if someone wishes to seek a wife and cannot do so by himself, he sends a greater friend to seek her for him. And there are many cases in which the greater one himself is chosen to be sent by the lesser. Why, then, do you want to make a false charge, that the one sent and the other was sent? The sun sends a ray and does not separate it; the moon sends its brightness and does not separate it; a lamp pours out its light and does not separate it: I see there a sending, and I see no separation.

For if you seek examples from human affairs, O heretical vanity—although, as I said a little before, human affairs themselves in certain examples refute and convict you—yet notice how different it is in human affairs, from which you want to draw examples to divine ones. The man who sends remains himself, and the one who is sent goes forth: does the man go along with the one he sends? But the Father, who sent the Son, did not withdraw from the Son. Hear the Lord himself saying: *Behold, the hour will come when each of you will scatter to his own and will leave me alone; yet I am not alone, for the Father is with me*[^43]. How did he send the one with whom he came? How did he send the one from whom he did not withdraw? In another place he said, *But the Father, abiding in me, does his works*[^44]. See, he is in him; see, he works. The sender did not withdraw from the one sent, because the one sent and the sender are one.
