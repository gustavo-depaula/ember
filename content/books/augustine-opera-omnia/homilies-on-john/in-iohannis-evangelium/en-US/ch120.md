# Tractate 120

## *One of the soldiers opened his side with a lance, and at once there came out blood and water* (Jn 19:31—20:9).

*He opened his side*, that there the door of life might in a manner be thrown open, from which the sacraments of the Church flowed, without which there is no entrance to the life that is true life. O death, by which the dead are brought back to life! What is cleaner than this blood? What more healing than this wound?

**1.** After the Lord Jesus, all things being accomplished which he foreknew had to be accomplished before his death, gave up his spirit when he willed, let us see what next followed, with the evangelist relating. *The Jews, then,* he says, *since it was the preparation, that the bodies might not remain on the cross on the sabbath (for that sabbath day was a great one), asked Pilate that their legs might be broken and that they might be taken away*[^1]. Not that their legs might be taken away, but those whose legs were broken so that they should die and be removed from the wood, lest, hanging on the crosses, they should defile the great festal day with the horror of their daylong torment.

## The evangelist used a watchful word.

**2.** *So the soldiers came and broke the legs of the first, and of the other who was crucified with him. But when they came to Jesus and saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs; but one of the soldiers opened his side with a lance, and at once there came out blood and water*[^2]. The evangelist used a watchful word, so as not to say: He pierced his side, or wounded it, or something else; but, *he opened*: that there the door of life might in a manner be thrown open, from which the sacraments of the Church flowed, without which there is no entrance to the life that is true life. That blood was poured out for the remission of sins; that water tempers the cup of salvation; it furnishes both the bath and the drink. This was foreshadowed by the fact that Noah was commanded to make a door in the side of the ark, by which the animals that were not to perish in the flood might enter, by which the Church was prefigured. On account of this the first woman was made from the side of the sleeping man, and was called life and the mother of the living. Surely it signified a great good, before the great evil of the transgression. Here the second Adam, his head bowed, slept on the cross, that from there a spouse might be formed for him, from that which flowed from the side of the sleeping one. O death, by which the dead are brought back to life! What is cleaner than this blood? What more healing than this wound?

**3.** *And he who saw it,* he says, *has borne witness, and his testimony is true; and he knows that he tells the truth, that you also may believe*[^3]. He did not say, that you also may know, but *that you may believe*: for he who saw it knows, whose testimony he who did not see may believe. But to believe pertains more to faith than to see. For what is it to believe but to lend faith? *For these things were done,* he says, *that the Scripture might be fulfilled: Not a bone of him shall be broken. And again another Scripture says: They shall look on him whom they pierced*[^4]. He gave two testimonies from the Scriptures for each of the things he related as done. For because he had said, *But when they came to Jesus and saw that he was already dead, they did not break his legs*, to this belongs the testimony: *Not a bone of him shall be broken*[^5]; which was commanded to those who were ordered to celebrate the Passover by the sacrificing of a sheep in the old law, which had gone before as a shadow of the Lord's passion. Hence *our Passover, Christ, was sacrificed*[^6]; of whom Isaiah the prophet too had foretold: *Like a sheep he was led to the sacrifice*[^7]. Again, because he had added, saying, *But one of the soldiers opened his side with a lance*, to this belongs the other testimony: *They shall look on him whom they pierced*[^8]; where Christ is promised as about to come in that same flesh in which he was crucified.

**4.** *After this, Joseph of Arimathea, because he was a disciple of Jesus, but secretly for fear of the Jews, asked Pilate that he might take away the body of Jesus; and Pilate gave him leave. So he came and took away the body of Jesus. Nicodemus also came, who had at first come to Jesus by night, bringing a mixture of myrrh and aloes, about a hundred pounds*[^9]. It is not to be so punctuated that we say, *at first bringing a mixture of myrrh*; but so that what is said, *at first*, belongs to the earlier sense. For Nicodemus had come to Jesus by night at first, which the same John related in the earlier parts of his gospel. Here, then, it is to be understood that Nicodemus came to Jesus not only then, but then for the first time; and that he kept coming afterward, so as to become a disciple by hearing: which surely is now, in the revelation of the body of the most blessed Stephen, made manifest to almost all nations. *So they took the body of Jesus and bound it in linen cloths with the spices, as is the custom of the Jews to bury*[^10]. It does not seem to me that the evangelist wished to say without purpose, *as is the custom of the Jews to bury*: for thus, unless I am mistaken, he gave warning that in such offices as are rendered to the dead the custom of each nation is to be observed.

**5.** *Now in the place where he was crucified there was a garden, and in the garden a new tomb, in which no one had yet been laid*[^11]. Just as in the womb of the Virgin Mary no one was conceived before him, no one after him, so in this tomb no one was buried before him, no one after him. *There, then, on account of the preparation of the Jews, because the tomb was near, they laid Jesus*[^12]. He wishes the burial to be understood as hastened, lest evening come on; for already, on account of the preparation, which the Jews among us more usually call in Latin the "pure supper," it was not lawful to do any such thing.

**6.** *Now on the first day of the week Mary Magdalene came early, while it was still dark, to the tomb; and she saw the stone taken away from the tomb*[^13]. The first day of the week is the day which Christian custom now calls the Lord's Day, on account of the Lord's resurrection; which Matthew alone among the evangelists named the first day of the sabbath. *So she ran and came to Simon Peter and to the other disciple whom Jesus loved, and said to them, They have taken the Lord from the tomb, and we do not know where they have laid him*[^14]. Some manuscripts, even Greek ones, have *They have taken my Lord*, which can be seen to be said with a more inclined affection of love or of service; but this we did not find in the more numerous manuscripts that we had at hand.

**7.** *So Peter and that other disciple went out and came to the tomb. They both ran together, and the other disciple outran Peter and came first to the tomb*[^15]. Here is to be noted and commended the recapitulation, how he returned to what had been passed over; and yet it is joined on as though it followed. For when he had already said, *they came to the tomb*, he went back to relate how they came; and he says, *they both ran together*, and so on. Where he shows that, running ahead, that other disciple, by whom he signifies himself, came first to the tomb—but he relates everything as though about another.

**8.** *And stooping down,* he says, *he saw the linen cloths lying there, yet he did not go in. Then Simon Peter came, following him, and went into the tomb; and he saw the linen cloths lying there, and the napkin which had been over his head, not lying with the linen cloths, but rolled up separately in a place by itself*[^16]. Do we suppose that these things signify nothing? By no means would I suppose this. But we hasten to other matters, in which we are compelled to linger by the necessity of some question or obscurity. For as to these things, which are clear in themselves, to inquire what each one also signifies is indeed a holy delight, but of those who have leisure, which we do not have.

**9.** *Then the other disciple also, who had come first to the tomb, went in*[^17]. He came first and entered last. This surely is not without meaning either, but I do not have leisure for these things. *And he saw,* he says, *and believed*[^18]. Here some, paying too little attention, think that John believed that Jesus had risen; but what follows does not indicate this. For what does he mean by what he at once added: *For as yet they did not know the Scripture, that he must rise from the dead*[^19]? He did not, then, believe that he had risen, whom he did not know was to rise. What, then, did he see? what did he believe? He saw, namely, the empty tomb, and believed what the woman had said, that he had been taken from the tomb. *For as yet they did not know the Scripture, that he must rise from the dead.* And therefore, when they heard this from the Lord himself, although it was said most plainly, by their habit of hearing parables from him they did not understand it, and believed that he meant something else.

But what follows let us put off to another homily.
