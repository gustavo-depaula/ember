# Tractate 33

## *Go, and now sin no more* (Jn 7:45-53; 8:1-11).

##  — The Lord condemned the sin, not the man. Let those, then, who love in the Lord his gentleness also fear the truth. For *the Lord is sweet and upright*. You love what is sweet; fear what is upright.

**1.** Your Charity remembers that in the earlier sermon, on the occasion of the gospel reading, we spoke to you of the Holy Spirit. When the Lord had invited those believing in him to drink of him, speaking among those who were thinking to seize him, and desiring to kill him yet were not able, because he was unwilling: when, then, he had said these things, a dissension arose about him in the crowd, some thinking that he himself was the Christ, others saying that the Christ would not arise from Galilee. But those who had been sent to seize him returned free from the crime, and full of wonder. For they bore witness to his divine teaching, when they said to those by whom they had been sent, who said, *Why have you not brought him?*, that they had never heard a man speak so: *For no man ever speaks thus*. But he spoke so, because he was God and man.

Yet the Pharisees, rejecting their witness, said to them: *Surely you too have not been led astray?* For we see that you have been delighted by his words. *Has any of the rulers believed in him, or any of the Pharisees? But this crowd, which does not know the Law, they are accursed*[^1]. Those who did not know the Law, they believed in him who had sent the Law; and him who had sent the Law, those despised who taught the Law: that what the Lord himself had said might be fulfilled: *I came that those who do not see may see, and that those who see may become blind*[^2]. For the Pharisees, the teachers, were made blind; the peoples were enlightened who did not know the Law, and believed in the author of the Law.

**2.** Nicodemus, however, one of the Pharisees, who had come to the Lord by night, and was himself not indeed unbelieving, but timid; for he had come by night to the light because he wished to be enlightened, and feared to be known: he answered the Jews: *Does our Law judge a man, unless it first hears from him and knows what he does?*[^3] For they perversely wished to be condemners before being knowers. For Nicodemus knew, or rather believed, that if only they were willing to hear him patiently, perhaps they would become like those who were sent to seize him, and preferred to believe. They answered, out of the prejudice of their own heart, what they answered to those others too: *Surely you too are not a Galilean?* That is, as if led astray by a Galilean. For the Lord was called a Galilean, since his parents were from the city of Nazareth. I said parents according to Mary, not according to male seed: for he sought on earth only a mother, who already had a Father above. For each of his nativities was wonderful; the divine without a mother, the human without a father.

What, then, did those as it were teachers of the Law say to Nicodemus? *Search the Scriptures, and see that a prophet does not arise from Galilee.* But the Lord of the Prophets arose from there. *They returned*, says the Evangelist, *each to his own house*[^4].

**3.** Thence Jesus went on to the mountain: to the Mount of Olives, the fruitful mountain, the mountain of ointment, the mountain of chrism. For where was it fitting for Christ to teach, except on the Mount of Olives? For the name of Christ comes from chrism: and chrism in Greek is in Latin called anointing. He anointed us for this reason, because he made us wrestlers against the devil. *And at daybreak he came again into the temple, and all the people came to him, and sitting he taught them*[^5]. And he was not seized, because he did not yet deign to suffer.

## Truth, gentleness, and justice.

**4.** Now then attend to where the Lord's gentleness was tested by his enemies. *And the scribes and Pharisees bring to him a woman caught in adultery, and they set her in the middle; and they said to him: Teacher, this woman was just now caught in adultery. But in the Law Moses commanded us to stone such; what then do you say? But this they said, tempting him, that they might be able to accuse him*[^6]. Accuse him of what? Surely they had not caught him in any crime, nor was that woman said to have pertained to him in any way? What is it, then, tempting him, that they might be able to accuse him?

We shall understand, brothers, that a wonderful gentleness pre-eminently stood out in the Lord. They had noticed that he was exceedingly mild, exceedingly gentle: for of him it had been foretold: *Gird your sword on your thigh, O most mighty one; in your comeliness and your beauty bend your bow, advance prosperously, and reign: for the sake of truth and gentleness and justice*[^7]. He brought, then, truth as a teacher, gentleness as a deliverer, justice as a knower. For these things the prophet had foretold that he would reign in the Holy Spirit. When he spoke, the truth was recognized: when against his enemies he was not moved, his gentleness was praised. Since, then, over these two, that is, over his truth and gentleness, his enemies were tortured with spite and envy; in the third, that is, justice, they laid the stumbling block.

Why?

Because the Law had commanded that adulterers be stoned; and surely the Law could not command what was unjust: if anyone said something other than the Law had commanded, he would be caught out as unjust.

They said, then, among themselves: He is thought truthful, he seems gentle; a calumny must be sought against him concerning justice. Let us bring before him a woman caught in adultery, let us say what is prescribed about her in the Law: if he commands her to be stoned, he will not have gentleness; if he judges that she should be let go, he will not hold to justice. But, they say, that he may not lose the gentleness by which he has already become lovable to the peoples, without doubt he is going to say that she ought to be let go. Hence we find an occasion of accusing, and we make him guilty as a transgressor of the Law: saying to him: You are an enemy of the Law, you answer against Moses, indeed against him who through Moses gave the Law; you are guilty of death, you yourself to be stoned along with her.

By these words and these thoughts envy could be inflamed, accusation could grow hot, condemnation could be demanded. But by whom against whom? Perversity against rectitude, falsity against truth, a corrupt heart against an upright heart, folly against wisdom. When would they prepare snares into which they did not first thrust their own head? Behold, the Lord in answering will both preserve justice, and not depart from gentleness. He for whom the snare was set was not caught, but rather those who set it were caught; because in him who could deliver them from the snares, they did not believe.

## There were left two, the wretched woman and mercy.

**5.** What, then, did the Lord Jesus answer? What did truth answer? What did wisdom answer? What did justice herself, against whom a calumny was being prepared, answer? He did not say: Let her not be stoned; lest he should seem to speak against the Law. But far be it that he should say: Let her be stoned: for he came not to lose what he had found, but *to seek what had perished*[^8]. What, then, did he answer? See how full it is of justice, full of gentleness and truth! *Whoever of you is without sin*, he says, *let him first cast a stone at her*[^9].

O answer of wisdom! How he sent them into themselves! For outwardly they were calumniating, within they were not examining themselves: they saw the adulteress, they did not look closely at themselves. Transgressors of the Law, they desired the Law to be fulfilled, and this by calumny; not truly, as though condemning adulteries by chastity.

You have heard, O Jews; you have heard, O Pharisees; you have heard, O teachers of the Law, the guardian of the Law, but you have not yet understood the Lawgiver. What else does it signify to you, when he writes with his finger on the ground? For by the finger of God the Law was written; but because of the hard it was written on stone[^10]. Now the Lord was writing on the ground, because he was seeking fruit. You have heard, then: Let the Law be fulfilled, let the adulteress be stoned: but in punishing her, is the Law to be fulfilled by those who are themselves to be punished?

Let each one of you consider himself, let him enter into himself, let him ascend the tribunal of his own mind, let him set himself before his own conscience, let him compel himself to confess. For he knows who he is: because *no one among men knows the things of a man, except the spirit of the man, which is in him*[^11]. Each one, attending to himself, finds himself a sinner. So indeed. Therefore either let this woman go, or together with her receive the penalty of the Law.

If he had said: Let not the adulteress be stoned; he would be convicted of injustice: if he had said: Let her be stoned; he would not seem gentle: let him say what he ought to say, both gentle and just: *Whoever of you is without sin, let him first cast a stone at her.* This is the voice of justice: Let the sinful woman be punished, but not by sinners; let the Law be fulfilled, but not by transgressors of the Law. This is altogether the voice of justice: by which justice those men, as though struck by a beam-like dart, looking upon themselves and finding themselves guilty, one after another all withdrew[^12]. There were left two, the wretched woman and mercy. But the Lord, when he had struck them with that dart of justice, did not deign to attend to them as they fell, but with his gaze turned away from them, again wrote with his finger on the ground.

**6.** But when that woman alone was left, and all had gone away, he lifted his eyes to the woman. We have heard the voice of justice, let us hear also that of gentleness. For more, I believe, was that woman terrified when she had heard the Lord say: *Whoever of you is without sin, let him first cast a stone at her.* They, then, attending to themselves, and by their very departure confessing about themselves, had left the woman with her great sin to him who was without sin. And because she had heard this: *Whoever is without sin, let him first cast a stone at her*; she expected to be punished by him in whom sin could not be found.

But he who had driven back her adversaries with the tongue of justice, lifting upon her the eyes of gentleness, asked her: *Has no one condemned you?* She answered: *Lord, no one.* And he: *Neither will I condemn you*; by whom perhaps you feared to be condemned, because in me you have not found sin. Neither will I condemn you.

What is this, Lord? Do you then favor sins?

Not at all so. Attend to what follows:

*Go, henceforth now sin no more*[^13].

Therefore the Lord also condemned, but the sin, not the man. For if he were a favorer of sins, he would say: Neither will I condemn you; go, live as you wish: be secure about my deliverance; however much you sin, I will free you from all penalty, even from the tormentors of Gehenna and hell. He did not say this.

**7.** Let those, then, who love in the Lord his gentleness also fear the truth. For *the Lord is sweet and upright*[^14]. You love what is sweet; fear what is upright. As gentle he said: *I have kept silence*: but as just: *Shall I always keep silence?*[^15] *The Lord is merciful and compassionate.* So indeed. Add further, long-suffering; add further, and very merciful: but fear what is at the end, *and truthful*[^16]. For those whom he now bears with as they sin, he is going to judge as they despise. Or *do you despise the riches of his long-suffering and gentleness, not knowing that the patience of God leads you to repentance? But you, according to the hardness of your heart and your impenitent heart, store up for yourself wrath on the day of wrath and of the revelation of the righteous judgment of God, who will render to each one according to his works*[^17].

The Lord is gentle, the Lord is long-suffering, the Lord is merciful: but the Lord is also just, and the Lord is truthful. He grants you space for correction: but you love delay more than amendment. Were you bad yesterday? Be good today. And have you spent today too in malice? At least change tomorrow. You always wait, and promise yourself very much from the mercy of God: as if he who promised you indulgence through repentance had also promised you a longer life. How do you know what tomorrow may bring forth? Rightly you say in your heart: When I have corrected myself, God will forgive me all my sins. We cannot deny that to the corrected and converted God has promised indulgence. For in which prophet do you read to me that he promised indulgence to the corrected, where you do not read to me that God promised you a long life?

## The mind wavers between hope and despair.

**8.** From both, then, men are in peril, both by hoping and by despairing, by contrary things, by contrary affections. Who is deceived by hoping? He who says: God is good, God is merciful, I will do what pleases me, what I like; I will loosen the reins to my desires, I will fulfill the longings of my soul.

Why this?

Because God is merciful, God is good, God is gentle.

These men are in peril through hope.

But through despair, those who, when they have fallen into grave sins, thinking that pardon can no longer be granted them even though they repent, and setting themselves down as without doubt destined to damnation, say among themselves: We are already to be damned, why do we not do what we wish? with the mind of gladiators destined to the sword. Therefore the despairing are troublesome: for now they have nothing they fear, and they are exceedingly to be feared.

These men despair kills, those hope. The mind wavers between hope and despair. We must beware lest hope kill you, and while you hope much from mercy, you fall into judgment: we must beware again lest despair kill you, and while you think that the grave things you have committed can no longer be forgiven you, you do not do penance, and you run into the Judge, Wisdom, who says: *And I too will laugh at your perdition*[^18].

What, then, does the Lord do with those who are imperiled by both diseases? To those who are imperiled by hope, he says this: *Do not delay to be converted to the Lord, and do not put it off from day to day; for his wrath will come suddenly, and in the time of vengeance he will destroy you*[^19]. To those who are imperiled by despair, what does he say? *On whatever day the wicked man is converted, I will forget all his iniquities*[^20]. For the sake, then, of those imperiled by despair, he set forth the harbor of indulgence: for the sake of those imperiled by hope and deluded by delays, he made the day of death uncertain. When the last day comes, you do not know. Are you ungrateful, because you have today, on which you may be corrected? So, then, to that woman: Neither will I condemn you; but, made secure about the past, beware of the future. Neither will I condemn you: I have blotted out what you committed, observe what I have commanded, that you may find what I have promised.
