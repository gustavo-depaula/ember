# Homily 8

## A marriage took place at Cana of Galilee[^1].

## The Lord, invited, came to the marriage. What wonder if he came to that house for a marriage, who came into this world for a marriage? For the Word is the bridegroom, and human flesh is the bride.

**1.** The miracle indeed of our Lord Jesus Christ, by which he made wine out of water, is no wonder to those who know that God did it. For he himself made the wine that day at the marriage in those six water jars, which he commanded to be filled with water—he who every year makes this in the vines. For just as what the servants put into the jars was turned into wine by the work of the Lord, so too what the clouds pour out is turned into wine by the work of the same Lord. But that we do not wonder at, because it happens every year: by its constancy it has lost its power to astonish. And yet it deserves greater consideration than what was done in the jars of water. For who is there who considers the works of God, by which this whole world is ruled and administered, and is not amazed and overwhelmed by miracles? If he were to consider the force of a single grain of any seed whatever, it is a great thing, an awe to one considering it.

But because human beings, intent on other things, have lost the consideration of the works of God, in which they might daily give praise to the Creator; God has, as it were, reserved to himself certain unusual things to do, so that, like sleeping men, he might rouse them by something more marvelous to worship him. A dead man rose again, and people marveled: so many are born every day, and no one marvels. If we consider more prudently, it is a greater miracle that one should exist who was not, than that one who was should come to life again. Yet the same God, the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, does all these things through his Word, and he who created governs.

The earlier miracles he did through his Word, God with him: the later miracles he did through that same Word of his, incarnate and made man for our sake. As we marvel at the things done through the man Jesus, let us marvel at the things done through the God Jesus. Through the God Jesus were made heaven and earth, the sea, and all the adornment of heaven, the riches of the earth, the fruitfulness of the sea; all these things that lie before our eyes were made through Jesus, God. And we see these things, and if his Spirit is in us, they so please us that the maker is praised: not so that, turning to the works, we are turned away from the maker, and, setting our face, as it were, toward the things he made, set our back toward him who made them.

**2.** And these things indeed we see, and they lie before our eyes: what of those things we do not see, such as the angels, the Virtues, Powers, Dominations, and every inhabitant of this supercelestial fabric, not lying before our eyes? Although often the angels too, when it was fitting, have shown themselves to human beings. Did not God make all these things through his Word, that is, through his only Son our Lord Jesus Christ?

What of the human soul itself, which is not seen, and yet through the works it exhibits in the flesh offers great wonder to those who consider well—by whom was it made, but by God? and through whom was it made, but through the Son of God? I do not yet speak of the soul of man. The soul of any beast whatever—how it rules its own mass! It puts forth all the senses, the eyes for seeing, the ears for hearing, the nostrils for perceiving smell, the judgment of the mouth for distinguishing flavors, and finally the limbs themselves for carrying out their offices. Is it the body that does these things, and not the soul, that is, the dweller in the body? And yet it is not seen by the eyes, and from the things it does it stirs wonder.

Let your consideration now pass also to the human soul, to which God has granted understanding to know its Creator, to discern and distinguish between good and evil, that is, between just and unjust: how great things it does through the body! Consider the whole world ordered in the very commonwealth of mankind: by what administrations, what orders of powers, conditions of cities, laws, customs, arts! All this is carried on through the soul, and this force of the soul is not seen. When it is withdrawn from the body, a corpse lies there: but when it is present to the body, it first, as it were, preserves it from rot. For all flesh is corruptible, and dissolves into decay, unless it is held together by a certain seasoning of the soul.

But this it has in common with the soul of a beast: more to be wondered at are the things I have spoken of, which pertain to the mind and understanding, where also it is renewed unto the image of its Creator, to whose image man was made. What will this force of the soul be, when *both this body has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality*[^2]? If it can do so much through corruptible flesh, what will it be able to do through a spiritual body after the resurrection of the dead? Yet this soul, as I have said, of a nature and substance to be wondered at, is an invisible and intelligible thing: and even this was made through Jesus, God, because he himself is the Word of God. *All things were made through him, and without him was nothing made*[^3].

## May he make us drunk with the invisible wine.

**3.** Since, then, we see such great things done through the God Jesus, why do we marvel at water turned into wine through the man Jesus? For he did not so become man that he lost what he was as God: man was added to him, God was not lost. He himself, then, did this, who did all those things. Let us therefore not marvel that God did it; but let us love that he did it among us, and did it for our restoration. For he intimates something to us in these very deeds. I think it was not without cause that he came to the marriage. Apart from the miracle, something of mystery and sacred sign lies hidden in the deed itself. Let us knock that he may open, and make us drunk with the invisible wine: because we too were water, and he made us wine, he made us wise; for we have a taste for faith in him, we who before were foolish. And perhaps it pertains to that very wisdom, with honor for God, and with praise of his majesty, and with love of his most powerful mercy, to understand what was accomplished in this miracle.

## And he himself, as it were, the bridegroom.

**4.** The Lord, invited, came to the marriage. What wonder if he came to that house for a marriage, who came into this world for a marriage? For if he did not come for a marriage, he has no bride here. And what is it that the Apostle says: *I have betrothed you to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ*[^4]? What is it that he fears, lest the virginity of Christ's bride be corrupted by the craft of the devil? *I fear*, he says, *lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his craftiness, so your minds too be corrupted from the simplicity and chastity that is in Christ*[^5]. He has here, then, a bride whom he redeemed with his blood, and to whom he gave the Holy Spirit as a pledge.

He rescued her from the bondage of the devil: he died for her offenses, he rose again for her justification. Who will offer such things to his bride? Let men offer whatever adornments of the earth; gold, silver, precious stones, horses, slaves, lands, estates: will anyone offer his own blood? For if he gave his blood to his bride, there would be no one to take a wife. But the Lord, dying without fear, gave his blood for her whom, rising again, he would have, whom he had already joined to himself in the womb of the Virgin. For the Word is the bridegroom, and human flesh the bride; and both are one Son of God, and the same Son of man: where he became head of the Church, that womb of the Virgin Mary was his bridal chamber, from there he came forth as a bridegroom from his chamber, as Scripture foretold: *And he, like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber, rejoiced like a giant to run the way*[^6]; he came forth from the chamber as a bridegroom, and, invited, came to the marriage.

**5.** By reason of a certain sacred sign, he seems not to acknowledge the mother from whom the bridegroom came forth, and to say to her: *What have I to do with you, woman? My hour has not yet come*[^7]. What is this? Did he come to the marriage for this, that he might teach mothers to be despised? Surely the one to whose marriage he had come was taking a wife in order to beget children; and from those whom he desired to beget, he surely wished to be honored: did he then come to the marriage in order to dishonor a mother, when marriages themselves are celebrated and wives taken for the sake of having children, to whom God commands that honor be rendered to parents? Without doubt, brothers, something lies hidden there.

For it is so great a matter that certain men—those whom the Apostle warned us to beware of, as we recalled above, saying: *I fear lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his craftiness, so your minds too be corrupted from the simplicity and chastity that is in Christ*—disparaging the Gospel and saying that Jesus was not born of the Virgin Mary, would try to take from this an argument for their error, so as to say: How was she his mother, to whom he said: *What have I to do with you, woman*?

We must therefore answer them, and explain why the Lord said this; lest they, raving against the sound faith, seem to have found something whereby the chastity of the virgin bride may be corrupted, that is, whereby the faith of the Church may be violated. For truly, brothers, the faith of those is corrupted who prefer falsehood to truth. For these men who seem so to honor Christ as to deny that he had flesh, proclaim him nothing other than a liar. Those, then, who build up falsehood in human beings, what do they expel from them, but the truth? They let in the devil, they shut out Christ; they let in the adulterer, they shut out the bridegroom: groomsmen, that is, or rather pimps of the serpent. For they speak to this end, that the serpent may possess and Christ be shut out. How does the serpent possess? When falsehood possesses. When falsity possesses, the serpent possesses: when truth possesses, Christ possesses. For he himself said: I am the truth[^8]; but of the serpent he said: And he did not stand in the truth, because the truth is not in him[^9]. Now Christ is the truth in such a way that you receive all that is true in Christ. The true Word, God equal to the Father; a true soul, true flesh, true man, true God, true nativity, true passion, true death, true resurrection. If you call any of these false, decay enters, from the venom of the serpent are born the worms of lies, and nothing will remain whole.

**6.** What, then, is it, he says, that the Lord says: *What have I to do with you, woman?* Perhaps in what follows the Lord shows us why he said this: *My hour*, he says, *has not yet come*. For thus he says: *What have I to do with you, woman? My hour has not yet come*[^10]. And why this was said must be inquired into. First, then, let us on this point withstand the heretics.

What does the drowsy serpent say, the ancient hisser and breather of poisons? What does he say?

Jesus did not have a woman for a mother.

How do you prove it?

Because he said, he replies: *What have I to do with you, woman?*

Who told this, that we should believe that he said this? Who told this? Surely John the Evangelist. But this same John the Evangelist said: *And the mother of Jesus was there*. For he so narrated it: *On the next day a marriage took place at Cana of Galilee, and the mother of Jesus was there. Now Jesus had come there, invited to the marriage with his disciples*[^11].

We hold two statements brought forth by the Evangelist. *The mother of Jesus was there*, the Evangelist said: what Jesus said to his mother, the same Evangelist said. And as he said that Jesus answered his mother, so that first he said: *His mother says to him*, see, brothers, that against the tongue of the serpent you have a fortified virginity of heart.

There in the Gospel itself, by the narration of that same Evangelist, it is said: *The mother of Jesus was there*; and: *His mother said to him*. Who told this? John the Evangelist. And what did Jesus answer his mother? *What have I to do with you, woman?* Who tells this? The same John the Evangelist. O most faithful and most truthful Evangelist, you tell me that Jesus said: *What have I to do with you, woman?* why did you set beside him a mother whom he does not acknowledge? For you said that the mother of Jesus was there, and that his mother said to him: why did you not rather say: *Mary was there*; and: *Mary said to him?* You tell both: *His mother said to him*; and: *Jesus answered her: What have I to do with you, woman?* Why this, except that both are true?

But those people are willing to believe the Evangelist in this, that he narrates Jesus to have said to his mother: *What have I to do with you, woman?* and unwilling to believe the Evangelist in this, that he says: *The mother of Jesus was there*; and: *His mother said to him*. But who is it that withstands the serpent and holds the truth, whose virginity of heart is not corrupted by the craft of the devil? He who believes both to be true; both that the mother of Jesus was there, and that Jesus answered his mother in that way. But if he does not yet understand in what way Jesus said: *What have I to do with you, woman?* let him meanwhile believe that he said it, and that he said it to his mother. Let there first be devotion in the believer, and there will be fruit in the one who understands.

## Let him approach the door of truth not by wrangling.

**7.** I ask you, O faithful Christians: Was the mother of Jesus there?

answer: She was.

How do you know?

answer: The Gospel says this.

What did Jesus answer his mother?

answer: What have I to do with you, woman? My hour has not yet come.

And this, how do you know?

answer: The Gospel says this.

Let no one corrupt this faith for you, if you wish to keep a chaste virginity for the bridegroom. But if it is asked of you why he answered his mother thus, let him who understands speak; but let him who does not yet understand nevertheless most firmly believe that Jesus answered this, and that he answered his mother. By this devotion he will deserve even to understand why he answered thus, if by praying he knocks, and approaches the door of truth not by wrangling. Only let him beware, lest while he thinks he knows, or is ashamed not to know why he answered thus, he be driven to believe either that the Evangelist lied who says: *The mother of Jesus was there*; or that Christ himself suffered a false death for our offenses, and showed false scars for our justification; and that he spoke falsely: *If you abide in my word, you are truly my disciples; and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free*[^12]. For if the mother is false, the flesh false, the death false, the wounds of the passion false, the scars of the resurrection false; then not the truth, but rather falsity, will set free those who believe in him.

Nay rather, let falsity yield to truth, and let all be confounded who wish to seem truthful for this reason, that they try to show Christ to be deceitful; and who are unwilling to have it said to them: We do not believe you, because you lie; while they say that the truth itself lied. To whom nevertheless, if we say: How do you know that Christ said: *What have I to do with you, woman?* they answer that they have believed the Gospel. Why do they not believe the Gospel when it says: *The mother of Jesus was there*; and: *His mother said to him*? Or if the Gospel lies in this, how is it believed in what Jesus said: *What have I to do with you, woman?* Why do not the wretches rather faithfully believe that the Lord answered thus not a stranger but his mother; and devoutly inquire why he answered thus?

For there is a great difference between him who says: I wish to know why Christ answered this to his mother; and him who says: I know that Christ did not answer this to his mother. It is one thing to wish to understand what is closed, another to refuse to believe what is open. He who says: I wish to know why Christ answered his mother thus, wishes the Gospel, which he believes, to be opened to him: but he who says: I know that Christ did not answer this to his mother, charges the Gospel itself with falsehood, where he believed that Christ answered thus.

## Let us believe the whole, and seek what we do not yet understand.

**8.** Now then, if you please, brothers, those men being repulsed, and ever wandering in their own blindness unless they are humbly healed, let us inquire why our Lord answered his mother thus. He was born in a singular way, of the Father without a mother, of the mother without a father; without a mother, God; without a father, man; without a mother before the ages, without a father at the end of the ages. What he answered, he answered his mother: because: *The mother of Jesus was there*; and: *His mother said to him*. All this the Gospel says. There we learn that the mother of Jesus was there, where we learn what he said to her: *What have I to do with you, woman? My hour has not yet come*. Let us believe the whole, and seek what we do not yet understand.

And first see this, lest perhaps, just as the Manichees found an occasion for their faithlessness because the Lord said: *What have I to do with you, woman?* so the astrologers may find an occasion for their fallacy because he said: *My hour has not yet come*. And if he said this according to the astrologers, we committed sacrilege by burning their books. But if we did rightly, as was done in the times of the Apostles, then the Lord did not say according to them: *My hour has not yet come*. For the empty talkers and seduced seducers say: You see that Christ was under fate, who says: *My hour has not yet come*.

To whom, then, must we answer first; the heretics, or the astrologers? For both come from that serpent, wishing to corrupt the virginity of the Church's heart, which she has in an unimpaired faith. First, if you please, let us answer those whom we had set before us, to whom indeed we have already for the most part answered. But lest they suppose that we have nothing to say about these words which the Lord answered his mother, we rather instruct you against them: for to refute them, I think that what has already been said is sufficient.

**9.** Why, then, does the son say to his mother: What have I to do with you, woman? My hour has not yet come. Our Lord Jesus Christ was both God and man: according to what he was as God, he had no mother; according to what he was as man, he had one. She was, then, the mother of his flesh, the mother of his humanity, the mother of the weakness which he took on for our sake. But the miracle which he was about to do, he was about to do according to his divinity, not according to his weakness; according to what he was as God, not according to what he was, born weak. But the weak thing of God is stronger than men[^13].

The mother, then, was demanding a miracle; but he, as though not acknowledging the human flesh, since he was about to do divine deeds, says, as it were: What in me works the miracle, you did not beget; my divinity you did not beget: but because you begot my weakness, I will acknowledge you then, when that very weakness shall hang upon the cross. For this is the meaning of: My hour has not yet come. For then he acknowledged her, who surely always knew her. And before he was born of her, he knew his mother in predestination; and before he himself, as God, created her from whom he himself, as man, would be created, he knew his mother: but at a certain hour he does not acknowledge her, in a mystery; and at a certain hour, which had not yet come, he again acknowledges her, in a mystery. For he acknowledged her then, when that which she bore was dying. For that by which Mary was made was not dying, but that which was made from Mary was dying: the eternity of the divinity was not dying, but the weakness of the flesh was dying.

He gave that answer, then, distinguishing, in the faith of believers, who he is, and by what means he came. For he came through a mother, a woman, being God and Lord of heaven and earth. According to that he is Lord of the world, Lord of heaven and earth, he is surely Lord also of Mary; according to that he is creator of heaven and earth, he is creator also of Mary: but according to what is said, *made of a woman, made under the Law*[^14], he is the son of Mary. He himself is the Lord of Mary, he himself the son of Mary: he himself the creator of Mary, he himself created from Mary. Do not wonder that he is both son and Lord: for as he was called the son of Mary, so also the son of David; and he is the son of David for this reason, that he is the son of Mary. Hear the Apostle saying openly: *Who was made for him of the seed of David according to the flesh*[^15]. Hear him also Lord of David; let David himself say this: *The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand*[^16]. And Jesus himself put this to the Jews, and from it confuted them.

As, then, he is both son and lord of David: son of David according to the flesh, lord of David according to the divinity: so he is the son of Mary according to the flesh, and the lord of Mary according to his majesty. Since, then, she was not the mother of his divinity, and it was through the divinity that the miracle was to come which she was asking; he answered her: *What have I to do with you, woman?* but lest you think that I deny you to be my mother: *My hour has not yet come*: for there I will acknowledge you, when the weakness whose mother you are shall begin to hang upon the cross.

Let us prove whether this is true. When the Lord suffered, as the same Evangelist says, who knew the Lord's mother, and who has shown us the Lord's mother also at this marriage, he himself narrates: *There was there, beside the cross, the mother of Jesus, and Jesus said to his mother: Woman, behold your son; and to the disciple: Behold your mother*[^17]. He commends his mother to the disciple: he commends his mother, being about to die before his mother, and to rise before his mother's death; man commends man to man. This Mary had borne. That hour had now come, of which he had then said: *My hour has not yet come*.

**10.** So far as I judge, brothers, the heretics have been answered: let us answer the astrologers. And from what do they themselves try to prove that Jesus was under fate? Because he himself says, they reply: *My hour has not yet come*. Therefore we believe him; and if he had said: I have no hour, he would have shut out the astrologers: but behold, they say, he himself said: *My hour has not yet come*. If, then, he had said: I have no hour, he would have shut out the astrologers, and there would have been no occasion for their calumny: but now, because he said: *My hour has not yet come*, what can we say against his own words?

It is strange that the astrologers, by believing the words of Christ, try to convince Christians that Christ lived under a fated hour. Let them, then, believe Christ saying: *I have power to lay down my soul, and to take it up again: no one takes it from me, but I lay it down of myself, and take it up again*[^18]. Is this power, then, under fate? Let them show a man who has the power, while he lives, of when he is to die: they will not show one at all. Let them, then, believe God saying: *I have power to lay down my soul, and to take it up again*: and let them inquire why it was said: *My hour has not yet come*, and not for that reason place under fate the founder of heaven, the creator and orderer of the stars. For if fate came from the stars, the founder of the stars could not be under the necessity of the stars. Add that not only did Christ not have what you call fate; but neither do you, nor I, nor he, nor any human being.

**11.** Nevertheless the seduced seduce, and put forth fallacies to human beings: they spread their nets to catch human beings, and that in the public squares. For those who spread nets to catch wild beasts do so in the woods and in the wilderness: how wretchedly vain are the human beings for whose catching nets are spread in the marketplace! Money is received when human beings sell themselves to human beings; these people give money to sell themselves to vanities. For they go in to the astrologer to buy for themselves masters, such as it shall please the astrologer to give—whether Saturn, or Jupiter, or Mercury, or whatever else of sacrilegious name. He went in free, that, having paid his money, he might go out a slave. Rather, he would not go in, if he were free: but he went in where his master, error, and his mistress, desire, dragged him. Whence also the Truth says: *Everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin*[^19].

## He who rose in the head will rise in the rest of the members.

**12.** Why, then, did he say: *My hour has not yet come?* Rather, because he had it in his power when he would die, he did not yet see that it was opportune to use that power. Just as we, brothers, for example, speak thus: It is now the fixed hour at which we should go out to celebrate the sacraments? If we go out before it is needful, are we not perverse and out of order? Since, then, we do not act except when it is opportune; do we for that reason, in these actions, when we speak so, take fate into account?

What, then, is: *My hour has not yet come?* When I know it is opportune for me to suffer, when my passion will be useful, that very hour has not yet come; then I will suffer of my own will: so that you may keep both: *My hour has not yet come*; and: *I have power to lay down my soul, and to take it up again*.

He had come, then, having in his power when he would die. But if he had died before he had chosen his disciples, it would surely have been out of order: if he were a man who did not have his hour in his power, he could die before he had chosen his disciples; and if perhaps he were to die when the disciples had already been chosen and trained, it would be granted to him, he would not do it himself. But truly he who had come holding in his hand when he would go, when he would return, how far he would run, to whom the lower regions would lie open, not only when dying but also when rising, so that he might show us the hope of his Church's immortality, showed in the head what the members ought to expect. He who rose in the head will rise also in the rest of the members. The hour, then, had not yet come, the opportunity was not yet.

The disciples were to be called, the kingdom of heaven was to be announced, mighty works were to be done, the divinity of the Lord was to be commended in miracles, the humanity of the Lord was to be commended in the very fellow-suffering of mortality. For he who hungered, because he was man, fed so many thousands with five loaves, because he was God: he who slept, because he was man, commanded the winds and the waves, because he was God. All these things were to be commended first, so that there might be something for the Evangelists to write, something to be preached to the Church. But when he had done as much as he judged sufficient, the hour came—not of necessity, but of will; not of constraint, but of power.

**13.** What, then, brothers? Because we have answered these and those, shall we say nothing of what the water jars mean, what the water turned into wine, what the steward of the feast, what the bridegroom, what the mother of Jesus mean in mystery, what the marriage itself! All these things are to be said, but you are not to be overburdened. I wished indeed, in the name of Christ, even yesterday, when a sermon is wont to be owed to your Charity, to do this with you, but I was not permitted, certain necessities hindering. If, then, it please your Holiness, let us defer to tomorrow what pertains to the mystery of this deed, and not overburden both your weakness and our own. There are perhaps many today who have gathered on account of the solemnity of the day, not on account of hearing a sermon. Tomorrow let those who will come, come to hear; so that we neither defraud the eager nor burden the disdainful.
