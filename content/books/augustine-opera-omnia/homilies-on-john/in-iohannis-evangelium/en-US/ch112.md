# Tractate 112

## *When Jesus had said these things, he went out with his disciples across the brook Kidron, where there was a garden*[^1].

## One word from the speaker, *I am he*, struck down, drove back, and laid low a crowd so fierce with hatred and so terrible in arms—without any weapon.

**1.** When the Lord had finished the great and lengthy discourse that he gave after supper to the disciples who were then with him—he who was now near to shedding his blood for us—and had added the prayer that he directed to the Father, John the evangelist then begins his account of the Lord's passion in this way: *When Jesus had spoken these words, he went forth with his disciples across the Kidron valley, where there was a garden, which he and his disciples entered. Now Judas, who betrayed him, also knew the place; for Jesus had often met there with his disciples*[^2]. When he relates that the Lord entered the garden with his disciples, this did not happen immediately after that prayer of his had ended—the prayer of whose words he says *When Jesus had spoken these words*—but certain other things came in between, things that this evangelist passes over and that are found in the other evangelists, just as in this one are found many things that they likewise left unmentioned in their own narratives. As for how they all agree with one another, so that the truth set forth by one is in no way contradicted by another, whoever desires to know this should seek it not in these homilies but in other, more laborious writings; and let him learn it not by standing and listening but rather by sitting and reading, or by lending to the reader the most attentive ear and mind. Yet let him believe it before he knows it—whether he can come to know it even in this life, or whether he cannot because of various hindrances—namely, that nothing has been written by any evangelist, as regards those whom the Church has received into canonical authority, that could be contrary to that evangelist's own truthful narrative, or to that of another no less truthful.

And so now let us look at this blessed John's narrative, which we have undertaken to expound, without comparing it with the others, not lingering over the things that are clear, so that we may do so where it is necessary, when the case requires it. Let us not, then, take what he says—*When Jesus had spoken these words, he went forth with his disciples across the Kidron valley, where there was a garden, which he and his disciples entered*—as though he entered that garden immediately after those words; rather, let what is said, *When Jesus had spoken these words*, carry only this much weight: that we should not suppose he entered before he had finished those words.

**2.** *Now Judas, who betrayed him,* he says, *also knew the place.* The order of the words is: he who betrayed him knew the place. *For,* he says, *Jesus had often met there with his disciples.* There, then, the wolf concealed in the sheep's skin, and tolerated among the sheep by the deep counsel of the Father of the household, learned where for a brief while he might scatter the flock, by lying in wait to attack the shepherd. *So Judas,* he says, *having procured a cohort and officers from the chief priests and the Pharisees, came there with lanterns and torches and weapons*[^3]. The cohort was not of Jews but of soldiers. Let it be understood, then, that it was procured from the governor, as if to seize an accused man, the order of lawful power being observed, so that no one would dare resist those making the arrest—although so great a band had also been gathered, and came so armed, that it might either terrify or even fight back, if anyone should dare to defend Christ. For thus was his power hidden and his weakness presented to view, so that these things seemed necessary to his enemies against him over whom nothing would have prevailed except what he himself had willed; good himself, he made good use of the wicked, and made good things out of evils, to make good people out of the wicked and to distinguish the good from the wicked.

**3.** *Jesus therefore*, as the evangelist goes on to add, *knowing all that was to come upon him, went forward and said to them, Whom do you seek? They answered him, Jesus of Nazareth. Jesus said to them, I am he. Judas, who betrayed him, was standing with them. When he said to them, I am he, they drew back and fell to the ground*[^4]. Where now is the cohort of soldiers and the officers of the chief priests and Pharisees? Where the terror and the protection of weapons? Surely one word from the speaker, *I am he*, struck down, drove back, and laid low a crowd so fierce with hatred and so terrible in arms—without any weapon. For God lay hidden in the flesh; and the everlasting day was so concealed in human members that he was sought by lanterns and torches, to be killed by darkness. *I am he,* he says; and he casts down the wicked. What will he do when he is to judge, who did this when he was to be judged? What will he be able to do when he is to reign, who was able to do this when he was to die? And now everywhere through the gospel Christ says, *I am he*; and the Jews await Antichrist, that they may go back and fall to the ground, since, deserting the things of heaven, they desire the things of earth. Surely his persecutors came with the betrayer to seize Jesus; whom they sought they found; they heard *I am he*: why did they not seize him, but go back and fall, except that he willed it so, who was able to do whatever he willed? Yet if he had never permitted himself to be seized by them, they indeed would not have done what they came for, but neither would he have done what he came for. They sought to kill him in their savagery; but he too was seeking us by dying. Therefore, since he showed his power to those who wished to seize him and could not, let them now seize him, that he may work his will upon those who know it not.

**4.** *Again therefore he asked them, Whom do you seek? And they said, Jesus of Nazareth. Jesus answered, I told you that I am he. So if you seek me, let these men go. This was to fulfill the word that he had spoken, Of those whom you gave me I lost not one*[^5]. *If you seek me,* he says, *let these men go.* He sees his enemies, and they do what he commands; they let go those whom he does not wish to perish. But were they not going to die later? Why then, if they had died at that moment, would he have lost them, except that they did not yet believe in him as those believe who do not perish?

## The author of the cup is also the very one who drinks it.

**5.** *Simon Peter, therefore, having a sword, drew it and struck the high priest's servant and cut off his right ear. The servant's name was Malchus*[^6]. This evangelist alone gave even the name of this servant, just as Luke alone records that the Lord touched his ear and healed him. Now "Malchus" means "one who will reign." What then does the ear cut off for the Lord's sake and healed by the Lord signify, except hearing renewed once the old condition is cut away, so that one is in the newness of the spirit and not in the oldness of the letter? And one to whom this has been granted by Christ—who can doubt that he will reign with Christ? But that he was found a servant—this too belongs to that old condition which begets into slavery, which is Hagar. Yet when soundness came, freedom too was figured. Still, the Lord disapproved of Peter's deed and forbade him to go further, saying, *Put your sword into its sheath. Shall I not drink the cup that the Father has given me?*[^7] For in his deed that disciple wished to defend his master; he was not thinking of what was to be signified. And so the one had to be admonished to patience, and this had to be recorded for our understanding. But that he says the cup of his passion was given him by the Father—this is surely what the Apostle says: *If God is for us, who is against us? He who did not spare his own Son but gave him up for us all*[^8]. Yet the author of this cup is also the very one who drinks it. Hence the same apostle says again: *Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, an offering and sacrifice to God as a fragrant odor*[^9].

**6.** *The cohort, the tribune, and the officers of the Jews seized Jesus and bound him*[^10]. They seized him to whom they did not draw near; for that was their day, but they remained in darkness, and they did not hear: *Draw near to him and be enlightened*[^11]. For if they had drawn near in this way, they would have laid hold of him not to kill him with their hands but to receive him in their heart. As it was, when they seized him in that manner, then they withdrew farther from him; and they bound him by whom they ought rather to have wished to be loosed. And perhaps there were among them some who at that time laid their fetters upon Christ and, later set free by him, said: *You have burst my bonds*[^12].

Let this be enough for today; what follows will be treated, God willing, in another homily.
