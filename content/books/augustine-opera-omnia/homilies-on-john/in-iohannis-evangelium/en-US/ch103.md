# Tractate 103

## *Be confident, I have overcome the world*[^1].

## They were confident and overcame. In whom, but in him? For he would not have overcome the world, if the world overcame his members.

**1.** What the disciples of Christ were like, when he spoke with them before the passion great things together with little ones — but in the way it was fitting that great things be said even to little ones, because, the Holy Spirit not yet received as they received him after his resurrection, whether by his breathing or from on high, they savored human things rather than divine — is declared by many tokens throughout the whole Gospel: whence also is this which they said in this passage. For the Evangelist says: *His disciples say to him: Behold, now you speak openly, and say no proverb: now we know that you know all things, and have no need that anyone question you; in this we believe that you came forth from God*[^2]. The Lord himself had said a little before: *These things I have spoken to you in proverbs: the hour comes when I will no longer speak to you in proverbs*[^3]. How, then, do these men say, *Behold, now you speak openly, and say no proverb*? Had the hour already come, in which he had promised that he would no longer speak in proverbs? That the hour had not yet come, the continuation of his words shows, which runs thus: *These things,* he says, *I have spoken to you in proverbs: the hour comes when I will no longer speak to you in proverbs, but openly will announce to you concerning my Father. In that day you will ask in my name: and I do not say to you that I will ask the Father concerning you; for the Father himself loves you, because you have loved me, and have believed that I came forth from God. I came forth from the Father, and came into the world: again I leave the world, and go to the Father*[^4]. Since through all these words he still promises that hour in which he will no longer speak in proverbs, but openly announce to them concerning the Father; in which hour he says they will ask in his name, and that he will not ask the Father concerning them, because the Father himself loves them, since they too have loved Christ, and have believed that he came forth from the Father and came into the world, again to leave the world and go to the Father: since, then, that hour in which he will speak without proverbs is still promised, why do these men say, *Behold, now you speak openly, and say no proverb*; unless because those things which he knows to be proverbs to those who do not understand, they so far fail to understand that they do not even understand that they do not understand? For they were little ones, and did not yet spiritually judge the things they heard concerning matters belonging not to the body but to the spirit.

**2.** Finally, admonishing them concerning their very age, still small and weak according to the inner man: *Jesus answered them: Do you now believe? Behold, the hour comes, and has already come, that you will be scattered, each one to his own, and will leave me alone. And I am not alone, because the Father is with me*[^5]. A little before he had said, *I leave the world, and go to the Father*; now he says, *The Father is with me.* Who goes to him who is with him? But this is a word to one who understands, a proverb to one who does not understand: yet so that what is now not understood by the little ones is in some way sucked; and to them, even if it does not offer solid food, because they do not yet grasp it, it at least does not deny the milky nourishment. From this nourishment is that they knew he knew all things, and that he had no need that anyone question him: why they said this can be asked.

For it seems rather that it ought to have been said: You have no need to question anyone; not, *that anyone question you.* For they said, *We know that you know all things*: and surely he who knows all things is rather wont to be questioned by those who do not know, so that the questioners may hear what they wish, from him who knows all things; not that he himself should question, as though wishing to know something, he who knows all things. What, then, do they mean, that of him whom they knew to know all things — when they seem to have ought to say, You have no need to question anyone — they thought it should rather be said, *You have no need that anyone question you*? And what of the fact that we read both done, namely that the Lord both questioned and was questioned? But this is quickly solved: because this was needful not to him, but rather to those whom he questioned, or by whom he was questioned. For he did not question any in order to learn anything from them, but rather to teach them. And those who questioned him, wishing to learn something from him, certainly needed this, that they might know some things from him who knew all things. Without doubt, then, for this reason there was no need that anyone should question him. For we, when we are questioned by those who wish to know something from us, learn from their very questions what they wish to learn; we therefore need to be questioned by those whom we wish to teach something, that we may know their inquiries to which we must respond: but he who knew all things did not even need this; nor did he need to learn through anyone's question what each one wished to know from him, because before he was questioned he knew the will of the one about to question. But he allowed himself to be questioned for this reason, that he might show, either to those who were then present, or to those who would hear these things spoken, or read them written, what sort those were by whom he was questioned; and in that way we might know either by what frauds he was not circumvented, or by what approaches one might advance with him. But to foresee the thoughts of men, and therefore to have no need that anyone question him, was not great for God, but was great for the little ones who said to him, *In this we believe that you came forth from God.* But much greater was that to which he wished them to be stretched and to grow: that when they said, and said truly, *You came forth from God,* he said, *The Father is with me*; lest they should so think that the Son came forth from the Father as to suppose that he had also withdrawn.

## The Lord spoke that the disciples might have peace in him.

**3.** Then, concluding this great and long discourse: *These things,* he says, *I have spoken to you, that you may have peace in me. In the world you will have tribulation; but be confident, I have overcome the world*[^6]. That tribulation was going to have its beginning in that of which, above, in order to show them to be little ones — to whom, still not understanding and supposing one thing for another, whatever great and divine things he said were in a manner proverbs — he said: *Do you now believe? Behold, the hour comes, and has already come, that you will be scattered, each one to his own.* Behold the beginning of the tribulation, but not one that would persevere in that manner. For what he added, *and will leave me alone,* he does not wish them to be such in the subsequent tribulation, which they were going to have in the world after his ascension, that they should leave him; but that they should have peace in him by remaining in him. For not only with their flesh, when he was seized, did they leave his flesh, but they left their faith with their mind too. To this belongs what he says: *Do you now believe? Behold, the hour comes, that you will be scattered to your own, and will leave me*: as if he said: Then you will be so troubled that you will leave even that which you now believe. For they came to such despair, and to such a death, so to speak, of their former faith, as appeared in that Cleophas, who, after his resurrection, not knowing that he was speaking with him, and narrating what had befallen him, said: *We were hoping that he was the one who would redeem Israel*[^7]. Behold how they had left him, forsaking even that very faith by which they had before believed in him. But in that tribulation which, after his glorification, the Holy Spirit having been received, they bore, they did not leave him: and although they fled from city to city, they did not flee from him; but that, having tribulation in the world, they might hold peace in him, they were not fugitives from him, but rather had him as their refuge. For, the Holy Spirit having been given them, there was brought about in them what has now been said to them: *Be confident, I have overcome the world.* They were confident, and overcame. In whom, but in him? For he would not have overcome the world, if the world overcame his members. Whence the Apostle says: *Thanks be to God, who gives us the victory*; and at once added, *through our Lord Jesus Christ*[^8]: who had said to his own, *Be confident, I have overcome the world.*
