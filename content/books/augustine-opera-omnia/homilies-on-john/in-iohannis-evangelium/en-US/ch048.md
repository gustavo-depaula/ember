# Tractate 48

## *I and the Father are one*[^1].

## *I said: You are gods.* If the word of God came to men that they should be called gods, the very Word of God which is with God, how is it not God? If through the word of God men become gods, if by participating they become gods, that in which they participate is not God?

**1.** What I have already commended to your Love, you ought firmly to remember: that the holy evangelist John does not wish us always to be nourished with milk, but to feed on solid food. But whoever is still less fit to take the solid food of the word of God, let him be nourished with the miLuke of faith, and let him not hesitate to believe the word he cannot understand. For faith is the merit, understanding the reward. In the very labor of attention the keen edge of our mind sweats, that it may lay aside the little filth of human mist, and be made serene toward the Word of God. Let the labor, then, not be refused, if love is present: for you know that he who loves does not labor. For all labor is heavy to those who do not love. If greed bears such great labors with the avaricious, does love not bear them with us?

**2.** Attend to the Gospel: *Now the Feast of Dedication took place in Jerusalem*[^2]. The "Encaenia" was the festival of the dedication of the temple. For in Greek whatever is new is so called; whenever something new has been dedicated, it is called "Encaenia." This word is even in common use: if anyone puts on a new tunic, he is said to "inaugurate" it. For that day on which the temple was dedicated the Jews solemnly celebrated: that very feast day was being kept, when the Lord spoke the things that were read.

## He who believes approaches; he who denies withdraws.

**3.** *It was winter. And Jesus was walking in the temple, in the porch of Solomon. So the Jews surrounded him, and said to him: How long do you take away our life? If you are the Christ, tell us plainly*[^3]. They did not desire the truth, but were preparing slander.

It was winter, and they were cold: for they were sluggish to approach that divine fire. But to approach is to believe: he who believes approaches; he who denies withdraws. The soul is not moved by feet, but by affections. They had grown cold from the love of loving, and burned with the desire of harming. They were far away, and were there: they did not approach by believing, and they pressed upon him by persecuting. They were seeking to hear from the Lord: I am the Christ: and perhaps they thought of Christ according to man. The Prophets preached Christ: but the divinity of Christ, both in the Prophets and in the Gospel itself, not even the heretics understand; how much less the Jews, as long as the veil is over their heart[^4]? Finally, in a certain place the Lord Jesus, knowing that they thought of Christ according to man, not according to God—according to that which he was man, not according to that which he remained God even when man was taken on—said to them: *What do you think about the Christ? Whose son is he?*[^5] They answered according to their opinion: David's. For so they had read, and this only they held: because they read his divinity, but did not understand it. But the Lord, that he might lift them up to seeking his divinity, whose weakness they despised, answered them: *How, then, does David in the Spirit call him Lord, saying: The Lord said to my Lord: Sit at my right hand, until I put your enemies under your feet? If, then, David in the Spirit calls him Lord, how is he his son?*[^6] He did not deny, but asked. Lest anyone, hearing this, think that the Lord Jesus denied that he was the son of David. If the Lord Christ denied himself to be the son of David, he would not enlighten the blind who so invoked him. For he was once passing by, and two blind men sitting beside the way cried out: *Have mercy on us, Son of David*[^7]. Hearing which voice, he had mercy; he stood, healed them, gave them light; because he acknowledged the name. The apostle Paul too says: *Who was made for him from the seed of David according to the flesh*[^8]: and to Timothy: *Remember that Jesus Christ rose from the dead, of the seed of David, according to my Gospel*[^9]. Because the Virgin Mary drew her origin from the seed of David, thence the Lord was of the seed of David.  
**4.** This the Jews sought from Christ as a great thing, that if he said: I am the Christ, according to that which they alone thought, of the seed of David, they might slander him as arrogating to himself royal power. What he answered them is more: they wished to slander about the son of David, he answered that he was the Son of God. And how? Hear: *Jesus answered them: I speak to you, and you do not believe; the works that I do in the name of my Father, these bear witness about me: but you do not believe, because you are not of my sheep*[^10]. You have already learned above what the sheep are: be sheep. They are sheep by believing, they are sheep by following the shepherd, they are sheep by not despising the redeemer, they are sheep by entering through the door, they are sheep by going out and finding pastures, they are sheep by enjoying eternal life. How, then, did he say to these: You are not of my sheep? Because he saw them predestined to everlasting ruin, not purchased by the price of his blood to eternal life.  
There no grass withers, all is green, all flourishes.  
**5.** *My sheep hear my voice, and I know them, and they follow me: and I give them eternal life*[^11]. Behold, there are the pastures. If you recall, he had said above: *And he will go in, and go out, and find pastures*[^12]. We have gone in by believing, we go out by dying. But as we have gone in through the door of faith, so let us go out as believers from the body: for so we go out through that very door, that we may be able to find pastures. The good pastures are called eternal life: there no grass withers, all is green, all flourishes. A certain plant is wont to be called "ever-living": there alone is found to live. *Eternal life,* he says, *I will give them*, to my sheep. You seek slanders for this reason, that you think about the present life.  
Let him who is wise grasp it; let him who does not grasp it believe, be nourished, and he will grasp it.  
**6.** *And they will not perish forever*: you understand, as though he said to them: You will perish forever, because you are not of my sheep. *No one will seize them out of my hand*[^13]. Receive it more attentively: *What my Father has given me is greater than all*[^14]. What can the wolf do? what can the thief and the robber do? They do not destroy any but those predestined to ruin. But of those sheep, of whom the Apostle says: *The Lord knows who are his*[^15]; and: *Those whom he foreknew, them he also predestined; but those whom he predestined, them he also called; but those whom he called, them he also justified; but those whom he justified, them he also glorified*[^16]: of these sheep neither does the wolf seize, nor the thief take, nor the robber kill. He is secure about their number, who knows what he gave for them. And this is what he says: *No one will seize them out of my hand*: and likewise to the Father: *What my Father has given me is greater than all.* What did the Father give to the Son greater than all? That he himself should be his only-begotten Son. What, then, is, he gave? Was there already one to whom he might give, or did he give by begetting? For if there was one to whom he might give that he should be the Son, at some time he was, and was not the Son: far be it that at some time the Lord Christ was, and was not the Son. Of us this can be said: at some time we were the children of men, we were not the children of God. For grace made us children of God, him nature, because he was so born. And it is not for you to say: He was not before he was born: for he never was unborn, who was coeternal with the Father. Let him who is wise grasp it; let him who does not grasp it believe, be nourished, and he will grasp it. The Word of God is always with the Father, and always the Word: and because the Word, therefore the Son. Always, then, the Son, and always equal. For not by growing, but by being born, is he equal, who is always born of the Father, the Son, God of God, of the eternal coeternal. But the Father is not God of the Son: the Son is God of the Father; therefore the Father by begetting gave to the Son that he should be God, by begetting gave that he should be coeternal with himself, by begetting gave that he should be equal. This is what is greater than all. How is the Son life, and the Son having life? What he has, that he is: you are one thing, you have another. For example, you have wisdom; are you wisdom itself? Finally, because you are not yourself what you have, if you lose what you have, you return to not having it: and now you take it up again, now you lose it. As our eye does not have light in itself inseparably; it is opened and takes it in, it is closed and loses it. Not so is God the Son of God, not so is the Word of the Father; not so is the Word that does not pass by sounding, but abides by being born. He so has wisdom that he himself is wisdom, and makes the wise; he so has life that he himself is life, and makes the living. This is what is greater than all. John the evangelist himself attended to heaven and earth, wishing to speak of the Son of God; he attended, and transcended. He thought of the thousands of armies of angels above heaven, he thought and transcended the whole creation with his mind, as the eagle the clouds: he transcended all great things, arrived at that which is greater than all; and said: *In the beginning was the Word*[^17]. But because he whose Word it is is not of the Word, while the Word is of him whose Word it is; therefore he says: *What my Father has given me*, that is, that I should be his Word, that I should be his only-begotten Son, that I should be the splendor of his light, is greater than all. Therefore: *No one,* he says, *seizes my sheep out of my hand. No one can seize them out of the hand of my Father.*  
**7.** Out of my hand, and out of the hand of my Father: what is this: No one seizes out of my hand, and: No one seizes out of the hand of my Father? Is the hand of the Father and the Son one, or is perhaps the Son himself the hand of his Father? If we understand the hand as power, the power of the Father and the Son is one; because the divinity is one: but if we understand the hand as it was said through the prophet: *And the arm of the Lord, to whom has it been revealed?*[^18], the hand of the Father is the Son himself. Which was not said as though God had a human form, and, as it were, the members of a body; but because through him all things were made. For men too are wont to say that other men are their hands, through whom they do what they will. Sometimes even the very work of a man is called the hand of a man, that which is done through the hand: as anyone is said to recognize his own hand, when he recognizes what he wrote. Since, then, in many ways even a man's hand is so called, who properly has a hand among the members of his body; how much more, in not one way only, is it to be understood when one reads the hand of God, who has no form of a body? And through this, in this place we better understand the hand of the Father and the Son as the power of the Father and the Son; lest perhaps, when we have taken the hand of the Father to be called the Son himself, a carnal thought begin to seek a Son even of the Son himself, whom it might similarly believe to be the hand of Christ. Therefore: *No one seizes out of the hand of my Father*; this is: No one seizes them from me.  
**8.** But lest perhaps you still waver, hear what follows: *I and the Father are one*[^19]. Up to this point the Jews could bear it: they heard: I and the Father are one, and they did not bear it; and, hard after their manner, they ran for stones. They took up stones, that they might stone him. The Lord, because he did not suffer what he did not wish to suffer, and did not suffer except what he willed to suffer, addresses them while they still desired to stone him. *The Jews took up stones, that they might stone him. Jesus answered them: Many good works I have shown you from my Father; for which of these works do you stone me? And they answered: For a good work we do not stone you, but for blasphemy, and because you, being a man, make yourself God*[^20]. To that they answered this, which he had said: I and the Father are one. Behold, the Jews understood what the Arians do not understand. For they were angry for this reason, that they perceived it could not be said: I and the Father are one, except where there is equality of the Father and the Son.  
**9.** But see what the Lord answered to the slow. He saw that they could not bear the splendor of the truth, and he tempered it in his words. *Is it not written in your Law*, that is, given to you, *that I said: You are gods?*[^21] God says through the prophet in the Psalm to men: *I said: You are gods*[^22]. And the Lord called "Law" generally all those Scriptures: although elsewhere he says "the Law" specifically, distinguishing it from the Prophets; as is: *The Law and the Prophets until John*[^23]; and: *On these two precepts the whole Law hangs, and the Prophets*[^24]. But sometimes he distributes the same Scriptures into three, where he says: *It was necessary that all things be fulfilled which are written in the Law, and the Prophets, and the Psalms about me*[^25]. Now indeed he called the Psalms too by the name of Law, where it is written: I said: You are gods. *If he called them gods to whom the word of God came, and the Scripture cannot be broken: of him whom the Father sanctified and sent into the world, you say: You blaspheme; because I said: I am the Son of God?*[^26] If the word of God came to men that they should be called gods, the very Word of God which is with God, how is it not God? If through the word of God men become gods, if by participating they become gods, that in which they participate is not God? If the enlightened lights are gods, is the light that enlightens not God? If those warmed in a manner by a saving fire are made gods, is that by which they are warmed not God? You approach the light and are enlightened, and are numbered among the children of God; if you withdraw from the light, you are darkened, and are reckoned in the darkness: yet that light neither approaches itself, because it does not withdraw from itself. If, then, the word of God makes you gods, how is the Word of God not God? Therefore the Father sanctified his Son, and sent him into the world. Perhaps someone says: If the Father sanctified him, then at some time he was not holy? He sanctified in the same way that he begot. For that he should be holy he gave him by begetting, because he begot him holy. For if what is sanctified was not holy before; how do we say to God the Father: *Hallowed be your name*[^27]?  
Recognize what is proper to the Lord, and the gift of the slave.  
**10.** *If I do not do the works of my Father, do not believe me: but if I do, and if you will not believe me, believe the works, that you may know and believe that the Father is in me, and I in him*[^28]. The Son does not say, the Father is in me, and I in him, in the way that men can say it. For if we think well, we are in God; and if we live well, God is in us: the faithful, partaking of his grace, enlightened by him, are in him, and he in us. But not so the only-begotten Son: he is in the Father, and the Father in him, as an equal in the one to whom he is equal. Finally, we at some time can say: We are in God, and God is in us: can we say, I and God are one? You are in God, because God contains you; God is in you, because you have been made the temple of God: but because you are in God, and God is in you, can you say: He who sees me sees God: as the Only-begotten said: *He who has seen me has seen the Father also*[^29]; and: I and the Father are one? Recognize what is proper to the Lord, and the gift of the slave. What is proper to the Lord is equality with the Father: the gift of the slave is partaking of the Savior.  
**11.** *So they sought to seize him*[^30]. Would that they had seized him, but by believing and understanding, not by raging and killing. For now, my brothers, when I speak such things—I, weak, things strong; I, small, things great; I, fragile, things solid—both you, as from the same lump from which I too am, and I myself who speak to you, all together wish to seize Christ. What is it to seize? You have understood, you have seized. But not so the Jews: you have seized that you may have, they wished to seize that they might not have. And because they wished so to seize him, what did he do to them? *He went out from their hands.* They did not seize him, because they did not have the hands of faith. The Word was made flesh: but it was not a great thing for the Word to cast its own flesh out of the hands of the flesh. To seize the Word with the mind, this is to seize Christ rightly.  
**12.** *And he went away again across the Jordan, to the place where John was at first baptizing, and remained there. And many came to him, and said: John indeed did no sign*[^31]. You remember it was said to you about John, that he was a lamp, and bore witness to the day[^32]. What, then, did these men say among themselves: John did no sign? John, they say, showed no miracle: he did not put demons to flight, he did not expel fever, he did not enlighten the blind, he did not raise the dead, he did not feed so many thousands of men from five or seven loaves, he did not waLuke upon the sea, he did not command the winds and the waves; none of these things did John do: and yet whatever he said, he bore witness to this one. Through the lamp let us come to the day. John did no sign. *But all the things, whatever John said about this one, were true*[^33]. Behold those who seized him, not as the Jews did. The Jews wished to seize him departing, these seized him abiding. Finally, what follows? *And many believed in him*[^34].
