# Tractate 115

## *My kingdom is not of this world* (Jn 18:33-40).

Here is his kingdom until the end of the age, having within it weeds mingled until the harvest; for the harvest is the end of the age, when the reapers will come, that is, the angels, and gather out of his kingdom all causes of offense—which surely would not happen if his kingdom were not here. Yet it is not from here, because it is on pilgrimage in the world.

**1.** What Pilate said to Christ, and what Christ answered Pilate, is to be considered and treated in this homily. For when it had been said to the Jews, *Take him yourselves and judge him according to your law*[^1], and they had answered, *It is not lawful for us to put anyone to death, Pilate entered the praetorium again and called Jesus and said to him, Are you the King of the Jews? And Jesus answered, Do you say this of yourself, or did others say it to you about me?*[^2] The Lord, of course, knew both what he himself asked and what the other would answer; yet he wished it to be said, not that he himself might know, but that there might be written what he wished us to know. *Pilate answered, Am I a Jew? Your own nation and the chief priests have handed you over to me; what have you done? Jesus answered, My kingdom is not of this world. If my kingdom were of this world, my servants would surely be fighting, that I might not be handed over to the Jews; but as it is, my kingdom is not from here*[^3]. This is what the good master wished us to know; but first the vain opinion of men—whether of the Gentiles or of the Jews, from whom Pilate had heard it—about his kingdom had to be shown to us: as though he were to be punished with death for this reason, that he had aspired to an unlawful kingdom; or because reigning men are wont to envy those who are to reign, and care had to be taken, evidently, lest his kingdom should be hostile either to the Romans or to the Jews. Now the Lord could have answered what he says—*My kingdom is not of this world*, and the rest—to the governor's first question, when he said to him, *Are you the King of the Jews?* But by questioning him in turn whether he said this of himself or had heard it from others, he wished, by the other's answer, to show that this had been thrown at him before Pilate as a kind of charge by the Jews; making plain to us the thoughts of men, which he himself knew to be vain. And to them, after Pilate's reply, now answering both Jews and Gentiles more opportunely and fittingly: *My kingdom is not of this world.* But if he had answered Pilate's question at once, he would have seemed to be answering not also the Jews, but only the Gentiles, who held this opinion of him. As it was, however, since Pilate answered, *Am I a Jew? Your own nation and the chief priests have handed you over to me*, he removed from himself the suspicion by which he might be thought to have said of himself that Jesus was King of the Jews, showing that he had heard this from the Jews. Then, by saying *What have you done?*, he sufficiently shows that this was charged against him as a crime—as if he said: If you deny that you are a king, what have you done that you should be handed over to me? As though it were no wonder if one who called himself a king were handed over to a judge to be punished; but if he did not say this, then it had to be asked of him what else he might perhaps have done, for which he deserved to be handed over to a judge.

## God has transferred us into the kingdom of the Son of his love.

**2.** Hear, then, Jews and Gentiles; hear, O circumcision; hear, O uncircumcision; hear, all you earthly kingdoms: I do not hinder your dominion in this world; *my kingdom is not of this world.* Do not fear with that most vain fear by which the elder Herod was terrified when it was announced that Christ was born, and slew so many infants that death might reach him too, made more cruel by fear than by anger: *My kingdom,* he says, *is not of this world.* What more do you want? Come to the kingdom that is not of this world; come by believing, and do not rage with fear. He says, indeed, in the prophecy concerning God the Father: *But I have been set up by him as king over Zion his holy mountain*[^4]; but that Zion and that mountain is not of this world. For what is his kingdom but those who believe in him, to whom he says: *You are not of the world, as I also am not of the world*[^5]? Although he wished them to be in the world; on which account he said of them to the Father: *I do not ask that you take them out of the world, but that you keep them from evil*[^6]. Hence here too he does not say, My kingdom is not *in* this world, but, *is not of this world.* And when he proved this, saying, *If my kingdom were of this world, my servants would surely be fighting, that I might not be handed over to the Jews*, he does not say, but as it is, my kingdom is not *here*, but, *is not from here.* For here is his kingdom until the end of the age, having within it weeds mingled until the harvest; for the harvest is the end of the age, when the reapers will come, that is, the angels, and gather out of his kingdom all causes of offense—which surely would not happen if his kingdom were not here. Yet it is not from here, because it is on pilgrimage in the world; for to his kingdom he says: *You are not of the world, but I chose you out of the world*[^7]. They were, then, of the world when they were not his kingdom, but belonged to the prince of the world. Of the world, therefore, is everyone among men who, though created by the true God, is begotten of the corrupt and condemned stock of Adam; but whatever has been regenerated in Christ from that stock has become a kingdom no longer of the world. For thus God *rescued us from the power of darkness and transferred us into the kingdom of the Son of his love*[^8]; of which kingdom he says: *My kingdom is not of this world*; or, *My kingdom is not from here.*

**3.** *Pilate therefore said to him, So you are a king? Jesus answered, You say that I am a king*[^9]. It was not that he feared to confess himself a king; but *You say* is so balanced that he neither denies himself to be a king—for he is a king whose kingdom is not of this world—nor confesses himself to be such a king as one whose kingdom is thought to be of this world. For such a one the man supposed him to be who had said, *So you are a king?* to whom the answer was, *You say that I am a king.* For it was said, *You say*, as if it had been said: A carnal man, you speak carnally.

**4.** Then he adds: *For this I was born, and for this I came into the world, to bear witness to the truth*[^10]. The vowel of this pronoun in what he says—*For this I was born*—is not to be drawn out, as though he said: In this thing I was born; but it is to be pronounced short, as though he said: For this purpose I was born, or for this I was born; as he says, *For this I came into the world.* For in the Greek of the gospel there is nothing ambiguous in this expression. Hence it is plain that he here mentioned his temporal birth, by which he came incarnate into the world; not that birth without beginning by which he was God, through whom the Father founded the world. He said, then, that he was born *for this*, that is, born on account of this, and had come into the world for this—namely, by being born of the Virgin—that he might bear witness to the truth.

But because faith is not in all, he added and said: *Everyone who is of the truth hears my voice*[^11]. He hears, indeed, with his inner ears, that is, he obeys my voice—which would have the same force as if he said: He believes me. So when Christ bears witness to the truth, he surely bears witness to himself; for it is his voice: *I am the truth*[^12]; and he said also in another place: *I bear witness to myself*[^13]. But in saying *Everyone who is of the truth hears my voice*, he commended the grace by which he calls according to his purpose. Of which purpose the Apostle says: *We know that for those who love God all things work together for good, for those who are called according to God's purpose*[^14]—the purpose, that is, of the one who calls, not of those called; which is set forth more plainly elsewhere thus: *Labor for the gospel according to the power of God, who saves us and calls us with his holy calling, not according to our works but according to his own purpose and grace*[^15]. For if we consider the nature in which we were created, since the truth created all, who is not of the truth? But not all are those to whom it is granted, by the truth itself, to hear the truth—that is, to obey the truth and to believe in the truth; without doubt with no preceding merits, lest grace be not grace. For if he had said, Everyone who hears my voice is of the truth, then one would be thought to be called of the truth because he obeys the truth; but he does not say this; rather he says, *Everyone who is of the truth hears my voice.* And so it is not because he hears his voice that he is of the truth, but because he is of the truth that he hears—that is, because this gift has been conferred on him by the truth. And what is this but that, by Christ's giving, he believes in Christ?

**5.** *Pilate said to him, What is truth?*[^16] Nor did he wait to hear an answer; but *when he had said this, he went out again to the Jews and said to them, I find no cause in him. But you have a custom that I release one man for you at the Passover; do you wish, then, that I release for you the King of the Jews?*[^17] I believe that when Pilate had said, *What is truth?*, the custom of the Jews at once came into his mind, by which one man used to be released to them at the Passover; and therefore he did not wait for Jesus to answer him what truth is, lest there be delay, since he had recalled the custom by which Jesus could be released to them at the Passover; for it is plain that he very much wished this. Yet it could not be torn from his heart that Jesus was King of the Jews, as though the truth itself, of which he had asked what it was, had fixed this there, as in the inscription. But when this was heard, *They all cried out again, saying, Not this man, but Barabbas. Now Barabbas was a robber*[^18]. We do not blame you, O Jews, for freeing a guilty man at the Passover, but for killing the innocent one; which, however, unless it had been done, the true Passover would not have come to pass. But the shadow of the truth was held by the erring Jews, and by a wonderful dispensation of divine wisdom the truth of that same shadow was being fulfilled through deceitful men; for in order that the true Passover might come to pass, Christ was being sacrificed like a sheep.

From here follow the insults that Pilate and his cohort heaped upon Christ; but these must be treated in another discussion.
