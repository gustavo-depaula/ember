# Tractate 64

## *Little children, yet a little while I am with you. You will seek me* (Jn 13:31-36).

## *Yet a little while I am with you.* Still, like them, he too was in the weakness of the flesh, until, namely, he should die and rise again; because after he rose, he was indeed with them forty days by the showing of bodily presence, but he was not with them in the fellowship of human weakness.

**1.** The ordered connection of the Lord's words is to be noted, dearly beloved. For when he had said above, after Judas had gone out, and was separated from that even bodily fellowship of the holy ones: *Now is the Son of man glorified, and God is glorified in him*: which he said either signifying the future kingdom, when the wicked will be separated from the good, or that his resurrection would then be, that is, would not have to be deferred, as ours is deferred to the end of the age: and then had added: *If God is glorified in him, God also will glorify him in himself, and at once will glorify him,* by which without any ambiguity he attested concerning his resurrection at once to be: he went on, and said: *Little children, yet a little while I am with you.* Lest, then, they should think that God would so glorify him that he would no longer be joined to them in that fellowship by which he is on earth; *Yet a little while,* he says, *I am with you*: as if he said: I shall indeed be glorified at once by the resurrection; yet I shall not at once ascend into heaven, but *yet a little while I am with you.* For, as it is written in the Acts of the Apostles, after the resurrection he spent forty days with them, going in and out, eating and drinking: not indeed having the need of hungering and thirsting, but commending even to that point the truth of the flesh, which had now not the necessity, but the power, of eating and drinking. Did he, then, signify these forty days by saying: *Yet a little while I am with you,* or something else? For it can also be understood thus: *Yet a little while I am with you*: Still, like you, I too am in this weakness of the flesh, until, namely, he should die and rise again: because after he rose, he was indeed with them, as has been said, forty days by the showing of bodily presence; but he was not with them in the fellowship of human weakness.

## A presence up to the passion and up to the ascension.

**2.** There is also another divine presence unknown to mortal senses, of which likewise he says: *Behold, I am with you, to the consummation of the age*[^1]. This surely is not: *Yet a little while I am with you*: for it is not a little while up to the consummation of the age. Or if this too is a little while (for the age flies, and in the eyes of God a thousand years are as one day, or as a watch in the night), yet he must not be believed to have wished to signify this now, since he went on to add: *You will seek me, and as I said to the Jews, where I go, you cannot come*[^2]. Surely after this little while in which I am with you: *You will seek me, and where I go, you cannot come.* Will they not be able, after the consummation of the age, to come where he himself goes? And where is that which a little after in this very discourse he is going to say: *Father, I will that where I am, they too may be with me*[^3]?

It was not, then, of that presence of his with his own, by which he is with them to the consummation of the age, that he now spoke, where he says: *Yet a little while I am with you*: but either of the mortal weakness, by which he was with them up to his passion; or of the bodily presence, by which he was going to be with them up to his ascension. Whichever of these one chooses, he does not contend with the faith.

**3.** But lest this sense should seem to anyone to shrink from the truth, by which we say that the Lord could have signified the communion of mortal flesh, in which he was with the disciples up to the passion, by saying: *Yet a little while I am with you*; let him attend also, in another evangelist, to his words after the resurrection, where he says: *These things I spoke to you, while I was yet with you*[^4]: as though he were not then with them, standing together with them, seeing, touching, conversing. What is it, then, *while I was yet with you,* but, while I was yet in mortal flesh, in which you also are? For then indeed he was raised up in the same flesh; but he was no longer with them in the same mortality. Wherefore, as there, already clothed with immortality of the flesh, he truly says, *while I was yet with you*; where we can understand nothing else but, while I was yet with you in the mortality of the flesh: so here too he is understood to have said, not absurdly: *Yet a little while I am with you,* as if he said, yet a little while, like you, I am mortal. Therefore let us see what follows.

## How we can go on to where he went before.

**4.** *You will seek me, and as I said to the Jews, where I go, you cannot come, and I say to you now*[^5]. This is, you cannot now. But when he said this to the Jews, he did not add *now.* Those, then, could not come then where he was going, but could afterward: for this he says a little after most plainly to the apostle Peter. For when Peter said: *Lord, where are you going?* he answered him: *Where I go, you cannot now follow me; but you will follow afterward*[^6]. But what this is, must not be carelessly passed over. For where could the disciples not then follow the Lord, but could afterward? If we should say, to death: for one born a man, what time is found when he is not fit for dying; since such is the lot of men in a corruptible body, that there is in it no life easier than death? Not yet, then, were they less fit to follow the Lord to death, but they were less fit to follow the Lord to the life that has no death. For the Lord was going there, that, rising from the dead, he might no longer die, and death would no more have dominion over him. For how were they going to follow the Lord, about to die for righteousness, being not yet ripe for martyrdom? Or the Lord, about to go to the immortality of the flesh, how were they going to follow, who would die at some time or other, but rise again at the end of the age? Or the Lord, about to go to the bosom of the Father, and not about to leave them, since he did not even withdraw from them when he had come to them, how were they going to follow; since no one can be in that felicity unless perfect in love? And so, teaching how they could be fit to go on to where he went before: *A new commandment,* he says, *I give you, that you love one another*[^7]. These are the steps by which Christ must be followed: but a fuller discourse on these must be deferred to another time.
