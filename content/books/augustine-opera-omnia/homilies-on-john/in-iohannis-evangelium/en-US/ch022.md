# Homily 22

## He who hears the words and believes has passed from death to life (Jn 5:24-30).

Do you wish to walk? *I am the way*. Do you not wish to be deceived? *I am the truth*. Do you not wish to die? *I am the life*[^1]. This your Savior says to you: There is nowhere for you to go, except to me; there is no way for you to go, except through me. Men who were dead rise; they pass to life, they live at the voice of the Son of God, they live from him, persevering in faith in him.

**1.** Today's Gospel reading follows the sermons delivered to you the day before yesterday and yesterday, and let us go through it in order—not according to its dignity, but according to our strength; for you too receive it, not according to the abundance of the overflowing spring, but according to your own measure. And we do not say into your ears as much as the spring itself pours forth, but as much as we are able to take in, that we may convey it into your perceptions, while he himself works more abundantly in your hearts than we do in your ears. For a great matter is being treated, and by no great men—indeed, by men very small; yet he gives us hope and confidence who, great as he is, became small for our sake. For if we were not encouraged by him, nor invited to understand him, but he abandoned us as contemptible—since we cannot grasp his divinity if he himself did not grasp our mortality and come to us to speak to us the Gospel; if he were unwilling to share with us what is abject and least in us—we would think that he who took up our small thing was unwilling to give us his great thing.

I have said these things lest anyone should either reproach us for treating these matters, as though we were too bold, or despair of himself, that he might be able to grasp by the gift of God what the Son of God deigned to speak to him. Therefore what he deigned to speak to us, we ought to believe, because he willed that we should understand. But if we cannot, he who granted the word unasked grants understanding when asked.

## Faith is a step of piety; understanding is the fruit of faith.

**2.** See, attend to what secrets of these words are here. *Amen, amen, I say to you, he who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life*[^2]. Certainly we are all striving toward eternal life; and he says, *He who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life*. Did he, then, will us to hear his word, but not will us to understand it? Since, if there is eternal life in hearing and believing, much more is there in understanding.

But faith is a step of piety, understanding the fruit of faith, that we may come to eternal life, where the Gospel will not be read to us; but he who has now dispensed the Gospel to us—when all the pages of the reading are removed, and the voice of the reader and the expositor—will appear to all his own who stand by with a cleansed heart, and in an immortal body now never to die, cleansing them and enlightening them, who live and see *that in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God*[^3].

Now, then, let us attend to who we are, and consider whom we hear. Christ is God, and he speaks with men: he wishes to be grasped, let him make us able to grasp; he wishes to be seen, let him open our eyes. Yet he does not speak to us without cause, since what he promises us is true.

**3.** *He who hears my words*, he says, *and believes him who sent me has eternal life, and will not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life*[^4]. Where, when, do we come from death to life, so as not to come into judgment? In this life one passes from death to life; in this life which is not yet life, hence one passes from death to life. What is that passage? *He who hears my words*, he said, *and believes him who sent me*. Keeping these things you believe, and you pass over. And is there one who passes while standing?

Clearly there is: for he stands in body, but passes over in mind.

Where was he, from where would he pass over, and to where does he pass?

He passes from death to life.

See one man standing, in whom all this that is said may be enacted. He stands, he hears; perhaps he did not believe, by hearing he believes: a little before he did not believe, now he believes; as it were he has made a passage from the region of unbelief to the region of faith, his heart moved, not his body—moved for the better; since again those who desert the faith are moved for the worse. See, in this life, which, as I said, is not yet life, one passes from death to life, so as not to come into judgment.

But why did I say that it is not yet life? If this were life, the Lord would not say to a certain man, *If you wish to come to life, keep the commandments*[^5]. For he did not say to him, If you wish to come to eternal life; he did not add eternal, but only said, life. Therefore this is not even to be called life, because it is not true life. What is true life, except that which is eternal life? Hear the Apostle saying to Timothy: *Charge the rich of this world not to be haughty, nor to set their hope on the uncertainty of riches, but on the living God, who furnishes us with everything abundantly to enjoy: let them do good, be rich in good works, give readily, share*. Why this? Hear what follows: *Let them store up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life*[^6]. If they ought to store up for themselves a good foundation for the future, that they may lay hold of the true life, then surely the life in which they were is a false life. For why would you want to lay hold of the true, if you already hold the true? Is the true to be laid hold of? Then one must migrate from the false. And by what way is one to migrate? To where? Hear, believe; and you make a passage from death to life, and you do not come into judgment.

**4.** What is this: *And you do not come into judgment*? And who will be better than the apostle Paul, who says, *We must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ, that each one may there receive what he has done through the body, whether good or evil*[^7]? Paul says, *We must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ*; and you dare to promise yourself that you will not come into judgment? Far be it, you say, that I should dare to promise myself this; but I believe him who promises.

The Savior speaks, the Truth promises; he himself said to me, *He who hears my words and believes him who sent me has eternal life, and makes a passage from death to life, and will not come into judgment*. I therefore have heard the words of my Lord, I have believed; though I was once an unbeliever, I have been made faithful: as he admonished me, I have passed from death to life, I do not come into judgment—not by my own presumption, but by his promise.

But does Paul speak against Christ, the servant against the Lord, the disciple against the master, the man against God—so that, while the Lord says that he who hears and believes passes from death to life and will not come into judgment, the Apostle says, *We must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ*? Or if he who is presented before the tribunal does not come to judgment, I do not know how I am to understand it.

## The judgment of discernment and the judgment of condemnation.

**5.** The Lord our God, then, reveals, and through his Scriptures admonishes us how the word "judgment" is to be understood when it is spoken. I urge you, therefore, to attend. Sometimes "judgment" means punishment; sometimes "judgment" means distinguishing. According to that mode in which judgment means distinguishing, *we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ, that there a man may receive what he has done through the body, whether good or evil*[^8]: for this is the distinguishing, that to the good good things and to the evil evil things may be distributed. For if judgment were always taken in a bad sense, the Psalm would not say, *Judge me, O God*[^9]. Someone perhaps hears it said, *Judge me, O God*, and is astonished. For a man is accustomed to say, May God forgive me; Spare me, O God: who is there who says, Judge me, O God? And sometimes in a psalm that very verse is set in the refrain, to be offered by the reader and answered by the people. Is not perhaps someone's heart struck, and he fears to sing to God and say, *Judge me, O God*? And yet the people sing, believing, and do not think they are wishing themselves ill in what they have learned from the divine reading; and if they understand little, they believe there is something good in what they sing.

And yet the Psalm itself did not leave a man without understanding. For going on, in the later words it showed what sort of judgment it meant; that it is not of condemnation but of discernment. For it says, *Judge me, O God*. What is, *Judge me, O God*? *And distinguish my cause from a nation that is not holy*[^10]. Therefore according to this judgment of discernment, we must all appear before the judgment seat of Christ. But according to the judgment of condemnation: *He who hears my words*, he says, *and believes him who sent me has eternal life, and will not come into judgment, but makes a passage from death to life*. What is, *will not come into judgment*? Will not come into condemnation.

Let us prove from the Scriptures that "judgment" is spoken where punishment is understood—although in this very reading too you will hear a little later that the word "judgment" itself is set only for condemnation and punishment. Yet the Apostle says in a certain place, writing to those who treated badly the Body which you, the faithful, know; and because they treated it badly, they were corrected by the scourge of the Lord: for he says to them, *For this reason many among you are weak and sick, and many sleep*. For many on this account were even dying. And he went on: *For if we judged ourselves, we would not be judged by the Lord*—that is, if we corrected ourselves, we would not be corrected by the Lord. *But when we are judged, we are corrected by the Lord, that we may not be condemned with this world*[^11].

There are, then, some who are judged here as to punishment, that they may be spared there; there are some who are spared here, that they may be more abundantly tormented there; but there are some to whom these same punishments are distributed without the scourge of punishment, if they have not been corrected by the scourge of God, so that, having here despised the father chastising them, they may there feel the judge punishing them. Therefore there is a judgment in which God, that is the Son of God, is going to send at the end the devil and his angels, and all the unbelieving and the ungodly with him: to this judgment he will not come who, now believing, makes a passage from death to life.

**6.** And indeed, lest you should think that by believing you will not die according to the flesh, and taking it carnally should say to yourself, My Lord has said to me, *He who hears my words and believes him who sent me has passed from death to life*; therefore I have believed, I shall not die: know that you will pay the death you owe to the punishment of Adam; for he received it, in whom we all then were, *You shall die the death*[^12]; nor can the divine sentence be made void. But when you have paid the death of the old man, you will be taken up into the eternal life of the new man, and you will make a passage from death to life.

For now, in the meantime, make the passage of life.

What is your life?

Faith: *The just lives by faith*[^13].

And the unbelievers, what of them?

They are dead. Among such dead men, in body, was the one of whom the Lord says, *Let the dead bury their dead*[^14]. Therefore in this life too there are the dead, there are the living, and as it were all are living.

Who are the dead?

Those who have not believed.

Who are the living?

Those who have believed.

What is said to the dead by the Apostle?

*Rise, you who sleep*.

But, you say, he said sleep, not death.

Hear what follows: *Rise, you who sleep, and arise from the dead*. And as though he were saying, Where shall I go? *And Christ will enlighten you*[^15]. Once Christ has enlightened you believing, you make a passage from death to life: remain in that to which you have passed over, and you will not come to judgment.

**7.** He himself now expounds it, and goes on: *Amen, amen, I say to you*. Lest perhaps, because he said *has passed from death to life*, we should understand this of the future resurrection, wishing to show how he who believes passes over, and that this is to pass from death to life—to pass from unbelief to faith, from injustice to justice, from pride to humility, from hatred to love—now he says, *Amen, amen, I say to you, the hour comes, and now is*. What is plainer? Now surely he has opened what he was saying, that what Christ urges us to is happening now. *The hour comes*.

What hour?

*And now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live*[^16].

We have already spoken of these dead. What do we think, my brothers? In this crowd that hears me, are there none who are dead? For those who believe and act according to true faith are alive and not dead; but those who either do not believe, or believe as the demons do, trembling and living badly[^17], confessing the Son of God and not having love, are rather to be reckoned dead. And yet this hour is still going on. For the hour of which the Lord spoke will not be one hour out of the twelve hours of one day. From the time he spoke until this present time, and until the end of the age, that one hour is going on, of which John says in his Letter, *Little children, it is the last hour*[^18]. Therefore now is.

He who lives, let him live; he who was dead, let him live; let him who lay dead hear the voice of the Son of God, rise, and live. The Lord cried out to the tomb of Lazarus, and the four-days-dead man rose again. He who stank came forth into the air; he had been buried, a stone had been placed over him, the voice of the Savior broke through the hardness of the stone: and is your heart so hard that not yet does that divine voice break through to you!

Rise in your heart, come forth from your tomb. For dead in your heart you lay as it were in a tomb, and you were weighed down as by the stone of a bad habit. Rise, and come forth.

What is, Rise and come forth?

Believe, and confess. For he who has believed has risen; he who confesses has come forth. Why did we say that the one who confesses has come forth? Because before he confessed, he was hidden; but when he confesses, he comes forth from darkness into light. And when he has confessed, what is said to the ministers? What was said at the funeral of Lazarus: *Loose him, and let him go*[^19].

How?

It was said to the ministers, the Apostles, *Whatever you loose on earth will be loosed also in heaven*[^20].

**8.** *The hour comes, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live*.

From what will they live?

From life.

From what life?

From Christ.

How do we prove that it is from the life, Christ?

*I am*, he says, *the way, the truth, and the life*[^21].

Do you wish to walk? *I am the way*. Do you not wish to be deceived? *I am the truth*. Do you not wish to die? *I am the life*. This your Savior says to you: There is nowhere for you to go, except to me; there is no way for you to go, except through me. Now, then, this hour is going on; this indeed is happening, and altogether it does not cease. Men who were dead rise; they pass to life, they live at the voice of the Son of God, they live from him, persevering in faith in him. For the Son has life, from which believers may live he has it.

**9.** And how has he it? As the Father has it. Hear him saying: *For as the Father has life in himself, so he has given to the Son also to have life in himself*[^22]. Brothers, I will speak as I can. For these are those words which disturb a small understanding. Why did he add, *in himself*? It would be enough to say, For as the Father has life, so he has given to the Son also to have life. He added, *in himself*: for the Father has life in himself, and the Son too has it in himself. He willed us to understand something in that he said, *in himself*. And here a secret is shut up in this word: let it be knocked at, that it may be opened.

O Lord, what is it that you have said? *In himself*, why did you add it? For did not the apostle Paul, whom you made to live, have life?

He had it, he says.

And as many men as are dead, that they may come to life again and pass over by believing in your word—when they have passed over, will they not have life in you?

They will have it; for I too said a little before, *He who hears my words and believes him who sent me has eternal life*[^23].

Therefore those who believe in you have life: and you did not say, in themselves. But when you were speaking of the Father, *As the Father has life in himself*; again, when you were speaking of yourself, you said, so he has given to the Son also to have life in himself. As he has it, so he has given him to have it.

Where does he have it?

In himself.

Where did he give him to have it?

In himself.

Where does Paul have it?

Not in himself, but in Christ.

And you, faithful one, where do you have it? Not in yourself, but in Christ. Let us see whether the Apostle says this: *It is no longer I who live, but Christ who lives in me*[^24]. Our life, considered as our own—that is, of our own will—will be nothing but evil, sinful, wicked; but the good life is from God in us, not from us; it is given us by God, not by ourselves. But Christ has life in himself as the Father does, because he is the Word of God. He does not now live badly and now live well; but man lives now badly, now well. He who lived badly was in his own life; he who lives well has passed over to the life of Christ. Made a partaker of life, you were not what you received, and you were one who would receive; but the Son of God was not, as it were, at first without life and then received life. For if he received it so, he would not have it in himself. For what is, *in himself*? That he himself should be that very life.

## Christ, the inextinguishable light.

**10.** Let me yet say something else, perhaps more plainly. Someone lights a lamp: for example, that lamp, so far as concerns the little flame that shines there, that fire has light in itself; but your eyes, which in the absence of a lamp lay idle and saw nothing, now they too have light, but not in themselves. Accordingly, if they turn away from the lamp, they are darkened; if they turn toward it, they are enlightened. But that fire, so long as it exists, shines: if you wish to take away its light, you at the same time extinguish it too; for without light it cannot remain.

But Christ is the inextinguishable light, coeternal with the Father, always glowing, always shining, always burning: for if he did not burn, would it be said in the Psalm, *Nor is there any who can hide himself from his heat*[^25]? But you in your sin were cold; you are converted that you may grow warm: if you withdraw, you grow cold. In your sin you were full of darkness; you are converted that you may be enlightened: if you turn yourself away, you will be darkened. Accordingly, because in yourself you were darkness, when you are enlightened you will not be light, although you are in the light. For the Apostle says, *You were once darkness; but now you are light in the Lord*[^26]. When he had said, *but now light*, he added, *in the Lord*. In yourself, then, darkness; light in the Lord. Why light? Because by participation in that light you are light. But if you withdraw from the light by which you are enlightened, you return to your own darkness.

Not so Christ, not so the Word of God.

But how?

As the Father has life in himself, so he has given to the Son also to have life in himself: that he may not live by participation, but live unchangeably, and altogether be himself life. So he has given to the Son also to have life. As he has it, so he has given it.

What is the difference?

That the one gave, the other received.

Did he then already exist when he received?

Are we to understand that Christ was ever without light, since he himself is the Wisdom of the Father, of which it is said, *She is the brightness of eternal light*[^27]? Therefore what is said, *he gave to the Son*, is the same as if it were said, he begot the Son: for by begetting he gave. As he gave him to be, so he gave him to be life, and so he gave him to be life in himself. What is, that he should be life in himself? That he should not need life from elsewhere, but should himself be the fullness of life, from which others, believing, might live while they lived. He gave him, then, to have life in himself: he gave it as to whom? As to his Word, as to him who *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God*[^28].

**11.** Then, because he was made man, what did he give him? *And he gave him power to do judgment, because he is the Son of man*[^29]. Inasmuch as he is the Son of God, *as the Father has life in himself, so he has given to the Son also to have life in himself*[^30]; but inasmuch as he is the Son of man, *he gave him power to do judgment*. This is what I expounded yesterday to your Charity, that in the judgment a man will be seen, but God will not be seen; but after the judgment God will be seen by those who have conquered in the judgment; but by the ungodly God will not be seen. Because, then, in the judgment a man will be seen in that form in which he will come as he ascended, therefore above he had said, *The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*[^31]. This too he repeats in this place, when he says, *And he gave him power to do judgment, because he is the Son of man*. As though you were to say: He gave him power to do judgment—why? When did he not have this power to do judgment? When *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*, when *all things were made through him*[^32], did he not have power to do judgment?

But I say it in this sense: He gave him power to do judgment because he is the Son of man; in this sense he received power to judge, because he is the Son of man. For inasmuch as he is the Son of God, he always had this power. He received it who was crucified. He who was in death is in life: the Word of God never in death, always in life. If you have believed, believe the whole.

**12.** Already, then, perhaps one of us was saying about the resurrection: See, we have risen again; he who hears Christ, who believes, both passes from death to life and will not come into judgment; *the hour comes, and now is*, that he who hears the voice of the Son of God may live; he was dead, he heard, see, he rises again: what is it that is later spoken of, a future resurrection?

Spare yourself, do not rush to a judgment, lest you go on after it. There is indeed that resurrection which happens now: the unbelievers were dead, the wicked were dead; the just live, they pass from the death of unbelief to the life of faith; but do not therefore believe that there will be no future resurrection of the body afterward—believe that there will be a resurrection of the body too.

For hear what follows after the commendation of this resurrection which happens through faith, lest anyone should think this is the only one and fall into that despair and error of the men who were perverting the perceptions of others, saying that the resurrection had already taken place, of whom the Apostle says, *And they pervert the faith of some*[^33]. For I believe that they said such words to them: See, where the Lord says, *And he who believes in me has passed from death to life*; the resurrection has already taken place in the faithful men who had been unbelievers: how is another resurrection spoken of?

Thanks be to the Lord our God; he props up the wavering, directs the hesitating, strengthens the doubting. Hear what follows, for you have no ground from which to make for yourself the darkness of death. If you have believed, believe the whole.

What whole, you say, am I to believe?

Hear what he says: *Do not marvel at this*, that he gave the Son power to do judgment.

I speak of the end, he says.

How of the end? *Do not marvel at this; for the hour comes*. Here he did not say, *and now is*. In that resurrection of faith, what did he say? *The hour comes, and now is*[^34]. In this resurrection which he commends as a future one, of dead bodies: *The hour comes*, he said; he did not say, *now is*; because it is to come at the end of the age.

**13.** And from where, you say, do you prove to me that he spoke of that resurrection?

If you listen patiently, you yourself will prove it to yourself in a moment. Let us follow on, then: *Do not marvel at this; for the hour comes, in which all who are in the tombs*[^35]. What is plainer than this resurrection? A while ago he had not said, *those who are in the tombs*, but, *the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God; and those who hear will live*[^36]. He did not say, some will live, others will be condemned; because all who believe will live. But of those in the tombs what does he say? *All who are in the tombs will hear his voice, and will come forth*. He did not say, will hear and will live. For if they lived badly and lay in the tombs, they will rise to death, not to life.

Let us see, then, who will come forth. Although a little before the dead lived by hearing and believing, no distinction was made there; it was not said: The dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and when they have heard, some will live, others will be condemned; but, *All who hear will live*: because those who believe will live, those who have love will live, and no one will die. But of those in the tombs: they will hear the voice, and will come forth, *those who have done good, to the resurrection of life; those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment*[^37]. This is the judgment, that punishment of which a little before he had said: *He who believes in me has passed from death to life, and will not come into judgment*[^38].

## In one Word God contains all things.

**14.** *I can do nothing of myself: as I hear, I judge, and my judgment is just*[^39].

If you judge as you hear, from whom do you hear?

If from the Father, surely *the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*[^40].

Are you in a manner a herald of the Father, so that what you hear, this you say?

What I hear, this I say, because what the Father is, this I am: for my speaking is my being; because I am the Word of the Father.

For this is what Christ says to you in your heart. What is, *As I hear, so I judge*, except, As I am? For how does Christ hear? Brothers, let us inquire, I beg you. Does Christ hear from the Father? How does the Father speak to him? Surely if he speaks to him, he makes words to him: for everyone who says anything to someone says it by a word. How does the Father speak to the Son, when the Son is the Word of the Father? Whatever the Father says to us, he says it by his Word: the Word of the Father is the Son; to the Word itself by what other word does he speak? There is one God, he has one Word, in one Word he contains all things. What is, then, *As I hear, so I judge*? As I am from the Father, so I judge. Therefore my judgment is just.

For if you do nothing of yourself, O Lord Jesus—as carnal men suppose—if you do nothing of yourself, how did you a little before say, *So also the Son gives life to whom he will*[^41]? Now you say, *I do nothing of myself*. But what does the Son commend, except that he is from the Father? He who is from the Father is not from himself. If the Son were from himself, he would not be Son: he is from the Father. The Father, that he may be, is not from the Son; the Son, that he may be, is from the Father. Equal to the Father; but yet this one is from that one, not that one from this one.

**15.** *Because I do not seek my own will, but the will of him who sent me*[^42]. The only Son says, *I do not seek my own will*; and men want to do their own will! He so humbles himself who is equal to the Father; and he so exalts himself who lies in the depths, and unless a hand is stretched out to him, does not rise! Let us, then, do the will of the Father, the will of the Son, the will of the Holy Spirit; because of this Trinity there is one will, one power, one majesty. Yet the Son says, *I came not to do my own will, but the will of him who sent me*[^43]; because Christ is not from himself, but is from his Father. But what he had so as to appear as man, he took from the creation which he himself had formed.
