# Tractate 44

## *Passing by, he saw a man blind from birth*[^1].

I commend the mystery of this blind man enlightened. This blind man is the human race; for this blindness befell in the first man through sin, from whom we all drew our origin, not only of death, but also of iniquity. For if blindness is unbelief, and enlightenment is faith, whom did Christ find faithful when he came?

**1.** About the man whom the Lord Jesus enlightened, who was born blind, a lengthy reading has been recited: which, if we should try to treat in its entirety, considering each point according to its dignity, as far as we are able, the day will not suffice. Accordingly, I ask and admonish your Charity, that in those things which are plain you not require our discourse: for it will be too long to dwell on each point. Briefly, then, I commend the mystery of this blind man enlightened.

For the things that our Lord Jesus Christ did, astounding and to be wondered at, are both works and words: works, because they were done; words, because they are signs. If, then, we consider what this that was done signifies, this blind man is the human race: for this blindness befell in the first man through sin, from whom we all drew our origin, not only of death, but also of iniquity. For if blindness is unbelief, and enlightenment is faith, whom did Christ find faithful when he came? Since the Apostle, born in the nation of the Prophets, says: *We too were once by nature children of wrath, like the rest*[^2]. If children of wrath, children of vengeance, children of punishment, children of hell. How by nature, except that, the first man having sinned, the vice grew up in place of nature? If the vice grew up in place of nature, every man is born blind as to the mind. For if he sees, he has no need of a guide; if he has need of a guide and enlightener, he is therefore blind from birth.

**2.** The Lord came: what did he do? He commended a great mystery. *He spat on the ground*[^3], made mud from his spittle: because *the Word was made flesh*[^4]. And he anointed the eyes of the blind man. He was anointed, and did not yet see. He sent him to the pool which is called Siloam. And it pertained to the Evangelist to commend to us the name of this pool; and he says: *Which is interpreted Sent*[^5]. Now you recognize who is the Sent one: for unless he had been sent, none of us would have been released from iniquity. So he washed his eyes in that pool which is interpreted Sent; he was baptized in Christ. If, then, when he in a manner baptized him in himself, he then enlightened him; when he anointed him, perhaps he made him a catechumen. The depth of so great a sacrament can indeed be expounded and treated in various ways: but let this suffice for your Charity; you have heard a great mystery.

Ask a man: Are you a Christian? He answers you: I am not, if he is a pagan or a Jew. But if he says: I am; you still ask of him: A catechumen, or one of the faithful? If he answers: A catechumen; he has been anointed, not yet washed. But whence anointed? Ask, and he answers; ask him in whom he believes: by the very fact that he is a catechumen, he says: In Christ. Behold, now I speak both to the faithful and to the catechumens. What did I say about the spittle and the mud? That the Word was made flesh. This the catechumens too hear: but it does not suffice them for that to which they have been anointed; let them hasten to the washing, if they seek the light.

**3.** So now, because of certain questions in this very reading, let us rather run through the words of the Lord and of the whole reading than treat them. *Passing by, he saw a man blind*: not blind in any way whatever, but, *from birth.* And his disciples asked him: *Rabbi*[^6]. You know that Rabbi means teacher. They called him teacher, because they desired to learn: for they put a question to the Lord as to a teacher: *Who sinned, this man or his parents, that he should be born blind? Jesus answered: Neither did this man sin, nor his parents*[^7], that he should be born blind.

What is it that he said? If no man is without sin, were the parents of this blind man without sin? Was he himself either born without original sin, or had he by living added nothing? Or because he had his eyes closed, did concupiscence not in the least keep awake? How many evils the blind commit! From what evil does an evil mind abstain, even with the eyes closed? He could not see, but he knew how to think, and perhaps to covet something that, being blind, he could not carry out, but which is judged in the heart by the searcher of the heart.

If, then, both his parents had sin, and he had sin; why did the Lord say: *Neither did this man sin, nor his parents*: except with regard to the matter about which he was asked, that he should be born blind? For his parents had sin, but it was not by that sin that it came about that he was born blind. If, then, it was not by his parents' sin that he was born blind, why was he born blind? Hear the teacher teaching: he seeks one who believes, that he may make him understand. He himself states the cause why that man was born blind: *Neither did this man sin,* he says, *nor his parents: but that the works of God might be made manifest in him*[^8].

**4.** Then what follows? *I must work the works of him who sent me.* Behold the Sent one, in whom the blind man washed his face. And see what he said: *I must work the works of him who sent me, while it is day.* Remember how he gives all the glory to him of whom he is: because that one has a Son who is of him, he himself has no one of whom he is. But why did you say: Lord, *while it is day?* Hear why. *Night comes, when no one can work*[^9]. Not even you, Lord? Will that night be so powerful that not even you can work in it, whose work the night is? For I think, Lord Jesus—nay, I do not think, but believe and affirm—that you were there when God said: *Let there be light; and there was light*[^10]. For if he made it by the Word, he made it through you: and therefore it was said: *All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^11]. *God divided between the light and the darkness; the light he called day, and the darkness he called night*[^12].

## I seek with you, with you I will find him of whom I seek.

**5.** What is that night, which, when it comes, no one will be able to work? Hear what day is, and then you will understand what night is. Whence are we to hear who this day is? Let him himself say it: *As long as I am in this world, I am the light of the world*[^13]. Behold, he himself is the day. Let the blind man wash his eyes in the day, that he may see the day. *As long as I am in the world,* he says, *I am the light of the world.* Therefore there will be some night, when Christ will not be there; therefore no one will be able to work.

It remains to inquire, my brothers; patiently bear with me as I inquire: I seek with you, with you I will find him of whom I seek. It is established, it is expressed and defined, that the Lord in this place called himself the day, that is, the light of the world: *As long as,* he says, *I am in this world, I am the light of the world.* Therefore he himself works. But how long is he in this world? Do we think, brothers, that he was here then, and now is not here? If, then, we think this, then after the Lord's ascension this dreaded night has come, in which no one can work: if after the Lord's ascension this night has come, whence did the Apostles work such great things? Was it this night, when the Holy Spirit, coming and filling all who were in one place, gave them to speak in the tongues of all nations[^14]? Was it night when that lame man was made whole at the word of Peter—nay, at the word of the Lord dwelling in Peter[^15]? Was it night when, as the disciples passed by, the sick were laid with their beds, that they might be touched even by the shadow of those passing[^16]? But the Lord, when he was here, made no one whole by his shadow as he passed: yet he himself had said to the disciples: *You will do greater things than these*[^17]. The Lord had indeed said: You will do greater things than these: but let not flesh and blood exalt itself; let it hear him saying: *Without me you can do nothing*[^18].

## If we work now, here is the day, here is Christ.

**6.** What then? What shall we say about that night? When will it be, when no one can work? That night will be the night of the impious: that night will be the night of those to whom it will be said at the end: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^19]. But it is called night, not flame, not fire. Hear that it is also night. Of a certain servant he says: *Bind his hands and feet, and cast him into the outer darkness*[^20]. Let a man, then, work while he lives, lest he be overtaken by that night, when no one can work. Now is the time for faith to work through love: and if we work now, here is the day, here is Christ. Hear him promising, and do not suppose him absent. He himself said: *Behold, I am with you.* How long? Let there be no anxiety in us who live: if it could be done, we would make even posterity, those who are to come, most secure from this saying. *Behold,* he says, *I am with you until the consummation of the age*[^21].

This day which is completed by the circuit of this sun has few hours; the day of Christ's presence is extended until the consummation of the age. But after the resurrection of the living and the dead, when to those set on the right he will have said: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*, but to those set on the left will have said: *Go into the eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^22]: then will be the night where no one can work, but only receive what he has worked. The time of working is one thing, the time of receiving another: for *the Lord will render to each according to his works*[^23]. While you live, do, if you are going to do: for then there will be a mighty night, which will wrap up the impious.

But even now every unbeliever, when he dies, is received into that night: there is no working anything there. In that night the rich man was burning, and asking for a drop of water from the finger of the poor man: he grieved, he was tormented, he confessed, and there was no helping him; and he tried to do good. For he said to Abraham: *Father Abraham, send Lazarus to my brothers, that he may tell them what is being done here, lest they too come into this place of torments*[^24]. O unhappy man! when you were living, then was the time of working; now you are already in the night, in which no one can work.

**7.** When he had said these things, *he spat on the ground, and made mud from the spittle, and smeared the mud over his eyes, and said to him: Go, and wash in the pool of Siloam (which is interpreted Sent). So he went and washed, and came seeing*[^25]. Because these things are plain, let us pass over them.

## The blind man was made an announcer of grace.

**8.** So the neighbors, and those who used to see him before, because he was a beggar, said: *Is this not the one who used to sit and beg? Some said: It is he: others: By no means, but he is like him.* The opened eyes had changed his face. He said: *It is I.* A grateful voice, lest he be condemned as ungrateful. *They said to him, then: How were your eyes opened? He answered: That man who is called Jesus made mud, and anointed my eyes, and said to me: Go to the pool of Siloam, and wash. And I went, and washed, and saw*[^26]. Behold, he was made an announcer of grace; behold, he evangelizes, he confesses, seeing. That blind man was confessing, and the heart of the impious was being broken; because they did not have in their heart what he already had on his face. *They said to him: Where is the one who opened your eyes? He says: I do not know*[^27]. In these words his mind was still like one anointed, not yet seeing. So let us suppose, brothers, that he had, as it were, that anointing in his mind. He preaches, and does not know whom he preaches.

**9.** They bring him who had been blind to the Pharisees. Now it was the Sabbath when Jesus made the mud and opened his eyes. So again the Pharisees asked him how he had come to see. But he said to them: *He put mud on my eyes, and I washed, and I see. So some of the Pharisees said*[^28]—not all, but some: for some were now being anointed. What, then, did they say, neither seeing nor anointed? *This man is not from God, who does not keep the Sabbath*[^29]. He rather kept it, who was without sin. For the spiritual Sabbath is this, to have no sin. Finally, brothers, this is what God admonishes when he commends the Sabbath: *You shall do no servile work*[^30]. These are the words of God commending the Sabbath: You shall do no servile work. Now ask the earlier readings what servile work is; and hear the Lord: *Everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin*[^31]. But these men, neither seeing, as I said, nor anointed, observed the Sabbath carnally, violated it spiritually.

*Others said: How can a man who is a sinner do these signs?*[^32] Behold, these are the anointed. *And there was a division among them.* That day had divided between the light and the darkness. So they say to the blind man again: *What do you say about him who opened your eyes?*[^33] What do you feel about him? What do you reckon? What do you judge? They were seeking how they might slander the man, so that he might be driven from the synagogue, but be found by Christ. But he steadily expressed what he felt. For he said: *He is a prophet.* Still indeed anointed in heart, he does not yet confess the Son of God, yet he does not lie. For the Lord himself says of himself: *A prophet is not without honor, except in his own country*[^34].

**10.** The Jews, then, did not believe about him that he had been blind and had come to see, until they called the parents of him who had seen[^35]: that is, of him who had been blind, and had seen. And they asked them, saying: *Is this your son, who you say was born blind? How, then, does he now see? His parents answered them and said: We know that this is our son, and that he was born blind: but how he now sees, we do not know; or who opened his eyes, we do not know. And they said: Ask him; he is of age, let him speak for himself*[^36]. He is indeed our son, but we would justly be compelled to speak for an infant, because he could not speak for himself: he has long spoken, now he sees; we know him as blind from birth, we know that he has long spoken, we now see him seeing: ask him, that you may be instructed; why do you slander us? His parents said these things, *because they feared the Jews. For the Jews had already conspired, that if anyone confessed him to be the Christ, he should be put out of the synagogue*[^37]. Now it was no longer an evil to be put out of the synagogue. They were casting out, but Christ was receiving. Therefore his parents said: *He is of age, ask him*[^38].

**11.** So again they called the man who had been blind, and said to him: *Give glory to God*[^39]. What is: *Give glory to God?* Deny what you have received. This plainly is not to give glory to God, but rather to blaspheme God. *Give,* they say, *glory to God. We know that this man is a sinner. So he said: Whether he is a sinner, I do not know; one thing I know, that, though I was blind, now I see. So they said to him: What did he do to you? How did he open your eyes?*[^40] And he, now galled against the hardness of the Jews, and seeing from being blind, not bearing the blind, answered them: *I have already told you, and you have heard; why do you wish to hear again? Do you too wish to become his disciples?*[^41] What is: *Do you too,* except, because I already am? *Do you too wish?* Now I see, but I do not envy.

**12.** They cursed him and said: *You be his disciple*[^42]. Let such a curse be upon us, and upon our children. For it is a curse, if you examine the heart, not if you weigh the words. *We are disciples of Moses: we know that God spoke to Moses, but this man we do not know whence he is*[^43]. Would that you knew that God spoke to Moses; you would know that through Moses God was foretold. For you have the Lord saying: *If you believed Moses, you would believe me too; for he wrote about me*[^44]. Do you so follow the servant, and put your back against the Lord? But you do not even follow the servant; for through him you would be led to the Lord.

**13.** That man answered and said to them: *In this it is marvelous, that you do not know whence he is, and he opened my eyes. But we know that God does not hear sinners; but if anyone is a worshiper of God, and does his will, him he hears*[^45]. He still speaks as one anointed. For God hears sinners too. For if God did not hear sinners, in vain would that tax collector, casting his eyes down to the ground and beating his breast, say: *Lord, be merciful to me a sinner*[^46]. And that confession merited justification, as this blind man merited enlightenment. *From the beginning of the world it has not been heard that anyone opened the eyes of one born blind. Unless this man were from God, he could not do anything*[^47]. Freely, steadily, truthfully. For these things that were done by the Lord—by whom would they be done, except by God? Or when would such things be done by the disciples, unless the Lord dwelt in them?

**14.** They answered and said to him: *You were born wholly in sins.* "Wholly"—what is that? With eyes closed. But he who opened the eyes saves the whole man too: he himself will give resurrection at the right hand, who in the face gave enlightenment. *You were born wholly in sins, and do you teach us? And they cast him out*[^48]. They themselves made him a teacher, they themselves so often questioned him that they might learn, and, ungrateful, they threw out their teacher.

**15.** But, as I said a little while ago, brothers, they cast out, the Lord receives: for, all the more because he was cast out, he was made a Christian. *Jesus heard that they had cast him out, and when he had found him, he said to him: Do you believe in the Son of God?*[^49] Now he washes the face of the heart. He answered and said, as though still anointed: *Who is he, Lord, that I may believe in him? And Jesus said to him: You have both seen him, and he who speaks with you, he it is*[^50]. He was sent—this man, washing his face in Siloam, which is interpreted Sent. Finally, now with the face of the heart washed and the conscience cleansed, recognizing him not only as the Son of man, which he had believed before, but now as the Son of God who had taken on flesh, he says: *I believe, Lord.* It is too little: I believe; do you wish to see what kind of believer? *Falling down, he adored him*[^51].

## Now is that day, distinguishing between light and darkness.

**16.** And Jesus said to him. Now is that day, distinguishing between light and darkness. *For judgment I came into this world, that those who do not see may see; and that those who see may become blind*[^52]. What is this, Lord? You have brought a great question upon the weary: but lift up our strength, that we may be able to understand what you have said. You came that those who do not see may see: rightly, because you are light; rightly, because you are day; rightly, because you set free from darkness: this every soul receives, every soul understands. What is this that follows: *And that those who see may become blind?* Is it, then, that because you came, those who saw will become blind? Hear what follows, and perhaps you will understand.

**17.** So some of the Pharisees were stirred by these words, and said to him: *Are we too blind?*[^53] Now hear what it was that moved them: *And that those who see may become blind.* *Jesus said to them: If you were blind, you would not have sin*[^54]. Since blindness itself is sin. *If you were blind*, that is, if you noticed that you were blind, if you called yourselves blind, and ran to the physician; if, then, you were blind in this way, you would not have sin: because I came to take away sin. *But now you say: We see: your sin remains.* Why? Because by saying: We see, you do not seek the physician, you remain in your blindness. This, then, is what a little before we had not understood, where he says: *I came, that those who do not see may see*: what is, that those who do not see may see? Those who confess that they do not see, and seek the physician, that they may see.

*And that those who see may become blind*: what is, that those who see may become blind? Those who think they see, and do not seek the physician, may remain in their blindness. So he called this distinguishing a judgment, when he said: *For judgment I came into this world*, whereby he distinguishes the cause of those who believe and confess from the proud, who think they see, and are therefore more grievously blinded; as though the confessing sinner, seeking the physician, had said to him: *Judge me, O God, and distinguish my cause from an unholy people*[^55]: that is, from those who say: We see, and their sin remains. But he did not yet bring on the world that judgment by which he will judge the living and the dead at the end of the age. For according to this he had said: *I judge no one*[^56]: since he first came, not to judge the world, but that the world might be saved through him[^57].
