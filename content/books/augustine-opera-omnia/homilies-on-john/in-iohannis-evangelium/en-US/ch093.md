# Tractate 93

## *And you also will bear witness, because you are with me from the beginning*[^1].

Surely, because he will bear witness, you also will bear witness: he in your hearts, you in your voices; he by inspiring, you by sounding forth, that it may be fulfilled: *Their sound has gone out into all the earth.* For it would have been too little to exhort them by example, unless he filled them with his Spirit.

**1.** In what precedes this chapter of the Gospel, the Lord, confirming his disciples to endure the hatreds of their enemies, also prepared them by his own example, that by imitating him they might become stronger: adding and promising them that the Holy Spirit would come, who would bear witness concerning him, and adjoining that they too would become his witnesses — this, surely, by the Holy Spirit working in them. For he says thus: *He will bear witness concerning me, and you will bear witness*[^2]. Surely, because he will bear witness, you also will bear witness: he in your hearts, you in your voices; he by inspiring, you by sounding forth: that it may be fulfilled, *Their sound has gone out into all the earth*[^3]. For it would have been too little to exhort them by his own example, unless he filled them with his Spirit. For the apostle Peter, when he had already heard his words, where he had said, *The servant is not greater than his master: if they persecuted me, they will also persecute you*[^4], and already saw this fulfilled in him, in whom, if the example sufficed, he ought to have imitated the patience of his Lord, gave way and denied, not bearing, surely, what he saw him bear. But when he truly received the gift of the Holy Spirit, he preached the one he had denied; and the one he had feared to confess, he did not fear to profess. For first, indeed, he had been instructed by the example, that he might know what it was fitting to be done; but he had not yet been propped up by power, that he might do what he knew: he was equipped to stand, but he was not made firm against falling. After this was done through the Holy Spirit, he proclaimed, even unto death, the one he had denied through fear of death.

Therefore the Lord, in this following chapter, of which we must now speak to you, says: *These things I have spoken to you, that you may not be scandalized*[^5]. For it is sung in the Psalm: *Much peace have they who love your law, and there is no scandal for them*[^6]. Rightly, then, having promised the Holy Spirit, by whose working in them they would become his witnesses, he added: *These things I have spoken to you, that you may not be scandalized.* For when the love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who is given to us, there comes much peace to those who love the law of God, so that there is no scandal for them.

**2.** Then, now expressing what they would suffer, he says: *They will put you out of the synagogues*[^7]. But what evil was it to the Apostles to be expelled from the Jewish synagogues, as though they were not going to separate themselves from them even if no one expelled them? But doubtless he wished to announce this: that the Jews were not going to receive Christ, from whom these were not going to withdraw; and therefore it would come about that those who could not be without him would be cast out together with him by those who would not be in him. For surely, since there was no other people of God than that seed of Abraham, if they acknowledged and received Christ, they would remain like natural branches in the olive tree; nor would the Churches of Christ be one thing, the Synagogues of the Jews another: for they would be the same, if they had wished to be in the same one. But because they refused, what remained but that, remaining outside Christ, they should put outside the synagogues those who would not forsake Christ? For, having received the Holy Spirit, made his witnesses, they would surely not be such as those of whom it is said: *Many of the rulers of the Jews believed in him; but on account of fear of the Jews, lest they be expelled from the synagogues, they did not dare to confess him: for they loved the glory of men more than that of God*[^8]. They believed in him, then, but not in the way he wished them to believe, who said: *How can you believe, who look for glory from one another, and do not seek the glory which is from God alone*[^9]?

To the disciples, then, believing in him in such a way that, filled with the Holy Spirit — that is, with the gift of the grace of God — they are not of the number of those who, *being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own, are not subject to the righteousness of God*[^10]; nor of those of whom it is said, *They loved the glory of men more than that of God*: to them that prophecy is suited which is found fulfilled concerning them: *O Lord, in the light of your countenance they will walk, and in your name they will exult all the day, and in your righteousness they will be exalted; for you are the glory of their strength*[^11]. Rightly is it said to them, *They will put you out of the synagogues*: those, namely, who *have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge*[^12]; on account of which, *being ignorant of the righteousness of God, and wishing to establish their own,* they expel those who are exalted not in their own but in God's righteousness, and who, expelled by men, do not blush, since the glory of their strength is he himself.

**3.** Finally, when he had said this to them, he added: *But the hour comes that everyone who kills you will think that he offers a service to God: and these things they will do to you, because they have not known the Father, nor me*[^13]. That is, they have not known God nor his Son, to whom they think they offer a service in killing you. These words the Lord so added as though by this he consoled his own, who would be expelled from the Jewish synagogues. For foretelling what evils they would endure for his testimony, he says, *They will put you out of the synagogues.* Nor does he say: And the hour comes that everyone who kills you will think that he offers a service to God. What, then, does he say? *But the hour comes*: as he would say if he were foretelling something good after these evils. What, then, does he mean by, *They will put you out of the synagogues: but the hour comes*? As though he were about to say: They indeed will separate you, but I will gather you; or: They indeed will separate you, but the hour of your joy comes. What, then, does this word do for you which he says, *But the hour comes,* as though he promised them consolation after tribulation, when he ought rather to seem to have said in the indicative mood, And the hour comes? But he does not say, And it comes, since he was foretelling that tribulation upon tribulation, not consolation after tribulation, would come to them. Or was that separation from the synagogues perhaps going to trouble them so much that they would rather die than dwell in this life without the congregations of the Jews? Far be it that those should be so troubled who sought the glory of God, not of men.

What is it, then: *They will put you out of the synagogues: but the hour comes*; when he ought rather to seem to have said: And the hour comes, *that everyone who kills you will think that he offers a service to God*[^14]? For it was not even said: But the hour comes that they will kill you, as though death came to them for a consolation in that separation; but: *The hour comes,* he says, *that everyone who kills you will think that he offers a service to God.* He seems to me to have wished to signify nothing else than that they should understand and rejoice that they would acquire so many for Christ, when they were expelled from the congregations of the Jews, that it would not suffice these to expel them, but they would not allow them to live, lest by their preaching they should convert all to the name of Christ, and turn them away from the observance of Judaism as from divine truth. For we ought to take this as said of the Jews, of whom he had said, *They will put you out of the synagogues.* For the witnesses, that is, the martyrs of Christ, even if they were slain by the Gentiles, yet those did not think that they offered a service to God, but to their false gods, when they did these things. But of the Jews, everyone who slew the preachers of Christ thought that he offered a service to God; believing that whoever were converted to Christ forsook the God of Israel. For by this reasoning too they were moved to slay Christ himself: for their words on this matter have also been written down. *You see that the whole world has gone after him*[^15]; *if we let him live, the Romans will come and take away from us both our place and our nation*[^16]. And what Caiaphas said: *It is expedient that one man die for the people, and that the whole nation not perish*[^17]. In this discourse too, then, he raised up his disciples by his own example, to whom he had said, *If they persecuted me, they will also persecute you*[^18]; so that, just as in killing him they thought they had offered a service to God, so also in killing them.

**4.** This, then, is the sense in these words: *They will put you out of the synagogues*; but do not dread solitude: for, separated from their congregation, you will gather so many in my name that they, fearing lest the temple which was among them, and all the sacraments of the old Law, be forsaken, will kill you; so shedding your blood as to think that they offer a service to God. Behold, this is what the Apostle said of these: *They have a zeal for God, but not according to knowledge*[^19]: they think they offer a service to God by killing the servants of God. O dreadful error! Is it thus, that in order to please God you strike one who pleases God; and the living temple of God is laid low by your striking, lest the stone temple of God be forsaken? O execrable blindness! But it came about in part in Israel, that the fullness of the Gentiles might enter: in part, I say, it came about, not in whole. For not all, but some of the branches were broken off, that the wild olive might be grafted in. For while the Holy Spirit filled the disciples of Christ, when they spoke with the tongues of all nations, when divine miracles were frequent through them, and divine eloquences were scattered abroad, even when slain Christ was so loved that his disciples, expelled from the congregations of the Jews, gathered from those same Jews a huge multitude, and dreaded no solitude. Hence, then, the rest, reprobate and blind, kindled, having a zeal for God but not according to knowledge, and believing that they offered a service to God, killed them. But the one slain for them was gathering them; he who, before he was slain, had instructed them concerning these future things, lest unforeseen and unexpected evils should trouble their unknowing and unprepared minds, though soon to pass, but rather, foreknown and patiently received, should lead them to eternal goods. For that this was the reason he foretold these things to them, he himself also showed, adding: *But these things I have spoken to you, that when their hour has come, you may remember that I told you*[^20]. Their hour is a dark hour, a nocturnal hour. But *in the day the Lord commanded his mercy, and in the night he declared it*[^21]: when the night of the Jews did not darken with any confusion the day of the Christians, separated from them; and when it could kill the flesh, it could not darken the faith.
