# Homily 13

## He who has the bride is the bridegroom[^1].

The whole Church is called a virgin. What is virginity? Whole faith, solid hope, sincere love. O you, virgin, if you keep your virginity for the Bridegroom, why do you run to him who says: I baptize, when the friend of your bridegroom says: This is he who baptizes? Then your bridegroom holds the whole world; why are you corrupted in a part?

**1.** The order of the Gospel reading according to John, as you can remember, you who take care for your own progress, so follows that this which was just read is set before us to be treated today. From the very beginning up to today's reading, what was said before, you remember has already been treated. And if perhaps you have forgotten much of it, surely at least our office remains in your memory. What you will hear here about the baptism of John, even if you do not hold it all, yet I believe you hold that you have heard it: what was said also, why the Holy Spirit appeared in the form of a dove; and how that most knotty question was solved, that John learned something or other which he did not know about the Lord through the dove, when he already knew him, when, as the Lord came to be baptized, he said: I ought to be baptized by you, and you come to me? When the Lord answered him: Allow it for now, that all righteousness may be fulfilled.

**2.** Now, then, the order of the reading compels us to return to the same John. He is the one who was prophesied through Isaiah: The voice of one crying in the wilderness: Prepare the way of the Lord, make his paths straight. Such testimony he gave to his Lord, and (because the Lord deigned it) to his friend: and his Lord and his friend himself also bore testimony to John. For he said of John: Among those born of women there has not arisen a greater than John the Baptist. But because he set himself before him, in that which was more than John, he was God. But he who is least, he says, in the kingdom of heaven, is greater than he. Least in nativity, greater in power, greater in divinity, majesty, glory: as, in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

But John had so borne testimony to the Lord in the earlier readings that he called him indeed the Son of God, but did not call him God, nor yet denied it: he had been silent about God, he had not denied God, but he was not altogether silent about God: for perhaps we find this in today's reading. He had called him the Son of God: but men too were called sons of God. He had said that he was of such great excellence that he himself was not worthy to loose the strap of his sandal. Now that greatness gives much to be understood, whose sandal strap he was not worthy to loose—he than whom no one greater had arisen among those born of women. For he was more than all men and angels. For we find that an angel forbade a man to fall at his feet. For when in the Apocalypse an angel was showing certain things to John, who wrote this Gospel, John, terrified by the greatness of the vision, fell at the feet of the angel. And he: Rise, see that you do not do this; adore God, for I am your fellow servant and of your brothers. The angel, then, forbade a man to fall at his feet. Is it not manifest that he is above all the angels, to whom such a man, than whom no greater has arisen among those born of women, says he himself is unworthy to loose the strap of his sandal?

## Against those who think Christ reigns in Africa alone.

**3.** Yet let John say something more evident, that our Lord Jesus Christ is God. Let us find this in the present reading, because perhaps we have sung of him too: God has reigned over all the earth: against which those are deaf who think he reigns in Africa alone. For it was not not said of Christ, when it was said: God has reigned over all the earth. For who is our other king, but our Lord Jesus Christ? He himself is our king. And what did you hear in that Psalm, in the recent verse just sung? Sing to our God, sing: sing to our king, sing. The one he called God, the same he called our king: Sing to our God, sing: sing to our king, sing with understanding. Do not wish to understand in one part the one to whom you sing: For God is king of all the earth.

And how is he king of all the earth, who was seen in one part of the lands, in Jerusalem, in Judea, walking among men, born, suckling, growing, eating, drinking, watching, sleeping, sitting wearied at the well, seized, lashed, smeared with spittle, crowned with thorns, hung on wood, struck with a lance, dead, buried? how, then, king of all the earth? What was seen in a place was flesh: to fleshly eyes the flesh appeared; in mortal flesh the immortal majesty was hidden. And with what eyes will the immortal majesty be able to be beheld through the framework of the flesh? There is another eye, there is an inner eye.

For Tobit too did not have no eyes, when, blind in his bodily eyes, he gave his son precepts of life. The one held his father's hand, that he might walk on his feet: the other gave his son counsel, that he might keep the way of justice. And here I see eyes, and here I understand eyes. And better are the eyes of the one giving counsel of life, than the eyes of the one holding the hand. Such eyes Jesus too sought, when he said to Philip: So long a time I am with you, and you have not known me? Such eyes he sought, when he said: Philip, he who sees me, sees also the Father. These eyes are in the understanding, these eyes are in the mind. Therefore when the Psalm had said: For God is king of all the earth; it added at once: Sing with understanding.

For what I say: Sing to our God, sing; I call God our king. But you saw our king among men as a man, you saw him suffer, crucified, dead: something lay hidden in that flesh which you could see with fleshly eyes. What lay hidden there? Sing with understanding; do not seek with the eyes what is beheld with the mind. Sing with the tongue, because among you is flesh; but because the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us, render the sound to the flesh, render to God the gaze of the mind. Sing with understanding, and you see that the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.

**4.** Let John too give testimony: After these things Jesus and his disciples came into the land of Judea, and there he stayed with them, and baptized. Having been baptized, he baptized. He did not baptize with the same baptism with which he was baptized. The Lord, baptized by a servant, gives baptism, showing the way of humility, and leading to the baptism of the Lord, that is, his own baptism, by giving an example of humility, because he himself did not refuse the baptism of the servant. And in the baptism of the servant the way was being prepared for the Lord, and the Lord, baptized, made himself the way for those who come. Let us hear him: I am the way, the truth, and the life. If you seek the truth, hold the way: for the way itself is the truth. It is the goal to which you go, it is the way by which you go; you do not go through one thing to another, you do not come through another to Christ: through Christ you come to Christ. How, through Christ to Christ?

Through Christ the man to Christ the God: through the Word made flesh, to the Word which in the beginning was God with God; from that which man ate, to that which the angels daily eat. For so it is written: He gave them the bread of heaven: man ate the bread of angels. What is the bread of angels? In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. How did man eat the bread of angels? And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.

## God is your all.

**5.** But because we have said that the angels eat, brothers, do not think it is done by bites. For if you understand this, God whom the angels eat is, as it were, torn to pieces. Who tears justice to pieces? But again someone says to me:

And who is it that eats justice?

Whence, then: Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for justice, for they will be filled? The food which you eat through the flesh, that you may be restored, fails; that it may repair you, it is consumed: eat justice, and you are restored, and it remains whole.

Just as by seeing this bodily light these eyes of ours are restored, and it is a bodily thing that is seen by the bodily eyes. For many, when they have been for a long time in darkness, their sight is weakened, as by a fasting from light. The eyes, defrauded of their food (for they feed on light), are wearied by the fast and weakened, so that they cannot see the very light by which they are restored: and if it is absent for a longer time, they are extinguished, and the very keenness of light, as it were, dies in them. What then? because so many eyes are daily fed by this light, does it become less? Both they are restored, and it itself remains whole. If God could show this with bodily light to bodily eyes; does he not show to clean hearts that light which is untiring, remaining whole, failing in no part?

What light?

In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God. Let us see whether it is light.

For with you is the fountain of life, and in your light we will see light. On earth the fountain is one thing, the light another. Thirsting, you seek a fountain, and to reach the fountain you seek light: and if it is not day, you light a lamp to reach the fountain. That fountain is itself the light; to the thirsty it is a fountain, to the blind it is light: let the eyes be opened to see the light, let the mouth of the heart be opened to drink the fountain; what you drink, this you see, this you hear. God becomes your all; because, of the things you love, he is your all.

If you attend to visible things, God is neither bread, nor is God water, nor is God this light, nor is God a garment, nor is God a house. For all these things are visible, and are single: what is bread, is not water; and what is a garment, is not a house; and what these things are, is not God: for they are visible. God is your all: if you hunger, he is bread to you; if you thirst, he is water to you; if you are in darkness, he is light to you, because he remains incorruptible; if you are naked, he is the garment of immortality to you, when this corruptible has put on incorruption, and this mortal has put on immortality. All things can be said of God, and nothing is worthily said of God. Nothing is broader than this poverty. You seek a fitting name, you do not find it; you seek to speak in any way at all, you find all things. What is alike, a lamb and a lion? Of Christ both are said: Behold the Lamb of God. How a lion? The lion of the tribe of Judah has conquered.

**6.** Let us hear John: Jesus baptized. We said that Jesus baptized. How, as Jesus? how, as Lord? how, as Son of God? how, as the Word? But the Word was made flesh. Now John too was baptizing in Aenon near Salim. Aenon is a certain pool. Whence is it understood that it was a pool? Because there were many waters there, and they came, and were baptized. For John had not yet been cast into prison. If you remember (behold, I say it again), I said why John baptized: because it was fitting that the Lord be baptized. And why was it fitting that the Lord be baptized? Because many were going to despise Baptism, on the ground that they seemed to be endowed already with a greater grace than they saw in other faithful. For example, a catechumen already living continently might despise a married man, and say that he was better than that faithful man. That catechumen could say in his heart: What need have I to receive Baptism, that I may have what this man too has, than whom I am already better?

Therefore, lest that neck cast down certain men greatly puffed up by the merits of their own righteousness, the Lord willed to be baptized by a servant; as though addressing his chief sons: Why do you exalt yourselves? why do you raise yourselves up, because you have, this one prudence, this one learning, this one chastity, this one strength of patience? Can you have as much as I who gave it? And yet I was baptized by a servant, and you disdain to be baptized by the Lord. This is: That all righteousness may be fulfilled.

**7.** But someone will say: It was enough, then, that John baptized the Lord; what need was there for others to be baptized by John? This too we have said: that if the Lord alone were baptized by John, this thought would not be lacking to men, that John had a better baptism than the Lord. For they would say: So great was the baptism that John had, that Christ alone was worthy to be baptized with it. Therefore, that the better baptism which the Lord was about to give might be shown, and that the one might be understood as, so to speak, the servant's, the other as, so to speak, the Lord's, the Lord was baptized, that he might give an example of humility: but he was not baptized alone by him, lest the baptism of John seem better than the baptism of the Lord.

But to this end our Lord Jesus Christ provided a way, as you have heard, brothers, that no one, arrogant because he has an abundance of some grace, should disdain to be baptized with the baptism of the Lord. For however much a catechumen progresses, he still carries the burden of his own iniquity: it is not forgiven him, until he comes to Baptism. Just as the people of Israel were not rid of the people of the Egyptians until they had come to the Red Sea; so no one is rid of the pressure of sins until he has come to the fountain of Baptism. The fountain never thirsts.

**8.** There arose, then, a question from the disciples of John with the Jews about purification. John was baptizing, Christ was baptizing: the disciples of John were stirred; people were running to Christ, they were coming to John. For those who came to John, he sent them to Jesus to be baptized; those were not sent to John who were baptized by Christ. The disciples of John were troubled, and began to treat the question with the Jews, as is wont to happen. You may understand that the Jews said that Christ was greater, and that people ought to run to his baptism. They, not yet understanding, were defending the baptism of John. It came to John himself, that he might solve the question. Let your Charity understand. And here too the very usefulness of humility is recognized, and it is shown whether, in that question, when men were erring, John wished to glory in himself.

For perhaps he said: You speak truly, you contend rightly, my baptism is better. For that you may know that my baptism is better, I baptized Christ himself. John could have said this, since Christ was baptized by him. As far as he might stretch himself, if he wished, where had he room to stretch himself? But he knew better before whom to humble himself: him whom he knew himself to precede by birth, to him he wished to yield by confession; he understood that his own salvation was in Christ. He had already said above: Of his fullness we have all received: And this is to confess God. For how do all men receive of his fullness, unless he be God? For if he were a man in such a way as not to be God, he too would receive of the fullness of God, and so would not be God. But if all men receive of his fullness, he is the fountain, they the drinkers. Those who drink the fountain can both thirst and drink; the fountain never thirsts, the fountain has no need of itself. Men have need of the fountain; with dry insides, with dry throats, they run to the fountain that they may be restored: the fountain flows that it may restore; so the Lord Jesus.

**9.** Let us see, then, what John answered: They came to John, and said to him: Rabbi, he who was with you beyond the Jordan, to whom you bore witness, behold, here he baptizes, and all come to him: that is: What do you say? are they not to be forbidden, that they may rather come to you? He answered and said: A man can receive nothing, unless it has been given him from heaven. Of whom do you suppose John said this? Of himself: As a man, he says, I received from heaven. Let your Charity attend. A man can receive nothing, unless it has been given him from heaven. You yourselves bear me witness that I said: I am not the Christ.

As though saying, why do you deceive yourselves? you yourselves, how did you put this question to me? What did you say to me? Rabbi, he who was with you beyond the Jordan, to whom you bore witness. You know, then, what testimony I bore him: am I now going to say that he is not the one I said he was? Therefore, because I received something from heaven that I might be something, do you wish me to be empty, so as to speak against the truth? A man can receive nothing, unless it has been given him from heaven. You yourselves bear me witness that I said: I am not the Christ.

You are not the Christ: but what, if you are greater than he, because you baptized him?

I was sent: I am a herald, he is the judge.

Let us hear the friend of the bridegroom, not the adulterers.

**10.** And hear a testimony much more vehement, much more express. See what is done with us; see what we ought to love; see that to love some man in place of Christ is adultery. Why do I say this? Let us attend to the voice of John: an error could have been made about him, he himself could have been thought to be the one he was not; he rejected from himself a false honor, that he might hold the solid truth. See what he says of Christ, what of himself: He who has the bride is the bridegroom. Be chaste, love the bridegroom. But what are you, who say to us: He who has the bride is the bridegroom? But the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices with joy because of the voice of the bridegroom.

The Lord our God will be present, in keeping with the stirring of my heart—for it is full of much groaning—to say what I grieve over: but I beseech you, by Christ himself, that what I shall not be able to say, you yourselves may think; for I know my grief cannot be expressed worthily enough. For I see many adulterers, who wish to possess the bride bought at so great a price, loved when she was foul that she might become beautiful, by that buyer, that liberator, that adorner; and they so act with their words that they may be loved in place of the bridegroom. Of him it was said: This is he who baptizes. Who comes forth here and says: I baptize? who comes forth here and says: What I shall give, this is holy? who comes forward here who says: It is good for you to be born of me? Let us hear the friend of the bridegroom, not the adulterers of the bridegroom: let us hear one zealous, but not for himself.

## The persecutors did not cut up the garment; Christians divide the Church.

**11.** Brothers, go back with your heart to your own homes—I speak of carnal things, I speak of earthly things; I speak in human terms, on account of the weakness of your flesh. Many of you have wives, many wish to have them, many, even if they do not wish it, have had them; many who do not at all wish to have wives, were born of the wives of your fathers: there is no heart that this affection does not touch; no one in human affairs is so far astray from the human race that he does not feel what I say.

Suppose someone, having set off abroad, had entrusted his bride to his friend: See, I beg you, you are dear to me, lest perhaps in my absence someone be loved in my place. What sort of man, then, is he who, guarding the bride or wife of his friend, takes pains indeed that no one else be loved, but if he wishes himself to be loved in place of his friend, and wishes to use her who was entrusted to him, how detestable does he appear to the whole human race? Let him see her attending a little too wantonly at the window, or joking with someone, he forbids it as though jealous: I see one jealous, but let me see for whom; whether for the absent friend, or for himself present.

Suppose our Lord Jesus Christ did this. He entrusted his bride to his friend, he set off abroad to receive a kingdom, as he himself says in the Gospel, and yet he is present in his majesty. Let the friend deceive him who has set off across the sea; and if he deceives him, woe to him who deceives! why do they try to deceive God, God who looks upon the hearts of all, and searches the secrets of all? Some heretic arises, and says: I give, I sanctify, I justify, I do not wish you to go to that sect. He is zealous indeed, but see for whom. Do not go to idols, he is rightly zealous: not to fortune-tellers, he is rightly zealous. Let us see for whom he is zealous: What I give is holy, because I give it; he whom I baptize is baptized, he whom I do not baptize is not baptized.

Hear the friend of the bridegroom, learn to be zealous for your friend: hear his voice: This is he who baptizes. Why do you wish to arrogate to yourself what is not yours? Is he who left his bride here so absent? Do you not know that he who rose from the dead sits at the right hand of the Father? If the Jews despised him hanging on the wood, do you despise him sitting in heaven?

Let your Charity know that I suffer great grief over this matter: but, as I said, I leave the rest to your thoughts. For I do not say, were I to speak the whole day; were I to weep the whole day, I am not equal to it: I do not say, had I, as the prophet says, a fountain of tears; but if I were turned into tears, and became tears, into tongues, and became tongues, it is too little.

**12.** Let us return, let us see what this man says: He who has the bride is the bridegroom; she is not my bride.

And do you not rejoice at the marriage?

Rather I rejoice, he says: But the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices with joy because of the voice of the bridegroom. I do not, he says, rejoice because of my own voice, but I rejoice because of the voice of the bridegroom. I am in the hearing, he in the speaking: for I am to be enlightened, he is the light; I am in the ear, he is the Word.

Therefore the friend of the bridegroom stands and hears him.

Why does he stand?

Because he does not fall.

Why does he not fall?

Because he is humble. See him standing on solid ground: I am not worthy to loose the strap of his sandal. You humble yourself well, deservedly you do not fall, deservedly you stand, deservedly you hear him, and rejoice with joy because of the voice of the bridegroom.

So too the Apostle, the friend of the bridegroom, is himself zealous, not for himself, but for the bridegroom. Hear the voice of one zealous: I am zealous for you with the zeal of God, he said; not with my own, not for myself, but with the zeal of God.

Whence? how? for whom are you zealous, or for whose sake are you zealous?

For I have betrothed you to one husband, to present you a chaste virgin to Christ.

What, then, do you fear? why are you zealous?

I fear, he says, lest, as the serpent seduced Eve by his craftiness, so your minds too be corrupted from the chastity that is in Christ.

The whole Church is called a virgin. You see that the members of the Church are diverse, and that they are endowed with diverse gifts and rejoice in them: some men married, some women married, some men widowed who seek wives no more, some women widowed who seek husbands no more, some who keep their integrity from their earliest age, some who have vowed their virginity to God; the gifts are diverse, but all these are one virgin. Where is this virginity? for it is not in the body. It belongs to few women, and, if virginity can be spoken of in men, the holy integrity even of the body, in few men, is in the Church, and is a more honorable member: but the other members keep virginity not in the body, but all in the mind.

What is the virginity of the mind? Whole faith, solid hope, sincere love. This virginity he feared, who was zealous for the bridegroom, lest it be corrupted by the serpent. For just as a member of the body is violated in a certain place, so the seduction of the tongue violates the virginity of the heart. Let her not be corrupted in the mind, who is unwilling without cause to keep the virginity of the body.

**13.** What, then, shall I say, brothers? The heretics too have virgins, and many are the virgins of the heretics. Let us see whether they love the bridegroom, for whose sake this virginity is kept.

For whom is it kept?

For Christ, she says.

Let us see whether for Christ, not for Donatus: let us see for whom this virginity is kept; you will be able to prove it quickly. Behold, I show the bridegroom, because he himself shows himself: John bears him witness: This is he who baptizes. O you, virgin, if you keep your virginity for this bridegroom, why do you run to him who says: I baptize; when the friend of your bridegroom says: This is he who baptizes?

Then your bridegroom holds the whole world; why are you corrupted in a part? Who is the bridegroom? For God is king of all the earth. Your bridegroom himself holds the whole, because he bought the whole. See at what price he bought it, that you may understand what he bought: what price did he give? He gave his blood. Where did he give it, where did he pour out his blood? In his passion. Do you not sing to your bridegroom, or pretend to sing, when the whole world was bought: They pierced my hands and my feet, they numbered all my bones: but they considered and gazed upon me; they divided my garments among themselves, and over my garment they cast lots? You are the bride, recognize the garment of your bridegroom. Over which garment was the lot cast? Ask the Gospel; see to whom you are betrothed, see from whom you receive the pledge.

Ask the Gospel; see what it says to you in the passion of the Lord. There was there a tunic: let us see of what kind: woven from the top. What does the tunic woven from the top signify, but love? what does the tunic woven from the top signify, but unity? Attend to this tunic, which not even the persecutors of Christ divided. For he says: They said among themselves: Let us not divide it, but cast lots over it. Behold whence you heard the Psalm. The persecutors did not cut up the garment: Christians divide the Church.

**14.** But what shall I say, brothers? Let us see openly what he bought. For there he bought, where he gave the price. For how much did he give? If he gave for Africa, let us be Donatists, and let us not be called Donatists, but Christians; because Christ bought Africa alone: although even here not the Donatists alone. But in his transaction he was not silent about what he bought. He made out a deed: thanks be to God, he did not deceive us. The bride needs to hear that deed, and there to understand for whom she vowed her virginity. There, in that very psalm where it was said: They pierced my hands and my feet, they numbered all my bones; where the passion of the Lord is most openly declared: which psalm is read every year in the last week, with all the people attentive, the passion of Christ being at hand—both among us and among them this psalm is read.

Attend, brothers, to what he bought there; let the deed of transaction be recited; what he bought there, hear: All the ends of the earth will be reminded and turned to the Lord; and all the families of the nations will adore in his sight: for the kingdom is his, and he will rule over the nations. Behold what he bought. Behold, because God is king of all the earth, he is your bridegroom. Why, then, do you wish so rich a one to be reduced to rags? Acknowledge: he bought the whole, and you say: You have a part here. O if only you pleased the bridegroom, O if only you spoke uncorrupted, and not corrupted, what is worse, in heart, not in body! You love a man in place of Christ, you love one who says: I baptize: you do not hear the friend of the bridegroom saying: This is he who baptizes; you do not hear him saying: He who has the bride is the bridegroom. I do not have the bride, he said: but what am I? The friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices with joy because of the voice of the bridegroom.

**15.** Evidently, then, my brothers, it profits these people nothing to keep virginity, to have continence, to give alms; all those things that are praised in the Church profit them nothing: because they tear apart unity, that is, that tunic of love. What do they do? Many among them are eloquent, of great tongues, rivers of tongues. Do they speak with the tongues of angels? Let them hear the friend of the bridegroom, zealous for the bridegroom, not for himself: If I speak with the tongues of men and of angels, but have not love, I have become as sounding brass, or a tinkling cymbal.

**16.** But what do they say?

We have Baptism.

You have it, but not your own. It is one thing to have, another to be its master. You have Baptism, because you received it that you might be baptized, you received it as one enlightened; if, however, you have not darkened yourself: and when you give, you give as a minister, not as a possessor; you cry out as a herald, not as a judge. Through the herald the judge speaks, and yet in the records it is not written: The herald said; but: The judge said. Accordingly, see whether what you give is your own, by your power. But if you received it, confess with the friend of the bridegroom: A man can receive nothing, unless it has been given him from heaven. Confess with the friend of the bridegroom: He who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom stands and hears him.

But O if only you stood and heard him, and did not fall so as to hear yourself! For by hearing him, you would stand and hear: but you speak, and puff up your own head. I, says the Church, if I am the bride, if I have received the pledge, if I am redeemed by the price of his blood, I hear the voice of the bridegroom; and I hear the voice of the friend of the bridegroom then, if he gives glory to my bridegroom, not to himself. Let the friend say: He who has the bride is the bridegroom: but the friend of the bridegroom stands and hears him, and rejoices with joy because of the voice of the bridegroom.

Behold, you have the Sacraments, and I grant it. You have the form, but you are a branch cut off from the vine; you show the form, I seek the root: from the form fruit does not come forth, except where the root is; but where is the root, except in love? And hear the form of the branches; let Paul speak: If I know, he says, all mysteries, and have all prophecy and all faith (and how great a faith?), so as to move mountains, but have not love, I am nothing.

## Apart from unity, even he who works miracles is nothing.

**17.** Therefore let no one sell you fables. Pontius too worked a miracle; and Donatus prayed, and God answered him from heaven. First, either they are deceived or they deceive. Lastly, suppose he move mountains: But have not love, he says, I am nothing. Let us see whether he had love. I would believe it, had he not divided unity. For against these miracle-mongers, so to speak, my God has made me cautious, saying: In the last times false prophets will arise, working signs and wonders, to lead into error, if it be possible, even the elect: behold, I have foretold it to you. The bridegroom, then, has made us cautious, that we ought not to be deceived even by miracles. For sometimes even a deserter frightens a provincial; but whether he is in the camp, and whether that mark with which he was sealed profits him anything, this he attends to who does not wish to be frightened and seduced.

Let us therefore hold to unity, my brothers: apart from unity, even he who works miracles is nothing. For the people of Israel were in unity, and did not work miracles: apart from unity were the magicians of Pharaoh, and they did things like those of Moses. The people of Israel, as I said, did not do them: who were saved with God, those who did them, or those who did not? Peter the apostle raised a dead man; Simon Magus did many things: there were there certain Christians who could do neither what Peter did, nor what Simon did; but in what did they rejoice? Because their names were written in heaven. For when the disciples returned, our Lord Jesus Christ said this, on account of the faith of the nations. For the disciples themselves said, glorying: Behold, Lord, in your name even the demons are subject to us. They confessed well indeed, they paid honor to the name of Christ; and yet what does he say to them? Do not glory in this, that the demons are subject to you; but rejoice, that your names are written in heaven.

Peter cast out demons; some little widow woman or other, some layman or other, of whatever sort, having love, holding the integrity of faith, does not do this: Peter is the eye in the body, the other is a finger in the body; yet he is in that body in which Peter too is; and if the finger is of less worth than the eye, it is nevertheless not cut off from the body. It is better to be a finger and to be in the body, than to be an eye and to be plucked out of the body.

**18.** Accordingly, my brothers, let no one deceive you, let no one seduce you: love the peace of Christ, who was crucified for you, though he was God. Paul says: Neither is he who plants anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase. And does any one of us say that he is something? If we say that we are something, and do not give him the glory, we are adulterers; we wish ourselves to be loved, not the bridegroom. Love Christ, you, and us in him, in whom you too are loved by us. Let the members love one another in turn, but let all live under the head. Compelled by grief indeed, my brothers, I have been forced to say many things, and have said little: I have not been able to finish the reading; the Lord will be present, that it may be opportunely finished. For I did not wish to burden your hearts further, which I wish to be free for groanings, and for prayers for those who are still deaf, and do not understand.
