# Tractate 73

## *And whatever you ask the Father in my name, this I will do* (Jn 14:12-14).

## As we pray, like men sowing, let us not grow weary; for in due time we shall reap.

**1.** A great hope the Lord promised to those of his own who hope, saying: *Because I go to the Father, and whatever you ask in my name, this I will do*. So, then, he went on to the Father in such a way that he did not leave the needy, but heard those who ask. But what is *whatever you ask,* when we see for the most part that his faithful ask, and do not receive? Is it perhaps because they ask badly? For this the apostle James reproached, saying: *You ask, and do not receive, because you ask badly, that you may spend it on your lusts*[^1]. He, then, who would use badly what he wishes to receive, by God's mercy rather does not receive. And accordingly, if that is asked of him by which a man, if heard, would be harmed, it is more to be feared lest what he could not give while favorable he should give while angry. Or do we not see that the Israelites obtained to their own harm what they asked with a blameworthy lust? For they desired to feed on flesh, to whom manna was being rained from heaven. For they disdained what they had; and what they did not have, they shamelessly asked: as though they would not better have asked, not that the food which was lacking should be granted to an unseemly desire, but that the food which was present should be taken with their loathing healed.

For when evil things delight us, and good things do not delight us, we ought rather to ask God that good things may delight us, than that evil things be granted. Not that it is evil to feed on flesh, since the Apostle, speaking of this matter, says: *Every creature of God is good, and nothing to be rejected that is received with thanksgiving*[^2]: but because, as likewise he himself says: *It is evil for a man who eats with offense*[^3]; and if a man's offense, how much more God's? Whose offense in the Israelites was not small, to refuse what wisdom was giving, and to ask what concupiscence was gaping after: although they did not even ask, but, because it was lacking, murmured. But that we might know that it is not God's creature that is blameworthy, but contumacious disobedience and disordered desire; not for the sake of a pig, but for the sake of an apple did the first man find death, and Esau lost his birthright not for the sake of a fowl, but for the sake of lentils.

**2.** How, then, is it to be understood: *Whatever you ask, this I will do,* if God, even by way of taking counsel for them, does not do some things for the faithful who ask? Or must we take it as said to the Apostles alone? Far be it. For whence he came to say this, he had said above: *He who believes in me, the works that I do he will do, and greater than these he will do*: of which we treated in the former discourse. And lest anyone should attribute this to himself, to show that he himself does even those greater works, he added, and said: *Because I go to the Father; and whatever you ask in my name, this I will do.* Did the Apostles alone believe in him? It was to them, then, that he was speaking, by saying: *He who believes in me,* among whom by his gift we too are, who surely do not receive whatever we ask. If we consider too the most blessed Apostles themselves, we find that he who labored more than all—yet not he, but the grace of God with him—asked the Lord three times that the messenger of Satan might depart from him, and yet did not receive what he had asked[^4]. What shall we say, dearly beloved? Shall we think that this promise, where he says: *Whatever you ask, this I will do,* was not fulfilled by him even for the Apostles? And for whom, after all, will he fulfill what he promises, if he defrauded his Apostles in his promise?

**3.** Awake, then, faithful man, and hear watchfully what is there set down, *in my name*: for he did not say, that very *whatever,* ask in whatever way; but, *in my name.* He, then, who promised so great a benefit, what is he called? Surely Christ Jesus: Christ means king, Jesus means Savior: surely it is not just any king who will make us safe, but the king the Savior; and through this, whatever we ask against the advantage of salvation, we do not ask in the name of the Savior. And yet he himself is the Savior, not only when he does what we ask, but also when he does not do it; since, when he sees a thing asked against salvation, by not doing it he rather shows himself the Savior. For the physician knows what the sick man demands for his health, and what against it; and therefore he does not do the will of one who demands contrary things, that he may bring about health. Wherefore, when we wish that he should do whatever we ask, let us not ask in whatever way, but in his name, that is, let us ask in the name of the Savior. Let us not, then, ask against our own salvation: for if he should do that, he does not do it as the Savior, which is his name to his faithful. For he is indeed a condemner to the ungodly, who deigns to be a Savior to the faithful. He, then, who believes in him, whatever he asks in that name, which belongs to those who believe in him, this he does; since this he does as Savior. But if one who believes in him asks something through ignorance against his own salvation, he does not ask in the name of the Savior; because he will not be his Savior, if he should do what hinders his salvation. Whence then it is rather expedient that, by not doing on account of which he is invoked, he do what he is called. On account of this, not only the Savior, but also the good master, that he may do whatever we ask, in the very prayer which he gave us, taught us what to ask; that even in this way we may understand that we do not ask in the name of the master, what we ask contrary to the rule of his very teaching.

## Let us ask in his name, that is, according to the Savior and master.

**4.** Indeed, some things, although we ask them in his name, that is, according to the Savior and according to the master, he does not do at the time when we ask, but yet he does them. For not because we ask this too, that the kingdom of God may come, does he therefore not do what we ask, because we do not at once reign with him in eternity: for what we ask is deferred, not denied. Yet, as we pray, like men sowing, let us not grow weary; for in due time we shall reap[^5]. And at the same time, when we ask well, let us ask that he not do what we do not ask well; for to this too pertains what in the same Lord's prayer we say: *Lead us not into temptation*. For it is no small temptation, if your asking be against your cause.

But it is not to be heard negligently, that the Lord, lest anyone should think that he would do without the Father what he promised to do for those who ask, when he had said: *Whatever you ask in my name, this I will do,* at once added: *That the Father may be glorified in the Son, if you ask anything in my name, this I will do*[^6]. In no way, then, does the Son do this without the Father, since he does it for this end, that the Father may be glorified in him. The Father, then, does it in the Son, that the Son may be glorified in the Father: and the Son does it in the Father, that the Father may be glorified in the Son; since the Father and the Son are one.

[^1]: Jas 4:3
[^2]: 1 Tim 4:4
[^3]: Rom 14:20
[^4]: See 2 Cor 12:8-9
[^5]: See Gal 6:9
[^6]: Jn 14:13
