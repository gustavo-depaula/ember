# Tractate 109

## *Not for these alone do I ask, but also for those who will believe in me through their word*[^1].

Those who were with him then preached to the rest what they heard from him; and so their word, that we too might believe, reached even to us, wherever his Church is, and will reach to those who come after, whoever, wherever, will afterward believe in him.  
He added in his prayer the rest too who were going to believe in him.

**1.** The Lord Jesus, his passion now drawing near, when he had prayed for his disciples, whom he also named Apostles, with whom he had eaten the last supper, from which his betrayer, made manifest by the morsel, had gone out, and with whom, after his going out, before he prayed for them, he had now spoken many things; added the rest too who were going to believe in him, and said to the Father: *But not for these alone do I ask*, that is, for the disciples who were then with him: *but also for those,* he says, *who will believe in me through their word*[^2]. Where he wished all his own to be understood, not only those who then were in the flesh, but also those who were to come. For as many as afterward believed in him, without doubt believed through the word of the Apostles, and until he comes will believe: for to them he had said, *And you also will bear witness, because you are with me from the beginning*[^3]; and through these the Gospel was ministered, even before it was written, and surely whoever believes in Christ believes the Gospel. Not, then, are those alone to be understood whom he says were going to believe in him through their word, who heard the Apostles themselves when they lived in the flesh; but even after their death, we too, born long after, have believed in Christ through their word. For those who were with him then preached to the rest what they heard from him: and so their word, that we too might believe, reached even to us, wherever his Church is; and will reach to those who come after, whoever, wherever, will afterward believe in him.

**2.** Jesus, then, can seem in this prayer not to have prayed for certain of his own, unless we carefully search his words in that same prayer. For if he first prayed for those, as we have already shown, who were then with him, but afterward also for those who were going to believe in him through their word; it can be said that he did not pray for those who neither were then with him when he said these things, nor afterward through their word, but who, whether through them or in any way whatever, had nevertheless believed in him before. For was Nathanael then with him? Was that Joseph of Arimathea, who asked the body of him from Pilate, whom this same evangelist John testifies to have been already his disciple? Was Mary his mother, and the other women, whom we have learned to have been already then his disciples in the Gospel? Were those then with him of whom the same evangelist John often says, *Many believed in him*? For whence was that multitude of those who, partly going before, partly following, with branches, as he sat on the beast, said, *Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord*; and with them the children, of whom he himself says it was foretold, *Out of the mouth of infants and sucklings you have perfected praise*[^4]? Whence the five hundred brothers, to whom together he would not have appeared after the resurrection, unless they had believed in him before? Whence those hundred and nine, who with these eleven were a hundred and twenty, when, gathered together after his ascension, they awaited and received the promised Holy Spirit? Whence were all these, but of those of whom it was said, *Many believed in him*? Not, then, did the Savior pray for these at that time, since he prayed for those who were then with him, and for others who through their word had not yet believed in him, but were going to believe. But these neither were then with him, and had already believed in him before. I forbear to speak of old Simeon, who believed in the infant; of Anna the prophetess; of Zachariah and Elizabeth, who prophesied him before he was born of the Virgin; of their son John, his forerunner, the friend of the bridegroom, who both knew him in the Holy Spirit, and preached him absent, and showed him to be known by others when he was present; these I forbear, since it can be answered that there was no need to pray for such dead, who had departed hence with their great merits, and, received, were resting; for this is likewise answered of the ancient righteous. For which of them could have been saved from the condemnation of the whole mass of perdition, which was brought about through one man, unless he had believed, the Spirit revealing it, in the one Mediator of God and men who was to come in the flesh? But was it needful for him to pray for the Apostles, and not needful to pray for so many who were still in this life, who were not then with him, and had already believed before? Who would say this?

**3.** It is to be understood, then, that they had not yet so believed in him as he wished men to believe in him: seeing that Peter himself too, to whom, confessing and saying, *You are the Christ the Son of the living God,* he had borne so great a testimony, was rather unwilling that he should die, than that he believed he would rise again when dead; whence soon he was called Satan by him. The dead, then, who had already departed, and did not doubt, the Spirit revealing it, that Christ would rise again, are found more faithful than those who, although they had believed that he would redeem Israel, when they saw his death lost all the hope which they had had of him. We believe nothing better, then, than that after his resurrection, the Holy Spirit being imparted, and the Apostles taught and confirmed, and they being first set up as teachers in the Church, others believed through their word in such a way as one ought to believe in Christ, that is, so that they held the faith of his resurrection. And through this all those too who already seemed to have believed in him belonged to the number of those for whom he prayed, saying: *But not for these alone do I ask, but also for those who will believe in me through their word.*

**4.** But there remain for us, for this question still to be solved, the blessed Apostle, and that robber, cruel in crime, faithful on the cross. For *Paul, an apostle not from men, nor through a man, but through Jesus Christ*[^5], says he was made an Apostle: and speaking of his own gospel he says: *For neither did I receive it from a man, nor learn it; but through the revelation of Jesus Christ*[^6]. How, then, was he among those of whom it was said, *they will believe in me through their word*? But that robber believed then, when in the very teachers the faith, such as it was, failed. Neither he, then, believed in Christ Jesus through their word: and yet he so believed that, whom he saw crucified, he confessed not only would rise again, but even would reign, by saying, *Remember me, when you come into your kingdom*[^7].

**5.** Accordingly it remains that, if the Lord Jesus is to be believed in this prayer to have prayed for all his own, whoever in this life, which is a temptation upon earth, either then were or were to be, we so understand what was said, *through their word,* that we believe the very word of faith which they preached in the world to be here signified; and that it was called their word, because by them it was first and chiefly preached. For it was already preached by them on earth, when through the revelation of Jesus Christ Paul received that very word of theirs. Whence he also conferred with them concerning the Gospel, lest perhaps he had run or were running in vain; and they gave him the right hands of fellowship: because in him too, although it was not given him through them, yet they found their own word, which they already preached, and in which they had been grounded. Of which word of the resurrection of Christ the same Apostle says: *Whether I or they, so we preach, and so you believed*[^8]; and again: *This is,* he says, *the word of faith which we preach, that if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is the Lord, and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved*[^9]. And in the Acts of the Apostles it is read that God has set forth faith for all in Christ, raising him from the dead[^10]. This word of faith, because it was preached principally and first through the Apostles, who had clung to him, is therefore called their word. Nor is it for that reason not the word of God, because it is called their word; since the same apostle says that the Thessalonians received from him, *not as the word of men, but, as it is,* he says, *truly the word of God*[^11]. Therefore it is God's, because God gave it; but it is called their word, because God committed to them first and chiefly the preaching of it. And through this even that robber had in his faith their word: which was called theirs for the reason that the preaching of it belonged first and chiefly to their office. Finally, when a murmur had arisen concerning the ministry of tables by the widows of the Greeks, before Paul had believed in Christ, the Apostles, who had clung to the Lord before, answered: *It is not good for us to leave the word of God, and serve tables*[^12]. Then they provided that deacons should be ordained, lest they themselves be called away from the office of preaching the word. Whence it was rightly called their word, which is the word of faith, through which all believed, or are to hear and believe, in Christ, from whatever source they heard it. Therefore, in that prayer, for all whom he redeemed, whether then living in the flesh, or to come afterward, our Redeemer prayed, when, asking for the Apostles who were then with him, he added also those who through their word were going to believe in him. But what he says next, those being added, must be treated in another discussion.
