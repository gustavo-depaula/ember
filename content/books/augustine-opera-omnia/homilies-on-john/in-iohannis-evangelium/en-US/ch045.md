# Tractate 45

## *And I came that they might have life, and might have it more abundantly*[^1].

*He will go in and go out.* We go in, when we think something inwardly; we go out, when we work something outwardly; and since through faith Christ dwells in our hearts, to go in through Christ is to think according to that faith; to go out through Christ is, according to that faith, to work even outwardly, that is, before men.

**1.** From that man who was enlightened, who was born blind, the Lord's discourse to the Jews arose. So that to this reading today's is joined, your Charity ought to know and be reminded. For when the Lord had said: *For judgment I came into this world, that those who do not see may see; and that those who see may become blind*[^2]—which, at the time when it was read, we expounded as we could—some of the Pharisees said: *Are we too blind?* To whom he answered: *If you were blind, you would not have sin; but now you say: We see: your sin remains*[^3]. To these words he joined those which we heard today as they were recited.

**2.** *Amen, amen, I say to you, he who does not enter through the door into the sheepfold, but climbs up another way, he is a thief and a robber*[^4]. For they said they were not blind: but they could then see, if they were the sheep of Christ. Whence did they usurp the light, who raged against the day? Because of their vain and proud and incurable arrogance, then, the Lord Jesus joined these things: in which, if we attend, he admonished us wholesomely. For there are many who, according to a certain habit of this life, are called good men, good men and good women, innocent, and, as it were, observing the things commanded in the law; rendering honor to their parents, not committing adultery, not perpetrating murder, not committing theft, not bearing false witness against anyone, and, as it were, observing the rest of the things commanded by the law, who are not Christians: and generally they boast like these men: Are we too blind? But because all these things they do, and they do not know to what end they refer them, they do them in vain; concerning his flock, and concerning the door by which one enters into the fold, the Lord set forth a comparison in today's reading.

For there are many who, according to a certain habit of this life, are called good men, good men and good women, innocent, and, as it were, observing the things commanded in the law; rendering honor to their parents, not committing adultery, not perpetrating murder, not committing theft, not bearing false witness against anyone, and, as it were, observing the rest of the things commanded by the law, who are not Christians: and generally they boast like these men: Are we too blind? But because all these things they do, and they do not know to what end they refer them, they do them in vain; concerning his flock, and concerning the door by which one enters into the fold, the Lord set forth a comparison in today's reading.

So let the pagans say: We live well. If they do not enter through the door, what does it profit them, the thing they boast of? For living well ought to profit each one to this, that he be given to live forever: for one who is not given to live forever, what does it profit to live well? Since they are not even to be said to live well, who either by blindness do not know, or by inflation despise, the end of living well. But there is for no one a true and certain hope of living forever, unless he acknowledge the life, which is Christ; and through the door enter into the fold.

## The pagans do not enter through the door.

**3.** So such men generally seek even to persuade men to live well, and not be Christians. By another part they wish to climb up, to seize and kill; not, as the shepherd does, to preserve and save. So there were certain philosophers, treating many subtle things about virtues and vices, dividing, defining, concluding most acute reasonings, filling books, fanning their own wisdom with cracking cheeks; who even dared to say to men: Follow us, hold to our sect, if you wish to live happily. But they had not entered through the door: they wished to destroy, to slaughter and kill.

## The heretics do not enter through the door.

**4.** What shall I say of these? Behold, the Pharisees themselves were reading, and in what they read, they sounded Christ, hoped that he would come, and did not recognize him present: they too boasted themselves among those who see, that is, among the wise, and they denied Christ, and did not enter through the door. So they too, if perhaps they should seduce any, would seduce them to be slaughtered and killed, not to be set free. And let us leave these aside: let us see whether those, perhaps, themselves enter through the door, who boast in the very name of Christ.

**5.** For there are innumerable ones who not only boast that they see, but wish to be seen as enlightened by Christ: but they are heretics. Did they perhaps enter through the door? Far be it.

Sabellius says: He who is the Son, he himself is the Father: but if he is the Son, he is not the Father. He does not enter through the door who calls the Son the Father.

Arius says: The Father is one thing, the Son another thing. He would speak rightly, if he said "another person"; not, "another thing." For when he says, "another thing," he contradicts him from whom he hears: *I and the Father are one*[^5]. So neither does he enter through the door: for he preaches a Christ such as he fashions for himself, not such as the truth declares. You have the name, you do not have the reality. Christ is the name of some reality: hold to the reality itself, if you wish the name to profit you.

Another, from I know not where, like Photinus: Christ, he says, is a man; he is not God. Neither does he enter through the door, because Christ is both man and God.

And what need is there to run through many things, and to enumerate the many empty things of the heresies? Hold this: that the sheepfold of Christ is the Catholic Church. Whoever wishes to enter into the fold, let him enter through the door, let him preach the true Christ. Let him not only preach the true Christ, but let him seek the glory of Christ, not his own: for many, by seeking their own glory, have scattered the sheep of Christ rather than gathered them. For the Lord Christ is a lowly door: he who enters through this door must humble himself, that with sound head he may be able to enter. But he who does not humble himself, but exalts himself, wishes to climb up by the wall: and he who climbs up by the wall is exalted only to fall.

**6.** Yet the Lord Jesus still speaks under a covering, he is not yet understood: he names the door, he names the fold, he names the sheep; he commends all these, but does not yet expound them. Let us read, then, for he is going to come to those words in which he deigns to expound to us some of the things he has said: from the exposition of which he will perhaps give us to understand also those things which he did not expound. For he feeds us with what is plain, he exercises us with what is obscure.

*He who does not enter through the door into the sheepfold, but climbs up another way.* Woe to the wretch, for he is going to fall! Let him, then, be lowly, let him enter through the door: let him come on level foot, and he will not stumble. *He,* he says, *is a thief and a robber.* He wishes to call sheep that are another's his own sheep: his own to this end, that is, taken by theft, not to save them, but to kill them. So he is a thief, because what is another's he calls his own: a robber, because what he has stolen, he kills. *But he who enters through the door is the shepherd of the sheep: to him the doorkeeper opens.*

Let us inquire about that doorkeeper then, when we have heard from the Lord himself what the door is, and who is the shepherd. *And the sheep hear his voice, and he calls his own sheep by name*[^6]. For he has their names written in the book of life. *He calls his own sheep by name.* Hence the Apostle says: *The Lord knows who are his*[^7]. *And he leads them out. And when he has sent out his own sheep, he goes before them: and the sheep follow him, because they know his voice. But a stranger they do not follow, but flee from him; because they do not know the voice of strangers*[^8]. These things are covered, full of questions, heavy with mysteries. Let us follow, then, and hear the teacher opening something of these obscure things; and through what he opens, perhaps making us enter.

**7.** *This proverb Jesus said to them; but they did not know what he was speaking to them*[^9]. Perhaps neither do we. What, then, is the difference between them and us, before we too recognize these words? That we knock that it may be opened to us; but they, by denying Christ, were unwilling to enter to be saved, but to remain outside to be lost. So in that we hear these things devoutly, in that, before we understand them, we believe them to be true and divine, we differ from these men by a great difference. For when two hear the words of the Gospel, one impious, the other devout, and they are such that perhaps both do not understand, one says: He said nothing; the other says: He spoke truly, and what he said is good, but we do not understand it: this latter, because he believes, already knocks, and is worthy that it be opened to him, if he persists in knocking; but the former still hears: *Unless you believe, you will not understand*[^10].

Why do I commend these things? Because even when I have expounded these obscure words, as I can—either because they are very hidden, and I either do not grasp the understanding of them, or do not have the ability to explain what I understand, or someone is so slow that he does not even follow the one expounding—let him not despair of himself:

let him remain in faith, let him waLuke in the way, let him hear the Apostle saying: *And if you think anything otherwise, this too God will reveal to you: nevertheless, in that to which we have come, in it let us walk*[^11].

## With him came those who were truthful.

**8.** Let us begin, then, to hear him expounding, whom we heard setting forth. So Jesus said to them again: *Amen, amen, I say to you, that I am the door of the sheep*[^12]. Behold, the very door which he had set down as closed, he opened.

He himself is the door. We have recognized it, let us enter, or let us rejoice that we have entered. *All, as many as have come, are thieves and robbers*[^13]. What is this, Lord: All, as many as have come? What then? Did you not come? But understand: I said, All, as many as have come, surely apart from me. Let us consider, then. Before his own coming the Prophets came; were they thieves and robbers? Far be it. They did not come apart from him, because they came with him. Being about to come, he sent heralds, but he possessed the hearts of those whom he sent. Do you wish to know that they came with him who is himself always? For he received flesh in time. What, then, is always? *In the beginning was the Word*[^14]. With him, then, came those who came with the Word of God. *I am,* he says, *the way and the truth and the life*[^15]. If he himself is the truth, with him came those who were truthful. As many, then, as came apart from him, are thieves and robbers; that is, to steal and to kill.

## In different signs the same faith.

**9.** *But the sheep did not hear them*[^16]. This is a greater question, the sheep did not hear them. Before the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ, by which he came lowly in the flesh, the just went before, believing in him who was to come in the same way that we believe in him who has come. The times were varied, not the faith. For the very words too are varied according to the time, when they are differently inflected: it has one sound: He is to come; another sound: He has come: the sound is changed, he is to come, and he has come: yet the same faith joins both, both those who believed that he was to come, and those who believed that he had come. At different times, indeed, but both, through the one door of faith, that is, through Christ, we see to have entered.

We believe that the Lord Jesus Christ, born of the Virgin, came in the flesh, suffered, rose again, ascended into heaven: all this, as you hear the words of past time, we believe to have already been fulfilled. In the fellowship of his faith there are with us those fathers also, who believed that he would be born of the Virgin, would suffer, would rise again, would ascend into heaven: for the Apostle shows them where he says: *But having the same spirit of faith, as it is written: I believed, therefore I have spoken; we too believe, therefore we also speak*[^17]. The prophet said: *I believed, therefore I have spoken*[^18]: the Apostle says: *We too believe, therefore we also speak.*

But that you may know that the faith is one, hear him saying: *Having the same spirit of faith, we too believe.* So too in another place: *For I do not wish you to be ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, and all were baptized into Moses, in the cloud and in the sea, and all ate the same spiritual food, and all drank the same spiritual drink*[^19]. The Red Sea signifies Baptism; Moses, the leader through the Red Sea, signifies Christ; the people passing through signify the faithful; the death of the Egyptians signifies the abolition of sins. In different signs the same faith: so in different signs, as in different words; because words change their sounds according to the times, and words are surely nothing else than signs. For by signifying they are words: take away the signification from a word, it is an empty noise.

So all things are signified. Did not those believe the same things, through whom these signs were administered, through whom the same things that we believe were foretold in prophecy? Surely they believed: but they that he was to come, and we that he has come. Therefore he also says thus: *They drank the same spiritual drink.* The same spiritual, for the bodily not the same. For what did they drink? *For they drank from the spiritual rock that followed: but the rock was Christ*[^20]. See, then, the faith abiding, the signs varied. There the rock was Christ; to us, Christ is what is placed on the altar of God. And they, for a great sacrament of the same Christ, drank the water flowing from the rock; what we drink, the faithful know. If you attend to the visible appearance, it is one thing: if to the intelligible signification, they drank the same spiritual drink.

As many, then, as at that time believed—whether Abraham, or Isaac, or Jacob, or Moses, or the other patriarchs, and the other prophets foretelling Christ—were sheep, and they heard Christ: they heard not a stranger's voice, but his own. The judge had been in the herald. For when the judge speaks through a herald, the recorder does not write: The herald said; but: The judge said. There are others, then, whom the sheep did not hear, in whom there was not the voice of Christ, erring, speaking false things, chattering empty things, fashioning vain things, seducing the wretched.

**10.** What is it, then, that I said: This is a greater question? What has it obscure and difficult to understand? Hear, I beseech you. Behold, the Lord himself, Jesus Christ, came, preached: surely much more was it the voice of the shepherd, uttered by the very mouth of the shepherd. For if through the Prophets it was the voice of the shepherd, how much more did the very tongue of the shepherd utter the voice of the shepherd? Not all heard. But what do we think? Were those who heard sheep? Behold, Judas heard, and was a wolf: he followed, but, covered with a sheep's skin, he lay in wait for the shepherd. But some of those who crucified Christ were not hearing, and were sheep: for he saw them in the crowd, when he said: *When you have exalted the Son of man, then you will know that I am*[^21]. How, then, is this question solved? Non-sheep hear, and sheep do not hear; certain wolves follow the voice of the shepherd, and certain sheep contradict him; finally, the sheep kill the shepherd. The question is solved: for someone answers and says: But when they were not hearing, they were not yet sheep, then they were wolves; the heard voice changed them, and from wolves made them sheep: when, then, they were made sheep, they heard, and found the shepherd, and followed the shepherd; they hoped for the shepherd's promises, because they did his commands.

**11.** This question is somehow solved, and may perhaps suffice for some. But it still moves me, and what moves me I share with you, that, in a way seeking with you, by his revealing I may deserve to find with you. So receive what moves me.

Through the prophet Ezekiel the Lord rebukes the shepherds, and says among other things about the sheep: *The straying sheep you have not called back*[^22]. He both calls it straying and calls it a sheep. If at the time it was straying it was a sheep, whose voice did it hear, that it strayed? For beyond doubt it would not stray, if it heard the voice of the shepherd: but it strayed for this reason, that it heard the voice of a stranger; it heard the voice of the thief and the robber. Surely the sheep do not hear the voice of robbers: *Those who came,* he says—and we understand: Apart from me; that is: Those who came apart from me—*are thieves and robbers, and the sheep did not hear them.*

Lord, if the sheep did not hear them, how do the sheep stray? If the sheep hear none but you, and you are the truth: whoever hears the truth surely does not stray. But those men stray, and are called sheep. For if in that error they were not called sheep, it would not be said through Ezekiel: *The straying sheep you have not called back.* How does it both stray, and is a sheep? Did it hear the voice of a stranger? Surely the sheep did not hear them.

Then now many are gathered to the fold of Christ, and from heretics become Catholics; they are taken from the thieves, restored to the shepherd: and sometimes they murmur, suffer annoyance at the one calling them back, and do not understand the one slaying them; nevertheless, even when those who are sheep have come reluctantly, they recognize the voice of the shepherd, and rejoice that they have come, and blush that they strayed. When, then, in that error they boasted as though in the truth, and surely did not hear the voice of the shepherd, and for that reason followed a stranger, were they sheep or were they not? If they were sheep, how do the sheep not hear strangers? If they were not sheep, why are those rebuked to whom it is said: The straying sheep you have not called back?

Even in those already made Catholic Christians, faithful of good hope, sometimes evils befall; they are seduced into error, and after error are called back: when they were seduced into error, and rebaptized, or after the fellowship of the Lord's fold rolled back again into their former error, were they sheep or were they not? Surely they were Catholics. If they were faithful Catholics, they were sheep. If they were sheep, how could they hear the voice of a stranger, since the Lord says, the sheep did not hear them?

**12.** You have heard, brothers, the depth of the question. So I say: *The Lord knows who are his*[^23]. He knows the foreknown, he knows the predestined: for of this it is said: *But those whom he foreknew, he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son, that he himself might be the firstborn among many brothers. But those whom he predestined, them he also called; and those whom he called, them he also justified; but those whom he justified, them he also glorified. If God is for us, who is against us?* Add further: *He who did not spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how has he not also with him given us all things?* But to which us? To the foreknown, the predestined, the justified, the glorified: of whom it follows: *Who will accuse against the elect of God?*[^24] The Lord, then, knows who are his; these are the sheep.

Sometimes they themselves do not know themselves, but the shepherd knows them, according to that predestination, according to that foreknowledge of God, according to the election of the sheep before the foundation of the world: for this too the Apostle says: *As he chose us in him before the foundation of the world*[^25]. According to that foreknowledge of God and predestination, then, how many sheep are outside, how many wolves inside; and how many sheep inside, and how many wolves outside!

What is it that I said, how many sheep are outside? How many now revel, who will be chaste; how many blaspheme Christ, who will believe in Christ; how many make themselves drunk, who will be sober; how many seize the goods of others, who will give away their own! Nevertheless, now they hear a stranger's voice, they follow strangers. Likewise, how many inside praise, who will blaspheme; are chaste, who will fornicate; are sober, who will later bury themselves in wine; stand, who will fall! They are not sheep. (For we speak of the predestined; we speak of those whom the Lord knows, who are his.) And yet they themselves, as long as they are rightly minded, hear the voice of Christ. Behold, these hear, those do not hear: and yet according to predestination these are not sheep, those are sheep.

## Let devotion abide, and the truth will be revealed.

**13.** Still the question remains, which for the moment now seems to me capable of being resolved thus. There is a certain voice, there is, I say, a certain voice of the shepherd, in which the sheep do not hear strangers, in which non-sheep do not hear Christ. What is that voice? *He who perseveres to the end, he will be saved*[^26]. This voice his own do not neglect, the stranger does not hear: for the stranger too preaches this to him, that he persevere with him to the end, but, by not persevering with him, he does not hear this voice. He came to Christ, heard one word and another, this and that, all true, all sound: among all of which is also that voice: *He who perseveres to the end, he will be saved.* He who hears this is a sheep. But some one or other was hearing it, and became foolish, grew cold, heard a stranger's voice: if he is predestined, he strayed for a time, he did not perish forever; he returns to hear what he neglected, to do what he heard. For if he is among those who are predestined, God foreknew both his error and his future conversion; if he strayed, he returns to hear that voice of the shepherd, and to follow him who says: *He who perseveres to the end, he will be saved.*

A good voice, brothers, a true one, a shepherd's; it is itself *the voice of salvation in the tabernacles of the just*[^27]. For it is easy to hear Christ, it is easy to praise the Gospel, easy to acclaim the one discussing: to persevere to the end, this belongs to the sheep that hear the voice of the shepherd. A temptation befalls, persevere to the end: because the temptation does not persevere to the end. To what end will you persevere? Until you finish the way. For as long as you do not hear Christ, your adversary is in this way, that is, in this mortal life. But what does he say?

*Agree with your adversary quickly, while you are with him on the way*[^28]. You have heard, believed, agreed. If you were opposing, agree. If it has been granted you to agree, do not quarrel any longer. For you do not know when the way is finished, but he knows. If you are a sheep, and if you persevere to the end, you will be saved: and through this, his own do not despise this voice, strangers do not hear it.

As I could, as he himself granted, I have either expounded to you a very deep question, or treated it with you. If some have understood less, let devotion abide, and the truth will be revealed: but let those who have understood not exalt themselves as the quicker over the slower, lest by exalting themselves they go off the road, and the slower more easily arrive. But may he lead all through, to whom we say: *Lead me, O Lord, in your way, and I will waLuke in your truth*[^29].

**14.** Through this, then, which the Lord expounded, that he himself is the door, let us enter to the things which he set forth, and did not expound. And who indeed the shepherd is, although he did not say in this reading which was recited today, yet in the one that follows he says most plainly: *I am the good shepherd*[^30]. And even if he did not say it, whom other than himself ought we to understand in those words, where he says: *He who enters through the door is the shepherd of the sheep. To him the doorkeeper opens: and the sheep hear his voice; and he calls his own sheep by name, and leads them out. And when he has sent out his own sheep, he goes before them: and the sheep follow him, because they know his voice*[^31]? For who else calls his own sheep by name, and leads them out from here to eternal life, except he who knows the names of the predestined? Hence he says to his disciples: *Rejoice that your names are written in heaven*[^32]: for from there he calls them by name. And who else leads them out, except he who forgives their sins, that they may be able, freed from hard bonds, to follow him? And who went before them, that they might follow him, except he who, rising from the dead, dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him[^33]: and who, when he was conspicuous here in the flesh, said: *Father, those whom you have given me, I will that where I am, they too may be with me*[^34]? Hence is that which he says: *I am the door: through me if anyone enters, he will be saved; and he will go in, and go out; and will find pastures*[^35]. In this he evidently shows that not only the shepherd, but also the sheep enter through the door.

**15.** But what is, *he will go in, and go out, and will find pastures?* To go into the Church through the door, Christ, is a very good thing: but to go out of the Church, as this same John the evangelist says in his Epistle: *They went out from us, but they were not of us*[^36], is surely not good. Such a going out, then, could not be praised by the good shepherd; so that he should say, *he will go in, and go out, and will find pastures.* There is, then, a certain going out, not only a going in, that is good, through the good door, which is Christ. But what is that praiseworthy and blessed going out?

I could indeed say that we go in, when we think something inwardly; but go out, when we work something outwardly: and since, as the Apostle says, *through faith Christ dwells in our hearts*[^37], to go in through Christ is to think according to that faith; but to go out through Christ is, according to that faith, to work even outwardly, that is, before men. Hence in the Psalm too it is read: *Man will go out to his work*[^38]: and the Lord himself says: *Let your works shine before men*[^39].

But what delights me more is that the truth itself, as a good shepherd, and therefore a good teacher, has in a manner admonished us how we ought to understand what he says, "he will go in, and go out, and will find pastures"; when, continuing, he added: *The thief does not come except to steal, and slaughter, and destroy: I came that they might have life, and might have it more abundantly*[^40]. For he seems to me to have said: That they who go in might have life, and they who go out might have it more abundantly. But no one can go out through the door, that is, through Christ, to eternal life, which will be in the vision, unless through that very door, that is, through the same Christ, he has entered into his Church, which is his fold, to temporal life, which is in faith. Therefore he says: *I came, that they might have life*, that is, faith, which works through love[^41]; through which faith they enter into the fold that they may live, because *the just lives by faith*[^42]: *and might have it more abundantly*, who, persevering to the end, through that door, that is, through the faith of Christ, go out, since true believers die; and they will have life more abundantly, by coming where that shepherd went before, where they may never thereafter die.

Although, then, even here in the fold itself pastures are not lacking—since we can understand what was said, *and will find pastures*, with regard to both, that is, both to the going in and to the going out—yet they will then find the true pastures, where those who hunger and thirst for righteousness[^43] are filled; such pastures as he found to whom it was said: *Today you will be with me in paradise*[^44].

But how he himself is the door, himself the shepherd, so that through himself, in a manner, he himself is understood both to go in and to go out, and who the doorkeeper is, it is too long to inquire today, and, by discussing, to explain as he himself shall grant.
