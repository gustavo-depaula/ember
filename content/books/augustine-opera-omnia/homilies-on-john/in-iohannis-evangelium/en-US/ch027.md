# Homily 27

The words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life (Jn 6:60-71).

This the Lord commended in the eating of his flesh and the drinking of his blood, that we should abide in him, and he in us. We abide in him when we are his members; he abides in us when we are his temple. But that we may be his members, unity joins us together. That unity may join us together, what brings it about but love?

**1.** We have heard the words of the Lord from the Gospel, which follow the earlier sermon. From this a sermon is owed to your ears and minds, and it is not unsuited to this present day: for it is about the body of the Lord, which he said he gave to be eaten for the sake of eternal life. And he expounded the manner of this bestowal and of his gift, how he would give his flesh to be eaten, saying: *He who eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, abides in me, and I in him*[^1]. The sign that one has eaten and drunk is this: if he abides and is abided in, if he dwells and is dwelt in, if he clings so as not to be deserted.

This, then, he taught and admonished us by mystical words, that we should be in his body, under him as head, among his members, eating his flesh, not abandoning his unity. But most of those who were present, by not understanding, were scandalized: for in hearing these things they thought of nothing but flesh, which they themselves were. But the Apostle says, and says truly: *To be wise according to the flesh is death*[^2]. The Lord gives us his flesh to eat, and to be wise according to the flesh is death; while of his flesh he says that there is eternal life. Therefore we ought not to be wise about the flesh according to the flesh, as in these words.

## The secret of God ought to make us attentive.

**2.** And so many hearing—not of his enemies, but of his disciples—said: *This is a hard saying; who can listen to it?*[^3] If the disciples found this saying hard, what of the enemies? And yet it had to be said in such a way that it would not be understood by all. The secret of God ought to make us attentive, not opposed. But these quickly failed, while the Lord Jesus was speaking such things: they did not believe that he was saying something great, and covering over by those words some grace; but they understood it as they wished, and after the manner of men, that Jesus was able—or that Jesus was so arranging—to distribute, as it were cut up, to those believing in him the flesh with which the Word was clothed. *This is a hard saying*, they say; *who can listen to it?*

**3.** *But Jesus, knowing within himself that his disciples were murmuring about it.* For they said these things among themselves in such a way as not to be heard by him; but he who knew them within themselves, hearing within himself, answered and said: *Does this scandalize you, that I said: I give you my flesh to eat, and my blood to drink—this surely scandalizes you. If, then, you should see the Son of man ascending where he was before?*[^4] What is this? Does he hereby resolve what had moved them? does he hereby open up the source of their being scandalized? Hereby plainly, if they understood. For they thought he was going to dole out his body; but he said he would ascend into heaven, surely whole. *When you see the Son of man ascending where he was before*; surely then at least you will see that he does not dole out his body in the way you think; surely then at least you will understand that his grace is not consumed by bites.

**4.** And he says: *It is the spirit that gives life, the flesh profits nothing*[^5]. Before we expound this, as the Lord grants, that must not be passed over carelessly which he said: *If, then, you should see the Son of man ascending where he was before?* For Christ is the Son of man, from the Virgin Mary. Therefore the Son of man began to be here on earth, where he took up flesh from the earth. Whence it had been said prophetically: *Truth has arisen from the earth*[^6]. What, then, does he mean when he says: *When you see the Son of man ascending where he was before?* For there would be no question if he had said: If you see the Son of God ascending where he was before? but since he said the Son of man ascending where he was before, was the Son of man in heaven before, when he began to be on earth?

Here indeed he said, *where he was before*, as though he were not then there when he was speaking these things. But in another place he says: *No one has ascended into heaven, except him who came down from heaven, the Son of man, who is in heaven*[^7]; he did not say, *was*, but: *The Son of man*, he says, *who is in heaven*. He was speaking on earth, and said he was in heaven. And he did not say it thus: No one has ascended into heaven, except him who came down from heaven, the Son of God, who is in heaven. To what does this pertain, except that we may understand—what I commended to your Charity in the earlier sermon too—that Christ, God and man, is one person, not two; lest our faith be not a Trinity, but a quaternity? Christ, then, is one: Word, soul, and flesh, one Christ; the Son of God and the son of man, one Christ. Always the Son of God, the son of man in time; yet one Christ according to the unity of person. He was in heaven, when he was speaking on earth. So the son of man was in heaven, just as the Son of God was on earth; the Son of God on earth in the flesh he had taken up, the son of man in heaven in the unity of person.

**5.** What, then, is it that he adds: *It is the spirit that gives life, the flesh profits nothing*? Let us say to him (for he allows us, not contradicting, but desiring to know): O Lord, good master, how does the flesh profit nothing, when you have said: *Unless one eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, he will not have life in him*[^8]? Or does life profit nothing? and for what are we what we are, except that we may have eternal life, which you promise by your flesh? what is it, then, that the flesh profits nothing? It profits nothing—but as they understood it: for they understood flesh as it is torn in a corpse, or sold in a meat market, not as it is given life by the spirit.

Accordingly it was said: *The flesh profits nothing*; as it was said: *Knowledge puffs up*. Ought we then now to hate knowledge? Far be it. And what is, *Knowledge puffs up*? Knowledge alone, without love: therefore he added: *But love builds up*[^9]. Add, then, love to knowledge, and knowledge will be useful; not by itself, but through love. So also now, the flesh profits nothing, but flesh alone: let the spirit come to the flesh, as love comes to knowledge, and it profits very much.

For if the flesh profited nothing, the Word would not have become flesh, to dwell among us. If Christ profited us much through the flesh, how does the flesh profit nothing? But through the flesh the Spirit did something for our salvation. The flesh was a vessel; attend to what it held, not to what it was. The Apostles were sent; did their flesh profit us nothing? If the flesh of the Apostles profited us, could the flesh of the Lord profit nothing? For from where does the sound of the word come to us, except through the voice of the flesh? from where the pen, from where the writing? All these are works of the flesh, but with the spirit moving it as its instrument. *It is the spirit, then, that gives life, but the flesh profits nothing*: as they understood the flesh, not so do I give my flesh to be eaten.

## These things are said that we may love unity.

**6.** Accordingly: *The words*, he says, *that I have spoken to you are spirit and life*[^10]. For we said, brothers, that the Lord commended this in the eating of his flesh and the drinking of his blood, that we should abide in him, and he in us. We abide in him when we are his members: he abides in us when we are his temple. But that we may be his members, unity joins us together. That unity may join us together, what brings it about but love? And the love of God, from where? Ask the Apostle: *The love of God*, he says, *has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^11]. Therefore it is the spirit that gives life: for the spirit makes living members. Nor does the spirit make any members living except those it has found in the body which that same spirit gives life to.

For the spirit that is in you, O man, by which you stand as a man, does it give life to a member which it has found separated from your flesh? I call your spirit your soul: your soul gives life only to the members that are in your flesh; if you take one away, it is no longer given life from your soul, because it is not joined to the unity of your body. These things are said that we may love unity, and fear separation. For nothing ought a Christian so to dread as to be separated from the body of Christ. For if he is separated from the body of Christ, he is not his member; if he is not his member, he is not given life by his Spirit: *But whoever*, says the Apostle, *does not have the Spirit of Christ, he is not his*[^12].

*It is the spirit, then, that gives life, but the flesh profits nothing. The words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life.* What is, *they are spirit and life*? They are to be understood spiritually. Have you understood spiritually? they are spirit and life. Have you understood carnally? even so they are spirit and life, but they are not so to you.

## By faith we are joined, by understanding we are given life.

**7.** *But there are some*, he says, *among you who do not believe*[^13]. He did not say: There are some among you who do not understand; but he said the cause why they do not understand. For there are some among you who do not believe; and therefore they do not understand, because they do not believe. For the prophet said: *Unless you believe, you will not understand*[^14]. By faith we are joined, by understanding we are given life. First let us cling by faith, that there may be something to be given life by understanding. For he who does not cling, resists; he who resists, does not believe. For he who resists, how is he given life? He is an adversary to the ray of light, by which he ought to be penetrated: he does not turn away his gaze, but shuts his mind.

There are, then, some who do not believe. Let them believe and open, let them open and they will be enlightened. *For Jesus knew from the beginning who were the believers, and who would betray him*[^15]. For Judas too was there. For some were scandalized: but he stayed to plot, not to understand. And because he had stayed for that reason, the Lord did not keep silent about him. He did not name him outright, but neither was he silent; that all might fear, although one would perish. But after he had spoken and distinguished the believers from the unbelievers, he expressed the cause why they do not believe: *For this reason I said to you*, he says, *that no one can come to me, unless it has been given him by my Father*[^16]. Therefore to believe too is given us: for to believe is not nothing. But if it is something great, rejoice that you have believed, but do not be lifted up: *for what have you that you have not received?*[^17]

**8.** *From this many of his disciples went back, and no longer walked with him*[^18]. They went back, but after Satan, not after Christ. For once the Lord Christ called Peter Satan, rather because he wished to go before his Lord, and to give counsel that he should not die who had come that he might die, lest we should die forever; and he said to him: *Get behind me, Satan; for you are not wise in the things of God, but in the things of man*[^19]. He did not drive him back to go after Satan, and call him Satan; but made him go behind himself, that he might not be Satan by walking behind the Lord.

But these went back in the way in which the Apostle says of certain women: *For some have turned back after Satan*[^20]. They no longer walked with him. See, cut off from the body, they lost life, because perhaps they were not even in the body. Among the unbelievers they too are to be reckoned, although they were called disciples. They went back, not few, but many.

This perhaps happened for our consolation, since it sometimes happens that a man says what is true, and what he says is not grasped, and those who hear are scandalized and depart. But the man is sorry to have said what is true: for the man says within himself: I ought not to have spoken so, I ought not to have said this. See, it happened to the Lord; he spoke, and lost many, and remained with few. But he himself was not disturbed, because from the beginning he knew both who were the believers, and who the unbelievers: but if it happens to us, we are disturbed. Let us find solace in the Lord, and yet let us speak our words cautiously.

## Peter answered, one for many, unity for all.

**9.** And he, to the few who had remained: *Jesus, therefore, said to the twelve*; that is, to those twelve who remained: *Do you too*, he says, *wish to go?* Not even Judas departed. But why he was staying was now apparent to the Lord; to us it was made manifest afterward. Peter answered for all, one for many, unity for all: *Simon Peter, therefore, answered him: Lord, to whom shall we go?* You drive us from you, give us another you. To whom shall we go? If we withdraw from you, to whom shall we go? *You have the words of eternal life*.

See how Peter, by God's giving, by the Holy Spirit's renewing, understood. Whence, except because he believed? *You have the words of eternal life.* For you have eternal life in the ministration of your body and blood. *And we have believed, and have known.* We did not know, and then believe, but we believed, and then knew. For we believed in order to know: for if we wished first to know, and then to believe, we would be able neither to know nor to believe. What did we believe, and what did we know? *That you are the Christ, the Son of God*[^21]; that is, that you are eternal life itself, and that in your flesh and blood you give nothing but what you are.

**10.** The Lord Jesus, then, says: *Did I not choose you twelve, and one of you is a devil?*[^22] Therefore he would say: Eleven I chose: or is even a devil chosen, and is a devil among the chosen? The chosen are usually spoken of in praise: or is this one too chosen, of whom, unwilling and unknowing, some great good would be done? This is proper to God; the contrary to the wicked. For as the wicked make evil use of the good works of God; so, on the contrary, God makes good use of the evil works of wicked men.

How good it is that the members of the body are so disposed as they could not be disposed except by God the craftsman! Yet how badly does wantonness make use of the eyes? How badly does deceit make use of the tongue? Does not a false witness first slaughter his own soul with his tongue, and try to harm another after destroying himself? He makes bad use of the tongue, nor is the tongue therefore evil: the tongue is a work of God, but that wickedness makes bad use of God's good work. How do those who run to crimes use their feet? how do murderers use their hands? and how badly do the wicked use the good creatures of God lying outside, around them? With gold they corrupt judgments, oppress the innocent. The wicked make bad use of this light: for by living badly they usurp even the very light by which they see for the service of their crimes. For the wicked man, that he may do some evil, wishes light for himself lest he stumble—he who has already stumbled and fallen within: what he fears in body, he has already incurred in heart.

Of all God's good things, then—not to run through them one by one, which would be long—the wicked man makes bad use: on the contrary, the good man makes good use of the evils of wicked men. And what is so good as the one God? Since the Lord himself said: *No one is good but the one God*[^23]. By how much, then, he is better, by so much he makes better use even of our evils. What is worse than Judas? Among all who clung to the Master, among the twelve, the purse was entrusted to him, and the dispensing for the poor distributed: ungrateful for so great a benefit, for so great an honor, he received money, lost righteousness: he, dead, betrayed life; whom he had followed as a disciple, he persecuted as an enemy. All this was the evil of Judas; but of his evil the Lord made good use. He bore being betrayed that he might redeem us. See, the evil of Judas was turned into good. How many martyrs has Satan persecuted? If Satan ceased from persecuting, today we would not be celebrating the so glorious crown of Saint Lawrence. If, then, God makes good use even of the evil works of the devil himself: what the wicked man does, by making bad use, harms himself; it does not contradict the goodness of God. The craftsman uses him; and the great craftsman, if he did not know how to use him, would not even permit him to exist.

Therefore *one of you is a devil*, he says, though I chose you twelve. What he says, *I chose twelve*, can also be understood thus, because the number is sacred. For because one perished from it, the honor of that number was not therefore diminished: for in the place of the one perishing, another was substituted[^24]. The consecrated number remained, the number twelve; because throughout the whole world, that is, through the four quarters of the world, they were to announce the Trinity. Therefore three times four. Judas, then, exterminated himself, he did not violate the number twelve: he himself deserted his teacher, for God appointed him a successor.

## Let us eat and drink unto the participation of the Spirit.

**11.** All this which the Lord spoke about his flesh and his blood, and that in the grace of its distribution he promised us eternal life, and that he wished those who eat and drink his flesh and blood to be understood as abiding in him and he in them, and that those who did not believe did not understand, and that by being wise about spiritual things carnally they were scandalized, and that when they were scandalized and perishing the Lord was present as a comfort to the disciples who had remained, whom, to test them, he asked: *Do you too wish to go?* that the answer of their remaining might be made known to us; for he knew that they were remaining: this whole, then, dearly beloved, let it avail us for this, that we may not eat the flesh of Christ and the blood of Christ only in the Sacrament, which many wicked men do too; but that we may eat and drink unto the participation of the Spirit, that we may abide in the body of the Lord as members, that we may be given life by his Spirit, and not be scandalized, even if many now eat and drink with us the Sacraments temporally, who will have eternal torments at the end.

For now the body of Christ is mixed as it were on the threshing floor: but *the Lord knows who are his*[^25]. If you know what you are threshing, that the lurking grain is there, and the threshing does not consume what the winnowing is to purge; we are certain, brothers, that all of us who are in the body of the Lord, and abide in him, that he too may abide in us, in this world must of necessity live among the wicked until the end. I do not mean among those wicked who blaspheme Christ: for rare now are those found who blaspheme with the tongue, but many who blaspheme by their life. It is necessary, then, that we live among them until the end.

**12.** But what is it that he says: *He who abides in me, and I in him*[^26]? What, except what the martyrs heard: *He who has persevered to the end, he will be saved*[^27]? How did Saint Lawrence, whose feast we celebrate today, abide in him? He abided to the testing, abided to the tyrannical interrogation, abided to the most savage threatening, abided to destruction: it is too little—he abided to a monstrous torturing. For he was not killed quickly, but tortured in the fire: he was permitted to live long; indeed, he was not permitted to live long, but compelled to die slowly. In that long death, then, in those torments, because he had eaten well and drunk well, as it were fattened by that food and inebriated by that cup, he did not feel the torments. For there was present he who said: *It is the spirit that gives life*[^28]. For the flesh burned, but the spirit gave life to the soul. He did not yield, and he passed into the kingdom.

Now the holy martyr Xystus, whose day we celebrated five days ago from here, had said to him: "Do not grieve, my son." For he was a bishop, this one a deacon. "Do not grieve," he said; "you will follow me after three days." And he said three days, the interval between the day of the passion of Saint Xystus, and the day of the present passion of Saint Lawrence. Three days is the interval. O consolation! he did not say: Do not grieve, my son; the persecution will cease, and you will be secure: but: Do not grieve; where I go before, you will follow; nor is your following deferred: three days will be the interval, and you will be with me. He received the oracle, conquered the devil, arrived at the triumph.
