# Homily 11

Unless one is born anew[^1].

There are two births: one is of the flesh, the other of the Spirit. But these two are each single; neither the one can be repeated, nor the other. Rightly understand the birth of the spirit as Nicodemus understood the birth of the flesh. He who is born of the Catholic Church is born, as it were, of Sarah; he who is born of heresy is born, as it were, of the maidservant, but from the seed of Abraham.

**1.** Opportunely the Lord has provided for us today the order of this reading: for since we undertook to consider and treat the Gospel according to John in order, I believe your Charity has noticed it. Opportunely, then, it has come about that today you heard from the Gospel: *Unless one is born again of water and the Spirit, he will not see the kingdom of God*[^2]. For it is time that we exhort you who are still catechumens: who have so believed in Christ that you still carry your sins. But no one laden with sins will see the kingdom of heaven; because unless they are forgiven him, he will not reign with Christ: and they cannot be forgiven except to him who has been born again of water and the Holy Spirit.

But let us notice how all the words stand, so that here those who are sluggish may find with what solicitude they must hasten to lay down their burden. For if they were carrying some heavy load, of stone, or of wood, or even of some gain—if they carried grain, if wine, if money—they would run to lay down the burdens: they carry the burden of sins, and are sluggish to run. One must run to lay down this burden; it presses, and it sinks one down.

## Jesus did not entrust himself to them.

**2.** Behold, you have heard that when the Lord Jesus Christ was *at Jerusalem at the Passover on the feast day, many believed in his name, seeing the signs that he did. Many believed in his name: and what follows? But Jesus himself did not entrust himself to them*[^3]. What, then, does this mean: They believed in his name; and: Jesus himself did not entrust himself to them? Or had they perhaps not believed in him, and were pretending that they had believed, and for that reason Jesus did not entrust himself to them? But the Evangelist would not say: *Many believed in his name*, unless he bore them true witness. A great thing, then, and a marvelous thing: men believe in Christ, and Christ does not entrust himself to men.

Especially since he is the Son of God, he surely suffered willingly; and if he were unwilling, he would never have suffered; and if he were unwilling, he would not even have been born: but if he willed this only, that he should merely be born, and not die, and should do whatever he willed; because the Son of the almighty Father is almighty. Let us prove it from the facts themselves. For when they wished to seize him, he withdrew from them; the Gospel says so: *And when they wished to cast him down from the brow of the hill, he withdrew from them unharmed*[^4]. And when they came to arrest him, already sold by Judas the betrayer, while that man thought he had it in his power to hand over his master and Lord; there too the Lord showed that he suffered by will, not by necessity. For when the Jews wished to arrest him, he said to them: Whom do you seek? *And they said: Jesus of Nazareth. And he: I am he. At this voice, they went backward, and fell*[^5]. In that, answering, he cast them down, he showed his power; that in that he was arrested by them, he might show his will. Therefore that he suffered was of mercy. *For he was handed over for our offenses, and rose again for our justification*[^6]. Hear his own words: *I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again: no one takes it from me, but I lay it down of myself, that I may take it up again*[^7].

Since, then, he had such great power, since he preached it by words and showed it by deeds; what does it mean that Jesus did not entrust himself to them, as if they could harm one unwilling, or do anything to one unwilling, especially since they had already believed in his name? And the Evangelist says of those same men: *They believed in his name*, of whom he says: *But Jesus himself did not entrust himself to them*. Why? *Because he himself knew all men, and because he had no need that anyone should bear witness about a man; for he himself knew what was in a man*[^8]. The maker knew better what was in his own work than the work itself knew what was in itself. The creator of man knew what was in man, which man himself, created, did not know. Do we not prove this from Peter, that he did not know what was in himself, when he said: *With you unto death?*[^9] Hear that the Lord knew what was in man: You with me unto death? *Amen, amen, I say to you, before the cock crows, you will deny me three times*[^10]. Man, then, did not know what was in himself; but the creator of man knew what was in man.

Yet many believed in his name, and Jesus himself did not entrust himself to them. What do we say, brothers? Perhaps what follows will show us what the mystery of these words means. That men had believed in him is manifest, is true: no one doubts it, the Gospel says so, the truthful Evangelist testifies. Likewise that Jesus himself did not entrust himself to them, this too is manifest, and no Christian doubts it; because this too the Gospel says, and the same truthful Evangelist testifies. Why, then, did they believe in his name, and Jesus did not entrust himself to them? Let us look at what follows.

## Such are all catechumens.

**3.** *Now there was a man of the Pharisees, Nicodemus by name, a ruler of the Jews: he came to him by night, and said to him, Rabbi*[^11]. You already know this, that Rabbi means teacher. *We know that you have come from God as a teacher: for no one can do these signs that you do, unless God is with him*[^12]. Therefore this Nicodemus was of those who had believed in his name, seeing the signs and wonders that he did. For above he said this: *But when he was at Jerusalem at the Passover on the feast day, many believed in his name*. Why did they believe? It follows and says, *seeing the signs that he did*. And what does it say of Nicodemus? *There was a ruler of the Jews, Nicodemus by name: he came to him by night, and said to him: Rabbi, we know that you have come from God as a teacher*. This man too, then, had believed in his name. And from what had he believed? It follows: *For no one can do these signs that you do, unless God is with him*.

If, then, Nicodemus was of those many who had believed in his name, let us now notice in this Nicodemus why Jesus did not entrust himself to them. *Jesus answered and said to him: Amen, amen, I say to you, unless one is born anew, he cannot see the kingdom of God*[^13]. To those, then, Jesus entrusts himself, who have been born anew. Behold, those men had believed in him, and Jesus did not entrust himself to them. Such are all catechumens: they already believe in the name of Christ, but Jesus does not entrust himself to them. Let your Charity attend and understand. If we say to a catechumen: Do you believe in Christ? he answers: I believe, and signs himself: he already carries the cross of Christ on his forehead, and is not ashamed of the cross of his Lord. Behold, he has believed in his name. Let us question him: Do you eat the flesh of the Son of man, and drink the blood of the Son of man? he does not know what we say, because Jesus has not entrusted himself to him.

**4.** Since, then, Nicodemus was of that number, he came to the Lord: but he came by night; and this perhaps pertains to the matter. He came to the Lord, and came by night: he came to the light, and came in darkness. But those born again of water and the Spirit, what do they hear from the Apostle? *You were once darkness; but now light in the Lord: walk as sons of light*[^14]: and again: *But let us who are of the day be sober*[^15]. Those, then, who have been born again, were of the night, and are of the day: they were darkness, and are light. Now Jesus entrusts himself to them, and they do not come to Jesus by night as Nicodemus did, they do not seek the day in darkness. For such men now even make profession: Jesus has come to them, he has wrought salvation in them: because he himself said: *Unless one eats my flesh and drinks my blood, he will not have life in him*[^16].

And in that the catechumens have the sign of the cross on their forehead, they already belong to the great house; but let them become from servants sons. For they are not nothing who already belong to the great house. But when did the people of Israel eat manna? When they had crossed the Red Sea. But what the Red Sea signifies, hear the Apostle: *But I do not wish you to be ignorant, brothers, that all our fathers were under the cloud, and all passed through the sea*[^17]. Why did they pass through the sea, as if you asked him; he goes on and says: *And all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea*[^18].

If, then, the figure of the sea availed so much, how much will the reality of Baptism avail? If what was done in the figure led the people, once they had crossed, to the manna; what will Christ show in the reality of his baptism, when his people have crossed through him? Through his baptism he brings the believers across, all their sins being slain, as enemies pursuing them, just as in that sea all the Egyptians perished. Where does he bring them across, my brothers? where does Jesus bring them across through Baptism, whose figure Moses then bore, who brought them across through the sea? Where does he bring them across? To the manna. What is the manna? I am, he says, *the living bread, who came down from heaven*[^19].

The faithful receive the manna, having now crossed through the Red Sea. Why the Red Sea? it is now a sea, why also red? That Red Sea signified the baptism of Christ. Whence is the baptism of Christ red, but as consecrated by the blood of Christ? Where, then, does he lead the believers and the baptized? To the manna. Behold, I say manna: it is known what the Jews, that people of Israel, received, it is known what God rained on them from heaven; and the catechumens do not know what the Christians receive. Let them be ashamed, then, that they do not know; let them cross through the Red Sea, let them eat the manna: so that, as they have believed in the name of Jesus, so Jesus may entrust himself to them.

**5.** Therefore attend, my brothers, to what this man answers who came to Jesus by night. Although he came to Jesus, yet because he came by night, he still speaks from the darkness of his flesh. He does not understand what he hears from the Lord, he does not understand what he hears from the light, *which enlightens every man coming into this world*. The Lord had already said to him: *Unless one is born anew, he will not see the kingdom of God. Nicodemus says to him: How can a man be born when he is old?*[^20] The Spirit speaks to him, and he has a taste for the flesh. He has a taste for his own flesh, because he does not yet have a taste for the flesh of Christ.

For when the Lord Jesus had said: *Unless one eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, he will not have life in him*; certain of those who followed him were scandalized, and said within themselves: *This is a hard saying; who can hear it?*[^21] For they thought Jesus said this, that they could cook him cut up like a lamb, and eat him: shrinking from his words, they withdrew, and followed him no more. So the Evangelist says: *And the Lord himself remained with the twelve: and they to him: Lord, behold those men have left you; and he: Do you too wish to go away?*[^22] Wishing to show that he was necessary to them, not that they were necessary to Christ. Let no one frighten Christ, when he is told to be a Christian; as if Christ will be more blessed if you are a Christian. It is good for you to be a Christian: for if you are not, it will be no evil to Christ. Hear the voice of the Psalm: *I said to the Lord, You are my God, for you have no need of my goods*[^23]. Therefore *you are my God, for you have no need of my goods*.

If you are without God, you will be the less; if you are with God, God will not be the greater. Not from you is he the greater, but you without him are the less. Grow, then, in him, do not withdraw yourself, as though he would thereby fail. You will be restored, if you draw near; you will fail, if you withdraw. He remains whole when you draw near, he remains whole even when you fall. When, then, he had said to the disciples: *Do you too wish to go away?* Peter, that rock, answered with the voice of all: *Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life*[^24]. In his mouth the flesh of the Lord had a good savor. But the Lord expounded it to them, and said: *It is the Spirit that gives life*: when he had said: *Unless one eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, he will not have life in him;* lest they understand it carnally: *It is the Spirit*, he says, *that gives life, but the flesh profits nothing: the words that I have spoken to you are spirit and life*[^25].

## There are two births.

**6.** This spirit and this life that Nicodemus had no taste for, who had come to Jesus by night. Jesus said to him: *Unless one is born anew, he will not see the kingdom of God*. And he, having a taste for his own flesh, in whose mouth the flesh of Christ had as yet no savor: *How*, he says, *can a man, when he is old, be born again? Can he enter again into his mother's womb, and be born?*[^26] This man knew only one birth, from Adam and Eve; from God and the Church he did not yet know: he knew only those parents who beget unto death; he did not yet know those parents who beget unto life: he knew only those parents who beget those who will succeed them; he did not yet know those who, ever living, beget those who will abide.

Since, then, there are two births, he understood one. One is from the earth, the other from heaven; one is from the flesh, the other from the Spirit; one is from mortality, the other from eternity; one is from male and female, the other from God and the Church. But these two are each single; neither the one can be repeated, nor the other. Rightly did Nicodemus understand the birth of the flesh: so do you too understand the birth of the spirit, as Nicodemus understood the birth of the flesh. What did Nicodemus understand? *Can a man enter again into his mother's womb, and be born?* So, whoever tells you to be born again spiritually, answer what Nicodemus said: *Can a man enter again into his mother's womb, and be born?* I am already born of Adam, Adam cannot beget me again: I am already born of Christ, Christ cannot beget me again. As the womb cannot be repeated, so neither can Baptism.

**7.** He who is born of the Catholic Church is born, as it were, of Sarah, is born of the free woman: he who is born of heresy is born, as it were, of the maidservant, but from the seed of Abraham. Let your Charity notice how great a sacred sign this is. God testifies, and says: *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*[^27]. Were there not other patriarchs? was there not before these the holy Noah, who alone in the whole human race, with his whole house, deserved to be set free from the flood, in whom and in his sons the Church was figured? By the wood that bore them they escape the flood.

Then afterward the great men whom we know, whom Sacred Scripture commends, *Moses faithful in all his house*[^28]. And those three are named, as if they alone had deserved well of him: *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob: this is my name forever*[^29]. A great sacred sign! The Lord is able to open both our mouths and your hearts, so that we may speak as he has deigned to reveal, and you may grasp as is good for you.

Three fathers and one people: there the figure, here the reality. 8. These three, then, are patriarchs, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. Now you know that the sons of Jacob were twelve, and from them the people of Israel: because Jacob himself is Israel, and the people of Israel are twelve tribes, belonging to the twelve sons of Israel. Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, three fathers, and one people. Three fathers, as it were at the beginning of the people; three fathers in whom the people was figured: and the people itself, the earlier, the present people. For in the people of the Jews the people of the Christians was figured. There the figure, here the reality; there the shadow, here the body, as the Apostle says: *But these things happened to them in a figure*. It is the Apostle's word: *They were written*, he says, *for our sake, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^30].

Let your mind now run back to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. In these three we find free women bearing, and maidservants too bearing: we find there the offspring of the free women, we find there too the offspring of the maidservants. The maidservant signifies nothing good: *Cast out the maidservant*, he says, *and her son; for the son of the maidservant will not be heir with the son of the free woman*[^31]. The Apostle recalls this; and in those two sons of Abraham, the Apostle says there was a figure of the two Testaments, the Old and the New[^32]. To the Old Testament belong the lovers of temporal things, the lovers of this world: to the New Testament, the lovers of eternal life. Therefore that Jerusalem on earth was a shadow of the heavenly Jerusalem, the mother of us all, which is in heaven: and these are the Apostle's words[^33]. And about that city from which we are pilgrims, you know much, you have already heard much.

But we find a marvelous thing in these births, that is, in these offspring, in these generations of free women and maidservants, namely four kinds of human beings: in which four kinds is completed the figure of the future Christian people, so that it is no wonder that of those three it was said: *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*. For among all Christians, brothers, attend: either through the bad the good are born, or through the good the bad are born, or through the good the good, or through the bad the bad: beyond these four kinds you cannot find any. These I will repeat again, notice, retain; shake out your hearts, do not be sluggish: grasp, lest you be caught, how there are four kinds of all Christians. Either through the good the good are born, or through the bad the bad are born, or through the good the bad, or through the bad the good. I think it is plain.

Through the good, the good: if both those who baptize are good, and those who are baptized believe rightly, then they are rightly numbered among the members of Christ. Through the bad, the bad: if both those who baptize are bad, and those who are baptized come to God with a double heart, and do not keep those ways of life which they hear in the Church, so that they are there not wheat but chaff. For how many such there are, your Charity knows. Through the bad, the good: sometimes an adulterer baptizes, and the one who is baptized is justified. Through the good, the bad: sometimes those who baptize are holy; those who are baptized refuse to keep the way of God.

**9.** I think, brothers, it is known in the Church, and is made manifest by daily examples, the things we say: but let us consider them in our earlier fathers, because they too had these four kinds. Through the good, the good: Ananias baptized Paul[^34]. What of through the bad, the bad? The Apostle speaks of certain preachers of the Gospel, whom he says are accustomed to announce the Gospel not chastely, whom he bears with in Christian fellowship, and says: *For what? as long as in every way, whether by occasion or in truth, Christ is announced, and in this I rejoice*[^35]. Was he malevolent, and did he rejoice over another's evil? But because even through the bad the truth was preached, and through the mouths of the bad Christ was preached; if these men baptized any like themselves, the bad were baptizing the bad: if these men baptized any such as the Lord admonishes when he says: *Do what they say; but what they do, do not do*[^36], the bad were baptizing the good. The good were baptizing the bad, as Simon Magus was baptized by the holy Philip.

These four kinds, then, are known, my brothers. Behold, again I repeat them, hold them, count them, notice them; beware of those that are evil, hold to those that are good. Through the good the good are born, when through the holy the holy are baptized: through the bad the bad, when both those who baptize and those who are baptized live wickedly and impiously: through the bad the good, when those who baptize are bad, and those who are baptized are good: through the good the bad, when those who baptize are good, and those who are baptized are bad. Ishmael and Isaac, the party of Donatus and the Church.

**10.** How do we find these things in those three names: *I am the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*? We take the maidservants for the bad, we take the free women for the good: the free women bear the good; Sarah bore Isaac: the maidservants bear the bad; Hagar bore Ishmael. We have in the one Abraham both that kind where through the good the good, and that kind where through the bad the bad. Through the good the bad—where are they figured? Rebecca the wife of Isaac was a free woman: read, she bore twins, one was good, the other bad. You have Scripture saying openly, with the voice of God: *Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated*[^37]. These two Rebecca bore, Jacob and Esau: one of them is chosen, the other rejected; one succeeds to the inheritance, the other is disinherited. God does not make his people from Esau; but he makes it from Jacob.

One seed, but diverse those who were conceived: one womb, but diverse those who were born. Did not the free woman bear Jacob, who as a free woman bore Esau? They struggled in their mother's womb, and it was said to Rebecca, while they struggled there: *Two peoples are in your womb*[^38]. Two men, two peoples; a good people, a bad people: but nevertheless in one womb they struggle. How many bad there are in the Church, and one womb carries them, until at the end they are distinguished: and the good cry out against the bad, and the bad cry back against the good, and both struggle in the bowels of one. Will they always be together? At the end one comes forth to the light, the birth is declared which here is figured in a sacred sign: and then it will appear: *Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated*.

**11.** Now then we have found, brothers, both from the good the good, from the free woman Isaac: and from the bad the bad, from the maidservant Ishmael: and from the good the bad, Esau from Rebecca. From the bad the good—where shall we find them? Jacob remains, that in the three patriarchs the completion of these four kinds may be concluded. Jacob had free women as wives, he had maidservants too: the free women bear, the maidservants too bear, and there come to be twelve sons of Israel. If you count all of whom they were born, not all are from the free women, not all from the maidservants: but nevertheless all from one seed. What, then, my brothers? did those who were born of the maidservants not possess the land of promise together with their brothers? We find there good sons of Jacob born of maidservants, and good sons of Jacob born of free women. Their birth from the wombs of maidservants did them no harm, since in their father they acknowledged their seed, and consequently held the kingdom with their brothers.

As, then, in the sons of Jacob it did not harm those who were born of maidservants, so as to keep them from holding the kingdom, and receiving the land of promise equally with their brothers; the birth from maidservants did not harm them, but the paternal seed prevailed: so whoever are baptized through the bad seem, as it were, born of maidservants; but nevertheless, because they are from the seed of the word of God, which is figured in Jacob, let them not be saddened, they will possess the inheritance together with their brothers.

Let him, then, be secure who is born of good seed; only let him not imitate the maidservant, if he is born of a maidservant. Do not imitate the bad maidservant in her pride. For why did the sons of Jacob, born of maidservants, possess the land of promise with their brothers, while Ishmael, born of a maidservant, was driven out of the inheritance? Why, but because he was proud, and they were humble? He raised his neck, and wished to seduce his brother, playing with him.

**12.** A great sacred sign is there. Ishmael and Isaac were playing together; Sarah saw them playing, and said to Abraham: *Cast out the maidservant and her son; for the son of the maidservant will not be heir with my son Isaac*[^39]. And when Abraham was saddened, the Lord confirmed for him the word of his wife. Here now the sacred sign is manifest, because that deed was bringing to birth some future thing. She saw them playing, and says: *Cast out the maidservant and her son*. What is this, brothers? For what evil had Ishmael done to the boy Isaac, that he was playing with him? But that playing was a mockery; that playing signified a deception. For let your Charity attend to a great sacred sign. The Apostle calls that a persecution; that very playing, that very sport, he calls a persecution: for he says: *But as then he who was born according to the flesh persecuted him who was born according to the spirit, so also now*[^40]; that is, those born according to the flesh persecute those born according to the spirit.

Who are those born according to the flesh? The lovers of the world, the lovers of this age. Who are those born according to the spirit? The lovers of the kingdom of heaven, the lovers of Christ, those desiring eternal life, worshiping God freely. They play, and the Apostle calls it persecution. For after the Apostle said these words: *And as then he who was born according to the flesh persecuted him who was born according to the spirit, so also now*; he went on, and showed of what persecution he was speaking: *But what does the Scripture say? Cast out the maidservant and her son; for the son of the maidservant will not be heir with my son Isaac*[^41]. We ask where Scripture says this, that we may see whether any persecution of Ishmael against Isaac went before: and we find that this was said by Sarah, when she saw the boys playing together. That playing which Scripture says Sarah saw, this the Apostle calls persecution.

More, then, do those persecute you who, by mocking you, seduce you: Come, come; be baptized here, here you have the true baptism. Do not play; there is one true baptism; that other is a sport: you will be seduced, and that persecution will be grievous to you. It would be better for you to win Ishmael to the kingdom; but Ishmael is unwilling, because he wishes to play. Hold you the inheritance of the father, and hear: *Cast out the maidservant and her son; for the son of the maidservant will not be heir with my son Isaac*.

**13.** These men even dare to say that they are accustomed to suffer persecution from Catholic kings, or from Catholic princes. What persecution do they endure? Affliction of the body: whether, however, they have ever suffered it, or how they have suffered it, let them know themselves, and consult their own consciences; yet they have suffered affliction of the body: the persecution which they inflict is more grievous. Beware when Ishmael wishes to play with Isaac, when he flatters you, when he offers another baptism: answer: I have Baptism already. For if this baptism is true, he who wishes to give you another wishes to mock you. Beware the persecutor of the soul. For if the party of Donatus has ever suffered anything from Catholic princes, it has suffered according to the body, not according to the mockery of the spirit.

Hear and see in those very ancient deeds all the signs and tokens of things to come. We find that Sarah afflicted the maidservant Hagar: Sarah is free: after the maidservant began to be proud, Sarah complained to Abraham, and said: Cast out the maidservant; she has raised her neck against me. And as though it were done by Abraham, the woman complained of Abraham. But Abraham, who was held by the maidservant not by the lust of using her, but by the office of begetting, since Sarah had given her to him that he might take up offspring by her, said to her: *Behold your maidservant, use her as you wish*[^42]. And Sarah afflicted her grievously, and she fled from her face.

Behold, the free woman afflicted the maidservant, and the Apostle does not call that persecution; the servant plays with the master, and he calls it persecution: that affliction is not called persecution, and that playing is called persecution. What seems to you, brothers? Do you not understand what is signified? So, then, when God wishes to stir up the powers against heretics, against schismatics, against the scatterers of the Church, against those who blow Christ away, against the blasphemers of Baptism, let them not wonder; because God stirs it up, that Hagar may be beaten by Sarah. Let Hagar know herself, let her lay down her neck: because when, humbled, she departed from her mistress, an angel met her, and said: *What is it, Hagar, maidservant of Sarah?* when she had complained of her mistress, what did she hear from the angel? *Return to your mistress*[^43]. To this end, then, she is afflicted, that she may return. And would that she may return; because her offspring, like the sons of Jacob, will hold the inheritance with their brothers.

**14.** But they wonder that the Christian powers are moved against the detestable scatterers of the Church. Should they then not be moved? And how would they render to God an account of their rule? Let your Charity attend to what I say, because this pertains to the Christian kings of this world, that in their times they should wish their mother the Church, from whom they were spiritually born, to be at peace.

We read the visions of Daniel and the prophetic deeds. Three boys in the fire praised the Lord: King Nebuchadnezzar marveled at the boys praising God, and at the fire around them being harmless; and when he had marveled, what did King Nebuchadnezzar say—he, neither a Jew nor circumcised, he who had set up his statue and compelled all to adore it; yet, moved by the praises of the three boys, when he saw the majesty of God present in the fire, what did he say? *And I issue a decree to all tribes and tongues in all the earth*. What decree? *Whoever speaks blasphemy against the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, will be destroyed, and their house unto perdition*[^44].

Behold how a foreign king rages, lest the God of Israel be blasphemed, because he was able to free three boys from the fire: and they are unwilling that Christian kings should be roused, because Christ is blown away, by whom not three boys, but the whole world together with the kings themselves is freed from the fire of hell? For those three boys, my brothers, were freed from a temporal fire. Is it not the same God of the Maccabees as of the three boys? Those he freed from the fire: these failed in body amid fiery torments, but in soul they remained in the lawful commandments. Those were openly freed, these secretly crowned. It is more to be freed from the flame of hell than from the furnace of a human power.

If, then, King Nebuchadnezzar praised and proclaimed and gave glory to God, because he freed three boys from the fire, and gave such great glory that he sent a decree throughout his kingdom: *Whoever speaks blasphemy against the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego, will be destroyed, and their house unto perdition*: how should these kings not be moved, who attend not to three boys freed from the flame, but to themselves freed from hell, when they see Christ, by whom they were freed, blown away among Christians, when they hear it said to a Christian: Say that you are not a Christian? Such things they wish to do, and yet they are unwilling even to suffer such things.

**15.** For see what kind of things they do, and what kind of things they suffer. They kill souls, they are afflicted in body: they cause everlasting deaths, and they complain of suffering temporal ones. And yet what do they suffer? They bring forth to us some martyrs or other of theirs in the persecution. Behold, Marculus was thrown down from a rock: behold, Donatus of Bagai was thrown into a well. When did the Roman powers decree such punishments, that men should be thrown down? But what do our people answer? What was done I do not know: yet what do our people hand down? That these men threw themselves down, and slandered the powers.

Let us recall the custom of the Roman powers, and see whom we should believe. Our people say that those men threw themselves down: if they are not the disciples of those very men who now throw themselves from rocks with no one persecuting them, let us not believe it: what wonder if those men did what they are accustomed to do? For the Roman powers never used such punishments. For could they not kill openly? But those who wished themselves to be honored as dead did not find a more notorious death. In short, whatever it is, I do not know it. And if you have suffered, O party of Donatus, bodily affliction from the Catholic Church, you, Hagar, have suffered from Sarah; *return to your mistress*.

This passage, being a necessary one, has held us somewhat longer, so that we were quite unable to expound the whole text of the Gospel reading. Brothers, let this for now suffice your Charity, lest these things that have been said be shut out of your hearts by the saying of others. Hold these things, say such things, go forth blazing, kindle the cold.
