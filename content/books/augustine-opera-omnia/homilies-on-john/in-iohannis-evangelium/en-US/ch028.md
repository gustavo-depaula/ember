# Homily 28

## He went up to the feast, not openly, but as it were in secret (Jn 7:1-14).

Since his members were to be—that is, his faithful, who would not have that power which our God himself had—the fact that he lay hidden, that he concealed himself as it were lest he be killed, this indicated what his members would do, among whom members he himself surely was. For Christ is not in the head and not in the body, but Christ whole is in the head and in the body.

**1.** In this chapter of the Gospel, brothers, our Lord Jesus Christ commended himself greatly to our faith according to his humanity. For he always does this in his words and deeds, that he may be believed God and man: God who made us, man who sought us; God always with the Father, man with us in time. For he would not seek the one he had made, unless he himself became what he had made. But remember this, and do not let it go from your hearts, that Christ was so made man as not to cease to be God. Remaining God, he took up a man, he who made man.

When, therefore, he lay hidden as a man, he is not to be thought to have lost his power, but to have offered an example to our weakness. For he was detained when he willed, killed when he willed. But since his members were to be—that is, his faithful, who would not have that power which our God himself had—the fact that he lay hidden, that he concealed himself as it were lest he be killed, this indicated what his members would do, among whom members he himself surely was. For Christ is not in the head and not in the body, but Christ whole is in the head and in the body. Therefore what his members are, he is: but what he is, his members are not at once. For if they were not himself his members, he would not say: *Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^1] For Saul was not persecuting him, but his members, that is, his faithful, on earth. Yet he was unwilling to say, my saints, my servants; finally, more honorably, my brothers: but: *Me*, that is, my members, of whom I am the head.

## He was consoling our frailty.

**2.** These things having been said beforehand, I think we will not have to labor over this chapter that has just been read: for often what was to be in the body was signified in the head. *After these things*, he says, *Jesus walked in Galilee: for he did not wish to walk in Judea, because the Jews were seeking to kill him*[^2]. This is what I said; he was offering an example to our weakness. He himself had not lost power, but was consoling our frailty. For it was to be, as I said, that some faithful one of his would hide himself, lest he be found by persecutors; and lest hiding be cast at him as a crime, there went before in the head what would be confirmed in the member.

For it was said thus: He did not wish to walk in Judea, because the Jews were seeking to kill him: as though Christ could not both walk among the Jews, and not be killed by the Jews. For this power he showed when he willed: for when, now about to suffer, they wished to seize him, he said to them: *Whom do you seek?* They answered: *Jesus*. And he: *I am he*; not concealing himself, but manifesting himself. Yet at that manifestation they did not stand, but, going back, they fell[^3]. And yet, because he had come to suffer, they rose, seized him, led him to the judge, and killed him. But what did they do? What a certain Scripture says: *The earth has been handed over into the hands of the wicked*[^4]: the flesh was given into the power of the Jews. And this for this reason, that the little bag might as it were be cut open, from which our price would flow.

**3.** *Now the Jews' feast of Tabernacles was at hand*[^5]. What "Scenopegia" is, those who have read the Scriptures know. On the feast day they made tabernacles, in likeness of the tabernacles in which they had dwelt when, led out of Egypt, they journeyed in the desert. This was the feast day, a great solemnity. The Jews celebrated this, as it were remembering the benefits of the Lord, they who were going to kill the Lord.

On this feast day, then (because there were several feast days; for among the Jews a feast day was so called that it was not one day, but several), his brothers spoke to the Lord Christ. Understand his brothers as you know: for it is not new what you hear. The blood relations of the Virgin Mary were called the brothers of the Lord. For it was a custom of the Scriptures to call brothers any blood relations and near of kin, even beyond our usage, not in the way we speak. For who would call an uncle and a sister's son brothers? Yet Scripture calls relationships of this kind brothers too. For Abraham and Lot were called brothers, when Abraham was Lot's uncle[^6]: and Laban and Jacob were called brothers, when Laban was Jacob's uncle[^7].

When, therefore, you hear the brothers of the Lord, think of Mary's blood kinship, not of any offspring of one bearing again. For just as in the tomb where the body of the Lord was placed, no dead man lay either before or after; so the womb of Mary conceived nothing mortal either before or after.

**4.** We have said who the brothers were; let us hear what they said. *Pass from here, and go into Judea, that your disciples too may see your works which you do.* The works of the Lord were not hidden from the disciples, but were hidden from these men. For these brothers, that is, blood relations, were able to have Christ as a blood relation, but out of that very nearness they disdained to believe in him. It was said in the Gospel: for we do not dare to suppose this, you have just heard it. They add, and urge: *For no one does anything in secret, and himself seeks to be in the open: if you do these things, manifest yourself to the world.* And immediately: *For not even his brothers believed in him*[^8]. Why did they not believe in him? Because they sought human glory. For in that they seem to urge him as brothers, they are taking thought for his glory: You do marvelous things, become known; that is, appear to all, that you may be praised by all. Flesh was speaking to flesh: but flesh without God, to flesh with God. For the prudence of the flesh was speaking to the Word that was made flesh and dwelt among us[^9].

## The homeland is lofty, the way is humble.

**5.** What does the Lord say to this? *Jesus, therefore, says to them: My time has not yet come, but your time is always ready*[^10]. What is this? Had Christ's time not yet come? Why then had Christ come, if his time had not yet come? Did we not hear the Apostle saying: *But when the fullness of time came, God sent his Son*[^11]? If, then, he was sent in the fullness of time; he was sent when he ought, he came when it was fitting: what is, *My time has not yet come*? Understand, brothers, with what mind those men were speaking, who seemed as it were to be urging their brother. They were giving him counsel for attaining glory, as it were urging him worldly and with earthly affection, that he should not be ignoble and lie hidden: so what the Lord says, *My time has not yet come*, he answered to those who were giving him counsel about glory: The time of my glory has not yet come.

See how deep it is: they were advising him about glory, but he wished to precede loftiness by humility, and to that very height to lay a road through humility. For those disciples too were surely seeking glory, who wished, one to sit at his right hand, and the other at his left: they attended to where, and did not see by what way; the Lord called them back to the way, that they might come to the homeland in order. For the homeland is lofty, the way is humble. The homeland is the life of Christ, the way is the death of Christ: the homeland is the dwelling of Christ, the way is the passion of Christ. He who refuses the way, why does he seek the homeland? In short, to them too, seeking the height, he answered this: *Can you drink the cup that I am about to drink?*[^12] See by what way one comes to the height you desire. For he was recalling the cup of humility and of the passion.

**6.** Therefore here too: *My time has not yet come*; but your time, that is, the glory of the world, is always ready. This is the time of which Christ speaks in prophecy, that is, the body of Christ: *When I receive the time, I will judge righteously*[^13]. For now is not the time of judging, but of bearing with the wicked. Let the body of Christ, then, now bear, and tolerate the iniquity of those who live badly. Yet let it have righteousness now, before it has judgment: for through righteousness it will come to judgment. For while the members tolerate the iniquity of this world, what does holy Scripture say in the Psalm? *The Lord will not cast off his people.* For his people labors among the unworthy, among the wicked, among blasphemers, among murmurers, detractors, harassers, and, if it be permitted, slayers. It labors indeed; but *the Lord will not cast off his people, and will not abandon his inheritance, until righteousness is turned into judgment*[^14]. Until the righteousness which now is in his saints is turned into judgment; when that will be fulfilled which was said to them: *You will sit upon twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^15]. The Apostle had righteousness, but not yet that judgment of which he says: *Do you not know that we will judge angels?*[^16]

Let it now, then, be the time of living justly, afterward it will be the time of judging those who have lived badly. *Until righteousness*, he says, *is turned into judgment.* This will be the time of judgment, of which the Lord just now said: *My time has not yet come.* For it will be the time of glory, that he who came in humility may come in loftiness. He who came to be judged will come to judge: he who came to be killed by the dead will come to judge the living and the dead. *God*, says the Psalm, *will come manifest, our God, and will not keep silent*[^17]. What is, *will come manifest*? Because he came hidden. Then he will not keep silent: for when he came hidden, *he was led like a sheep to the slaughter, and like a lamb before its shearer he did not open his mouth*[^18]. He will come and will not keep silent. *I have kept silent*, he says, *shall I always keep silent?*[^19]

When our time will come.

**7.** But now what is necessary for those who have righteousness? What is read in that very psalm: *Until righteousness is turned into judgment; and all who have it are upright in heart*[^20]. You ask perhaps who are the upright in heart? We find those upright in heart in Scripture who tolerate the evils of the world, and do not accuse God. See, brothers; this is a rare bird of which I speak. For somehow, when something evil happens to a man, he runs to accuse God, who ought to accuse himself. When you do something good, you praise yourself: when you suffer something evil, you accuse God. This, then, is a crooked heart, not upright. If you are corrected from this distortion and crookedness, what you used to do will be turned into the contrary. For before, what did you do? You praised yourself in God's goods, you accused God in your evils: with the heart converted and straightened, you will praise God in his goods, you will accuse yourself in your evils. These are the upright in heart.

In short, that man not yet upright in heart, to whom the happiness of the wicked and the labor of the good was displeasing, says when corrected: *How good is the God of Israel to the upright in heart! But my feet, when I was not upright in heart, were almost moved, my steps were a little short of being poured out.* Why? *Because I was zealous over sinners, beholding the peace of sinners*[^21]. I saw, he says, the wicked happy, and God was displeasing to me; for this is what I wished, that God would not permit the wicked to be happy. Let a man understand: God never permits this; but the wicked man is thought happy for this reason, because what happiness is, is not known.

Let us, then, be upright in heart: the time of our glory has not yet come. Let it be said to the lovers of this world, such as the Lord's brothers were: Your time is always ready; our time has not yet come. For let us too dare to say this. And since we are the body of our Lord Jesus Christ, since we are his members, since we gratefully recognize our head, let us say it outright; since for our sake he too deigned to say this. When the lovers of this world insult us, let us say to them: Your time is always ready; our time has not yet come. For the Apostle said to us: *For you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God.* When will our time come? *When Christ*, he says, *your life, appears, then you too will appear with him in glory*[^22].

**8.** What then does he add? *The world cannot hate you.* What is this, except: The world cannot hate its own lovers, the false witnesses? For you call good the things that are evil, and evil the things that are good. *But me it hates, because I bear witness about it, that its works are evil. Go up to this feast.* What is, *this*? Where you seek human glory. What is, *this*? Where you wish to prolong carnal joys, not to think of eternal things. *I do not go up to this feast, because my time is not yet fulfilled*[^23]. At this feast you seek human glory; but my time, that is, of my glory, has not yet come. That will be my feast day, not running past and passing away with these days, but abiding forever: that will be the festivity, joy without end, eternity without blemish, serenity without cloud.

*When he had said these things, he himself remained in Galilee. But when his brothers had gone up, then he too went up to the feast; not openly, but as it were in secret*[^24]. Therefore not to this feast, because he desired not to glory temporally, but to teach something wholesomely, to correct men, to admonish them about the eternal feast, to turn love away from this world, and to convert it to God. But what is it that as it were secretly he went up to the feast? This too of the Lord's is not idle. It seems to me, brothers, that even from this—that he went up as it were secretly—he wished to signify something: for what follows will show that he went up so when the feast was already half over, that is, when those days were already half spent, that he might even teach openly. But it said, as it were secretly, lest he show himself to men. It is not idle that Christ went up secretly to the feast, because he himself lay hidden in that feast. Even now what I have said is in a hiding place. Let it be made manifest, then, let the veil be taken away, and let what was secret appear.

## The Feast of Tabernacles was a shadow of things to come.

**9.** All the things that were said to the ancient people of Israel in the manifold writing of the holy Law—what they should do, whether in sacrifices, or in priesthoods, or on feast days, and in general in whatever matters by which they worshiped God—whatever was said and commanded to them, were shadows of things to come.

Of what things to come?

Things that are fulfilled in Christ. Whence the Apostle says: *For however many the promises of God, in him*[^25]: that is, in him they are fulfilled. Then he says in another place: *All these things happened to them in figure; but they were written for our sake, upon whom the end of the ages has come*[^26]. He said elsewhere too: *For the end of the Law is Christ*[^27]. Likewise in another place: *Let no one judge you in food, or in drink, or in respect of a feast day, or of a new moon, or of sabbaths, which are a shadow of things to come*[^28].

If, then, all those things were shadows of things to come; the Feast of Tabernacles too was a shadow of things to come. Let us inquire, then, of what thing to come this feast day was a shadow. I have expounded what the Feast of Tabernacles was: it was a celebration of tabernacles, for this reason, because the people, freed from Egypt and journeying through the desert to the land of promise, dwelt in tabernacles. Let us take note of what it is, and we will be it; we, I say, who are the members of Christ, if we are: but by his deigning we are, not by our deserving. Let us attend, then, brothers: we have been led out of Egypt, where we served the devil as Pharaoh: where we did muddy works in earthly desires, and labored much in them. For to us, making as it were bricks, Christ cried out: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened*[^29].

Led out from here through Baptism as through the Red Sea—red for this reason, because consecrated by the blood of Christ—with all our enemies who pursued us dead, that is, with all our sins blotted out, we were brought across. Now, then, before we come to the homeland of promise, that is, the eternal kingdom, we are in the desert in tabernacles. Those who recognize these things are in tabernacles: for it was to be that some would recognize this. For he is in tabernacles who understands himself to be a pilgrim in the world. He understands himself to be journeying who sees himself sighing for the homeland.

But when the body of Christ is in tabernacles, Christ is in tabernacles. But then not evidently, but in secret. For the shadow still obscured the light: with the light coming, the shadow was removed. Christ was in secret, in the Feast of Tabernacles Christ was, but Christ hidden. Now, when these things have been made manifest, we recognize that we are making a journey in the wilderness: for if we recognize it, we are in the wilderness.

What is, in the wilderness?

In the desert.

Why in the desert?

Because in this world, where one thirsts on a waterless road. But let us thirst, that we may be satisfied. *For blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be satisfied*[^30]. And our thirst is filled from the rock in the wilderness: *for the rock was Christ*[^31], and the rod struck it, that water might flow. But that it might flow, it was struck twice[^32], because there are two woods of the cross.

All these things, then, which happened in figure, are made manifest in us. And it is not idle what was said of the Lord: *He went up to the feast, not openly, but as it were in secret.* For he himself was figured in secret, because in that very feast day Christ lay hidden; because that feast day signified the members of Christ that were to journey as pilgrims.

## Think of the body of Christ still in the world.

**10.** The Jews, therefore, sought him on the feast day[^33], before he went up. For his brothers went up first, and he did not go up then, when they thought and wished: that this too might be fulfilled which he said: Not to this one, that is, to the one you wish, the first or second day. But he went up afterward, as the Gospel says, when the feast was half over[^34], that is, when already so many days of that feast had passed as had remained. For the festivity itself, as far as is to be understood, they celebrated for several days.

**11.** They said, therefore: *Where is he? And there was much murmuring about him in the crowd*[^35].

From where the murmuring?

On the dispute.

What was the dispute?

For some were saying, *He is a good man*; others, however, *No, but he is leading the crowds astray*[^36]. This is to be understood of all his servants: it is said in this way. For whenever anyone has stood out in some spiritual grace, surely some say, *He is good*; others, *No, but he is leading the crowds astray*.

Why is this?

Because *our life is hidden with Christ in God*[^37].

And so one may say of people in winter: That tree is dead—a fig tree, for example, or a pear tree, or a tree of fruits of this kind—it is like one that has dried up; and as long as it is winter, this does not show. Summer proves it, judgment proves it. Our summer is the revealing of Christ: *God will come manifestly, our God, and he will not keep silence*; *fire will go before him*[^38]: this fire will set his enemies ablaze; the fire will lay hold of the dried-up trees. For then they will appear dried up, when it will be said to them, *I was hungry, and you gave me no food*[^39]; but on the other side, that is, on the right, the fruitfulness of fruits will appear, and the worthiness of leaves; there will be greenness, there will be eternity. To those, then, as to dried-up trees, it will be said, *Go into the eternal fire*[^40]. For behold, he says, *the axe is laid to the root of the trees. Every tree therefore that does not bear good fruit will be cut down and thrown into the fire*[^41].

Let them say of you, then, if you are making progress in Christ, let people say, *He is leading the crowds astray*. This is said of him, of the whole body of Christ. Think of the body of Christ as still in the world, think of the body of Christ as still on the threshing floor; see how it is blasphemed by the chaff. They are indeed threshed together; but the chaff is crushed, the grain is cleansed. What was said, then, of the Lord avails for the consolation of any Christian whatever of whom this may be said.

**12.** *Yet no one spoke openly of him, for fear of the Jews*[^42]. But who were they who did not speak of him for fear of the Jews? Surely those who were saying, *He is good*—not those who were saying, *He is leading the crowds astray*. Those who said, *He is leading the crowds astray*, made a sound that was heard like dried-up leaves. *He is leading the crowds astray* rang out more clearly; *He is good* was whispered more softly. But now, brothers, although that glory of Christ which is to make us eternal has not yet come, yet now his Church so grows, he has so deigned to spread it through all places, that already it is whispered, *He is leading the crowds astray*, and rings out more clearly, *He is good*.
