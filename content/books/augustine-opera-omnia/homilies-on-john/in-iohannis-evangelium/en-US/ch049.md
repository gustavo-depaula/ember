# Tractate 49

## *I am the resurrection and the life*[^1].

It is more to create men than to raise them. Yet the Lord deigned both to create and to raise: to create all, to raise some. It suffices you to know that, if he willed, he would raise all the dead. And this indeed he reserved for himself to the end of the age.  
It is more to create men than to raise them.

**1.** Among all the miracles that our Lord Jesus Christ did, the resurrection of Lazarus is especially proclaimed. But if we attend to who did it, we ought to be delighted rather than to marvel. He raised a man, who made man; for he himself is the Only Son of the Father, through whom, as you know, all things were made. If, then, all things were made through him, what wonder is it if one rose again through him, when so many are born every day through him? It is more to create men than to raise them. Yet he deigned both to create and to raise; to create all, to raise some. For although the Lord Jesus did many things, not all were written down; as the same holy evangelist John testifies that the Lord Christ both said and did many things which are not written: but those were chosen to be written which seemed to suffice for the salvation of believers. For you have heard that the Lord Jesus raised the dead man: it suffices you to know that, if he willed, he would raise all the dead. And this indeed he reserved for himself to the end of the age. For he whom you have heard, by a great miracle, raised four days dead from the tomb, *the hour will come,* as he himself says, *when all who are in the tombs will hear* his *voice and will come forth.* He raised one already stinking, but yet in the stinking corpse the form of the members still remained; he, on the last day, at one voice, will restore ashes to flesh. But it was necessary that he do certain things now, by which, given as it were as tokens of his power, we might believe in him, and be prepared for that resurrection which will be unto life, not unto judgment. For so he says: *The hour will come when all who are in the tombs will hear* his *voice: and those who have done good will come forth, to the resurrection of life; those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment.*

## The Lord's deeds are signs.

**2.** Yet we read in the Gospel of three dead persons raised by the Lord, and perhaps not in vain. For the Lord's deeds are not only deeds, but signs. If, then, they are signs, besides being marvels, they surely signify something: and to find the signification of these deeds is somewhat more laborious than to read or hear them.

We were hearing with wonder, as though a spectacle of a great miracle were set before our eyes, when the Gospel was read, how Lazarus came back to life. If we attend to the more wonderful works of Christ, everyone who believes rises again: if we attend to all, and understand the more detestable deaths, everyone who sins dies. But every man fears the death of the flesh, few the death of the soul. For the death of the flesh, which will without doubt come at some time, all take care that it not come: thence it is that they labor. A man labors that he may not die, who is going to die, and does not labor that he may not sin, who is going to live forever. And when he labors that he may not die, he labors without cause; for he brings it about that death be much deferred, not that it be escaped: but if he should be unwilling to sin, he will not labor, and will live forever. Oh, if only we could rouse men, and together with them be roused, to be such lovers of the abiding life as men are lovers of the fleeting life!

What does a man not do when placed under peril of death? With the sword hanging over their necks, men have given up whatever they were keeping for themselves to live on. Who has not at once given it up, that he might not be struck? And after the betrayal perhaps he was struck. Who has not, that he might live, at once been willing to lose that whereby he lived, choosing a beggar's life rather than a swift death? Who, when it was said to him: Set sail, lest you die; deferred it? Who, when it was said to him: Labor, lest you die; was lazy?

God commands light things, that we may live forever; and we neglect to obey. God does not say to you: Lose whatever you have, that you may live for a brief time anxious in labor; but: Give to the poor of what you have, that you may live always secure without labor. The lovers of temporal life accuse us, which they have neither when they will, nor as long as they will: and we in turn do not accuse one another, so lazy, so lukewarm to lay hold of eternal life, which, if we will, we shall have; when we have it, we shall not lose; but this death which we fear, even if we are unwilling, we shall have.

## Three dead persons raised by Christ.

**3.** If, then, the Lord by his great grace and his great mercy raises souls, that we may not die forever; we rightly understand those three dead persons whom he raised in body to signify and figure something about the resurrections of souls which take place through faith: he raised the daughter of the ruler of the synagogue, still lying in the house; he raised the young man, the son of the widow, carried out beyond the gates of the city; he raised Lazarus, buried four days.

Let each one look upon his own soul: if it sins, it dies; sin is the death of the soul. But sometimes one sins in thought. What is evil has delighted, you have consented, you have sinned; that consent has killed you: but the death is within, because the evil thought has not yet gone forth into deed. Signifying that he raises such a soul, the Lord raised that girl who had not yet been carried out, but lay dead in the house, as the sin lay hidden. But if you have not only consented to the evil delight, but have also done the evil itself; you have carried out, as it were, a dead man beyond the gate: now you are outside, and have been carried out dead. Yet the Lord raised even this one, and restored him to his widowed mother. If you have sinned, repent: and the Lord raises you, and will restore you to your mother the Church. The third dead man is Lazarus. There is a monstrous kind of death, it is called evil habit. For it is one thing to sin, another to make a habit of sinning. He who sins and is at once corrected revives quickly: because he is not yet entangled in habit, he is not buried. But he who is accustomed to sin is buried, and it is well said of him, *he stinks*: for he begins to have a most evil reputation, as it were a most foul odor. Such are all those accustomed to crimes, lost in their ways. You say to him: Do not do it. When does he hear you whom the earth so presses, and is corrupted with rot, and is weighed down by the mass of habit? Yet even to raise him the power of Christ was not less. We have known, we have seen, we daily see, men who, their most evil habit changed, live better than those who reproached them lived. You used to detest a man: behold, that very sister of Lazarus (if indeed it is she who anointed the feet of the Lord with ointment, and wiped them with her hair which she had washed with her tears) was raised better than her brother: she was set free from a great mass of evil habit. For she was a notorious sinner: and of her it was said: *Many sins are forgiven her, because she loved much.* We see many, we know many: let no one despair, let no one presume of himself. Both to despair is evil, and to presume of oneself. Do not despair in such a way that you choose what you ought to presume on.

**4.** So the Lord raised Lazarus too. You have heard what kind, that is, what the resurrection of Lazarus signifies. Let us, then, now read; and since many things in this reading are plain, let us not seek an exposition of each, that we may treat the necessary ones.

*Now there was a certain man sick, Lazarus of Bethany, of the town of Mary and Martha his sisters.* In the earlier reading you remember that the Lord went out from the hands of those who had wished to stone him, and departed across the Jordan where John was baptizing. So with the Lord established there, Lazarus was sick in Bethany, which town was near to Jerusalem.

## It suffices that he know; for he does not love and desert.

**5.** *Now Mary was the one who anointed the Lord with ointment, and wiped his feet with her hair, whose brother Lazarus was sick. So his sisters sent to him, saying.* Now we understand where they sent, where the Lord was: since he was absent, namely across the Jordan. They sent to the Lord, announcing that their brother was sick: that, if he deigned, he might come and free him from his sickness. He put off healing, that he might be able to raise.

What, then, did his sisters report? *Lord, behold, he whom you love is sick.* They did not say: Come: for it was enough to announce it to one who loves. They did not dare to say: Come and heal: they did not dare to say: Command there, and it will be done here. For why not these women too, if the faith of that centurion is praised on that score? For he says: *I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof, but only say the word, and my servant will be healed.* None of these things did these women say, but only: *Lord, behold, he whom you love is sick.* It suffices that you know; for you do not love and desert.

Someone says: How was a sinner signified by Lazarus, and so loved by the Lord? Let him hear him saying: *I came not to call the righteous, but sinners.* For if God did not love sinners, he would not have come down from heaven to earth.

**6.** *But when Jesus heard it, he said to them: This sickness is not unto death, but for the glory of God, that the Son of God may be glorified through it.* Such glorification of him did not increase him, but profited us. So he says, *it is not unto death*, because that very death was not unto death; but rather unto a miracle, by which, once done, men would believe in Christ, and avoid the true death. Indeed, see how, as it were obliquely, the Lord called himself God, for the sake of certain ones who deny that the Son is God. For there are heretics who deny this, that the Son of God is God. Behold, let them hear: *This sickness,* he says, *is not unto death, but for the glory of God.* What glory? of what God? Hear what follows: *That the Son of God may be glorified. This sickness, then,* he says, *is not unto death, but for the glory of God, that the Son of God may be glorified through it.* Through what? Through that sickness.

**7.** *Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister Mary, and Lazarus.* He was sick, they were sad, all were beloved: but he loved them, the Savior of the sick, nay even the raiser of the dead, and the consoler of the sad. *So when he heard that he was sick, he then indeed remained in the same place two days.* So they announced it to him; he remained there: the time was drawn out so long, until the four days were completed. Not in vain, except that perhaps—nay, certainly—the very number of days too intimates some mystery.

*Then after this he says again to his disciples: Let us go into Judea again*: where he had almost been stoned, who for that reason seemed to have departed thence, lest he be stoned. For he departed as a man; but in returning, as though forgetting his weakness, he showed his power. *Let us go,* he says, *into Judea.*

**8.** Then, this said, see how the disciples were terrified. *The disciples say to him: Rabbi, the Jews were just now seeking to stone you, and do you go there again? Jesus answered: Are there not twelve hours of the day?* What does that answer mean? They said: *The Jews were just now wishing to stone you, and do you go there again,* that they may stone you? And the Lord: *Are there not twelve hours of the day? If anyone walks in the day, he does not stumble, because he sees the light of this world: but if anyone walks in the night, he stumbles, because the light is not in him.* He spoke indeed of the day, but in our understanding it is, as it were, still night. Let us invoke the day, that it may drive out the night, and enlighten the heart with light. For what did the Lord wish to say? As far as it seems to me, as far as the height and depth of the meaning glimmers through, he wished to refute their doubt and unbelief. For they wished to give counsel to the Lord that he not die, who had come to die, lest they themselves die.

So too in another place holy Peter, loving the Lord, but not yet fully understanding why he had come, feared lest he die, and displeased life, that is, the Lord himself: for when he indicated to the disciples that he was going to suffer at Jerusalem from the Jews, Peter answered among the rest, and said: *Far be it from you, Lord, be merciful to yourself, this shall not happen.* And at once the Lord: *Get behind me, Satan, for you do not savor the things of God, but the things of men.* And a little before, confessing the Son of God, he had merited praise: for he had heard: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, for flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven.* To him he had said: *Blessed are you*; to him he says: *Get behind me, Satan*: because he was not blessed of himself. But whence? *Because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven.* Behold whence blessed, not of your own, but of mine. Not because I am the Father, but because *all things that the Father has are mine.*

If blessed of the Lord himself; Satan of whom? There he says it: for he gave the reason of the blessedness, by saying: *Flesh and blood did not reveal* this *to you, but my Father who is in heaven*: this is the cause of your blessedness. But as to what I said: *Get behind me, Satan,* hear too the cause of this: *For you do not savor the things of God, but the things of man.* Let no one, then, flatter himself; of his own he is Satan, of God he is blessed. For what is of his own, except of his own sin? Take away the sin, which is yours? Righteousness, he says, is of mine. *For what do you have that you have not received?*

When, then, men wished to give counsel to God, disciples to the master, slaves to the Lord, the sick to the physician; he rebuked them, and said: *Are there not twelve hours of the day? If anyone walks in the day, he does not stumble.* Follow me, if you do not wish to stumble: do not give counsel to me, you who ought to receive counsel from me. To what, then, does it pertain: *Are there not twelve hours of the day?* Because, to show that he was the day, he chose twelve disciples. If I am, he says, the day, and you the hours, do the hours give counsel to the day? The hours follow the day, not the day the hours. If, then, they are the hours, what of Judas there? Was he too among the twelve hours? If he was an hour, he shone; if he shone, how did he betray the day to death? But the Lord in this word foresaw not Judas himself, but his successor. For Judas falling, Matthias succeeded, and the number of twelve remained. Not in vain, then, did the Lord choose twelve disciples, except that he himself is the spiritual day. Let the hours, then, follow the day, let the hours proclaim the day, let the hours be lit by the day, let the hours be enlightened by the day, and through the proclamation of the hours let the world believe in the day. This, then, he said in brief: Follow me, if you do not wish to stumble.

**9.** *And after this he says to them: Lazarus our friend sleeps; but I go, that I may wake him from sleep.* He spoke truly. To his sisters he was dead, to the Lord he was sleeping. To men he was dead, who could not raise him: for the Lord raised him from the tomb with as great ease as you do not wake a sleeper from his bed. So according to his power he called him sleeping: because others too who are dead are often called sleeping in the Scriptures, as the Apostle says: *But concerning those who sleep, I do not wish you to be ignorant, brothers, that you may not be saddened, as the rest also who have no hope.* Therefore he too called them sleeping, because he foretold that they would rise again.

Every dead person, then, sleeps, both the good and the evil. But just as, among those who daily sleep and rise, it makes a difference what each one sees in his sleep: some feel glad dreams, others tormenting ones, so that one waking fears to sleep, lest he return again to them: so each man sleeps with his own cause, rises with his own cause. And it makes a difference under what kind of custody each is received, to be afterward brought before the judge. For receptions into custody too are applied according to the merits of the cases: some are commanded to be guarded by lictors, a humane and mild and civil office; others are handed over to deputy officers; others are sent into prison: and in the prison itself not all, but according to the merits of graver cases, are thrust into the depths of the prison. As, then, there are diverse custodies of those acting under office; so there are diverse custodies of the dead, and diverse merits of those rising again. The poor man was received, the rich man was received: but the one into the bosom of Abraham; the other where he would thirst, and find not a drop.

## All together are going to receive what was promised.

**10.** All souls, then—that from this occasion I may instruct your Charity—all souls have, when they have gone out from the world, their diverse receptions. The good have joy, the evil torments. But when the resurrection has taken place, both the joy of the good will be greater, and the torments of the evil heavier; when they will be tormented with the body. Received into peace are the holy Patriarchs, the Prophets, the Apostles, the Martyrs, the good faithful; yet all, still, at the end, are going to receive what God promised: for there is promised the resurrection also of the flesh, the consuming of death, eternal life with the angels. This we are all going to receive together: for the rest which is given immediately after death, if one is worthy of it, each one then receives when he dies. The Patriarchs received it earlier: see how long they rest: the Prophets later, the Apostles more recently, the holy Martyrs much more recently, the good faithful daily. And some have been in this rest a long time now, others not so long, others fewer years, others not even for a recent time. But when they shall wake from this sleep, all together are going to receive what was promised.

**11.** *Lazarus our friend sleeps; but I go, that I may wake him from sleep. So the disciples said*: as they understood, so they answered: *Lord, if he sleeps, he will be safe.* For the sleep of the sick is wont to be a sign of health. *But Jesus had spoken of his death; they, however, thought that he spoke of the sleep of slumber. Then, therefore, Jesus said to them plainly.* For he had said somewhat obscurely, *he sleeps*: so he says plainly: *Lazarus is dead: and I rejoice for your sake, that you may believe; because I was not there.* Both I know that he is dead, and I was not there: for he had been announced as sick, not dead. But what could be hidden from him who had created him, and to whose hands the soul of the dying man had gone out? This is what he says: *I rejoice for your sake, that you may believe; because I was not there*: that they might now begin to wonder, because the Lord could say that he was dead, which he had neither seen nor heard.

Where indeed we ought to remember that even the faith of the disciples themselves, who had already believed in him, was being built up by miracles: not that what was not might begin to be, but that what had already begun to be might grow; although he used such a word, as though they then began to believe. For he does not say: I rejoice for your sake, that your faith may be increased, or strengthened; but he says, *that you may believe*: which is to be understood, that you may believe more and more robustly.

## What the four-days-dead man signifies.

**12.** *But let us go to him. Then Thomas, who is called Didymus, said to his fellow disciples: Let us too go, and die with him. So Jesus came, and found him already four days in the tomb.* About the four days many things indeed can be said, as the obscure passages of the Scriptures are wont to bear, which, according to the diversity of those who understand, bring forth many senses. Let us too say what seems to us to be signified by the four-days-dead man. For as in that blind man we understand in a manner the human race, so perhaps in this dead man we are going to understand many: for in diverse ways one thing can be signified.

When a man is born, he is already born with death; because he draws sin from Adam. Hence the Apostle says: *Through one man sin entered into the world, and through sin death; and so it passed into all men, in whom all sinned.* Behold, you have one day of death, which a man draws from the propagation of death.

Then he grows, begins to approach the years of reason, that he may know the natural law, which all have fixed in the heart: What you do not wish to be done to you, do not do to another. Is this learned from pages, and not in a manner read in nature itself? Do you wish to suffer theft? Surely you do not. Behold the law in your heart: What you do not wish to suffer, do not do. And men transgress this law: behold the second day of death.

The Law too was given divinely through the servant of God, Moses: it was said there: *You shall not kill; You shall not commit adultery; You shall not bear false witness; Honor your father and mother; You shall not covet your neighbor's property; You shall not covet your neighbor's wife.* Behold, the Law is written, and it too is despised: behold the third day of death. What remains?

The Gospel too came, the kingdom of heaven is preached, Christ is spread abroad everywhere, hell is threatened, eternal life is promised, and it too is despised. Men transgress the Gospel: behold the fourth day of death. Deservedly now he stinks. Is mercy, then, to be denied even to such? Far be it: even to such the Lord does not disdain to draw near to raise them.

**13.** *Now many of the Jews had come to Martha and Mary, to console them about their brother. So Martha, when she heard that Jesus was coming, ran to meet him: but Mary was sitting at home. Then Martha said to Jesus: Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died; but even now I know that whatever you ask of God, God will give you.* She did not say: But even now I ask you to raise my brother. For whence did she know whether it would be useful for her brother to rise again? She said only this: I know that you can, if you will, you do it: for whether you do it is of your judgment, not of my presumption. *But even now I know that whatever you ask of God, God will give you.*

**14.** *Jesus says to her: Your brother will rise again.* This was ambiguous. For he did not say: I now raise your brother; but: *Your brother will rise again. Martha says to him: I know that he will rise again in the resurrection, on the last day.* Of that resurrection I am secure, of this I am uncertain. *Jesus says to her: I am the resurrection.* You say: My brother will rise again on the last day: it is true; but he through whom he will then rise can do it even now, because *I am,* he says, *the resurrection and the life.*

Hear, brothers, hear what he says. Surely the whole expectation of those standing around was that Lazarus might come back to life, one man four days dead: let us hear, and rise again. How many are there in this people, whom the mass of habit presses down! Perhaps some hear me, to whom it is said: *Do not be drunk with wine, in which is debauchery*: they say: We cannot. Perhaps some unclean ones hear me, defiled with lewdness and disgraceful deeds, to whom it is said: Do not do this, lest you perish: and they answer: We cannot be taken away from our habit. O Lord, raise these. *I am,* he says, *the resurrection and the life.* The resurrection, therefore, because the life.

## The soul of your soul is faith.

**15.** *He who believes in me, even if he dies, will live: and everyone who lives and believes in me will not die forever.* What is this? *He who believes in me, even if he dies,* as Lazarus died, *will live*; because he is not the God of the dead, but of the living. Of the fathers long dead, that is, of Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob, he gave such an answer to the Jews: *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob; he is not the God of the dead, but of the living: for all live to him.* Believe, then; and if you die, you will live: but if you do not believe, even while you live, you are dead.

Let us prove this too, that if you do not believe, even though you live, you are dead. To a certain man putting off following him, and saying: *Let me first bury my father: Let,* he says, *the dead bury their dead; you come, follow me.* There was there a dead man to be buried, and there were there too dead men to bury the dead man: that one dead in flesh, these in soul. Whence death in the soul? Because faith is not there. Whence death in the body? Because the soul is not there. Therefore the soul of your soul is faith. *He who believes in me,* he says, *even if he dies* in the flesh, *will live* in the soul; until the flesh too rise, never afterward to die. This is: *He who believes in me, although he die, will live. And everyone who lives* in the flesh *and believes in me,* even though he will die for a time on account of the death of the flesh, *will not die forever* on account of the life of the spirit, and the immortality of the resurrection.

This is what he says: *And everyone who lives and believes in me will not die forever. Do you believe this? She says to him: Yes, Lord, I have believed that you are the Christ, the Son of God, who have come into the world.* When I believed this, I believed that you are the resurrection, I believed that you are the life; I believed that he who believes in you, even though he die, will live; and he who lives and believes in you will not die forever.

**16.** *And when she had said this, she went away, and called Mary her sister silently, saying: The Master is here, and calls you.* It is to be noted how he called a suppressed voice "silently." For how did she keep silent, who said: *The Master is here, and calls you?* It is also to be noted how the Evangelist did not say where or when or how the Lord called Mary, that this might rather be understood in the words of Martha, the brevity of the narration being preserved.

**17.** *When she heard it, she rises quickly, and comes to him. For Jesus had not yet come into the town, but was still in that place where Martha had met him. So the Jews who were with her in the house, and were consoling her, when they saw Mary, that she rose quickly and went out, followed her, saying: She goes to the tomb, to weep there.* Why did it pertain to the Evangelist to narrate this? That we may see what occasion brought it about that more were there when Lazarus was raised. For the Jews, thinking that she hastened for this reason, to seek by tears the solace of her grief, followed her; so that so great a miracle of the four-days-dead man rising again might find very many witnesses.

**18.** *But Mary, when she had come where Jesus was, seeing him, fell at his feet, and said to him: Lord, if you had been here, my brother would not have died. So Jesus, when he saw her weeping, and the Jews who were with her weeping, groaned in spirit, and troubled himself, and said: Where have you laid him?* He intimated to us I know not what by groaning in spirit, and troubling himself. For who could trouble him, except he himself? So, my brothers, first here attend to the power, and then inquire into the signification. You are troubled unwillingly; Christ was troubled because he willed it. Jesus hungered, it is true, but because he willed it; Jesus slept, it is true, but because he willed it; Jesus was saddened, it is true, but because he willed it; Jesus died, it is true, but because he willed it: it was in his power to be affected thus or thus, or not to be affected. For the Word took on a soul and flesh, fitting to itself the nature of the whole man in the unity of person. For the soul of an Apostle too was enlightened by the Word, the soul of Peter was enlightened by the Word, the souls of Paul, of the other Apostles, of the holy Prophets were enlightened by the Word: but of none was it said: *The Word was made flesh*; of none was it said: *I and the Father are one.* The soul and flesh of Christ with the Word of God is one person, is one Christ. And through this, where there is the highest power, weakness is dealt with according to the nod of the will: that is, *he troubled himself.*

## In the voice of one groaning appears the hope of one rising again.

**19.** I have spoken of the power, attend to the signification. A great guilty man is he whom the four days of death and that burial signify. What, then, is it that Christ troubles himself, except to signify to you how you ought to be troubled, when you are weighed down and pressed by so great a mass of sin? For you have attended to yourself, you have seen yourself guilty, you have reckoned to yourself: I did that, and God spared me; I committed that, and he bore with me; I heard the Gospel, and despised it; I was baptized, and again rolled back to the same things: what am I doing? where am I going? whence shall I escape? When you say these things, Christ already groans; because faith groans. In the voice of one groaning appears the hope of one rising again. If that faith is within, there is Christ groaning: if faith is in us, Christ is in us. For what else does the Apostle say: *That Christ may dwell through faith in your hearts?* Therefore your faith about Christ is Christ in your heart.

Hence is that which he slept in the boat: and when the disciples were in peril, with shipwreck now imminent they came to him, and woke him: Christ rose, commanded the winds and the waves, and there came a great calm. So you too: the winds enter your heart, surely where you sail, where you cross this life as a stormy and perilous sea; the winds enter, they move the waves, they trouble the ship. What are the winds? You have heard an insult, you are angered; the insult is the wind, the anger is the wave: you are in peril, you make ready to answer, you make ready to render curse for curse, now the ship draws near to shipwreck; wake the sleeping Christ. For you toss, and prepare to render evil for evil, because Christ sleeps in the ship. For in your heart the sleep of Christ is the forgetfulness of faith. For if you wake Christ, that is, recall faith, what does Christ, as it were waking, say to you in your heart? I heard: *You have a demon*, and prayed for them: the Lord hears, and bears it; the slave hears, and is indignant! But you wish to be avenged. What then? am I already avenged? When your faith says these things to you, it is as though the winds and the waves are commanded, and there comes a great calm.

As, then, this is to wake Christ in the ship, to wake faith; so in the heart of a man whom a great mass and habit of sin presses, in the heart of a man who transgresses even the holy Gospel, a despiser of eternal punishments, let Christ groan, let the man rebuke himself. Hear further: Christ wept, let the man weep for himself. For why did Christ weep, except because he taught man to weep? Why did he groan and trouble himself, except because the faith of a man deservedly displeasing to himself ought in a manner to groan in the accusation of evil works, that the habit of sinning may yield to the violence of repenting?

**20.** *And he said: Where have you laid him?* You knew that he was dead, and do you not know where he is buried? This too is a signification, that God in a manner does not know a man thus lost. I did not dare to say: He does not know: for what does he not know? But, as it were, he does not know. Whence do we prove this? Hear the Lord, who will say in the judgment: *I do not know you; depart from me.* What is: *I do not know you?* I do not see you in my light, I do not see you in that righteousness which I know. So here too, as though not knowing such a sinner, he said: *Where have you laid him?* Such is the voice of God in paradise after the man sinned: *Adam, where are you?* *They say to him: Lord, come and see.* What is, *see?* Have mercy. For the Lord sees, when he has mercy. Whence it is said to him: *See my lowliness and my labor, and forgive all my sins.*

**21.** *Jesus wept. So the Jews said: Behold how he loved him.* What is, *he loved him? I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance.* *But some of them said: Could not this man, who opened the eyes of the blind man, have caused that this man too should not die?* He who was unwilling to cause that he should not die—greater is what he is going to do, that the dead man be raised.

**22.** *Jesus therefore, again groaning within himself, came to the tomb.* Let him groan within you too, if you intend to come back to life. To every man who is weighed down by a most evil habit it is said: *He came to the tomb. Now it was a cave, and a stone was laid over it*. The dead man under the stone is the guilty man under the law. For you know that the law that was given to the Jews was written on stone. Now all the guilty are under the law; those who live well are with the law. The law is not laid down for the righteous man. What then does *Take away the stone* mean? Preach grace. For the apostle Paul calls himself a minister of the New Testament, not of the letter but of the spirit: *for the letter,* he says, *kills, but the spirit gives life*[^2]. The letter that kills is, as it were, a stone that weighs down. *Take away,* he says, *the stone.* Take away the burden of the Law; preach grace. *For if a law had been given that could give life, then righteousness would indeed be by the Law. But the Scripture has shut up all things under sin, that the promise by faith in Jesus Christ might be given to those who believe*[^3]. Therefore *take away the stone.*

**23.** *Martha, the sister of him who was dead, said to him: Lord, by this time he stinks, for he has been dead four days. Jesus said to her: Did I not tell you that, if you believe, you will see the glory of God*? What does *you will see the glory of God* mean? That he raises up even one who stinks and has been dead four days. *For all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God*[^4]; and: *Where sin abounded, grace abounded all the more*[^5].

**24.** *So they took away the stone. And Jesus, lifting up his eyes, said: Father, I thank you that you have heard me. I knew that you always hear me, but because of the people who stand around I have said it, that they may believe that you have sent me. When he had said this, he cried out with a loud voice*. He groaned, he wept, he cried out with a loud voice. How hard it is for one whom the mass of an evil habit weighs down to rise! Yet he does rise: a hidden grace within gives him life; he rises after the loud voice. What happened? *He cried out with a loud voice: Lazarus, come forth. And at once he who had been dead came out, bound hand and foot with bandages*; *and his face was bound with a cloth*[^6]. You marvel that he came out with his feet bound, and you do not marvel that he rose after four days? In both it was the Lord's power, not the strength of the dead man. He came out, and was still bound; still wrapped up, yet now he had come out. What does this signify? When you hold God in contempt, you lie dead; and if you hold in contempt such things as I have said, you lie buried. When you confess, you come forth. For what is it to come forth but to be made manifest, as though coming out from what was hidden? But that you may confess, God works by crying out with a loud voice, that is, by calling with great grace. And so, when the dead man had come out still bound, confessing yet still guilty, the Lord said this to his ministers, that his sins might be loosed: *Loose him, and let him go*[^7]. What does *Loose him, and let him go* mean? Whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed in heaven also[^8].

**25.** *Many therefore of the Jews who had come to Mary, and had seen the things that Jesus did, believed in him. But some of them went away to the Pharisees, and told them the things that Jesus had done*. Not all of the Jews who had gathered to Mary believed, but still many did. *But certain ones of them,* whether of the Jews who had gathered or of those who had believed, *went away to the Pharisees, and told them the things that Jesus had done*: either to announce it, so that they too might believe, or rather to betray it, so that they might rage. But in whatever manner and by whatever persons, these things were carried to the Pharisees.

**26.** *The chief priests and the Pharisees gathered a council, and said: What are we doing*? Yet they did not say: Let us believe. For these lost men were thinking more about how they might do harm so as to destroy than about how they might take counsel for themselves so as not to perish; and yet they were afraid, and were as it were taking counsel. For they said: *What are we doing? For this man works many signs. If we let him go on like this, all will believe in him; and the Romans will come and take away both our place and our nation*[^9]. They were afraid to lose temporal things, and they did not think of eternal life; and so they lost both. For after the Lord's passion and glorification the Romans did take from them both their place and their nation, by conquering them and carrying them off; and there followed for them what was said elsewhere: *But the children of this kingdom will go into the outer darkness*[^10]. But they were afraid of this: that if all should believe in Christ, no one would remain to defend the city and temple of God against the Romans, since they perceived that the teaching of Christ was against that very temple and against their ancestral laws.

**27.** *But one of them, Caiaphas, being the high priest of that year, said to them: You know nothing, nor do you consider that it is expedient for us that one man should die for the people, and that the whole nation perish not. Now this he did not say of himself, but being the high priest of that year, he prophesied*[^11]. Here we are taught that the Spirit of prophecy foretells things to come even through evil men; yet the Evangelist attributed this to the divine mystery, because he was high priest, that is, chief priest.

It may, however, raise a difficulty how he is called the high priest of that year, since God appointed one high priest, whom, when he died, one would succeed. But it must be understood that afterward, through the ambitions and contentions among the Jews, it was arranged that there should be several, and that they should serve in turn year by year. For this is said of Zechariah as well: *And it came to pass that, while he was performing the priestly office in the order of his turn before God, according to the custom of the priesthood, it fell to him by lot to offer incense, when he had entered the temple of the Lord*[^12]. From this it is clear that they were several, and that they had their turns; for it was not lawful for anyone but the high priest to offer incense. And perhaps several even administered a single year, whom in another year others succeeded, and from among them it came out by lot who should offer incense.

What then is it that Caiaphas prophesied? *That Jesus would die for the nation; and not for the nation only, but that he might gather into one the children of God who were scattered abroad*[^13]. This the Evangelist added; for Caiaphas prophesied only of the nation of the Jews, in which were the sheep of whom the Lord himself says: *I was not sent except to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel*[^14]. But the Evangelist knew that there were other sheep that were not of this fold, which had to be brought, that there might be one fold and one shepherd[^15]. Now these things were said according to predestination; for those who had not yet believed were as yet neither his sheep nor children of God.

**28.** *From that day, therefore, they took counsel to put him to death. Jesus therefore no longer walked openly among the Jews, but went away into a region near the wilderness, into a city called Ephrem, and there he stayed with his disciples*[^16]. Not because his power had failed—by which surely, if he had willed, he might both have lived openly among the Jews and they would have done nothing to him—but in the weakness of a man he was showing his disciples an example of how to live, in which it might appear that there is no sin if his faithful ones, who are his members, withdraw themselves from the eyes of those who persecute them, and by hiding avoid the fury of the wicked rather than inflame it the more by offering themselves.
