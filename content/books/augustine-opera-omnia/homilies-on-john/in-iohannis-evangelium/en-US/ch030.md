# Tractate 30

## *Do not judge according to appearance, but judge with right judgment* (Jn 7:19-24).

## Let us so hear the gospel as though the Lord were present; and let us not say: O how happy were those who were able to see him! The Lord is on high; but the Lord who is the truth is also here. For the body of the Lord, in which he rose, can be in one place; his truth is spread everywhere.

**1.** Upon the reading of the holy Gospel, of which we spoke to your Charity recently, there follows this passage that has now been read, today's reading. Both the disciples and the Jews heard the Lord speaking; both the truthful and the lying heard the truth speaking; both friends and enemies heard love speaking; both the good and the evil heard the good speaking. They heard, but he distinguished; and he saw and foresaw to whom the discourse would profit and was going to profit.

For in those who then were, he saw; in us who were to be, he foresaw. Let us, therefore, so hear the Gospel as though the Lord were present; and let us not say: O how happy were those who were able to see him! For many among those who saw also killed; while many among us who did not see also believed. For what sounded precious from the mouth of the Lord was both written for our sake, and preserved for us, and recited for our sake, and will be recited also for the sake of our descendants, and until the world comes to an end. The Lord is on high: but the Lord who is the truth is also here. For the body of the Lord, in which he rose, can be in one place: his truth is spread everywhere. Let us, then, hear the Lord, and what he himself grants from his words, let us too say.

**2.** *Did not Moses*, he says, *give you the Law, and none of you keeps the Law? Why do you seek to kill me?*[^1] For you seek to kill me for this reason, because none of you keeps the Law: for if you kept the Law, you would acknowledge Christ in those very writings, and you would not kill him present. And they answered: *The crowd answered him*. It answered as a crowd, with things pertaining not to order but to disorder: in short, see what the disturbed crowd answered: *You have a demon; who seeks to kill you?*[^2] As if it were not worse to say, You have a demon, than to kill him. For it was said to him that he had a demon—to him who cast out demons. What else could a turbulent crowd say? What else could stirred-up filth give off as a stench? The crowd was disturbed; by what? By the truth. The brightness of the light disturbed the bleary-eyed crowd. For eyes that do not have soundness cannot bear the brightness of the light.

## The Lord, calm in his own truth.

**3.** But the Lord, not at all disturbed, but calm in his own truth, did not return *evil for evil, nor curse for curse*[^3]. If he had said to them, You have a demon, he would surely have said the truth. For they would not have said such things to the truth, had not the falsity of the devil provoked them. What, then, did he answer? Let us hear calmly, and drink in what is calm: *I have done one work, and you all marvel*[^4]. As if to say: What if you saw all my works? For the works they saw in the world were his works, and they did not see the very one who made all things:

he did one thing, and they were disturbed, because he made a man whole on the Sabbath. As if, indeed, if anyone were healed sick on the Sabbath, it had been someone other than he who made him sound—he who scandalized them because he made one man whole on the Sabbath. For who else made others sound but soundness itself, who gives also to beasts that soundness which he gave to this man? For it was a bodily soundness. The soundness of the flesh is both repaired and dies; and when it is repaired, death is deferred, not removed. Yet, brothers, even that soundness is from the Lord, by whomever it may be given: by whatever healer and minister it is imparted, it is given by him from whom is all soundness, to whom it is said in the Psalm: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord; as you have multiplied your mercy, O God*[^5]. For because you are God, your multiplied mercy reaches even to the soundness of human flesh, reaches even to the soundness of dumb animals: but you who give the soundness of the flesh common to men and beasts—is there no soundness that you keep for men?

There is certainly another that is not only not common to men and beasts, but is not even common among men themselves, the good and the evil. In short, when he had there spoken of that soundness which cattle and men receive in common, then, on account of that soundness which men ought to hope for—but good men—he went on to add: *But the sons of men will hope under the covering of your wings; they will be inebriated with the abundance of your house, and you will give them to drink of the torrent of your delight; for with you is the fountain of life, and in your light we shall see light*[^6]. This is the soundness that pertains to the good, whom he called sons of men; since above he had said: *Men and beasts you will save, O Lord*.

For what? Were those men not sons of men, that when he had said, Men, he should go on and say, But the sons of men—as if men were one thing, and sons of men another? Yet I do not think the Holy Spirit said this without some signification of distinction. Men with reference to the first Adam, sons of men with reference to Christ. For perhaps men pertain to the first man, while sons of men pertain to the Son of man.

**4.** *I have done one work, and you all marvel.* And immediately he adds: *Therefore Moses gave you circumcision.* It was well done that you received circumcision from Moses. *Not that it is from Moses, but from the fathers*[^7]. For Abraham first received circumcision from the Lord[^8]. *And on the Sabbath you circumcise.* Moses convicted you: in the Law you received that you should circumcise on the eighth day[^9]; you received in the Law that you should rest on the seventh day[^10]: if the eighth day of one who is born falls on the seventh day, the Sabbath, what will you do? Will you rest, in order to keep the Sabbath; or will you circumcise, in order to fulfill the sacrament of the eighth day?

But I know, he says, what you do. You circumcise the man. Why? Because circumcision pertains to some seal of soundness, and men ought not on the Sabbath to rest from soundness. Therefore do not be angry at me either, because I made a whole man sound on the Sabbath: *if a man receives circumcision on the Sabbath, so that the law of Moses may not be broken*[^11] (for something was salutarily instituted through Moses in that ordinance of circumcision), why are you indignant at me for working soundness on the Sabbath?

**5.** For perhaps that circumcision signified the Lord himself, at whose healing and curing these men were indignant. For circumcision was commanded to be applied on the eighth day: and what is circumcision, except a stripping of the flesh? This circumcision, then, signifies a stripping from the heart of fleshly desires. Not without cause, then, was it given, and commanded to be done on that member; since through that member the creature of mortals is procreated. And *through one man came death, just as through one man the resurrection of the dead*[^12]: and *through one man sin entered into the world, and through sin death*[^13].

Therefore everyone is born with a foreskin, because every man is born with the defect of his stock: and God does not cleanse us either from the defect with which we are born, or from the defects we add by living badly, except through the stony knife, the Lord Christ. For *the rock was Christ*[^14]. For they circumcised with stony knives, and by the name of the rock they figured Christ: and they did not acknowledge him present, but moreover desired to kill him.

But why on the eighth day, unless because after the seventh day, the Sabbath, the Lord rose on the Lord's day? Therefore the resurrection of Christ, which took place on the third day indeed from his Passion, but on the eighth day in the days of the week, this circumcises us. Hear, you who are circumcised by the true rock, as the Apostle admonishes: *If, then, you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; relish the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth*[^15]. He speaks to the circumcised: Christ has risen, he has taken away your fleshly desires, he has taken away your evil concupiscences, he has taken away the superfluous thing with which you were born, and the much worse thing that you had added by living badly; you who are circumcised by the rock, why do you still relish the earth?

And finally, because Moses gave the Law, and you circumcise a man on the Sabbath, understand that this signifies the good work that I did, making a whole man sound on the Sabbath; because he was both cured so as to be sound in body, and believed so as to be sound in soul.

## We have been made a new man.

**6.** *Do not judge by persons, but judge with right judgment*[^16]. What is this? Just now you who circumcise on the Sabbath by the law of Moses are not angry at Moses; and because I made a man sound on the Sabbath day, you are angry at me. You judge by persons; attend to the truth. I do not prefer myself to Moses, says the Lord, who was also the Lord of Moses himself. Attend in this way to us as two men, as though both of us were men: judge between us, but judge with true judgment; do not, by honoring me, condemn him, but, by understanding him, honor me. For this he said to them in another place: *If you believed Moses, you would surely also believe me; for he wrote of me*[^17]. But in this place he did not wish to say this, as though he and Moses were set before them. Because of the law of Moses you circumcise even when the Sabbath falls; and yet you do not wish me to show the beneficence of soundnesses to be made on the Sabbath?

Because the Lord of circumcision and the Lord of the Sabbath is the author of soundness: and you were forbidden to do servile works on the Sabbath; if you truly understand servile works, you do not sin. For *he who commits sin is the slave of sin*[^18]. Is it a servile work to heal a man on the Sabbath? You eat and drink (to say something from the admonition of our Lord Jesus Christ, and from his words); surely why do you eat and drink on the Sabbath, unless because what you do pertains to soundness? By this you show that works of soundness are in no way to be omitted on the Sabbath day. Therefore do not judge by persons, but judge with right judgment. Attend to me as a man, attend to Moses as a man: if you judge according to the truth, you will condemn neither Moses nor me; and when the truth is known, you will know me, because *I am the truth*[^19].

**7.** This fault, brothers, which the Lord marked in this place, it is a great labor to escape in this world: not to judge by persons, but to hold to right judgment. The Lord indeed admonished the Jews, but he also admonished us: them he convicted, us he instructed; them he refuted, us he sharpened. Let us not think this was not said for us merely because we were not then there. It is written, it is read, when it was recited we heard it; but we heard it as said to the Jews: let us not put ourselves behind ourselves, and as it were look on at our enemies being reproved, while we ourselves do what the truth itself reproves in us.

The Jews indeed judged by persons, but for that reason they do not pertain to the New Testament, for that reason they do not have in Christ the kingdom of heaven, for that reason they are not joined to the fellowship of the holy angels: they sought earthly things from the Lord; for the land of promise, victory over enemies, fruitfulness in childbearing, the multiplication of sons, the abundance of fruits—all of which were promised to them by a God indeed true and good, yet promised to them as to carnal people—all these things made the Old Testament for them. What is the Old Testament? As it were an inheritance pertaining to the old man. We have been renewed, we have been made a new man; because that man too came new.

What is so new as to be born of a virgin? Because, then, there was nothing in him that a precept might renew, since he had no sin, a new birth was given. In him a new birth, in us a new man. What is a new man? One renewed from oldness. For what thing renewed? For desiring the heavenly, for craving the everlasting, for desiring the homeland that is above and fears no enemy, where we do not lose a friend, we do not fear an enemy; where we live with good affection, without any defect; where no one is born, because no one dies; where no one any longer makes progress, and no one falls away; where there is no hunger and no thirst, but immortality is the fullness, and truth is the food. Having these promises, and pertaining to the New Testament, and made heirs of a new inheritance, and coheirs of the Lord himself, we have a far different hope: let us not judge by persons, but hold to right judgment.

**8.** Who is it that does not judge by persons? He who loves equally. Equal love brings it about that persons are not shown partiality. It is not when we honor people in different ways according to their ranks that we must then fear lest we show partiality to persons. But when we judge between two, and sometimes between kinsmen, there sometimes arises a judgment between father and son; the father complains of a bad son, or the son complains of a harsh father: we preserve the honor owed to the father by the son; we do not make the son equal to the father in honor; but we set him before the father, if he has a good cause: let us make the son equal to the father in truth; and so let us render the honor owed, in such a way that equity does not lose its due. Thus by the words of the Lord we make progress, and that we may make progress, we are helped by his grace.
