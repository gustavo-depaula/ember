# Tractate 67

## *In my Father's house are many mansions* (Jn 14:1-4).

## *Many mansions.* Since God is love, through love it comes about that what individuals have is common to all. For each one in this way has it himself, when he loves in another what he himself does not have. There will be no envy of unequal brightness, then, since the unity of love will reign in all.

**1.** We must raise a greater attention to God, brothers, that we may somehow be able to grasp even with the mind the words of the holy Gospel which just now sounded in our ears. For the Lord Jesus says: *Let not your heart be troubled. You believe in God, believe also in me*[^1]. Lest they should fear death like men, and therefore be troubled, he consoles them, attesting that he too is God. *You believe,* he says, *in God, believe also in me.* For it follows that if you believe in God, you ought also to believe in me: which would not follow, if Christ were not God. *You believe in God, believe also in* him to whom it is nature, not robbery, to be equal to God: for he emptied himself; yet not losing the form of God, but taking the form of a servant[^2]. You fear death for this form of a servant; *let not your heart be troubled,* the form of God will raise it up.

## The unity of love will reign in all.

**2.** But what is it that follows: *In my Father's house are many mansions,* but that they feared also for themselves? Whence they ought to have heard: *Let not your heart be troubled.* For which of them would not fear, when it had been said to Peter, the more confident and the readier: *The cock will not crow until you deny me three times*? As though, then, they were about to perish through that, they were rightly troubled: but when they hear: *In my Father's house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you that I go to prepare a place for you*[^3]; they are restored from their disturbance, sure and confident that even after the perils of temptations they will remain with God, with Christ. For although one is stronger than another, one wiser than another, one more righteous than another, one holier than another; *in the Father's house are many mansions*; none of them will be made an alien from that house, where each one will receive a mansion according to his merit.

That denarius indeed is equal for all, which the householder commands to be given to all who worked in the vineyard, making no distinction in it between those who labored less and those who labored more[^4]: by which denarius, surely, eternal life is signified, where no one lives more than another, since there is no diverse measure of living in eternity. But the many mansions signify the diverse dignities of merits in the one eternal life. For there is one glory of the sun, another glory of the moon, another glory of the stars: for star differs from star in glory; so also is the resurrection of the dead[^5]. As stars, the saints obtain by lot diverse mansions of diverse brightness, as in heaven, in the kingdom; but on account of the one denarius no one is separated from the kingdom: and so God will be all in all, so that, since God is love, through love it comes about that what individuals have is common to all. For each one in this way has it himself, when he loves in another what he himself does not have. There will be no envy of unequal brightness, then, since the unity of love will reign in all.

**3.** Accordingly, those are to be rejected from the Christian heart who think that it was said there are many mansions for this reason, that there will be something outside the kingdom of heaven, where the blessed innocents may abide who departed from this life without Baptism, since without it they will not be able to enter the kingdom of heaven. This faith is not faith, since it is not the true and catholic faith. Is it, then, after all, foolish men, blinded by carnal thoughts, that, when you would be worthy of reproof if you separated from the kingdom of heaven the mansion—I do not say of Peter and Paul or any of the Apostles, but of any baptized little child—you do not think you are worthy of reproof, who separate from it the house of God the Father? For the Lord did not say: In the whole world, or, in the whole creation, or, in everlasting life or blessedness are many mansions; but: *In the house,* he says, *of my Father are many mansions.* Is not this the house *where we have a building from God, a house not made with hands, eternal in the heavens*[^6]? Is not this the house of which we sing to the Lord: *Blessed are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you*[^7]? Will you, then, dare to separate from the kingdom of heaven, not the house of any baptized brother, but the house of God the Father himself, to whom we all say, as brothers: *Our Father, who art in heaven*, or to divide it so that some of its mansions are in the kingdom of heaven, but some outside the kingdom of heaven? Far be it, far be it, that those who wish to dwell in the kingdom of heaven should wish to dwell with you in this folly: far be it, I say, that, since the whole house of the reigning sons is nowhere else but in the kingdom, any part of the royal house itself should not be in the kingdom.

**4.** *And if I go,* he says, *and prepare a place for you; I come again, and will receive you to myself, that where I am you too may be. And where I go you know, and the way you know*[^8]. O Lord Jesus, how do you go to prepare a place, if there are already many mansions in your Father's house, where your own will dwell with you? Or if you receive them to yourself, how do you come again, who do not withdraw? These things, dearly beloved, if we should try to explain them briefly, as much as seems enough for today's sermon, will surely, being cramped, not become clear, and that very brevity will be a further obscurity: accordingly, let us defer this debt, which we may more opportunely pay you, our Householder granting it.

[^2]: See Phil 2:6-7
[^3]: Jn 14:2
[^4]: See Mt 20:9-10
[^5]: See 1 Cor 15:41-42
[^6]: 2 Cor 5:1
[^7]: Ps 84:4 (83:5)
[^8]: Jn 14:3-4
