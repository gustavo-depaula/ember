# Homily 4

## *Behold the Lamb of God* [^1]

## Behold the Lamb of God. He took only flesh from Adam, he did not take on sin. He who did not take on sin from our mass is the very one who takes away our sin. Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sin of the world.

**1.** Most often your Holiness has heard, and you know very well, that John the Baptist, the more distinguished he was among those born of women, and the more humble in acknowledging the Lord, so much the more did he merit to be the friend of the bridegroom; zealous for the bridegroom, not for himself; not seeking his own honor, but that of his judge, before whom he went as a herald. And so to the preceding prophets it was granted to foretell things to come concerning Christ; but to this one, to point him out with the finger. For just as Christ was unknown to those who did not believe the prophets before he came, so he was unknown to them when present. For he had come, first, humbly and hidden; the more hidden, the more humble: but the peoples, spurning through their pride the humility of God, crucified their Savior, and made him their condemner.

## He appeared humble.

**2.** But he who first came hidden, because he came humble, will he not afterward come manifest, because exalted? You heard just now the Psalm: *God will come manifest, our God, and will not keep silence* [^2]. He kept silence that he might be judged, he will not keep silence when he begins to judge. It would not be said, *he will come manifest*, unless he had first come hidden; nor would it be said, *he will not keep silence*, except that he first kept silence.

How did he keep silence?

Question Isaiah:

*He was led like a sheep to the slaughter, and like a lamb before its shearer he was without voice, so he did not open his mouth* [^3].

But he will come manifest, and will not keep silence.

How manifest?

*A fire will go before him, and around him a mighty tempest* [^4]. That tempest is to take all the chaff from the threshing floor, which is now being threshed; and the fire is to burn what the tempest has taken away.

But now he is silent: he is silent in judgment, but he is not silent in precept. For if Christ is silent, what do these Gospels mean? What do the apostolic voices mean, what the songs of the Psalms, what the utterances of the prophets? For in all these Christ is not silent. But he is silent now, so as not to take vengeance: he is not silent, so as not to admonish.

But he will come splendid for vengeance, and will appear to all, even to those who do not believe in him. But now, because even when present he was hidden, it was fitting that he should be despised. For unless he were despised, he would not be crucified: if he were not crucified, he would not pour out blood, the price by which he redeemed us. But that he might give a price for us, he was crucified; that he might be crucified, he was despised; that he might be despised, he appeared humble.

**3.** Yet because he appeared, as it were in the night, in a mortal body, he lit a lamp for himself by which he might be seen. That lamp was John, of whom you have already heard much: and the present reading of the Gospel contains the words of John, first—what is chief—confessing that he himself was not the Christ. But there was such excellence in John that he could have been believed to be the Christ: and in this his humility was proved, that he said he was not, when he could have been believed to be. Therefore: *This is the testimony of John, when the Jews sent priests and Levites from Jerusalem to him, to ask him: Who are you?* [^5] They would not have sent unless they were moved by the excellence of his authority, because he dared to baptize. *And he confessed, and did not deny* [^6]. What did he confess? *And he confessed: I am not the Christ* [^7].

**4.** *And they asked him: What then? Are you Elijah?* [^8] For they knew that Elijah was to precede Christ. For the name of Christ was not unknown to anyone among the Jews. They did not think this man was the Christ: they did not think at all that Christ was not to come. While they hoped he would come, they stumbled thus over him when present, they stumbled as over a humble stone. For that stone was still small, though already cut from the mountain without hands: as the prophet Daniel says he saw a stone cut from the mountain without hands. But what follows? *And that stone grew*, he says, *and became a great mountain, and filled the whole face of the earth* [^9].

Let your Charity see what I say: Christ, before the Jews, was already cut from the mountain. By the mountain he wishes the kingdom of the Jews to be understood. But the kingdom of the Jews had not filled the whole face of the earth. From there that stone was cut, because from there the Lord was born into their presence. And why without hands? Because without a man's work the Virgin bore Christ. So then that stone was already cut without hands, before the eyes of the Jews: but it was humble. Not undeservedly; because that stone had not yet grown and filled the whole world: which it showed in its kingdom, which is the Church, by which it filled the whole face of the earth. Because, then, it had not yet grown, they stumbled over it as over a stone: and there was fulfilled in them what is written: *Whoever falls upon this stone will be broken to pieces; and on whomever that stone falls, it will crush them* [^10]. First they fell upon him as humble; he is to come exalted over them: but that he might, coming as exalted, crush them, first as humble he shook them. They stumbled over him, and were shaken; not crushed, but shaken: he will come exalted, and will crush them. But the Jews are to be pardoned, because they stumbled over the stone that had not yet grown. What sort are those who stumbled over the mountain itself? You already recognize of whom I speak. Those who deny the Church spread over the whole world stumble, not over the humble stone, but over the mountain itself: which that stone became as it grew. The blind Jews did not see the humble stone: how great a blindness it is not to see the mountain!

Because, then, it had not yet grown, they stumbled over it as over a stone: and there was fulfilled in them what is written: *Whoever falls upon this stone will be broken to pieces; and on whomever that stone falls, it will crush them* [^11]. First they fell upon him as humble; he is to come exalted over them: but that he might, coming as exalted, crush them, first as humble he shook them. They stumbled over him, and were shaken; not crushed, but shaken: he will come exalted, and will crush them. But the Jews are to be pardoned, because they stumbled over the stone that had not yet grown. What sort are those who stumbled over the mountain itself? You already recognize of whom I speak. Those who deny the Church spread over the whole world stumble, not over the humble stone, but over the mountain itself: which that stone became as it grew. The blind Jews did not see the humble stone: how great a blindness it is not to see the mountain!

## For this reason the lamp was lit.

**5.** Therefore they saw him humble, and did not recognize him. He was being pointed out to them through the lamp. For first that man, than whom no one greater had arisen among those born of women, said: *I am not the Christ*. And it was said to him: *Are you Elijah? He answered, I am not* [^12]. For Christ sends Elijah ahead before himself: and he said, *I am not*, and made a question for us. For it is to be feared lest those who understand less should think John said the contrary of what Christ said. For in a certain place, when the Lord Jesus Christ in the Gospel was saying certain things about himself, those disciples answered him: *How then do the scribes*—that is, the experts in the Law—*say that Elijah must first come?* And the Lord said: *Elijah has already come, and they did to him what they would; and if you wish to know, he is John the Baptist* [^13]. The Lord Jesus Christ said: *Elijah has already come, and he is John the Baptist*: but John, when questioned, confessed himself not to be Elijah, just as he confessed himself not to be the Christ. And surely, as he confessed truly that he was not the Christ, so he confessed truly that he was not Elijah either. How, then, shall we compare the words of the herald with the words of the judge? Far be it that the herald should lie: for he speaks what he hears from the judge. Why, then, does that one say, *I am not Elijah*: and the Lord, *He is Elijah*? Because in him the Lord Jesus Christ wished to prefigure his own coming to be, and to say this, that John was in the spirit of Elijah. And what John was at the first coming, that Elijah will be at the second coming. As there are two comings of the judge, so there are two heralds. The judge indeed is the same one, but the heralds are two: not two judges. For it was fitting that the judge should first come to be judged. He sent the first herald before him, he called him Elijah; because at the second coming Elijah will be what John was at the first.

**6.** For let your Charity attend how truly I speak. When John was conceived, or rather when he was born, the Holy Spirit prophesied this concerning that man as something to be fulfilled: *And he will be*, he says, *the forerunner of the Most High, in the spirit and power of Elijah* [^14]. Therefore not Elijah, *but in the spirit and power of Elijah*. What is, *in the spirit and power of Elijah*? In the same Holy Spirit, in the place of Elijah. Why in the place of Elijah? Because what Elijah was for the second coming, that John was for the first. Rightly, then, did John just now answer in the proper sense. For the Lord spoke figuratively: *He is Elijah, John*: but this man, as I said, in the proper sense: *I am not Elijah*. If you note the figure of the forerunning, John himself is Elijah: for what the one was at the first coming, that the other will be at the second. If you ask about the proper sense of the person, John is John, Elijah is Elijah; the Lord, then, rightly with respect to the prefiguration: He is Elijah: but John rightly with respect to the proper sense: I am not Elijah. Neither did John speak falsely, nor the Lord falsely: neither the herald falsely, nor the Judge falsely; provided that you understand.

But who will understand? He who has imitated the humility of the herald, and has recognized the loftiness of the judge. For nothing is humbler than the herald himself. My brothers, John had no merit so great as that of this humility, that, when he could deceive men and be thought the Christ and be held for the Christ (for he was of such great grace and such great excellence), he nevertheless confessed openly, and said: *I am not the Christ*.

*Are you Elijah?* Now if he had said: I am Elijah; then Christ would already be coming at the second coming to judge, not still being judged at the first. As if saying: Elijah too is to come; *I am not*, he says, *Elijah*. But observe the humble one before whom John came, lest you should perceive the exalted one before whom Elijah is to come. For the Lord also completed it thus: *He is John the Baptist who is to come* [^15]. By the very prefiguration this man came, by which in the proper sense Elijah is to come. Then Elijah, in the proper sense, will be Elijah; now, by likeness, he was John. Now John, in the proper sense, is John, by likeness is Elijah. Both heralds gave their likenesses to each other, and kept their proper identities: but the Lord judge is one, whether that herald goes before, or this one.

**7.** *And they asked him: What then? Are you Elijah? And he said: No. And they said to him: Are you the prophet? And he answered: No. They said therefore to him: Who are you? that we may give an answer to those who sent us. What do you say of yourself? He said: I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness* [^16]. Isaiah said that. In John this prophecy was fulfilled: *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness*. Crying what? *Make straight the way of the Lord, make straight the paths of our God* [^17]. Does it not seem to you to belong to a herald to say: Come out, make a way? Except that the herald says, Come out: John says, Come. The herald drives away from the judge, John calls to the judge. Rather, John calls to the humble one, lest the judge be perceived as exalted. *I am the voice of one crying in the wilderness, make straight the way of the Lord, as Isaiah the prophet said*. He did not say: I am John, I am Elijah, I am a prophet. But what did he say? This I am called: *The voice of one crying in the wilderness: Make straight the way of the Lord*: I am the prophecy itself.

**8.** *And those who had been sent were of the Pharisees*: that is, of the leaders of the Jews. *And they questioned, and said to him: Why then do you baptize, if you are not the Christ, nor Elijah, nor the prophet?* [^18] It seemed, as it were, a matter of boldness to baptize, as if: In what character? We ask whether you are the Christ; you say you are not: we ask whether perhaps you are his forerunner, because we know that before the coming of Christ Elijah is to come; you deny that you are: we ask whether perhaps you are some herald going far in advance—that is, a prophet—and have received this power; you do not say that you are a prophet either. And John was not a prophet: he was greater than a prophet. The Lord gave such a testimony of him: *What did you go out into the wilderness to see? A reed shaken by the wind?* Surely: Not shaken by the wind, you understand; for John was not this, like one moved by the wind: for he who is moved by the wind is blown about by every seducing breath. *But what did you go out to see? A man clothed in soft garments?* For John was clothed in rough garments—that is, a tunic made of camel's hair. See, those who are clothed in soft garments are in the houses of kings. Therefore you did not go out to see a man clothed in soft garments. *But what did you go out to see? A prophet? Yes, I tell you, and more than a prophet is this man* [^19]: because the prophets foretold long before, John pointed him out when present.

**9.** *Why then do you baptize, if you are not the Christ, nor Elijah, nor the prophet? John answered them, and said: I baptize in water; but there has stood in your midst one whom you do not know* [^20]. For he was not seen, being humble, and therefore the lamp was lit. See how he gives place, who could be thought to be something else. *He it is who comes after me, who has been set before me*. As we have already said, that is, has been placed ahead of me. *The strap of whose sandal I am not worthy to loosen* [^21]. How greatly he abased himself! And therefore he was greatly raised up; since *he who humbles himself will be exalted* [^22].

From this your Holiness ought to see that, if John so humbled himself as to say, *I am not worthy to loosen the strap*; how greatly ought those to be humbled who say: We baptize, what we give is ours, and what is ours is holy. He says: Not I, but he: they say: We. John is not worthy to loosen the strap of his sandal: but if he said he was worthy, how humble would he be? And if he said he was worthy, and said it thus: He comes after me, who has been set before me, the strap only of whose sandal I am worthy to loosen; he would have humbled himself greatly. But when he does not say he is worthy even for this, he was truly full of the Holy Spirit, who as a servant so acknowledged his Lord, and merited to become a friend out of a servant.

**10.** *These things were done in Bethany beyond the Jordan, where John was baptizing. The next day John saw Jesus coming to him, and said: Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sin of the world* [^23]. Let no one arrogate it to himself and say that he himself takes away the sin of the world. Now attend, against what proud men John was pointing his finger. The heretics were not yet born, and already they were being exposed: against those he cried out then from the river, against whom now he cries out from the Gospel.

Jesus comes; and what does he say? *Behold the Lamb of God*. If a lamb is innocent, John too is a lamb. Or was John not himself innocent? But who is innocent? How far innocent? All come from that transmission and from that stock of which David, groaning, sings: *I was conceived in iniquity, and in sins my mother nourished me in the womb* [^24]. He alone, then, is the Lamb who did not come thus. For he was not conceived in iniquity; because he was not conceived from mortality: nor did his mother nourish him in sins in the womb, whom a virgin conceived, a virgin bore; because she conceived by faith, and received him by faith. Therefore, *behold the Lamb of God*. This one has no transmission from Adam: he took only flesh from Adam, he did not take on sin. He who did not take on sin from our mass is the very one who takes away our sin. *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sin of the world*.

**11.** You know that certain men sometimes say: We, who are holy, take away sins from men: for if the one who baptizes is not holy, how does he take away another's sin, when he himself is a man full of sin? Against these disputations let us not speak our own words, let us read this one: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sin of the world*. Let men have no presumption in men: let the sparrow not migrate to the mountains [^25], let it trust in the Lord; and if it lifts its eyes to the mountains, from where its help will come, let it understand that its help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth [^26].

John was of such great excellence; it is said to him: Are you the Christ? He says: No. Are you Elijah? He says: No. Are you the prophet? He says: No. Why then do you baptize? *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sin of the world: this is he of whom I said: After me comes a man who has been set before me, because he was before me* [^27]. He comes after me, because he was born later: he has been set before me, because he was preferred to me: he was before me, because in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God.

**12.** *And I did not know him*, he said: *but that he might be made manifest to Israel, for this reason I came baptizing in water. And John bore witness, saying: I saw the Spirit descending from heaven like a dove, and it remained upon him: and I did not know him, but he who sent me to baptize in water, he said to me: He upon whom you see the Spirit descending and remaining upon him, this is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit: and I saw, and bore witness that this is the Son of God* [^28].

Let your Charity attend a little: when did John learn who Christ was? For he was sent to baptize in water. It was asked, why? *That he might be made manifest to Israel*, he said. What did the baptism of John profit? My brothers, if it profited anything, it would remain even now, and men would be baptized with the baptism of John; and so they would come to the baptism of Christ. But what does he say? *That he might be made manifest to Israel*: that is, that to Israel itself, to the people of Israel, Christ might be made manifest, he came to baptize in water. John received the ministry of baptism, in the water of repentance to prepare the way of the Lord, not being himself the Lord: but when the Lord was known, the way was prepared for him superfluously; because to those who know him he himself was made the way: and so the baptism of John did not last long. But how was the Lord shown? Humble; so that for this reason John received a baptism, in which the Lord himself might be baptized.

## Did the Lord need to be baptized?

**13.** And did the Lord need to be baptized? And I, questioning, quickly answer: Did the Lord need to be born? Did the Lord need to be crucified? Did the Lord need to die? Did the Lord need to be buried? If, then, he undertook so great a humility for us, was he not going to undertake baptism?

And what did it profit that he undertook the baptism of a servant?

That you might not disdain to undertake the baptism of the Lord.

Let your Charity attend. There were going to be in the Church certain catechumens of loftier grace. For it sometimes happens that you see a catechumen abstaining from all intercourse, bidding farewell to the world, renouncing all that he possessed, distributing it to the poor; and he is a catechumen, instructed perhaps even in saving doctrine above many of the faithful. It is to be feared lest this man say within himself, concerning holy Baptism by which sins are forgiven: What more am I going to receive? See, I am better than this believer and that believer: thinking of the faithful who are either married, or perhaps unlearned, or who have and possess their own goods, which he himself has already distributed to the poor, and reckoning himself better than the one already baptized, he might disdain to come to Baptism, saying: Am I going to receive this, which this man and that man have? And he might set before himself those whom he despises, and, as it were, it might seem beneath him to receive what his inferiors received, because he now seems to himself better: and yet all his sins are upon him, and unless he comes to saving Baptism, where sins are loosed, he cannot, with all his excellence, enter into the kingdom of heaven.

But that he might invite that excellence to his baptism, that sins might be forgiven it, the Lord himself came to the baptism of his servant; and although he himself had nothing that should be forgiven him, nor anything that should be washed away in him, he undertook baptism from a servant; and, as it were, he addressed the proud son, exalting himself and disdaining perhaps to receive, along with the unlearned, that from which salvation could come to him, and as if saying: How far do you stretch yourself? How far do you exalt yourself? How great is your excellence? How great your grace? Can it be greater than mine? If I came to a servant, do you disdain to come to the Lord? If I undertook the baptism of a servant, do you disdain to be baptized by the Lord?

**14.** For that you may know, my brothers, that not out of any necessity of a bond of sin the Lord was coming to John; as the other evangelists say, when the Lord came to him to be baptized, John himself said: *Do you come to me? I ought to be baptized by you* [^29]. And what did he himself answer him? *Allow it for now; let all righteousness be fulfilled* [^30]. What is, *let all righteousness be fulfilled*? I came to die for men, do I not have to be baptized for men? What is, let all righteousness be fulfilled? Let all humility be fulfilled. What then? Was he not going to undertake baptism from a good servant, who undertook his passion from bad servants? Attend, then. The Lord being baptized, if John baptized for this reason, that in his baptism the Lord might show humility, would no one else be baptized with the baptism of John? But many were baptized with the baptism of John; the Lord was baptized with the baptism of John, and the baptism of John ceased: thereafter John was sent into prison; afterward no one is found baptized with that baptism.

If, then, John too came baptizing for this reason, that the humility of the Lord might be shown to us, so that, because he undertook it from a servant, we should not disdain to undertake it from the Lord; would John baptize the Lord alone? But if John baptized the Lord alone, there would be no lack of those who would think the baptism of John holier than that of Christ: as if Christ alone had merited to be baptized with the baptism of John, but with the baptism of Christ the human race. Let your Charity attend. With the baptism of Christ we have been baptized, not only we, but also the whole world, and it is baptized until the end. Which of us can in any part be compared to Christ, the strap of whose sandal John said he was unworthy to loosen? If, then, that Christ, a man of such great excellence, God, alone were baptized with the baptism of John, what would men be going to say? What kind of baptism did John have? He had a great baptism, an ineffable sacrament: see, only Christ merited to be baptized with the baptism of John. And so the baptism of the servant would seem greater than the baptism of the Lord. Others too were baptized with the baptism of John, lest the baptism of John seem better than that of Christ; but the Lord too was baptized, so that, the Lord undertaking the baptism of a servant, other servants should not disdain to undertake the baptism of the Lord: for this, then, John had been sent.

**15.** But did he know Christ, or did he not know him? If he did not know him, why did he say, when Christ came to the river: *I ought to be baptized by you*? that is: I know who you are. If, then, he already knew him, surely he then recognized him when he saw the dove descending. It is plain that the dove did not descend upon the Lord except after he ascended from the water of baptism. The Lord, being baptized, ascended from the water, the heavens were opened, and he saw the dove upon him. If, then, after the baptism the dove descended, and before the Lord was baptized John said to him: *Do you come to me? I ought to be baptized by you?*—before, he knew the one to whom he said: *Do you come to me? I ought to be baptized by you?*—how then did he say: *And I did not know him; but he who sent me to baptize in water, he said to me: He upon whom you see the Spirit descending like a dove, and remaining upon him, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit* [^31]?

It is no small question, my brothers. If you have seen the question, you have seen no small thing: it remains for the Lord himself to give the solution of it. Yet this I say: if you have seen the question, it is no small thing. See, John has been placed before your eyes, John the Baptist standing at the river; see, the Lord comes, still to be baptized, not yet baptized: hear the voice of John: *Do you come to me? I ought to be baptized by you*; see, he now recognizes the Lord, by whom he wishes to be baptized. The Lord, baptized, ascends from the water, the heavens are opened, the Spirit descends, now John recognizes him: if he recognizes him only now, why did he say before, *I ought to be baptized by you*? But if he does not recognize him only now, because he already knew him, what is it that he said: *I did not know him; but he who sent me to baptize in water, he said to me: He upon whom you see the Spirit descending, and remaining upon him like a dove, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit?*

## Seek among yourselves and question others.

**16.** Brothers, if this question were solved today, it would burden you, I do not doubt, because much has already been said. But know that this question is such that this alone destroys the party of Donatus. I have said this to your Charity in order to make you attentive, as I usually do; and at the same time that you may pray for us and for yourselves, that the Lord may grant us to speak worthy things, and that you may merit to grasp worthy things. For now, deign to put it off today.

But this I say briefly for now, until it is solved: question peacefully, without quarrel, without contention, without altercations, without enmities; and seek among yourselves, and question others, and say: This question our bishop proposed to us today, intending to solve it sometime, if the Lord grant it. But whether it is solved or not solved, suppose that I have proposed what moves me: for I am moved greatly.

John says: *I ought to be baptized by you*; as though Christ were known to him. For if he did not know him by whom he wished to be baptized, he was speaking rashly: *I ought to be baptized by you*. He knew him, then. If he knew him, what then is it that he says: *I did not know him; but he who sent me to baptize in water, he himself said to me: He upon whom you see the Spirit descending and remaining upon him, like a dove, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*? What shall we say? That we do not know when the dove came? Lest perhaps they hide there, let the other evangelists be read, who said it more plainly: and we find most openly that the dove descended then, when the Lord ascended from the water. For over the baptized one the heavens were opened, and he saw the Spirit descending. If he recognized him already baptized, how does he say to him as he comes to baptism: *I ought to be baptized by you*?

Chew this over among yourselves for now, confer about it among yourselves, treat it among yourselves. May the Lord our God grant that, before you hear it from me, he may reveal it to one of you first. Yet, brothers, know this, that through the solution of this question the party of Donatus will have no voice at all concerning the grace of Baptism—where they spread mists before the unskilled, and stretch nets for flying birds—if they have any shame: their mouths will be entirely shut.
