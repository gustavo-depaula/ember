# Homily 5

## *I did not know him . . .* [^1]

## That an example of humility might be given us by the Lord, for receiving the salvation of baptism, Christ undertook what he had no need of, but which for our sake was needed.

**1.** As the Lord willed, we have arrived at the day of our promise: he will also grant this, that we may be able to arrive at the rendering of the promise itself. For then the things we say, if they are useful both to us and to you, are from him: but the things that are from man are lies; as our Lord Jesus Christ himself said: *He who speaks a lie, speaks from his own* [^2]. No one has of his own except a lie and sin. But if a man has anything of truth and righteousness, it is from that fountain which we ought to thirst for in this desert, so that, bedewed by it as with certain drops, and meanwhile consoled in this pilgrimage, lest we faint on the way, we may be able to come to its rest and satisfaction.

If, then, *he who speaks a lie, speaks from his own*; he who speaks the truth, speaks from God. John is truthful, Christ is the truth; John is truthful, but everyone truthful is truthful from the truth: if, then, John is truthful, and a man cannot be truthful except from the truth; from whom was he truthful, except from him who said: *I am the truth* [^3]? Therefore neither could the truth speak against the truthful one, nor the truthful one against the truth. The truth sent the truthful one; and therefore he was truthful, because he was sent by the truth.

If the truth had sent John, Christ had sent him. But what Christ does with the Father, the Father does: and what the Father does with Christ, Christ does. Neither does the Father do anything separately without the Son; nor the Son anything separately without the Father: inseparable love, inseparable unity, inseparable majesty, inseparable power, according to these words which he himself set down: *I and the Father are one* [^4]. Who, then, sent John? If we say, the Father, we say true; if we say, the Son, we say true: but to say it more plainly, the Father and the Son. But the one whom the Father and the Son sent, one God sent; because the Son said: *I and the Father are one*.

How, then, did he not know the one by whom he was sent? For he said: *I did not know him; but he who sent me to baptize in water, he himself said to me* [^5].

I question John: He who sent you to baptize in water, what did he say to you?

*He upon whom you see the Spirit descending, like a dove, and remaining upon him, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit* [^6].

Did he who sent you, O John, say this to you?

Plainly he did.

Who, then, sent you?

Perhaps the Father.

The Father is true God, and the Son, God, is the truth: if the Father sent you without the Son, God sent you without the truth: but if you are truthful for this reason, that you speak the truth, and speak from the truth; the Father did not send you without the Son, but the Father and the Son sent you together: if, then, the Son too sent you with the Father, how did you not know him by whom you were sent? The one whom you had seen in the truth, he himself sent you that he might be recognized in the flesh, and said: *He upon whom you see the Spirit descending like a dove, and remaining upon him, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit* [^7].

**2.** Did John hear this, that he might know the one whom he did not know, or that he might more fully know the one whom he already knew? For if in every respect he did not know him, he would not say to him, as he came to the river to be baptized: *I ought to be baptized by you, and do you come to me?* He knew him, then. But when did the dove descend? When the Lord was already baptized, and ascending from the water.

But if he who sent him said: *He upon whom you see the Spirit descending like a dove, and remaining upon him, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*; and he did not know him, but recognized him when the dove descended; but the dove descended then, when the Lord ascended from the water; and yet John had recognized the Lord when the Lord was coming to him at the water: it is made manifest to us that in some respect John knew the Lord, in some respect he did not yet know him. But unless we understand this, he was a liar.

How was he truthful in recognizing him, who says: *Do you come to me* to be baptized, and *I ought to be baptized by you*? Is he truthful when he says this? And how, again, is he truthful when he says: *I did not know him; but he who sent me to baptize in water, he himself said to me: He upon whom you see the Spirit descending like a dove, and remaining upon him, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*? The Lord became known through the dove, not to him who did not know him, but to him who in him knew something, did not know something. It is for us, then, to seek what John did not yet know in him, and learned through the dove.

## Humility was to be fulfilled by him.

**3.** Why was John sent baptizing? I remember that I have already said to your Charity what I could. For if the baptism of John were necessary to our salvation, it ought to be practiced even now. For it is not the case that men are not saved now, or that more are not saved now, or that there was one salvation then and another now. If Christ has been changed, salvation too has been changed: if salvation is in Christ, and Christ himself is the same, salvation is the same for us.

But why was John sent baptizing?

Because it was fitting that Christ should be baptized.

Why was it fitting that Christ should be baptized?

Why was it fitting that Christ should be born? Why was it fitting that Christ should be crucified? For if he had come to demonstrate the way of humility, and to make himself the very way of humility, in all things humility was to be fulfilled by him. He deigned to give authority to his own baptism from this, that servants might recognize with what eagerness they ought to run to the baptism of the Lord, since he himself did not disdain to undertake the baptism of a servant. For this was granted to John, that the baptism should be called his own.

**4.** Let your Charity attend to this, and distinguish, and know it. The baptism that John received was called the baptism of John: he alone received such a gift: no one before him among the righteous, no one after him, received a baptism that should be called his own baptism. He received it, indeed; for of himself he could do nothing: for if anyone speaks from himself, he speaks a lie from his own. And from where did he receive it, except from the Lord Jesus Christ?

From him he received the power to baptize, whom he afterward baptized. Do not marvel: for Christ did this in John just as he did a certain thing in his mother. For of Christ it was said: *All things were made through him* [^8]: if all things through him, then Mary too was made through him, of whom Christ was afterward born. Let your Charity attend: just as he created Mary, and was created through Mary; so he gave baptism to John, and was baptized by John.

**5.** For this reason, then, he received baptism from John, that, receiving from an inferior what was inferior, he might exhort his inferiors to that which was superior. But why was he not the only one baptized by John, if John had been sent for this, by whom Christ might be baptized, that he might *prepare the way of the Lord* [^9], that is, of Christ himself? This too we have already said; but we recall it, because it is necessary to the present question. If our Lord Jesus Christ alone had been baptized with the baptism of John—hold to what we say; let the world not have so much power as to delete from your hearts what the Spirit of God wrote there; let the thorns of cares not have so much power as to choke the seed that is sown in you: for why are we compelled to repeat the same things, except that we are not secure about the memory of your heart?—if, then, the Lord alone had been baptized with the baptism of John, there would be no lack of those who would so regard it as to think the baptism of John greater than the baptism of Christ. For they would say: That baptism is so far greater that Christ alone merited to be baptized with it.

Therefore, that an example of humility might be given us by the Lord, for receiving the salvation of baptism, Christ undertook what he had no need of, but which for our sake was needed. And again, lest this very thing that Christ received from John should be preferred to the baptism of Christ, others too were permitted to be baptized by John. But for those who were baptized by John, it did not suffice them: for they were baptized with the baptism of Christ; because the baptism of John was not the baptism of Christ. Those who receive the baptism of Christ do not seek the baptism of John: those who received the baptism of John sought the baptism of Christ.

Therefore the baptism of John sufficed for Christ. How would it not suffice, when it was not even necessary for him? For no baptism was necessary for him; but to exhort us to his own baptism, he undertook the baptism of a servant. And lest the baptism of a servant should be preferred to the baptism of the Lord, others were baptized with the baptism of a fellow servant. But those who were baptized with the baptism of a fellow servant, it was fitting that they should be baptized with the baptism of the Lord: but those who are baptized with the baptism of the Lord have no need of the baptism of a fellow servant.

## Ministry and power.

**6.** Since, then, John had received a baptism that should properly be called John's; but the Lord Jesus Christ was unwilling to give his own baptism to anyone, not so that no one should be baptized with the baptism of the Lord, but so that the Lord himself should always baptize: this was brought about, that the Lord should baptize even through ministers—that is, that those whom the Lord's ministers were going to baptize, the Lord should baptize, not they. For it is one thing to baptize through ministry, another to baptize through power.

For baptism is such as is the one in whose power it is given; not such as is the one through whose ministry it is given. The baptism of John was such as John was: a righteous baptism, as of a righteous man, yet of a man; but one who had received from the Lord this grace, and so great a grace, that he was worthy to go before the judge, and to point him out with the finger, and to fulfill the voice of that prophecy: *The voice of one crying in the wilderness, prepare the way of the Lord* [^10]. But the baptism of the Lord is such as the Lord is: therefore the baptism of the Lord is divine, because the Lord is God.

**7.** But the Lord Jesus Christ could, if he willed, give to some servant of his the power to give his baptism, as it were in his stead, and transfer from himself the power of baptizing, and establish it in some servant of his, and give to the baptism transferred into a servant as great a force as the baptism given by the Lord would have. He was unwilling to do this, for this reason, that in him the hope of the baptized might rest, by whom they would recognize themselves as baptized. He was unwilling, then, that a servant should place hope in a servant. And therefore the Apostle cried out, when he saw men wishing to place hope in himself: *Was Paul crucified for you? or were you baptized in the name of Paul?* [^11] Paul, then, baptized as a minister, not as the power itself: but the Lord baptized as the power.

Attend. He could also give this power to servants, and was unwilling. For if he gave this power to servants—that is, that what was the Lord's should be theirs—there would be as many baptisms as there were servants; so that, as it was called the baptism of John, so it would be called the baptism of Peter, so the baptism of Paul, so the baptism of James, the baptism of Thomas, of Matthew, of Bartholomew: for that baptism was called the baptism of John. But perhaps someone resists, and says: Prove to us that that baptism was called the baptism of John. I will prove it, the Truth himself saying it, when he questioned the Jews: *The baptism of John, from where is it? from heaven, or from men?* [^12] Therefore, lest there should be called as many baptisms as there were servants who baptized after receiving power from the Lord, the Lord kept the power of baptizing for himself, and gave the ministry to servants. A servant says that he baptizes; he says it rightly, as the Apostle says: *I baptized also the household of Stephanas* [^13]: but as a minister. Therefore, if he is even bad, and it happens that he has the ministry, and if men do not know him, yet God knows him: God permits baptism to be given through him, who kept the power for himself.

**8.** But this John did not know in the Lord. That he was the Lord, he knew; that he ought to be baptized by him, he knew: and he confessed that he was the truth, and that he himself was truthful, sent by the truth; this he knew. But what did he not know in him? That he was going to keep for himself the power of his baptism, and not transmit and transfer it into any servant: but that, whether a good servant baptized in ministry, or a bad servant baptized in ministry, the one who was baptized would not know himself to be baptized, except by the one who kept the power of baptizing for himself.

And that you may know, brothers, that John did not know this in him, and learned it through the dove: for he knew the Lord, but that he was going to keep the power of baptizing for himself, and give it to no servant, he did not yet know; according to this he said: *And I did not know him*. And that you may know that he learned this there, attend to what follows: *But he who sent me to baptize in water, he himself said to me: He upon whom you see the Spirit descending, as a dove, and remaining upon him, he it is*.

He it is—what?

The Lord.

But he already knew the Lord. Suppose, then, that John said up to this point: *I did not know him; but he who sent me to baptize in water, he himself said to me*.

We ask, what did he say?

It follows: *He upon whom you see the Spirit descending, as a dove, and remaining upon him*. I do not say the words that follow; for now attend:

*He upon whom you see the Spirit descending, like a dove, and remaining upon him, he it is*.

But he it is—what? What did the one who sent me wish to teach me through the dove? That he was the Lord? I already knew the one by whom I had been sent: I already knew the one to whom I said: *Do you come to me* to be baptized? *I ought to be baptized by you*: so far, then, did I know the Lord, that I wished to be baptized by him, not that he should be baptized by me: and then he said to me: *Allow it for now; let all righteousness be fulfilled*; I came to suffer, do I not come to be baptized? *Let all righteousness be fulfilled*, my God says to me; let all righteousness be fulfilled, let me teach full humility: I know the proud in my future people, I know that there will be certain men in some more excellent grace, so that, when they see certain unlearned people baptized, they—because they seem to themselves better, whether in continence, or in almsgiving, or in doctrine—may disdain perhaps to receive what those inferiors received; it is fitting that I heal them, that they may not disdain to come to the baptism of the Lord, because I came to the baptism of a servant.

## None of the apostles said: my baptism.

**9.** So then John already knew this, and knew the Lord. What, then, did the dove teach? What did he who had sent him, to whom he says, *He upon whom you see the Spirit descending, as a dove, and remaining upon him, he it is*, wish to teach through the dove—that is, through the Holy Spirit coming thus?

Who is he?

The Lord.

I know.

But did you already know this, that this Lord, having the power of baptizing, would give that power to no servant, but would keep it for himself, so that everyone who is baptized through the ministry of a servant should attribute it not to the servant, but to the Lord? Did you already know this?

I did not know this: indeed, what did he say to me? *He upon whom you see the Spirit descending as a dove, and remaining upon him, he it is who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*.

He does not say: He it is who is the Lord; he does not say, he it is who is Christ; he does not say, he it is who is God; he does not say, he it is who is Jesus; he does not say, he it is who was born of the Virgin Mary, later than you, before you: he does not say this; for John already knew this. But what did he not know? That this same Lord was going to have so great a power of Baptism and keep it for himself, whether present on earth, or absent in body in heaven and present in majesty, that he would keep the power of Baptism for himself: lest Paul say, My baptism; lest Peter say, My baptism. Therefore see, attend to the words of the Apostles. None of the Apostles said: My baptism. Although the Gospel of all was one, yet you find them say: My gospel: you do not find them say: My baptism.

**10.** This, then, John learned, my brothers. What John learned through the dove, let us too learn. For the dove did not teach John, and not teach the Church, to which Church it was said: *My dove is one* [^14]. Let the dove teach the dove; let the dove know what John learned through the dove. The Holy Spirit descended in the form of a dove. But this that John was learning in the dove, why did he learn it in a dove? For it was fitting that he should learn it: and perhaps it was not even fitting except that he should learn it through a dove.

What shall I say about the dove, my brothers? Or when is the capacity of either heart or tongue sufficient for me to say it as I wish? And perhaps I do not even wish it worthily, as it ought to be said; and even if I can say it as I wish: how much less as it ought to be said? I would rather hear this from a better man than say it to you.

**11.** John learns him whom he knew: but he learns in him that in which he did not know him; in that in which he knew him he does not learn. And what did he know? The Lord. What did he not know? That the power of the Lord's baptism would pass from the Lord into no man, but that the ministry would plainly pass: the power from the Lord into no one, the ministry into both good and bad. Let the dove not shudder at the ministry of the bad, let it look to the power of the Lord. What does a bad minister do to you, where the Lord is good? What does a malicious herald hinder you, if the judge is benevolent? John learned this through the dove. What is it that he learned? Let him himself repeat it: *He himself said to me*, he says: *He upon whom you see the Spirit descending, as a dove, and remaining upon him, this is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*. Therefore let not the seducers deceive you, O dove, who say: We baptize. O dove, recognize what the dove taught. This is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit. Through the dove it is learned that this is he; and do you think you are baptized by the power of the one by whose ministry you are baptized? If you think this, you are not yet in the body of the dove; and if you are not in the body of the dove, it is no wonder that you do not have simplicity. For simplicity is especially designated through the dove.

**12.** Why did John learn, through the simplicity of the dove, that *this is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*, my brothers, except that those who scattered the Church were not doves? They were hawks, they were kites. The dove does not tear. And you see them stir up ill will against us, as if over the persecutions they suffered. They suffered, indeed, bodily persecutions, as it were, when they were the scourges of the Lord plainly giving discipline for a time, lest he condemn forever, if they did not recognize it and correct themselves.

But those persecute the Church who persecute by deceit; those wound the heart more grievously who wound with the sword of the tongue; those shed blood more bitterly who slay Christ, as far as in them lies, in a man. They seem terrified, as it were at the judgment of the authorities. What does the authority do to you, if you are good? But if you are bad, fear the authority: *For it does not bear the sword in vain* [^15], says the Apostle. Do not draw your own sword, with which you strike Christ. Christian, why do you persecute in a Christian? What did the emperor persecute in you? He persecuted the flesh; you persecute the spirit in a Christian. You do not slay the flesh. And yet they do not spare the flesh either: as many as they could, they killed by striking; they spared neither their own nor others. This is known to all. The authority stirs up ill will, because it is lawful: he acts in a way that stirs up ill will who acts by right: he acts without stirring up ill will who acts outside the laws.

Let each one of you attend, my brothers, to what a Christian has. That he is a man, he has in common with many: that he is a Christian, sets him apart from many; and what he is as a Christian belongs to him more than what he is as a man. For in that he is a Christian, he is renewed to the image of God, by whom man was made to the image of God: but in that he is a man, even a bad man could be one, even a pagan, even an idolater.

This you persecute in a Christian, the better thing he has: for you wish to take from him that by which he lives. For he lives temporally according to the spirit of life by which the body is animated; but he lives unto eternity according to the Baptism which he received from the Lord: this you wish to take from him, which he received from the Lord; this you wish to take from him, that by which he lives. Robbers wish to despoil those whom they wish to despoil in such a way that they themselves may have more, and the others have nothing: you both take from this man, and there will be no more with you; for you do not gain more, because you take from this man. But truly they do what those do who take away the soul; they both take from another, and they themselves do not have two souls.

## This is he who baptizes.

**13.** What, then, do you wish to take away? From where does the one whom you wish to rebaptize displease you? You cannot give what he already has, but you make him deny what he has. What did the pagan, the persecutor of the Church, do more bitterly? Swords were drawn against the martyrs, beasts were let loose, fires were brought near: why these things? That the one who suffered them might say: I am not a Christian. What do you teach the one whom you wish to rebaptize, except that he should first say: I am not a Christian? To which the persecutor once held out the flame, to this you bring forth the tongue: by seducing you do what he did not do by killing.

And what is it that you are going to give, and to whom are you going to give it? If he tells you the truth, and, not seduced by you, does not lie, he will say: I have it.

You ask: Do you have Baptism?

I have it, he says.

As long as you say 'I have it,' you say, I am not going to give it.

Then do not give it: for what you wish to give cannot cling to me; because what I have received cannot be taken from me. But yet wait; let me see what you wish to teach me.

Say, he says, first: I do not have it.

But this I have: if I say, I do not have it, I lie; for what I have, I have.

You do not have it, he says.

Teach me that I do not have it.

A bad man gave it to you.

If Christ is bad, a bad one gave it to me.

No, he says, Christ is not bad: but Christ did not give it to you.

Who, then, gave it to me? Answer: I know that I received it from Christ.

He gave it to you, he says, but some traitor, I know not who, not Christ.

I will look at who the minister was, I will look at who the herald was; I do not dispute about the officer, I attend to the judge: and perhaps what you charge against the officer, you lie: but I do not wish to discuss it; let the Lord of both take cognizance of the case of his officer: perhaps, if I require you to prove it, you do not prove it; rather, you lie: it has been proved that you could not prove it:

but I do not place my case there, lest, when I begin earnestly to defend innocent men, you should think that I had placed my hope in innocent men: let the men have been whatever sort they were, I received from Christ, I was baptized by Christ.

No, he says, but that bishop baptized you, and that bishop is in communion with those people.

I was baptized by Christ, I know it.

From where do you know? The dove that John saw taught me. O wicked kite, do not tear me from the bowels of the dove: I am numbered among the members of the dove; because what the dove taught, this I know. You say to me: This man baptized you, or that man baptized you; through the dove it is said to me and to you: *This is he who baptizes*: whom do I believe, the kite, or the dove?

**14.** Just tell me, that you may be confounded through that lamp, by which the earlier enemies too were confounded, your equals the Pharisees, who, when they questioned the Lord by what authority he did these things:

*I too will ask you*, he says, *this question: Tell me: The baptism of John, from where is it? from heaven or from men?* And those who were preparing to hurl their deceits were ensnared by the question, and began to turn it over among themselves, and to say: *If we say that it is from heaven, he will say to us: Why did you not believe him?* For John had said of the Lord: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sin of the world* [^16]. Why, then, do you ask by what authority I do it? O wolves, by the authority of the Lamb I do what I do. But that you might know the Lamb, why did you not believe John, who said: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sin of the world?* Knowing, then, what John had said of the Lord, they said among themselves: *If we say that the baptism of John is from heaven, he will say to us: Why then did you not believe him? If we say that it is from men, we will be stoned by the people; for they hold John as a prophet* [^17]. On the one side they feared men, on the other they were confounded to confess the truth. Darkness answered darkness, but they were overcome by the light. For what did they answer? *We do not know* [^18]: what they knew, they said, *we do not know*. And the Lord, *neither do I tell you*, he says, *by what authority I do these things* [^19].

And the first enemies were confounded. From what? From the lamp. Who was the lamp? John. Do we prove that he was a lamp? We prove it. For the Lord says: *He was a burning and shining lamp* [^20]. Do we prove that the enemies were confounded over him too? Hear the Psalm: *I have prepared*, he says, *a lamp for my Christ; his enemies I will clothe with confusion* [^21]. The gift of Christ is not defiled.

**15.** As long as we walk in the darkness of this life by the lamp of faith, let us too hold to the lamp John, and let us through it too confound the enemies of Christ; rather, let him himself confound his enemies through his lamp. Let us too ask what the Lord asked the Jews, let us ask and say: *The baptism of John, from where is it? from heaven, or from men?* See what they are going to say, whether they too, like enemies, are not confounded over the lamp. What are they going to say? If they say: From men; their own people too will stone them: but if they say: From heaven; let us say to them: *Why then did you not believe him?*

They say perhaps: We believe him.

How, then, do you say that you baptize, and John says: *This is he who baptizes?*

But the ministers, they say, of so great a judge, through whom one is baptized, ought to be righteous.

And I say, and we all say, that the ministers of so great a judge ought to be righteous: let the ministers be righteous, if they will; but if those who sit in the seat of Moses are unwilling to be righteous, my master has made me secure, of whom his Spirit said: This is he who baptizes. How has he made me secure? *The scribes*, he says, *and the Pharisees sit in the seat of Moses; what they say, do; but what they do, do not do: for they say, and do not* [^22].

If the minister is righteous, I count him with Paul, I count him with Peter; with these I count righteous ministers: because truly righteous ministers do not seek their own glory; for they are ministers, they do not wish to be held as judges, they shudder at hope being placed in them: therefore I count a righteous minister with Paul. For what does Paul say? *I planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase: neither is he who plants anything, nor he who waters; but God who gives the increase* [^23].

But the minister who is proud is counted with the devil: yet the gift of Christ is not defiled, which flows pure through him, which passes through him clear and comes to fertile ground. Suppose that he himself is stony, that out of water he can bear no fruit: yet water passes through the stony channel, the water passes to the garden beds; in the stony channel it generates nothing, but yet it brings very much fruit to the gardens.

For the spiritual power of the Sacrament is like the light: it is received pure by those to be enlightened, and if it passes through the unclean, it is not soiled. Let the ministers plainly be righteous, and not seek their own glory, but the glory of him whose ministers they are: let them not say: The baptism is mine; because it is not theirs. Let them attend to John himself. See, John was full of the Holy Spirit; and he had a baptism from heaven, not from men: but to what extent did he have it? He himself said: *Prepare the way of the Lord* [^24]. But when the Lord was known, he himself was made the way; there was no longer need of the baptism of John, by which the way of the Lord might be prepared.

**16.** Yet what are they accustomed to say to us? See, after John there was baptizing. For before this question was well treated in the Catholic Church, many erred in it, even great and good men: but because they were of the members of the dove, they did not cut themselves off, and there was fulfilled in them what the Apostle said: *If you think otherwise in anything, this too God will reveal to you* [^25]. From this those who separated themselves were made unteachable. What, then, are they accustomed to say? See, after John there was baptizing; after heretics is there no baptizing? Because certain men who had the baptism of John were ordered by Paul to be baptized: for they did not have the baptism of Christ [^26]. Why, then, do you exaggerate the merit of John, and, as it were, cast away the unhappiness of heretics? I too grant you that heretics are wicked: but the heretics gave the baptism of Christ, which baptism John did not give.

**17.** I return to John, and say: *This is he who baptizes*. For John is better than a heretic in the same way that John is better than a drunkard, in the same way that John is better than a murderer. If we ought to baptize after a worse man, because the Apostles baptized after a better man; whoever among them shall have been baptized by a drunkard—I do not say by a murderer, I do not say by the henchman of some criminal, I do not say by a plunderer of others' goods, I do not say by an oppressor of orphans, not by a separator of married couples; I say none of these: I say what is customary, I say what is daily, I say that by which all are summoned, even in this city, when it is said to them: Let us feast, may it be well with us, and on such a feast day of the Kalends of January you ought not to fast; I say those light, daily things: when one is baptized by a drunken man, who is better: John or the drunkard? Answer, if you can, that your drunkard is better than John: you will never dare this. Therefore you, because you are sober, baptize after your drunkard. For if the Apostles baptized after John, how much more ought a sober man to baptize after a drunkard? Or do you say: The drunkard is in unity with me? Then was John, the friend of the bridegroom, not in unity with the bridegroom?

**18.** But I say to you yourself, whoever you are: Are you better, or John? You will not dare to say: I am better than John. Therefore let your people baptize after you, if they are better than you. For if there was baptizing after John, be ashamed that there is no baptizing after you. You are going to say: But I have the baptism of Christ, and I teach it.

At some point, then, acknowledge the judge, and do not be a proud herald. You give the baptism of Christ, therefore there is no baptizing afterward: after John there was baptizing for this reason, that he gave not the baptism of Christ, but his own; because he had so received it that it should be his. Therefore you are not better than John: but the baptism that is given through you is better than that of John. For it is Christ's, but that one was John's.

And what was given by Paul, and what was given by Peter, is Christ's: and if it was given by Judas, it was Christ's. Judas gave it, and there was no baptizing after Judas; John gave it, and there was baptizing after John: because if baptism was given by Judas, it was Christ's; but that which was given by John was John's. Not Judas to John, but the baptism of Christ given even through the hands of Judas, we rightly prefer to the baptism of John given even through the hands of John. For indeed it was said of the Lord, before he suffered, that he baptized more than John; then it was added: *Although he himself did not baptize, but his disciples* [^27]. He himself, and not he himself: he himself by power, they by ministry; they applied servitude to baptizing, the power of baptizing remained in Christ.

Therefore his disciples baptized, and Judas was still there among his disciples: whom, then, Judas baptized, were they not baptized again; and whom John baptized, were they baptized again? Plainly again, but not with a repeated baptism. For those whom John baptized, John baptized: but those whom Judas baptized, Christ baptized. So then those whom a drunkard baptized, those whom a murderer baptized, those whom an adulterer baptized, if it was the baptism of Christ, Christ baptized. I do not fear the adulterer, nor the drunkard, nor the murderer; because I attend to the dove, through which it is said to me: *This is he who baptizes*.

**19.** Moreover, my brothers, it is madness to say that he was of better merit—I do not say Judas, but any of men—than the one of whom it was said: *Among those born of women none has arisen greater than John the Baptist* [^28]. Therefore not any servant is preferred to this man, but the baptism of the Lord, given even through a bad servant, is preferred to the baptism even of the friend who was a servant. Hear what sort of false brothers the apostle Paul commemorates, preaching the word of God out of envy, and what he says of them: *And in this I rejoice, yes, and I will rejoice* [^29]. For they announced Christ, out of envy indeed, but yet Christ. Look not at the means, but at whom: is Christ preached to you out of envy? Look at Christ, avoid envy. Do not imitate the bad preacher, but imitate the good one who is preached to you.

Christ, then, was preached by some out of envy. And what is it to envy? A dreadful evil. By this very evil the devil was cast down, this most malignant plague cast down many: and certain preachers of Christ had it, whom nevertheless the Apostle permits to preach. Why? Because they preached Christ. But he who envies, hates; and he who hates, what is said of him? Hear the apostle John: *He who hates his brother is a murderer* [^30]. See, after John there was baptizing, after a murderer there was no baptizing; because John gave his own baptism, the murderer gave the baptism of Christ. Which Sacrament is so holy that it is not polluted even when a murderer ministers it.

**20.** I do not reject John, but rather I believe John. What do I believe John? What he learned through the dove. What did he learn through the dove? *This is he who baptizes in the Holy Spirit*. Now then, brothers, hold this, and fix it in your hearts. For if today I should wish to say more fully why through a dove, time would not suffice. For because the thing to be learned was imparted to John through the dove—the thing that John did not know in Christ, although he already knew Christ—I have set forth, as far as I judge, to your Holiness: but why this very thing had to be shown through a dove, if it could be said briefly, I would say it: but because it must be said at length, and I do not wish to burden you, then, just as I have been helped by your prayers to fulfill what I promised; with the further help, again and again, of your devout attention and good wishes, that too will become clear to you, why John, what he learned in the Lord—that he himself is the one who baptizes in the Holy Spirit, and transferred the power of baptizing to none of his servants—ought not to learn except through the dove.
