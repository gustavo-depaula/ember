# Tractate 35

## *And if I bear witness of myself, my witness is true* (Jn 8:13-14).

The Wisdom of God, the Word of God, the Lord Jesus Christ is everywhere present; because everywhere is truth, everywhere is wisdom. But he came such that a lamp had to bear witness to him. The lamps, that is, the prophecies, bear witness to the day on account of our weakness, because we cannot bear and see the brightness of the day.

**1.** Of the words of our Lord Jesus Christ, where he says: I am the light of the world: he who follows me will not walk in darkness, but will have the light of life[^1], you who were present yesterday remember that there was a long discussion: and if we should still wish to discuss that light, we can speak long; for we cannot explain it briefly. And so, my brothers, let us follow Christ, the light of the world, that we may not walk in darkness. Darkness is to be feared—the darkness of morals, not of the eyes: and if of the eyes, not of the outer, but of the inner, by which not white and black is distinguished, but the just and the unjust.

**2.** When, then, our Lord Jesus Christ had said these things, the Jews answered: *You speak witness of yourself; your witness is not true*[^2]. Before our Lord Jesus Christ came, he kindled and sent before himself many prophetic lamps. Among these too was John the Baptist, to whom that great Light itself, who is the Lord Christ, bore such witness as to no man: for he says: *Among those born of women there has not arisen one greater than John the Baptist*[^3]. Yet this man, than whom no one was greater among those born of women, says of the Lord Jesus Christ: *I indeed baptize you in water; but he who comes is mightier than I, whose sandal I am not worthy to loose*[^4].

See how the lamp submits itself to the day. That John himself was a lamp, the Lord himself attests: *He was*, he says, *a burning and shining lamp; and you wished for an hour to exult in his light*[^5]. But when the Jews said to the Lord: *Tell us, by what power you do these things*[^6], the Lord, knowing that they held John the Baptist as great, and that he whom they held as great had borne witness to them about the Lord, answered them: *I too will ask you one word: tell me, the baptism of John, whence is it? from heaven, or from men?*[^7] They, troubled, thought within themselves that if they said: From men, they could be stoned by the crowd, which believed John to be a prophet: if they said: From heaven, he would answer them: He whom you confess to have had his prophecy from heaven bore witness to me, and from him you have heard by what power I do these things. They saw, then, that whichever of these they answered, they would fall into the snare; and they said: We do not know. And the Lord to them: *Neither do I tell you by what power I do these things*[^8]. I do not tell you what I know, because you do not wish to confess what you know. Most justly repelled, they withdrew confounded: and what God the Father says in the Psalm through the Prophet was fulfilled: *I have prepared a lamp for my Christ*, that is, John himself; *I will clothe his enemies with confusion*[^9].

## The light that enlightens, and the light that is enlightened.

**3.** The Lord Jesus Christ, then, had the witness of the Prophets sent before him, the judge of the heralds who went before; he had witness from John: but he himself was the greater witness, which he bore to himself. But they with weak eyes sought lamps, because they could not bear the day: for John—the same apostle whose Gospel we have in hand—in the very head of his Gospel says of John: *There was a man sent from God, whose name was John: he came for witness, that he might bear witness of the light, that all might believe through him. He was not the light, but that he might bear witness of the light. It was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into the world*[^10].

If every man, then John too. Whence John himself also says: *We have all received of his fullness*[^11]. Distinguish these things, then, that your mind may make progress in the faith of Christ; lest you always be infants seeking the breast, and shrinking back from solid food. You ought, with mother Church, the holy Church of Christ, to be nourished and weaned, and to come to more solid foods, by mind, not by belly. Distinguish this, then: that the light which enlightens is one thing, and that which is enlightened is another. For our eyes too are called lights; and each one so swears, touching his eyes, by his lights: So may my lights live: it is a customary oath. But these lights, if they are lights, let the light be lacking in your closed chamber, let them open and shine for you: surely they cannot.

As, then, these lights which we have in our face, and call lights, even when they are sound and when they are open, need from without the help of light; which being taken away or not brought, they are sound, they are open, yet do not see: so our mind, which is the eye of the soul, unless it is irradiated by the light of truth, and wonderfully illumined by him who enlightens and is not enlightened, can come neither to wisdom nor to justice. For this is our way: to live justly. But how does one not stumble on a way for whom the light does not shine? And therefore on such a way to see is needful, on such a way to see is great. For Tobias had his eyes closed in his face, and the son gave his hand to the father; the father showed the way to the son by instructing him[^12].

## The light bears witness to itself.

**4.** The Jews, then, answered: *You speak witness of yourself; your witness is not true*[^13]. Let us see what they hear: let us too hear, but not as they did. They despising, we believing: they wishing to kill Christ, we desiring to live through Christ. Meanwhile let this difference distinguish our ears and minds, and let us hear what the Lord answered the Jews. *Jesus answered and said to them: Even if I bear witness of myself, my witness is true; because I know whence I came, and where I go*[^14]. The light shows both other things and itself. You light a lamp, for example, to seek a tunic, and the burning lamp enables you to find the tunic: do you light the lamp in order to see the burning lamp? For the burning lamp is fit both to lay bare other things that were covered by darkness, and to show itself to your eyes.

So too the Lord Christ distinguished both among his faithful and among his enemies the Jews, as between light and darkness; as between those whom he flooded with the ray of faith, and those whose closed eyes he surrounded. For even this sun illumines the face both of the seeing and of the blind: both standing alike, and having their face toward the sun, are illumined in the flesh, but not both are enlightened in their sight; the one sees, the other does not see; the sun is present to both, but to the present sun one is absent. So too the Wisdom of God, the Word of God, the Lord Jesus Christ is everywhere present; because everywhere is truth, everywhere is wisdom. One in the east understands justice; another in the west understands justice: is the justice that the one understands different from that which the other understands? They are separated in body, and have the sights of their minds upon one thing. The justice which I, set here, see, if it is justice, the just man sees, separated from me by I know not how many dwellings in the flesh, and joined to me in the light of that justice.

Therefore the light bears witness to itself: it opens sound eyes, and is a witness to itself, that the light may be known. But what do we do about the unbelievers? Is it not present to them? It is present to them too, but they do not have the eyes of the heart by which to see it. Hear about them the sentence brought forth from the Gospel itself: *And the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it*[^15]. The Lord, then, says, and says truly: Even if I bear witness of myself, my witness is true; because I know whence I came, and where I go. He wished the Father to be understood; the Son was giving glory to the Father. The equal glorifies him by whom he was sent: how much ought a man to glorify him by whom he was created?

**5.** I know whence I came, and where I go. This one who at present speaks to you has what he did not desert, yet came: for in coming he did not depart thence, nor in returning did he forsake us. Why do you marvel? He is God. This cannot be done by a man; this cannot be done even by the sun itself. When it goes to the west, it deserts the east, and until it is to rise it returns to the east, it is not in the east: but our Lord Jesus Christ both came, and is there; and returns, and is here.

Hear the Evangelist himself saying in another place, and if you can, grasp it; if you cannot, believe it: *No one*, he says, *has ever seen God, except the only-begotten Son, who is in the bosom of the Father; he has declared him*[^16]. He did not say: He was in the bosom of the Father, as though by coming he had deserted the bosom of the Father. Here he was speaking, and he said he was there: he who was also to depart from here, what did he say? *Behold, I am with you until the end of the world*[^17].

## The Lord came such that he needed a lamp.

**6.** Therefore the witness of the light is true, whether it shows itself or other things; because without light you cannot see light, and without light you cannot see anything else that is not light. If light is fit to show those things that are not lights, does it fail in itself? does it not open itself, without which other things cannot be open? The Prophet spoke truth; but whence would he have it, unless he drew from the fountain of truth? John spoke truth; but whence he spoke, ask him: *We have all*, he says, *received of his fullness*[^18]. Therefore our Lord Jesus Christ is fit to bear witness to himself.

But plainly, my brothers, in the night of this world let us also hear prophecy attentively: for now our Lord wished to come, humble, to our frailty and the inner nocturnal darkness of our heart. He came as a man to be despised and to be honored, to be denied and to be confessed; to be despised and denied by the Jews, to be honored and confessed by us: to be judged and to judge; to be judged unjustly, to judge justly. He came, then, such that a lamp had to bear witness to him. For what need was there that John as a lamp should bear witness to the day, if the day itself could be seen by our weakness? But we could not: he became weak for the weak, through weakness he healed weakness; through mortal flesh he took away the death of the flesh; from his own body he made an eye-salve for our lights. Because, then, the Lord came, and we are still in the night of the world, we must also hear the prophecies.

**7.** For from prophecy we convict the gainsaying pagans.

Who is Christ? says the pagan.

To whom we answer: He whom the Prophets foretold.

And he: What Prophets?

We recite Isaiah, Daniel, Jeremiah, the other holy Prophets; we say how long before him they came, by how much time they preceded his coming. This, then, we answer: The Prophets came before him, they foretold that he would come.

Some one of them answers: What Prophets?

We recite those who are recited to us daily.

And he: Who are these Prophets?

We answer: Those who also foretold the things that we see come to pass.

And he says: You invented these things for yourselves, you saw them come to pass, and as though they had been foretold as to come, you wrote them in the books you wished.

Here, against our enemies the pagans, the witness of other enemies comes to our aid. We bring forth the books from the Jews, and answer: Surely both you and they are enemies of our faith. For this reason they were scattered among the nations, that from some enemies we might convict others. Let the book of Isaiah be brought forth by the Jews, let us see whether I do not read there: *Like a sheep he was led to be sacrificed, and like a lamb before its shearer he was without voice, so he did not open his mouth. In humility his judgment was taken away; by his bruise we are healed; we all like sheep have gone astray, and he himself was handed over for our sins*[^19].

Behold one lamp. Let another be brought forth, let the Psalm be opened, let the Passion of Christ foretold there too be recited. *They have pierced my hands and my feet, they have numbered all my bones: but they themselves considered and gazed upon me, they divided my garments among them, and over my garment they cast lots. With you is my praise; in the great Church I will confess to you. All the ends of the earth will remember and be converted to the Lord: and all the families of the nations will adore in his sight; for the kingdom is the Lord's, and he will have dominion over the nations*[^20]. Let one enemy blush, because another enemy furnishes me the book.

But behold, from the books brought forth by one enemy I have conquered another: and let the very one who brought me the book not be left out; let it be brought forth by him, whence he too may be conquered. I read another prophet, and I find the Lord speaking to the Jews: *I have no pleasure in you, says the Lord, nor will I accept a sacrifice from your hands: for from the rising of the sun to its setting, a pure sacrifice is offered to my name*[^21]. You do not come, O Jew, to the pure sacrifice; I convict you of being unclean.

## The lamps bear witness to the day.

**8.** Behold, the lamps too bear witness to the day on account of our weakness, because we cannot bear and see the brightness of the day. For we ourselves too, Christians, in comparison with unbelievers are already light; whence the Apostle says: *For you were once darkness; but now you are light in the Lord, walk as children of light*[^22]: and elsewhere he said: *The night has gone before, but the day has drawn near: let us therefore cast off the works of darkness, and put on the armor of light; let us walk honorably as in the day*[^23].

Yet because, in comparison with that light to which we are to come, even the day in which we are is still night, hear the apostle Peter: he says that a voice was brought to the Lord Christ from the magnificent power: *You are my beloved Son, in whom I am well pleased. This voice*, he says, *we heard brought from heaven, when we were with him on the holy mountain*[^24]. But because we were not there, and did not then hear that voice from heaven; Peter himself says to us: *And we have the more sure prophetic word.* You did not hear the voice brought from heaven, but you have the more sure prophetic word.

For the Lord Jesus Christ, foreseeing that there would be certain impious men who would calumniate his miracles, attributing them to magic arts, sent the Prophets before. For surely, if he was a magician and by magic arts brought it about that he was worshiped even when dead, was he a magician before he was born? Hear the Prophets, O man dead and calumniously breeding worms, hear the Prophets: I read, hear those who came before the Lord. *We have*, says the apostle Peter, *the more sure prophetic word, to which you do well to attend, as to a lamp in a dark place, until the day dawns, and the morning star rises in your hearts*[^25].

## Let us feel that we are pilgrims here.

**9.** When, then, our Lord Jesus Christ has come, and, as the apostle Paul also says, *has enlightened the hidden things of darkness, and made manifest the thoughts of the heart, that praise may be to each one from God*[^26]; then, with such a day present, lamps will not be necessary: no Prophet will be read to us, no book of the Apostle will be opened, we will not require the witness of John, we will not need the Gospel itself. Therefore all the Scriptures will be removed from our midst, which in the night of this world were kindled for us like lamps, lest we remain in darkness: all these being taken away, lest they shine as for us who need them, and the men of God themselves, through whom these things were ministered, seeing with us that true and clear light—these helps, then, being removed, what shall we see? whence shall our mind be fed? whence shall that gaze rejoice? whence shall be that joy *which neither eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man*[^27]? what shall we see?

I beseech you, love with me, run by believing with me: let us desire the heavenly homeland, let us sigh for the heavenly homeland, let us feel that we are pilgrims here. What shall we then see? Let the Gospel now say: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^28]. Whence dew was sprinkled on you, you will come to the fountain: whence a ray was sent to you, through crooked and winding ways, to your darkened heart, you will see the bare light itself, for the seeing and bearing of which you are being cleansed. Beloved, what I recalled yesterday too, John himself says: *We are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be: we know that when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^29]. I feel your affections lifted up with me into the heights: but *the body which is corrupted weighs down the soul; and the earthly habitation presses down the mind that thinks on many things*[^30].

I too am about to lay down this book, and you are about to depart, each to his own. It has been well with us in the common light, well have we rejoiced, well have we exulted: but when we withdraw from one another, let us not withdraw from him.
