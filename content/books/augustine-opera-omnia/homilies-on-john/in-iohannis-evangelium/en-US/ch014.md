# Homily 14

## He must increase, but I must decrease[^1].

Christ was born when the days were already beginning to grow longer, John was born when the days began to grow shorter. Creation itself bore witness to the words of John saying: He must increase, but I must decrease. Let the glory of God, then, increase in us, and let our glory decrease, so that ours too may increase in God.

**1.** This reading from the holy Gospel teaches us the excellence of the divinity of our Lord Jesus Christ, and the humility of the man who deserved to be called the friend of the bridegroom; that we may distinguish what difference there is between man, a man, and the man who is God. For the man who is God is our Lord Jesus Christ, God before all ages, and man in our age: God of the Father, man of the Virgin; yet one and the same Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, the Son of God, God and man. But John was sent before him as one of excellent grace; enlightened by him who is the light.

For of John it was said: He was not the light, but that he might bear witness to the light. He can indeed be called light, and he too is rightly called light; but enlightened, not enlightening. For the light that enlightens is one thing, and the light that is enlightened another: for our eyes too are called lights, and yet in darkness they are open, and do not see. But the enlightening light is light of itself, and is light to itself, and does not need another light that it may be able to shine, but the rest need it that they may shine.

## He who wishes to rejoice in himself will be sad.

**2.** John, then, confessed, as you have heard, that when Jesus was making many disciples, and it was reported to him as though to provoke him—for they narrated it as though to one envious: Behold, he makes more disciples than you—he confessed what he was, and from this deserved to belong to him, because he did not dare to say of himself what the other is. This, then, John said: A man can receive nothing, unless it has been given him from heaven. Therefore Christ gives, the man receives. You yourselves bear me witness that I said: I am not the Christ, but that I was sent before him. He who has the bride is the bridegroom: but the friend of the bridegroom, who stands and hears him, rejoices with joy because of the voice of the bridegroom.

He did not make himself a joy out of himself. For he who wishes to rejoice in himself will be sad: but he who wishes to rejoice in God will always rejoice; because God is everlasting. Do you wish to have everlasting joy? Cleave to him who is everlasting. Such John said himself to be. Because of the voice of the bridegroom the friend of the bridegroom rejoices, he says, not because of his own voice: and he stands, and hears him. If, then, he falls, he does not hear him: for of a certain one who fell, it was said: And he did not stand in the truth; it was said of the devil. Therefore the friend of the bridegroom ought to stand and hear. What is it to stand? To remain in the grace which he received. And he hears the voice at which he may rejoice.

So was John: he knew whence he rejoiced, he did not arrogate to himself what he himself was not; he knew himself enlightened, not the enlightener. But he was the true light, says the Evangelist, which enlightens every man coming into this world. If, then, every man, John himself too; because he too is of men. For although no one greater than John arose among those born of women, yet he too is one of those who are born of women. Is he to be compared with him who, because he willed it, was born; and therefore by a new birth, because newly born? For both generations of the Lord are unusual, both the divine and the human: the divine has no mother, the human has no father.

John, then, is one of the rest, but yet of greater grace, so that among those born of women no one arose greater than he; and he gave so great a testimony to our Lord Jesus Christ that he calls him the bridegroom, himself the friend of the bridegroom, yet not worthy to loose the strap of his sandal. Of this your Charity has now heard much: let us see what follows; for it is somewhat dense to understand. But since John himself says that a man can receive nothing, unless it has been given him from heaven; whatever we do not understand, let us ask him who gives from heaven: because we are men, and can receive nothing, unless he gives who is not a man.

**3.** This, then, follows, and John says: This my joy, therefore, is fulfilled. What is his joy? That he rejoices at the voice of the bridegroom. It is fulfilled in me, I have my grace, I take no more upon myself, lest I lose even what I have received. What is this joy? He rejoices with joy because of the voice of the bridegroom.

Let a man understand, then, that he ought not to rejoice in his own wisdom, but in the wisdom that he received from God. Let him seek nothing more, and he does not lose what he found. For many became foolish for this reason, that they said they were wise. The Apostle reproves them, and says of them: Because what is known of God is manifest to them: for God has manifested it to them. Of certain ungrateful, impious men, hear what he says: For God has manifested it to them. For his invisible things, from the creation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood through the things that are made; his everlasting power also and divinity, so that they themselves are inexcusable. Why inexcusable? Because, knowing God; he did not say, because they did not know him; knowing God, they did not glorify him as God or give thanks; but they became vain in their thoughts, and their foolish heart was darkened: for saying that they were wise, they became fools.

For if they had known God, they would at the same time have known that none but God had made them wise. They would not, then, attribute to themselves what they did not have of themselves, but to him from whom they had received it. But by not giving thanks they became foolish. Therefore God took from the ungrateful what he had given freely. John was unwilling to be this; he wished to be grateful: he confessed that he had received, and said that he rejoiced because of the voice of the bridegroom, and said: This my joy, therefore, is fulfilled.

**4.** He must increase, but I must decrease. What is this? He must be exalted, but I must be humbled. How does Jesus increase? how does God increase? The perfect does not increase. But God neither increases nor decreases. For if he increases, he is not perfect: if he decreases, he is not God. But Jesus, who is God, how does he increase? If in age, because he deigned to be man, and was a child; and though he is the Word of God, as an infant he lay in the manger, and though he himself founded his own mother, he sucked the milk of infancy from his mother: because, then, Jesus increased in the age of the flesh, for this reason perhaps it was said: He must increase, but I must decrease.

But why this too? John and Jesus, as far as the flesh is concerned, were of the same age: there were six months between them, they had grown up alike; and if our Lord Jesus Christ had wished to be here longer before his death, and John himself to be here with him, as they had grown up alike, so they could grow old alike: why, then: He must increase, but I must decrease? First, because the Lord was already thirty years old: does a young man, if he is already thirty years old, still increase? Already from that very age men begin to decline, and to incline toward a heavier age, and from there to old age. But even if they were both boys, he would not say: He must increase, but I must decrease; but he would say: We must increase together. But now he is thirty years old, and so is the other: the six months that were between them distinguish no age; reading finds that rather than looking does.

## Let God increase in you.

**5.** What, then, is: He must increase, but I must decrease? A great sacred sign is this! let your Charity understand. Before the Lord Jesus came, men gloried in themselves: that man came, that the glory of man might decrease, and the glory of God increase. For he came without sin, and found all with sin. If he came thus, to forgive sins, let God bestow, let man confess. For the confession of man is the humility of man: the compassion of God is the loftiness of God. If, then, he came to forgive man his sins, let man acknowledge his humility, and let God show his mercy.

He must increase, but I must decrease: that is, he must give, but I must receive; he must be glorified, but I must confess. Let man understand his rank, and confess to God, and hear the Apostle saying to the proud and puffed-up man, wishing to exalt himself: For what have you that you did not receive? but if you received it, why do you glory as though you had not received it? Let man, then, understand that he has received, who wished to call his own what is not his, and let him decrease: for it is good for him that God be glorified in him. Let him decrease in himself, that he may be increased in God.

These testimonies and this truth Christ and John signified even by their own passions. For John was beheaded, Christ was exalted on the cross; so that there too might appear what is: He must increase, but I must decrease. Then Christ was born when the days were already beginning to grow longer, John was born when the days began to grow shorter. Creation itself and the passions themselves bore witness to the words of John saying: He must increase, but I must decrease.

Let the glory of God, then, increase in us, and let our glory decrease, so that ours too may increase in God. For this the Apostle says, this Holy Scripture says: He who glories, let him glory in the Lord. Do you wish to glory in yourself? you wish to increase: but to your own harm you increase badly. For he who increases badly is justly decreased. Let God, then, increase, who is always perfect, let him increase in you. For the more you understand God, and the more you grasp, God seems to increase in you: but in himself he does not increase, but is always perfect. You understood a little yesterday, you understand more today, you will understand much more tomorrow: the very light of God increases in you; thus God, as it were, increases, who remains always perfect.

Just as if someone's eyes were being cured from former blindness, and he began to see a little light, and on another day saw more, and on the third day still more, the light would seem to him to increase: yet the light is perfect, whether he sees it or does not see it. So it is also with the inner man: he progresses indeed in God, and God seems to increase in him; yet he himself decreases, that he may fall from his own glory, and rise into the glory of God.

**6.** And now distinctly and manifestly appears what we have just heard. He who comes from above is above all. See what he says of Christ. What of himself? He who is of the earth is of the earth, and speaks of the earth. He who comes from above is above all; that is Christ: But he who is of the earth is of the earth, and speaks of the earth; that is John. And is this the whole of it, John is of the earth, and speaks of the earth? all the testimony that he bears about Christ, does he speak of the earth? are the voices of God not heard from John, where he bears witness about Christ? How, then, does he speak of the earth? But he was speaking of the man. As far as the man himself is concerned, he is of the earth, and speaks of the earth: but if he speaks anything divine, he is enlightened by God. For if he were not enlightened, the earth would speak earth.

Therefore the grace of God is one thing, the nature of man another. Question the nature of man now: he is born and grows, he learns these customary things of men. What does he know but earth from the earth? He speaks human things, knows human things, has a taste for human things; carnal, he judges carnally, suspects carnally: behold, that is the whole man.

Let the grace of God come, let it enlighten his darkness, as it says: You will enlighten my lamp, O Lord; my God, enlighten my darkness; let it take up the human mind, turn it to its own light: it begins now to say what the Apostle says: Not I, however, but the grace of God with me; and: But I live now, not I, but Christ lives in me. This is: He must increase, but I must decrease. Therefore John, as far as John is concerned, is of the earth, and speaks of the earth: if you have heard anything divine from John, it is of the one enlightening, not of the one receiving.

## Attend to your heart. When you conceive a word . . .

**7.** He who comes from heaven is above all; and what he has seen and heard, this he testifies; and no one receives his testimony. From heaven he comes, he is above all, our Lord Jesus Christ: of whom it was said above: No one has ascended into heaven, except him who descended from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven. And he is above all; and what he has seen and heard, this he speaks. For the Son of God himself also has a Father; he has a Father, and hears from the Father. And what he hears from the Father, what is it? who unfolds this? When can my tongue, when can my heart be sufficient, either the heart to understand, or the tongue to bring forth, what it is that the Son heard from the Father? Perhaps the Son heard the Word of the Father? Rather, the Son is the Word of the Father. You see how here every human effort is wearied; you see how here all the conjecture of our breast fails, and all the straining of our darkened mind.

I hear Scripture saying that the Son speaks what he hears from the Father; and again I hear Scripture saying that the Son himself is the Word of the Father: In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. We speak words flying and passing away: as soon as your word has sounded in your mouth, it passes; it makes its noise and passes into silence. Can you follow your sound, and hold it that it may stand still? Yet your thought remains, and from that very remaining thought you say many passing words. What do we say, brothers? When God spoke, did he employ a voice, employ sounds, employ syllables? If he employed these, in what language did he speak? Hebrew, or Greek, or Latin? There languages are necessary, where there is the distinction of nations. But there no one can say that God spoke in this language, or in that language.

Attend to your heart. When you conceive a word that you may speak—for I will say, if I can, what we may attend to in ourselves, not whereby we may comprehend that—when, then, you conceive a word that you may bring forth, you wish to say a thing, and the very conception of the thing in your heart is already a word; it has not yet come forth, but it is already born in the heart, and remains that it may come forth: but you attend to him to whom it may come forth, with whom you speak; if he is Latin, you seek a Latin word; if he is Greek, you ponder Greek words; if he is Punic, you attend whether you know the Punic language; according to the diversity of hearers you employ diverse languages, that you may bring forth the conceived word: but that which you had conceived in the heart was held by no language.

Since, then, God speaking did not seek a language, and did not assume a manner of speaking, how was he heard by the Son, when God spoke the Son himself? For just as you have the word that you speak in your heart, and it is with you, and the conception itself is spiritual (for just as your soul is spirit, so the word too that you have conceived is spirit; for it has not yet received sound so as to be divided through syllables, but remains in the conception of the heart and in the mirror of the mind): so God uttered the Word, that is, begot the Son. And you indeed beget a word in time even in the heart: God begot the Son without time, through whom he created all times. Since, then, the Word of God is the Son, and the Son has spoken to us, not his own word, but the Word of the Father, he wished to speak himself to us, who was speaking the Word of the Father.

This, then, as was fitting and proper, John said: as we have been able, we have expounded it. He to whose heart a worthy understanding of so great a matter has not yet come, has where to turn himself, has where to knock, has one from whom to seek, has one from whom to ask, has one from whom to receive.

**8.** *He who comes from heaven is above all; and what he has seen and heard, this he testifies; and no one receives his testimony*[^2]. If no one, why did he come? Then it is the no one of certain people. There is a people prepared for the wrath of God, to be condemned with the devil; of these no one receives the testimony of Christ. For if it were absolutely no one, no human being at all, then what is it that follows: *He who has received his testimony has set his seal that God is true*[^3]? Surely, then, it is not no one, if you yourself say, *He who has received his testimony has set his seal that God is true*. John, then, if questioned, would perhaps answer and say: I know what I said—no one. For there is a certain people born for the wrath of God, and foreknown to this end. For God knows who are going to believe and who are not going to believe; who will persevere in what they have believed, and who will fall away, God knows; and all who are to be in eternal life are numbered before God, and he already knows that people set apart. And if he himself knows it, and gave the prophets to know it through his Spirit, he gave John to know it too.

John, then, was attending—not with his own eye, for as far as he himself is concerned he is of the earth and speaks of the earth, but in that grace of the Spirit which he had received from God he saw a certain people, ungodly and unbelieving; and attending to that people in its unbelief, he said, *The testimony of him who comes from heaven, no one receives*. Of whom, no one? Of those who are to be at the left hand, those to whom it will be said, *Go into the eternal fire which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^4]. Who, then, are those who receive it? Those who are to be at the right hand, those to whom it will be said, *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom which has been prepared for you from the foundation of the world*[^5].

He attended, then, in the spirit to the division, though in the human race there is a mixture; and what is not yet separated in place he separated in his understanding, separated by the gaze of his heart; and he saw two peoples, of the faithful and of the unfaithful. He attended to the unfaithful and said, *He who comes from heaven is above all; and what he has seen and heard, this he testifies; and no one receives his testimony*. Then he turned himself away from the left and looked to the right, and went on to say, *He who has received his testimony has set his seal that God is true*[^6].

What is *he has set his seal that God is true*, except that man is a liar, and God is true? For no human being can say what belongs to the truth unless he is enlightened by him who cannot lie. God, then, is true; but Christ is God. Do you wish to prove it? Take his testimony, and you find it: *He who has received his testimony has set his seal that God is true*. Who? He himself who comes from heaven and is above all is the true God. But if you do not yet understand him to be God, you have not yet received his testimony; receive it, and you set your seal; understanding with conviction, you acknowledge with certainty that God is true.

The peace of unity in the Trinity.

**9.** *For he whom God has sent speaks the words of God*[^7]. He himself is the true God, and God sent him: God sent God. Join the two together: one God, true God sent by God. Ask about each one, God; and ask about both, God. Not each one God and both gods, but each single one God and both God. For so great there is the love of the Holy Spirit, so great the peace of unity, that when you ask about each one, God is the answer given you; when you ask about the Trinity, God is the answer given you.

For if a man's spirit, when it clings to God, is one spirit—the Apostle saying openly, *He who clings to the Lord is one spirit*[^8]—how much more is the Son, who is equal and clings to the Father, together with him one God? Hear another testimony. You know how many believed when they sold all that they had and laid it at the feet of the apostles, that it might be distributed to each as each had need; and of that gathering of holy ones what does Scripture say? *They had one soul and one heart in the Lord*[^9]. If love made one soul out of so many souls, and one heart out of so many hearts, how great is the love between the Father and the Son? Surely it can be greater than among those men who had one heart.

If, then, the heart of many brothers is one because of love, and the soul of many brothers is one because of love, will you say that God the Father and God the Son are two? If they are two gods, there is not there the highest love. For if here there is so great a love that it makes your soul and your friend's soul one soul, how is it that there the Father and the Son are not one God? Far be it that a faith unfeigned should hold this. Indeed, understand from this how far that love surpasses. The souls of many men are many, and if they love one another, they are one soul; yet in men they may also be called many souls, because the union is not so great. But there you may say one God; you may not say two or three gods. From this is commended to you the surpassing excellence and the very summit of a love so great that no greater can be.

**10.** *For he whom God has sent speaks the words of God*[^10]. This he was certainly saying about Christ, to distinguish himself from him. For what? Did not God send John himself? Or did he not himself say, *I am sent before him*[^11], and, *He who sent me to baptize in water*[^12]; and was it not said of him, *Behold, I send my angel before you, and he will prepare your way*[^13]? Does not he too speak the words of God, of whom it was even said that he is more than a prophet[^14]? If, then, God sent him too, and he too speaks the words of God, how do we take it that he said of Christ, by way of distinction, *For he whom God has sent speaks the words of God*?

But see what he adds: *For God does not give the Spirit by measure*[^15]. What is this: *For God does not give the Spirit by measure*? We find that God does give the Spirit by measure. Hear the Apostle saying, *According to the measure of the gift of Christ*[^16]. To men he gives by measure; to the only Son he does not give by measure. How does he give to men by measure? *To one indeed is given through the Spirit the utterance of wisdom, to another the utterance of knowledge according to the same Spirit, to another faith in the same Spirit, to another prophecy, to another the discernment of spirits, to another kinds of tongues, to another the gift of healings*[^17]. *Are all apostles? Are all prophets? Are all teachers? Are all workers of miracles? Do all have gifts of healing? Do all speak with tongues? Do all interpret?*[^18] This one has one gift, that one another; and what that one has, this one does not have: it is a measure, a certain dividing out of gifts.

To men, then, it is given by measure, and there concord makes one body. Just as the hand receives one thing that it may work, the eye another that it may see, the ear another that it may hear, the foot another that it may walk, yet the soul is one which does all things—in the hand that it may work, in the foot that it may walk, in the ear that it may hear, in the eye that it may see—so too the gifts of the faithful are diverse, distributed as it were to the members, to each its own measure. But Christ, who gives, does not receive by measure.

The Father, in sending the Son, sent another self.

**11.** For hear still what follows; for of the Son he had said, *For God does not give the Spirit by measure*: *The Father loves the Son, and has given all things into his hand*[^19]. He added, *He has given all things into his hand*, that you might know here too with what distinction it was said, *The Father loves the Son*. For why? Does the Father not love John? And yet he has not given all things into his hand. Does the Father not love Paul? And yet he has not given all things into his hand. The Father loves the Son—but as a Father the Son, not as a master a servant; as an Only-begotten, not as one adopted. And so he has given all things into his hand. What is *all things*? That the Son is as great as the Father is. For he begot him equal to himself, who counted it no robbery to be in the form of God, equal to God[^20]. *The Father loves the Son, and has given all things into his hand*. Therefore, when he deigned to send the Son to us, let us not suppose that something less than the Father has been sent to us. The Father, in sending the Son, sent another self.

**12.** For the disciples, still supposing that the Father is something greater than the Son, seeing the flesh and not understanding the divinity, said to him, *Lord, show us the Father, and it is enough for us*[^21]. As though they said: We now know you, and we bless you because we know you; for we give you thanks because you have shown yourself to us; but the Father we do not yet know. Therefore our heart burns, and is busy with a certain holy desire of seeing your Father who sent you; show him to us, and we will desire nothing more from you; for it is enough for us when he has been shown, than whom no one can be greater.

A good longing, a good desire—but small understanding. For the Lord Jesus himself, attending to little ones seeking great things, and himself great among the little ones and little among the little ones, said to Philip, who had said this, one of the disciples, *Have I been with you so long a time, and have you not known me, Philip?*[^22] Here Philip could answer: We have known you; but did we say to you, Show us yourself? You we have known, but the Father we are seeking. He at once added, *He who has seen me has seen the Father also*[^23].

If, then, one equal to the Father has been sent, let us not estimate him by the weakness of the flesh, but let us think of the majesty clothed in flesh, not crushed by flesh. For remaining God with the Father, he became a man among men, that you, through him who for your sake became a man, might be made such as can grasp God. For man could not grasp God: man could see man, but could not grasp God. Why could he not grasp God? Because he had not the eye of the heart by which to grasp him. There was, then, something within that was wounded, and something without that was whole: he had the eyes of the body whole, the eyes of the heart wounded. He was made man for the eye of the body, that by believing in him who could be seen bodily, you might be healed unto seeing the very one whom you could not see spiritually.

*Have I been with you so long a time, and have you not known me, Philip? He who has seen me has seen the Father also*. Why did they not see him? Behold, they saw him, and they did not see the Father: they saw the flesh, but the majesty lay hidden. What the disciples who loved him saw, the Jews who crucified him also saw. He was, then, wholly within, and so within in the flesh that he remained with the Father; for he did not forsake the Father when he came to the flesh.

**13.** A carnal way of thinking does not grasp what I say: let it defer understanding, and begin from faith; let it hear what follows: *He who believes in the Son has eternal life; but he who is unbelieving toward the Son will not see life, but the wrath of God remains upon him*[^24]. He did not say, the wrath of God comes to him, but, the wrath of God remains upon him. All who are born mortal have the wrath of God with them. What wrath of God? The wrath which the first Adam received. For if the first man sinned and heard, *You shall die the death*[^25], he was made mortal, and we began to be born mortal; we were born with the wrath of God. From there the Son came, having no sin, and was clothed with flesh, was clothed with mortality. If he shared with us the wrath of God, are we slow to share with him the grace of God?

He, then, who is unwilling to believe in the Son, the wrath of God remains upon him. What wrath of God? That of which the Apostle says, *We too were by nature children of wrath, like the rest*[^26]. All, then, are children of wrath, because they come from the curse of death. Believe in Christ, made mortal for your sake, that you may receive him immortal; for when you have received his immortality, you too will not be mortal. He was living, you were dying: he died, that you might live. He brought the grace of God, he took away the wrath of God. God conquered death, lest death conquer man.
