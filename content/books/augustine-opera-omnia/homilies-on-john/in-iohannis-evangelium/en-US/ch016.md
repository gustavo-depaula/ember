# Homily 16

*Unless you see signs and wonders, you do not believe*[^1].

I am speaking to the people of God: so many of us have believed—what signs did we see? We heard the Gospel, we consented to the Gospel, through the Gospel we believed in Christ; we saw no signs, we demand none.

**1.** Today's Gospel reading follows yesterday's reading, and is set before us for discussion. In it, indeed, the meanings are not difficult to investigate, but worthy to be preached, worthy of admiration and praise. Accordingly, let us rather recall this passage of the Gospel with commendation than treat it as difficult. For *Jesus went away, after the two days that he had spent in Samaria, into Galilee, where he had been brought up. And the Evangelist went on to say: For Jesus himself bore witness that a prophet has no honor in his own country*[^2]. Jesus did not depart from Samaria after two days because he had no honor in Samaria; for Samaria was not his own country, but Galilee was. Since, then, he had so quickly left it, and had come to Galilee where he had been brought up, how does he bear witness that a prophet has no honor in his own country? It would seem rather that he could have borne witness that a prophet has no honor in his own country if he had scorned to go on into Galilee and had remained in Samaria.

**2.** Let your Charity, then, attend to a sacred sign of no small import suggested to us, the Lord prompting and granting what I am to say. You have learned the question proposed; seek out its solution. But let us repeat the proposition, that we may make the solution welcome. It moves us why the Evangelist said, *For Jesus himself bore witness that a prophet has no honor in his own country*. Moved by this, we unfold the preceding words, that we may find why the Evangelist wished to say this; and we find his preceding words narrating thus: that after two days he set out from Samaria into Galilee.

Was it for this, then, O Evangelist, that you said Jesus bore witness that a prophet has no honor in his own country—because after two days he left Samaria and hastened to come into Galilee? On the contrary, I seem to myself to understand more fittingly that, if Jesus had no honor in his own country, he would not, having left Samaria, hasten to that very country. But unless I am mistaken—indeed, because it is true, and I am not mistaken—the Evangelist saw better than I what he was saying; he saw the truth better than I, who drank it from the Lord's breast. For this is John the Evangelist, who among all the disciples lay upon the Lord's breast[^3]; and whom the Lord, owing love to all, yet loved beyond the rest[^4]. Would he, then, be mistaken, and I think rightly? Rather, if I am wise with godliness, let me hear obediently what he said, that I may deserve to perceive what he perceived.

## We are fellow students in one school.

**3.** Receive, therefore, dearly beloved, what I here perceive, without prejudice, if you have perceived anything better. For we all have one Master, and in one school we are fellow students. This, then, is what I perceive; and see whether what I perceive is not either true, or close to the truth.

In Samaria he spent two days, and the Samaritans believed in him; so many days he spent in Galilee, and the Galileans did not believe in him. Unfold, or recall to memory, yesterday's reading and sermon. He came into Samaria, where first that woman had proclaimed him, with whom he had spoken great mysteries at Jacob's well: when he had been seen and heard, the Samaritans believed in him because of the woman's word, and believed more firmly because of his word, and more believed: so it is written. There, after spending two days (by which number of days the number of the two commandments is mystically commended, on which two commandments the whole Law and the Prophets depend[^5], as you remember we commended yesterday), he goes on into Galilee, and comes to the city of Cana of Galilee, where he made the water wine[^6]. But there, when he turned the water into wine, as John himself writes, his disciples believed in him; and surely the house was full of crowds of banqueters. So great a miracle was done, and no one believed in him but his disciples[^7].

This city of Galilee he now visited again. *And behold, a certain royal official, whose son was sick, came to him, and began to ask him to come down* to that city or house, *and heal his son; for he was beginning to die*[^8]. Did the one who asked not believe? What do you expect to hear from me? Ask the Lord what he perceived about him. For when asked, he answered such things: *Unless you see signs and wonders, you do not believe*[^9]. He reproved the man as lukewarm in faith, or cold, or of altogether no faith—but as one desiring, by means of his son's health, to test what Christ was, who he was, how much he could do. For we have heard the words of the one asking; we do not see the heart of the one distrusting; but he declared it, who both heard the words and looked into the heart.

In short, the Evangelist himself shows by the testimony of his narrative that the man who desired the Lord to come to his house, to heal his son, had not yet believed. For after it was announced to him that his son was well, and he found him healed at the very hour at which the Lord had said, *Go, your son lives*, then, it says, *he himself believed, and his whole household*[^10]. If, then, he himself and his whole household believed because his son was announced to him as well, and he compared the hour of the announcers with the hour of the one foretelling, then when he was asking, he did not yet believe.

The Samaritans had awaited no sign, they had believed only at his word; but his own townsmen deserved to hear, *Unless you see signs and wonders, you do not believe*; and yet there, when so great a miracle had been done, no one believed but he himself and his household. At his word alone the many Samaritans believed; at that miracle only that one household believed, where it was done.

What, then, brothers, what does the Lord commend to us? Then Galilee of Judea was the Lord's own country, because he had been brought up there; but now, because that matter portends something—for "wonders" are not so called without cause, except that they portend something; for a "wonder" (prodigium) is so named as it were "porro-dicium," because it says something further off, signifies something further off, and portends that something is to be—because, then, all those things were portending something, all those things were foretelling something, let us now make the country of our Lord Jesus Christ according to the flesh (for he had no country on earth except according to the flesh, which he took on earth); let us make, then, the country of the Lord the people of the Jews. Behold, in his own country he has no honor.

Now attend to the crowds of the Jews; attend to that nation now scattered over the whole world, and torn up by its roots; attend to the branches broken, cut off, scattered, dried up, by whose breaking the wild olive deserved to be grafted in[^11]: see the crowd of the Jews; what does it say now? The one you worship, the one you adore, was our brother. And let us answer: A prophet has no honor in his own country. In short, they saw the Lord Jesus walking on earth and doing miracles—giving light to the blind, opening the ears of the deaf, loosing the mouths of the mute, binding tight the limbs of the paralyzed, walking on the sea, commanding the winds and the waves, raising the dead; they saw him doing such great signs, and scarcely a few of them believed.

I am speaking to the people of God: so many of us have believed—what signs did we see? Therefore that which was done then portended what is being done now. The Jews were, or are, like the Galileans; we are like those Samaritans. We heard the Gospel, we consented to the Gospel, through the Gospel we believed in Christ; we saw no signs, we demand none.

## In place of the broken branches we have been grafted in.

**4.** For although he was one of the twelve elect and holy ones, yet he was an Israelite, of the Lord's nation, that Thomas who desired to put his fingers into the places of the wounds. The Lord reproved him as he did this royal official. To this one he said, *Unless you see signs and wonders, you do not believe*; but to that one he said, *Because you have seen, you have believed*. He had come to the Galileans after the Samaritans who had believed at his word, among whom he had done no miracles, whom, firm in faith, he had securely left so soon, because he had not left them by the presence of his divinity. Therefore, when the Lord said to Thomas, *Come, put in your hand, and do not be unbelieving, but believing*; and when he, the places of the wounds being touched, cried out and said, *My Lord and my God*, he is rebuked and it is said to him, *Because you have seen, you have believed*[^12]. Why, except that a prophet has no honor in his own country? But because among the foreigners this prophet has honor, what follows? *Blessed are those who do not see and believe*[^13].

We have been foretold; and what the Lord praised beforehand, he deigned to fulfill in us. Those who crucified him saw and touched, and yet few believed; we did not see, we did not handle, we heard, we believed. May the blessedness which he promised come to be in us, be perfected in us; both here, because we have been preferred to his own country; and in the age to come, because in place of the broken branches we have been grafted in.

**5.** For he was showing that he would break these branches, and graft in this wild olive, when, moved by the faith of the centurion, who said to him, *I am not worthy that you should come under my roof; but only say the word, and my servant will be healed; for I too am a man set under authority, having soldiers under me, and I say to this one, Go, and he goes; and to another, Come, and he comes; and to my servant, Do this, and he does it*: turning to those who were following him, he said, *Amen I say to you, I have not found such great faith in Israel*[^14]. Why did he not find such great faith in Israel? Because a prophet has no honor in his own country.

Could not the Lord also have said to that centurion what he said to this royal official, *Go, your son lives*[^15]? See the distinction: this royal official desired the Lord to come down to his house; that centurion said he was unworthy. To the one it was said, *I will come and heal him*; to the other it was said, *Go, your son lives*. To the one he promised his presence; the other he healed by a word. Yet this one wrung from him his presence; that one said he was unworthy of his presence. Here he yielded to pride; there he conceded to humility. As if he said to this one: *Go, your son lives*, do not make me weary. *Unless you see signs and wonders, you do not believe*: you want my presence in your house; I can also command by a word; do not you believe because of signs: the foreign centurion believed that I could do it by a word, and before I did it he believed; you, unless you see signs and wonders, do not believe.

Therefore, if it is so, let the proud branches be broken, let the humble wild olive be grafted in: yet let the root remain, those being cut off, these being received. Where does the root remain? In the patriarchs. For the country of Christ is the people of Israel, because from them he came according to the flesh; but the root of this tree is Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, the holy patriarchs. And where are they? In rest with God, in great honor: so that into Abraham's bosom that poor man, helped, was lifted up after the body's departure, and in Abraham's bosom was seen from afar by the proud rich man[^16]. Therefore the root remains, the root is praised; but the proud branches deserved both to be cut off and to wither; and the humble wild olive, by their cutting off, found a place.

**6.** Hear, then, how the natural branches are cut off, how the wild olive is grafted in, from that very centurion, whom I thought I should mention because of the comparison with this royal official. *Amen*, he says, *I say to you, I have not found such great faith in Israel: therefore I say to you, that many from the east and the west*[^17]. How widely the wild olive had occupied the earth! This world was a bitter forest; but because of humility, because of "I am not worthy that you should come under my roof," *many from the east and the west will come*. And suppose they will come: what will become of them? For if they will come, they have already been cut off from the forest: where are they to be grafted, lest they wither? *And they will recline*, he says, *with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob*[^18]. At what banquet?—lest perhaps you should invite to living forever, but to much drinking. *They will recline with Abraham, and Isaac, and Jacob*. Where? *In the kingdom*, he says, *of heaven*. And what will become of those who came from the stock of Abraham? What will become of the branches with which the tree was full? What, except that they will be cut off, that these may be grafted in? Show that they will be cut off: *But the sons of the kingdom will go into the outer darkness*[^19].

**7.** Let the prophet, then, have honor among us, because he had no honor in his own country. He had no honor in the country in which he was founded; let him have honor in the country which he founded. For in it the founder of all things was founded; he was founded in it according to the form of a servant. For that very city in which he was founded, that very Zion, that very nation of the Jews, that very Jerusalem, he himself founded, when he was, with the Father, the Word of God: *for all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^20].

Of that man, then, of whom we have heard today—the one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus—a Psalm too has spoken beforehand, saying: *"Mother Zion," a man will say*[^21]. A certain man, the mediator of God and men, the man, says, *"Mother Zion."* Why does he say, *"Mother Zion"*? Because from there he received flesh, from there the Virgin Mary, from whose womb the form of a servant was taken, in which he deigned to appear most lowly. *"Mother Zion," a man says*; and this man who says *"Mother Zion"* was made in her, was made man in her. For God was before her, and was made man in her. He who was made man in her, he himself, the Most High, founded her—not the most lowly. He was made man in her, most lowly, because *the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us*[^22]: he, the Most High, founded her, because *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God; all things were made through him*[^23]. But because he founded that country, let him have honor here. The country in which he was begotten cast him out; let the country which he regenerated receive him.
