# Tractate 107

## *That they may have my joy fulfilled in themselves*[^1].

## This joy of his, that is, conferred by him upon them, he says is to be fulfilled in them, for the sake of which he said he spoke in the world. This is that peace and blessedness in the age to come, for the obtaining of which one must live temperately and justly and devoutly in this age. —  — 

**1.** When the Lord was speaking to the Father concerning those whom he already had as disciples, among other things he said this also: *I ask for them: I do not ask for the world, but for those whom you gave me*[^2]. By the world he now wishes to be understood those who live according to the desire of the world, and are not in that lot of grace, as to be chosen by him out of the world. Not, then, for the world, but for those whom the Father gave him, he says he asks: for through this, that the Father already gave them to him, it came about that they do not belong to that world for which he does not ask.

**2.** Then he adds: *Because they are yours*[^3]. For not because the Father gave them to the Son did he himself lose those whom he gave: since the Son still follows, and says, *And all that is mine is yours, and yours is mine*[^4]. Where it sufficiently appears how all things that are the Father's are the only-begotten Son's; through this, surely, that he himself too is God, and was born of the Father equal to the Father: not as it was said to one of two sons, namely the elder, *You are always with me, and all that is mine is yours*[^5]. For that was said of all those creatures which are below the holy rational creature, which are surely subject to the Church; in which whole Church those two sons too are understood, the elder and the younger, with all the holy angels, to whom we shall be equal in the kingdom of Christ and God: but this was said thus, *And all that is mine is yours, and yours is mine,* so that here there is also that very rational creature, which is subject to none but God, that to it all things below it may be subject. This, then, since it is God the Father's, would not at the same time be the Son's too, unless he were equal to the Father: for of it he was treating, when he said, *I do not ask for the world, but for those whom you gave me: because they are yours, and all that is mine is yours, and yours is mine.* Nor is it lawful that the saints, of whom he spoke these things, should be anyone's except his by whom they were created and sanctified: and through this also all things that are theirs must needs be his whose they themselves too are. Therefore, since they are both the Father's and the Son's, they show those to be equal whose they equally are. But that which he says, when he was speaking of the Holy Spirit, *All things that the Father has are mine; therefore I said that he will receive of mine, and will announce it to you*[^6], he said of those things which belong to the very divinity of the Father, in which he is equal to him, by having all things that he has. For the Holy Spirit was not going to receive of the creature which is subject to the Father and the Son, that which he says, *he will receive of mine*; but certainly of the Father, from whom the Spirit proceeds, of whom the Son too was born.

**3.** *And I have been glorified,* he says, *in them*[^7]. Now he speaks of his glorification as though it were done, when it was still to come: but above he was asking of the Father that it might be done. But whether this is the same glorification of which he had said, *And now glorify me, Father, with yourself, with the glory which I had with you before the world was,* must certainly be inquired. For if *with you,* how *in them*? Or is it because this very thing becomes known to them, and through them to all who believe with them as witnesses? We can plainly understand the Lord to have said of the Apostles that he was glorified in them: for by saying it was already done, he shows it was already predestined, and he wished it to be held certain that it would be.

**4.** *And now,* he says, *I am no longer in the world, and these are in the world*[^8]. If you attend to that very hour in which he was speaking, both were still in the world; both he himself, and those of whom he was saying this: for we cannot and ought not to take it according to the advancement of heart and life, so that they should for that reason be said to be still in the world, because they still savored worldly things; but he no longer to be in the world, by savoring divine things. For one word is here put which does not at all allow us so to understand it: for he does not say, And I am not in the world; but, *I am no longer in the world*: by this showing that he had been in the world, and now was not. Is it then lawful for us to believe that he at some time savored worldly things, and, freed from this error, now does not savor them? Who would put on so impious a sense? It remains, therefore, that, according to that whereby he himself too was first in the world, he said that he was now no longer in the world; surely showing, by bodily presence, his own absence from the world, now soon to come, but theirs more slowly, in that he said that he was now not here, but they were here, when both he himself was here and they still were. For so he spoke, a man suiting himself to men, as the human manner of speaking goes. Or do we not daily say, He is no longer here, of someone soon to depart? And this is especially wont to be said of the dying. Although the Lord himself too, as it were foreseeing what might disturb those about to read, added: *And I come to you*: thus in a manner explaining why he said, *I am no longer in the world.*

**5.** He commends them, then, to the Father, whom he is going to leave by bodily absence, saying: *Holy Father, keep in your name those whom you gave me*[^9]. Surely, as a man he asks God for his disciples, whom he received from God. But attend to what follows: *That they may be,* he says, *one as we also are.* He does not say: That they may be one with us, or, that we and they may be one, as we are one; but he says: *That they may be one as we also are.* They, surely, in their nature may be one, as we also in ours are one. Which without doubt he would not say truly, unless he said it according to that whereby God is of the same nature as the Father, according to which he said elsewhere, *I and the Father are one*[^10]: not according to that whereby he is also a man; for according to this he said, *The Father is greater than I*[^11]. But since God and man are one and the same person, we understand the man in that which he asks: but we understand God in that which they are one, both he and the one whom he asks. But there is still a place in what follows where this matter must be more carefully discussed.

## If we have known him descending, let us rise with him as he lifts us up.

**6.** But here follows: *When I was with them, I kept them in your name*[^12]. As I come to you, he says, keep them in your name, in which, when I was with them, I myself too kept them. In the name of the Father the Son as man kept his disciples, being established with them by human presence: but the Father too kept in the name of the Son those whom, asking in the name of the Son, he heard. For to these the same Son had said: *Amen, amen, I say to you, if you ask anything of the Father in my name, he will give it to you*[^13]. Nor ought we to take this so fleshly, as though the Father and the Son keep us in turn, the keeping of both alternating in guarding us, as if one succeeds when the other has departed: for the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, who is the one true and blessed God, keep us at the same time. But Scripture does not lift us up, unless it descends to us: just as the Word, made flesh, descended to lift us up, did not fall to lie low. If we have known him descending, let us rise with him as he lifts us up; and let us understand that, when he so speaks, he distinguishes persons, not separates natures. When, therefore, the Son kept his disciples by bodily presence, the Father did not wait to succeed to the keeping when the Son departed; but both kept them by spiritual power: and when the Son took bodily presence away from them, he held, with the Father, the spiritual keeping. For both, when the Son as man received them to be kept, he did not take them away from the paternal keeping: and when the Father gave them to the Son to be kept, he did not give them without him to whom he gave; but he gave them to the man, the Son, not without God, that same Son.

**7.** The Son follows, then, and says: *Those whom you gave me, I guarded: and no one of them perished, except the son of perdition, that the Scripture might be fulfilled*[^14]. The betrayer of Christ was called the son of perdition, predestined to perdition, according to the Scripture which is prophesied of him especially in the hundred and eighth psalm.

**8.** *But now,* he says, *I come to you; and these things I speak in the world, that they may have my joy fulfilled in themselves*[^15]. Behold, he says he speaks in the world, who a little before had said, *I am no longer in the world*: why he said this, we have explained there, nay rather, we have shown that he himself explained it. Therefore both, because he had not yet departed, he was still here; and because he was soon going to depart, he was here in a manner already not here. But what this joy is of which he says, *that they may have my joy fulfilled in themselves,* has already been expressed above, where he says, *That they may be one as we also are.* This joy of his, that is, conferred by him upon them, he says is to be fulfilled in them; for the sake of which he said he spoke in the world. This is that peace and blessedness in the age to come, for the obtaining of which one must live temperately and justly and devoutly in this age.
