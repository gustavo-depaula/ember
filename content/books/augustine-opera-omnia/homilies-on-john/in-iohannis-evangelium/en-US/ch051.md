# Tractate 51

## *Unless a grain of wheat...* (Jn 12:12-26).

## He himself was the grain that had to be put to death and multiplied: put to death by the unbelief of the Jews, multiplied by the faith of the peoples.

**1.** After the Lord raised up the man who had been dead four days, while the Jews were astonished, and some of them believed by seeing while others perished by envying, on account of the good fragrance which is to some unto life, to others unto death; after he reclined at table in the house with Lazarus, who had been dead and was raised, reclining with him, after the ointment was poured out over his feet, by which the house was filled with the fragrance; after the Jews, with lost hearts, conceived their empty savagery and their most foolish and most mad wickedness even of killing Lazarus—of all which things we have spoken in the previous sermons as we could, what the Lord gave: now let your Charity attend to how great a fruit of his preaching appeared before the Lord's passion, and how great a flock of sheep, from those of the house of Israel that were lost, heard the voice of the shepherd.

## He does not lose his divinity when he teaches us humility.

**2.** For thus the Gospel speaks, which you heard just now as it was read: *On the next day a great crowd that had come to the feast, when they heard that Jesus was coming to Jerusalem, took branches of palms, and went out to meet him, and cried out: Hosanna, blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord, the king of Israel*[^1]. The branches of palms are praises, signifying victory; because the Lord was about to overcome death by dying, and to triumph over the devil, the prince of death, with the trophy of the cross. And "Hosanna" is the voice of one entreating, as some who know the Hebrew tongue say, indicating an affection rather than signifying any particular thing—as there are in the Latin tongue what they call interjections, as when in grief we say "Alas!" or when we are delighted we say "Ah!" or when we marvel we say "Oh, what a great thing!"; for then "Oh" signifies nothing but the affection of one who marvels. And this is to be believed to be so because neither a Greek nor a Latin was able to translate it: as with that word, *Whoever says to his brother, Raca*[^2]. For this too is said to be an interjection, showing the affection of one who is indignant.

## That he willed to be king of Israel is a condescension, not a promotion.

**3.** And *blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord, the king of Israel* is rather to be taken so that "in the name of the Lord" is understood as in the name of God the Father; although it can also be understood as in his own name, because he too is Lord. Hence it is also written elsewhere: *The Lord rained from the Lord*[^3]. But his own words better direct our understanding, where he says: *I have come in the name of my Father, and you did not receive me; another will come in his own name, him you will receive*[^4]. For Christ is the teacher of humility, who *humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even death on a cross*[^5]. He does not, then, lose his divinity when he teaches us humility: in the one he is equal to the Father, in the other he is like us; by that whereby he is equal to the Father, he created us that we might be; by that whereby he is like us, he redeemed us that we might not perish.

**4.** These praises the crowd was speaking to him: *Hosanna, blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord, the king of Israel.* What torment of mind could the envy of the Jewish leaders suffer, when so great a multitude was crying out that Christ was their king? But what was it to the Lord to be king of Israel? What great thing was it for the king of the ages to become a king of men? For Christ is not king of Israel to exact tribute, or to arm an army with the sword, or to vanquish enemies in a visible way; but king of Israel in that he rules minds, that he takes counsel for eternity, that he leads into the kingdom of heaven those who believe, hope, and love.

That the Son of God, then, equal to the Father, the Word through whom all things were made, willed to be king of Israel, is a condescension, not a promotion; it is a sign of mercy, not an increase of power. For he who was called on earth king of the Jews is in heaven the Lord of the angels.

**5.** *And Jesus found a young ass, and sat upon it.* This is said here briefly: for how it was done is read most fully in the other evangelists. And a prophetic testimony is brought to bear on this deed, that it might appear that the malicious leaders of the Jews did not understand him, in whom were being fulfilled the things that they read. *Jesus* therefore *found a young ass, and sat upon it: as it is written: Fear not, daughter of Sion; behold, your king comes, sitting upon the foal of an ass*[^6]. In that people, then, was the daughter of Sion: she is Jerusalem, which is Sion. In that people, I say, reprobate and blind, there was nonetheless a daughter of Sion, to whom it might be said: *Fear not: behold, your king comes, sitting upon the foal of an ass.* This daughter of Sion, to whom these things are said by God, was among those sheep that heard the voice of the shepherd; she was in that multitude which praised the Lord as he came with such devotion, and escorted him with so great a throng. To her it was said: *Fear not*: acknowledge him who is praised by you, and do not tremble when he suffers; because that blood is poured out by which your fault may be blotted out, and life restored to you.

But by the foal of an ass on which no one had sat (for this is found in the other evangelists) we understand the people of the Gentiles, who had not received the Law of the Lord. And by the ass (since both beasts were brought to the Lord) his people who came from the people of Israel, not wholly untamed, but who recognized the Lord's manger.

**6.** *These things his disciples did not understand at first; but when Jesus was glorified,* that is, when he showed the power of his resurrection, *then they remembered that these things were written of him, and that they had done these things to him*[^7]—that is, they had done to him none other than the things that were written of him. For, recalling according to Scripture the things that were fulfilled before the Lord's passion or in the Lord's passion, they found there this also, that according to the words of the Prophets he sat upon the foal of an ass.

**7.** *The crowd therefore that was with him when he called Lazarus out of the tomb, and raised him from the dead, bore witness. For this reason the crowd also came out to meet him, because they heard that he had done this sign. The Pharisees therefore said among themselves: Do you see that we are accomplishing nothing? Behold, the whole world has gone after him*[^8]. The crowd disturbed the crowd. But why do you envy, blind crowd, because the world goes after him through whom the world was made?

**8.** *Now there were certain Gentiles among those who had gone up to worship on the feast day. These therefore came to Philip, who was of Bethsaida of Galilee, and asked him, saying: Lord, we wish to see Jesus. Philip came and told Andrew; Andrew again and Philip told Jesus*[^9]. Let us hear what the Lord answered to this. Behold, the Jews wish to kill him, the Gentiles to see him; but those too who cried out, *Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord, the king of Israel*, were of the Jews. Behold, those of the circumcision and those of the uncircumcision, like two walls coming from different directions, and meeting in the one faith of Christ with the kiss of peace: let us hear, then, the voice of the cornerstone.

*But Jesus,* he says, *answered them, saying: The hour has come that the Son of man should be glorified*[^10]. Here someone perhaps thinks that he said he was glorified because the Gentiles wished to see him. It is not so. But he saw that those same Gentiles, after his passion and resurrection, would believe among all nations: because, as the Apostle says, *Blindness in part has happened to Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles should enter in*[^11]. So on the occasion of these Gentiles who desired to see him, he announces the coming fullness of the Gentiles; and he promises that the hour of his glorification is now at hand, which, accomplished in heaven, the Gentiles were going to believe. Hence it was foretold: *Be exalted above the heavens, O God, and your glory above all the earth*[^12]. This is the fullness of the Gentiles, of which the Apostle says: *Blindness in part has happened to Israel, until the fullness of the Gentiles should enter in.*

## The humility of the passion preceded the height of the glorification.

**9.** But it was fitting that the humility of the passion should precede the height of the glorification: and so he went on to add: *Amen, amen I say to you, unless a grain of wheat falling into the ground dies, it remains alone; but if it dies, it bears much fruit*[^13]. He was speaking of himself. He himself was the grain that had to be put to death and multiplied: put to death by the unbelief of the Jews, multiplied by the faith of the peoples.

**10.** And now, exhorting them to follow in the footsteps of his passion, he says: *He who loves his life will lose it*[^14]. This can be understood in two ways. *He who loves it will lose it,* that is: If you love it, lose it; if you desire to keep your life in Christ, do not fear death for Christ. Again, in another way: *He who loves his life will lose it.* Do not love it, lest you lose it; do not love it in this life, lest you lose it in eternal life. But this latter sense which I have given seems rather to hold the Gospel meaning; for there follows: *And he who hates his life in this world will keep it unto life eternal*[^15]. Therefore in what was said above, *He who loves,* there is understood, in this world; he will surely lose it: *but he who hates it,* surely in this world, will himself keep it unto life eternal. A great and wondrous saying, that there is in a man such a love of his own life that he perishes, and such a hatred that he does not perish! If you love badly, then you hate; if you hate well, then you have loved. Happy are those who hate by keeping, lest they lose by loving.

But see that it does not steal upon you to want to kill yourself, by so understanding what you ought to hate in this world, your life. For from this some malicious and perverse men, more cruel and more wicked murderers against themselves, give themselves to the flames, drown themselves in waters, dash themselves to pieces by a fall, and perish. Christ did not teach this; rather, even to the devil when he suggested a fall he answered: *Get back, Satan; it is written: You shall not tempt the Lord your God*[^16]. But to Peter he said, signifying by what death he was to glorify God: *When you were younger, you girded yourself, and went where you wished; but when you have grown old, another will gird you, and carry you where you do not wish*[^17]. There he expressed clearly enough that one who follows the footsteps of Christ ought to be killed not by himself, but by another. When, therefore, the crisis of the matter comes, so that this condition is set before one—either that he must act against God's command, or that he must depart from this life, of which two a man is forced to choose one, while the persecutor threatens death—there let him choose to die for the love of God rather than to live by offending him; there let him hate his life in this world, that he may keep it unto life eternal.

**11.** *If anyone serves me, let him follow me*[^18]. What is *let him follow me,* but, let him imitate me? *For Christ suffered for us,* says the apostle Peter, *leaving us an example, that we should follow his footsteps*[^19]. Behold what was said: *If anyone serves me, let him follow me.* With what fruit? with what reward? with what prize? *And where I am,* he says, *there will my servant be also.* Let him be loved freely, so that the price of the work by which he is served may be to be with him. For where will it be well without him, or when could it be ill with him?

Hear it more plainly. *If anyone serves me, my Father will honor him*[^20]. With what honor, but that he should be with his Son? For what he said above, *Where I am, there will my servant be also*, this is understood to have been explained when he says, *my Father will honor him.* For what greater honor can an adopted one receive than to be where the Only-begotten is; not made equal to the divinity, but joined to the eternity?

## They serve Christ who do not seek their own things, but the things of Jesus Christ.

**12.** But what it is to serve Christ, to which work so great a reward is set forth, this we ought rather to inquire. For if we should think that to serve Christ is to prepare the things necessary for the body, or to cook and set out food for one who dines, or to give a cup and mix a drink, this was done by those who were able to have him present in the body, as Martha and Mary, when Lazarus too was one of those reclining at table. But in that way even the lost Judas served Christ; for he too had the moneybags: and although he stole most wickedly from what was put into them, yet through him too the necessary things were prepared. Hence comes that passage where, when the Lord said to him, *What you do, do quickly*[^21], some thought that he had ordered him to prepare the things necessary for the feast day, or to give something to the needy. In no way, then, would the Lord say of servants of this kind: *Where I am, there will my servant be also*; and: *If anyone serves me, my Father will honor him*: since we see Judas, who served in such ways, reproved rather than honored.

Why, then, do we seek elsewhere what it is to serve Christ, and not rather recognize it in these very words? For when he said, *If anyone serves me, let him follow me*, he wished it to be understood as if he said: If anyone does not follow me, he does not serve me. They serve Jesus Christ, then, who do not seek their own things, but the things of Jesus Christ. For this is *let him follow me,* let him walk my ways, not his own: as it is written elsewhere: *He who says that he abides in Christ ought himself also to walk as he walked*[^22]. He ought also, if he holds out bread to one who is hungry, to do it out of mercy, not out of ostentation; to seek nothing there but the good work, the left hand not knowing what the right hand does[^23]—that is, that the aim of greed may be made foreign to the work of love. He who serves in this way serves Christ; and it will rightly be said to him: *When you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^24]. And not only doing the things that pertain to bodily mercy, but doing all good works for the sake of Christ (for then they will be good, since *Christ is the end of the Law, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^25]), he is a servant of Christ, even up to that work of great love, which is to lay down one's life for the brothers: for this is also to lay it down for Christ. Because this too he is going to say on account of his members: When you did it for these, you did it for me. For of such a work he deigned even to make and call himself a servant, where he says: *As the Son of man did not come to be served, but to serve, and to lay down his life for many*[^26]. Hence, then, each one is a servant of Christ from the same source whence Christ also is a servant. The one who serves Christ in this way his Father will honor with that great honor, that he should be with his Son, and that his happiness should never fail.

**13.** When, therefore, you hear, brothers, the Lord saying: *Where I am, there will my servant be also*, do not think only of good bishops and clergy. You too, according to your measure, serve Christ, by living well, by giving alms, by preaching his name and his teaching to whomever you can; so that each head of a household may recognize that he owes by this name a fatherly affection to his household. For the sake of Christ and of eternal life let him admonish, teach, exhort, and correct all his own; let him bestow goodwill, let him exercise discipline: so in his own house he will fulfill an ecclesiastical and in a certain way an episcopal office, serving Christ that he may be with him forever. For that greatest service of the passion many of your number have also rendered: many who are neither bishops nor clergy, young men and virgins, the older with the younger, many married men and women, many fathers and mothers of households, serving Christ, have laid down even their lives in his martyrdom, and, the Father honoring them, have received most glorious crowns.
