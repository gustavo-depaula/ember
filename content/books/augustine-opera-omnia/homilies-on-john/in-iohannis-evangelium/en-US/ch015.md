# Homily 15

## *Jesus, wearied from the journey, was sitting thus by the spring*[^1].

## Now the mysteries begin. For Jesus is not wearied in vain; the power of God is not wearied in vain. We find a strong Jesus, and we find a weak Jesus. The strength of Christ created you; the weakness of Christ re-created you. He founded us by his strength; he sought us by his weakness.

**1.** It is not strange to the ears of your Charity that John the evangelist flies higher like an eagle, and transcends the mist of the earth, and gazes upon the light of truth with steadier eyes. For many things from his Gospel have already, by our ministry and with the Lord's help, been treated; and in order there follows this reading, which has been recited today. The things I am going to say, with the Lord's gift, many of you will hear in such a way that you recognize them again rather than learn them. Yet your attention ought not on that account to be sluggish, because it is not a coming to know, but a recognizing.

This has been read, and this reading we carry in our hands to be treated: that the Lord Jesus was speaking at Jacob's well with the Samaritan woman. For great mysteries are spoken there, and the likenesses of great things, feeding the hungry soul, refreshing the weary.

**2.** For when the Lord had heard this, that the Pharisees had learned that he was making more disciples than John, and baptizing more (although Jesus did not baptize, but his disciples), *he left the land of Judea, and went away again into Galilee*[^2]. On this there is no need to dispute at length, lest by lingering on what is plain we suffer shortness of time for scrutinizing and opening up the obscure things. Surely, if the Lord had known that the Pharisees had so learned about him—that he was making more disciples and baptizing more—that this would have availed them to salvation in following him, so that they too might be disciples and themselves wish to be baptized by him, he would all the more not have left the land of Judea, but for their sake would have remained there; but because he knew their knowledge, he knew along with it their envy too, for they did not learn this in order to follow but in order to persecute; and so he went away from there.

He indeed, even when present, could have not been seized by them if he had not willed it; not been killed, if he had not willed it; for he could even have not been born, if he had not willed it. But because in every matter that he did as man he was offering an example to those who would believe in him (for any servant of God does not sin if he withdraws to another place, seeing perhaps the fury of those persecuting him or seeking his life for evil; yet the servant of God would seem to himself to sin if he did this, unless the Lord had gone before him in doing it), this good Master did this in order to teach, not because he was afraid.

## Jesus still baptizes.

**3.** Perhaps this too may trouble someone: why it was said, *Jesus baptized more than John*; and after it was said, *he baptized*, it was added, *although Jesus did not baptize, but his disciples*[^3]. What then? Was a false thing said, and corrected, when it was added, *although Jesus did not baptize, but his disciples*? Or are both true, because Jesus both baptized and did not baptize? For he baptized, because he himself cleansed; he did not baptize, because he himself did not dip in the water. The disciples furnished the ministry of the body; he furnished the help of his majesty. For when would he cease from baptizing, as long as he does not cease from cleansing? Of him it was said by the same John, speaking in the person of John the Baptist, *This is he who baptizes*[^4]. Therefore Jesus still baptizes; and as long as we must be baptized, Jesus baptizes. Let a man come without anxiety to the lower minister, for he has a higher Master.

**4.** But perhaps someone says: Christ indeed baptizes in spirit, not in body—as though anyone were imbued even with the sacrament of bodily and visible Baptism by another's gift than his. Do you wish to know that he himself baptizes, not only in spirit, but also in water? Hear the Apostle: *As Christ*, he says, *loved the Church, and gave himself up for her, cleansing her by the washing of water in the word, that he might present the Church to himself glorious, not having spot or wrinkle or any such thing*[^5]. *Cleansing her*. By what? *By the washing of water in the word*. What is the baptism of Christ? The washing of water in the word. Take away the water, it is not baptism; take away the word, it is not baptism.

**5.** Now, then, these things having been laid down first, by which he came to the conversation with that woman, let us see what remains—things full of mysteries and pregnant with sacred signs. *But it was necessary*, he says, *for him to pass through Samaria. He came, then, to a city of Samaria which is called Sychar, near the estate which Jacob gave to his son Joseph. Now Jacob's spring was there*[^6]. It was a well; but every well is a spring, though not every spring is a well. For where water flows from the earth and is offered for the use of those who draw it, it is called a spring; but if it is ready at hand and on the surface, it is called only a spring; if, however, it is in a deep and low place, it is so called a well that it does not lose the name of spring.

**6.** *Jesus, then, wearied from the journey, was sitting thus by the spring. It was about the sixth hour*[^7]. Now the mysteries begin. For Jesus is not wearied in vain; the Power of God is not wearied in vain; he is not wearied in vain, by whom the wearied are refreshed; he is not wearied in vain, by whose forsaking us we are wearied, by whose presence we are made firm. Yet Jesus is wearied, and wearied from the journey, and sits, and sits beside a well, and at the sixth hour sits wearied. All these things hint at something, mean to indicate something; they make us attentive, they urge us to knock. May he himself, then, open both to us and to you, who deigned so to urge us that he said, *Knock, and it will be opened to you*[^8].

For you Jesus was wearied from the journey. We find a strong Jesus, and we find a weak Jesus: a strong and a weak Jesus. Strong, because *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God: this was in the beginning with God*[^9]. Do you wish to see how strong this Son of God is? *All things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^10]; and they were made without labor. What, then, is stronger than he through whom all things were made without labor? Do you wish to know him weak? *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^11]. The strength of Christ created you; the weakness of Christ re-created you. The strength of Christ brought it about that what was not should be; the weakness of Christ brought it about that what was should not perish. He founded us by his strength; he sought us by his weakness.

## His journey is the flesh taken up for us.

**7.** He, then, being weak, nourishes the weak, like a hen her chicks; for to this he made himself like: *How often*, he says to Jerusalem, *would I have gathered your children under my wings, as a hen gathers her chicks, and you would not?*[^12] But you see, brothers, how a hen grows weak with her chicks. No other bird is recognized as a mother. We see any sparrows you like building nests before our eyes; we daily see swallows, storks, doves building nests; yet, except when we see them in their nests, we do not recognize them as parents. But the hen so grows weak among her chicks that, even if the chicks themselves do not follow, even if you do not see her young, you still recognize the mother. So it is, with wings drooping, feathers bristling, voice hoarse, all her limbs drooping and cast down, that, as I said, even if you do not see her young, you still understand her to be a mother.

So, then, Jesus is weak, wearied from the journey. His journey is the flesh taken up for us. For how does he who is everywhere, who is nowhere absent, have a journey? Where does he go, or whence does he come, except that he would not come to us unless he took up the form of visible flesh? Because, then, he deigned to come to us in this way, to appear in the form of a servant by taking up flesh, that very taking-up of flesh is his journey. Therefore *wearied from the journey*—what else is it than wearied in the flesh? Jesus is weak in the flesh; but do not you be weak; in his weakness be you strong, because *what is weak of God is stronger than men*[^13].

His weakness is our strength.

**8.** Under this image of things Adam, who was *a figure of the one to come*[^14], gave us a great token of a sacred sign; or rather God gave it in him. For he merited even in his sleep to receive a wife, and from his rib a wife was made for him; because from Christ sleeping on the cross the Church was to come from his side, from the side, that is, of one sleeping; for from the side too of him hanging on the cross, struck by the lance, the sacraments of the Church flowed forth.

But why did I wish to say this, brothers? Because the weakness of Christ makes us strong. A great image went before there. God was able to draw flesh from the man, from which to make the woman, and that would seem rather to have been the more fitting. For the weaker sex was being made, and weakness ought rather to have been made from flesh than from bone; for bones are stronger than flesh. He did not draw off flesh, from which to make the woman, but drew off a bone, and, the bone being drawn off, the woman was formed, and in the place of the bone flesh was filled in. He could have restored bone for bone; he could, to make the woman, have drawn off not a rib but flesh. What, then, did he signify? The woman was made in respect of the rib, as it were strong; Adam was made in respect of the flesh, as it were weak. It is Christ and the Church: his weakness is our strength.

**9.** Why, then, the sixth hour? Because it is the sixth age of the world. Reckon in the Gospel as it were one hour, one age, from Adam to Noah; a second from Noah to Abraham; a third from Abraham to David; a fourth from David to the deportation to Babylon; a fifth from the deportation to Babylon to the baptism of John: from there the sixth is being enacted.

Why do you wonder? Jesus came, and by humbling himself he came to the well. He came wearied, because he bore weak flesh. The sixth hour, because it is the sixth age of the world. To the well, because to the depth of this dwelling of ours. Whence it is said in the Psalms, *Out of the depths I have cried to you, O Lord*[^15]. He sat, as I said, because he was humbled.

This woman, who bore the type of the Church, came from foreigners.

**10.** *And a woman came*[^16]. A figure of the Church, not yet justified, but now to be justified; for this is what the discourse is about. She came in ignorance, found him, and the matter is transacted with her. Let us see what, let us see why: *A woman came from Samaria to draw water*. The Samaritans did not belong to the nation of the Jews; for they were foreigners, although they inhabited neighboring lands. It would be long to recount the origin of the Samaritans, lest many things hold us and we fail to speak of necessary matters; it is enough, then, that we reckon the Samaritans among foreigners. And lest you suppose I have said this more boldly than truly, hear the Lord Jesus himself, what he said about that Samaritan, one of the ten lepers whom he had cleansed, who alone returned to give thanks: *Were not ten cleansed? And where are the nine? Was there no one to give glory to God but this foreigner?*[^17]

It belongs to the image of the matter that this woman, who bore the type of the Church, came from foreigners; for the Church was to come from the Gentiles, a foreigner to the race of the Jews. Let us, then, hear ourselves in her, and in her recognize ourselves, and in her give thanks to God for ourselves. For she was a figure, not the truth; because she herself both first showed forth a figure, and became the truth. For she believed in him who was presenting in her a figure to us. She came, then, to draw water. Simply she had come to draw water, as men or women are wont to do.

**11.** *Jesus says to her: Give me to drink. For his disciples had gone away into the city to buy food. That Samaritan woman, then, says to him: How is it that you, being a Jew, ask drink of me, who am a Samaritan woman? For Jews do not associate with Samaritans*[^18]. You see the foreigners: the Jews would not at all use their vessels. And because the woman was carrying with her a vessel with which to draw water, she wondered at this, that a Jew asked drink of her, which the Jews were not wont to do. But he who was asking for drink was thirsting for the faith of that very woman.

The Holy Spirit is the gift of God.

**12.** In short, hear who it is that asks for drink. *Jesus answered and said to her: If you knew the gift of God, and who it is that says to you, Give me to drink, you perhaps would have asked of him, and he would have given you living water*[^19]. He asks for drink, and he promises drink. He is in need, as one about to receive, and he overflows, as one about to satisfy. *If you knew*, he says, *the gift of God*. The gift of God is the Holy Spirit. But he still speaks to the woman in a hidden way, and little by little enters into her heart. Perhaps he is now teaching. For what is sweeter and kinder than this exhortation? *If you knew the gift of God, and knew who it is that says to you, Give me to drink, you perhaps would ask, and he would give you living water*: thus far he held her in suspense.

Living water is the common name for that which comes out from a spring. For that which is collected from rain in pools or cisterns is not called living water. And even if it has flowed from a spring and stood collected in some place, and has not let in to itself that from which it was flowing, but, its passage broken, has been as it were separated from the channel of the spring, it is not called living water; but that water is called living which is received flowing. Such was the water in that spring. What, then, was he promising who was asking?

**13.** Yet the woman, still in suspense, says: *Lord, you have nothing to draw with, and the well is deep*[^20]. See how she understood living water—the water, that is, which was in that spring. You wish to give me living water, and I carry something to draw with, and you do not. The living water is here; how are you going to give it to me? Understanding one thing, and being carnally minded, she in a manner knocks, that the Master may open what is closed. She was knocking out of ignorance, not out of zeal—still to be pitied, not yet to be instructed.

**14.** The Lord says something more plainly about that living water. For the woman had said: *Are you greater than our father Jacob, who gave us the well, and himself drank from it, and his sons, and his cattle?*[^21] This living water you cannot give me, since you have nothing to draw with; do you perhaps promise some other spring? Can you be better than our father, who dug this well, and himself with his household used it?

Let the Lord, then, say what he called living water. *Jesus answered and said to her: Everyone who drinks of this water will thirst again; but he who drinks of the water that I will give him will not thirst forever; but the water that I will give him will become in him a spring of water welling up to eternal life*[^22]. The Lord spoke more openly: *It will become in him a spring of water welling up to eternal life. He who drinks of this water will not thirst forever.* What is plainer than that he was promising not visible but invisible water? What is plainer than that he was speaking not carnally but spiritually?

**15.** Yet still that woman is minded toward the flesh: she was delighted not to thirst, and supposed that this had been promised her by the Lord according to the flesh. This indeed will be, but in the resurrection of the dead. Already she wanted this. For God had once granted to his servant Elijah that for forty days he should neither hunger nor thirst[^23]. He who could grant this for forty days, could he not grant it always?

Yet she was sighing, unwilling to be in need, unwilling to toil. She was compelled to come constantly to that spring, to be burdened with a weight by which her need might be supplied; and when what she had drawn was used up, she was compelled to return again; and this toil was daily for her, because that neediness was refreshed, not extinguished. Delighted, then, with such a gift, she asks that he would give her the living water.

**16.** Nevertheless, let us not pass it by, since the Lord was promising something spiritual. What is, *He who drinks of this water will thirst again*? It is true according to this water; and it is true according to what that water signified. For the water in the well is the pleasure of the world in its dark depth; from there men draw it with the water-jar of desires. For bending down they let down their desire, that they may come to the pleasure drawn from the depth; and they enjoy the pleasure, their desire going before and being sent ahead.

For he who has not sent his desire ahead cannot come to the pleasure. Set down, then, as the water-jar, desire; and as the water from the depth, pleasure: when anyone has come to the pleasure of this world—it is food, it is drink, it is the bath, it is the spectacle, it is intercourse—will he not thirst again? Therefore *he who drinks of this water*, he says, *will thirst again*; but if he receives from me water, he will not thirst forever. *We shall be satisfied*, he says, *with the good things of your house*[^24]. Of what water, then, is he going to give, except of that of which it is said, *With you is the spring of life*[^25]? For how shall they thirst who *will be made drunk with the abundance of your house*[^26]?

**17.** He was promising, then, a certain fullness and satiety of the Holy Spirit; and she did not yet understand; and not understanding, what did she answer? *The woman says to him: Lord, give me this water, that I may not thirst, nor come here to draw*[^27]. Need was compelling her to the toil, and weakness was refusing the toil. Would that she had heard: *Come to me, all you who labor and are burdened, and I will refresh you*[^28]. For this is what Jesus was saying to her, that she might no longer toil; but she did not yet understand.

**18.** In short, willing that she should understand, Jesus says to her: *Go, call your husband, and come here*[^29]. What is, *Call your husband*? Did he wish to give her that water through her husband? Or, because she did not understand, did he wish to teach her through her husband? Perhaps as the Apostle says of women: *But if they wish to learn anything, let them ask their husbands at home*[^30]? But there it is said, *let them ask their husbands at home*, where there is no Jesus to teach; in short, it is said to the women whom the Apostle was forbidding to speak in church. But when the Lord himself was present, and present was speaking to one present, what need was there that he should speak to her through her husband? Did he speak through her husband to Mary sitting at his feet and taking in his word, when Martha, very busy about much serving, was murmuring even about her sister's happiness[^31]?

Therefore, my brothers, let us hear and understand what the Lord says to the woman: *Call your husband*. For perhaps he says even to our soul: Call your husband. Let us also inquire about the husband of the soul. Why is not Jesus himself now the true husband of the soul? Let understanding be present, since what we are going to say is scarcely grasped except by the attentive: let understanding, then, be present that it may be grasped, and that very understanding will perhaps be the husband of the soul.

**19.** Seeing, then, that the woman did not understand, and wishing her to understand, he says, *Call your husband*. This is why you do not know what I say—because your understanding is not present: I am speaking according to the spirit, you are hearing according to the flesh. The things I speak pertain neither to the pleasure of the ears, nor to the eyes, nor to smell, nor to taste, nor to touch; by the mind alone are they grasped, by understanding alone are they drawn in: that understanding is not present to you; how do you grasp what I say? *Call your husband*: present your understanding.

For what is it to you to have a soul? It is no great thing, for the beast too has one. By what are you better? Because you have understanding, which the beast does not have. What, then, is, *Call your husband*? You do not grasp me, you do not understand me: I speak to you of the gift of God, but you are thinking of flesh; you are unwilling to thirst according to the flesh, while I am addressing the spirit; your understanding is absent: *Call your husband*. *Do not be like the horse and the mule, which have no understanding*[^32].

Therefore, my brothers, to have a soul and not to have understanding—that is, not to apply it, nor to live according to it—is a beast's life. For there is in us something beastlike by which we live in the flesh, but it must be ruled by understanding. For the movement of the soul, moving itself according to the flesh and desiring to flow out immoderately into carnal delights, the understanding rules from above. Which ought to be called the husband? The one who rules, or the one who is ruled? Without doubt, when life is well ordered, the understanding rules the soul, while itself belonging to the soul. For it is not something other than the soul, but the understanding is something of the soul; just as the eye is not something other than the flesh, but the eye is something of the flesh. But although the eye is something of the flesh, yet it alone enjoys the light; the other carnal members can be flooded with light, but cannot perceive light; the eye alone is both flooded with it and enjoys it.

So in our soul there is something which is called understanding. This very part of the soul, which is called understanding and mind, is illuminated by a higher light. Now that higher light, by which the human mind is illuminated, is God; for *it was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world*[^33]. Such a light was Christ, such a light was speaking with the woman; and she was not present in understanding by which she might be illuminated by that light, and not only flooded but also enjoy it. Therefore the Lord, as if to say: I wish to illumine, and there is no one present whom I may illumine; *Call*, he says, *your husband*: apply the understanding through which you may be taught, by which you may be ruled.

So, then, set down the soul apart from understanding as a woman, and to have understanding as a husband. But this husband does not rule his woman well unless he himself is ruled by a higher one. For *the head of the woman is the man, but the head of the man is Christ*[^34]. The head of the man was speaking with the woman, and the husband was not present. And as if the Lord said: Apply your head, that he may take up his head: therefore call your husband, and come here; that is, be present, be here; for you are as if absent while you do not understand the voice of the truth present; be present, but do not be alone; with your husband be here.

**20.** And still she, that husband not yet called, does not understand—still she is minded toward the flesh; for the husband is absent: *I have*, she says, *no husband*[^35]. And the Lord follows on, and speaks mysteries. You may understand that in reality this woman did not then have a husband, but was associating with some not lawful husband, an adulterer rather than a husband. And the Lord to her: *You have well said: I have no husband*[^36]. How, then, did you say, Call your husband? And hear that the Lord well knew that she had no husband: *He says to her*, and so on. Lest perhaps the woman should suppose that the Lord said, *You have well said: I have no husband*, because he had learned this from the woman, not because he himself knew it by his divinity, hear something that you did not say: *For you have had five husbands, and the one whom you now have is not your husband; this you have said truly*[^37].

**21.** Again he compels us to search out something more subtle about these five husbands. For many have understood—not indeed absurdly, nor in every way improbably—that the five husbands of this woman are the five books of Moses. For the Samaritans used them, and were under the same Law; for from there they had circumcision as well.

But since what follows constrains us—*and the one whom you now have is not your husband*—it seems to me that we can more easily take the five former husbands of the soul to be the five senses of the body. For when anyone is born, before he can use mind and reason, he is ruled only by the senses of the flesh. The soul in a little child desires or shuns what is heard, what is seen, what is smelled, what is tasted, what is felt by touch. It desires whatever soothes, it shuns whatever offends these five senses. For pleasure soothes these five senses, pain offends them. According to these five senses, as it were five husbands, the soul first lives, because it is ruled by them.

But why are they called husbands? Because they are lawful. For they were made by God, and given by God to the soul. Weak as yet is the soul that is ruled by these five senses, and acts under these five husbands; but when it has come to the years of exercising reason, if the best discipline and the teaching of wisdom takes it up, there succeeds to those five husbands, to rule it, none but a true, lawful husband, better than they, one who rules better, and who rules unto eternity, cultivates unto eternity, instructs unto eternity. For those five senses do not rule us unto eternity, but unto these temporal things, to be either desired or shunned.

But when the understanding, imbued with wisdom, has begun to rule the soul, it now knows not only to shun the pit and to walk on the level, which the eyes show to the weak soul; nor only to hear tuneful voices sweetly and to repel discordant ones; or to be delighted by pleasant smells and to spit out stenches; or to be charmed by sweetness and offended by bitterness; or to be soothed by smooth things and hurt by rough ones. For all these things are necessary to the weak soul. What rule, then, is applied through that understanding? It is not white and black that it is going to distinguish, but just and unjust, good and evil, useful and useless, chastity and unchastity—this that it may love, that that it may avoid; love and hatred—in this that it may abide, in that that it may not.

**22.** This husband had not succeeded to those five husbands in this woman. For where he does not succeed, error reigns. For when the soul has begun to be capable of reason, it is ruled either by a wise mind or by error; but error does not rule, it destroys. After those five senses, then, that woman was still wandering, and error was tossing her about. But this error was not a lawful husband, but an adulterer; therefore the Lord said to her: *You have well said: I have no husband. For you have had five husbands*: the five senses of the flesh first ruled you; you came to the age for using reason, and you did not arrive at wisdom, but fell into error. So after those five husbands, the one whom you now have is not your husband.

And what was it, if it was not a husband, but an adulterer? Call, then, not the adulterer, but your husband; that you may grasp me by understanding, and not, through error, hold some false opinion about me. For the woman was still wandering, who was thinking of that water, while the Lord was already speaking of the Holy Spirit. Why was she wandering, except that she had an adulterer, not a husband? Take away, then, from here this adulterer who corrupts you, and go, call your husband. Call, and come, that you may understand me.

**23.** *The woman says to him: Lord, I see that you are a prophet*[^38]. The husband began to come, he had not yet fully come. She supposed the Lord to be a prophet. He was indeed also a prophet, for he says of himself, *A prophet is not without honor, except in his own country*[^39]. Likewise it was said of him to Moses, *I will raise up for them a prophet from among their brothers, like you*[^40]—like, that is, in the form of the flesh, not in the eminence of his majesty. Therefore we find the Lord Jesus called a prophet. And so this woman is now not greatly mistaken. *I see*, she says, *that you are a prophet*. She begins to call her husband, to shut out the adulterer: *I see that you are a prophet*. And she begins to ask about what was wont to trouble her.

For there had been a dispute between the Samaritans and the Jews, because the Jews adored God in the temple built by Solomon, while the Samaritans, placed far from there, did not adore in it. Therefore the Jews boasted that they were better, because they adored God in the temple. *For Jews do not associate with Samaritans*, because they said to them: How do you boast, and claim to be better than we, because you have a temple which we do not have? Did our fathers, who pleased God, adore in that temple? Did they not adore on this mountain, where we are? Better, then, do we, they say, entreat God on this mountain, where our fathers entreated him. Both sides were contending in ignorance, because they had no husband: those puffed up for the temple, these for the mountain, against each other.

**24.** The Lord, however, what does he now teach the woman, as one whose husband has begun to be present? *The woman says to him: Lord, I see that you are a prophet. Our fathers adored on this mountain, and you say that in Jerusalem is the place where one ought to adore. Jesus says to her: Woman, believe me*[^41]. For the Church will come, as is said in the Song of Songs; she will come and pass over from the beginning of faith. She will come that she may pass over, and she cannot pass over except from the beginning of faith. Deservedly, now that the husband is present, she hears, *Woman, believe me*. For there is now in you one who may believe, because your husband is present. You began to be present in understanding when you called me a prophet. *Woman, believe me*: for *unless you believe, you will not understand*[^42].

Therefore: *Woman, believe me, that an hour is coming when neither on this mountain nor in Jerusalem will you adore the Father. You adore what you do not know; we adore what we know, because salvation is from the Jews. But an hour is coming*[^43]. When? *And now is*. What hour, then? *When the true adorers will adore the Father in spirit and in truth*[^44]—not on that mountain, not in the temple, but in spirit and in truth. *For the Father too seeks such to adore him*[^45].

Why does the Father seek such to adore him—not on a mountain, not in a temple, but in spirit and in truth? *God is spirit*[^46]. If God were a body, he would have to be adored on a mountain, because a mountain is corporeal; he would have to be adored in a temple, because a temple is corporeal. *God is spirit; and those who adore him must adore in spirit and in truth*[^47].

## Within, show yourself a temple to God.

**25.** We have heard, and it is plain; we had gone outside, we have been sent within.

Oh, if I could find, you used to say, some high and solitary mountain! For I believe that God is on high, and the more from on high he hears me.

Because you are on a mountain, do you suppose yourself near to God, and that you are quickly heard, as crying out as if from close by? *He dwells on high, but he looks upon the lowly things*[^48].

*The Lord is near*[^49].

To whom? Perhaps to the lofty?

*To those who are of a bruised heart*[^50].

A wonderful thing: he both dwells on high and draws near to the lowly: *he looks upon the lowly things, but the lofty he knows from afar*[^51]. He sees the proud from afar, and the loftier they seem to themselves, the less near he is to them. Were you seeking a mountain, then? Come down, that you may reach him. But do you wish to ascend? Ascend; do not seek a mountain. *The ascents*, it says, *are in his heart* (so the Psalm says), *in the valley of weeping*[^52]. The valley has lowliness. Therefore do the whole thing within. And if perhaps you are seeking some high place, some holy place, within show yourself a temple to God. *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^53]. Do you wish to pray in a temple? Pray in yourself. But first be a temple of God, because he will hear the one who prays in his temple.

**26.** *The hour comes, then, and now is, when the true adorers will adore the Father in spirit and in truth. We adore what we know, you adore what you do not know, because salvation is from the Jews*[^54]. He gave much to the Jews; but do not take those reprobate ones. Take that wall to which another is joined, so that, made at peace in the cornerstone, which is Christ, they may be coupled together. For there is one wall from the Jews, one from the Gentiles: far apart are those walls, but only until they are joined in the corner. *But the foreigners were strangers, and pilgrims from the covenants of God*[^55]. According to this, then, it was said, *We adore what we know*. It was said in the person of the Jews indeed, but not of all the Jews, not of the reprobate Jews, but of such as the apostles were, such as the prophets were, such as were all those holy ones who sold all their goods and laid the prices of their things at the feet of the apostles. *For God did not reject his people, whom he foreknew*[^56].

**27.** This woman heard this, and added something. She had a while ago called him a prophet; she saw that the one with whom she spoke said such things as were now more than belonged to a prophet; and see what she answered: *The woman says to him: I know that the Messiah is coming, who is called the Christ; when, then, he comes, he will show us all things*[^57]. What is this? Now, she says, the Jews are contending about the temple, and we are contending about the mountain; when he comes, he will both spurn the mountain and overthrow the temple; he will teach us all things, that we may know how to adore in spirit and in truth. She knew who could teach her, but she did not yet recognize the one who was teaching. Now, then, she was worthy that it should be revealed to her. Now "Messiah" is "anointed"; "anointed" in Greek is "Christ"; in Hebrew it is "Messiah"; whence also in Punic "Messe" is said for "anoint." For these tongues are kindred and neighbors—Hebrew, Punic, and Syriac.

**28.** So, then, *the woman says to him: I know that the Messiah is coming, who is called the Christ; when, then, he comes, he will announce all things to us. Jesus says to her: I am he, who am speaking with you*[^58]. She called her husband; her husband became the head of the woman, Christ became the head of the husband. Now the woman is set in order in faith, and is ruled so as to live well. After she heard this, *I am he, who am speaking with you*, what could she now say further, when Christ the Lord willed to manifest himself to the woman to whom he had said, *Believe me*?

**29.** *And immediately his disciples came, and wondered that he was speaking with a woman*[^59]. Because he was seeking the lost, he who had come to seek what had perished, this they wondered at. For they wondered at a good thing, they did not suspect anything evil. Yet no one said, *What are you seeking, or why are you speaking with her?*[^60]

**30.** *The woman, then, left her water-jar*[^61]. Having heard, *I am he, who am speaking with you*, and having received Christ the Lord into her heart, what should she do but now leave the water-jar and run to evangelize? She threw away desire, and hastened to announce the truth. Let those who wish to evangelize learn: let them throw the water-jar down at the well. Recall what I said above about the water-jar: it was the vessel from which water was drawn; it is called by a Greek name "hydria," because in Greek "water" is "hydor"—as if one were to say "water-pot." She threw away, then, the water-jar, which now was not for use but for a burden; for greedily she had desired to be satisfied with that water. To announce Christ, with the burden cast off, she ran to the city, and says to those men, *Come and see a man who told me everything that I ever did*. Step by step—lest they should as it were be angered, and indignant, and persecute. *Come and see a man who told me everything that I ever did; can he be the Christ?*[^62] *They went out of the city, and came to him*[^63].

**31.** *And meanwhile the disciples were asking him, saying: Rabbi, eat. For they had gone to buy food, and had come back. But he said: I have food to eat that you do not know. The disciples, then, said to one another: Has anyone brought him something to eat?*[^64] What wonder if that woman did not understand the water? Behold, the disciples do not yet understand the food. But he heard their thoughts, and now instructs them as a master—not roundabout, as he did with her whose husband he was still requiring, but now openly: *My food*, he says, *is to do the will of him who sent me*[^65].

Therefore his drink too was in that woman, that he might do the will of him who had sent him. This is why he said, *I thirst, give me to drink*; namely, that he might work faith in her, and drink her faith, and transfer her into his own body; for his body is the Church. He himself, then, he says, *is my food, that I may do the will of him who sent me*.

**32.** *Do you not say that there are yet four months, and the harvest comes?*[^66] He was burning for the work, and was arranging to send out laborers. You reckon four months until the harvest; I show you another harvest, white and ready. *Behold, I say to you, lift up your eyes and see that the regions are already white for the harvest*[^67]. He is going, then, to send reapers. For in this is the saying true, that *one is he who reaps, and another he who sows; so that he who sows and he who reaps may rejoice together. I sent you to reap what you did not labor for; others labored, and you have entered into their labor*[^68].

What then? Did he send reapers, not sowers? Where the reapers? Where others had already labored. For where there had already been labor, surely there had been sowing; and what had been sown was now made ripe, and was calling for the sickle and the threshing. Where, then, were the reapers to be sent? Where the prophets had already preached; for they are the sowers. For if they were not the sowers, whence had it come to that woman, *I know that the Messiah is coming*? Now that woman was ripe fruit, and the harvests were white, and were calling for the sickle. *I sent you*, then: where? To reap what you did not sow: *others sowed, and you have entered into their labors*.

Who labored? Abraham himself, Isaac, and Jacob. Read their labors: in all their labors is the prophecy of Christ; and therefore they are the sowers. Moses and the other patriarchs and all the prophets—how much did they endure in that cold when they were sowing? Therefore now in Judea the harvest was ready. Deservedly there was a ripe crop there, when so many thousands of men brought the prices of their goods and, laying them at the feet of the apostles, with shoulders freed from worldly burdens, followed Christ the Lord: truly a ripe harvest.

What came of this? From that harvest a few grains were cast out, and they sowed the whole world, and another harvest rises which is to be reaped at the end of the world. Of this harvest it is said, *Those who sow in tears will reap in joy*[^69]. To this harvest, then, not the apostles, but the angels will be sent: *The reapers*, he says, *are the angels*[^70]. This harvest, then, grows among the weeds, and waits to be purged at the end.

But that harvest was already ripe, to which the disciples were first sent, where the prophets had labored. Yet, brothers, see what was said: *That he who sows and he who reaps may rejoice together*. They had labors of different times; but they will enjoy joy alike, they are about to receive together the reward, eternal life.

Christ is announced through Christian friends.

**33.** *Now from that city many of the Samaritans believed in him, because of the word of the woman bearing witness: He told me everything that I ever did. When the Samaritans, then, had come to him, they asked him to remain with them; and he remained there two days. And many more believed because of his word; and they said to the woman: We now believe, not because of your speech; for we ourselves have heard, and we know that this is truly the Savior of the world*[^71].

And this little is to be noticed, because the reading is finished. The woman announced first, and at the woman's testimony the Samaritans believed, and asked him to remain with them, and he remained there two days, and more believed; and when they had believed, they said to the woman: *We no longer believe because of your word, but we ourselves have come to know, and we know that this is truly the Savior of the world*—first by report, afterward by his presence. So it is done today with those who are outside, and are not yet Christians: Christ is announced through Christian friends; as if through that woman, that is, through the Church announcing him, they come to Christ, they believe through this report; he remains with them two days, that is, he gives them the two commandments of love; and many more, and more firmly, believe in him, because he is truly the Savior of the world.
