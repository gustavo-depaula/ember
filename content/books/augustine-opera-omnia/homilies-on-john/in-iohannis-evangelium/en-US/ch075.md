# Tractate 75

## *In that day you will know that I am in my Father, and you in me, and I in you* (Jn 14:17-21).

*In that day you will know.* In what day but that of which he says: *and you will live*? For then it will come about that we can see what we believe. For even now he is in us, and we in him; but this we now believe, then we shall also know, although even now by believing we have come to know it, but then by contemplating we shall know it.

**1.** After the promise of the Holy Spirit, lest anyone should think that the Lord was going to give him as it were in his own stead, so that he himself would not also be with them, he added and said: *I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you*[^1]. "Orphans" are wards. For the former is the Greek name for the same thing, the latter the Latin: for in the psalm too, where we read: *You will be a helper to the orphan*, the Greek has "orphan." Although, then, the Son of God adopted us as sons to his Father, and willed that we have the same Father through grace who is his Father by nature; yet he himself too shows in a certain way a fatherly affection toward us, when he says: *I will not leave you orphans; I will come to you.* Hence it is that he also calls us the bridegroom's sons, where he says: *The hour will come that the bridegroom is taken from them, and then the sons of the bridegroom will fast*[^2]. But who is the bridegroom, but the Lord Christ?

**2.** Then there follows, and he says: *Yet a little while, and the world now sees me no more*[^3]. What? did the world then see him; since by the name of the world he wishes to be understood those of whom he spoke above, saying of the Holy Spirit: *Whom the world cannot receive, because it does not see him, nor know him*? The world plainly saw him with carnal eyes, conspicuous in the flesh, but it did not see what lay hidden in the flesh, the Word: it saw the man, it did not see God; it saw the garment, it did not see him who was clothed. But since after the resurrection he willed to show even that flesh of his, which he showed to his own not only to be seen but even to be handled, not to those who were not his own; hence perhaps it is to be understood that it was said: *Yet a little while, and the world now sees me no more: but you will see me; because I live, and you will live*[^4].

## He promises two resurrections, his own and ours.

**3.** What is, *because I live, and you will live*? Why did he say of himself that he lives in the present, but of them that they would live in the future, except that the life even of the flesh that surely rises again, such as preceded in him, he promised would follow also for them? And because his own resurrection was soon to be, he set down a verb of the present time to signify its swiftness: but theirs, since it is deferred to the end of the age, he did not say, you live; but, *you will live.* Two resurrections, then, his own namely soon to be, and ours to come at the end of the age, he elegantly and briefly promised by two verbs of the present and future time. *Because I,* he says, *live, and you will live*: because he lives, therefore we too shall live. *For by a man came death, and by a man the resurrection of the dead. For as in Adam all die, so in Christ all will be made alive*[^5]. Since no one comes to death except through him, no one to life except through Christ. Because we lived for ourselves, we died: because he lives, we shall live. We died to him, when we lived for ourselves: but because he died for us, he lives both for himself and for us. For because he lives, we too shall live. For as through ourselves we could have death, not so can we have life through ourselves.

**4.** *In that day,* he says, *you will know that I am in my Father, and you in me, and I in you*[^6]. In what day but that of which he says, *and you will live*? For then it will come about that we can see what we believe. For even now he is in us, and we in him: but this we now believe, then we shall also know; although even now by believing we have come to know it, but then by contemplating we shall know it. *For as long as we are in the body* such as it now is, that is, corruptible, which weighs down the soul, *we are pilgrims from the Lord: for we walk by faith, not by sight*[^7]. Then, therefore, by sight, *for we shall see him as he is*[^8]. For if even now Christ were not in us, the Apostle would not say: *But if Christ is in you, the body indeed is dead because of sin, but the spirit is life because of righteousness*[^9]. But that we too even now are in him, he shows clearly enough, where he says: *I am the vine, you the branches*[^10]. In that day, then, when we shall live with that life by which death will be swallowed up, we shall know that he is in the Father, and we in him, and he in us; because then will be perfected this very thing which even now is begun already through him, that he be in us and we in him.

**5.** *He who has,* he says, *my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me*[^11]. He who has them in memory, and keeps them in life; who has them in words, and keeps them in conduct; who has them by hearing, and keeps them by doing; or who has them by doing, and keeps them by persevering; *he it is,* he says, *who loves me.* Love is to be shown by work, lest the calling by the name be fruitless. *And he who loves me,* he says, *will be loved by my Father; and I will love him, and will manifest myself to him*[^12]. What is *I will love*? as though he will love then, and now does not love? Far be it. For how would the Father love us without the Son, or the Son without the Father? How, since they work inseparably, do they love separably? But he said, *I will love him,* with a view to that which follows, *and will manifest myself to him. I will love, and will manifest*; that is, I will love to this end, that I may manifest. For now he loved to this end, that we may believe, and hold the commandment of faith; then he will love to this end, that we may see, and receive the very vision as the reward of faith: because we too now love, by believing what we shall see; but then we shall love, by seeing what we believe.
