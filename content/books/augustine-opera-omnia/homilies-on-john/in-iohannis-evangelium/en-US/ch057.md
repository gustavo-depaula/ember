# Tractate 57

## *He who is washed has no need but to wash his feet* (Jn 13:10).

When we preach you, we tread the earth in order to open to you, Christ; and therefore, if we are blamed, we are disturbed; if we are praised, we are puffed up. Wash our feet, cleansed before, but soiled when we go through the earth to open to you.  
The Church fears to soil its feet while it goes to Christ.

**1.** Not unmindful of my debt, I now recognize the time to pay it. May he grant whence I may pay, who granted that I should owe. For he granted the love of which it was said: *Owing nothing to anyone, except to love one another*[^1]; may he grant also the discourse which I see I owe to the beloved. For I had deferred your expectation to this end, that I might explain as I could how Christ is come to even through the earth; though we are rather bidden to seek the things that are above, not the things that are on the earth. For Christ is above, sitting at the right hand of the Father: but assuredly he is here too; on account of which he says to Saul raging on the earth: *Why do you persecute me*[^2]?

But that we might undertake this inquiry, the matter being treated was that the Lord washed the disciples' feet, when the disciples themselves were already washed, and had no need but to wash their feet. Where it seemed to be understood that a man is indeed washed all over by Baptism; but while he afterward lives in this world, treading the earth as it were with his feet by his human affections, that is, by the very conversation of this life, he contracts that whereof he says: *Forgive us our debts*. And so from this too he is cleansed by him who washed the feet of his disciples, and does not cease to intercede for us. Hence there came up the words of the Church from the Song of Songs, saying: *I have washed my feet; how shall I soil them*? When it wished to go and open to him who had come to it, and had knocked, and had asked that it open to him, he, *beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*[^3]. Hence arose the question which we were unwilling to cramp within the narrowness of time, and therefore deferred: in what way the Church fears to soil its feet, while it goes to Christ, which feet it had washed in the baptism of Christ.

**2.** For it says thus: *I sleep, and my heart watches; the voice of my kinsman knocks at the door.* Then he too says: *Open to me, my sister, my neighbor, my dove, my perfect one; for my head is filled with dew, and my locks with the drops of the night*. And she answers: *I have put off my tunic; how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet; how shall I soil them*[^4]? O wonderful sacrament! O great mystery! Does it then fear to soil its feet, by coming to him who washed the feet of his disciples? It fears, surely, because it comes through the earth to him who is also on the earth, since he does not forsake his own who are established here. Or does he not himself say: *Behold, I am with you always, to the consummation of the age*[^5]? Or does he not himself say: *You will see the heavens opened, and the angels of God ascending and descending to the Son of man*[^6]? If they ascend to him because he is above, how do they descend to him, if he is not also here?

The Church says, then: *I have washed my feet; how shall I soil them?* It says this in those who, cleansed of all filth, can say: *I desire to be dissolved and to be with Christ, but to remain in the flesh is more necessary for your sake*[^7]. It says this in those who preach Christ and open the door to him, that he may dwell through faith in the hearts of men. It says this in them, when they deliberate whether to undertake such a ministry, for which they think themselves less fit, so as to fulfill it without fault, lest perhaps, while preaching to others, they themselves become reprobate[^8]. For the truth is more safely heard than preached: since, when it is heard, humility is kept; but when it is preached, scarcely does not some little boasting steal upon any one of men, in which surely the feet are soiled.

**3.** Therefore, as the apostle James says: *Let every man be swift to hear, but slow to speak*[^9]. Another man of God too says: *To my hearing you will give joy and gladness, and the humbled bones shall exult*[^10]. This is what I said: When the truth is heard, humility is kept. Another too says: *But the friend of the bridegroom stands and hears him, and rejoices with joy because of the voice of the bridegroom*[^11]. Let us enjoy the hearing, the truth speaking to us within without noise. Yet even when it sounds from without through one who reads, who announces, who preaches, who disputes, who commands, who consoles, who exhorts, even through one who sings and chants psalms; let those very ones who do these things fear to soil their feet, when, by a stealthy love of human praise, they seek to please men. But he who hears them willingly and devoutly has no occasion to boast of himself in the labors of others; and, not with bones puffed up, but humbled, rejoices with joy because of the voice of the Lord's truth.

Accordingly, in those who have learned to hear willingly and humbly, and who lead a quiet life in sweet and wholesome studies, let the holy Church delight, and say: *I sleep, and my heart watches.* What is *I sleep, and my heart watches,* but, I so rest that I hear? My leisure is not spent in nourishing idleness, but in receiving wisdom. *I sleep, and my heart watches*: I am at leisure, and I see that you are the Lord: because *the wisdom of the scribe comes in the time of leisure; and he who is lessened in action shall receive it*[^12]. *I sleep, and my heart watches*: I rest from busy actions, and my mind directs itself to divine affections.

## The voice of Christ knocks at the door.

**4.** But in those who in this way sweetly and humbly rest, while the Church takes its delight at leisure, behold, he knocks who says: *What I tell you in the darkness, speak in the light; and what you hear in the ear, preach upon the housetops*[^13]. His voice, then, knocks at the door, and says: *Open to me, my sister, my neighbor, my dove, my perfect one; for my head is filled with dew, and my locks with the drops of the night*. As if he said: You are at leisure, and the door is shut against me: you study the leisure of a few, and, iniquity abounding, the love of many grows cold[^14]. For night is iniquity: but its dew and drops, these are they who grow cold and fall, and make the head of Christ grow cold, that is, so that God is not loved. For the head of Christ is God[^15]. But they are carried in the locks, that is, they are borne with in the visible sacraments: they by no means touch the inner things of the mind.

He knocks, then, to shake off rest from the holy ones at leisure, and cries out: *Open to me,* by my blood *my sister,* by my approach *my neighbor,* by my Spirit *my dove,* by my discourse which you have more fully learned out of your leisure *my perfect one*; open to me, preach me. For to those who have shut the door against me, how shall I enter without one to open? *For how shall they hear without one to preach*[^16]?

**5.** Hence it comes about that even those who love the leisure of good studies, and are unwilling to endure the troubles of laborious actions, because they feel themselves less fit to administer these things and to do them without blame, would prefer, if it could be done, that the holy apostles and the ancient preachers of the truth be roused up against the abundance of iniquity by which the fervor of love has grown cold. But in those who have already gone forth from the body, and are stripped of the garment of the flesh (for they are not separated from it), the Church answers: *I have put off my tunic; how shall I put it on?* That tunic will indeed be received back, and in those who are already stripped the Church will again be clothed with flesh: yet not now, when the cold are to be warmed, but then, when the dead are to rise.

Having suffered, then, difficulty because of the lack of preachers, and recalling those members of hers, sound in discourse, holy in conduct, but now stripped of their bodies, the Church groans and says: *I have put off my tunic; how shall I put it on?* Those members of mine, who could most excellently open to Christ by preaching the gospel, how can they now return to the bodies of which they were stripped?

## But behold, I rise, and open.

**6.** Then, looking to those who are able somehow to preach, and to gain and govern peoples, and so to open to Christ, but who fear to sin amid these difficulties of action: *I have washed,* it says, *my feet; how shall I soil them?* For whoever does not offend in word, he is a perfect man. And who is perfect? who is there who does not offend in so great an abundance of iniquity, so great a coldness of love? *I have washed my feet; how shall I soil them?* Since I read, and hear: *Do not become many masters, brothers, since you incur a greater judgment: for in many things we all offend*[^17]. *I have washed my feet; how shall I soil them?*

But behold, I rise, and open. Christ, wash them: *Forgive us our debts,* since our love is not extinguished: because *we too forgive our debtors*. When we hear you, the humbled bones exult with you in the heavenly places. But when we preach you, we tread the earth in order to open to you: and therefore, if we are blamed, we are disturbed; if we are praised, we are puffed up. Wash our feet, cleansed before, but soiled when we go through the earth to open to you.

Let these things be enough for you today, dearly beloved. If perhaps, saying anything otherwise than was fitting, we have offended, or have been lifted up by your praises more immoderately than was fitting; obtain cleansing for our feet by your prayers pleasing to God.
