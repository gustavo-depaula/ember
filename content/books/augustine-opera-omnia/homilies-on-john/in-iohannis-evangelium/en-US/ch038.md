# Tractate 38

## *I am the beginning, who also speak to you* (Jn 8:21-25).

## Who are you, that we may believe? And he: The beginning. Behold what it is to be. The beginning cannot be changed; the beginning abides in itself, and renews all things; the beginning is that to which it is said: *But you are the same, and your years will not fail*.

**1.** The reading of the holy Gospel which preceded today's had so concluded, that the Lord spoke, teaching in the treasury, what he willed, and what you have heard: and no one seized him, because his hour had not yet come[^1]. Hence there was a discussion on the Lord's day, which he himself deigned to grant. We intimated to your Charity why it was said: His hour had not yet come; lest any impiety should wickedly dare to suspect Christ to be set under some fatal necessity. For the hour had not yet come in which, by his own order, according to the things foretold of him, he would not unwillingly be compelled to die, but, ready, be killed.

## How Christ is to be sought.

**2.** But now of his own Passion, which was set not in his necessity, but his power, he spoke to the Jews, saying: *I go.* For to Christ the Lord, death was a departure thither whence he had come, and whence he had not withdrawn. *I*, he says, *go, and you will seek me*[^2]: not from desire, but from hatred. For after he withdrew from the eyes of men, both those who hated and those who loved sought him: the former by persecuting, the latter by desiring to have him. In the Psalms the Lord himself says through the prophet: *Flight has perished from me, and there is no one who seeks my soul*[^3]: and again he says in another place in the Psalm: *Let them be confounded and put to shame who seek my soul*[^4]. He blamed those who did not seek, he condemned those who sought. For it is evil not to seek the soul of Christ, but in the way the disciples sought it; and it is evil to seek the soul of Christ, but in the way the Jews sought it: for the former that they might have it, the latter that they might destroy it.

Finally, because these men sought thus in a bad manner, with a perverse heart, what did he go on to add? You will seek me, and—lest you think that you will seek me well—*you will die in your sin*[^5]. This is to seek Christ badly: to die in one's sin: this is to hate him through whom alone one could be saved. For whereas men whose hope is in God ought not to return evil even for evil, these returned evil for good. The Lord, then, foretold to them, and being foreknowing spoke the sentence, that they would die in their sin. Then he adds: *Where I go, you cannot come*. This he said to the disciples too in another place; yet he did not say to them: You will die in your sin. But what did he say? What he said to these: Where I go, you cannot come. He did not take away hope, but foretold delay. For when the Lord said this to the disciples, then they could not come where he was going, but afterward they were to come: but these never, to whom, being foreknowing, he said: You will die in your sin.

**3.** But these words being heard, in the way they are accustomed who think of fleshly things, and judge according to the flesh, and hear and relish all things carnally, they said: *Surely he will not kill himself; because he said: Where I go, you cannot come?*[^6] Foolish words, and altogether full of unwisdom. For what? could they not come thither where he had gone, if he killed himself? Were they themselves not going to die? What is it, then: Surely he will not kill himself; because he said: Where I go, you cannot come? If he spoke of the death of a man, what man does not die? Therefore by "where I go" he meant, not where one goes when one goes to death, but where he himself was going after death. They, then, not understanding, answered these things.

## Christ from on high, that is, from the Father.

**4.** And the Lord, to those who relished earth, what does he say? *And he said to them: You are from below.* Therefore you relish earth, because like serpents you eat earth.

What is, you eat earth?

You are fed on earthly things, you are delighted by earthly things, you gape after earthly things, you do not have your heart above. You are from below: I am from on high. You are of this world: *I am not of this world*[^7]. For how was he of the world, through whom the world was made? All are of the world after the world; because first the world, and so man of the world: but first Christ, then the world; since before the world Christ, before Christ nothing: because in the beginning was the Word; all things were made through him[^8]. So, then, he was from on high.

From what on high? From the air?

Far be it: there the birds too fly.

From the heaven that we see?

This too, far be it; there the stars and the sun and the moon go round.

From the angels?

Do not understand this either: through him the angels too were made, through whom all things were made.

From what on high, then, is Christ? From the Father himself. Nothing is higher than that God, who begot the Word equal to himself, coeternal with himself, only-begotten, without time, through whom he founded times.

So, then, understand Christ to be from on high, that you may exceed in your thought everything that has been made; absolutely every creature, every body, every created spirit, every thing in any way changeable: exceed the whole, as John exceeded it, that he might touch: In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God[^9].

**5.** I, then, he says, am from on high. You are of this world: I am not of this world. I have told you, then, that you will die in your sins[^10]. He expounded to us, brothers, what he wished to be understood: You are of this world. For he said: You are of this world, because they were sinners, because they were unjust, because they were unbelievers, because they relished earthly things.

For what seems to you of the holy Apostles? How much was there between the Jews and the Apostles? How much between darkness and light, how much between faith and unbelief, how much between devotion and impiety, how much between hope and despair, how much between love and cupidity: much, then, was between them. What then? because there was so much between them, were the Apostles not of the world? If you think how they were born, and whence they came; because they all came from Adam, they were of this world. But what did the Lord himself say to them? *I have chosen you out of the world*[^11]. Those, then, who were of the world, were made not of the world, and began to belong to him through whom the world was made. But these remained of the world, to whom it was said: You will die in your sins.

**6.** Let no one, then, say, brothers: I am not of this world. Whoever you are, O man, you are of this world: but he came to you who made the world, and freed you from this world. If the world delights you, you always wish to be unclean; but if this world no longer delights you, now you are clean. But yet if through some weakness the world still delights you, let him dwell in you who cleanses, and you will be clean. But if you have been made clean, you will not remain in the world; nor will you hear what the Jews heard: You will die in your sins.

For we were all born with sin; we all by living added to that for which we were born, and we became more of the world than then when we were born of our parents. And where would we be, if he had not come, who had no sin at all, that he might loose all sin? In whom because the Jews did not believe, they deservedly heard: You will die in your sins: because to have no sin you could in no way, who were born with sin; but yet if you believe in me, he says, you were indeed born with sin, but you will not die in your sin.

The whole unhappiness of the Jews, then, was this—not to have sin, but to die in their sins. This is what every Christian ought to flee: for this we run to Baptism; for this those who are imperiled by sickness or otherwise desire to be helped; for this even the suckling infant is carried by its mother in pious hands to the Church, lest it depart without Baptism, and die in the sin in which it was born. Most unhappy condition, wretched lot of those who heard from the truthful mouth: You will die in your sins.

## Hope is restored to the despairing.

**7.** Yet whence this should befall them, he expounds: *For if you do not believe that I am, you will die in your sins*[^12]. I believe, brothers, that in that multitude which heard the Lord, there were also those who were to believe. But since that most severe sentence had gone forth as if upon all: You will die in your sin; and thereby even from those who were to believe hope had been taken away: they raged, they feared; indeed they did not fear, but already despaired. He recalled them to hope; for he added: If you do not believe that I am, you will die in your sins. Therefore if you believe that I am, you will not die in your sins. Hope was restored to the despairing, a rousing was made to the sleeping, they awoke in their hearts: thence very many believed, as the sequel of the Gospel itself attests.

For there were there members of Christ, which had not yet adhered to the body of Christ: and in that people by whom he was crucified, by whom he was hung on the wood, by whom hanging he was mocked, by whom he was wounded with a lance, by whom he was given gall and vinegar to drink, there were members of Christ, for whom he said: Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do[^13]. But what is not forgiven to one converted, if the shed blood of Christ is forgiven? What murderer should despair, if he is restored to hope by whom even Christ was killed? Thence many believed; the blood of Christ was granted to them, that they might rather drink it whereby they would be freed, than be held guilty of its shedding: who should despair?

And if on the cross the robber was saved—a little before a murderer, a little after accused, convicted, condemned, hung, freed—do not marvel[^14]. Where convicted, there condemned: but there freed, where changed. In this people, then, to whom the Lord was speaking, there were those who were to die in their sin: there were also those who were to believe in him who was speaking, and to be freed from all sin.

**8.** Yet attend to what the Lord Christ says: If you do not believe that I am, you will die in your sins. What is: If you do not believe that I am? I am, what? He added nothing; and because he added nothing, it is much that he commended. For it was expected that he would say what he was, and yet he did not say.

What was expected that he would say? Perhaps: Unless you believe that I am the Christ; unless you believe that I am the Son of God; unless you believe that I am the Word of the Father; unless you believe that I am the founder of the world; unless you believe that I am the former and re-former of man, the creator and re-creator, the maker and re-maker: unless you believe this, that I am, you will die in your sins.

It is much that he said this very thing: I am: because so God too said to Moses: *I am who am.* Who can worthily express what "am" is? God was sending through his angel his servant Moses to free his people from Egypt (you have read what you have heard, and you know; yet I recall it); he was sending him trembling, excusing himself, but obedient. When, then, he excused himself, he says to God, whom he understood to be speaking in the angel: If the people say to me: And who is the God who sent you, what shall I say to them? And the Lord to him: *I am who am*: and he repeated: *You shall say to the sons of Israel: He who is has sent me to you.* He did not say there either: I am God; or: I am the maker of the world; or: I am the creator of all things; or: I am the propagator of this very people to be freed: but only this: I am who am; and: You shall say to the sons of Israel: He who is; he did not add: He who is your God, He who is the God of your fathers, but only said this: *He who is has sent me to you*[^15].

Perhaps it was much even for Moses himself, as it is much even for us, and much more for us, to understand what was said: I am who am; and: He who is has sent me to you. And if perhaps Moses grasped it, when would those grasp it to whom he was sent? The Lord, then, deferred what man could not grasp, and added what he could grasp: for he joined and said: *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*[^16]. This you can grasp: for "I am who am," what mind can grasp it?  
Shall I dare to question the Lord himself?

**9.** What then of us? shall we dare to say anything from what was said: I am who am: or rather from what you have heard the Lord say: Unless you believe that I am, you will die in your sins? Shall I, then, with these my so little and almost no strength, dare to discuss what it is that the Lord Christ says: Unless you believe that I am? I will dare to question the Lord himself: hear me questioning rather than discussing, seeking rather than presuming, learning rather than teaching, and certainly in me or through me question also yourselves. The Lord himself too, who is everywhere, is at hand; let him hear the affection of one questioning, and grant the effect of understanding. For with what words, even if perhaps I grasp something, can I bring what I grasp to your hearts? What sound suffices? what eloquence is at hand? what strength of understanding? what faculty of bringing forth?

**10.** I will say, then, to our Lord Jesus Christ, I will say, and let him hear me. I believe him present, I do not doubt at all; for he himself said: *Behold, I am with you until the consummation of the world*[^17].

O Lord our God, what is it that you said: Unless you believe that I am? For what is there not of the things you have made? Is not heaven? is not earth? are not the things that are in earth and in heaven? is not man himself to whom you speak? is not the angel whom you send? If all these things are, which were made through you, what is it that you have kept for yourself something proper—to be itself—which you did not give to others, that you alone might be? For how do I hear: I am who am; as though other things were not? and how do I hear: Unless you believe that I am? For were they not, who were hearing? And if they were sinners, they were men. What, then, do I do? Let him say to the heart what "to be" itself is, let him say it within, let him speak within; let the inner man hear, let the mind grasp that truly to be is to be always in the same way.

(For any thing whatsoever: as though I began to discuss, and ceased to inquire; perhaps I wish to speak what I have heard, may he give exultation to my hearing and to yours when I speak): any thing whatsoever, of whatever excellence, if it is changeable, is not truly; for there is not true being where there is also not-being. For whatever can be changed, when changed, is not what it was: if it is not what it was, a certain death has taken place there; something that was there has been destroyed, and is not. Blackness has died in the head of the whitening old man, beauty has died in the body of the wearied and bent old man, strength has died in the body of the languishing, standing has died in the body of the walker, walking has died in the body of the stander, walking and standing have died in the body of the one lying down, speech has died on the tongue of the silent: whatever is changed and is what it was not, I see there a certain life in what is, and death in what was. Finally, of a dead man, when it is asked: Where is that man? it is answered: He was.

O truth, who truly are! For in all our actions and motions, and in absolutely every agitation of the creature, I find two times, past and future. I seek the present, nothing stands still: what I have said is now not; what I am going to say is not yet: what I have done is now not; what I am going to do is not yet: what I have lived is now not; what I am going to live is not yet. Past and future I find in every motion of things: in the truth that abides, past and future I do not find, but only present, and that incorruptibly, which in the creature is not. Examine the changes of things, you will find Was and Will Be: think of God, you will find Is, where Was and Will Be cannot be.

That you too, then, may be, transcend time. But who will transcend by his own strength? Let him lift him thither who said to the Father: *I will that where I am, they too may be with me*[^18]. And so, promising that we shall not die in our sins, the Lord Jesus Christ seems to me to have said nothing else by these words, Unless you believe that I am: absolutely nothing else does he seem to me to have said by these words than this: Unless you believe that I am God, you will die in your sins. Well: thanks be to God, that he said: Unless you believe: he did not say: Unless you grasp. For who could grasp this? Or truly, because I dared to speak, and you seemed to understand, have you grasped something of so great an ineffability? If, then, you do not grasp, faith frees you. Therefore the Lord did not say: Unless you grasp that I am; but he said what they were able to do: Unless you believe that I am, you will die in your sins.

## Abiding in himself and renewing all things.

**11.** And they, always relishing earthly things and always hearing and answering according to the flesh, what did they say to him? *Who are you?*[^19] For when you said: Unless you believe that I am, you did not add what you were. Who are you, that we may believe? And he: The beginning. Behold what it is to be. The beginning cannot be changed: the beginning abides in itself, and renews all things; the beginning is that to which it is said: *But you are the same, and your years will not fail*[^20]. The beginning, he says, because I also speak to you[^21]. Believe me to be the beginning, that you may not die in your sins. For as though by what they said: Who are you? they said nothing else than: What do we believe you to be? he answered: The beginning; that is: Believe me to be the beginning.

For in the Greek tongue something is distinguished which cannot be in Latin. For among the Greeks "beginning" (*principium*) is of the feminine gender, just as among us "law" is of the feminine gender, which among them is masculine: as "wisdom" both among us and among them is of the feminine gender. The custom of speech therefore varies the genders of words through the diverse languages, because in the things themselves you do not find sex. For wisdom is not truly a woman, since Christ is the Wisdom of God[^22], and Christ is called by the masculine gender, wisdom by the feminine.

When, then, the Jews said: Who are you? he who knew that there were among them certain who were to believe, and who had therefore said: Who are you? that they might know what they ought to believe him to be, answered: The beginning: not as though he said: I am the beginning; but as though he said: Believe me to be the beginning. Which in the Greek speech, as I said, evidently appears, where "beginning" is of the feminine gender.

As if he wished to say that he was the truth, and to those saying: Who are you? answered: The truth; whereas it would seem that to what was said: Who are you? he ought to have answered: I am the truth.

But he answered more deeply, when he saw them to have said: Who are you? as if they said: Since from you we have heard: Unless you believe that I am, what shall we believe you to be? to this he answered: The beginning: as if he said: Believe me to be the beginning. And he added, because I also speak to you: that is, because, made humble for your sake, I have descended to these words. For if the beginning, as it is, so remained with the Father, that it did not take the form of a servant and as man speak to men; how would they believe in him, since weak hearts could not hear the intelligible Word without a sensible voice? Therefore, he says, believe me to be the beginning; because, that you may believe, I not only am, but also speak to you.

But on this matter it is now much to speak to you: let it please your Charity, then, that what remains we keep, with his help, to render tomorrow.
