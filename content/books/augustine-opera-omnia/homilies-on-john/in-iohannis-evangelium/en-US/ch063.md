# Tractate 63

## *Now is the Son of man glorified* (Jn 13:31-32).

More deeply must the saying of the Most High be searched out, who reveals himself somewhat that we may find, and again hides himself that we may inquire, and that from the things found we may press on, as by steps, to the things to be found. I see here something that prefigures something great. Judas went out, and Jesus was glorified.  
Let us seek him to be found, let us seek him having been found.

**1.** Let us direct the gaze of the mind, and, with the Lord's help, seek God. The voice of the divine song is: *Seek God, and your soul shall live*[^1]. Let us seek him to be found, let us seek him having been found. That he may be sought to be found, he is hidden; that, having been found, he may be sought, he is immense. Whence it is said elsewhere: *Seek his face always*[^2]. For he satisfies the seeker as far as he can grasp; and he makes the finder more capacious, that he may again seek to be filled, when he shall have begun to grasp more. Therefore it was not said: *Seek his face always,* in the way it is said of certain ones: *Always learning, and never coming to the knowledge of the truth*[^3]; but rather as he says: *When a man has finished, then he begins*[^4]: until we come to that life where we shall be so filled that we are not made more capacious, because we shall be so perfect that we no longer advance. For then will be shown to us that which suffices us. But here let us always seek, and let not the fruit of finding be the end of inquiring.

For it is not "not always" because it is only here; but we say that here we must always seek, lest at some time here we should think that we must cease from inquiring. For even of those of whom it was said: *Always learning, and never coming to the knowledge of the truth*; here indeed they are always learning; but when they have gone forth from this life, they will no longer be learning, but receiving the reward of their error. For it was said: *Always learning, and never coming to the knowledge of the truth*; as if it were said: Always walking, and never coming to the way. But let us always walk in the way, until we come where the way leads; let us nowhere remain in it, until it leads us where we may remain: and so, both by seeking we strive, and by finding we come to something, and to what remains by seeking and finding we pass on, until there be made an end of seeking, where to the perfect there remains no purpose of advancing.

May this preface, dearly beloved, have made your Charity attentive to that discourse of the Lord, which he held to the disciples before his passion: for it is deep, and surely where the expositor must labor much, the hearer ought not to be slack.

## A glorification, or rather a humiliation?

**2.** What, then, does the Lord say after Judas went out, to do quickly what he was about to do, that is, to betray the Lord? What does the day say, when the night had gone out? What does the Redeemer say, when the seller had gone out? *Now,* he says, *is the Son of man glorified.* Why *now*? Is it because the betrayer has gone out, because those who will seize and kill him are at hand? Is he then *now glorified,* because it is near that he be humbled further, who is now threatened with being bound, judged, condemned, mocked, crucified, slain? Is this glorification, or rather humiliation? Did not John himself say of him, when he was working miracles: *The Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*? Was he then not yet glorified, when he was raising the dead; and is he now glorified, when he draws near to the dead? Was he not yet glorified doing divine things, and is he glorified about to suffer human things? It would be strange if God that teacher signified and taught this by these words. More deeply must the saying of the Most High be searched out, who reveals himself somewhat that we may find, and again hides himself that we may inquire, and that from the things found we may press on, as by steps, to the things to be found.

I see here something that prefigures something great. Judas went out, and Jesus was glorified; the son of perdition went out, and the Son of man was glorified. For he had gone out, on account of whom it had been said to them: *And you are clean, but not all*. And so, the unclean one going out, all remained clean, and remained with their cleanser. Something of the kind will be when this world, conquered by Christ, passes away, and no one will remain unclean among the people of Christ; when, the tares being separated from the wheat, the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father[^5]. Foreseeing that this would be, and attesting that it was now signified, Judas departing as the tares separated, the holy Apostles remaining as the wheat: *Now,* he says, *is the Son of man glorified*: as if he said: Behold, in that glorification of mine what will be, where there will be none of the evil, where none of the good perishes. But it was not said thus: Now is signified the glorification of the Son of man; but it was said: *Now is the Son of man glorified*: just as it was not said: The rock signified Christ; but: *The rock was Christ*[^6]. Nor was it said: The good seed signified the children of the kingdom, or, the tares signified the children of the evil one; but it was said: *The good seed, these are the children of the kingdom; but the tares, the children of the evil one*[^7]. As, then, Scripture is wont to speak, calling the things that signify by the names of those which are signified; so the Lord spoke, saying: *Now is the Son of man glorified*: after, the most wicked one being separated thence, and the holy ones remaining with him, his glorification was signified, when, the wicked being separated, he will remain in eternity with the holy ones.

**3.** But when he had said: *Now is the Son of man glorified*; he added: *And God is glorified in him*[^8]. For this is the glorification of the Son of man, that God be glorified in him. For if it is not he himself in himself, but God in him that is glorified, then God glorifies him in himself. Finally, as it were expounding these things, he adds, and says: *If God is glorified in him, God also will glorify him in himself*[^9]. This is: *If God is glorified in him,* because he came not to do his own will, but the will of him who sent him; *God also will glorify him in himself,* so that the human nature in which he is the son of man, which was taken up by the eternal Word, may be endowed also with immortal eternity. *And at once,* he says, *he will glorify him.* Foretelling, namely, by this attestation, his resurrection, not as ours at the end of the age, but to be at once. For this is the glorification of which the Evangelist had already said, which I recalled a little before, that for this reason the Spirit was not yet given in that new manner to those to whom, believing in that manner, it was to be given after the resurrection, because Jesus was not yet glorified: that is, mortality was not yet clothed with immortality, and temporal weakness changed into eternal power. It can also seem that of this glorification it was said: *Now is the Son of man glorified*; so that what he says *now* may be believed to pertain not to the impending passion, but to the near resurrection, as though that had been done which was so very soon to be.

Today let these things be enough for your Love; when the Lord grants it, we will discuss what follows.
