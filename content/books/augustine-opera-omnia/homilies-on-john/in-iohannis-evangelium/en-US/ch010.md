# Homily 10

## But he was speaking of the temple of his body[^1].

Adam was broken, and, scattered, is gathered, and as it were is melted into one by spiritual fellowship and concord. He is renewed in Christ, who came to restore in himself Adam, the image of God. From Adam, then, is the flesh of Christ; from Adam, then, is the temple which the Jews destroyed and the Lord raised up in three days.

**1.** In the Psalm you have heard the groan of the poor man, whose members throughout the whole earth suffer tribulations until the end of the age. Take care, my brothers, to be among these members and of these members: for the tribulation will all pass away. Woe to those who rejoice! The Truth says: *Blessed are those who mourn, for they will be consoled*[^2]. God became man: what will man be, for whom God became man? Let this hope console us in every tribulation and temptation of this life. For the enemy does not cease to persecute; and if he does not rage openly, he acts by ambush. For what does he do? *And upon their anger they acted deceitfully*[^3]. Hence he is called a lion and a dragon. But what is said to Christ? *And you will trample the lion and the dragon*[^4]. The lion on account of open anger, the dragon on account of hidden ambushes. The dragon cast Adam out of paradise; the same one, as a lion, has persecuted the Church, as Peter says: *Because your adversary the devil goes about like a roaring lion, seeking whom he may devour*[^5]. Do not let the devil seem to you to have lost his ferocity: when he flatters, then he is the more to be guarded against.

But amid all these ambushes and his temptations, what shall we do, but what we heard there: *But I, when they were troublesome to me, clothed myself with sackcloth, and humbled my soul in fasting*[^6]. There is one who hears; do not hesitate to pray: but he who hears abides within. Do not direct your eyes to some mountain, do not lift your face to the stars or the sun or the moon. Do not suppose that you are then heard, when you pray over the sea: rather, detest such prayers. Only cleanse the chamber of your heart: wherever you are, wherever you pray, he is within who hears, within in the secret place, which he calls the bosom when he says: *And my prayer will return into my bosom*[^7]. He who hears you is not outside you. Do not go far away, nor exalt yourself, as if to touch him with your hands. Rather, if you exalt yourself, you will fall: if you humble yourself, he himself will draw near. Here is the Lord our God, the Word of God, the Word made flesh, the Son of the Father, the Son of God, the Son of man: lofty that he might make us, humble that he might restore us, walking among human beings, suffering human things, hiding divine things.

## Scripture has its own language.

**2.** *He went down*, as the Evangelist says, *to Capernaum, he and his mother, and his brothers, and his disciples, and they remained there not many days*[^8]. Behold, he has a mother, has brothers, has disciples too: from the same source brothers, from which a mother. For our Scripture is not accustomed to call brothers only those who are born from the same man and woman, or from the same womb, or from the same father though from different mothers; or at least from the same degree, such as cousins on the father's or mother's side: our Scripture knows how to call not only these brothers. As it speaks, so it is to be understood. It has its own language: whoever does not know this language is troubled, and says: Whence brothers for the Lord? Did Mary then bear again? Far be it. From this began the dignity of virgins. That woman could be a mother, she could not be a wife. But she was called woman according to the female sex, not according to a corruption of her integrity: and this from the language of Scripture itself. For Eve too, made at once from the side of her husband, not yet touched by her husband, you know was called woman: *And he formed her into a woman*[^9].

Whence, then, brothers? Kinsmen of Mary are the Lord's brothers, kinsmen of whatever degree. By what do we prove it? From Scripture itself. Lot was called the brother of Abraham; he was the son of his brother[^10]. Read, and you will find that Abraham was the uncle of Lot, and they were called brothers. Whence, but because they were kinsmen? Likewise Jacob had Laban the Syrian as his maternal uncle: for Laban was the brother of Jacob's mother, that is, of Rebecca the wife of Isaac. Read the Scripture, and you will find that an uncle and a sister's son are called brothers. This rule being known, you will find that all the blood-kin of Mary are brothers of Christ.

**3.** But those disciples were brothers in a higher sense; because even those kinsmen would not be brothers, if they were not disciples: and they would be brothers to no purpose, if they did not acknowledge the master as brother. For in a certain place, when his mother and his brothers had been announced to him as standing outside, while he was speaking with his disciples, he said: *Who is my mother, or who are my brothers? And stretching out his hand over his disciples, he said: These are my brothers: and: Whoever does the will of my Father, he is to me mother, and brother, and sister*[^11].

Therefore Mary too, because she did the will of the Father. This the Lord magnified in her, that she did the will of the Father, not that flesh begot flesh. Let your Charity attend. Therefore when the Lord seemed admirable in the crowd, doing signs and wonders, and showing what lay hidden in the flesh, certain souls, in admiration, said: *Blessed is the womb that bore you*[^12]. And he: *Rather, blessed are those who hear the word of God, and keep it*[^13]. This is to say: Even my mother, whom you have called blessed, is blessed for this reason, that she keeps the word of God: not because in her *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*; but because she keeps that very Word of God through which she was made, and which was made flesh in her. Let human beings not rejoice in temporal offspring; let them exult if they are joined to God in spirit. These things we have said on account of what the Evangelist says, that with his mother and his brothers and his disciples he dwelt in Capernaum a few days.

**4.** What follows from there? *And the Passover of the Jews was near, and he went up to Jerusalem*[^14]. He narrates another matter, as the recollection of the one announcing it had it. *And he found in the temple those selling oxen and sheep and doves, and the money changers sitting: and when he had made, as it were, a whip of small cords, he cast them all out of the temple; the oxen also and the sheep, and he poured out the money of the money changers, and overturned the tables; and to those who sold doves, he said: Take these things hence, and do not make my Father's house a house of trade*[^15].

What have we heard, brothers? Behold, that temple was as yet a figure, and the Lord casts out of it all who were seeking their own, who had come to the market. And what were they selling there? The things that human beings needed in the sacrifices of that time. For your Charity knows that to that people, on account of its carnality and its heart still of stone, such sacrifices were given, by which it might be held back from sliding into idols; and they offered there sacrifices, oxen, sheep, and doves: you know, because you have read it. It was not, then, a great sin, if they sold in the temple what was bought to be offered in the temple; and yet he cast them out of it.

What, if he had found drunkards there, what would the Lord do; if those selling things that are lawful, and not against justice (for what is honestly bought is not unlawfully sold), he nevertheless drove out, and did not allow the house of prayer to become a house of trade? If the house of God ought not to become a house of trade, ought it to become a house of drinking? But when we say these things, they gnash their teeth against us: and the psalm which you heard consoles us: *They gnashed their teeth at me*[^16]. We too know how to hear that by which we may be cured, even though the lashes are redoubled upon Christ, because his word is lashed: *Lashes were gathered against me*, he says, *and they did not know it*[^17]. He was lashed with the lashes of the Jews, he is lashed with the blasphemies of false Christians: they multiply lashes for their Lord, and do not know it. Let us do, so far as he himself helps: *But I, when they were troublesome to me, clothed myself with sackcloth, and humbled my soul in fasting*.

**5.** Yet we say, brothers (for he himself too did not spare them: he who was to be lashed by them, first lashed them), that he showed us a certain sign in making a whip of small cords, and with it lashing the undisciplined who were making trade of the temple of God. For indeed each one weaves himself a cord by his sins. The prophet says: *Woe to those who draw their sins like a long cord!*[^18] Who makes a long cord? He who adds sin to sin. How are sins added to sins? When the sins that have been committed are covered over by other sins.

He committed a theft; that it may not be found out that he did it, he seeks an astrologer. It would have been enough to have committed the theft; why do you wish to add sin to sin? behold two sins. When you are forbidden to go to the astrologer, you blaspheme the bishop: behold three sins. When you hear: Put him outside the Church; you say: I take myself off to the party of Donatus: behold you add a fourth. The cord grows: fear the cord. It is good for you to be corrected here, while you are lashed for it; lest at the end it be said: *Bind his feet and hands, and cast him into the outer darkness*[^19]. For *each one is bound fast by the cords of his own sins*[^20]. The Lord says the one, another Scripture says the other; but the Lord says both. By their own sins human beings are bound, and cast into the outer darkness.

## Those who have everything for sale.

**6.** Who are they, however, who sell oxen? that we may seek in the figure the mystery of the deed. Who are they who sell sheep and doves? They are those who *seek their own in the Church, not the things of Jesus Christ*[^21]. They have everything for sale, who refuse to be redeemed: they refuse to be bought, and they wish to sell. For it is good for them to be redeemed by the blood of Christ, that they may come to the peace of Christ. For what does it profit to acquire in this world anything temporal and transitory, whether it be money, or the pleasure of the belly and the throat, or honor in human praise? Are not all things smoke and wind? do not all things pass away, run away? And woe to those who cling to the things that pass away, because they pass away together with them. Are not all things a torrent rushing headlong into the sea? And woe to him who falls in, because he will be dragged into the sea.

Therefore we ought to hold back all our affections from such desires. My brothers, those who seek such things, sell. For that Simon too wished to buy the Holy Spirit for this reason, that he wished to sell the Holy Spirit; and he supposed the Apostles to be such merchants as the Lord cast out of the temple with the whip. For he himself was such, and wished to buy what he might sell: he was of those who sell doves. For indeed in a dove the Holy Spirit appeared. Those, then, who sell doves, brothers, who are they, but those who say: We give the Holy Spirit? For why do they say this, and at what price do they sell? At the price of their own honor. They receive as their price temporal chairs, so that they themselves may seem to sell doves. Let them beware of the whip of small cords. The dove is not for sale: it is given freely, because it is called grace.

Therefore, my brothers, just as you see those who sell, the hucksters, each one praises what he sells: how many platforms have they set up? Primian has one platform at Carthage, Maximian has another, Rogatus has another in Mauretania, this man and that have another in Numidia, whom we can no longer even name. So someone goes about to buy a dove, and each one, on his own platform, praises what he sells. Let that man's heart be turned away from every seller; let him come where it is received freely. Nor are they thus ashamed, brothers, that through their own bitter and malicious dissensions, when they attribute to themselves what they are not, when they are puffed up thinking themselves to be something when they are nothing, they have made so many parties out of themselves. But what has been fulfilled in them, that they refuse to be corrected, but what you heard in the Psalm: *They were torn asunder, and were not pricked to compunction*[^22]?

## The people of God, the Church of God.

**7.** Who, then, sell oxen? By oxen are understood those who dispensed the holy Scriptures to us. The Apostles were oxen, the Prophets were oxen. Whence the Apostle says: *You shall not muzzle the ox that treads out the grain*. *Is God concerned about oxen? Or does he say it for our sake? For he says it for our sake; because he who plows ought to plow in hope, and he who threshes, in hope of having a share*[^23]. Therefore those oxen left us the record of the Scriptures. For they did not dispense from their own, because they sought the glory of the Lord. For what did you hear in that Psalm? And let them say always: *Let the Lord be magnified, who wish the peace of his servant*[^24]. The servant of God, the people of God, the Church of God. Let those who wish the peace of his Church magnify the Lord, not the servant; *and let them say always: Let the Lord be magnified*. Who should say it? *Those who wish the peace of his servant*.

That voice is the manifest voice of that very people, that very servant, which you heard in the lamentations in the Psalm, and you were moved when you heard it, because you are of it. What was sung by one resounded from all hearts. Blessed are those who recognized themselves in those voices as in a mirror. Who, then, wish the peace of his servant, the peace of his people, the peace of the one whom he calls his only one, and whom he wishes to be rescued from the lion: *Rescue my only one from the hand of the dog*[^25]? *Those who say always: Let the Lord be magnified*.

Therefore those oxen magnified the Lord, not themselves. See the ox magnifying his Lord, because *the ox knew its owner*[^26]: notice the ox fearing lest the owner of the ox be forsaken, and presumption be placed in the ox; how it dreads those who wish to place hope in it: *Was Paul crucified for you? or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^27] What I gave, I did not give: you received it freely, the dove descended from heaven. *I*, he says, *planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase: neither is he who plants anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase*[^28]. *And let them say always: Let the Lord be magnified, who wish the peace of his servant*.

**8.** But these men deceive the peoples by the Scriptures themselves, so that they may receive honors and praises from them, and that the people may not be turned to the truth. But because they deceive the peoples by the very Scriptures, from whom they seek honors; they sell the oxen, they sell the sheep too, that is, the very common folk. And to whom do they sell them, but to the devil? For indeed, my brothers, if Christ's Church is one, and is unique; whatever is cut off from it, who carries it away, but *that roaring lion going about, seeking whom he may devour*? Woe to those who are cut off! for she will remain whole. For *the Lord knows who are his*[^29].

Yet, so far as it is in them, they sell oxen and sheep, they sell doves too: let them watch out for the whip of their own sins. Surely when they suffer something of the kind on account of these iniquities of theirs, let them recognize that the Lord has made a whip of small cords, and admonishes them by it to change themselves, so that they may not be traders: for if they do not change themselves, they will hear at the end: *Bind their hands and feet, and cast them into the outer darkness*[^30].

## Let zeal for the house of God consume you.

**9.** The disciples then remembered that it was written: *Zeal for your house has consumed me*[^31]: because it was by zeal for the house of God that the Lord cast those men out of the temple. Brothers, let each Christian, as a member of Christ, be consumed by zeal for the house of God. Who is consumed by zeal for the house of God? He who, in whatever perverse thing he perhaps sees there, takes pains to have it corrected, desires to have it amended, does not rest: if he cannot amend it, he bears with it, he groans. The grain is not shaken out of the threshing floor, it endures the chaff; that it may enter the granary, when the chaff has been separated. You, before the granary, if you are grain, do not let yourself be shaken out of the threshing floor; lest you be gathered up by the birds before you are gathered into the granary. For the birds of heaven, the airy powers, wait to snatch something from the threshing floor, and they snatch only what has been shaken out from it.

Therefore let zeal for the house of God consume you: let zeal for the house of God consume each Christian, in which house of God he is a member. For your own house is not more yours than the house where you have everlasting salvation. You enter your own house for temporal rest; you enter the house of God for everlasting rest. If, then, you take pains that nothing perverse be done in your own house; in the house of God, where salvation is set before you and rest without end, ought you to allow, so far as it is in you, anything perverse that you perhaps see? For example, you see a brother running to the theater? forbid him, admonish him, be saddened, if zeal for the house of God consumes you. You see others running and wishing to get drunk, and to do this in holy places, which is fitting nowhere? forbid those you can, hold back those you can, frighten those you can, coax those you can; only do not rest.

Is he a friend? let him be admonished gently. Is she a wife? let her be most severely restrained. Is she a maidservant? let her be checked even with blows. Do whatever you can, according to the person you bear; and you fulfill: *Zeal for your house has consumed me*. But if you are cold, slack, looking only to yourself, and as it were self-sufficient, and saying within yourself of your own affairs: What is it to me to care about other people's sins? my own soul is enough for me; let me keep it whole for God: come now, does that servant not come into your mind who hid his talent, and refused to put it out? For was he not accused, not because he lost it, but because he kept it without profit?

So then hear, my brothers, that you may not rest. I am about to give you counsel: let him give it who is within; because even if he gives it through me, it is he who gives. You know what each of you does in his own house, with a friend, with a lodger, with his client, with a greater, with a lesser: as God gives an entrance, as he opens a door by his word, do not rest from winning for Christ; because you were won by Christ.

## Christ dies that the Church may come to be.

**10.** The Jews said to him: What sign do you show us, that you do these things? And the Lord: Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up. The Jews therefore said: This temple was built in forty-six years, and you say: In three days I will raise it up?[^32] They were flesh, they had a taste for carnal things: but he was speaking spiritually. But who could understand of what temple he was speaking? But we do not seek much; through the Evangelist he opened it to us, he said of what temple he spoke: Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up. This temple was built in forty-six years, and you will raise it up in three days? But he was speaking, the Evangelist says, of the temple of his body[^33]. And it is manifest that the Lord, slain, rose again after three days.

In this way it is known to us all: and though it be closed to the Jews, because they stand outside; yet to us it is open, because we know in whom we believe. The destroying and rebuilding of this very temple we are about to celebrate in the annual solemnity: to which we exhort you, that you prepare yourselves, if any of you are catechumens, to receive grace; now is the time, now let be conceived what may then be born. Therefore this we know.

**11.** But perhaps this is required of us, whether the temple built in forty-six years has any sacred sign. There are indeed many things that could be said on this; but what can be said briefly and easily understood, this for now we say. Brothers, we have already said, unless I am mistaken, yesterday, that Adam was one man, and that he himself is the whole human race. For so we said, if you remember. He was, as it were, broken, and, scattered, is gathered, and as it were is melted into one by spiritual fellowship and concord. And one poor man groans now, that very Adam, but is renewed in Christ: because Christ came without sin, that he might dissolve Adam's sin in his own flesh, and that he might restore in himself Adam, the image of God.

From Adam, then, is the flesh of Christ: from Adam, then, is the temple which the Jews destroyed, and which the Lord raised up in three days. For he raised up his own flesh: see, because he was God equal to the Father. My brothers, the Apostle says: He who raised him from the dead. Of whom does he speak? Of the Father: *Having become*, he says, *obedient unto death, even the death of the cross: for which reason God also raised him from the dead, and gave him a name that is above every name*[^34]. The Lord was raised up and exalted. Who raised him up? The Father, to whom he said in the Psalms: *Raise me up, and I will repay them*[^35]. Therefore the Father raised him up. Did he not himself? But what does the Father do without the Word? what does the Father do without his Only-begotten? For hear that he too was God: *Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up*. Did he say: Destroy the temple, which the Father may raise up in three days? But as, when the Father raises up, the Son too raises up; so, when the Son raises up, the Father too raises up: because the Son said: *I and the Father are one*[^36].

**12.** What, then, does the number forty-six mean? For now, because that very Adam is throughout the whole world, you heard yesterday in the four Greek letters of four Greek words. For if you write those four words one beneath another, that is, the names of the four parts of the world, of the East, the West, the North, and the South, which is the whole world (whence the Lord says that he will gather his elect from the four winds, when he comes to judgment[^37]): for if you make those four Greek names: ἀνατολὴ, which is East; δύσις, which is West; ἄρκτος, which is North; μεσημβρία, which is South: the first letters of the words make Adam.

How, then, do we find there also the number forty-six? Because the flesh of Christ was from Adam. The Greeks reckon numbers by letters. What we call A, they in their own language put as alpha, and alpha is called one. But where in numbers they write beta, which is their B, it is called two in numbers. Where they write gamma, it is called three in their numbers. Where they write delta, it is called four in their numbers: and so through all the letters they have numbers. M, which we call M, and they call mu, signifies forty: for they call it mu. Now see what number these letters have; and there you will find the temple built in forty-six years. For Adam has alpha, which is one: it has delta, which is four; you have five: it has alpha again, which is one; you have six: it has also mu, which is forty: you have forty-six.

These things, my brothers, were said even by our earlier elders, and this number forty-six was found in the letters. And because our Lord Jesus Christ took a body from Adam, but drew no sin from Adam; he took thence a bodily temple, not the iniquity which is to be driven from the temple: but that very flesh which he drew from Adam (for Mary is from Adam, and the Lord's flesh from Mary) the Jews crucified; and he was to raise up in three days that very flesh which they were going to kill on the cross: they destroyed the temple built in forty-six years, and he in three days raised it up.

**13.** We bless the Lord our God, who has gathered us for spiritual gladness. Let us be always in humility of heart, and let our joy be with him. Let us not be puffed up by any prosperity of this age, but let us know that our happiness is not except when these things have passed away. Now let our joy, my brothers, be in hope: let no one rejoice as in a present thing, lest he stick fast on the way. Let the whole joy be of future hope, let the whole desire be of eternal life. Let all our sighs pant for Christ: let him, the one most beautiful, who loved even the foul that he might make them beautiful, be desired; let us run to him alone, let us groan to him alone; and let them say always: Let the Lord be magnified, who wish the peace of his servant[^38].
