# Homily 1

## *In the beginning was the Word . . .* [^1]

> *Do not depart from the Christ who was born in the flesh until you arrive at the Christ who was born of the one Father, the Word, God with God, through whom all things were made; for that is the life which is in him, the life that is the light of men.*

## John, a lofty mountain.

**1.** When I consider what we have just heard from the apostolic reading, that the unspiritual man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God [^2], and when I think that here in this present gathering of your Charity there must be many who are unspiritual, who still are wise according to the flesh and cannot yet raise themselves to a spiritual understanding, I am in great doubt how I may, as the Lord shall grant, manage to say or to explain according to my small measure what has been read from the Gospel: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^3]; for the unspiritual man does not perceive this. What then, brothers? Shall we be silent about it? Then why is it read, if it is to be passed over in silence? Or why is it heard, if it is not explained? But again, what good is it explained, if it is not understood? And so, since I do not doubt that there are again among your number some who can not only grasp what is explained but even understand it before it is explained, I will not cheat those who can grasp it out of fear of being superfluous to the ears of those who cannot.

Finally, the mercy of God will be present, so that perhaps enough is given to all, and each one grasps what he can; for the one who speaks also says what he can. For who can say it as it is? I dare to say, my brothers, that perhaps not even John himself said it as it is, but he too said it as he could; for he was a man speaking about God, inspired by God indeed, yet still a man. Because he was inspired, he said something; if he had not been inspired, he would have said nothing. But because he was an inspired man, he did not say the whole of what is, but he said what a man could say.

**2.** For this John, dearest brothers, was one of those mountains of which it is written: *Let the mountains receive peace for your people, and the hills righteousness* [^4]. The mountains are lofty souls; the hills are little souls. But the mountains receive peace for this reason, that the hills may be able to receive righteousness. What is the righteousness that the hills receive? Faith, for *the righteous one lives by faith* [^5]. But the lesser souls would not receive faith unless the greater souls, which are called mountains, were illuminated by Wisdom herself, so that they might be able to pass on to the little ones what the little ones can grasp, and the hills might live by faith, because the mountains receive peace.

It was by these mountains that it was said to the Church, *Peace be with you* [^6]; and these mountains, in announcing peace to the Church, did not divide themselves against him from whom they received peace, so that they might announce peace truly and not falsely.

**3.** For there are other mountains that cause shipwreck, against which, when anyone drives his ship, it is broken apart. For when land is seen by those in peril, it is easy, so to speak, to make for the land; but sometimes land is seen in a mountain, and rocks lie hidden beneath the mountain, and when someone makes for the mountain, he runs upon the rocks; and there he finds no harbor, but lamentation. So there were certain mountains, and they appeared great among men; and they made heresies and schisms, and divided the Church of God. But those who divided the Church of God were not those mountains of which it was said: *Let the mountains receive peace for your people* [^7]. For how did they receive peace who divided unity?

**4.** But those who received peace to announce to the people contemplated Wisdom herself, as far as it could be reached by human hearts—that which eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man [^8]. If it did not enter into the heart of man, how did it enter into the heart of John? Or was John not a man? Or perhaps it did not enter into the heart of John, but the heart of John entered into it? For what enters into the heart of man comes up to man from below; but where the heart of man enters, that is above man.

Even so, brothers, it can be said that if it entered into the heart of John, if it can be put in any way, it entered into the heart of John insofar as John himself was not a man. What does this mean, 'was not a man'? Insofar as he had begun to be an angel; for all the saints are angels, because they are announcers of God. Therefore, to the carnal and unspiritual who are unable to perceive the things that are of God, what does the Apostle say? *For when you say, I am of Paul, I am of Apollos, are you not men?* [^9] What did he wish to make of those whom he reproached for being men? Do you wish to know what he wished to make of them? Hear in the Psalms: *I said, You are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High* [^10]. To this, then, God calls us, that we should not be men. But we shall not be men, for the better, unless we first acknowledge ourselves to be men—that is, that we may rise from humility to that height—lest, when we think we are something though we are nothing [^11], we not only fail to receive what we are not but even lose what we are.

**5.** Therefore, brothers, John too was one of these mountains, who said: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^12]. This mountain had received peace; he was contemplating the divinity of the Word. What kind of mountain was this? How lofty? He had passed beyond all the peaks of the earth, passed beyond all the expanses of the air, passed beyond all the heights of the stars, passed beyond all the choirs and legions of angels. For unless he had passed beyond all these things that were created, he would not have arrived at the one through whom all things were made. You cannot conceive what he passed beyond unless you see where he arrived. Do you ask about heaven and earth? They were made. Do you ask about the things that are in heaven and on earth? Surely these too, much more, were made.

Do you ask about the spiritual creatures—the angels, archangels, thrones, dominions, virtues, principalities? These too were made. For when the Psalm enumerated all these things, it concluded thus: *He himself spoke, and they were made; he himself commanded, and they were created* [^13]. If *he spoke, and they were made*, they were made through the Word; but if they were made through the Word, the heart of John could not have arrived at what he says—*In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^14]—unless he had passed beyond all the things that were made through the Word. What kind of mountain, then, is this, how holy, how high among those mountains that received peace for the people of God, so that the hills might receive righteousness!

## Lift up your eyes to the mountain.

**6.** See, then, brothers, whether John is perhaps one of those mountains of which we sang a little while ago: *I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains, from where help will come to me* [^15]. Therefore, my brothers, if you wish to understand, lift up your eyes to this mountain; that is, raise yourselves to the Evangelist, raise yourselves to his meaning. But because these mountains receive peace, and the one who places his hope in man cannot be in peace, do not so lift up your eyes to the mountain as to think that your hope is to be placed in man; rather, say, *I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains, from where help will come to me*, so that you immediately add: *My help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth* [^16].

Therefore let us lift up our eyes to the mountains, from where help will come to us; and yet it is not the mountains themselves in which our hope is to be placed, for the mountains receive what they may minister to us. Therefore, where the mountains too receive it, there our hope is to be placed. When we lift up our eyes to the Scriptures, since the Scriptures have been ministered through men, we lift up our eyes to the mountains, from where help will come to us; but yet, because it was men who wrote the Scriptures, they did not shine of themselves; rather, he *was the true light, who enlightens every man coming into this world* [^17]. John the Baptist too was a mountain, who said: *I am not the Christ* [^18]; lest anyone, placing his hope in the mountain, fall away from the one who illuminates the mountains, he himself confessed and said: *For of his fullness we have all received* [^19]. So you ought to say, *I have lifted up my eyes to the mountains, from where help will come to me*, lest you credit to the mountains the help that comes to you; but follow on, and say: *My help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth* [^20].

**7.** Therefore, brothers, I have given these warnings for this reason: that when you lifted up your heart to the Scriptures, as the Gospel sounded forth—*In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^21], and the rest that was read—you might understand that you had lifted your eyes to the mountains. For unless the mountains said these things, you would not find from where to think of them at all. Therefore help came to you from the mountains, that you might at least hear these things; but you cannot yet understand what you have heard. Call for help from the Lord, who made heaven and earth; for the mountains were able to speak in such a way that they cannot themselves give light, since they too were enlightened by hearing. From there that John received what he said, brothers—he who reclined upon the breast of the Lord [^22] and drank from the breast of the Lord what he might give us to drink.

But he gave us words to drink; the understanding, however, you must take from the same source from which he too had drunk who gave you to drink: so that you lift your eyes to the mountains, from where help will come to you, that from there you may receive, as it were, the cup—that is, the word given you to drink; and yet, because your help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth, you may fill your breast from the same source from which he filled his—from where you said, *My help is from the Lord, who made heaven and earth* [^23]. Let him, therefore, who can, fill himself.

Brothers, I have said this: let each one lift up his heart in whatever way he sees fit, and grasp what is said. But perhaps you will say that I am more present to you than God is. God forbid. He is far more present; for I appear to your eyes, but he presides over your consciences. Lend your ears to me, your heart to him, that you may fill both. See, you lift up your eyes and these bodily senses to us—and not to us, for we are not of those mountains, but to the Gospel itself, to the Evangelist himself; but the heart is to be filled by turning to the Lord. And let each one so lift it up that he sees what he lifts and where he lifts it. What did I mean by 'what he lifts and where he lifts it'? Let him see what sort of heart he lifts, since he lifts it to the Lord, lest, weighed down by the burden of carnal pleasure, it fall before it has been lifted up. But does anyone see himself carrying the burden of the flesh? Let him take pains, through continence, to purify what he lifts up to God. For *blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God* [^24].

**8.** For see, what good is it that the words sounded forth—*In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^25]? We too said words when we spoke. Was the Word with God a word like that? Did not the things we said sound forth and pass away? Did the Word of God, then, sound forth and come to an end? How were ***all things made through him, and without him was nothing made*** [^26]? How is that which was created through him governed through him, if it sounded forth and passed away? What kind of word, then, is it that is both spoken and does not pass away?

Let your Charity attend: it is a great matter. By daily speaking, words have become cheap to us, because, by sounding and passing, they have become cheap, and they seem to be nothing other than words. There is a word even within man himself, which remains inside; for the sound proceeds from the mouth. There is a word that is spoken truly, spiritually—that which you understand from the sound, not the sound itself. See, I speak a word when I say 'God.' How short is what I have said—four letters and two syllables! Is this the whole of God, four letters and two syllables? Or, as cheap as this is, so precious is what is understood in them? What happened in your heart when you heard 'God'? What happened in my heart when I said 'God'? Some great and supreme substance was thought of, one that transcends every changeable creature, carnal and spiritual.

And if I should ask you, 'Is God changeable or unchangeable?' you will answer at once: 'Far be it from me either to believe or to think God changeable: God is unchangeable.' Your soul, however small, however perhaps still carnal, could not answer me except that God is unchangeable; but every creature is changeable. How, then, were you able to flash up to that which is above every creature, so that you answered me with certainty that God is unchangeable? What, then, is that in your heart when you think of a certain living substance, everlasting, almighty, infinite, present everywhere, everywhere whole, nowhere enclosed? When you think these things, this is a word about God in your heart. But is this the sound that consists of four letters and two syllables? Therefore whatever things are spoken and pass away are sounds, are letters, are syllables. This word passes away, the word that sounds; but that which the sound signified is both in the one who thinks who spoke it, and in the one who understands who heard it, and it remains while the sounds pass away.

## The Word of God.

**9.** Turn your mind to that word. Suppose you can have a word in your heart, like a plan born in your mind, so that your mind gives birth to the plan, and the plan is present like the offspring of your mind, like a son of your heart. For first the heart begets a plan to construct some building, to undertake something large on the earth; the plan is already born, and the work is not yet completed. You see what you are going to do; but another does not marvel at it until you have done it and built the structure, and brought that work to completion and perfection. Then men gaze at the marvelous structure and admire the plan of the builder; they are astonished at what they see, and they love what they do not see. Who is there who can see a plan?

If, then, the human plan is praised from some great structure, do you wish to see what sort of plan of God the Lord Jesus Christ is—that is, the Word of God? Consider this structure of the world; see what things were made through the Word, and then you will know what sort of Word it is. Consider these two bodies of the world, heaven and earth: who can unfold in words the adornment of heaven? Who can unfold in words the fruitfulness of the earth? Who worthily praises the changes of the seasons? Who worthily praises the power of seeds? You see what things I pass over in silence, lest by mentioning them at length I say too little, perhaps, of what you can conceive.

From this structure, then, consider what sort of Word it is through which it was made; and it was not the only thing made. For all these things are seen, because they belong to the bodily senses. Through that Word the angels too were made; through that Word the archangels too were made; the powers, thrones, dominions, principalities; through that Word were made ***all things*** [^27]: from this consider what sort of Word it is.

**10.** Perhaps someone now answers me, I know not who: And who can conceive this Word? Do not, then, form for yourself something cheap, as it were, when you hear 'Word,' and connect it with the words you hear every day: 'He said such words, he spoke such words, you are telling me such words'; for by constantly speaking the names of words, words have become cheap, as it were. And when you hear, *In the beginning was the Word* [^28], lest you should think it something cheap, of the sort you are accustomed to conceive when you were used to hearing human words, hear what you should think: *the Word was God* [^29].

**11.** Let some unbelieving Arian now come forth, I know not who, and say that the Word of God was made. How can it be that the Word of God was made, when God made all things through the Word? If the Word of God itself was also made, through what other word was it made? If you say this—that there is a Word of the Word through which it was made—I say that that one is the only-begotten Son of God. But if you do not speak of a Word of the Word, grant that the one through whom all things were made was not made. For that through which all things were made could not be made through itself.

Believe the Evangelist, then. For he could have said, 'In the beginning God made the Word,' just as Moses said, *In the beginning God made heaven and earth* [^30]; and he enumerates everything thus: God said, Let it be made, and it was made. If he said it, who said it? Surely God. And what was made? Some creature. Between God who speaks and the creature that was made, what is there through which it was made, except the Word? For God said, *Let it be made*, and it was made. This Word is unchangeable; although changeable things are made through the Word, the Word itself is unchangeable.

## May he who made you remake you.

**12.** Do not, then, believe that the one through whom all things were made was made, lest you not be remade through the Word, through whom all things are remade. For you have already been made through the Word, but you must be remade through the Word; but if your faith concerning the Word should be bad, you will not be able to be remade through the Word. And if it has befallen you to be made through the Word, so that you were made through him, yet through yourself you fall away; if you fall away through yourself, let him who made you remake you; if you are made worse through yourself, let him who created you recreate you. But how shall he recreate you through the Word, if you think something bad about the Word? The Evangelist says, *In the beginning was the Word*; and you say, In the beginning the Word was made. He says, *All things were made through him*; and you say that the Word itself too was made. The Evangelist could have said, In the beginning the Word was made; but what does he say? *In the beginning was the Word* [^31]. If it 'was,' it was not made, so that all these things might be made through it, and without it nothing. If, then, *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^32]—if you cannot conceive what it is, wait until you grow. That is solid food; take milk, that you may be nourished, that you may be strong enough to take solid food [^33].

## The whole creation was made through him.

**13.** Certainly, brothers, as to what follows—*All things were made through him, and without him was nothing made* [^34]—see that you do not think that 'nothing' is something. For many who understand badly are accustomed, from *without him was nothing made*, to suppose that 'nothing' is something. Sin, indeed, was not made through him; and it is plain that sin is nothing, and that men become nothing when they sin. An idol, too, was not made through the Word; it does have a certain human form, but man himself was made through the Word; for the form of a man in the idol was not made through the Word; and it is written: *We know that an idol is nothing* [^35].

Therefore these things were not made through the Word; but whatever things have been made naturally, whatever things are in the creatures, absolutely all things that are fixed in heaven, that shine from above, that fly beneath the sky, and that move throughout the whole nature of things, the whole creation without exception—let me say it more plainly, let me say it, brothers, that you may understand—from the angel down to the little worm. What is more splendid than an angel among creatures? What is lower than a little worm among creatures? Through the one through whom the angel was made, through him the little worm too was made; but the angel is worthy of heaven, the little worm of earth. He who created them, he himself arranged them. If he were to place the worm in heaven, you would find fault; if he wished angels to be born from rotting flesh, you would find fault; and yet God does nearly this, and is not to be found at fault. For all men, born of flesh, what are they but worms? And from worms he makes angels.

For if the Lord himself says, *But I am a worm, and not a man* [^36], who hesitates to say what is written also in Job: *How much more is man rottenness, and the son of man a worm* [^37]? First he said, *man is rottenness*; and afterward, *the son of man a worm*: because the worm is born from rottenness, therefore *man is rottenness*, and *the son of man a worm*. See what he willed to become for your sake, he who *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^38]. Why was this done for your sake? That you might suckle, you who could not eat solid food.

In every way, then, brothers, take it thus: *All things were made through him, and without him was nothing made* [^39]. For the whole creation was made through him, greater and lesser; through him the things above were made, the things below; spiritual, corporeal—through him they were made. For no form, no structure, no harmony of parts, no substance of any kind whatever, which can have weight, number, and measure, exists except through that Word, and from that creating Word, to whom it was said: *You have arranged all things in measure, and number, and weight* [^40].

**14.** Therefore let no one deceive you, when perhaps you feel disgust at flies. For some have been mocked by the devil and captured by means of flies. For fowlers are accustomed to place flies in a trap, that they may deceive hungry birds; so these men too were deceived by the devil by means of flies. For someone, I know not who, was feeling disgust at flies; a Manichee found him afflicted with disgust; and when he said that he could not endure flies and hated them violently, that one at once said: Who made them? And because he was afflicted with disgust and hated them, he did not dare to say, God made them—yet he was a Catholic. The other at once added: If God did not make them, who made them? Plainly, said he, I believe that the devil made flies. And the other at once: If the devil made the fly, as I see you confess, since you understand prudently, who made the bee, which is a little larger than a fly? That man did not dare to say that God made the bee and did not make the fly, because the case was a near one. From the bee he led him to the locust, from the locust to the lizard, from the lizard to the bird, from the bird he led him to the beast, then to the ox, then to the elephant, and at last to man; and he persuaded the man that man was not made by God. So that wretch, when he felt disgust at flies, became a fly himself, whom the devil might possess. For Beelzebub is said to be interpreted 'prince of flies'; of which it is written: *Dying flies spoil the sweetness of the oil* [^41].

**15.** What then, brothers? Why have I said these things? Close the ears of your heart against the wiles of the enemy; understand that God made all things and set them in their own ranks. But why do we suffer many evils from a creature that God made? Because we have offended God. Do the angels suffer these things? Perhaps in this life too we would not fear them. Blame your sin for your punishment, not the judge. For on account of pride God appointed this least and most abject creature to torment us: so that, when a man has been proud and has exalted himself against God; and, though he is mortal, has terrified a mortal; and, though he is a man, has not recognized his neighbor as a man; when he has lifted himself up, he is made subject to fleas.

Why is it that you puff yourself up with human pride? A man hurled an insult at you, and you were afraid, and you grew angry; resist the fleas so that you may sleep: recognize who you are. For that you may know, brothers, that these things, which would be troublesome to us, were created to tame our pride: God was able to tame the proud people of Pharaoh by means of bears, lions, and serpents; yet he sent flies and frogs upon them, that pride might be tamed by the most worthless things.

## In him all things are life.

**16.** *All things*, therefore, brothers, absolutely *all things were made through him, and without him was nothing made* [^42]. But how were all things made through him? *What was made in him is life* [^43]. For it can be said thus: *What was made, in him is life*; therefore the whole is life, if we so pronounce it. For what was not made in him? For he is the Wisdom of God; and it is said in the Psalm: *You have made all things in Wisdom* [^44]. If, then, Christ is the Wisdom of God, and the Psalm says, *You have made all things in Wisdom*, then just as all things were made through him, so they were made in him. If, then, all things are in him, dearest brothers, and what was made in him is life, then earth too is life, then wood too is life. We do indeed speak of wood as life, but according to the understanding of the wood of the cross, from which we received life. Therefore the stone too is life.

It is shameful to understand it so, lest again that most filthy sect of the Manichees creep in upon us and say that the stone has life, and the wall has a soul, and the cord has a soul, and the wool and the garment too. For madmen are accustomed to say this, and when they have been checked and repulsed, they bring it forward, as it were, from the Scriptures, saying: Why is it said, *What was made in him is life* [^45]? For if all things were made in him, all things are life. Do not let them lead you astray: pronounce it thus: *What was made*; here make a slight pause, and then add, *in him is life*. What does this mean? The earth was made; but the earth itself which was made is not life. Yet in Wisdom herself there is, spiritually, a certain rational principle by which the earth was made; and this is life.

**17.** I will tell your Charity how I can. A carpenter makes a chest. First he has the chest in his skill; for if he did not have the chest in his skill, from where would he produce it by making it? But the chest is in the skill in such a way that it is not the very chest that is seen by the eyes. In the skill it exists invisibly; in the work it will exist visibly. See, it has been made in the work; has it ceased to be in the skill? Both that has been made in the work, and that which is in the skill remains: for that chest can rot away, and from the one that is in the skill another can be made. Consider, then, the chest in the skill, and the chest in the work. The chest in the work is not life; the chest in the skill is life, because the soul of the craftsman lives, in which all these things exist before they are produced.

So then, dearest brothers, because the Wisdom of God, through whom all things were made, contains all things according to skill before it fashions all things; therefore the things that are made through that skill are not at once life, but whatever was made, in him is life. You see earth; there is earth in the skill: you see heaven; there is heaven in the skill: you see the sun and the moon; these too are in the skill: but outside they are bodies, in the skill they are life.

See, if you can in any way; for a great thing has been said: and if not by me as a great man, or not through me as something great, yet it is from the Great One. For these things were not said by me, a little one; but he to whom I look in order to speak is no little one. Let each one grasp it as he can, as far as he can; and let him who cannot, nourish his heart, that he may be able. From where should he nourish it? Let him nourish it with milk, that he may come to solid food. Let him not depart from the Christ who was born in the flesh until he arrives at the Christ who was born of the one Father, the Word, God with God, through whom all things were made; for that is the life which is in him, the life that is the light of men.

## And that life itself is light.

**18.** For this is what follows: *And the life was the light of men* [^46]; and from that life men are enlightened. Cattle are not enlightened, because cattle do not have rational minds that can see wisdom. But man was made to the image of God [^47]; he has a rational mind through which he can perceive wisdom. Therefore that life through which all things were made, that life itself is light: and not the light of just any animals, but the light of men. From which a little later he says: *It was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world* [^48]. By that light John the Baptist was enlightened; by that same light John the Evangelist too. He was full of that light who said: *I am not the Christ; but he who comes after me, the strap of whose sandal I am not worthy to loosen* [^49]. By that light he was enlightened who said: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God* [^50]. Therefore that life is the light of men.

**19.** But perhaps foolish hearts cannot yet grasp this light, because they are weighed down by their sins, so that they cannot see it. Let them not on that account think the light is, as it were, absent, because they cannot see it: for they themselves, on account of their sins, are darkness. *And the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it* [^51]. Therefore, brothers, just as a man placed in the sun but blind has the sun present to him, while he himself is absent from the sun, so every fool, every wicked man, every impious man is blind in heart. Wisdom is present, but when it is present with a blind man, it is absent from his eyes: not because it is absent from him, but because he is absent from it.

What, then, should such a man do? Let him cleanse the means by which God can be seen. Just as, if he could not see because he had dirty and wounded eyes, with dust or rheum or smoke rushing in, the physician would say to him: Clean out of your eye whatever evil there is, that you may be able to see the light of your eyes. The dust, the rheum, the smoke are sins and iniquities; remove all these from there, and you will see the wisdom that is present: for God is that very wisdom; and it is said: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God* [^52].
