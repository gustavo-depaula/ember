# Tractate 106

## *And they have known truly that I came forth from you, and have believed that you sent me*[^1].

## The Lord gave them the words, as he said, which the Father gave him; but when they received them not outwardly in the ears, but inwardly in their hearts spiritually, then they truly received them, because then they truly knew; and they truly knew because they truly believed.

**1.** We are about to discuss in this sermon, as he himself shall grant, these words of the Lord, which run thus: *I have manifested your name to the men whom you gave me out of the world*[^2]. But if he speaks only of those disciples with whom he had supped, and to whom, before he began to pray, he had spoken so many things; this does not pertain to that glorification — or, as others have rendered it, glorification — of which he was speaking above, by which the Son glorifies the Father. For how great or what sort of glory is it, to have become known to twelve, or rather eleven, mortals? But if by what he says, *I have manifested your name to the men whom you gave me out of the world,* he wished all to be understood, even those who were going to believe in him, belonging to his great Church which was to be from all nations, of which it is sung in the Psalm, *In the great Church I will confess to you*[^3]; this is plainly that glorification by which the Son glorifies the Father, when he makes his name known to all nations, and to so many generations of men. And this which he says, *I have manifested your name to the men whom you gave me out of the world,* is such as that which he had said a little before, *I have glorified you upon the earth*; putting both there and here the past for the future time, as one who knew it was predestined that it should be, and therefore saying that he had done what he was without any doubt going to do.

**2.** But that he said what he said — *I have manifested your name to the men whom you gave me out of the world* — of those who were already his disciples, not of all who were going to believe in him, the things that follow show to be more credible. For when he had said this, he added: *They were yours, and you gave them to me, and they have kept your word: now they have known that all things which you gave me are from you; because the words which you gave me, I have given to them: and they have received them, and have known truly that I came forth from you, and have believed that you sent me*[^4]. Although all these things too could be said of all future believers, the hope being already perfect, while they were still to come: but that this is understood to be spoken of those alone whom he then had as disciples, that rather presses which he says a little after: *When I was with them, I kept them in your name: those whom you gave me I guarded, and no one of them perished, except the son of perdition, that the Scripture might be fulfilled*[^5]; signifying Judas who betrayed him: for of that number of twelve Apostles he alone perished. Then he adds: *But now I come to you*[^6]. Whence it is manifest that he said this of his bodily presence: *When I was with them, I kept them,* as though now he were not with them by that presence. For in this way he wished to signify his ascension, soon to come, of which he said, *But now I come to you*: about to go, surely, to the right hand of the Father; whence he is to come to judge the living and the dead, likewise by bodily presence, according to the rule of faith and sound doctrine: for by spiritual presence he was certainly going to be with them after his ascension, and with his whole Church in this world until the consummation of the age. Not rightly, then, are those understood of whom he said, *When I was with them, I kept them,* except those whom, believing in him, he had already begun to keep by bodily presence, and whom he was going to leave by bodily absence, that he might keep them with the Father by spiritual presence. But afterward he adds his others too, where he says: *But not for these alone do I ask, but also for those who will believe in me through their word*[^7]. Where he shows more manifestly that he was not speaking above, from that place where he says, *I have manifested your name to the men whom you gave me,* of all who belong to him; but only of those who, when he said these things, were hearing him.

## This is the glorification of the Father which is brought about concerning all men.

**3.** And so, from the very beginning of his prayer, where, *lifting up his eyes to heaven, he said: Father, the hour has come, glorify your Son, that your Son may glorify you,* as far as that which he says a little after, *And now glorify me, Father, with yourself, with the glory which I had with you before the world was*; he wished all his own to be understood, by making the Father known to whom he glorifies him. For when he had said, *that your Son may glorify you,* he soon showed how this would come about, saying: *As you have given him power over all flesh, that to all whom you have given him he may give eternal life: but this is eternal life, that they may know you, the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent.* For the Father cannot be glorified by the knowledge of men, unless also he be known through whom he is glorified, that is, through whom he becomes known to the peoples. This is the glorification of the Father, which was brought about not concerning those Apostles alone, but is brought about concerning all men, of whom, as his members, Christ is the head. For it cannot be understood of the Apostles alone: *As you have given him power over all flesh, that to all whom you have given him he may give eternal life*; but certainly of all to whom, believing in him, eternal life is given.

**4.** Now, then, let us see what he says concerning those disciples of his by whom he was then heard. *I have manifested,* he says, *your name to the men whom you gave me.* Did they not, then, know the name of God, since they were Jews? And where is that which is read: *God is known in Judea; in Israel his name is great*[^8]? Therefore, *I have manifested your name to* these *men whom you gave me out of the world,* who hear me saying these things: not that name of yours by which you are called God, but that by which you are called my Father; which name could not be manifested without the manifestation of the Son himself. For that he is called God of the whole creation, even to all nations before they believed in Christ, this name could not in every way be unknown. For this is the force of true divinity, that to the rational creature now using reason, it cannot be utterly and entirely hidden. For, with the exception of a few in whom nature is excessively depraved, the whole human race confesses God to be the author of this world.

In this, then, that he made this world conspicuous in heaven and earth, even before they were imbued in the faith of Christ, God is known to all nations. But in this, that he is not to be worshiped with injuries to himself together with false gods, God is known in Judea. But in this, that he is the Father of this Christ, through whom he takes away the sin of the world, this name of his, before hidden from all, he has now manifested to those whom the Father himself gave him out of the world. But how did he manifest it, if the hour had not yet come of which he had said above, that the hour would come *when I will no longer speak to you in proverbs, but openly will announce to you concerning my Father*[^9]? Or will an announcement in proverbs be thought manifest? Why, then, was it said, *I will announce openly to you,* unless because *in proverbs* is not *openly*? But what is not hidden in proverbs, but manifested in plain words, is without doubt said openly. How, then, did he manifest what he did not yet say openly? Accordingly, it is to be understood that the past was put for the future time, just as that, *All things that I heard from my Father I have made known to you*: which he had not yet done, but spoke as though he had done, because he knew it was immovably fixed that he should do it.

**5.** But what is, *whom you gave me out of the world*? For it was said of them that they were not of the world. But this regeneration bestowed on them, not generation. What too is that which follows, *They were yours, and you gave them to me*? Were they ever the Father's, when they were not the only-begotten Son's; and did the Father ever have anything without the Son? Far be it. Yet God the Son had at one time something which the same Son as man did not yet have; because he was not yet made man of a mother, when nevertheless he had all things with the Father. Wherefore in saying, *They were yours,* God the Son did not separate himself from there, without whom the Father never had anything; but he is wont to attribute to him every thing that he can, from whom is he himself who can.

For from whom he has it that he is, from him he has it that he can; and he always had both together, because he never was and could not. Wherefore whatever the Father could, the Son could always with him; since he who never was and could not, was never without the Father, and the Father was never without him. And through this, just as the Father is eternal almighty, so the Son is coeternal almighty; and if almighty, surely all-holding (omnitenens). For we rather render this word, word for word, if we wish to speak properly, which is said by the Greeks; which our translators would not have so rendered as to say "almighty," when it is "all-holding," unless they perceived it to be worth just as much. What, then, could the eternal all-holding one ever have, which the coeternal all-holding one did not have together with him? What he says, then, *And you gave them to me,* shows that he, as man, received this power, that he should have them; since he who was always almighty was not always man. Wherefore, although he seems rather to have attributed to the Father that he received them from him — since from him is whatever he is who is of him — he himself too gave them to himself, that is, Christ, God with the Father, gave men to the man Christ, which the man is not with the Father.

Finally, he who in this place says, *They were yours, and you gave them to me,* had already said above to these same disciples, *I chose you out of the world*[^10]. Let fleshly thinking here be crushed, and perish. The Son says that men were given to him out of the world by the Father, to whom in another place he says, *I chose you out of the world.* Those whom God the Son chose out of the world with the Father, that same Son as man received out of the world from the Father: for the Father would not have given them to the Son, unless he had chosen them. And through this the Son, just as he did not separate the Father from there when he said, *I chose you out of the world,* since the Father chose them together; so he did not separate himself from there when he said, *They were yours,* because they were equally the Son's too. But now this same Son as man received those who were not his, because God too as man received the form of a servant which was not his.

**6.** He follows and says: *And they have kept your word: now they have known that all things which you gave me are from you*[^11]: that is, they have known that I am from you. For the Father gave all things at the same time that he begot him who should have all things. *Because the words,* he says, *which you gave me, I have given to them, and they have received them*: that is, they have understood and held them. For a word is then received when it is perceived by the mind. *And they have known,* he says, *truly that I came forth from you, and have believed that you sent me.* And here too *truly* is to be understood: for what he said, *they have known truly,* he wished to explain by adding, *and have believed.* This, then, *they believed truly,* which *they knew truly*: for *I came forth from you* is the same as *you sent me.* When, therefore, he had said, *they have known truly,* lest anyone should think this knowledge was already by sight, not by faith; by way of explanation he added, *and have believed,* so that we may understand *truly,* and understand that this was said, *they have known truly,* which is *they have believed truly*: not in the way which he signified a little before, when he said, *Do you now believe? The hour comes, and has already come, that you will be scattered, each one to his own, and will leave me alone*[^12]. But *they believed truly,* that is, in the way one ought to believe, unshaken, firmly, stably, strongly; not now to return to their own, and leave Christ.

As yet, then, the disciples were not such as he calls them by words of past time, as though they already were, foretelling what they were going to be — namely, the Holy Spirit being received, who, as was promised, would teach them all things. Before they received him, how did they keep his word, which he said they had as it were done concerning him; when the first of them denied him three times, although he had heard from his mouth what would befall the man who denied him before men? He gave them, then, the words, as he said, which the Father gave him: but when they received them not outwardly in the ears, but inwardly in their hearts spiritually, then they truly received them, because then they truly knew; and they truly knew because they truly believed.

**7.** But how the Father gave to the Son himself those words — in what words will a man be able to explain it? The question seems indeed easier, if according to that which he is the son of man he be believed to have received those words from the Father. Although, who will narrate when and how, born of the Virgin, he learned them; when even that very generation of his which was brought about of the Virgin, who will narrate? But if, according to that which he is begotten of the Father and coeternal with the Father, he is thought to have received those words from the Father, let nothing of time be thought there, as though he first was who did not have them, and received, in order to have, what he did not have; since whatever God the Father gave to God the Son, he gave by begetting. For so the Father gave to the Son the things without which the Son could not be, just as he gave him to be. For how else would he give any words to the Word, in whom he ineffably said all things? But now what follows is to be awaited in another sermon.
