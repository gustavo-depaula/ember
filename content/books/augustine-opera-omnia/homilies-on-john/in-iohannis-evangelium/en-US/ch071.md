# Tractate 71

## *He who believes in me, the works that I do he too will do, and greater than these he will do* (Jn 14:10-14).

## Let not the servant exalt himself above the Lord, and the disciple above the master; he says they will do greater things than he himself does; but with him doing them in them or through them, not they themselves as of themselves.

**1.** Hear with your ears, receive with your minds, dearly beloved—we indeed speaking, but he teaching who does not withdraw from us. The Lord says, which just now you heard as it was read: *The words that I speak to you I do not speak of myself: but the Father, abiding in me, he does the works*[^1]. Are words, then, also works? Plainly so. For surely he who edifies his neighbor by speaking works a good work. But what is *I do not speak of myself,* but, I am not of myself who speak? For he attributes to him what he does, of whom is he himself who does it. For God the Father is not of anyone, but the Son is God indeed equal to the Father, but of God the Father. Therefore the former is God, but not of God; and light, but not of light: but the latter is God of God, light of light.

**2.** For in these two sentences, one in which it was said: *I do not speak of myself*: the other in which it was said: *But the Father, abiding in me, he does the works*: holding to each one singly, diverse heretics oppose us, who, striving not from one side, but in contrary directions, stray from the way of truth. For the Arians say: Behold, the Son is unequal to the Father, he does not speak of himself. The Sabellians say on the contrary, that is, the Patripassians: Behold, he who is the Father is himself also the Son; for what is: *The Father, abiding in me, he does the works,* but, I abide in myself who do them? You say contrary things: but not in the way that the false is contrary to the true, but as two false things are contrary to each other. By erring in diverse directions, in the middle is the way which you have left. You are separated from each other by a longer interval than from the very way of which you are deserters.

You from here, and you from there, come here: do not pass over from one to the other, but, by coming from here and from there to us, find one another. Sabellians, acknowledge him whom you pass over; Arians, make equal him whom you set beneath, and you will walk in the true way with us. For there is that whereof you both ought mutually to be admonished, the one by the other.

You from here, and you from there, come here: do not pass over from one to the other, but, by coming from here and from there to us, find one another. Sabellians, acknowledge him whom you pass over; Arians, make equal him whom you set beneath, and you will walk in the true way with us. For there is that whereof you both ought mutually to be admonished, the one by the other. Hear, Sabellian: so far is the Son not the Father, but another, that the Arian asserts him unequal to the Father. Hear, Arian: so far is the Son equal to the Father, that the Sabellian says he is the very same as the Father. You, add him whom you take away; you, fill up him whom you lessen, and you both stand with us: because neither do you take away, nor do you lessen him who is both another from the Father, that you may convict the Sabellian, and equal to the Father, that you may convict the Arian. For to both he cries out: *I and the Father are one*[^2]. What he says, *one,* let the Arians hear; what he says, *are,* let the Sabellians hear: and let neither the former, by denying him equal, nor the latter, by denying him another, be vain. If, then, because he said: *The words that I speak to you I do not speak of myself,* he is for this reason thought to be of so unequal a power that he does not do what he himself wills; let what he said be heard: *As the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so the Son too gives life to whom he wills*[^3]. Likewise, if because he said: *The Father, abiding in me, he does the works,* he is for this reason thought not to be one and the Father another; let what he said be heard: *Whatever the Father does, these the Son also does likewise*[^4]; and let it be understood not as one twice, but as two, one.

But because the one is so equal to the other that yet the one is of the other, therefore he does not speak of himself, because he is not of himself: and therefore the Father, abiding in him, does the works himself, because he through whom and with whom he does them is only of him. Finally, he adds, and says: *Do you not believe that I am in the Father, and the Father in me? Otherwise, believe on account of the works themselves*[^5]. Before, Philip alone was reproved, but now it is shown that he was not the only one there who needed to be reproved. *On account of the works themselves,* he says, *believe that I am in the Father, and the Father in me*: for if we were separate, we could in no way work inseparably.

## He promises that he himself will do greater works.

**3.** But what is it that follows: *Amen, amen I say to you, he who believes in me, the works that I do he too will do, and greater than these he will do; because I go to the Father: and whatever you ask in my name, this I will do. That the Father may be glorified in the Son, if you ask anything in my name, this I will do*[^6]? Therefore he promised that he himself would do those greater works also. Let not the servant exalt himself above the Lord, and the disciple above the master: he says that they will do greater things than he himself does; but with him doing them in them or through them, not they themselves as of themselves. For to him it is sung: *I will love you, O Lord, my strength*[^7]. But what, after all, are these greater works? Is it perhaps that, as they passed by, even their shadow healed the sick[^8]? For it is greater that a shadow should heal than a fringe[^9]. The latter he did through himself, the former through them; but yet both he himself. But yet when he said these things, he was commending the works of his words: for he had said thus: *The words that I speak to you I do not speak of myself; but the Father, abiding in me, he does the works.* What works did he then mean, but the words which he was speaking? They heard and believed, and the fruit of those same words was their faith: but yet, the disciples preaching the gospel, not so few as they were, but the nations too believed; these are without doubt greater works. Yet he did not say: Greater than these you will do, that we should think the Apostles alone would do them; but: *He who believes in me,* he says, *the works that I do he too will do, and greater than these he will do.* Does, then, whoever believes in Christ do the works that Christ does, or greater than Christ? These things are not to be treated in passing, nor ought they to be rushed by haste; but this discourse, now to be concluded, compels them to be deferred.
