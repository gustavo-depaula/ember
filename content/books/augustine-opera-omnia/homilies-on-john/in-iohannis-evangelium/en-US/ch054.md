# Tractate 54

## *The things that I speak, as the Father said to me, so I speak* (Jn 12:44-50).

## He has roused us to a great longing for his inner sweetness; but we grasp it by growing, we grow by walking, we walk by advancing, that we may be able to arrive.

**1.** While our Lord Jesus Christ was speaking among the Jews and doing such great signs of miracles, some believed, predestined to eternal life, whom he also called his sheep: but some did not believe, nor could they believe, in that by the hidden, yet not unjust, judgment of God they had been blinded and hardened, he forsaking them who resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble[^1].

And of those who believed, some confessed so far as to come, with branches of palms received, to meet him as he came, rejoicing in the same confession of praise: but others, of the rulers, did not dare to confess, lest they be put out of the synagogue; whom the Evangelist marked, saying that *they loved the glory of men more than the glory of God*. And of those who did not believe, some were afterward going to believe, whom he foresaw, where he says: *When you shall have lifted up the Son of man, then you will know that I am he*[^2]: but others were going to remain in the same unbelief, whose imitator is also that nation of the Jews, which afterward, having been conquered, was scattered over almost the whole world, to bear witness to the prophecy that is written of Christ.

## He who believes in the Father must believe in the Son.

**2.** These things being so, and his passion now drawing near: *Jesus cried out, and said*; whence today's reading began: *He who believes in me believes not in me, but in him who sent me; and he who sees me sees him who sent me*[^3]. He had already said in a certain place: *My doctrine is not mine, but his who sent me*[^4]. Where we understood that he called his doctrine the Word of the Father, which he himself is; and that he signified this by saying, *My doctrine is not mine, but his who sent me,* namely that he himself was not from himself, but had one from whom he was. For he is God of God, the Son of the Father: but the Father is not God of God, but God the Father of the Son.

But now, as for what he says: *He who believes in me believes not in me, but in him who sent me,* how are we to understand it, except that as a man he appeared to men, while as God he was hidden? And lest they should think him to be only what they saw, wishing himself to be believed such and so great as the Father also is: *He who believes in me,* he says, *believes not in me,* that is, in this which he sees; *but in him who sent me,* that is, in the Father. But he who believes in the Father must believe him to be the Father; and he who believes him to be the Father must believe him to have a Son: and through this, he who believes in the Father must believe in the Son. But lest anyone should believe this of the only-begotten Son, which is of those who are called sons of God according to grace, not nature, as the Evangelist says: *He gave them power to become sons of God*[^5]; whence also is that which the Lord himself recalled as said in the Law: *I said: You are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High*[^6]: therefore he said: *He who believes in me believes not in me*; lest the whole that is believed of Christ should be believed according to the man. He believes in me, then, he says, who believes not in me according to that which he sees of me, but in him who sent me: so that, when he believes in the Father, he may believe him to have a Son equal to himself, and then truly believe in me. For if he should think him to have only sons according to grace, who are surely his creature, not the Word, but made through the Word, and not to have a Son equal to himself and coeternal, always born, equally unchangeable, in nothing unlike or unequal; he does not believe in the Father who sent me, because the Father who sent me is not such.

**3.** And therefore, when he had said: *He who believes in me believes not in me, but in him who sent me*; lest it should be thought that he wished the Father to be understood as the Father of many sons regenerated through grace, not of the one Word equal to himself, he at once added: *And he who sees me sees him who sent me.* Did he say: He who sees me sees not me, but him who sent me; as he had said: *He who believes in me believes not in me, but in him who sent me*? For he said the former lest, as he was seen, he should be believed to be only the son of man: but he said this latter, that he should be believed equal to the Father. He who believes in me, he says, believes not in this, that he sees me, but believes in him who sent me. Or, when he believes in the Father, who begot me equal to himself, let him believe in me not as he sees me, but as he believes in him who sent me: for so far is there no distance between him and me, that he who sees me sees him who sent me.

His apostles, certainly, the Lord Christ himself sent, as their very name indicates: for as in Greek "angels," in Latin are called "messengers," so in Greek "apostles," in Latin are called "those sent." Yet never would any of the apostles dare to say: *He who believes in me believes not in me, but in him who sent me*: for he would not say at all: *He who believes in me.* For we believe an apostle, but we do not believe in an apostle: for an apostle does not justify the ungodly. *But to one who believes in him who justifies the ungodly, his faith is reckoned unto righteousness*[^7]. An apostle could say: He who receives me receives him who sent me; or: He who hears me hears him who sent me: for this the Lord himself said to them: *He who receives you receives me, and he who receives me receives him who sent me*[^8]. Because the lord is honored in the servant, and the father in the son: but the father as in a son, the lord as in a servant. But the only-begotten Son could rightly say: *Believe in God, and believe in me*[^9]; and what he now says: *He who believes in me believes not in me, but in him who sent me.* He did not take away faith in himself from the believer, but he was unwilling that the believer should remain in the form of a servant: since, when anyone believes in the Father who sent him, he assuredly believes in the Son, without whom he knows the Father not to be; and he so believes as to believe him equal, since there follows: *And he who sees me sees him who sent me.*

**4.** Attend to the rest: *I have come as a light into the world, that everyone who believes in me may not remain in darkness*[^10]. He said in a certain place to his disciples: *You are the light of the world. A city set on a mountain cannot be hidden, nor do men light a lamp and put it under a bushel, but on a lampstand, that it may give light to all who are in the house: so let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^11]: yet he did not say to them: You have come as a light into the world, that everyone who believes in you may not remain in darkness. Nowhere, I affirm, can this be read. Lights, then, are all the saints; but by believing they are enlightened by him, from whom whoever withdraws will be in darkness. But that light by which they are enlightened cannot withdraw from itself; because it is altogether unchangeable. We believe, then, the enlightened light, as a prophet, as an apostle: but we believe it for this reason, that we may not believe in that which is enlightened, but may with it believe in that light by which it is enlightened; that we too may be enlightened, not by it, but with it, by him by whom it is. But when he says: *That everyone who believes in me may not remain in darkness*; he shows clearly enough that he found all in darkness: but that they may not remain in that darkness in which they were found, they ought to believe in the light which came into the world, because through it the world was made.

## Now is the time of mercy, afterward will be the time of judgment.

**5.** *And if anyone hears my words,* he says, *and does not keep them, I do not judge him*[^12]. Remember the things I know you have heard in the previous readings; and you who perhaps have forgotten, recall them; and you who were not present, but are present now, hear how the Son says: *I do not judge him*; though he says in another place: *The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*[^13]: except that it must be understood: Now I do not judge him. Why does he not judge now? Attend to what follows: *For I came not,* he says, *to judge the world, but to save the world*[^14]: that is, to make the world safe. Now, then, is the time of mercy, afterward will be the time of judgment: because: *Mercy,* he says, *and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord*[^15].

**6.** But concerning that very last judgment to come, see what he says: *He who despises me and does not receive my words has one who judges him: the word that I have spoken, that will judge him on the last day*[^16]. He did not say: He who despises me and does not receive my words, I do not judge him on the last day. For if he had said this, I do not see how it could fail to be contrary to that sentence where he says: *The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son.* But when he said: *He who despises me and does not receive my words has one who judges him*; to those waiting to know who that might be, he went on to add: *The word that I have spoken, that will judge him on the last day*: he showed clearly enough that he himself will judge on the last day. For he spoke himself, he announced himself, he set himself as the door, by which he himself, as the shepherd, would enter to the sheep. So those who did not hear will be judged in one way, those who heard and despised in another. *For those who sinned without the Law,* says the Apostle, *will also perish without the Law: and those who sinned in the Law will be judged by the Law*[^17].

## All the commandments of the Father are in the Word of the Father.

**7.** *For I,* he says, *have not spoken of myself*[^18]. He says he has not spoken of himself, because he is not of himself. This we have already often said; this, as something most well known, we ought not to teach but to recall. *But the Father who sent me, he gave me a commandment what I should say and what I should speak*[^19]. We would not labor, if we knew we were speaking with those with whom we spoke the previous things, and with those very ones, not all, but those who retain in memory what they heard: but now, because perhaps some are present who did not hear, and are like those who have forgotten what they heard, on their account let those bear with our delays who remember what they heard.

How does the Father give a commandment to the only Son? By what word does he speak to the Word, since the Son himself is the only-begotten Word? Is it through an angel, since through him the angels were created? Is it through a cloud, which, when it sounded to the Son, did not sound for his own sake—which he himself says elsewhere—but for the sake of others who needed to hear thus? Is it through a sound sent forth by lips, who has no body; nor is the Son separated from the Father by any interval of place, so that there should be air between them, by the striking of which a voice comes to be and reaches the ear? Far be it that we should suspect such things of that incorporeal and ineffable substance.

The only Son is the Word of the Father, and the Wisdom of the Father; in it are all the commandments of the Father. For the Son did not at some time not know the Father's commandment, so that it was necessary for him to have in time what before he did not have. For he so received from the Father what he has, that he received it by being born, and the Father gave it him by begetting. For he is also life, and he received life surely by being born, not by first existing without life. For the Father too has life, and what he has, he is: yet he did not receive it, because he is not from another. But the Son received life, the Father giving it, from whom he is: and he too is what he has; for he has life, and is life. Hear him speaking: *As the Father has,* he says, *life in himself, so he has given to the Son also to have life in himself*[^20]. Did he give it to one existing and not having it? But he gave it by the very act by which he begot, who begot life, and as life begot life. And because he begot an equal, not an unequal life; therefore it was said: *As he has life in himself, so he has given to the Son also to have life in himself.* He gave life, because by begetting life, what did he give but to be life? And because the very birth is eternal, the Son who is life was never not, the Son was never without life; and as the birth is eternal, so he who was born is eternal life. So too the commandment which the Son did not have, the Father did not give; but, as I said, in the Wisdom of the Father, which is the Word of the Father, are all the commandments of the Father. But it is said that a commandment is given, because the one to whom it is said to be given is not of himself: and this is to give to the Son, without whom the Son never was, namely to beget the Son who never was not.

**8.** And there follows: *And I know that his commandment is life eternal*[^21]. If, then, the Son himself is life eternal, and the Father's commandment is life eternal, what else has been said than: I am the commandment of the Father? Accordingly, that too which he adds, and says: *The things that I speak, as the Father said to me, so I speak*; let us not take *the Father said to me* as though the Father spoke words to the only Word, or the Word of God needs words of God. The Father, then, said to the Son, as he gave life to the Son: not that he was ignorant or did not have it, but that he himself was the Son. But what is *as he said to me, so I speak,* but: I speak the truth? As he said as the truthful one, so the Son speaks these things as the truth. But the truthful one begot the truth. What, then, could he now say to the truth? For the truth was not imperfect, that something true should be added to it. He said, then, to the truth, because he begot the truth. Moreover, the truth itself so speaks as it was said to it: but to those who understand, whom it teaches as it was born. But that men might believe what they are not yet able to understand, words sounded from the mouth of flesh, and passed away; flying past, the sounds resounded, the little spans of their times being finished: but the things themselves, of which the sounds are signs, transferred in a certain way into the memory of those who heard, have come even to us through letters, which are visible signs. The truth does not speak so: it speaks within to understanding minds, it instructs without sound, it floods them with intelligible light. He, then, who can see in it the eternity of its birth, hears it speaking thus, as the Father said to it what it should speak. He has roused us to a great longing for his inner sweetness: but we grasp it by growing, we grow by walking, we walk by advancing, that we may be able to arrive.
