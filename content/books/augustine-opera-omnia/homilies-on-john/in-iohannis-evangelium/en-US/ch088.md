# Tractate 88

## *All these things they will do to you on account of my name*[^1].

## What else is this to say than: they will persecute me in you, and they will not keep your word, because it is mine?

**1.** The Lord, exhorting his servants to bear patiently the hatreds of the world, sets before them no greater and better example than from himself: since, as the apostle Peter says, *Christ suffered for us, leaving us an example, that we should follow in his steps*[^2]. And if we do this, we do it by his help, who said, *Without me you can do nothing*[^3]. Finally, to those to whom he had already said, *If the world hates you, know that it hated me before you*[^4], now also, in that which you heard when the Gospel was read, he says: *Remember my word that I said to you: The servant is not greater than his master. If they persecuted me, they will also persecute you; if they kept my word, they will keep yours also*[^5]. But by saying, *The servant is not greater than his master,* does he not plainly show how we ought to understand what he had said above, *I will no longer call you servants*[^6]? For see, here he calls them servants. For what else is, *The servant is not greater than his master: if they persecuted me, they will also persecute you*? It is manifest, therefore, that the servant who *does not abide in the house forever*[^7], the one belonging to the fear which love casts out, is to be understood where it is said, *I will no longer call you servants*; but here, where it is said, *The servant is not greater than his master: if they persecuted me, they will also persecute you,* the servant signified is the one belonging to the chaste fear, which remains forever and ever. For this servant will hear: *Well done, good servant, enter into the joy of your Lord*[^8].

**2.** *But all these things,* he says, *they will do to you on account of my name, because they do not know him who sent me*[^9]. What *all things will they do,* if not what he said — namely, they will hate, and persecute, and despise the word? For if they did not keep their word, yet did not hate them, nor persecute them; or even if they hated them, yet did not persecute them: they would not do all these things. But *all these things they will do to you on account of my name* — what else is this to say than, they will hate me in you, they will persecute me in you, and they will not keep your word, because it is mine? For *all these things they will do to you on account of my name*: not yours, but *mine.* So much the more wretched, then, are those who do these things on account of this name, as those are the more blessed who suffer these things on account of this name: as he himself says elsewhere, *Blessed are those who suffer persecution for the sake of righteousness*[^10]. For this is on account of me, or *on account of my name*: because, as the Apostle teaches, *he was made for us wisdom from God, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption; that, as it is written, He who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^11]. For the evil do these things to the evil; but not for the sake of righteousness: and therefore both are wretched, both those who do and those who suffer them. The good also do them to the evil: where, even if these do them for the sake of righteousness, yet those do not suffer them for the sake of righteousness.

## For the sake of righteousness, or for the sake of iniquity?

**3.** But someone says: If, when the evil persecute the good for the name of Christ, the good suffer for the sake of righteousness, then surely the evil do these things to them for the sake of righteousness; and if this is so, then also, when the good persecute the evil for the sake of righteousness, the evil too suffer for the sake of righteousness. For if the evil can persecute the good for the name of Christ, why can the evil not suffer persecution from the good for the name of Christ; and what is that, but for the sake of righteousness? For if the evil suffer not on account of that for which the good act — because the good act for the sake of righteousness, the evil suffer for the sake of unrighteousness — then neither can the evil act on account of that for which the good suffer, because the evil act for the sake of unrighteousness, the good suffer for the sake of righteousness. How, then, will it be true, *All these things they will do to you on account of my name,* when they do not act for the name of Christ, that is, not for the sake of righteousness, but for the sake of their own iniquity?

This question is solved thus, if we understand it said, *All these things they will do to you on account of my name,* so that the whole is referred to the righteous; as if it were said: All these things you will suffer from them on account of my name — so that *they will do to you* means, you will suffer from them. But if *on account of my name* is so taken as though he said, on account of my name which they hate in you, it can also be taken, and on account of the righteousness which they hate in you: and through this, the good, when they persecute the evil, can rightly be said both to act for the sake of righteousness, which they love in persecuting the evil, and for the sake of iniquity, which they hate in those same evil men: so therefore the evil too can be said to suffer both on account of the iniquity that is punished in them, and on account of the righteousness that is exercised in their punishment.

**4.** Again it can be asked: if the evil too persecute the evil — as impious kings and judges, when they were persecutors of the pious, certainly also punished murderers and adulterers and any malefactors whom they found to have acted against the public laws — how is what the Lord says to be understood: *If you were of the world, the world would love what was its own*[^12]? For the world does not love those whom it punishes, by whom we see the aforesaid kinds of crimes for the most part punished — unless it be that the world is in those by whom such crimes are punished, and the world is in those by whom such crimes are loved. And so that world which is understood in the evil and impious both hates what is its own, in that part of men by which it harms the criminal; and loves what is its own, in that part of men by which it favors those same criminals.

Therefore: *All these things they will do to you on account of my name,* was said either thus, on account of that which you suffer; or thus, on account of that which they themselves do: because this too they hate in you while they persecute. And he added: *Because they do not know him who sent me*[^13]. This is to be understood as said according to that knowledge, of which it is written elsewhere: *But to know you is consummate sense*[^14]. For those who by this knowledge know the Father by whom Christ was sent in no way persecute those whom Christ gathers; because they too are gathered with them by Christ.
