# Tractate 46

## *The good shepherd gives his life for his sheep. But the hireling . . .*[^1].

## Even hirelings are necessary. For many in the Church, pursuing earthly advantages, yet preach Christ, and through them the voice of Christ is heard; and the sheep follow, not the hireling, but the voice of the shepherd through the hireling.

**1.** The Lord Jesus, speaking to his sheep, both present and future—those who were then present, because they were where his sheep already were, who were to be his sheep: likewise to those present and future, both them and us, and as many as even after us shall be his sheep—showed who had been sent to them. All, then, hear the voice of their shepherd saying: *I am the good shepherd*[^2]. He would not add, good, unless there were bad shepherds. But the bad shepherds are themselves thieves and robbers: or at any rate, at most, hirelings. For all the persons that he set down here we ought to seek out, distinguish, know. For the Lord has now opened two things, which he had in a manner set forth as closed: now we know that he himself is the door, we know that he himself is the shepherd. Who the thieves and robbers are was made plain in yesterday's reading; but today we have heard of the hireling, we have heard too of the wolf: yesterday the doorkeeper too was named. Among the good, then, are the door, the doorkeeper, the shepherd, and the sheep: among the evil, the thieves and robbers, the hirelings, the wolf.

**2.** We take the door to be the Lord Christ, and himself the shepherd: but whom the doorkeeper? For these two things he himself expounded; the doorkeeper he left us to seek. And what does he say about the doorkeeper? *To him,* he says, *the doorkeeper opens*[^3]. To whom does he open? To the shepherd. What does he open to the shepherd? The door. And what is the door itself? The shepherd himself. Surely, if the Lord Christ had not expounded, had he not himself said: *I am the shepherd*, and: *I am the door*[^4], would any of us have dared to say that Christ himself is both shepherd and door? For if he had said: I am the shepherd, and had not said: I am the door; we would have been going to ask what the door was, and perhaps, thinking it something else, would have been going to remain before the door. His grace and mercy expounded the shepherd to us, said himself; expounded the door, said himself: the doorkeeper he left us to seek.

Whom shall we call the doorkeeper? Whomever we find, care must be taken that he not be reckoned greater than the door itself; because in the houses of men the doorkeeper is greater than the door. For the doorkeeper is set over the door, the door is not set over the doorkeeper; because the doorkeeper guards the door, the door does not guard the doorkeeper. I do not dare to say that anyone is greater than the door; for I have now heard what the door is: it is not hidden from me, I am not left to my own conjecture, no human surmise is allowed me; God said it, the truth said it, what the unchangeable one said cannot be changed.

**3.** I will say, then, in the depth of this question what seems to me: let each one choose what pleases him; yet let him think devoutly, as it is written: *Think of the Lord in goodness, and in simplicity of heart seek him*[^5]. Perhaps we ought to take the doorkeeper to be the Lord himself. For shepherd and door are far more different from each other, in human affairs, than doorkeeper and door; and yet the Lord called himself both shepherd and door. Why, then, should we not understand him to be also the doorkeeper? For if we consider the properties, the Lord Christ is neither a shepherd, as we are accustomed to know and see shepherds; nor is he a door, for a craftsman did not make him: but if according to some likeness, he is both door and shepherd; I dare to say, he is also a sheep: a sheep is surely under a shepherd, yet he is both shepherd and sheep.

Where is the shepherd? Behold, here you have it, read the Gospel: *I am the good shepherd.* Where is the sheep? Ask the prophet: *Like a sheep he was led to the slaughter*[^6]. Ask the friend of the bridegroom: *Behold the Lamb of God, behold him who takes away the sin of the world*[^7]. Still something more marvelous, according to these likenesses, I am going to say. For both lamb and sheep and shepherd are friendly to one another; but against lions the sheep are wont to be guarded by the shepherds: and yet of Christ, since he is both sheep and shepherd, we read it said: *The Lion of the tribe of Judah has conquered*[^8].

All these things, brothers, take according to likenesses, not according to properties. We are accustomed to see shepherds sitting upon a rock, and from there guarding the flocks committed to them: surely the shepherd is better than the rock on which the shepherd sits; and yet Christ is both shepherd and rock. All this according to likeness.

But if you ask of me the property: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^9]. If you ask of me the property: the only Son, of the Father, eternally begotten from eternity, equal to the one who begets, through whom all things were made, unchangeable with the Father, and, the form of a man being taken on, not changed, from the incarnation a man, the son of man and the Son of God. All this that I have said is not a likeness, but the reality.

## Who the doorkeeper is.

**4.** Let it not, then, irk us, brothers, to take him, according to certain likenesses, as the door, and himself the doorkeeper. For what is the door? That by which we enter. Who is the doorkeeper? He who opens. Who, then, opens himself, except he who expounds himself? Behold, the Lord had said "door," we had not understood; when we did not understand, it was closed: he who opened it, he is the doorkeeper. There is, then, no necessity to seek anything else, no necessity: but perhaps there is the will. If there is the will, do not go off the road, do not depart from the Trinity. If you seek another person as doorkeeper, let the Holy Spirit come forward: for the Holy Spirit will not disdain to be the doorkeeper, since the Son deigned to be the door itself. See, perhaps, the doorkeeper as the Holy Spirit: the Lord himself says to his disciples about the Holy Spirit: *He will teach you all truth*[^10]. What is the door? Christ. What is Christ? The truth. Who opens the door, except he who teaches all truth?

## Who the hireling is.

**5.** But what do we say about the hireling? He is not mentioned here among the good. *The good shepherd,* he says, *gives his life for the sheep. The hireling, and he who is not a shepherd, whose own the sheep are not, sees the wolf coming, and leaves the sheep, and flees; and the wolf seizes and scatters the sheep*[^11]. The hireling here does not bear a good character, and yet he is in some way useful: nor would he be called a hireling, unless he received a wage from the one who hired him. Who, then, is this hireling, both blameworthy and necessary? Here indeed, brothers, let the Lord himself shine for us, that we may both understand hirelings, and not be hirelings.

Who, then, is the hireling? There are in the Church certain ones set over others, of whom the apostle Paul says: *Seeking their own, not the things of Jesus Christ*[^12]. What is, seeking their own? Not loving Christ freely, not seeking God for God's sake; pursuing temporal advantages, gaping after gains, seeking honors from men. When these things are loved by one set over others, and God is served for the sake of these, whoever is such is a hireling, let him not count himself among the sons. For of such the Lord too says: *Amen, I say to you, they have received their reward*[^13]. Hear what the apostle Paul says about holy Timothy: *But I hope in the Lord Jesus to send Timothy to you soon, that I too may be of good cheer, when I know the things that concern you: for I have no one of one mind, who is genuinely solicitous about you. For all seek their own, not the things of Jesus Christ*[^14]. Among hirelings the shepherd groaned: he sought someone who would sincerely love the flock of Christ, and around him, among those who were with him at that time, did not find one. For there was not then in the Church of Christ, besides the apostle Paul and Timothy, anyone who was genuinely solicitous about the flock: but it had happened that at the time when he sent Timothy, he had around him no other of the sons; but only hirelings were with him, seeking their own, not the things of Jesus Christ. And yet he himself, genuinely solicitous about the flock, preferred to send the son, and to remain among the hirelings.

We find hirelings too; the Lord alone examines them; he who inspects the heart, he examines: yet sometimes they are understood by us. For not in vain did the Lord himself say even of wolves: *By their fruits you will know them*[^15]. Temptations question many, and then thoughts appear: but many lie hidden. Let the Lord's fold have those set over it, both sons and hirelings. But those set over it who are sons are shepherds. If they are shepherds, how is there one shepherd, except that they all are members of the one shepherd, whose own the sheep are? For they too are members of that one sheep; because *like a sheep he was led to the slaughter*[^16].

## Even hirelings are necessary.

**6.** But hear, that even hirelings are necessary. For many in the Church, pursuing earthly advantages, yet preach Christ, and through them the voice of Christ is heard: and the sheep follow, not the hireling, but the voice of the shepherd through the hireling. Hear the hirelings pointed out by the Lord himself: *The scribes,* he says, *and the Pharisees sit on the chair of Moses: what they say, do; but what they do, do not do*[^17]. What else did he say, except: Through the hirelings hear the voice of the shepherd? For by sitting on the chair of Moses they teach the law of God: so through them God teaches. But if they wish to teach their own things, do not hear, do not do. For surely such men seek their own, not the things of Jesus Christ: yet no hireling has dared to say to the people of Christ: Seek your own, not the things of Jesus Christ. For what he does badly, he does not preach from the chair of Christ: he wounds from where he does evil, not from where he says good. Pluck the grape, beware the thorn. Good, for you have understood; but for the sake of the slower I will say this same thing more plainly.

How did I say: Pluck the grape, beware the thorn; when the Lord says: *Do they gather a grape from thorns, or a fig from thistles?*[^18] It is altogether true; and yet I too said something true: Pluck the grape, beware the thorn. For sometimes a grape, having sprung up from the root of the vine, hangs on a hedge, the shoot grows, is inserted among thorns, and the thorn bears a fruit not its own. For the vine did not bring forth the thorn, but the shoot leaned upon the thorns. Do not ask anything except the roots. Seek the root of the thorn, you find it outside the vine; seek the origin of the grape, the vine brought it forth from its root.

The chair of Moses, then, was the vine: the ways of the Pharisees were the thorns. True doctrine through evil men is a shoot on a hedge, a grape among thorns. Read carefully, lest, while you seek the fruit, you tear your hand: and when you hear one saying good things, do not imitate one doing evil things. *What they say, do*, pick the grapes: *but what they do, do not do*, beware the thorns. Even through hirelings hear the voice of the shepherd, but do not be hirelings, since you are members of the shepherd. But Paul himself, the holy apostle, who said: *I have no one who is genuinely solicitous about you; for all seek their own, not the things of Jesus Christ*[^19]; in another place, distinguishing between hirelings and sons, see what he said: *Some out of envy and contention; but some also out of good will preach Christ: some out of love, knowing that I am set for the defense of the Gospel; but some out of contentiousness announce Christ, not purely, supposing to stir up tribulation for my chains*[^20]. These were hirelings, they envied the apostle Paul. Why did they envy, except because they sought temporal things? But attend to what he adds: *For what? So long as in every way, whether by occasion or in truth, Christ is announced; and in this I rejoice, yes, and I will rejoice*[^21]. Christ is the truth: let the truth be announced by hirelings by occasion, let the truth be announced by sons in truth: the sons patiently await the eternal inheritance of the Father, the hirelings hastily long for the temporal wage of the one who hires them: let my human glory, which I see the hirelings envying, be diminished; and yet through the tongues both of hirelings and of sons let the divine glory of Christ be spread abroad, since, whether by occasion or in truth, Christ is announced.

## Shepherds are members of the Shepherd.

**7.** We have seen who the hireling too is. Who is the wolf, except the devil? And what was said of the hireling? *When he sees the wolf coming, he flees; because the sheep are not his own, nor has he care for the sheep*[^22]. Was the apostle Paul such? Far be it. Was Peter such? Far be it. Were the other Apostles such, except Judas the son of perdition? Far be it. Were those, then, shepherds? Plainly shepherds. And whence one shepherd? I have already said, shepherds, because members of the shepherd. They rejoiced in that head, they were in concord under that head, they lived by one spirit in the framework of one body: and through this all belonged to one shepherd.

If, then, they were shepherds, not hirelings, why did they flee when they suffered persecution? Expound to us, O Lord. I have seen in the Epistle Paul fleeing: *he was let down through the wall in a basket, to escape the hand of the persecutor*[^23]. Had he, then, no care for the sheep, whom he was deserting when the wolf came? He had, plainly, but he was commending them by his prayers to the shepherd seated in heaven: and he was preserving himself for their usefulness by fleeing, as he says in a certain place: *To remain in the flesh is necessary for your sake*[^24]. For from the shepherd himself all had heard: *If they persecute you in one city, flee to another*[^25]. May the Lord deign to expound this question to us. Lord, you said to those whom you surely willed to be faithful shepherds, whom you were forming to be your members: *If they persecute you, flee.* You do them an injury, then, when you reproach the hirelings, who see the wolf coming and flee. We ask that you show us what the depth of the question holds: let us knock, he will be present who will open himself, the doorkeeper of the door, which is himself.

## We pasture the sheep and at the same time are pastured.

**8.** Who is the hireling, who sees the wolf coming and flees? He who seeks his own, not the things of Jesus Christ: he does not dare freely to convict the one sinning[^26]. Behold, some one or other has sinned, gravely sinned; he is to be rebuked, to be excommunicated: but, excommunicated, he will be an enemy, will lie in wait, will harm when he can. Now that man who seeks his own, not the things of Jesus Christ, lest he lose what he pursues, the advantage of human friendship, and incur the trouble of human enmities, keeps silent, does not correct. Behold, the wolf has seized the throat of the sheep: the devil has persuaded a believer to adultery; you keep silent, you do not rebuke: O hireling, you saw the wolf coming, and you fled. He perhaps answers and says: Behold, here I am, I did not flee. You fled, because you kept silent; you kept silent, because you feared. Flight of the mind is fear. In body you stood, in spirit you fled: which he was not doing who said: *Although in body absent, in spirit I am with you*[^27]. For how was he fleeing in spirit, who even when absent in body convicted the fornicators by letters?

Our affections are movements of the mind. Joy is the diffusion of the mind; sadness, the contraction of the mind: desire, the going forward of the mind; fear, the flight of the mind. For you are diffused in mind, when you are delighted; you are contracted in mind, when you are troubled: you go forward in mind, when you long for something; you flee in mind, when you are afraid. Behold whence that hireling, when the wolf is seen, is said to flee. Why? Because he has no care for the sheep. Why has he no care for the sheep? Because he is a hireling. What is, he is a hireling? Seeking a temporal wage, and he will not dwell in the house forever.

There are still things here to be sought out, and discussed with you, but to burden you is not advisable. For we minister the Lord's food to our fellow slaves; in the Lord's pastures we pasture the sheep, and at the same time are pastured. As what is necessary must not be denied, so a weak heart must not be overloaded with a multitude of fodder. Let it not, then, be irksome to your Charity, that all the things which I judge still to be discussed here, I do not discuss today: but again, in the name of the Lord, on the days for delivering the sermon, the same reading will be recited to us, and, with his help, will be more diligently treated.
