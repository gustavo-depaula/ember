# Homily 3

## *And of his fullness we have received . . .* [^1]

Of his fullness we have received grace for grace. What grace do we first receive? Faith. Having attained this grace of faith, you will be righteous by faith; and you will merit God by living from faith: when you have merited God by living from faith, you will receive as your reward immortality, and eternal life. And that too is grace.

**1.** The grace and truth of God, with which the only-begotten Son, our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, appeared full to the saints, we have undertaken in the name of the Lord, and promised to your Charity, to distinguish from the Old Testament, since it is a matter of the New Testament. Be present, then, attentive, so that God may grant both what I grasp and what you grasp to hear. For it will remain that, if the seed which is scattered in your hearts the birds have not carried off, nor the thorns choked, nor the heat scorched, when the rain of daily exhortations comes, and with your good thoughts—by which that is done in the heart which is done in a field with the harrow, so that the clod is broken and the seed is covered and can sprout—you may bring forth fruit, at which the husbandman may rejoice and be glad [^2]. But if, in return for good seed and good rain, we have brought forth not fruit but thorns, neither will the seed be accused, nor will the rain be at fault, but fire due to thorns is prepared [^3].

## From where humbled, from there glorified.

**2.** We are men—which, I think, need not long be urged upon your Charity—Christians; and if Christians, then surely by that very name belonging to Christ. We bear his sign on our forehead: of which we are not ashamed, if we also bear it in our heart. His sign is his humility. By a star the Magi recognized him; and this was a sign given concerning the Lord, heavenly and splendid: he did not will a star on the forehead to be his sign for the faithful, but his cross. From where he was humbled, from there he was glorified: from there he raised up the humble, to where he himself, humbled, descended.

We belong, then, to the Gospel, we belong to the New Testament. ***The Law was given through Moses, but grace and truth came through Jesus Christ*** [^4]. We question the Apostle, and he tells us that we are not under the Law, but under grace. *He sent*, then, *his Son, made of a woman, made under the Law, to redeem those who were under the Law, that we might receive the adoption of sons* [^5]. See, for this Christ came, to redeem those who were under the Law: that we might now be not under the Law, but under grace. Who, then, gave the Law? He gave the Law who also gave grace: but the Law he sent through a servant, while with grace he himself descended. And from what cause had men become under the Law? By not fulfilling the Law. For he who fulfills the law is not under the law, but with the law: but he who is under the law is not lifted up, but pressed down by the law. And so the Law makes all men who are placed under the Law guilty; and to this end it is over their head, to show sins, not to take them away.

The Law, then, commands; the giver of the law has mercy in that which the law commands. Men, trying to fulfill by their own strength what was commanded by the law, by their own rash and headlong presumption fell; and they are not with the law, but were made guilty under the law: and since by their own strength they could not fulfill the law, having been made guilty under the law, they implored the help of a deliverer; and the guilt of the law made the proud sick. The sickness of the proud became the confession of the humble: now the sick confess that they are sick; let the physician come, and heal the sick.

**3.** Who is the physician? Our Lord Jesus Christ. Who is our Lord Jesus Christ? He who was seen even by those by whom he was crucified. He who was seized, buffeted, scourged, smeared with spittle, crowned with thorns, hung on the cross, who died, was wounded with a lance, taken down from the cross, placed in a tomb. That same one is our Lord Jesus Christ; that very one indeed, and he is wholly the physician of our wounds, that crucified one who was insulted, at whose hanging his persecutors wagged their heads and said: *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross* [^6]: he is wholly our physician, he indeed.

Why, then, did he not show those who insulted him that he was the Son of God: so that, if he allowed himself to be raised upon the cross, at least when they said to him, *If he is the Son of God, let him come down from the cross*, he would then have come down, and shown them that he was truly the Son of God, whom they had dared to mock?

He would not. Why would he not? Was it perhaps because he could not?

He plainly could. For which is greater, to come down from the cross, or to rise from the tomb? But he bore with those who insulted him: for the cross was undertaken not as a demonstration of power, but as an example of patience. There he cured your wounds, where he long bore his own: there he healed you from everlasting death, where he deigned to die in time. And did he die, or did death die in him? What kind of death is it that slew death?

## Where is the image of God?

**4.** Is he, then, our Lord Jesus Christ wholly the one who was seen, and held, and crucified? Is he wholly this?

He is that one indeed, but not wholly that which the Jews saw; the whole Christ is not this.

And what is he?

*In the beginning was the Word* [^7].

In what beginning?

*And the Word was with God* [^8].

And what sort of Word?

*And the Word was God* [^9].

Was this Word perhaps made by God?

No. For *this was in the beginning with God* [^10].

What then? Are the other things that God made not like the Word?

No: because *all things were made through him, and without him was nothing made* [^11].

How were all things made through him?

Because *what was made, in him was life* [^12]: and before it was made, it was life. What was made is not life: but in the skill, that is, in the Wisdom of God, before it was made, it was life. What was made passed away; what is in Wisdom cannot pass away. Life, then, was in him, that which was made.

And what sort of life?

For the soul too is the life of the body: our body has its own life; and when it has lost that life, there is the death of the body:

was that life, then, of such a kind?

No: *but the life was the light of men* [^13].

Is it the light of cattle?

For this light is the light both of men and of cattle. There is a certain light of men: let us see in what men differ from cattle, and then we will understand what the light of men is.

You do not differ from cattle except in understanding: do not boast of anything else. Do you presume on strength? You are beaten by the beasts. Do you presume on speed? You are beaten by flies. Do you presume on beauty? How great is the beauty in the feathers of a peacock! From where, then, are you better? From the image of God. Where is the image of God? In the mind, in the understanding.

If, then, you are better than the beast for this reason, that you have a mind by which you understand what the beast cannot understand; and a man is a man from this, that he is better than the beast; the light of men is the light of minds. The light of minds is above minds, and surpasses all minds. This was that life through which all things were made.

**5.** Where was it? Was it here; or was it with the Father, and not here? Or, what is truer, was it both with the Father and here? If, then, it was here, why was it not seen? Because *the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it* [^14]. O men, do not be darkness, do not be unbelieving, unjust, wicked, rapacious, greedy, lovers of the world: for these are darkness. The light is not absent, but you are absent from the light. A blind man in the sun has the sun present, but he himself is absent from the sun. Do not, then, be darkness. For this is perhaps the grace of which I am about to speak, that we should no longer be darkness, and the Apostle may say to us: *For you were once darkness; but now you are light in the Lord* [^15].

Because, then, the light of men—that is, the light of minds—was not seen, it was necessary that a man should bear testimony concerning the light, not indeed a man of darkness, but one already enlightened. Yet not because he was enlightened was he himself the light; *but that he might bear witness concerning the light*. For *he was not the light* [^16].

And what was the light?

*It was the true light, which enlightens every man coming into this world* [^17].

And where was it?

*It was in this world* [^18].

And how *was it in this world*? Is this light in the world in the way that this light of the sun, the moon, and lamps is?

No. Because *the world was made through him, and the world did not know him* [^19]: that is, *the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it* [^20]. For the world is darkness; because the lovers of the world are the world.

For did the creature not recognize its Creator? Heaven gave testimony by the star; the sea gave testimony, it carried the Lord walking upon it; the winds gave testimony, at his command they grew still; the earth gave testimony, it trembled when he was crucified: if all these gave testimony, how did the world not know him, except that the world means the lovers of the world, dwelling with their heart in the world? And the world is bad, because the inhabitants of the world are bad: just as a house is bad—not the walls, but the inhabitants.

**6.** *He came to his own*, that is, he came to his own things: *and his own did not receive him* [^21]. What hope is there, then, except that *as many as received him, he gave them power to become sons of God* [^22]? If they become sons, they are born: if they are born, how are they born? Not of the flesh, *not of bloods, not of the will of the flesh, not of the will of a man; but they were born of God* [^23]. Let them rejoice, then, that they were born of God; let them presume that they belong to God; let them receive the proof that they were born of God: *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us* [^24]. If the Word was not ashamed to be born of man, are men ashamed to be born of God? But because he did this, he healed us: because he healed us, we see. For this, that *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*, became a medicine for us, so that, because we had been blinded by earth, we might be healed by earth: and, being healed, what should we see? *And we saw*, he says, *his glory, glory as of the Only-begotten from the Father, full of grace and truth* [^25].

**7.** *John bears witness concerning him, and cries out, saying: This was he of whom I said: He who comes after me has been set before me* [^26]. He comes after me, and has gone before me.

What does *has been set before me* mean? He has gone before me: not, he was made before I was made; but, he has been placed ahead of me: this is, *has been set before me*.

Why has he been set before you, since he came after you?

*Because he was before me* [^27].

Before you, O John? What is so great, if he was before you? Well, since you bear witness to him: let us hear him himself saying: *And before Abraham was, I am* [^28].

But Abraham too arose in the midst of the human race; many before him, many after him: hear the voice of the Father to the Son: *Before the morning star I begot you* [^29]. He who was begotten before the morning star himself enlightens all. For a certain one who fell was called the morning star: for he was an angel, and was made the devil; and Scripture said of him: *The morning star, who rose in the morning, has fallen* [^30]. From what cause a morning star? Because, being enlightened, he shone. From what cause was he made dark? Because he did not stand in the truth [^31]. Therefore he is before the morning star, before every enlightened being; since the one from whom all who can be enlightened are enlightened must be before every enlightened being.

**8.** Therefore this follows: *And of his fullness we all have received* [^32].

What have you received?

*And grace for grace* [^33].

For the words of the Gospel stand thus, compared with the Greek copies. He does not say: *And of his fullness we all have received grace for grace*; but he says thus: And of his fullness we all have received, and grace for grace—that is, we have received: so that he wished us to understand that we have received I know not what of his fullness; and over and above, grace for grace. For of his fullness we received, first, grace; and again we received grace, grace for grace. What grace did we first receive? Faith. Walking in faith, we walk in grace. For from where did we merit this? By what preceding merits of ours? Let no one flatter himself, let him return into his conscience, let him search the hidden places of his thoughts, let him return to the sequence of his deeds: let him not consider what he is, if he is now something; but what he was, that he might become something; he will find that he was worthy of nothing but punishment.

If, then, you were worthy of punishment, and he came who would not punish sins but forgive sins; grace was given to you, not wages repaid. Why is it called grace? Because it is given freely. For you did not buy by preceding merits what you received. This first grace, then, the sinner received, that his sins might be forgiven. What did he merit? Let him question justice; he finds punishment: let him question mercy; he finds grace. But this God had also promised through the prophets: and so, when he came to give what he had promised, he gave not only grace, but also truth. How was the truth displayed? Because what was promised was done.

## By faith we merit God.

**9.** What, then, is *grace for grace*? By faith we merit God; and because we were not worthy to have our sins forgiven, from the fact that we received so great a gift though unworthy, it is called grace. What is grace? What is given freely. What is given freely? Given as a gift, not repaid. If it was owed, wages were repaid, not grace given as a gift; but if it was truly owed, you were good: but if, as is true, you were bad, yet you believed in him who justifies the impious (what is, who justifies the impious? Out of the impious he makes the pious); consider what ought to have hung over you through the law, and what you have attained through grace. Having attained this grace of faith, you will be righteous by faith. For *the righteous one lives by faith* [^34]; and you will merit God by living from faith: when you have merited God by living from faith, you will receive as your reward immortality, and eternal life. And that too is grace. For by what merit do you receive eternal life? By grace. For if faith is grace, and eternal life is, as it were, the wages of faith: God indeed seems to repay eternal life as something owed (Owed to whom? To the faithful one, because he merited it through faith); but because faith itself is grace, eternal life too is grace for grace.

## God crowns his own gifts.

**10.** Hear the Apostle Paul confessing grace, and afterward demanding what is owed. What is the confession of grace that is in Paul? *I who was formerly a blasphemer, and a persecutor, and insolent: but, he says, I obtained mercy* [^35]. He said he was unworthy to obtain it: yet he obtained it, not through his own merits, but through the mercy of God. Hear him now demanding what is owed, who first had received undeserved grace: *For I*, he says, *am now being poured out, and the time of my dissolution is at hand. I have fought the good fight, I have finished the course, I have kept the faith: for the rest, there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness* [^36]. Now he demands what is owed, now he requires what is owed. For see the words that follow: *which the Lord, the just judge, will repay me on that day* [^37]. To receive grace beforehand, he had need of a merciful father: to receive the reward of grace, of a just judge. He who did not condemn the impious, will he condemn the faithful? And yet, if you think rightly, he himself first gave the faith by which you merited him: for you did not merit, out of your own, that anything should be owed to you.

Therefore, when he afterward bestows the reward of immortality, he crowns his own gifts, not your merits. Therefore, brothers, *we all have received of his fullness*: of the fullness of his mercy, of the abundance of his goodness we have received. What? The remission of sins, that we might be justified by faith. And over and above, what? *And grace for grace*; that is, in return for this grace in which we live by faith, we are to receive another: and what, indeed, but grace? For if I say that this too is owed, I attribute something to myself, as to one to whom it is owed. But God crowns in us the gifts of his mercy: provided that we walk perseveringly in that grace which we first received.

**11.** *For the Law was given through Moses*, which held men guilty. For what does the Apostle say? *The Law slipped in, that the offense might abound* [^38]. This was profitable to the proud, that the offense might abound: for they gave themselves much, and attributed much, as it were, to their own strength; and they could not fulfill righteousness, unless he helped who had commanded. God, wishing to tame their pride, gave the Law, as if saying: See, fulfill it, lest you think there is no one to command. There is no lack of one to command, but there is a lack of one to fulfill.

**12.** If, then, there is a lack of one to fulfill, from what cause does he not fulfill? Because he was born with the transmission of sin and death. Born of Adam, he drew with him what was there conceived. The first man fell; and all who were born of him drew from him the concupiscence of the flesh. It was necessary that another man should be born who drew no concupiscence. Man, and man: a man unto death, and a man unto life. So the Apostle says: *For since indeed by a man came death, by a man also came the resurrection of the dead* [^39].

Through what man came death, and through what man the resurrection of the dead?

Do not hurry: it follows, and he says: *For as in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive* [^40].

Who belong to Adam?

All who were born of Adam.

Who belong to Christ?

All who were born through Christ.

Why are all in sin?

Because no one was born apart from Adam. But to be born of Adam was a matter of necessity, out of condemnation: to be born through Christ is a matter of will and of grace. Men are not compelled to be born through Christ: it was not because they willed it that they were born of Adam. Yet all who are of Adam are sinners with sin: all who are through Christ are justified and righteous, not in themselves, but in him. For if you ask about them in themselves: they are Adam: if you ask about them in him, they are Christ's.

Why?

Because that Head, our Lord Jesus Christ, did not come with the transmission of sin: but yet he came with mortal flesh.

You died in Adam, rise again in Christ.

**13.** The death of sinners was a punishment: in the Lord it was a gift of mercy, not the punishment of sin. For the Lord had nothing for which he should justly die. He himself says: *Behold, the prince of this world comes, and in me he finds nothing* [^41].

Why, then, do you die?

*But that all may know that I do the will of my Father, arise, let us go from here* [^42]. He had no reason for which he should die, and he died: you have a reason, and do you disdain to die? Deign to suffer with an even mind, through your own merit, what he deigned to suffer in order to free you from everlasting death.

Man, and man: but the one, only a man; this one, God-man. The one, a man of sin; this one, of righteousness. You died in Adam, rise again in Christ: for both are owed by you. You have now believed in Christ, yet you will pay what you owe from Adam. But the bond of sin will not hold you forever; because the temporal death of your Lord slew your eternal death. This is grace, my brothers, this too is truth, because it was promised and displayed.

**14.** This was not in the Old Testament, because the Law threatened, it did not assist; it commanded, it did not heal; it showed the sickness, it did not take it away: but it was preparing for that physician who would come with grace and truth; just as, to someone whom a physician wishes to cure, he might first send his servant, that he may find him bound. He was not well, he was unwilling to be healed, and, lest he be healed, he boasted that he was well: the Law was sent, it bound him; he finds himself guilty, now he cries out about his bonds. The Lord comes, he cures with somewhat bitter and sharp medicines: for he says to the sick man: Bear it; he says: Endure; he says: Do not love the world, have patience, let the fire of continence cure you, let your wounds endure the knife of persecutions. You were afraid, though bound: he, free and not bound, drank what he was giving you; he suffered first, that he might console you, as if saying: What you fear to suffer for yourself, I suffer first for you. This is grace, and great grace. Who worthily praises it?

## Consider the humility of Christ.

**15.** I am speaking of the humility of Christ, my brothers. The majesty of Christ and the divinity of Christ—who can speak of it? In explaining and stating, so as to speak somehow of the humility of Christ, we are not sufficient, indeed we fall short: we commit the whole to those who think, we do not fulfill it for those who hear. Consider the humility of Christ. But who, you say, explains it to us, unless you speak? Let him speak within. Better does he speak who dwells within than he who cries out without. Let him himself show you the grace of his humility, who has begun to dwell in your hearts. But now, if in explaining and dispensing his humility we fall short, who can speak of his majesty? If *the Word was made flesh* troubles us: *In the beginning was the Word*—who will explain it? Hold fast, then, brothers, to this solid ground.

**16.** *The Law was given through Moses, grace and truth came through Jesus Christ* [^43]. Through a servant the Law was given; it made men guilty: through the Emperor indulgence was given; it freed the guilty. The Law was given through Moses. Let the servant not attribute to himself anything more than what was done through him. Chosen for a great ministry, like a faithful one in the house, but yet a servant, he can act according to the Law, he cannot loose from the guilt of the Law. *The Law*, therefore, *was given through Moses, grace and truth came through Jesus Christ*.

**17.** And lest perhaps someone say: Were not grace and truth given through Moses, who saw God? he at once added: *No one has ever seen God* [^44].

And from where did God become known to Moses?

Because the Lord revealed himself to his servant.

What Lord?

Christ himself, who sent the Law ahead through a servant, that he himself might come with grace and truth. For *no one has ever seen God* [^45].

And from where did he appear to that servant, as far as he could grasp it?

But *the only-begotten*, he says, *Son who is in the bosom of the Father, he himself has made him known* [^46].

What does *in the bosom of the Father* mean?

In the secret place of the Father. For God does not have a bosom, as we have in our garments, nor is he to be thought of as sitting as we do, nor perhaps girded so as to have a fold: but because our bosom is within, the secret place of the Father is called the bosom of the Father. In the secret place of the Father, he who knew the Father, he himself made him known. For *no one has ever seen God* [^47].

He himself, then, came, and told whatever he saw. What did Moses see? Moses saw a cloud, saw an angel, saw fire: all that is a creature; it bore the type of its Lord, it did not present the presence of the Lord himself. For you have it openly in the Law: *And Moses spoke with the Lord face to face, as a friend with his friend* [^48]. You follow that same Scripture, and you find Moses saying: *If I have found grace in your sight, show me yourself plainly, that I may see you* [^49]. And it is not enough that he said it: he received the answer: *You cannot see my face* [^50]. Therefore, my brothers, an angel was speaking with Moses, bearing the type of the Lord: and all those things that were done there through the angel were promising this grace and truth that was to come. Those who search the Law well know this: and when it is fitting that we too should say something of it, as far as the Lord reveals, we do not keep silent to your Charity.

## The Wisdom of God cannot be seen.

**18.** But know this, that all the things that were seen bodily were not that substance of God. For we see those things with the eyes of the flesh: from where is the substance of God seen? Question the Gospel: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God* [^51]. There were men who, deceived by the vanity of their own heart, would say: The Father is invisible, but the Son is visible. From where visible? If on account of the flesh, because he took on flesh; that is plain. For of those who saw the flesh of Christ, some believed, some crucified him: and those who believed wavered when he was crucified; and unless they touched it after the resurrection, faith would not have been called back to them. If, then, the Son is visible on account of the flesh; we too grant it, and it is the catholic faith: but if before the flesh, as they say—that is, before he was incarnate—they rave greatly, and err greatly. For those visible things were made bodily through a creature, in which a type might be shown: the substance itself was certainly not demonstrated and made manifest. And let your Charity attend to this gentle proof. The Wisdom of God cannot be seen with the eyes. Brothers, if Christ is the Wisdom of God, and the Power of God; if Christ is the Word of God; the word of a man is not seen with the eyes—can the Word of God be seen so?

**19.** Drive out, then, carnal thoughts from your hearts, that you may truly be under grace, that you may belong to the New Testament. Therefore eternal life is promised in the New Testament. Read the Old Testament, and see that to a people still carnal the same things indeed were enjoined as to us. For to worship one God is enjoined on us too. *You shall not take the name of the Lord your God in vain* [^52] is enjoined on us too: which is the second precept. *Observe the sabbath day* [^53] is enjoined on us all the more: because it is enjoined to be observed spiritually. For the Jews observe the sabbath day in a servile way, for luxury, for drunkenness. How much better would their women make wool than dance on that day on the balconies? Far be it, brothers, that we should say they observe the sabbath. The Christian observes the sabbath spiritually, abstaining from servile work. For what is it to abstain from servile work? From sin. And from where do we prove it? Question the Lord: *Everyone who commits sin is a slave of sin* [^54]. Therefore the observance of the sabbath is enjoined on us too, spiritually. Now all those precepts are enjoined on us all the more, and are to be observed: *You shall not kill, You shall not commit adultery, You shall not steal, You shall not bear false witness, Honor your father and mother, You shall not covet your neighbor's goods, You shall not covet your neighbor's wife* [^55]. Are not all these enjoined on us too?

But seek the reward, and you will find it said there: *That your enemies may be driven from before your face, and that you may receive the land which God promised to your fathers*. Because they could not grasp invisible things, they were held by visible ones. Why were they held? Lest they perish utterly and slip away to idols. For they did this, my brothers, as we read, forgetting the great miracles that God did before their eyes. The sea was divided; a way was made amid the waves; their pursuing enemies were covered by the same waters through which they passed: and when Moses, the man of God, had withdrawn from their eyes, they asked for an idol, and said: *Make us gods to go before us, for that man has abandoned us* [^56]. All their hope was placed in a man, not in God. See, the man died: did God, who had drawn them out of the land of Egypt, die? And when they had made themselves the image of a calf, they adored it, and said: *These are your gods, O Israel, who freed you from the land of Egypt* [^57]. How soon they forgot so manifest a grace! By what means, then, was such a people to be held, except by carnal promises?

**20.** The things enjoined there in the Decalogue of the Law are the same as those enjoined on us; but the things promised are not the same as those promised to us. What is promised to us? Eternal life. *And this is eternal life, that they may know you, the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent* [^58]. The knowledge of God is promised: that is grace for grace. Brothers, now we believe, we do not see: for this faith there will be a reward, to see what we believe. The prophets knew this, but it was hidden before he came.

For a certain sighing lover says in the Psalms: *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek after* [^59]. And do you ask what he seeks? Perhaps he asks carnally for a land flowing with milk and honey, although these things are to be sought and asked for spiritually: or perhaps the subjection of his enemies, or the death of his foes, or the empires and resources of this world. For he burns with love, and sighs greatly, and is on fire, and pants. Let us see what he asks: *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek after*. What is this that he seeks after? *That I may dwell*, he says, *in the house of the Lord, all the days of my life* [^60]. And suppose that you dwell in the house of the Lord, from what will your joy there come? *That I may contemplate*, he says, *the delight of the Lord* [^61].

## May you always be satisfied and never be satisfied.

**21.** My brothers, from where do you cry out, from where do you exult, from where do you love, except that there is there a spark of this love? What do you desire, I ask you? Can it be seen with the eyes? Can it be touched? Is it some beauty that delights the eyes? Were not the martyrs vehemently loved; and when we commemorate them, do we not catch fire with love? What do we love in them, brothers? Limbs torn by wild beasts? What is more foul, if you question the eyes of the flesh? What more beautiful, if you question the eyes of the heart?

How does a most handsome young thief seem to you? How your eyes recoil! Do the eyes of the flesh recoil? If you question them, nothing is more shapely than that body, nothing more well-ordered; both the symmetry of the limbs and the delightfulness of color entice the eyes: and yet when you hear that he is a thief, you flee the man in your mind. On the other side you see an old man, bent, leaning on a staff, scarcely moving himself, furrowed all over with wrinkles: what do you see that delights the eyes? You hear that he is righteous; you love him, you embrace him.

Such rewards have been promised us, my brothers: love such a thing, sigh for such a kingdom, desire such a homeland; if you wish to arrive at that with which our Lord came—that is, at grace and truth. But if you covet bodily rewards from God, you are still under the Law, and therefore you will not fulfill the Law itself. For when you see these temporal things abound in those who offend God, your steps waver, and you say to yourself: See, I worship God, every day I run to church, my knees are worn out with prayers, and I am constantly sick: men commit murders, commit robberies; they exult and abound, it is well with them.

Were such things, then, what you were seeking from God? Surely you belonged to grace. If God gave you grace for this reason, that he gave it freely, love freely. Do not love God for a reward; let him himself be your reward. Let your soul say: *One thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek after, that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, that I may contemplate the delight of the Lord* [^62]. Do not fear that you will faint with weariness: such will be that delight of beauty, that it is always present to you, and you are never satisfied; rather, you are always satisfied, and never satisfied. For if I say that you will not be satisfied, there will be hunger; if I say that you will be satisfied, I fear weariness: where there will be neither weariness nor hunger, I know not what to say; but God has what he may display to those who do not find how to say it, and what believers may receive.
