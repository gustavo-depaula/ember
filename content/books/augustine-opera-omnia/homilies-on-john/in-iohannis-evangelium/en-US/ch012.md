# Homily 12

## No one has ascended into heaven, except him who descended from heaven[^1].

## If no one but Christ descended and ascended, what hope is there for the rest? This is the hope for the rest, that he descended for this reason, that in him and with him those might be one who through him were to ascend. Therefore be in one, be one thing, be one.

**1.** From the fact that yesterday we made your Charity attentive, we understand that you have gathered more eagerly and in greater numbers: but for now let us render, if you please, the sermon owed to the Gospel reading in order; then your Charity will hear about the peace of the Church, either what we have done, or what we hope still to do.

Now, then, let the whole attention of the heart be brought to the Gospel; let no one think of anything else. For if he who is wholly present scarcely takes it in; he who divides himself among diverse thoughts, does he not pour out even what he had taken in? But your Charity remembers that last Lord's day, so far as the Lord deigned to help, we discoursed on spiritual regeneration: which reading we have had read to you again, so that what was not then said we may, in the name of Christ and with the help of your prayers, complete.

## Spiritual regeneration is one.

**2.** Spiritual regeneration is one, just as carnal generation is one. And what Nicodemus said to the Lord, he said truly, that a man, when he is old, cannot return again into his mother's womb, and be born. He indeed said that a man, when he is old, cannot do this, as if, even were he an infant, he could. For he absolutely cannot, whether fresh from the womb, or already of advanced age, return again into the maternal bowels, and be born. But just as for carnal nativity the female bowels avail for bearing once; so for spiritual nativity the bowels of the Church avail, that each may be baptized once.

Therefore, lest perhaps anyone say: But this man was born in heresy, and this one was born in schism; all that has been cut away, if you remember, which was discussed with you concerning our three fathers, of whom God willed to be called the God, not because they alone existed, but because in them alone was completed the wholeness of signifying the future people. For we found one born of a maidservant disinherited, one born of a free woman an heir: again we found one born of a free woman disinherited, one born of a maidservant an heir. Born of a maidservant, disinherited, Ishmael; born of a free woman, an heir, Isaac; born of a free woman, disinherited, Esau; born of maidservants, heirs, the sons of Jacob. And so in those three fathers the whole figure of the future people was seen: and not undeservedly God said: I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob: this, he says, is my name forever.

Rather let us remember what was promised to Abraham himself: for this was promised to Isaac, this was promised also to Jacob. What do we find? In your seed all the nations will be blessed. One man then believed what he did not yet see: men see, and are blinded. That has been fulfilled in the nations which was promised to one: and they are separated from the communion of the nations, who refuse even to see what has been fulfilled. But what does it profit them that they refuse to see? They see, willing or unwilling; the open truth strikes even closed eyes.

**3.** An answer was given to Nicodemus, who was of those who had believed in Jesus, and Jesus himself did not entrust himself to them. For to certain men he did not entrust himself, although they had already believed in him. So you have it written: Many believed in his name, seeing the signs that he did. *But Jesus himself did not entrust himself to them. For he had no need that anyone should bear witness about a man; for he himself knew what was in a man*. Behold, those men already believed in Jesus, and Jesus himself did not entrust himself to them[^2].

Why? Because they were not yet born again of water and the Spirit. Hence we exhorted, and we exhort, our brothers the catechumens. For if you question them, they have already believed in Jesus: but because they do not yet receive his flesh and blood, Jesus has not yet entrusted himself to them. What should they do, that Jesus may entrust himself to them? Let them be born again of water and the Spirit, let the Church bring forth those whom she labors to bear. They have been conceived, let them be brought forth to the light: they have breasts at which to be nursed, let them not fear lest, once born, they be suffocated, let them not withdraw from the maternal breasts.

**4.** No man can return into his mother's bowels, and be born again. But someone was born of a maidservant? Did those then who were born of maidservants return into the wombs of free women, that they might be born anew? The seed of Abraham was in Ishmael too; and that Abraham might be able to make a son of the maidservant, his wife was the author of it. He was born of the man's seed, and not of her womb, but only of the wife's consent. Was he disinherited because he was of a maidservant? If he were disinherited for the reason that he was born of a maidservant, no sons of maidservants would be admitted to the inheritance. The sons of Jacob were admitted to the inheritance: but Ishmael was disinherited not because he was born of a maidservant, but because he was proud toward his mother, proud against his mother's son; for his mother was rather Sarah than Hagar. Her womb was lent, her will came in; Abraham would not do what Sarah did not wish: rather, then, he was Sarah's son.

But because he was proud toward his brother, and proud in playing, because in mocking; what does Sarah say? Cast out the maidservant and her son; for the son of the maidservant will not be heir with my son Isaac. Therefore it was not the bowels of the maidservant that cast him outside, but the servile neck. And if a free man is proud, he is a servant; and, what is worse, of a bad mistress, of pride itself.

And so, my brothers, answer that a man cannot be born again; answer in security that a man cannot be born again. Whatever is done a second time is a mockery; whatever is done a second time is a sport. Ishmael plays, let him be put outside. For Sarah noticed them playing, the Scripture says, and said to Abraham: Cast out the maidservant and her son. The playing of the boys displeased Sarah; she saw the boys playing at something new. Do not those who have sons wish this, to see their sons playing? She saw it, and disapproved. She saw something or other in the play: she saw a mockery in that play, she noticed the pride of the servant; it displeased her, she cast him outside.

Those born of maidservants, being wicked, are put outside, and one born of a free woman, Esau, is put outside. Let no one, then, presume because he is born of the good, let no one presume because he is baptized through the holy. He who is baptized through the holy, let him still beware lest he be not Jacob but Esau. This, then, brothers, I would say: it is better to be baptized by men who seek their own and love the world, which the name of the maidservant signifies, and to seek spiritually the inheritance of Christ, so as to be like a son of Jacob from a maidservant, than to be baptized through the holy and to be proud, so as to be an Esau to be put outside, though born of a free woman.

These things, brothers, hold fast. We do not stroke you; let no hope of yours be in us: we flatter neither ourselves nor you; each carries his own burden. Ours it is to speak, lest we be judged badly: yours it is to hear, and to hear with the heart, lest what we give be demanded back; rather, when it is demanded, let gain be found, not loss.

## In the spirit we are born by word and sacred sign.

**5.** The Lord says to Nicodemus, and expounds to him: Amen, amen, I say to you, unless one is born again of water and the Spirit, he cannot enter into the kingdom of God. You, he says, understand carnal generation, when you say: Can a man return into his mother's bowels? he must be born of water and the Spirit for the sake of the kingdom of God. If he is born for the sake of the temporal inheritance of a human father, let him be born of the bowels of a carnal mother: if for the sake of the everlasting inheritance of God the Father, let him be born of the bowels of the Church. A father who will die begets through a wife a son who will succeed him: God begets of the Church sons who will not succeed him, but will abide with him.

And it follows: What is born of the flesh is flesh; and what is born of the Spirit is spirit. Spiritually, then, we are born, and in the spirit we are born by word and sacred sign. The Spirit is present, that we may be born: the Spirit is invisibly present from whom you are born, because you too are born invisibly. For it follows, and says: Do not wonder that I said to you, you must be born anew: the Spirit breathes where it wills, and you hear its voice, but you do not know whence it comes, or where it goes. No one sees the Spirit: and how do we hear the voice of the Spirit? A Psalm sounds, it is the voice of the Spirit; the Gospel sounds, it is the voice of the Spirit; the divine word sounds, it is the voice of the Spirit. You hear its voice, and you do not know whence it comes, and where it goes. But if you too are born of the Spirit, you will be such that he who is not yet born of the Spirit does not know about you, whence you come, and where you go. For having said this, he says: So is everyone who is born of the Spirit.

**6.** Nicodemus answered and said to him: How can these things be? And indeed, carnally, he did not understand. In him was happening what the Lord had said, he was hearing the voice of the Spirit, and did not know whence it had come and where it was going. Jesus answered and said to him: You are a teacher in Israel, and you do not know these things? O brothers, what, do we suppose the Lord wished, as it were, to insult this teacher of the Jews? The Lord knew what he was doing, he wished him to be born of the Spirit. No one is born of the Spirit unless he is humble: because humility itself makes us born of the Spirit; for the Lord is near to the contrite of heart. The man was puffed up with his teaching, and seemed to himself to be of some importance, because he was a teacher of the Jews: he lays aside his pride for him, that he may be able to be born of the Spirit: he insults him as though unlearned; not because the Lord wishes to seem superior. What is great in it, God toward a man, the truth toward a lie? Should Christ be called greater than Nicodemus, can he be called so, is it even to be thought? If Christ were called greater than the angels, it would be laughable: for he is incomparably greater than every creature, through whom every creature was made. But he stirs up the pride of the man: You are a teacher in Israel, and you do not know these things? As though saying: Behold, you know nothing, proud ruler; be born of the Spirit: for if you are born of the Spirit, you will keep the ways of God, so as to follow the humility of Christ.

For he is high above all the angels in this way, that though he was in the form of God, he did not count it robbery to be equal to God; but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man: he humbled himself, having become obedient unto death (and lest any kind of death please you), even the death of the cross. He hung, and was insulted. He could have come down from the cross; but he delayed, that he might rise from the tomb. The Lord bore with proud servants; the physician, with the sick. If he did this, what of those who must be born of the Spirit? if he did this, the true teacher in heaven, not of men only, but also of angels. For if the angels are taught, they are taught by the Word of God. If they are taught by the Word of God, seek whence they are taught; and you will find: In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. The man's neck is taken away, but the rough and hard one, that there may be a gentle neck to bear the yoke of Christ, of which it is said: My yoke is gentle, and my burden is light.

## Because one descended, one ascended.

**7.** And it follows: If I have told you earthly things, and you do not believe; how, if I tell you heavenly things, will you believe? What earthly things did he say, brothers? Is "Unless one is born anew" an earthly thing? Is "The Spirit breathes where it wills, and you hear its voice, and you do not know whence it comes, and where it goes" an earthly thing? For if he were speaking of this wind, as some have understood, when it was asked of them what earthly thing the Lord said, when he says: If I have told you earthly things, and you do not believe; how, if I tell you heavenly things, will you believe?

So when it was asked of certain people what earthly thing the Lord said, in their straits they said: What he says, "The Spirit breathes where it wills, and you hear its voice, and you do not know whence it comes, and where it goes," he said of this wind. For what did he name as earthly? He was speaking of spiritual generation: he goes on and says: So is everyone who is born of the Spirit. Then, brothers, which of us does not see, for example, the south wind going from the south to the north; or another wind coming from the east to the west? how, then, do we not know whence it comes and where it goes?

What, then, did he say as earthly, which men did not believe? Was it that which he had said about the raising up of the temple? For he had taken his body from the earth, and was preparing to raise up that very earth, taken from an earthly body. He was not believed when he said he would raise up the earth. If, he says, I have told you earthly things and you do not believe: how, if I tell you heavenly things, will you believe? That is, if you do not believe that I can raise up the temple cast down by you; how will you believe that through the Spirit men can be regenerated?

**8.** And it follows: And no one has ascended into heaven, except him who descended from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven. Behold, he was here, and was in heaven: he was here in the flesh, in heaven in his divinity; nay, everywhere in his divinity. Born of a mother, not withdrawing from the Father. Two nativities of Christ are understood; one divine, the other human: one through which we were made, the other through which we were remade: both marvelous; the one without a mother, the other without a father. But because he had taken a body from Adam, because Mary was from Adam, and he was to raise up that very body: he had said something earthly: Destroy this temple, and in three days I will raise it up. But he said something heavenly: Unless one is born again of water and the Spirit, he will not see the kingdom of God.

Come now, brothers, God willed to be the Son of man, and willed men to be sons of God. He himself descended for our sake; let us ascend for his sake. For he alone descended and ascended, who says this: No one has ascended into heaven, except him who descended from heaven. Will those then not ascend into heaven whom he makes sons of God? They will surely ascend: this is the promise to us: They will be equal to the angels of God. How, then, does no one ascend, except him who descended? Because one descended, one ascended. What of the rest? what is to be understood, but that they will be his members, so that one ascends? Therefore it follows: No one has ascended into heaven, except him who descended from heaven, the Son of man who is in heaven. Do you wonder that he was both here and in heaven? Such did he make his disciples. Hear the apostle Paul saying: But our citizenship is in heaven. If Paul the apostle, a man, walked in the flesh on earth, and had his citizenship in heaven, could not the God of heaven and earth be both in heaven and on earth?

## Woe to those who hate unity.

**9.** If, then, no one but he descended and ascended, what hope is there for the rest? This is the hope for the rest, that he descended for this reason, that in him and with him those might be one who through him were to ascend. He does not say: And to seeds, says the Apostle, as in many; but as in one: And to your seed, which is Christ. And to the faithful he says: But you are Christ's; and if Christ's, then you are the seed of Abraham. What he called one, by this he said that we all are. Therefore in the Psalms sometimes many sing, that it may be shown that of many one is made; sometimes one sings, that it may be shown what is made of many. Therefore one man was healed in that pool; and whoever else went down was not healed. Therefore this one man commends the unity of the Church.

Woe to those who hate unity, and make parties for themselves among men! Let them hear him who wished to make them one in the one toward the One: let them hear him saying: Do not make yourselves many: I planted, Apollos watered; but God gave the increase: but neither is he who plants anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase. They were saying: I am Paul's, I am Apollos's, I am Cephas's. And he: Is Christ divided? Be in one, be one thing, be one: No one has ascended into heaven, except him who descended from heaven. Behold, we wish to be yours, they said to Paul. And he: I do not wish you to be Paul's, but be his whose, together with you, Paul is.

**10.** For he descended and died, and by that very death freed us from death: slain by death, he slew death. And you know, brothers, that this death entered the world through the envy of the devil. God did not make death, Scripture says; nor does he rejoice, it says, in the destruction of the living: for he created all things that they might be. But what does it say there? But through the envy of the devil death entered the world. To the death offered him by the devil, man would not have come led by force: for the devil did not have the power of compelling, but the cunning of persuading. Had you not consented, the devil would have brought in nothing: your consent, O man, led you to death. From a mortal, mortals are born; from immortals, mortals made. From Adam, all men are mortal: but Jesus the Son of God, the Word of God, through whom all things were made, the Only-begotten equal to the Father, was made mortal; because the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.

## Death was swallowed up in the body of Christ.

**11.** Therefore he took on death, and hung death on the cross; and from that very death mortals are freed. What was done in figure among the ancients, the Lord recalls: And as, he says, Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of man be lifted up; that everyone who believes in him may not perish, but have eternal life. A great sacred sign, and those who have read it, know it. But let them hear, either those who have not read it, or those who perhaps have forgotten what they read or heard.

The people of Israel were being laid low in the wilderness by the bites of serpents, a great slaughter of many dead was taking place: for it was a plague of God chastising and lashing, that he might train them. A great sacred sign of a thing to come was shown there—the Lord himself testifies in this reading, so that no one can interpret it otherwise than as the Truth itself indicates about itself. For it was said to Moses by the Lord that he should make a bronze serpent, and lift it up on wood in the wilderness, and admonish the people of Israel, that if anyone were bitten by a serpent, he should look at that serpent lifted up on the wood.

It was done: men were bitten, they looked, and were healed. What are the biting serpents? The sins arising from the mortality of the flesh. What is the serpent lifted up? The death of the Lord on the cross. For because death came from the serpent, it was figured by the likeness of a serpent. The bite of the serpent is deadly, the death of the Lord life-giving. The serpent is looked at, that the serpent may avail nothing. What is this? Death is looked at, that death may avail nothing. But whose death? The death of life: if it can be said: The death of life; nay, because it can be said, it is said marvelously. But shall what was to be done not be said? Shall I hesitate to say what the Lord deigned to do for me? Is not Christ life? and yet Christ on the cross. Is not Christ life? and yet Christ dead. But in the death of Christ, death died; because life dead slew death, the fullness of life swallowed up death: death was swallowed up in the body of Christ.

So we too will say in the resurrection, when, now triumphing, we will sing: Where, O death, is your strife? where, O death, is your sting? Meanwhile now, brothers, that we may be healed of sin, let us look upon Christ crucified; because as Moses, he says, lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of man be lifted up; that everyone who believes in him may not perish, but have eternal life. As those who looked upon that serpent did not perish by the bites of the serpents; so those who look by faith upon the death of Christ are healed of the bites of sins. But those were healed from death unto temporal life: but here he says, that they may have eternal life. For this is the difference between the figured image and the reality itself: the figure bestowed temporal life; the reality itself, of which that was the figure, bestows eternal life.

**12.** For God did not send his Son into the world to judge the world, but that the world might be saved through him. Therefore, so far as it is in the physician, he came to heal the sick. He destroys himself who refuses to observe the precepts of the physician. The Savior came to the world: why was he called Savior of the world, but that he might save the world, not judge the world? You do not wish to be saved by him; from yourself you will be judged. And why do I say, you will be judged? See what he says: He who believes in him is not judged; but he who does not believe: what do you expect him to say but, is judged? already, he says, he has been judged. The judgment has not yet appeared, but the judgment is already done. For the Lord knows who are his: he knows who will remain unto the crown, who will remain unto the flame; he knows the wheat on his threshing floor, he knows the chaff; he knows the crop, he knows the weeds. Already he has been judged who does not believe. Why judged? Because he has not believed in the name of the only-begotten Son of God. Blot out what you made, that God may save what he made.

**13.** But this is the judgment, that the light came into the world, and men loved darkness more than the light: for their works were evil. My brothers, whose works did the Lord find good? No one's: he found everyone's works evil. How, then, did certain men do the truth, and come to the light? For this too follows: But he who does the truth comes to the light, that his works may be made manifest, because they are done in God. How did certain men do a good work, that they might come to the light, that is, to Christ? and how did certain men love darkness? For if he found all sinners, and heals all from sin, and that serpent in which the death of the Lord was figured heals those who had been bitten, and on account of the bite of the serpent the serpent was raised up, that is, the death of the Lord, on account of mortal men, whom he found unjust; how is it understood: This is the judgment, that the light came into the world, and men loved darkness more than the light: for their works were evil?

What is this? for whose were the good works? Did you not come to justify the impious? But they loved, he says, darkness more than the light. There he placed the force: for many loved their sins, many confessed their sins: because he who confesses his sins, and accuses his sins, already acts with God. God accuses your sins: if you too accuse them, you are joined to God. There are, as it were, two things: man and sinner. What you hear, man, God made: what you hear, sinner, man himself made. Blot out what you made, that God may save what he made.

You must hate in yourself your own work, and love in yourself the work of God. But when what you made has begun to displease you, from there your good works begin, because you accuse your evil works. The beginning of good works is the confession of evil works. You do the truth, and come to the light. What is: You do the truth? You do not stroke yourself, you do not flatter yourself, you do not fawn on yourself; you do not say: I am just, when you are unjust, and you begin to do the truth. But you come to the light that your works may be made manifest, because they are done in God; because this very thing, that your sin displeased you, would not displease you, unless God shone upon you, and his truth showed it to you.

But he who, even when admonished, loves his sins, hates the admonishing light and flees it, that the evil works he loves may not be reproved. But he who does the truth accuses his evil things in himself; he does not spare himself, he does not pardon himself, that God may pardon: because what he wishes God to pardon, he himself acknowledges, and comes to the light; to whom he gives thanks, because he has shown him what to hate in himself. He says to God: Turn your face from my sins: and with what countenance does he say it, unless he say again: For I acknowledge my misdeed, and my sin is ever before me. Let that be before you which you do not wish to be before God. But if you put your sin behind you, God will twist it back to you before your eyes; and he twists it back then, when there will be no fruit of repentance.

**14.** Run, lest the darkness overtake you, my brothers: awake to your salvation, awake while it is time; let no one be held back from the temple of God, let no one be held back from the work of the Lord, let no one be called away from continual prayer, let no one be defrauded of his customary devotion. Awake, then, while it is day: the day shines, Christ is the day. He is ready to pardon, but those who acknowledge; but to punish those who defend themselves, and boast themselves just, and think themselves to be something, when they are nothing. But he who walks in his love and in his mercy, even though freed from those deadly and grievous sins, such as misdeeds, murders, thefts, adulteries; on account of those that seem to be small sins of the tongue, or of thoughts, or of immoderation in things permitted, does the truth of confession, and comes to the light in good works: since the small sins, if neglected, when many, slay.

Small are the drops that fill the rivers: small are the grains of sand; but if much sand be laid on, it presses and crushes. What a neglected bilge does is what an inrushing wave does: little by little it comes in through the bilge; but by coming in for a long time and not being bailed out, it sinks the ship. But what is it to bail out, but by good works to act so that sins do not overwhelm—by groaning, fasting, giving, pardoning?

But the journey of this age is troublesome, it is full of temptations; in prosperity, lest it puff up; in adversity, lest it break. He who gave you the happiness of this age, gave it for your consolation, not for your corruption. Again, he who lashes you in this age does it for amendment, not for condemnation. Bear the father training you, lest you feel the judge punishing you. These things we say to you daily, and they are often to be said, because they are good and saving.
