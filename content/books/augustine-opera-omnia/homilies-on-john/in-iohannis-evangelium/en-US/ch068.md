# Tractate 68

## *I go to prepare a place for you* (Jn 14:2-4).

## So, Lord, you prepare us also for yourself, and prepare yourself for us, since you prepare a place both for yourself in us, and for us in you. For you said: *Abide in me, and I in you. — *

**1.** We acknowledge, dearly beloved brothers, that we owe you, and must now repay, what we had deferred: how it can be understood that these two things are not contrary to each other—that, when the Lord had said: *In my Father's house are many mansions: if it were not so, I would have told you that I go to prepare a place for you*; where he shows clearly enough that he said this to them because there are already there many mansions, and there is no need to prepare any: he again says: *And if I go, and prepare a place for you; I come again, and will receive you to myself, that where I am you too may be.* How does he go, and prepare a place, if there are already many mansions? If it were not so, he would have said: *I go to prepare.* Or if it is yet to be prepared, why would he not rightly have said: *I go to prepare*? Or are these mansions both already in existence, and yet to be prepared? For if they were not, he would have said: *I go to prepare.* And yet, because they are in such a way that they are yet to be prepared, he does not go to prepare them as they are: but if he goes and prepares them as they will be, coming again he will receive his own to himself, that where he is, they too may be.

How, then, are the mansions in the Father's house not other, but the very same, and without doubt both already in existence, in that they are not to be prepared, and not yet in existence, in that they are to be prepared? How do we think, except as the prophet too proclaims God, that he made the things that are to come? For he does not say: Who is going to make the things that are to come, but: *Who made the things that are to come*[^1]. Therefore he both made them, and is going to make them. For neither are they made, if he did not make them: nor are they to come, if he will not make them. He made them, then, by predestinating, he is going to make them by working. As the Gospel shows clearly enough when he chose the disciples; surely then, when he called them: and yet the Apostle says: *He chose us before the foundation of the world*[^2]: by predestinating, surely, not by calling. *But those whom he predestined, them also he called*[^3]: he chose by predestinating before the foundation of the world, he chose by calling before the consummation of the world. So too he both prepared the mansions and prepares them; nor others, but those which he prepared, these he prepares, who made the things that are to come: those which he prepared by predestinating, he prepares by working. Already, then, they are in predestination: if it were not so, he would have said: I will go and prepare, that is, predestinate. But because they are not yet in operation: *And if I go,* he says, *and prepare a place for you; I come again, and will receive you to myself.*

## Now it is already called a kingdom, but is still being called together.

**2.** He prepares the mansions in a certain way by preparing for the mansions those who will dwell in them. For when he said: *In my Father's house are many mansions*; what do we think the house of God is, but the temple of God? But what that is, let the Apostle be asked, and let him answer: *For the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^4]. This is also the kingdom of God, which the Son will hand over to the Father: whence the same apostle says: *The first-fruits Christ, then those who are Christ's at his coming: then the end, when he will have handed over the kingdom to God and the Father*[^5]; that is, those whom he redeemed by his blood, he will have handed over to his Father, even for contemplation. This is the kingdom of heaven, of which it is said: *The kingdom of heaven is like a man sowing good seed in his field. But the good seed, these are the children of the kingdom*[^6]: who, although they now have tares mingled with them, the King himself will at the end send his angels, *and they will gather out of his kingdom all scandals. Then the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father*[^7]. The kingdom will shine in the kingdom, when to the kingdom the kingdom will have come, which we now pray for, and say: *Your kingdom come*[^8].

Now, then, it is already called a kingdom, but is still being called together: for if it were not called a kingdom, it would not be said: *They will gather out of his kingdom all scandals.* But this kingdom does not yet reign. Accordingly, it is already a kingdom in such a way that, when all scandals will have been gathered out of it, it will then come to the kingdom; so that it may have not only the name of a kingdom, but also the power of reigning. For to this kingdom standing at the right hand it will be said at the end: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*[^9]: that is, you who were the kingdom and did not reign, come, reign; that what you were in hope, you may also be able to be in reality. This house of God, then, this temple of God, this kingdom of God, and kingdom of heaven, is still being built, still being fashioned, still being prepared, still being gathered. In it there will be mansions, as the Lord still prepares them: in it they already are, as the Lord has already predestined them.

**3.** But what is it that he went away in order to prepare, when surely he was going to prepare us ourselves, which he will not do if he leaves us? I acknowledge, Lord, as I can: surely you signify this, that, in order that these mansions may be prepared, the righteous man must live by faith. For he who is a pilgrim from the Lord has need to live by faith; because through this he is prepared for contemplating the form. *For blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^10]: and: *By faith he cleanses their hearts*[^11]. The former is read in the Gospel, the latter in the Acts of the Apostles.

But the faith by which the hearts of those who are to see God are cleansed, while they are pilgrims, believes what it does not see: for if you see, it is not faith. To the believer merit is gathered, to the seer the reward is repaid. Let the Lord, then, go and prepare a place; let him go that he may not be seen, let him be hidden that he may be believed. For then a place is prepared, if one lives by faith. Let him who is believed be desired, that, being desired, he may be possessed: the desire of love is the preparation of the mansion.

So, Lord, prepare what you prepare: for you prepare us for yourself, and prepare yourself for us; since you prepare a place both for yourself in us, and for us in you. For you said: *Abide in me, and I in you*[^12]. As much as each one will have been a partaker of you, one less, another more, this will be the diversity of rewards according to the diversity of merits: this will be the multitude of mansions according to the disparity of the dwellers; but yet of all who live in eternity, and are blessed without end. What is it that you go? what is it that you come? If I understand you well, you neither withdraw from where you go, nor from where you come: you go by being hidden, you come by appearing. But unless you abide by ruling, that we may advance by living well; how will the place be prepared where we may abide by full enjoyment?

Let these things, of the words of the Gospel that were read, be enough to have been said, up to where the Lord says: *I come again, and will receive you to myself.* But what follows: *That where I am, you too may be. And where I go you know, and the way you know*; after the question that follows, made by the disciple, as though through him we too should ask, we shall hear better and treat more opportunely.
