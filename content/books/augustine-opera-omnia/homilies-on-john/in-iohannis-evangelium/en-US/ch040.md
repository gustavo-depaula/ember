# Tractate 40

## *And you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free*[^1].

What does the Lord promise to those who believe? *And you will know the truth.* For we believe in order to know, we do not know in order to believe. *If you remain in my word, you will truly be my disciples*—that we may contemplate the truth itself as it is; not through resounding words, but through shining light, when it satisfies us.

**1.** Of the holy Gospel according to John, which you see us carrying in our hands, your Charity has already heard many things, which, as God granted and as we were able, we have discussed, commending to you above all that this evangelist chose to speak of the Lord's divinity, according to which the only Son of God is equal to the Father, and that for this reason he is compared to the eagle; for no bird is said to fly higher. Accordingly, listen most attentively to what follows in order, as the Lord shall grant us to treat it.  
That exaltation was a humiliation.

**2.** We spoke to you about the preceding reading, suggesting how the Father is to be understood as true, the Son as truth. But when the Lord Jesus had said, *He who sent me is true*[^2], the Jews did not understand that he was speaking to them about the Father. And he said to them what you heard just now as it was read: *When you have exalted the Son of man, then you will know that I am, and that of myself I do nothing, but as the Father taught me, these things I speak*[^3]. What is this? For he seems to have said nothing else than that after his passion they would know who he was. Beyond doubt, then, he saw there certain ones whom he himself knew, whom he himself, with the rest of his saints, had chosen by foreknowledge before the foundation of the world, who after his passion would believe.

These are the very ones whom we constantly commend, and whom with great exhortation we set forth for imitation. For when the Holy Spirit had been sent from above after the Lord's passion and resurrection and ascension, and when miracles were being worked in the name of him whom the Jews, persecuting him as one dead, had despised, they were stung in heart; and those who in their rage had killed him, being changed, believed; and the blood which in their rage they had shed, in believing they drank: those three thousand, and those five thousand of the Jews[^4], whom he saw there when he said, *When you have exalted the Son of man, then you will know that I am.* As though saying: I defer your knowing, that I may fulfill my passion; in your own order you will know who I am.

Not that all who were hearing were then going to believe, that is, after the Lord's passion; for a little later he says, *While he was saying these things, many believed in him*[^5], and the Son of man had not yet been exalted. For he speaks of the exaltation of the passion, not of the glorification; of the cross, not of heaven; because there too he was exalted, when he hung upon the wood. But that exaltation was a humiliation. For then *he became obedient unto death, even the death of the cross*[^6]. This had to be fulfilled by the hands of those who were afterward going to believe, to whom he says, *When you have exalted the Son of man, then you will know that I am.* Why this, except that no one might despair, however conscious of evil in himself in any crime whatever, when he saw murder forgiven to those who had killed Christ?

**3.** Recognizing these, then, in that crowd, the Lord said: *When you have exalted the Son of man, then you will know that I am.* You already know what I am; nor must it be repeated constantly, lest so great a matter breed weariness. Recall that saying: *I am who am*, and *He who is sent me*[^7]; and you will recognize what was said: *Then you will know that I am*—but the Father also is, and the Holy Spirit is. To this very being belongs the whole Trinity.

But because the Lord was speaking as the Son, lest perhaps in his saying, *Then you will know that I am*, the error of the Sabellians—that is, the Patripassians—should creep in, which error I have commended to you as one not to be held but to be guarded against, namely, of those who said: he himself is the Father, he himself is the Son; there are two names, but one reality—for the sake, then, of guarding against that error, when the Lord had said, *Then you will know that I am*, lest he himself be understood as the Father, he at once added, *And of myself I do nothing, but as the Father taught me, these things I speak.* The Sabellian had already begun to rejoice, having found occasion for his error; but as soon as he had lifted himself up, as it were, in the dark, he was confounded by the light of the following statement. You had thought him to be the Father, because he said, *I am*; hear that he is the Son: *And of myself I do nothing.* What is, *Of myself I do nothing?* Of myself I am not. For the Son is God from the Father; but the Father is not God from the Son: the Son is God from God; but the Father is God, yet not from God: the Son is light from light; but the Father is light, yet not from light: the Son is, but is from one from whom he is; the Father is, but is not from one from whom he is.

**4.** As to what he added, *As the Father taught me, these things I speak*—let no carnal thought steal upon any of you, my brothers. For human weakness cannot think except what it is accustomed to do or to hear. Do not, then, set before your eyes, as it were, two men, the one a father, the other a son, and the father speaking to the son, as you do when you say some words to your son, admonishing him and instructing him how to speak, so that whatever he has heard from you he may commit to memory; and when he has committed it to memory, may bring it forth even with his tongue, distinguish it by sounds, and convey to the ears of others what he received in his own. Do not think thus, lest you fashion idols in your heart.

A human form, the lineaments of human limbs, the figure of human flesh, these conspicuous senses, the stature and movements of a body, the office of the tongue, the distinctions of sounds—do not think of these in that Trinity, except what pertains to the form of a slave, which the only-begotten Son took on when *the Word was made flesh, that he might dwell among us*[^8]. There I do not forbid you, human weakness, to think what you know; rather, I even compel you. If the faith in you is true, think of such a Christ: but think of him as such from the Virgin Mary, not from God the Father. He was an infant, he grew up as a man, he walked as a man, he hungered, he thirsted as a man, he slept as a man, finally he suffered as a man, was hung upon the wood, killed, buried as a man; in the same form he rose again, in the same form before the eyes of his disciples he ascended into heaven, in the same form he is to come to judgment. For the voice of the angels is set down in the Gospel: *He will come in the same way as you saw him going into heaven*[^9].

When, therefore, you think of the form of a slave in Christ, think of a human likeness, if faith is in you; but when you think, *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^10], let all human shaping perish from your heart; let there be driven from your thoughts whatever is bounded by a bodily limit, whatever is contained within the space of a place, or is spread out in whatever mass; let such a figment utterly perish from your heart. Think, if you can, of the beauty of wisdom; let the beauty of justice come before you. Is it a shape? Is it a stature? Is it a color? It is none of these, and yet it is; for if it were not, it would neither be loved, nor rightly praised, nor, being loved and praised, held in heart and conduct. But as it is, men become wise; whence would they become so, if there were no wisdom? Furthermore, O man, if you cannot see your own wisdom with the eyes of flesh, nor think of it with such imagination as bodily things are thought of, do you dare to thrust the form of a human body upon the wisdom of God?

## Understanding is the hand of God.

**5.** What, then, do we say, brothers? How did the Father speak to the Son, since the Son says, *As the Father taught me, these things I speak?* Did he speak to him? When the Father taught the Son, did he make words, as you do when you teach your son, you make words? How does he make words to the Word? What words would be made many to the one Word? Did the Word of the Father have ears for the mouth of the Father? These are carnal things; let them perish from your hearts.

For I say this: see, if you have understood what I said, I certainly have spoken, and my words have sounded, and have struck the ears with sounds, and through the sense of your hearing have carried the meaning to the heart, if you have understood. Suppose someone who knows the Latin tongue has heard, has only heard, and yet has not understood what I said: as far as concerns the noise sent forth from my mouth, the one who did not understand became a partaker of it just as you did; he heard the sound, the same syllables struck his ears; but in his heart they begot nothing. Why? Because he did not understand. But you, if you have understood—whence have you understood? I sounded at the ear; have I kindled a light in the heart?

Beyond doubt, if what I said is true, and you have not only heard this truth but have also understood it, two things have been done there; distinguish them: hearing and understanding. The hearing was done through me; the understanding through whom? I spoke at the ear that you might hear; who spoke to your heart that you might understand? Without doubt someone spoke something to your heart as well, so that not only did this noise of words strike your ear, but also something of truth came down into your heart: someone spoke to your heart as well, but you do not see him; if you have understood, brothers, it was spoken to your heart as well. Understanding is the gift of God. Who said this in your heart, if you have understood? He to whom the Psalm says: *Give me understanding, that I may learn your commandments*[^11].

For example, the bishop has spoken.

What has he spoken? someone says.

You answer what he has spoken, and you add: He spoke truly.

Then the other, who did not understand, says: What did he say, or what is it that you praise? Both heard me, to both I spoke; but to one of them God spoke. If small things may be compared with great—since what are we toward him?—

yet God does in us something incorporeal and spiritual, which is neither a sound that strikes the ear, nor a color that is discerned by the eyes, nor an odor that is caught by the nostrils, nor a flavor that is judged by the palate, nor anything hard or soft that is felt by touching; yet there is something which it is easy to perceive, impossible to unfold. If, then, God, as I had begun to say, speaks in our hearts without sound, how does he speak to his Son? Thus, then, brothers, thus consider, as far as you can, as I said—if small things may be compared with great in some way—thus consider: incorporeally the Father spoke to the Son, because incorporeally the Father begot the Son.

Nor did he teach him as though he had begotten him untaught; rather, to have taught him is the same as to have begotten him knowing; and this is, *The Father taught me*—that is, the Father begot me knowing. For if, as few understand, the nature of truth is simple, then for the Son to be is the same as to know. From him, therefore, he has it that he knows, from whom he has it that he is: not that he first existed from him and afterward knew from him; but just as the Father by begetting gave him to be, so by begetting he gave him to know; because, as has been said, to the simple nature of truth to be and to know are not one thing and another, but this same thing.

**6.** He said these things, then, to the Jews, and added: *And he who sent me is with me*[^12]. He had already said this before, but he constantly recalls a great matter: he sent me, and is with me. If, then, he is with you, O Lord, the one was not sent by the other, but both of you came.

And yet, though both are together, the one was sent, the other sent; for the sending is the incarnation, and the incarnation itself is of the Son only, not also of the Father. So the Father sent the Son, but did not depart from the Son. For it is not the case that, where he sent the Son, the Father was not there. For where is he not who made all things? Where is he not who said, *I fill heaven and earth*[^13]? But perhaps the Father is everywhere, and the Son is not everywhere? Hear the evangelist: *He was in this world, and the world was made through him*[^14].

Therefore, he says, he who sent me, by whose authority, as of a father, I was made incarnate, is with me; he has not left me. Why has he not left me? *He has not left me alone, because I always do the things that are pleasing to him*[^15]. That equality is always, not from some beginning and thereafter, but without beginning, without end. For the generation of God has no beginning in time, since through the begotten one times were made.

**7.** *While he was saying these things, many believed in him*[^16]. Would that, while I too am speaking, many who thought otherwise might understand and believe in him. For perhaps there are some Arians in this multitude. I do not dare suspect that there are Sabellians, who say that the Father himself is the one who is the Son; for that heresy is too ancient, and has been gradually disemboweled. But that of the Arians still seems to have some twitchings, as of a putrefying corpse; or at most, as of a man giving up the soul: the rest must be set free from it, as many have been set free. And indeed this city did not have them; but after many foreigners came, some of these too came. See, while the Lord was speaking, many Jews believed in him; see, let the Arians too, while I am speaking, believe—not in me, but with me.

## Great is what begins from faith.

**8.** The Lord, then, was saying to the Jews who had believed in him: *If you remain in my word*[^17]. For this reason, if you remain: because you have been initiated, because there too you have begun to be. *If you remain*—that is, in the faith which has begun to be in you who believe—where will you arrive? See what kind of beginning, and to what it leads. You have loved the foundation; attend to the summit, and from this lowliness seek another loftiness. For faith has lowliness; knowledge and immortality and eternity do not have lowliness, but loftiness; an uplifting, no falling away, an eternal stability, no being stormed by the enemy, no fear of failing.

Great is what begins from faith; but it is despised. The foundation too is wont to be despised by the unskilled even in a building. A great trench is made, the stones are thrown in anyhow, here and there; no polish, no beauty appears there: just as in the root of a tree no beauty appears; yet the whole of whatever delights you in the tree has risen up from the root. But you see the root and are not delighted; you see the tree and you marvel. Fool, what you marvel at has risen up from what does not delight you.

The faith of believers seems some small thing; you have no scale on which to weigh it. Hear, then, where it arrives, and see how great it is: as the Lord himself says in another place, *If you have faith like a grain of mustard seed*[^18]. What more lowly, what more powerful? What more minute, what more fervent? Therefore you also, he says, if you remain in my word, in which you have believed—where will you be led? *You will truly be my disciples.* And what does it profit us? *And you will know the truth*[^19].

**9.** What does he promise those who believe, brothers? *And you will know the truth.* What then? Had they not known it, when the Lord was speaking? If they had not known it, how did they believe? They did not believe because they knew; they believed in order to know. For we believe in order to know, we do not know in order to believe. For what we are going to know, *eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man*[^20]. For what is faith, except to believe what you do not see?

Faith, then, is to believe what you do not see; truth is to see what you have believed—as he himself says in a certain place. Therefore the Lord first, to bring faith about, walked on earth. He was a man, he had been made lowly; he was seen by all, yet not known by all; by many he was rejected, by the crowd he was killed, by a few he was grieved for: but yet even by those by whom he was grieved for he was not yet recognized as he was. All this is, as it were, the beginning of the lineaments of faith and of the structure to come. Attending to which, the Lord himself says in a certain place: *He who loves me keeps my commandments; and he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him, and will show myself to him*[^21]. Those who were hearing him surely already saw him; yet to them, if they loved, he promised that he would be seen.

So here too: *You will know the truth.*

What then? Is what you have said not the truth?

It is the truth, but as yet it is believed, not yet seen. If one remains in what is believed, one arrives at what is seen. Hence John himself, the holy evangelist, in his Epistle: *Beloved,* he says, *we are the children of God, but it has not yet appeared what we shall be. We are already, and we shall be something. What more shall we be than we are? Hear: It has not yet appeared what we shall be: we know that when he appears, we shall be like him*.

Why?

*Because we shall see him as he is*[^22].

A great promise; but it is the wage of faith. You ask for a wage; let the work go first. If you believe, demand the wage of faith; but if you do not believe, with what face do you ask the wage of faith? *If, therefore, you remain in my word, you will truly be my disciples*—that you may contemplate the truth itself as it is; not through resounding words, but through shining light, when it satisfies us, which is read in the Psalm: *The light of your countenance has been sealed upon us, O Lord*[^23]. We are God's coin; we have strayed as a coin from the treasury. By straying, what had been stamped on us has been worn away; he has come who would reform it, for he himself had formed it: he too seeks his own coin, as Caesar his own coin; therefore he says, *Render to Caesar the things that are Caesar's, and to God the things that are God's*[^24]: to Caesar the coins, to God yourselves. Then, therefore, the truth will be stamped in us.

## Love with me.

**10.** What shall I say to your Charity? Oh, if only there were a heart sighing in any way at all for that ineffable glory! Oh, if only we felt our pilgrimage in groaning, and did not love the world, and with a devout mind knocked perpetually at the door of him who called us! Desire is the bosom of the heart; we will receive, if we stretch our desire as far as we can. This is what the divine Scripture does with us, this the congregation of peoples, this the celebration of the sacraments, this holy baptism, this the songs of God's praise, this our very discussion: that this desire may not only be sown and sprout, but may even be increased to so great a capacity that it is fit to take in what eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has entered into the heart of man.

But love with me. He who loves God does not love money much. And I have touched on weakness; I have not dared to say: he does not love money; but, he does not love money much—as though money were to be loved, but not much. Oh, if we loved God worthily, we would not love money at all! Money will be for you an instrument of pilgrimage, not an incitement of greed; something to use for necessity, not to enjoy for delight. Love God, if he has done something in you which you hear and praise. Use the world, let not the world take hold of you. Where you have entered, you are making a journey; you have come to go out, not to remain: you are making a journey; this life is an inn. Use money as a traveler in an inn uses the table, the cup, the pitcher, the bed—meaning to leave them, not to stay forever.

If you are such, lift up the heart, you who can, and hear me: if you are such, you will come to his promises. For it is not too much for you, because great is the hand of him who called you. He called, let him be invoked; let it be said to him: You called us, we invoke you; behold, we have heard you calling, hear us invoking: lead us through where you have promised, perfect what you have begun; do not desert your gifts, do not desert your field, let your shoots enter into the barn. Temptations abound in the world; but greater is he who made the world: temptations abound; but he does not fail who places his hope in him in whom there is no failing.

**11.** To this I have exhorted these things, brothers, because the freedom of which our Lord Jesus Christ speaks does not belong to this time. See what he added: *You will truly be my disciples, and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free*[^25]. What is, *it will set you free?* It will make you free. Then the carnal Jews, judging according to the flesh—not those who had believed, but those in that crowd who did not believe—thought an injury had been done them, because he said to them: *The truth will set you free.* They were indignant that they were signified to be slaves. And truly they were slaves: and he expounds to them what slavery is, and what the freedom to come is which he himself promises. But about this freedom and that slavery it is too long to discuss today.
