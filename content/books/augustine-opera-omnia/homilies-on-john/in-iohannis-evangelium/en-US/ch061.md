# Tractate 61

## *Amen I say to you, that one of you will betray me* (Jn 13:21-27).

Perhaps the Lord deigned by his being troubled to signify this also to us: namely, that false brothers, and those tares of the Lord's field, must so be borne with among the wheat until the time of harvest, that when an urgent cause compels some of them to be separated even before the harvest, it cannot be done without disturbance of the Church.

**1.** This chapter of the Gospel, brothers, is proposed to us to be expounded in this reading in such a way that we ought now to say something also about the betrayer of the Lord, expressed clearly enough by the bread dipped and handed to him. And concerning him indeed—that, being now about to point him out, Jesus was troubled in spirit—I discoursed in the previous sermon: but perhaps what I did not say there, the Lord deigned by his being troubled to signify this also to us: namely, that false brothers, and those tares of the Lord's field, must so be borne with among the wheat until the time of harvest, that when an urgent cause compels some of them to be separated even before the harvest, it cannot be done without disturbance of the Church. Foretelling in a certain way this disturbance of his holy ones, to come through schismatics and heretics, the Lord prefigured it in himself, when, Judas the evil man being about to go out, and to leave by a most open separation the mixture of the wheat in which he had long been tolerated, he was troubled not in flesh, but in spirit. For his spiritual ones are troubled in scandals of this kind not by perversity, but by love; lest perhaps, in the separation of some tares, some wheat too be rooted up along with them[^1].

**2.** *Jesus,* then, *was troubled in spirit, and testified, and said: Amen, amen I say to you, that one of you will betray me.* One of you in number, not in merit; in appearance, not in virtue; by bodily mingling, not by spiritual bond; by union of flesh, not a companion by unity of heart: accordingly, not one who is of you, but one who is about to go out from you. For how will it be true that the Lord testified, and said: *One of you*; if it is true what the same one says in his Epistle, whose Gospel this is: *They went out from us, but they were not of us: for if they had been of us, they would surely have remained with us*[^2]? Judas, then, was not of them: for he would have remained with them, if he had been of them. What, then, is *One of you will betray me*; but, one of you is about to go out, who will betray me? For he too who says: *If they had been of us, they would surely have remained with us*; had already said: *They went out from us.* And through this both are true, both *of us* and *not of us*: in one respect *of us,* in another *not of us*; according to the communion of the sacraments *of us,* according to the property of their own crimes *not of us.*

**3.** *The disciples therefore looked at one another, doubting of whom he spoke*[^3]. For there was in them such devout love toward their Master, that nevertheless human weakness made them anxious, one about another. The conscience of each was indeed known to himself; yet, because it was unknown to his neighbor, each one was so certain of himself that they were singly uncertain about the rest, and the rest about each one.

## A writer of deeds done, not a herald of himself.

**4.** *Now there was reclining one of his disciples in the bosom of Jesus, whom Jesus loved*[^4]. What he meant by *in the bosom,* he says a little after, where he says, *upon the breast of Jesus.* It is John himself, whose Gospel this is, as he afterward makes plain. For this was the custom of those who ministered the sacred Writings to us, that when divine history was being narrated by any of them, when he came to himself, he spoke as of another; and so inserted himself into the order of his narration, as a writer of deeds done, not as a herald of himself. For holy Matthew too did this, who, when in the text of his narration he had come to himself: *He saw,* he says, *a certain tax collector sitting at the tax office, named Matthew, and said to him: Follow me*[^5]: he did not say: He saw me, and said to me. This too blessed Moses did, who narrated all things of himself as of another, and said: *The Lord said to Moses.* More unusually, the apostle Paul did this not in a history, where the narration of deeds done is undertaken to be set forth, but in a Letter. For surely of himself he says: *I know a man in Christ fourteen years ago (whether in the body or out of the body, I do not know; God knows), such a one caught up to the third heaven*[^6]. Wherefore, that here too the blessed Evangelist did not say: I was reclining in the bosom of Jesus, but said: *There was reclining one of his disciples*; let us recognize the custom of our authors rather than wonder at it. For what is lost to the truth, when both the thing itself is told, and by a certain manner of telling boasting is avoided? For he was narrating that which pertained to his greatest praise.

**5.** But what is *whom Jesus loved*? As though he did not love the others, of whom the same John says above: *He loved them to the end*. And the Lord himself: *Greater love than this no one has, than that one lay down his life for his friends*[^7]. And who could enumerate all the testimonies of the divine pages, by which the Lord Jesus is shown to be a lover, not of him only nor of those who then were, but also of his members who would be afterward, and of his whole Church? But assuredly something lies hidden here, and pertains to the bosom in which he was reclining who said these things. For by the bosom what else is signified but a secret? But there is another more fitting place, where the Lord may grant us to say something of this secret, so far as may suffice.

## The breast of Jesus, the secret of wisdom.

**6.** *Simon Peter therefore beckoned to him, and says to him*[^8]. The expression is to be noted, that something is said not by sounding, but only by beckoning: *he beckoned,* he says, *and says*; surely he says by beckoning. For if something is said by thinking, as Scripture speaks: *They said within themselves*; how much more by beckoning, where now what had been conceived in the heart is brought forth by signs of whatever kind? What, then, did he say by beckoning? What, but what follows? *Who is it of whom he speaks?* These words Peter beckoned; for he said them not by the sound of the voice, but by the motion of the body. *And so, when that disciple had reclined upon the breast of Jesus.* This surely is the bosom of the breast, the secret of wisdom. *He says to him: Lord, who is it? Jesus answered: He it is for whom I shall dip the bread and hand it. And when he had dipped the bread, he gave it to Judas, the son of Simon Iscariot. And after the bread, then Satan entered into him*[^9]. The betrayer was pointed out, the lurking places of darkness were laid bare. It is a good thing that he received, but he received it to his own harm, because the evil man received the good thing in an evil way.

But concerning that dipped bread which was handed to the feigner, and concerning the things that follow, many things are to be said: for which more time is needed than we now have, already at the end of this sermon.
