# Tractate 82

## *Abide in my love*[^1].

## See whence our good works come. For whence should they come to us, unless because faith works through love? And whence should we love, unless we were first loved? — We are God's handiwork, created in Christ Jesus.

**1.** The Savior, more and more commending to the disciples by his speech the grace by which we are saved, says: *In this my Father is glorified, that you bear very much fruit and become my disciples*[^2]. Whether one says *glorified* or *made glorious,* both are translated from a single Greek word; for what is called "doxa" (δόξα) in Greek is in Latin "glory." This I thought ought to be mentioned, because the Apostle says: *If Abraham was justified by works, he has glory, but not before God*[^3]. This is glory before God, by which not man but God is glorified, if a man is justified not by works but by faith, so that even his good working is from God; since the branch, as I have already said above, cannot bear fruit of itself. For if in this God the Father is glorified, that we bear very much fruit and become Christ's disciples, let us not attribute this to our own glory, as if we had it of ourselves. For this is his grace, and therefore in this the glory is not ours but his. Whence elsewhere too, when he had said, *So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works*, lest they should think their good works were of themselves, he soon added: *And glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^4]. For in this the Father is glorified, that we bear very much fruit and become Christ's disciples. By whom are we made such, except by him whose mercy goes before us? *For we are his handiwork, created in Christ Jesus in good works*[^5].

**2.** *As the Father has loved me,* he says, *I also have loved you: abide in my love*[^6]. See whence our good works come. For whence should they come to us, unless because faith works through love? And whence should we love, unless we were first loved? This same evangelist said it most plainly in his epistle: *Let us love God, because he first loved us*[^7]. But when he says, *As the Father has loved me, I also have loved you,* he does not show an equality of our nature with his, such as is between the Father and himself, but the grace by which the mediator of God and men is the man Christ Jesus. For the mediator is shown when he says, *The Father me, and I you.* For the Father certainly loves us too, but in him; because in this the Father is glorified, that we bear fruit in the vine, that is, in the Son, and become his disciples.

**3.** *Abide,* he says, *in my love.* How shall we abide? Hear what follows: *If you keep my commandments,* he says, *you will abide in my love*[^8]. Does love make the commandments to be kept, or does the keeping of the commandments make love? But who can doubt that love comes first? For he who does not love has no source from which to keep the commandments. What he says, then — *If you keep my commandments, you will abide in my love* — shows not whence love is generated, but whence it is shown. As if he said: Do not think that you abide in my love if you do not keep my commandments; for if you keep them, you will abide. That is, from this it will appear that you will abide in my love, if you keep my commandments. So that no one may deceive himself by saying that he loves him if he does not keep his commandments. For we love him just so far as we keep his commandments; and just so far as we keep them less, we love less.

Although when he says, *Abide in my love,* it does not appear which love he meant — whether that by which we love him, or that by which he loves us; but it is discerned from the preceding word. For he had said, *I also have loved you*; and to that word he at once joined, *Abide in my love*[^9]: that love, then, by which he loved us. What is it, then — *Abide in my love* — but, abide in my grace? And what is — *If you keep my commandments, you will abide in my love* — but, from this you will know that you will abide in my love, by which I love you, if you keep my commandments? Not, then, that we first keep his commandments in order that he may love us; but unless he loves us, we cannot keep his commandments. This is the grace that lies open to the humble and lies hidden from the proud.

**4.** But what is it that he adds: *As I also have kept my Father's commandments, and abide in his love*[^10]? Surely here too he wished that love of the Father to be understood by which the Father loves him. For he had said thus: *As the Father has loved me, I also have loved you*; and to these words he joined those: *Abide in my love,* doubtless that by which I have loved you. Therefore what he says also of the Father, *I abide in his love,* is to be taken as that by which the Father has loved him. But is grace here too to be understood, by which the Father loves the Son, as it is grace by which the Son loves us — seeing that we are sons by grace, not by nature, whereas the Only-begotten is so by nature, not by grace? Or is this too in the Son himself to be referred to the man? Yes, indeed. For by saying, *As the Father has loved me, I also have loved you,* he shows the grace of the mediator. But the mediator of God and men is Christ Jesus, not insofar as he is God, but insofar as he is man. And assuredly it is according to that which he is as man that it is read of him: *And Jesus advanced in wisdom and age, and in grace with God and men*[^11]. According to this, then, we can rightly say that, although human nature does not belong to the nature of God, yet human nature belongs by grace to the person of the only-begotten Son of God; and so great a grace that none is greater, none indeed equal. For no merits preceded that taking up of the man, but from that taking up all his merits began. The Son, then, abides in the love by which the Father has loved him, and therefore he kept his commandments. For what is even that man, except that God is the one who takes him up? *For the Word was God*[^12], the Only-begotten coeternal with the one who begets; but that a mediator might be given to us, through ineffable grace the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us[^13].
