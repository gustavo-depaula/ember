# Tractate 47

## *I am the good shepherd*[^1].

You are the sheep of Christ, you have been purchased with the blood of Christ. You recognize your price, which is not given by me, but proclaimed through me. The good shepherd lays down his life for his sheep. He alone did not do this; and yet, if those who did it are his members, the same one alone did it. For he could do it without them; they without him could not.

**1.** You who hear the discourse of our God not only gladly, but also carefully, beyond doubt remember our promise. For the very evangelical reading that was read on the last Lord's Day was read today also: because, detained by certain necessities, we could not discuss all the things we owed to your understanding. Accordingly, the things already said and treated we do not take up again today; lest, by still repeating the same things, we be not at all permitted to arrive at those that have not yet been said.

You now know, in the name of the Lord, who the good shepherd is, and how the good shepherds are his members, and therefore there is one shepherd: you know who the hireling is who is to be borne with; who the wolf, and the thieves, and the robbers to be guarded against; what the sheep are, what the door is by which both the sheep enter and the shepherd; how the doorkeeper is to be understood: you know too that whoever does not enter through the door is a thief and a robber, and comes only to steal, and kill, and destroy. All these things have been said and, as I judge, sufficiently treated.

Today we ought to say, as far as the Lord helps (since the same Jesus Christ our Savior both said that he was the shepherd, and the door, and said that the good shepherd enters through the door), how he himself enters through himself. For if no one is a good shepherd except him who enters through the door, and he himself is preeminently the good shepherd, and he himself is the door; I cannot understand otherwise than that he himself enters through himself to his sheep, and gives them his voice that they may follow him, and that they, going in and going out, find pastures, which is eternal life.

## Recognize your price.

**2.** So I say it quickly. I, seeking to enter to you, that is, to your heart, preach Christ: if I should preach something else, I shall be trying to climb up by another part. Christ, then, is for me the door to you; through Christ I enter, not to your walls, but to your hearts. Through Christ I enter, you have gladly heard Christ in me. Why have you gladly heard Christ in me? Because you are the sheep of Christ, you have been purchased with the blood of Christ. You recognize your price: which is not given by me, but proclaimed through me. For he bought you who poured out his precious blood: precious is his blood, free from sin.

Yet he himself also made precious the blood of his own, for whom he gave the price of his blood: for if he did not make the blood of his own precious, it would not be said: *Precious in the sight of the Lord is the death of his saints*[^2]. So too what he says: *The good shepherd lays down his life for the sheep*[^3], he alone did not do this: and yet, if those who did it are his members, the same one alone did it. For he could do it without them; how could they do it without him, since he himself said: *Without me you can do nothing*[^4]? But that others too did it we show from this, that the apostle John himself, who preached this Gospel which you have heard, said in his Epistle: *As Christ laid down his life for us, so we too ought to lay down our lives for the brothers*[^5]. We ought to, he said; he made us debtors who first displayed it. Therefore in a certain place it is written: *If you sit down to dine at the table of a powerful man, wisely understand what is set before you: and put forth your hand, knowing that you must prepare such things*[^6].

What the table of the powerful man is, you know; there is the body and blood of Christ: he who approaches such a table, let him prepare such things. And what is, let him prepare such things? As he himself laid down his life for us, so we too ought, for the building up of the people and the assertion of the faith, to lay down our lives for the brothers. Therefore to Peter, whom he wished to make a good shepherd, not in Peter himself, but in his body, he said: *Peter, do you love me? Feed my sheep*[^7]. This once, this a second time, this a third time, to the point of his sadness. And when the Lord had asked as much as he judged ought to be asked, that he who had three times denied might three times confess, and had a third time commended his sheep to him to be fed; he said to him: *When you were younger, you girded yourself, and walked where you wished: but when you have grown old, you will stretch out your hands, and another will gird you, and lead you where you do not wish.* And the Evangelist expounded what the Lord said: *This,* he says, *he said, signifying by what death he would glorify God*[^8]. To this, then, pertains: *Feed my sheep*: that you lay down your life for my sheep.

## Christ preaches Christ.

**3.** Now that which he says: *As the Father knows me, and I acknowledge the Father*[^9], who does not know? For he himself acknowledges the Father through himself, we through him. That he himself through himself acknowledges, we know: that we too through him, this also we know; because this too we know through him. For he himself said: *No one has ever seen God, except the only-begotten Son who is in the bosom of the Father; he himself has declared him*[^10]. Therefore we too through him, to whom he declared him. Likewise elsewhere he says: *No one knows the Son, except the Father; nor does anyone know the Father, except the Son, and he to whom the Son shall will to reveal him*[^11]. As, then, he himself through himself knows the Father, but we through him know the Father; so he enters into the fold through himself, and we through him.

We were saying that we have the door to you through Christ; why? Because we preach Christ. We preach Christ; and therefore we enter through the door. But Christ preaches Christ, because he preaches himself; and therefore the shepherd enters through himself. Light, when it shows other things that are seen in the light, does it need any other thing that it may be shown? Light, then, both shows other things and shows itself. Whatever we understand, we understand by the understanding; and the understanding itself, whence do we understand it, except by the understanding? Do you so, with the eye of the flesh, see both other things and itself? For although men see with their eyes, yet they do not see their own eyes. The eye of the flesh sees other things, itself it cannot: but the understanding both understands other things, and itself. As the understanding sees itself, so Christ too preaches himself. If he preaches himself, and by preaching enters to you, he enters to you through himself. And he himself is the door to the Father; because there is no way to come to the Father except through him. For *there is one God, and one mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^12]. By the word many things are said: these very things that I have said, surely I have said by the word. If I should wish to say the word itself, whereby do I say it, except by the word? And through this, by the word other things too are said, which are not what the word is; and the word itself cannot be said, except by the word. With the Lord's help we have abounded in examples. Hold, then, how the Lord Jesus Christ is both the door and the shepherd: the door by opening himself, the shepherd by entering through himself. And indeed, brothers, that he is the shepherd, he gave also to his members: for Peter is a shepherd, and Paul is a shepherd, and the other Apostles are shepherds, and the good bishops are shepherds. But none of us calls himself the door; this he kept as proper to himself, the way by which the sheep enter. Finally, the apostle Paul fulfilled the office of a good shepherd, when he preached Christ, because he entered through the door. But when the undisciplined sheep began to make schisms, and to set up for themselves other doors, not by which they might enter to be gathered together, but by which they might stray to be divided, some saying: *I am of Paul*; others: *I am of Cephas*; others: *I am of Apollos*; others: *I am of Christ*: alarmed at those who said: I am of Paul, as though crying out to the sheep: Wretched ones, where are you going? I am not the door: *Was Paul,* he says, *crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^13] But those who said: I am of Christ, had found the door.  
**4.** But about the one fold and the one shepherd, you are now indeed accustomed to hear constantly: for we have much commended the one fold, preaching unity, that through Christ all the sheep might enter, and that none might follow Donatus. Nevertheless, in what sense the Lord said this properly is plain enough. For he was speaking among the Jews, and he had been sent to those same Jews, not for the sake of certain ones stubborn with monstrous hatred and persevering in darkness, but for the sake of certain ones in that very nation whom he calls his sheep: of whom he says: *I was not sent except to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel*[^14]. He knew them even in the crowd of those raging, and foresaw them in the peace of those believing. What, then, is: *I was not sent except to the sheep that were lost of the house of Israel*; except that he did not display his bodily presence except to the people of Israel? To the Gentiles he did not go himself, but sent; but to the people of Israel he both sent, and came himself; so that those who despised him might receive a greater judgment, because his presence too was displayed to them. The Lord himself was there, there he chose his mother; there he willed to be conceived, there to be born, there to shed his blood; there are his footprints, now adored, where he last stood, whence he ascended into heaven: but to the Gentiles he sent.  
**5.** But perhaps someone thinks that, since he himself did not come to us, but sent to us, we did not hear his voice, but the voice of those whom he sent. Far be it; let that thought be driven from your hearts: even in those whom he sent, he himself was. Hear Paul himself, whom he sent; for to the Gentiles especially he sent Paul as apostle: and Paul himself says, terrifying not about himself, but about him: *Do you wish to receive proof of him who speaks in me, Christ?*[^15] Hear too the Lord himself: *And I have other sheep*, that is, among the Gentiles: *which are not of this fold*, that is, of the people of Israel: *them too I must bring.* So even through his own he, not another, brings them. Hear further: *They will hear my voice.* Behold, even through his own he himself speaks, and through those whom he sends his voice is heard. *That there may be one fold and one shepherd*[^16]. For these two flocks, as for two walls, he was made the cornerstone[^17]. Therefore he is both the door, and the cornerstone: all by likeness, none of these properly.  
Property and likeness.  
**6.** For I have already said, and commended strongly, and those who grasp it are wise, nay, those who are wise grasp it; and those who are not yet wise by understanding, let them hold by faith what they cannot yet understand. By likeness Christ is many things which by property he is not. By likeness Christ is both the Rock, and Christ is the door, and Christ is the cornerstone, and Christ is the shepherd, and Christ is the lamb, and Christ is the lion. How many things by likenesses, and others, which it is long to recall! But if you examine the properties of the things which you are accustomed to see; he is not a rock, because he is not hard and without sense; nor a door, because a craftsman did not make him; nor a cornerstone, because he was not composed by a builder; nor a shepherd, because he is not a keeper of four-footed sheep; nor a lion, because he is not a wild beast; nor a lamb, because he is not a beast. All these things, then, by likeness. What, then, by property? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^18]. What of the man who appeared? *And the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^19].  
**7.** Hear the rest too. *For this reason the Father loves me,* he says; *because I lay down my life, that I may take it up again*[^20]. What does he say? For this reason the Father loves me: because I die that I may rise again. For with great weight it was said, I: *Because I lay down,* he says, *I lay down my life, I lay it down.* What is, I lay it down? I lay it down: let not the Jews boast; they could rage, they could not have power: let them rage as much as they can; if I am unwilling to lay down my life, what will they do by raging? By one answer they were laid low: when it was said to them: *Whom do you seek?* they said: Jesus; and he said to them: *I am he*: they went backward, and fell[^21]. Those who fell at one voice of Christ about to die, what will they do under his voice about to judge? *I, I,* I say, *lay down my life, that I may take it up again.* Let not the Jews boast, as though they prevailed; he himself laid down his life. *I slept,* he says. You know the Psalm: *I slept, and took rest; and I rose up, because the Lord will sustain me*[^22]. The Psalm itself was just now read, just now we heard: *I slept, and took rest; and I rose up, because the Lord will sustain me.* What is: I slept? Because I willed, I slept. What is, I slept? I died. Did he not sleep, who, when he willed, rose from the tomb as from a bed? But he loves to give glory to the Father, that he may build us up to give glory to the Creator. For as to what he added, *I rose up, because the Lord will sustain me*; do you think that here, as it were, his power failed, so that by his own power he could die, but by his own power could not rise again? For so the words seem to sound, if not more attentively understood: *I slept*; that is, because I willed, I slept. *And I rose up*: why? *Because the Lord will sustain me.* What then, would you not have had strength to rise of yourself? If you did not have strength, you would not say: *I have power to lay down my life, and I have power to take it up again*[^23]. Hear in another place in the Gospel that not only did the Father raise up the Son, but the Son also raised himself. *Destroy,* he says, *this temple, and in three days I will raise it up.* And the Evangelist: *This,* he says, *he was saying about the temple of his body*[^24]. For that was raised which was dying. For the Word did not die, that soul did not die. If not even yours dies, would the Lord's die?  
**8.** Whence do I know, you say, that mine does not die? Let it not be killed by you, and it does not die. How, you say, can I kill my soul? To pass over other sins for the moment: *The mouth that lies kills the soul*[^25]. How, you say, am I secure that it does not die? Hear the Lord himself giving security to the slave: *Do not fear those who kill the body, and afterward have nothing they can do.* But what, plainly, does he say? *Fear him who has power to kill both body and soul in hell*[^26]. Behold that it dies, behold that it does not die. What is its dying? What is the dying of your flesh? For your flesh to die is to lose its life: for your soul to die is to lose its life. The life of your flesh is your soul: the life of your soul is your God. As the flesh dies when the soul is lost, which is its life; so the soul dies when God is lost, who is its life. Surely, then, the soul is immortal. Plainly immortal, because it lives even when dead. For what the Apostle said of the self-indulgent widow can be said also of the soul, if it has lost its God: *Living, she is dead*[^27].  
Against the Apollinarians.  
**9.** How, then, does the Lord lay down his life? Brothers, let us inquire into this a little more attentively. The hour does not press us as on the Lord's Day: we have leisure; let those gain by it who come to the word of God even on this day. *I lay down,* he says, *my life.* Who lays it down? what does he lay down? What is Christ? Word and man. And not man as flesh only: but because man, he consists of flesh and soul, and the whole man is in Christ. For he would not have taken on the worse part, and deserted the better part: for the better part of man is the soul rather than the body. Because, then, the whole man is in Christ, what is Christ? Word, I say, and man. What is Word and man? Word, soul, and flesh. Hold this, because heretics have not been lacking even in this opinion, driven indeed long ago from the Catholic truth: but yet, as thieves and robbers not entering through the door, they do not cease to lie in wait for the fold. The Apollinarians were so called, who dared to teach as dogma that Christ is nothing but Word and flesh: they contend that he did not take on a human soul. For some of them could not deny that there was a soul in Christ. See the absurdity and intolerable madness. When they were willing for him to have an irrational soul, they denied the rational: they gave him the soul of a beast, they took away that of a man. But they took away reason from Christ, by not holding to reason. Far be this from us, nourished and grounded in the Catholic faith. From this occasion, then, let me admonish your Charity, that, as in the earlier readings we sufficiently instructed you against the Sabellians and Arians—the Sabellians who say: He is the Father who is the Son; the Arians who say: The Father is one thing, the Son another thing, as though the Father and the Son were not of the same substance: we instructed you also, as far as you remember, and ought to remember, against the Photinian heretics, who said that Christ is a man only, without God; against the Manichees, who said God only, without man: from this occasion let us instruct you about the soul also against the Apollinarians, who say that our Lord Jesus Christ did not have a human soul, that is, a rational soul, an understanding soul, a soul, I say, in which we differ from a beast, in that we are men.  
**10.** How, then, did the Lord here say: *I have power to lay down my life?* Who lays down the life, and takes it up again? Does Christ, from the fact that he is the Word, lay down the life, and take it up again? Or, from the fact that it is a human soul, does it itself lay itself down, and itself take itself up again? Or, from the fact that it is flesh, does the flesh lay down the soul, and again take it up? I have set forth three things; let us treat them all, and choose the one that agrees with the rule of truth. For if we say that the Word of God laid down its life, and again took it up; it is to be feared lest a perverse thought creep in, and it be said to us: Then that soul was at some time separated from the Word, and at some time that Word, from which it took on that soul, was without a soul. For I see that the Word was without a human soul, but when in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. For from the time that the Word was made flesh, that it might dwell among us[^28], and the man was taken on by the Word, that is, the whole man, soul and flesh; what did the passion do, what did death do, except separate the body from the soul? But the soul it did not separate from the Word. For if the Lord died—nay, because the Lord died; for he died for us on the cross; without doubt his flesh breathed out the soul: for a brief time the soul left the flesh, but, the soul returning, the flesh would rise again. But that the soul was separated from the Word I do not say. To the soul of the robber he said: *Today you will be with me in paradise*[^29]. The faithful soul of the robber he was not deserting, and was he deserting his own? Far be it: but the robber's soul he guarded as Lord, his own indeed he held inseparably. But if we say that the soul itself laid itself down, and itself again took itself up, the sense is most absurd: for that which was not separated from the Word could not be separated from itself.  
Understand the whole there, both the Word, and the soul, and the flesh.  
**11.** Let us say, then, both what is true, and what can easily be understood. Behold, any man whatever, not consisting of Word and soul and flesh, but of soul and flesh: let us ask about this man how any man lays down his life. Or does perhaps no man lay down his life? You can say to me: No man has power to lay down his life, and again to take it up. Unless a man could lay down his life, the apostle John would not say: *As Christ laid down his life for us, so we too ought to lay down our lives for the brothers*[^30]. Therefore it is permitted to us also (if we too are filled with his power, because without him we can do nothing) to lay down our lives for the brothers. When any holy martyr laid down his life for the brothers, who laid it down, and what did he lay down? If we understand this, we shall see there how it was said by Christ: *I have power to lay down my life.* O man, are you ready to die for Christ? I am ready, he says. I will say this in other words. Are you ready to lay down your life for Christ? And to these words he answers me thus: I am ready; as he answered me when I said: Are you ready to die? This, then, is to lay down the life, which is to die. But for what is the contest there? For all men, when they die, lay down the life; but not all lay it down for Christ. And no one has power to take up what he laid down: but Christ both laid it down for us, and when he willed, laid it down; and when he willed, took it up. To lay down the life, then, is to die. So too the apostle Peter said to the Lord: *I will lay down my life for you*[^31]: that is, I will die for you. Attribute this to the flesh: the flesh lays down its soul, and the flesh again takes it up; yet not by its own power the flesh, but by the power of him who inhabits the flesh: the flesh, then, lays down its soul by breathing out. See the Lord himself on the cross; he said, *I thirst*: those who were present dipped a sponge in vinegar, bound it to a reed, and put it to his mouth; and when he had received it, he said: *It is finished*: what is, It is finished? All the things that, before my death, had been prophesied as to come concerning me are fulfilled. And because he had power, when he willed, to lay down his life; after he said: It is finished, what does the Evangelist say? *And bowing his head, he handed over the Spirit*[^32]. This is to lay down the life. Now here let your Charity attend. Bowing his head, he handed over the spirit. Who handed it over? whom did he hand over? He handed over the spirit, the flesh handed it over. What is, the flesh handed it over? The flesh sent it forth, the flesh breathed it out. For to "expire" is so called, to be made outside the spirit. As to "exile" is to be made outside the soil; to "go off course" is to be made outside the track: so to "expire" is to be made outside the spirit; which spirit is the soul. When, then, the soul goes out from the flesh, and the flesh remains without a soul, then the man is said to lay down his soul. When did Christ lay down his soul? When the Word willed. For the sovereignty was in the Word; there was the power, when the flesh should lay down the soul, and when it should take it up.  
**12.** If, then, the flesh laid down the soul, how did Christ lay down his soul? For is not the flesh Christ? So indeed, both the flesh is Christ, and the soul is Christ, and the Word is Christ: nor yet are these three things three Christs, but one Christ. Ask man, and, from yourself make a step to the things that are above you, and, if not yet to be understood, at least to be believed. For as one man is soul and body; so one Christ is Word and man. See what I have said, and understand. Soul and body are two things, but one man: Word and man are two things, but one Christ. Therefore ask about man. Where is the apostle Paul now? If anyone answers: In rest with Christ; he speaks truly. Likewise if anyone answers: At Rome in the tomb; he too speaks truly. The former answers me about the soul, the latter about his flesh. Nor yet do we therefore say two apostle Pauls; one who rests in Christ, another who is placed in the tomb: although we say that the apostle Paul lives in Christ, and the same apostle Paul we say lies dead in the tomb. Someone dies, we say: A good man, a faithful man, he is at peace with the Lord: and at once: Let us go to his funeral, and bury him. You are going to bury him whom you had already said to be at peace with God: since the soul is one thing, which flourishes immortally, the body another, which lies corruptibly. But from the time that the partnership of flesh and soul received the name "man," now also, singly and separately, either of them holds the name "man."  
The flesh lays down the soul, but by the power of the Word.  
**13.** Let no one, then, waver, when he hears the Lord saying: *I lay down my life, and again I take it up*[^33]. The flesh lays it down, but by the power of the Word: the flesh takes it up, but by the power of the Word. And the Lord Christ himself was called the flesh alone. How, you say, do you prove it? I dare to say, the flesh of Christ alone too was called Christ. We surely believe not in God the Father alone, but also in Jesus Christ his only Son, our Lord: now I have said the whole, in Jesus Christ his only Son our Lord. Understand the whole there, both the Word, and the soul, and the flesh. But surely you confess also that which the same faith holds, that you believe in that Christ who was crucified and buried. Therefore you do not deny that Christ too was buried: and yet the flesh alone was buried. For if the soul was there, he was not dead: but if it was a true death, that his resurrection might be true, he had been without a soul in the tomb; and yet Christ was buried. Therefore Christ was the flesh even without a soul, because nothing was buried except the flesh. Learn this too in the apostolic words. *Have this mind in you,* he says, *which was also in Christ Jesus: who, though he was in the form of God, did not consider it robbery to be equal to God.* Who, except Christ Jesus, as far as concerns that which is the Word, God with God? But see what follows: *But he emptied himself, taking the form of a slave; made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man.* And this, who, except the same Christ Jesus himself? But here now all things are present, both the Word in the form of God, which took the form of a slave; and the soul and flesh in the form of a slave, which was taken on by the form of God. *He humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death*[^34]. Now in death the flesh alone was killed by the Jews. For if he said to the disciples: *Do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*[^35]; could they in him kill more than the body? And yet, the flesh being killed, Christ was killed. So when the flesh laid down the soul, Christ laid down the soul; and when the flesh, that it might rise again, took up the soul, Christ took up the soul. Yet this was not done by the power of the flesh; but of him who took on both soul and flesh, where these things might be fulfilled.  
**14.** *This commandment,* he says, *I have received from my Father*[^36]. The Word received the commandment not by a word, but in the Word, the only-begotten of the Father, is the whole commandment. But when the Son is said to receive from the Father what he has substantially, as it was said: *As the Father has life in himself, so he gave to the Son to have life in himself*[^37], since the Son himself is life; the power is not lessened, but his generation is shown. Since the Father did not, as it were, add something to a son born imperfect; but to the one whom he begot perfect, he gave all things by begetting. So he gave him his own equality, whom he did not beget unequal. But, the Lord speaking these things, since the light shone in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it[^38]: *A dissension arose again among the Jews because of these words. And many of them said: He has a demon, and is mad; why do you listen to him?* These were the densest darkness. *Others said: These are not the words of one who has a demon; can a demon open the eyes of the blind?*[^39] Now the eyes of these were beginning to be opened.
