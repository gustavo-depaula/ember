# Tractate 65

## *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another, as I have loved you* (Jn 13:34-35).

## Christ gave us a new commandment, that we love one another, as he too loved us. This love makes us new, that we may be new men, heirs of the New Testament, singers of a new song. — A new commandment, that we may be new men.

**1.** The Lord Jesus attests that he gives a new commandment to his disciples, that they love one another: *A commandment,* he says, *new I give you, that you love one another.* Was not this commandment already in the ancient law of God, where it is written: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^1]? Why, then, is it called new by the Lord, which is shown to be so old? Or is it a new commandment because, having stripped off the old, it clothes us with the new man? For it makes new the one who hears, or rather the one who obeys, not every love, but that love which the Lord, in order to distinguish it from carnal love, added, *as I have loved you.* For husbands and wives love one another, parents and children, and whatever other human kinship binds men together: to say nothing of the blameworthy and damnable love by which adulterers and adulteresses love one another, fornicators and harlots, and whatever others not human kinship, but the harmful baseness of human life joins together.

Christ, then, gave us a new commandment, that we love one another, as he too loved us. This love makes us new, that we may be new men, heirs of the New Testament, singers of a new song. This love, dearly beloved brothers, made new even the righteous of old, the Patriarchs and Prophets of that time, as afterward the blessed Apostles: this same love now too makes the nations new, and out of the whole human race that is spread throughout the whole world makes and gathers a new people, the body of the newly wedded bride of the only-begotten Son of God, of whom it is said in the Song of Songs: *Who is this that comes up made white*[^2]? Made white, surely, because made new; and whence but by the new commandment? On account of which the members in her are anxious for one another; and *if one member suffers, all the members suffer with it, and if one member is glorified, all the members rejoice together*[^3]. For they hear, and keep: *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another*: not as those love one another who corrupt, nor as men love one another because they are men; but as they love one another because they are gods and all sons of the Most High, that they may be brothers of his only Son, loving one another with that love by which he loved them, about to lead them to that end which suffices them, where their desire shall be satisfied with good things. For then nothing will be lacking to desire, when God will be all in all. Such an end has no end. No one dies there, where no one comes, unless he dies to this world, not with the death of all, by which the body is forsaken by the soul; but with the death of the elect, by which, even while one yet remains in mortal flesh, the heart is set above. Of which death the Apostle spoke: *For you are dead, and your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^4]. Hence perhaps it was said: *Love is strong as death*[^5]. For by this love it comes about that, established still in this corruptible body, we die to this world, and our life is hidden with Christ in God: rather, this love itself is our death to the world, and our life with God. For if it is death when the soul goes out from the body, how is it not death when our love goes out from the world? Love, then, is strong as death. What is stronger than it, by which the world is overcome?

## He who loves his neighbor loves God in him.

**2.** Do not, then, my brothers, think that in this which the Lord says: *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another,* that greater commandment is passed over, by which it is commanded that we love the Lord our God with our whole heart, with our whole soul, and with our whole mind: for, as if this were passed over, it seems to be said, *that you love one another,* as though this did not pertain to that other where it was said: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself. For on these two commandments,* he says, *hang the whole Law and the Prophets*[^6]. But to those who understand well, each is found in each. For both he who loves God cannot despise him who commands that he love his neighbor; and he who loves his neighbor in a holy and spiritual way, what does he love in him but God?

This is the very love distinguished from all worldly love, which the Lord, in distinguishing it, added, *as I have loved you.* For what did he love in us but God? Not that we had him, but that we might have him: that he might lead us, as I said a little before, where God is all in all. So too a physician is rightly said to love the sick: and what does he love in them but health, which he certainly desires to recall, not the disease, which he comes to expel? So, then, let us too love one another, that, as far as we can, we may by the care of love draw one another to have God in us. This love he himself bestows on us, who says: *As I have loved you, that you also love one another.* To this end, then, he loved us, that we too might love one another; conferring this on us by loving us, that we might be bound by mutual love among ourselves, and, as members joined by so sweet a bond, be the body of so great a head.

**3.** *By this,* he says, *all will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another*[^7]: as if he said: My other gifts even those who are not mine have in common with you, not only nature, life, sense, reason, and that health which is common to men and beasts; but even tongues, sacraments, prophecy, knowledge, faith, the distribution of their goods to the poor, and the handing over of their body to be burned: but since they do not have love, they sound like clashing cymbals, they are nothing, it profits them nothing[^8]. Not, then, by those gifts of mine, however good, which even those who are not my disciples can have; but *by this all will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another.*

O bride of Christ, beautiful among women! O you made white, coming up, and leaning upon your kinsman! For by whose light you are illumined that you may shine, by his help you are upheld that you may not fall: O how well is it sung to you in that Song of Songs as in your wedding song: *For love is among your delights*[^9]! It does not destroy your soul along with the ungodly; it pleads your cause, and is strong as death, and is among your delights. How wondrous a kind of death it is, for which it was too little not to be among punishments, unless it were besides among delights!

But let this discourse now be closed: the things that follow must be treated from a new beginning.
