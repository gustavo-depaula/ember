# Tractate 83

## *These things I have spoken to you, that my joy may be in you and your joy may be made full*[^1].

## What is the joy of Christ in us, but that he deigns to rejoice over us? And what is our joy, which he says is to be made full, but to have fellowship with him?

**1.** You have heard, dearly beloved, the Lord saying to his disciples: *These things I have spoken to you, that my joy may be in you, and your joy may be made full*[^2]. What is the joy of Christ in us, but that he deigns to rejoice over us? And what is our joy, which he says is to be made full, but to have fellowship with him? On account of which he had said to blessed Peter: *If I do not wash you, you will have no part with me*[^3]. His joy in us, then, is the grace that he has bestowed on us: this same grace is also our joy. But in this he rejoiced even from eternity, when he chose us before the foundation of the world. Nor can we rightly say that his joy was not full; for God never rejoiced imperfectly. But that joy of his was not in us, because we, in whom it could be, did not yet exist; nor, when we began to exist, did we begin to exist with him. But it was always in him, who rejoiced that we would be his by the most certain truth of his foreknowledge. Accordingly he already had a perfect joy over us, when he rejoiced by foreknowing and predestining us; for there could be no fear in that joy of his, lest perhaps that should not come to pass which he foreknew he would do. But neither, when he began to do that which he foreknew he would do, did his joy, by which he is blessed, grow; otherwise he was made more blessed because he made us. Far be this, brothers: since the blessedness of God was no less without us, it is not made greater from us. His joy, therefore, over our salvation, which was always in him when he foreknew and predestined us, began to be in us when he called us; and this joy we rightly call ours, by which we too shall be blessed: but this joy of ours grows and advances, and by persevering presses on toward its perfection. It is begun, then, in the faith of those who are reborn; it will be made full in the reward of those who rise again.

See whence I think it was said: *These things I have spoken to you, that my joy may be in you, and your joy may be made full*[^4]; *may be in you,* mine; *may be made full,* yours. For mine was always full, even before you were called, when you were foreknown by me as to be called; but it comes to be in you also, when you become that which I foreknew concerning you. *But your joy may be made full*; because you will be blessed, which you are not yet, just as you were created, who were not.

**2.** *This is my commandment,* he says, *that you love one another, as I have loved you*[^5]. Whether one says "precept" or "commandment," both are rendered from a single Greek word, which is "entole" (ἐντολή). Now he had said this saying before also, on which you ought to remember that I discussed with you as I was able. For there he says thus: *A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another, as I have loved you, that you also love one another*[^6]. The repetition of this commandment, therefore, is a commending of it: except that there he says, *A new commandment I give to you*; but here, *This is my commandment*: there, as though there had not been before such a commandment; here, as though there were no other commandment of his. But there it was said *new,* lest we persevere in our oldness; here it was said *my,* lest we should think it ought to be despised.

## Where there is love, there of necessity are faith and hope.

**3.** But as for his saying here, *This is my commandment,* as though there were no other, what do we think, my brothers? Is his only commandment about that love by which we love one another? Is there not also another, greater one, that we love God? Or did God command us about love alone, so that we are to seek nothing else? Three things, certainly, the Apostle commends, saying: *Now there abide faith, hope, love, these three: but the greatest of these is love*[^7]. And even if those two commandments are summed up in charity, that is, in love, it was nevertheless said to be the greatest, not the only one. How many things, then, are commanded us concerning faith, how many concerning hope — who can gather them all, who can suffice to enumerate them?

But let us consider what the same apostle says: *The fullness of the law is love*[^8]. Where, then, there is love, what is there that can be lacking? But where it is not, what is there that can be of profit? The demon believes, but does not love; no one loves who does not believe. In vain, indeed, yet still he can hope for pardon who does not love; but no one can despair who loves. And so where there is love, there of necessity are faith and hope; and where there is love of neighbor, there of necessity is also love of God. For he who does not love God, how does he love his neighbor as himself, since he does not love even himself? For he is impious and unrighteous; and *he who loves iniquity* plainly does not love, but *hates his own soul*[^9].

Let us, then, hold to this commandment of the Lord, that we love one another; and whatever else he has commanded, we shall do: since whatever else there is, we have it here. For this love is distinguished from that love by which men love one another as men; for that it might be distinguished, there was added, *as I have loved you.* For why does Christ love us, if not that we may be able to reign with Christ? To this end, then, let us also love one another, that we may distinguish our love from that of others, who do not love one another to this end, because they do not even love. But those who love one another for the sake of having God, love themselves: therefore, that they may love themselves, they love God. This love is not in all men; few love themselves for this reason, *that God may be all in all*[^10].
