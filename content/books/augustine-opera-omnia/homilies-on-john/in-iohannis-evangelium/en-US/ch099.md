# Tractate 99

## *For he will not speak from himself, but whatever he will hear, he will speak*[^1].

## The Holy Spirit always hears, because he always knows; for to hear is for him the same as to know, and to know is for him the same as to be. From him, therefore, he has heard, hears, and will hear, from whom he is; from him he is, from whom he proceeds.

**1.** What is it that the Lord says of the Holy Spirit, when he promised that he would come and would teach his disciples all truth, or would lead them into all truth: *For he will not speak from himself, but whatever he will hear, he will speak*[^2]? For this is like what he said of himself: *I can do nothing from myself: as I hear, I judge*[^3]. But when we explained that, we said it could be taken according to the man: so that the Son might seem to have foretold his own obedience, by which he was made obedient even to the death of the cross, and that it would be present in the judgment, in which he will judge the living and the dead; because he is going to do this through that whereby he is the son of man. On account of which he said: *The Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*[^4]: because in the judgment the form of God, by which he is equal to the Father, cannot be seen even by the impious, but the form of man will appear, by which he was made even a little less than the angels; although he is now to come in glory, not in his former lowliness, yet to be visible to both the good and the evil. Hence he says this too: *And he has given him power to make judgment, because he is the son of man*[^5]. In which words of his it is made manifest that not that form is to be presented to the judgment, in which, when he was, he did not count it robbery to be equal to God; but that which he received when he had emptied himself. For he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant: in which he seems to have commended his obedience even for making judgment, when he said: *I can do nothing from myself: as I hear, I judge.*

For Adam, through whose disobedience, the disobedience of one man, the many were made sinners, did not judge as he heard; because what he heard he transgressed, and from himself he did the evil that he did, because he did not do God's will, but his own: but this one, through whose obedience, the obedience of one man, the many are made righteous, was not only obedient even to the death of the cross, in which, living, he was judged by the dead; but, promising that he would be obedient in the very judgment also, in which he is to judge the living and the dead, says: *I can do nothing from myself: as I hear, I judge.*

But shall we dare to suppose that what was said of the Holy Spirit, *For he will not speak from himself, but whatever he will hear, he will speak,* was said according to a man or according to the assumption of any creature? For in the Trinity the Son alone received the form of a servant, which form was fitted to him into the unity of the person, that is, so that the Son of God and the son of man might be one Jesus Christ; lest not a Trinity, but a quaternity be preached by us — which far be it from us. On account of which one person, consisting of two substances, the divine and the human, sometimes he speaks according to that which he is as God, as is that which he says, *I and the Father are one*[^6]: sometimes according to that which he is as man, as is that, *For the Father is greater than I*[^7]; according to which we have taken to be said by him also this, on which I now discuss: *I can do nothing from myself: as I hear, I judge.* But in the person of the Holy Spirit, how are we to take what he says, *For he will not speak from himself, but whatever he will hear, he will speak*; when in it there is not one substance of divinity, another of humanity, or of any other creature — a great difficulty arises.

## A great difficulty arises.

**2.** For that the Holy Spirit appeared like a dove in a bodily appearance was a vision made for an hour and passed away: as also when he came upon the disciples, there were seen by them tongues divided as if of fire, which also settled upon each one of them. He, then, who says that the dove was joined to the Holy Spirit in the unity of the person, so that from it and God (because the Holy Spirit is God) one person of the Holy Spirit was constituted, is compelled to say this also of that fire, so that he may understand that he ought to say none of these things. For these things, which presented themselves to the bodily senses of men in some way, as it was needful, to be signified, concerning the substance of God, and passed away, were made for an hour by divine power out of a serving creature, not out of that very ruling nature, which, abiding in itself, moves what it wills, and, unchangeable, changes what it wills. As also that voice from the cloud certainly touched the bodily ears, and that bodily sense which is called hearing; nor yet is it in any way to be believed that the Word of God, which is the only-begotten Son, because it is called Word, is bounded by syllables and sounds: for not all can sound at the same time when speech is made, but, as if being born and dying in their order, the sounds, whatever they be, succeed one another, so that the whole of what we speak is completed by the last syllable. Far be it that the Father so speak to the Son, that is, God to his Word, who is God. But it belongs to those to grasp this, as far as it can be grasped by a man, to whom not milk pertains, but solid food.

Since, then, the Holy Spirit by no assumption of a man was made man, by no assumption of an angel was made an angel, by no assumption of any creature was made a creature; how is what the Lord says to be understood of him: *For he will not speak from himself, but whatever he will hear, he will speak*? An arduous question, exceedingly arduous. May the Spirit himself be present, that at least, as we can think it, so we may be able to utter it, and so it may reach your understanding according to the capacity of my measure.

**3.** First, then, you ought to know — and to understand, those of you who can; but to believe, those who cannot yet understand — that in that substance which is God there are not, as it were through the mass of a body, senses distributed in their proper places, as in the mortal flesh of any animals sight is in one place, hearing in another, taste in another, smell in another, but touch throughout the whole. Far be it to believe this of that incorporeal and unchangeable nature. To hear there, then, and to see, is the same thing. Smell too is spoken of in God: whence the Apostle says, *As Christ also loved us, and handed himself over for us, an offering and sacrifice to God for an odor of sweetness*[^8]. And taste can be understood, according to which God both hates those who embitter, and vomits out of his mouth those who are neither cold nor hot, but lukewarm: and God Christ says, *My food is to do the will of him who sent me*[^9]. There is also that divine touch, whence the bride says of the bridegroom: *His left hand is under my head, and his right hand will embrace me*[^10]. These things are not in God through diverse places of a body. For when he is said to know, there are all these things; both to see, and to hear, and to smell, and to taste, and to touch; without any change of his substance, without any mass which in one part is greater, in another less: even in old men he is thought of with a childlike heart, when God is thus thought of.

## The divine substance is that which it has.

**4.** And do not wonder that the ineffable knowledge of God, by which he knows all things, is named, through the various modes of human speech, by the names of all these bodily senses: when even our own mind, that is, the inner man — to whom, knowing uniformly, diverse things are reported through these five, as it were, messengers of the body — when it understands, chooses, loves the unchangeable truth, both sees the light of which it is said, *It was the true light*[^11]; and hears the word of which it is said, *In the beginning was the Word*[^12]; and takes the fragrance of which it is said, *After the odor of your ointments we will run*[^13]; and drinks the fountain of which it is said, *With you is the fountain of life*[^14]; and enjoys the touch of which it is said, *But for me it is good to cleave to God*[^15]: nor is it one thing and another, but one understanding is named by the names of so many senses.

When, therefore, it is said of the Holy Spirit, *For he will not speak from himself, but whatever he will hear, he will speak,* much more there is the simple nature, where it is most truly simple, to be either understood or believed, which far and high exceeds the nature of our mind. For our mind is changeable, which receives by learning what it did not know, and loses by unlearning what it knew; and it is deceived by the likeness of the true, so that it approves the false for the true, and is hindered by its own obscurity, as by certain shadows, from reaching the true. And therefore that substance is not most truly simple, for which to be is not the same as to know: for it can be, and not know. But that divine substance cannot, because it is that which it has. And through this it does not so have knowledge that the knowledge by which it knows is one thing for it, the essence by which it is another; but both are one. Nor are both to be said, that which is simply one. *As the Father has life in himself,* nor is he himself anything other than the life which is in him; *and has given to the Son to have life in himself*[^16], that is, he begot the Son, who himself too should be life.

So, then, we ought to take what was said of the Holy Spirit, *For he will not speak from himself, but whatever he will hear, he will speak,* so that we understand that he is not from himself. For the Father alone is not from another. For both the Son is born of the Father, and the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father: but the Father is neither born of another, nor proceeds. Nor, indeed, let any inequality in that highest Trinity occur to human thought: for both the Son is equal to him of whom he is born, and the Holy Spirit to him from whom he proceeds. But what difference there is between proceeding and being born, is both long to discuss by inquiring, and rash to define when you have discussed it: because both for the mind in some way to comprehend it, and, if perhaps the mind comprehends anything of it, for the tongue to explain it, is most difficult, however great a teacher presides, however great a hearer is present. *He will not, then, speak from himself*: because he is not from himself. *But whatever he will hear, he will speak*: he will hear from him from whom he proceeds. To hear is for him to know; but to know is to be, as was discussed above. Since, then, he is not from himself, but from him from whom he proceeds; from whom he has essence, from him knowledge: from him, therefore, hearing, which is nothing other than knowledge.

## The Spirit always hears, who always knows.

**5.** Nor let it disturb you that a word of future tense is used. For it was not said, whatever he has heard, or, whatever he hears; but, *whatever he will hear, he will speak.* For that hearing is everlasting, because the knowledge is everlasting. But in that which is everlasting, without beginning and without end, whatever word of time be used, whether of the past, or of the present, or of the future, it is not used falsely. For although that unchangeable and ineffable nature does not admit "It was" and "It will be," but only "It is" — for it itself truly is, because it cannot be changed; and therefore it alone befitted it to say, *I am who I am*; and, *You will say to the children of Israel: He who is has sent me to you*[^17] — yet, on account of the changeableness of the times in which our mortality and our changeableness are engaged, we do not say falsely both "it was," and "it will be," and "it is." It was, in past ages; it is, in present; it will be, in future. It was, because it was never wanting; it will be, because it will never be wanting; it is, because it always is. For not, as one who now is not, does it fall away with things past; nor, as one who does not abide, does it slip away with things present; nor, as one who had not been, does it arise with things future.

Accordingly, since human speech is varied according to the rolls of times, of him who through no times could be, or can be, or will be wanting, words of any time whatever are said truly. The Holy Spirit, therefore, always hears, because he always knows: he both knew, and knows, and will know; and through this he both heard, and hears, and will hear: because, as we have already said, to hear is for him the same as to know, and to know is for him the same as to be. From him, therefore, he heard, hears, and will hear, from whom he is: from him he is, from whom he proceeds.

**6.** Here perhaps someone may ask whether the Holy Spirit proceeds also from the Son. For the Son is Son of the Father alone, and the Father is Father of the Son alone: but the Holy Spirit is not the Spirit of one of them, but of both. You have the Lord himself saying: *For it is not you who speak, but the Spirit of your Father who speaks in you*[^18]: you have also the Apostle: *God sent the Spirit of his Son into your hearts*[^19]. Are there two, one of the Father, another of the Son? Far be it. For *one body,* he says, when he signified the Church; and at once added, *and one Spirit.* And see how there he fills out the Trinity. *As you were called,* he says, *in one hope of your calling. One Lord* — here he certainly wished Christ to be understood: it remains that he also name the Father: there follows, then: *One faith, one Baptism: one God and Father of all, who is above all, and through all, and in us all*[^20]. Since, then, as there is one Father, and one Lord, that is, the Son, so there is also one Spirit; surely he is the Spirit of both: seeing that Christ Jesus himself says, *The Spirit of your Father who speaks in you*; and the Apostle says, *God sent the Spirit of his Son into your hearts.* You have in another place the same apostle saying: *But if the Spirit of him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you*[^21] — here he certainly wished the Spirit of the Father to be understood: of whom, however, in another place he says: *But whoever does not have the Spirit of Christ, this one is not his*[^22]. And there are many other testimonies by which this is evidently shown, that he is the Spirit both of the Father and of the Son who in the Trinity is called the Holy Spirit.

**7.** Nor for any other reason do I think that he is properly called the Spirit: since, even if we are questioned about them individually, we cannot but call both the Father and the Son spirit; for *God is spirit*[^23], that is, God is not a body, but spirit. What, therefore, is the common name of the individuals, it was fitting that he be properly called by it who is not one of them, but in whom the commonness of both appears. Why, then, should we not believe that the Holy Spirit also proceeds from the Son, since he is the Spirit of the Son too? For if he did not proceed from him, he would not, after the resurrection, presenting himself to his disciples, have breathed upon them saying: *Receive the Holy Spirit*[^24]. For what else did that breathing signify, but that the Holy Spirit proceeds also from him? To this belongs also what he said of the woman who suffered an issue of blood: *Someone touched me; for I perceived power going out from me*[^25]. For that the Holy Spirit is also called by the name of power is clear from that place where the angel, to Mary saying, *How shall this be, since I do not know a man*, answered: *The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you*[^26]: and the Lord himself, promising him to the disciples, says: *But sit in the city until you are clothed with power from on high*[^27]; and again: *You will receive,* he says, *the power of the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and you will be my witnesses*[^28]. Of this power the evangelist is to be believed to speak: *Power went out from him, and healed all*[^29].

**8.** If, then, the Holy Spirit proceeds both from the Father and from the Son, why did the Son say, *He proceeds from the Father*[^30]? Why, do you think, unless in the way in which he is wont to refer to him even that which is his own, from whom he himself also is? Whence is that which he says: *My doctrine is not mine, but his who sent me*[^31]. If, then, here his doctrine is understood, which yet he said was not his, but the Father's; how much more there is the Holy Spirit to be understood as proceeding from him, where he so says, *He proceeds from the Father,* as not to say: He does not proceed from me? But from whom the Son has it that he is God (for he is God of God), from him he certainly has it that the Holy Spirit also proceeds from him: and through this the Holy Spirit has it from the Father himself that he proceeds also from the Son, as he proceeds from the Father.

**9.** Here in some way is understood also this — as far as it can be understood by such as we are — why the Holy Spirit is not said to be born, but rather to proceed. Since, if he too were called Son, he would surely be called the Son of both, which is most absurd. For no one is the son of two, except of a father and a mother. But far be it that between God the Father and God the Son we should suspect any such thing. For not even a son of men proceeds at once both from his father and from his mother: but when he proceeds from his father into his mother, he does not then proceed from his mother; and when he proceeds from his mother into this light, he does not then proceed from his father. But the Holy Spirit does not proceed from the Father into the Son, and proceed from the Son to sanctify the creature; but he proceeds at once from both: although the Father has given this to the Son, that as he proceeds from himself, so he proceeds also from him.

For neither can we say that the Holy Spirit is not life, since the Father is life, and the Son is life. And through this, just as the Father, although he has life in himself, has given to the Son also to have life in himself; so he has given to him to have life proceeding from him, as it proceeds also from himself.

But there follow the words of the Lord saying: *And the things that are to come, he will announce to you. He will glorify me, because he will receive of mine, and will announce it to you. All things whatsoever the Father has are mine: therefore I said that he will receive of mine, and will announce it to you*[^32]. But because this one is already long, these are to be deferred to another sermon.
