# Tractate 98

## *But you cannot bear them now*[^1].

## Any spiritual man can teach another man what he knows, if the Holy Spirit, by his advancing, makes him more capacious — the Holy Spirit in whom the teacher himself too was able to learn something further, so that both may be taught by God.

**1.** I recall that I deferred the difficult question that arose from the words of our Lord, where he says, *I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now,* so that it might be treated more at leisure, because a fitting measure compelled me to end that sermon. Now, then, since it is time to render what was promised, let it be treated as the Lord himself shall grant, who put it into our heart that it should be set forth.

But this is the question: whether spiritual men have anything in their doctrine which they keep silent from the fleshly, and speak to the spiritual. For if we say, They do not, it will be answered us: What is it, then, that the Apostle said, writing to the Corinthians: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to fleshly. As to little ones in Christ, I gave you milk to drink, not food; for you were not yet able: but neither indeed are you yet able; for you are still fleshly*[^2]? But if we say, They do, it is to be feared and guarded against, lest under this occasion nefarious things be taught in secret, and, under the name of spiritual things — as things which the fleshly cannot grasp — they seem not only to be whitewashed with excuse, but even to be praised with proclamation.

**2.** First, then, your Charity ought to know that Christ himself crucified, with whom as with milk the Apostle says he fed the little ones; but his very flesh, in which his true death was accomplished and the true wounds of him pierced, and the blood of him struck — these are not thought of by the fleshly in the way they are thought of by the spiritual, and to the former they are milk, to the latter food; because even if they do not hear more, they understand more. For that which is received in faith equally by both is not equally perceived by the mind. So it comes about that Christ crucified, preached by the Apostles, was both to the Jews a scandal and to the Gentiles foolishness, but to the very Jews and Greeks who were called the Power of God and the Wisdom of God: but to the fleshly little ones holding it only by believing, while to the more capacious spiritual ones discerning it also by understanding; to those, then, as a milky drink, to these as solid food: not because the former came to know this in one way among the peoples, the latter in another way in their chambers; but because both, when it was said openly, heard it in the same way, and each grasped it according to his own measure. For since Christ was crucified for this reason, that he might shed his blood for the remission of sins — by which passion of the Only-begotten the divine grace is commended, that no one may glory in man — how did those understand Christ crucified who still said, *I am of Paul*[^3]? Surely not as Paul himself did, who said, *But far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^4]? Of Christ crucified, therefore, he himself both took food according to his capacity, and nourished those with milk according to their weakness. Finally, knowing that those things which he wrote to the Corinthians could certainly be understood in one way by the little ones themselves, in another by the more capacious, he says: *If anyone among you is a prophet or spiritual, let him acknowledge that the things I write to you are the Lord's commandment: but if anyone is ignorant, he will be ignored*[^5]. He wished, surely, the knowledge of the spiritual to be solid, where not faith alone should be applied, but a certain cognition should be held; and through this they believed those very things which the spiritual moreover acknowledged. But *he will be ignored,* he says, who *is ignorant*; because it has not yet been revealed to him, that he may know what he believes. And when this comes about in a man's mind, he himself is said to be known by God; because God makes him to be one who knows, as he says elsewhere: *But now knowing God, or rather known by God*[^6]. For God had not then come to know them, foreknown and chosen before the foundation of the world; but he had then made them to know himself.

**3.** This, then, being first understood — that the very things which the spiritual and the fleshly hear together, each grasps according to his own measure; the latter as little ones, the former as grown men; the latter as a nourishment of milk, the former as a solidity of food — there seems to be no necessity that any secrets of doctrine be kept silent and hidden from the faithful little ones, to be said apart to the grown — that is, the more intelligent; and that this should be thought to be done because the Apostle said, *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to fleshly.* For this very thing, that he judged not to know among them anything but Jesus Christ, and him crucified, he could not speak to them as to spiritual men, but as to fleshly; because they were not able to grasp it as the spiritual do. But whoever among them were spiritual grasped by a spiritual understanding the same thing which the others heard as fleshly: so that what he says, *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to fleshly,* may be understood as if he said: You could not grasp what I was speaking as spiritual men, but as fleshly. For *the animal man,* that is, he who savors according to man — called animal from the soul (anima), fleshly from the flesh, because the whole man consists of soul and flesh — *does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God*[^7]; that is, what grace the cross of Christ confers on believers; and he thinks that only this was accomplished by that cross, that an example might be set for us to imitate as we contend even unto death for the truth. For if such men, who wish to be nothing but men, knew how Christ crucified *was made for us wisdom from God, and righteousness, and sanctification, and redemption, that, as it is written, He who glories, let him glory in the Lord*[^8], without doubt they would not glory in man, nor say in a fleshly way, *I indeed am of Paul; but I of Apollos; but I of Cephas*[^9]: but spiritually, *I am of Christ.*

**4.** But this still makes a question, which is read in the Epistle to the Hebrews: *When by this time you ought to be teachers, you again need teaching as to what are the elements of the words of God; and you have become such as have need of milk, not solid food. For everyone who is fed with milk is unskilled in the word of righteousness; for he is an infant. But solid food is for the perfect, for those who by habit have their senses exercised to separate good from evil*[^10]. For here we see, as it were, a definition of what he calls the solid food of the perfect; and that this is what is written to the Corinthians: *We speak wisdom among the perfect*[^11]. But whom he wished to be understood as the perfect in this place, he added, saying: *Those who by habit have their senses exercised to separate good from evil.*

Those, then, who with a weak and unexercised mind cannot do this — surely, unless they are held by a kind of milk of faith, that they may believe both the invisible things which they do not see, and the intelligible things which they do not yet understand — are easily led to vain and sacrilegious fables by the promise of knowledge: so that they think of both good and evil only by bodily images, and suppose God himself to be some kind of body, and cannot think of evil except as a substance; whereas it is rather a kind of defect, away from the unchangeable substance, of changeable substances, which the unchangeable and highest substance itself, who is God, made from nothing. And whoever not only believes this, but also understands, perceives, and knows it by the exercised inner senses of the mind, need no longer fear that he may be seduced by those who, thinking evil to be a substance which God did not make, make God himself a changeable substance, like the Manicheans, or whatever other plagues are thus foolish.

**5.** But to those still little ones in mind, whom the Apostle calls fleshly, to be nourished with milk, all discourse on this matter — by which it is brought about that what is said is not only believed, but also understood and known — is burdensome to those not able to perceive such things, and more easily weighs them down than feeds them. From which it comes about that the spiritual do not altogether keep silent these things from the fleshly, on account of the catholic faith, which is to be preached to all; nor yet do they so discuss them that, wishing to lead them to an understanding not capacious enough, they more easily cause the discourse in the truth to be disdained than the truth in the discourse to be perceived.

Therefore he says, writing to the Colossians: *And though I am absent in body, yet I am with you in spirit, rejoicing and seeing your good order, and that which is lacking to your faith in Christ*[^12]. And to the Thessalonians: *Night and day,* he says, *praying more abundantly that we may see your face, and may supply what is lacking to your faith*[^13]. They are certainly to be understood as first so catechized that they were nourished with milk, not solid food: whose milk's abundance is recalled in the Epistle to the Hebrews to those whom he wished now to feed with the solidity of food. On account of which he says: *And so, leaving the word of the beginning of Christ, let us look toward perfection; not again laying the foundation of repentance from dead works, and of faith toward God, of the teaching of baptisms, and of the laying on of hands, of the resurrection also of the dead, and of eternal judgment*[^14]. This is the abundance of milk, without which they do not live who already indeed use reason so that they can believe, but cannot separate good from evil — not only by believing, but also by understanding (which belongs to solid food). But that in the mention of milk he also placed teaching, this is the teaching that is handed down through the creed and the Lord's prayer.

## Christ crucified is milk for those who suck and food for those who advance.

**6.** But far be it that to this milk the food — to be grasped by a firm understanding of spiritual things — should be contrary, which was lacking to the Colossians and Thessalonians, and was to be supplied. For when what was lacking is supplied, what was there is not disapproved. For even in the very nourishments which we take, solid food is so far from being contrary to milk that it itself turns to milk, so that it can be suited to infants, to whom it comes through the flesh of the mother or nurse: as wisdom herself, the mother, did, who, although she is solid food in the heights for the angels, deigned in a manner to turn to milk for the little ones, when *the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us*[^15]. But the man Christ himself, who by true flesh, true cross, true death, true resurrection is called the pure milk of the little ones, when he is rightly grasped by the spiritual, is found to be the Lord of the angels.

Accordingly, the little ones are not to be fed with milk in such a way that they never understand Christ to be God; nor to be weaned in such a way that they forsake the man Christ. Which in another way may be said thus: they are not to be fed with milk in such a way that they never understand Christ to be the Creator; nor to be weaned in such a way that they ever forsake Christ the mediator.

For in this the comparison of maternal milk and solid food does not suit this matter, but rather the comparison of a foundation: because even a child, when he is weaned, that he may now withdraw from the nourishments of infancy, does not, among solid foods, return to the breasts which he was sucking; but Christ crucified is both milk for those who suck and food for those who advance. But the comparison of a foundation is for this reason more apt, because, that what is built may be brought to completion, the building is added, the foundation is not taken away.

**7.** Since these things are so, O whoever you are who without doubt are many little ones in Christ, advance to the solid food of the mind, not of the belly. Advance to separating good from evil, and cleave more and more to the Mediator, through whom you are freed from evil; which is not to be separated from you by place, but rather to be healed in you. But whoever says to you: Do not believe that Christ is a true man, or that the body of any man or of any living thing was created by the true God, or that the Old Testament was given by the true God, and anything of this kind — for these things were for this reason not said to you before, when you were nourished with milk, because you did not yet have a heart fit to grasp the truth — that man is not preparing food for you, but poison.

On account of which the blessed Apostle, addressing those who already seemed to themselves to be perfect, when he had called himself imperfect: *As many of us, then,* he says, *as are perfect, let us be of this mind: and if you are of any other mind, this also God will reveal to you*[^16]. And lest perhaps they should fall in with seducers, who would wish to turn them from the faith by promising the knowledge of truth, and should think this to be what the Apostle said, *this also God will reveal to you,* he at once added: *Nevertheless, in that to which we have come, let us walk in it*[^17]. If, then, you understand anything that is not against the rule of the catholic faith, to which, as to a way that leads you to the homeland, you have come; and if you so understand it that you ought in no way to doubt it: add the building, yet do not leave the foundation.

So ought the grown to teach the little ones something, that they may not say that Christ, the Lord of all, and the Prophets and Apostles, far greater than themselves, lied in anything. But not only must you beware of vain talkers and seducers of the mind, prating fabulous and false things, and in those vanities promising, as it were, a deep knowledge against the rule of faith which you have received as catholic: you must beware also of those very men who discuss truly concerning the unchangeableness of the divine nature, or the incorporeal creature, or the Creator, and prove what they say altogether by most certain demonstrations and reasonings, and yet attempt to turn you away from the one Mediator of God and men — flee them as a plague more insidious than the rest. For such are those of whom the Apostle says: *That, knowing God, they did not glorify him as God*[^18]. For what does it profit to have a true understanding of the unchangeable good, for him who does not hold the one through whom he is freed from evil? Let the admonition of the most blessed Apostle by all means not depart from your hearts: *If anyone preaches to you a gospel beside that which you have received, let him be anathema*[^19]. He does not say, more than you have received; but, *beside that which you have received.* For if he said the former, he would prejudge himself, who desired to come to the Thessalonians to supply what was lacking to their faith. But he who supplies adds what was less, he does not take away what was there: but he who oversteps the rule of faith does not advance on the way, but withdraws from the way.

**8.** As for what the Lord says, then — *I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now* — the things they did not know were to be added to them, not the things they had learned to be overturned. And he indeed, as I have already explained in the previous sermon, could say this in this way: because those very things which he had taught, if he wished so to open them to them as they are conceived in him by the angels, the human weakness in which they still were could not bear it. But any spiritual man can teach another man what he knows, if the Holy Spirit, by his advancing, makes him more capacious — the Holy Spirit in whom the teacher himself too was able to learn something further, so that both may be taught by God. Although even among the spiritual themselves some are surely more capacious and better than others; so that a certain one of them came to those things which it is not lawful for a man to speak. On which occasion certain vain men, with most foolish presumption, feigned an Apocalypse of Paul, full of I know not what fables, which the sound Church does not receive; saying that this is the source whence he had said he was caught up into the third heaven, and heard there ineffable words *which it is not lawful for a man to speak*[^20]. However tolerable their boldness might be, if he had said that he heard things which it is not yet lawful for a man to speak: but since he said, *which it is not lawful for a man to speak,* who are these who dare to speak them impudently and unhappily?

But I will now conclude this sermon with this end; through which I desire you to be wise indeed in good, but uncorrupted from evil.
