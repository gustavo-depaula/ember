# Tractate 97

## *I still have many things to say to you*[^1].

That you may grow, and grasp him, and the more you grow the more and more you may grasp, you ought to ask and to hope, not from that teacher who sounds in your ears, that is, who by working from outside plants and waters, but from him who gives the increase.  
The more you grow, the more and more you will grasp.

**1.** The Holy Spirit, whom the Lord promised he would send to his disciples, who would teach them all truth, which then, when he was speaking with them, they could not bear: of which Holy Spirit, as the Apostle says, we have now received the *pledge*[^2], by which word we may understand that his fullness is reserved for us in the other life: that same Holy Spirit, then, both now teaches the faithful, as much of spiritual things as each one can grasp; and kindles their breasts with a greater desire, if each one advances in that love by which he both loves what is known and desires what is to be known: so that even those very things which now in some way he knows, he knows that he does not yet know as they are to be known in that life which *neither eye has seen, nor ear has heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man*[^3]. In which manner of knowing, if the inner master now wished to say these things, that is, to open them to our mind and show them, human weakness could not bear it. Whence your Charity remembers that I have already spoken, when we treated the words of the holy Gospel, where the Lord says, *I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now.* Not that in these words of the Lord we should suspect I know not what secrets too deeply hidden, which, though they could be said by the teacher, could not be borne by the learner: but those very things which in the doctrine of religion we read and write, hear and say, in the knowledge of any men whatever, if Christ wished to say to us in the manner in which he says them to the holy angels in himself, the only-begotten Word of the Father, coeternal with the Father — what men could bear them, even if they were already spiritual, such as the Apostles were not yet, when the Lord was speaking these things to them, and such as they afterward became when the Holy Spirit came?

For surely whatever can be known of the creature is less than the Creator himself, who is the highest and true and unchangeable God. And who is silent about him? Where is he not named — by readers, disputers, seekers, answerers, praisers, singers, by those who speak in any way at all, and finally even by those who blaspheme? And since no one is silent about him, who is there who grasps him as he is to be understood, when he does not depart from the mouths and ears of men? Who is there whose keen edge of mind reaches to him? Who is there who would have known him to be a Trinity, unless he himself had willed so to make himself known? And what man now keeps silent about that Trinity: and yet what man savors the Trinity as the angels do?

Those very things, then, which concerning God's eternity, truth, holiness are said openly and plainly without ceasing, are by some well understood, by others ill: nay, by some understood, by others not understood. For he who understands ill does not understand. But by those very persons by whom they are well understood, they are discerned by some less, by others more, according to the liveliness of mind, and by no man are they grasped as by the angels. In the mind itself, then, that is, in the inner man, there is a kind of growth, not only that one may pass from milk to food, but also that the food itself may be taken more and more. But the growth is not by spacious mass, but by luminous understanding; because the food itself is intelligible light.

That you may grow, then, and grasp him, and the more you grow, the more and more you may grasp; you ought to ask and to hope, not from that teacher who sounds in your ears, that is, who by working from outside plants and waters, but from him who gives the increase.

**2.** Accordingly, as I admonished in the previous sermon, beware — especially you who are little ones and still need milk nourishment — lest, men being deceived under this occasion, and being deceivers, because the Lord says, *I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now,* you lend a curious ear to learn unknown things, while you have minds too weak to judge between true and false: especially on account of the most obscene basenesses which Satan has taught to unstable and fleshly souls, God permitting this to the end that his judgments may everywhere be dreaded, and that in comparison with impure wickedness the most pure discipline may grow sweet; and that one may give honor to him, but fear or shame to himself, who under his ruling either did not fall into those evils, or, he lifting him up, rose from them.

Beware, by fearing and praying, lest you rush into that enigma of Solomon, where *the foolish and bold woman, having become destitute of bread,* calls together those passing by, saying: *Gladly partake of hidden loaves, and of the sweetness of stolen water*[^4]. For this woman is the vanity of the impious, who, though they are most foolish, suppose that they know something, as it was said of this woman, *having become destitute of bread.* She, though destitute of bread, promises loaves; that is, though ignorant of the truth, she promises the knowledge of the truth. Yet she promises hidden loaves, which she says are gladly partaken of, and the sweetness of stolen water; so that, namely, those things may be heard and done the more gladly and sweetly which are openly forbidden to be said and believed in the Church. For by that very concealment the nefarious teachers in a manner season their poisons for the curious; so that they think they are learning something great, because it has merited to have a secret, and so that they drink more sweetly the folly which they think knowledge, whose forbidden hearing they in a manner steal.

**3.** Hence too she commends her nefarious rites, by the teaching of the magic arts, to men deceived by sacrilegious curiosity or to be deceived. Hence those unlawful divinations, by inspecting the entrails of slaughtered beasts, or by the voices and flights of birds, or by the manifold signs of demons, are whispered into the ears of men about to perish, through the conversations of the lost. On account of these unlawful and punishable secrets, that woman is called not only *foolish,* but also *bold.* But these things are foreign not only to the reality, but also to the name of our religion. What of the fact that this foolish and bold woman, under the Christian name, has founded so many wicked heresies, has feigned so many nefarious fables? Would that they were such as are sung or danced or laughed at with mimic scurrility in the theaters; and not such as we so grieve that she could feign against God in that folly, that we wonder at her boldness! But all the most foolish heretics, who wish to be called Christians, attempt to color the audacities of their fabrications, which the human sense especially abhors, by the occasion of the Gospel saying, where the Lord says, *I still have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now*: as if these were the very things which the disciples could not then bear, and the Holy Spirit taught those things which to teach and preach openly, however great the boldness it bears, the unclean spirit blushes.

**4.** The Apostle, foreseeing these in the Holy Spirit, says: *For there will be a time when they will not endure sound doctrine, but according to their own desires they will heap up for themselves teachers, having itching ears: and they will turn away their hearing from the truth, but will be turned to fables*[^5]. For that mention of secret and theft, by which it is said, *Gladly partake of hidden loaves, and of the sweetness of stolen water,* makes an itching in the ears of those who hear, fornicating spiritually, just as by a certain itching of lust the integrity of chastity is corrupted even in the flesh. Hear, then, the Apostle foreseeing such things, and salutarily admonishing that they are to be avoided: *Avoid,* he says, *profane novelties of words: for they advance much toward impiety, and their speech spreads like a cancer*[^6]. And he does not say, novelties of words; but he added, *profane.* For there are also novelties of words agreeable to the doctrine of religion, as the very name of Christians, when it began to be said, is recorded. For in Antioch first, after the Lord's ascension, the disciples were called Christians, as it is read in the Acts of the Apostles: and guesthouses and monasteries were afterward called by new names, yet the things themselves existed even before their names, and are confirmed by the truth of religion, by which they are also defended against the wicked. Against the impiety of the Arian heretics too they founded the new name "Homoousion" of the Father: but they did not signify a new thing by such a name; for that is called "Homoousion" which is, *I and the Father are one*[^7], namely of one and the same substance. For if all novelty were profane, the Lord would not have said, *A new commandment I give to you*[^8]; nor would the Testament be called New, nor would a new Canticle be sung in all the earth. But profane are the novelties of words where *the foolish and bold woman* says, *Gladly partake of hidden loaves, and of the sweetness of stolen water.* From this promise of false knowledge the Apostle also forbids in that place where he says: *O Timothy, guard the deposit, avoiding the profane novelties of words, and the contradictions of falsely named knowledge, which some, professing, have fallen away concerning the faith*[^9]. For these men love nothing so much as to promise knowledge; and to deride, as inexperience, the faith of true things which the little ones are bidden to believe.

**5.** Someone will say: Do spiritual men have nothing in their doctrine which they keep silent from the fleshly, and speak to the spiritual? If I answer, They do not, at once it will be said to me from the Epistle of the apostle Paul to the Corinthians: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to fleshly. As to little ones in Christ, I gave you milk to drink, not food; for you were not yet able: but neither indeed are you yet able; for you are still fleshly*[^10]; and that, *We speak wisdom among the perfect*[^11]; and that, *Comparing spiritual things with spiritual: but the animal man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God; for it is foolishness to him*[^12]. What all this may be — lest again, on account of these words of the Apostle, secrets be sought with profane novelties of words, and the things which the spirit and body of the chaste ought to avoid be said to be such as the fleshly cannot bear — must be discussed in another sermon, if the Lord grants, so that we may now at last close this one.
