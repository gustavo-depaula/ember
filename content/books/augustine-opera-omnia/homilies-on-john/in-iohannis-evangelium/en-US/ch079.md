# Tractate 79

## *And now I have told you before it comes to pass, so that when it has come to pass you may believe*[^1].

## When that which he had foretold had come to pass in him, that faith which was small at the time he was speaking to them, and which when he was dying was almost none at all, both revived and grew.

**1.** Our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ had said to his disciples: *If you loved me, you would indeed rejoice, because I go to the Father, for the Father is greater than I*[^2]. That he said this from the form of a servant, not from the form of God in which he is equal to the Father, is known to the faith that is fixed in religious minds, not feigned by slanderous and demented ones.

Then he added: *And now I have told you before it comes to pass, so that when it has come to pass you may believe*[^3]. What is this, since a man ought rather to believe before that which is to be believed comes to pass? For this is the praise of faith, if what is believed is not seen.

For what great thing is it if what is seen is believed, according to that saying of the same Lord, when he rebuked the disciple, saying: *Because you have seen, you have believed; blessed are they who do not see and yet believe*[^4]? And I do not know whether anyone is to be said to believe what he sees; for faith itself, in the Epistle that is written to the Hebrews, is thus defined: *Now faith is the substance of things hoped for, the conviction of things that are not seen*[^5]. Wherefore, if faith is of the things that are believed, and this same faith is of the things that are not seen, what does the Lord mean when he says: *And now I have told you before it comes to pass, so that when it has come to pass you may believe*[^6]? Should it not rather have been said: And now I have told you before it comes to pass, so that you may believe, that when it has come to pass you may see? For even he to whom it was said, *Because you have seen, you have believed,* did not believe what he saw; but he saw one thing and believed another: for he saw the man, he believed the God. He beheld and touched the living flesh which he had seen dying; and he believed the God hidden in that very flesh. He believed, then, in mind what he did not see, through that which appeared to the senses of the body. But even if the things that are seen are said to be believed — as each one says he has believed his own eyes — yet this is not the faith that is built up in us; rather, from the things that are seen, it is brought about in us that those things are believed which are not seen.

Wherefore, dearly beloved — for now my discourse is on this, that the Lord says, *And now I have told you before it comes to pass, so that when it has come to pass you may believe*[^7] — by *when it has come to pass* he certainly means this: that they were going to see him living after death and ascending to the Father; and having seen this, they were going to believe that he himself was Christ the Son of the living God, who was able to do this when he had foretold it, and to foretell it before he did it. But they were going to believe this not with a new faith, but with an increased one; or rather, with a faith that, when he had died, had failed, and when he had risen again was restored. For they did believe him to be the Son of God even before; but when that which he had foretold had come to pass in him, that faith which was small at the time he was speaking to them, and which when he was dying was almost none at all, both revived and grew.

**2.** Then what does he say? *I will no longer speak much with you; for the prince of this world is coming*[^8]. Who but the devil? *And in me he has nothing* — that is, no sin at all. For thus he shows that the devil is the prince, not of creatures, but of sinners, whom he now calls by the name of this world. And as often as the name of the world is put in an evil signification, it indicates none but the lovers of this world; of whom it is written elsewhere: *Whoever wishes to be a friend of this world is made an enemy of God*[^9]. Far be it, then, that the devil should be understood as prince of the world in such a way that he holds the principality of the whole world, that is, of heaven and earth and all that is in them — the world of which it was said, when the discourse was about Christ the Word: *And the world was made through him*[^10]. The whole world, therefore, from the highest heavens down to the lowest earth, is subject to its creator, not to a deserter; to its redeemer, not to its destroyer; to its liberator, not to its captor; to its teacher, not to its deceiver.

But in what way the devil is to be understood as prince of the world, the apostle Paul more clearly disclosed when, after he had said, *Our wrestling is not against flesh and blood,* that is, against men, he added, saying, *but against the principalities and powers and rulers of this world of darkness*[^11]. For by the following word he explained what he had said, *of the world,* when he added, *of this darkness*: lest anyone understand by the name of the world the whole creation, of which the deserter angels are in no way rulers. *Of this darkness,* he says — that is, of the lovers of this world; from among whom, however, those have been chosen, not by their own merit but by the grace of God, to whom he says: *For you were once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^12]. For all were under the rulers of this darkness, that is, of impious men, as darkness under darkness; but *thanks be to God, who has rescued us,* as the same apostle says, *from the power of darkness, and has transferred us into the kingdom of the Son of his love*[^13]. In him the prince of this world — that is, of this darkness — had nothing; because God had not come with sin, nor had the Virgin given birth to his flesh from the propagation of sin.

And as if it were said to him, Why then do you die, if you have no sin for which the penalty of death is owed? he immediately added: *But that the world may know that I love the Father, and as the Father has given me commandment, so I do; arise, let us go from here*[^14]. For he was speaking, as he reclined, to those reclining with him. But where did he say *let us go,* if not to that place from which he was to be handed over to death — he who had no merit of death? But he had, in order that he might die, the commandment of the Father, as the one of whom it had been foretold: *What I did not seize, then I was paying back*[^15] — about to pay death without owing it, and to redeem us from death that was owed. But Adam had seized sin when, deceived by presumption, he stretched out his hand to the tree, that he might invade the incommunicable name of an unconceded divinity, which nature had conferred on the Son of God, not robbery.
