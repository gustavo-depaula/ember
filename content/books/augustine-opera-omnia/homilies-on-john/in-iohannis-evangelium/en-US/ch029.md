# Tractate 29

## *My teaching is not mine, but his who sent me* (Jn 7:14-18).

Do you wish to understand? Believe. For understanding is the reward of faith. What is it, then, to believe in him? By believing, to love him; by believing, to cherish him; by believing, to go into him and be incorporated into his members. This, then, is the very faith that God requires of us; and he does not find what he requires unless he has given what he is to find.

**1.** Let us in turn consider what follows in the Gospel and was read today; and what the Lord grants, let us say on the matter. Yesterday the reading reached this point: that although they had not seen the Lord Jesus in the temple during the feast, they were nevertheless speaking of him: *And some said, He is good; others, however, No, but he is leading the crowds astray*[^1]. For this was said for the consolation of those who, afterward preaching the word of God, were going to be regarded as deceivers though truthful[^2]. For if to lead astray is to deceive, then neither was Christ a deceiver, nor his apostles, nor ought any Christian to be a deceiver; but if to lead astray is to lead someone from one place to another by persuasion, we must ask from where and to where: if from evil to good, he is a good leader-astray; if from good to evil, he is a bad leader-astray. In this sense, then, in which people are led astray from evil to good, would that we were all both called and were leaders-astray!

**2.** The Lord, then, went up afterward to the feast, in the middle of the feast, and taught. *And the Jews marveled, saying, How does this man know letters, since he has not studied?*[^3] He who was hiding was teaching; and he spoke openly, and was not seized. For that he was hiding was a matter of example, this of power. But when he taught, the Jews marveled. All, as I judge, marveled; but not all were converted.

And whence the wonder?

Because many knew where he had been born and how he had been brought up; they had never seen him learning letters, yet they heard him disputing about the Law, bringing forward testimonies of the Law that no one could bring forward unless he had read them, and no one could read unless he had learned letters; and therefore they marveled.

But their wonder became, for the Master, an occasion for instilling a deeper truth. From their wonder and their words the Lord said something profound, worthy of being examined and discussed more diligently. For this reason I make your Charity attentive, not only to listen for your own sakes, but also to pray for us.

## What is the teaching of the Father, except the Word of the Father?

**3.** What, then, did the Lord answer them as they marveled how he knew the letters he had not learned? *My*, he says, *teaching is not mine, but his who sent me*[^4]. This is the first depth: for in few words he seems to have said, as it were, contrary things. For he did not say, This teaching is not mine, but, My teaching is not mine. If not yours, how is it yours? If yours, how is it not yours? For you yourself say both: my teaching, and not mine.

For if he had said, This teaching is not mine, there would be no question. But as it is, brothers, first attend to the question, and then in order await the solution. For one who does not see the question that is posed, how does he understand what is set forth? This, then, is in the question, that he says, mine, not mine: this seems to be contrary—how mine, how not mine.

If, then, we look diligently at what the holy Evangelist himself says at the outset: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^5]—from there hangs the solution of this question. What, then, is the teaching of the Father, except the Word of the Father? Christ himself, then, is the teaching of the Father, if he is the Word of the Father. But since the Word cannot be no one's, but someone's, he called the teaching both his own—himself—and not his own, because it is the Word of the Father. For what is so much yours as you yourself? And what is so much not yours as you yourself, if what you are belongs to someone else?

**4.** The Word, then, is both God and the Word of an abiding teaching—not a teaching that can be sounded out through syllables and that flies away, but one abiding with the Father, to which abiding teaching we are to be turned, admonished by passing sounds. For that which passes does not so admonish us as to call us to passing things. We are admonished to love God. All that I have said was syllables; they struck and beat the air, that they might reach the sense of your ears, and in sounding they passed away; yet that to which I admonished you ought not to pass away, because he whom I admonished you to love does not pass away; and when, admonished by the passing syllables, you have been turned to him, you too will not pass away, but will abide with the one who abides.

This, then, is what is great in the teaching: the high and eternal thing that abides, to which call all things that pass away in time, when they signify well and are not uttered falsely. For all the signs that we utter by sounds signify something that is not a sound. For the two short syllables "God" are not God, and we worship two short syllables, and we adore two short syllables, and we desire to come to two short syllables—syllables that almost cease to sound before they have begun, and in which there is no place for the second unless the first has passed away. There abides, then, something great that is called God, although the sound does not abide when "God" is said. Attend thus to the teaching of Christ, and you will come to the Word of God; and when you have come to the Word of God, attend: *God was the Word*[^6]; and you will see that it was truly said, my teaching; attend also whose Word it is, and you will see that it was rightly said, it is not mine.

**5.** Briefly, then, I say to your Charity: this is what the Lord Jesus Christ seems to me to have said: *My teaching is not mine*—as if he were saying, I am not from myself. For although we say and believe that the Son is equal to the Father, and that there is no distance of nature or substance between them, and that no interval of time came between the one begetting and the one begotten, yet we say these things keeping and preserving this: that the one is the Father, the other the Son. But the Father is not, if he does not have a Son; and the Son is not, if he does not have a Father: yet the Son is God from the Father, while the Father is God, but not from the Son. The Father of the Son, not God from the Son; but the Son of the Father, and God from the Father. For the Lord Christ is called Light from Light. The Light, then, that is not from light, and the equal Light that is from Light, together are one Light, not two lights.

## Do you wish to understand? Believe.

**6.** If we have understood, thanks be to God; but if anyone has understood too little, the man has done what he could, and let him look for the rest from where he may hope for it. Outwardly, as workers, we can plant and water, but it is God's to give the increase[^7]. *My*, he says, *teaching is not mine, but his who sent me*. Let him hear counsel who says, I have not yet understood. For when a great and profound thing had been said, the Lord Christ himself surely saw that not all would understand something so profound, and in what followed he gave counsel. Do you wish to understand? Believe. For God said through the prophet, *Unless you believe, you will not understand*[^8]. To this pertains what the Lord here added in what follows: *If anyone wishes to do his will, he will know concerning the teaching, whether it is from God, or whether I speak from myself*[^9].

What is this: *If anyone wishes to do his will?* But I had said, If anyone believes; and this counsel I had given. If you have not understood, I say, believe. For understanding is the reward of faith. Therefore do not seek to understand in order to believe, but believe in order to understand; since unless you believe, you will not understand. Since, then, I gave as counsel toward the possibility of understanding the obedience of believing, and said that the Lord Jesus Christ added this very thing in the sentence that follows, we find that he said, *If anyone wishes to do his will, he will know concerning the teaching*.

What is, he will know? This is, he will understand. And what is, *If anyone wishes to do his will*, this is to believe. But because everyone understands that he will know, that is, he will understand, while the fact that what he says, *If anyone wishes to do his will*, pertains to believing needs to be understood more diligently, we have need of our Lord himself as expositor, that he may show us whether to do the will of his Father truly pertains to believing. Who does not know that this is to do the will of God: to work his work, that is, what pleases him? But the Lord himself plainly says in another place: *This is the work of God, that you believe in him whom he sent*[^10]. That you believe in him; not, that you believe him. But if you believe in him, you believe him: yet not at once does the one who believes him believe in him. For the demons also believed him, and did not believe in him. Again, even of his apostles we can say: We believe Paul; but not: We believe in Paul. We believe Peter; but not: We believe in Peter.

For to the one who believes in him who justifies the ungodly, *his faith is reckoned as righteousness*[^11]. What is it, then, to believe in him? By believing, to love him; by believing, to cherish him; by believing, to go into him and be incorporated into his members. This, then, is the very faith that God requires of us; and he does not find what he requires unless he has given what he is to find. And what faith, except the one the Apostle defined most fully in another place when he said: *Neither circumcision avails anything, nor uncircumcision, but faith that works through love*[^12]? Not any sort of faith, but faith that works through love: let this be in you, and you will understand concerning the teaching. For what will you understand? That this teaching is not mine, but his who sent me[^13]: that is, you will understand that Christ the Son of God, who is the teaching of the Father, is not from himself, but is the Son of the Father.

## The Sabellian heresy.

**7.** This statement dissolves the Sabellian heresy. For the Sabellians dared to say that the Son himself is also the Father; that there are two names, but one thing. If there were two names and one thing, it would not be said, *My teaching is not mine*. Surely, if your teaching is not yours, O Lord, whose is it, unless there be another whose it is? What you said the Sabellians do not understand: for they did not see the Trinity, but followed the error of their own heart.

Let us, worshippers of the Trinity and of the unity of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and of the one God, understand concerning the teaching of Christ that it is not his. And therefore he said that he does not speak from himself, since Christ is the Son of the Father, and the Father of Christ is the Father, and the Son is God from God the Father; but the Father is God, not God from God the Son.

**8.** *He who speaks from himself seeks his own glory*[^14]. This will be the one who is called Antichrist, exalting himself, as the Apostle says, *above everything that is called God, and that is worshiped*[^15]. For the Lord, announcing that this very one would seek his own glory, not the glory of the Father, says to the Jews: *I have come in the name of my Father, and you did not receive me; another will come in his own name, him you will receive*[^16]. He signified that they would receive Antichrist, who is going to seek the glory of his own name, puffed up, not solid; and therefore not stable, but altogether ready to fall.

But our Lord Jesus Christ gave us a great example of humility: for he is equal to the Father, surely *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^17]; surely he himself said, and said most truly, *Have I been so long a time with you, and you have not known me? Philip, he who has seen me has also seen the Father*[^18]; surely he himself said, and said most truly, *I and the Father are one*[^19]. If, then, he is one with the Father, equal to the Father, God from God, God with God, coeternal, immortal, equally unchangeable, equally without time, equally creator and orderer of times; yet because he came in time, and took the form of a servant, and was found in fashion as a man[^20], he seeks the glory of the Father, not his own: what ought you, O man, to do, who, when you do something good, seek your own glory, but when you do something evil, devise a calumny against God?

Attend to yourself; you are a creature, acknowledge the Creator; you are a servant, do not despise the Lord; you have been adopted, but not by your own merits; seek the glory of him from whom you have this grace, O man adopted, whose glory was sought by the one who is born from him as the only Son. *But he who seeks the glory of him who sent him, this one is truthful, and there is no unrighteousness in him*[^21]. But in Antichrist there is unrighteousness, and he is not truthful; because he is going to seek his own glory, not the glory of him by whom he was sent: for he was not sent, but permitted to come. Let us all, then, who belong to the body of Christ, that we may not be led into the snares of Antichrist, not seek our own glory. But if he sought the glory of him who sent him, how much more should we seek the glory of him who made us?
