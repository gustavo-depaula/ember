# Homily 26

## The bread that I will give is my flesh for the life of the world (Jn 6:41-51).

## O sacrament of piety! O sign of unity! O bond of love! He who wishes to live has where to live, has from what to live. Let him draw near, let him believe, let him be incorporated, that he may be given life.

**1.** When our Lord Jesus Christ, as we heard when the Gospel was read, had said that he is the bread that came down from heaven, the Jews murmured and said: *Is not this Jesus the son of Joseph, whose father and mother we know? How then does this man say: I came down from heaven?*[^1] These men were far from the bread from heaven, and did not know how to hunger for it. They had a sick gullet of the heart, with open ears they were deaf, they saw and stood blind. For this bread requires the hunger of the inner man: whence in another place he says, *Blessed are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness, for they will be satisfied*[^2]. And that Christ is righteousness for us, the apostle Paul says[^3]. And so let him who hungers for this bread hunger for righteousness; but the righteousness that comes down from heaven, the righteousness that God gives, not that which a man makes for himself. For if a man made no righteousness for himself, the same apostle would not say of the Jews: *For being ignorant of God's righteousness, and wishing to establish their own, they have not submitted to the righteousness of God*[^4]. Of that sort were these who did not understand the bread coming down from heaven, because, satisfied with their own righteousness, they did not hunger for the righteousness of God. What is this, the righteousness of God and the righteousness of man? The righteousness of God is here so called, not that by which God is righteous, but that which God gives to a man, that a man may be righteous through God. But what was their righteousness? That by which they presumed upon their own strength, and called themselves fulfillers of the Law out of their own power. But no one fulfills the Law except him whom grace has helped, that is, the bread that came down from heaven. For the fullness of the Law, in sum, as the Apostle says, is love[^5]: love not of money, but of God; love not of earth, not of heaven, but of him who made heaven and earth. From where comes this love to a man? Let us hear him himself: *The love of God*, he says, *has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^6]. About to give the Holy Spirit, then, the Lord said he is the bread that came down from heaven, exhorting us to believe in him. For to believe in him is to eat the living bread. He who believes eats; invisibly he is fattened, because invisibly he is reborn. He is an infant within, he is new within: where he is made new, there he is satisfied.  
From the root of the heart rises confession unto salvation. 2. What, then, did Jesus answer to such murmurers? *Do not murmur among yourselves.* As though saying: I know why you do not hunger, and do not understand or seek this bread. *Do not murmur among yourselves: no one can come to me, unless the Father who sent me draws him*[^7]. A great commendation of grace! No one comes unless drawn. Whom he draws and whom he does not draw, why he draws this one and does not draw that one, do not wish to judge, if you do not wish to err. Once for all accept it, and understand: are you not yet drawn? Pray that you may be drawn. What do we say here, brothers? If we are drawn to Christ, then we believe unwillingly; then force is applied, the will is not aroused. Someone can enter the church unwilling, can approach the altar unwilling, can receive the Sacrament unwilling: he cannot believe except willing. If one believed with the body, it would happen in the unwilling: but one does not believe with the body. Hear the Apostle: *With the heart one believes unto righteousness.* And what follows? *But with the mouth confession is made unto salvation*[^8]. From the root of the heart rises this confession. Sometimes you hear one confessing, and do not know whether he believes. But you ought not to call one a confessor whom you judge not to believe. For this is to confess—to say what you have in your heart: but if you have one thing in your heart, and say another, you are speaking, not confessing. Since, then, one believes in Christ with the heart, which surely no one does unwilling, but he who is drawn seems to be compelled as it were unwilling; how do we resolve this question: *No one comes to me unless the Father who sent me draws him*?  
**3.** If he is drawn, says someone, he comes unwilling. If he comes unwilling, he does not believe; if he does not believe, he does not come either. For we do not run to Christ by walking, but by believing; nor do we draw near by a movement of the body, but by the will of the heart. Therefore that woman who touched the fringe touched more than the crowd that pressed. Therefore the Lord said: *Who touched me?* And the disciples, marveling, said: *The crowds press you, and you say: Who touched me?* And he repeated: *Someone touched me*[^9]. She touches, the crowd presses. What is, she touched, except she believed? Whence also he said to that woman after the resurrection, when she wished to throw herself at his feet: *Do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to the Father*[^10]. What you see, you think that I am this alone; do not touch me. What does this mean? You think that I am only this which I appear to you; do not believe so: this is, *Do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to the Father*; for you I have not ascended, for from there I have never departed. On earth she did not touch him standing; how would she touch him ascending to the Father? Yet so, so he willed to be touched: so he is touched by those by whom he is touched well—ascending to the Father, remaining with the Father, equal to the Father.  
Each one is drawn by his own delight. 4. So here too, if you observe: *No one comes to me unless the Father draws him.* Do not think you are drawn unwilling: the mind is drawn also by love. Nor ought we to fear that we may perhaps be reproached in this evangelical word of the holy Scriptures by men who weigh words, and are far removed from understanding things especially divine, and that it be said to us: How do I believe by the will, if I am drawn? I say: it is too little to be drawn by the will, you are drawn even by delight. What is it to be drawn by delight? *Delight in the Lord, and he will give you the petitions of your heart*[^11]. There is a certain delight of the heart, to which that heavenly bread is sweet. Moreover, if it was lawful for the poet to say, *Each one is drawn by his own delight*[^12]—not necessity, but delight; not obligation, but pleasure—how much more strongly ought we to say that a man is drawn to Christ who delights in truth, delights in blessedness, delights in righteousness, delights in everlasting life, all of which Christ is? Or do the senses of the body have their delights, and is the mind left without its delights? If the mind has no delights of its own, from where is it said: *But the sons of men will hope under the covering of your wings: they will be inebriated from the abundance of your house, and you will give them to drink from the torrent of your delight; for with you is the fountain of life, and in your light we shall see light*[^13]? Give me one who loves, and he perceives what I say. Give me one who desires, give me one who hungers, give me one journeying in this wilderness and thirsting, and sighing for the fountain of the eternal homeland: give me such a one, and he knows what I say. But if I speak to a cold man, he does not know what I am saying. Such were these who murmured among themselves. *The Father*, he says, *whom he draws, comes to me.*  
That revelation is itself the drawing. 5. But what is: *The Father, whom he draws*, when Christ himself draws? Why did he wish to say: *The Father, whom he draws*? If we are to be drawn, let us be drawn by him to whom one says—she who loves certain things: *After the fragrance of your ointments we will run*[^14]. But let us notice, brothers, what he wished to be understood, and let us grasp it as far as we can. The Father draws to the Son those who believe in the Son because they think that he has God as his Father: for God the Father begot a Son equal to himself: so that whoever thinks, and in his faith perceives and ponders, that the one in whom he believed is equal to the Father, that one the Father draws to the Son. Arius believed him a creature, the Father did not draw him; because he does not consider the Father who does not believe the Son equal. What do you say, O Arius? What, heretic, do you say? What is Christ? Not, he says, true God; but one whom the true God made. The Father did not draw you: for you did not understand the Father, whose Son you deny: you think of something else, it is not the Son himself; you are neither drawn by the Father, nor drawn to the Son: for the Son is one thing, and what you say is another. Photinus said: Christ is only a man, he is not also God. He who believes thus, the Father did not draw him. He whom the Father drew said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.* Not as a prophet, not as John, not as some great righteous man; but as the only Son, as the equal one, you are the Christ, the Son of the living God. See that he was drawn, and drawn by the Father. *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jona, because flesh and blood did not reveal it to you, but my Father who is in heaven*[^15]. That revelation is itself the drawing. You show a green branch to a sheep, and you draw it. Nuts are shown to a child, and he is drawn: he is drawn to where he runs, drawn by loving, drawn without injury to the body, drawn by a bond of the heart. If, then, these things which are revealed to lovers among earthly delights and pleasures draw them—since it is true, "Each one is drawn by his own delight"—does not Christ, revealed by the Father, draw? For what does the soul more strongly desire than truth? For what ought it to have greedy jaws, for what to wish that the palate within be sound for judging true things, except that it may eat and drink wisdom, righteousness, truth, eternity?  
**6.** But where is this? There it is better, there truer, there fuller. For here we can more easily hunger—and this if we have good hope—than be satisfied: *For blessed*, he says, *are those who hunger and thirst for righteousness*—but here; *for they will be satisfied*[^16]—but there. Therefore, when he had said: *No one comes to me unless the Father who sent me draws him*, what did he add? *And I will raise him up on the last day*[^17]. I give back to him what he loves, I give back what he hopes for: he will see what so far, without seeing, he believed; he will eat what he hungers for, he will be satisfied with that which he thirsts for. Where? In the resurrection of the dead, because *I will raise him up on the last day*.  
To draw is God's work. 7. For it is written in the Prophets: *And they shall all be taught by God*[^18]. Why did I say this, O Jews? The Father has not taught you; how can you recognize me? All the men of that kingdom will be taught by God, they will not hear from men. And even if they hear from men, yet what they understand is given within, flashes within, is revealed within. What do men do who announce from without? What do I do now when I speak? I bring the din of words to your ears. Unless, then, he who is within reveals it, what do I say, or what do I speak? The cultivator of a tree is outside, the Creator is within. He who plants and he who waters works from without: this we do. But neither is he who plants anything, nor he who waters; but God who gives the increase[^19]: this is, *They shall all be taught by God*. Who, all? *Everyone who has heard from the Father and has learned comes to me*[^20]. See how the Father draws: by teaching he delights, not by imposing necessity. See how he draws. *They shall all be taught by God*: to draw is God's work. *Everyone who has heard from the Father and has learned comes to me*: to draw is God's work.  
**8.** What then, brothers? If everyone who has heard from the Father and has learned comes to Christ, did Christ teach nothing here? What of the fact that men did not see the Father as their teacher, but saw the Son? The Son spoke, but the Father taught. I, since I am a man, whom do I teach? Whom, brothers, except him who hears my word? If I, since I am a man, teach him who hears my word, the Father too teaches him who hears his Word: if the Father teaches him who hears his Word; seek what Christ is, and you will find his Word: *In the beginning was the Word*. Not: In the beginning God made the Word; as: *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^21]: see, then, that he is not a creature. Learn to be drawn to the Son by the Father; let the Father teach you, hear his Word. What Word of his, you say, do I hear? *In the beginning was the Word*; he was not made, but he was: *And the Word was with God, and the Word was God.* How shall men set in the flesh hear such a Word? Because *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^22].  
**9.** He himself expounds this too, and shows us what he said: *He who has heard from the Father and has learned comes to me.* He at once added what we might think: *Not that anyone has seen the Father, except him who is from God; he has seen the Father*[^23]. What does he mean? I have seen the Father, you have not seen the Father; and yet you do not come to me, unless you are drawn by the Father. But what is it for you to be drawn by the Father, except to learn from the Father? What is it to learn from the Father, except to hear from the Father? What is it to hear from the Father, except to hear the Word of the Father, that is, me? Lest then, perhaps, when I say to you: *Everyone who has heard from the Father and has learned*, you say among yourselves: But we have never seen the Father; how could we learn from the Father? hear from me: *Not that anyone has seen the Father, but he who is from God; he has seen the Father.* I know the Father, I am from him: but as a word is from him whose word it is; not the word that sounds and passes, but the word that abides with the speaker, and draws the hearer.  
**10.** Let what follows admonish us: *Amen, amen, I say to you, he who believes in me has eternal life*[^24]. He wished to reveal what he was: for in sum he could have said: He who believes in me has me. For Christ himself is true God and eternal life. He who believes in me, then, he says, goes into me; and he who goes into me has me. But what is it to have me? To have eternal life. Eternal life took up death, eternal life willed to die; but from yours, not from its own: it received from you that wherein it might die for you. For from men it took up flesh, but not in the manner of men. For, having a Father in heaven, he chose a mother on earth: and there born without a mother, and here without a father. Life took up death, then, that life might slay death. For he who believes in me, he says, has eternal life: not what is open, but what is hidden. For eternal life is the Word: *In the beginning it was with God, and the Word was God, and the life was the light of men*[^25]. He, eternal life, gave eternal life also to the flesh he took up. He came to die, but on the third day he rose again. Between the Word taking up and the flesh rising again, death in the middle was consumed.  
**11.** *I am*, he says, *the bread of life.* And from where did they grow proud? *Your fathers*, he says, *ate manna in the desert, and died*[^26]. What is it of which you grow proud? They ate manna, and died. Why did they eat, and die? Because what they saw, they believed: what they did not see, they did not understand. Therefore *your* fathers, because you are like them. For as far as concerns this visible and bodily death, brothers, do we not die who eat the bread coming down from heaven? Those men died just as we are going to die; as far as concerns, as I said, the visible and carnal death of this body. But as far as concerns that death of which the Lord gives warning, by which the fathers of these men died: Moses too ate manna, Aaron too ate manna, Phinehas too ate manna, many ate there who pleased the Lord, and they did not die. Why? Because they understood the visible food spiritually, hungered spiritually, tasted spiritually, that they might be satisfied spiritually. For we too today receive visible food: but the Sacrament is one thing, the power of the Sacrament another. How many receive from the altar and die, and by receiving die? Whence the Apostle says: *He eats and drinks judgment to himself*[^27]. For the Lord's morsel was not poison to Judas. And yet he received it, and when he received it, the enemy entered into him: not because he received an evil thing, but because, evil, he received a good thing evilly. See, then, brothers, eat the heavenly bread spiritually, bring innocence to the altar. Though sins are daily, at least let them not be deadly. Before you approach the altar, attend to what you say: *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^28]. If you forgive, it will be forgiven you: approach secure, it is bread, not poison. But see whether you forgive: for if you do not forgive, you lie, and you lie to him whom you do not deceive. You can lie to God, you cannot deceive God. He knows what you do. Within he sees you, within he examines you, within he inspects you, within he judges, within he either condemns or crowns. But the fathers of these men, that is, the evil fathers of the evil, the unbelieving fathers of the unbelieving, the murmuring fathers of the murmurers. For in nothing was that people said to have offended the Lord more than by murmuring against God. Therefore the Lord too, wishing to show them to be sons of such men, began with this against them: *Why do you murmur among yourselves*[^29], murmurers, sons of murmurers? Your fathers ate manna, and died: not because the manna was evil, but because they ate it evilly.  
**12.** *This is the bread that comes down from heaven*[^30]. The manna signified this bread, the altar of God signified this bread. Those were Sacraments: in their signs they are different; in the thing which is signified they are equal. Hear the Apostle: *For I do not wish you to be ignorant, brothers, that our fathers were all under the cloud, and all crossed the sea, and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea, and all ate the same spiritual food*[^31]. The same spiritual food indeed; for a different bodily food, because they had manna, we have something else: but the same spiritual food as we. But our fathers, not the fathers of those men: to whom we are like, not to whom those men were like. And he adds: *And all drank the same spiritual drink.* They one thing, we another; but in visible appearance, which yet signified this same thing in spiritual power. For how the same drink? *They drank*, he says, *of the spiritual rock following them: and the rock was Christ*[^32]. From there the bread, from there the drink. The rock was Christ in sign, the true Christ in the Word and in the flesh. And how did they drink? *The rock was struck with the rod twice*[^33]: the double striking signifies the two woods of the cross. This, then, is the bread coming down from heaven, that if anyone eats of it, he may not die. But what pertains to the power of the Sacrament, not what pertains to the visible Sacrament: he who eats within, not without; he who eats in the heart, not he who presses with the tooth.  
None lives by the Spirit of Christ except the body of Christ. 13. *I am the living bread, who came down from heaven.* Living for this reason, because I came down from heaven. Manna too came down from heaven: but manna was a shadow, this is the truth. *If anyone eats of this bread, he will live forever: and the bread that I will give is my flesh for the life of the world*[^34]. When would the flesh grasp what he called bread, flesh? It is called flesh, what flesh does not grasp: and therefore the more flesh does not grasp it, because it is called flesh. For this they shrank from, this they said was too much for them, this they thought could not be done. *My flesh*, he says, *is for the life of the world.* The faithful know the body of Christ, if they do not neglect to be the body of Christ. Let them become the body of Christ, if they wish to live by the Spirit of Christ. None lives by the Spirit of Christ except the body of Christ. Understand, my brothers, what I have said. You are a man, and you have a spirit, and you have a body. I call spirit that which is called the soul, by which it stands that you are a man: for you stand from soul and body. You have, then, an invisible spirit, a visible body. Tell me which lives from which: does your spirit live from your body, or your body from your spirit? Everyone who lives answers: but he who cannot answer this, I do not know whether he lives: what does everyone who lives answer? My body, surely, lives from my spirit. Do you too, then, wish to live by the Spirit of Christ? Be in the body of Christ. For does my body live by your spirit? Mine lives by my spirit, and yours by yours. The body of Christ cannot live except by the Spirit of Christ. Hence it is that the apostle Paul, expounding this bread to us, says: *One bread, one body, are we many*[^35]. O sacrament of piety! O sign of unity! O bond of love! He who wishes to live has where to live, has from what to live. Let him draw near, let him believe that he may be incorporated, that he may be given life. Let him not shrink from the framework of the members, let him not be a rotten member that deserves to be cut off, not a deformed one to be ashamed of: let him be beautiful, let him be fit, let him be sound; let him cling to the body, let him live for God by God: let him now labor on earth, that afterward he may reign in heaven.  
**14.** The Jews, then, disputed among themselves, saying: *How can this man give us his flesh to eat?*[^36] They disputed among themselves, since they did not understand the bread of concord, nor wished to take it: for those who eat such bread do not dispute among themselves; since *one bread, one body, are we many*[^37]. And through this God makes those of one mind to dwell in a house[^38].  
**15.** But what they seek, disputing among themselves—how the Lord can give his flesh to eat—they do not hear at once: but it is still said to them: *Amen, amen, I say to you, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man, and drink his blood, you will not have life in you.* How indeed it may be eaten, and what the manner of eating this bread may be, you do not know; nevertheless, unless you eat the flesh of the Son of man, and drink his blood, you will not have life in you. He spoke these things, surely, not to corpses, but to the living. Whence, lest they, understanding this life and disputing about this matter, he went on and added: *He who eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, has eternal life*[^39]. He, then, does not have this who does not eat this bread, nor drink this blood: for men can have temporal life without it, but eternal life they absolutely cannot. He, then, who does not eat his flesh, nor drink his blood, has not life in him: and he who eats his flesh, and drinks his blood, has life. And to both he answers by what he said, eternal. It is not so in this food, which we take for the sake of sustaining this temporal life. For he who does not take it will not live: nor yet will he who takes it live forever. For it can happen that very many, even of those who take it, die through old age, or disease, or some mishap. But in this food and drink, that is, the body and blood of the Lord, it is not so. For both he who does not take it does not have life: and he who takes it has life, and this surely eternal. And so by this food and drink he wishes the fellowship of his body and members to be understood, which is the holy Church in the predestined and called, and justified, and glorified saints, and his faithful ones. Of these the first is already done, that is, predestination: the second and third are done, and are being done, and will be done, that is, calling and justification: but the fourth is now in hope, and in reality will be in the future, that is, glorification. The Sacrament of this thing, that is, of the unity of the body and blood of Christ, is prepared in some places daily, in some places at fixed intervals of days, on the Lord's table, and is taken from the Lord's table: for some unto life, for some unto destruction: but the thing itself, of which it is the sacrament, is for every man unto life, for none unto destruction, whoever has been a partaker of it.  
**16.** But lest they should think that eternal life is so promised in this food and drink that those who take it would not even die in body; he deigned to meet this thought. For when he had said: *He who eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, has eternal life*; he at once added: *And I will raise him up on the last day*[^40]. That meanwhile, according to the spirit, he may have eternal life in the rest which receives the spirits of the saints: but as far as concerns the body, neither shall it be defrauded of eternal life, but at the resurrection of the dead on the last day.  
The fellowship of the saints. 17. *For my flesh*, he says, *is truly food, and my blood is truly drink*[^41]. For since by food and drink men seek this—that they may not hunger or thirst—this is truly furnished only by that food and drink which makes those by whom it is taken immortal and incorruptible, that is, the very fellowship of the saints, where there will be peace and unity full and perfect. For this reason indeed, as men of God understood this even before us, our Lord Jesus Christ commended his body and blood in those things which are reduced to some one thing out of many. For one thing is made into one out of many grains: another flows together into one out of many berries.  
**18.** In short, he now expounds how that comes about which he says, and what it is to eat his body, and to drink his blood. *He who eats my flesh, and drinks my blood, abides in me, and I in him*[^42]. This, then, is to eat that food and to drink that drink: to abide in Christ, and to have him abiding in oneself. And through this, he who does not abide in Christ, and in whom Christ does not abide, without doubt neither eats his flesh, nor drinks his blood, but rather eats and drinks the Sacrament of so great a thing unto judgment to himself, because, unclean, he presumed to approach the Sacraments of Christ, which no one takes worthily except one who is clean; of whom it is said: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God*[^43].  
**19.** *As*, he says, *the living Father sent me, and I live because of the Father; and he who eats me, he too will live because of me*[^44]. He did not say: As I eat the Father, and I live because of the Father; and he who eats me, he too will live because of me. For the Son does not become better by participation in the Father, who was born equal; just as by participation in the Son, through the unity of his body and blood, which that eating and drinking signifies, we are made better. We live, then, because of him, by eating him; that is, by receiving him, eternal life, which we did not have of ourselves: but he himself lives because of the Father, sent by him; because he emptied himself, having become obedient unto the death of the cross[^45]. For if we take it according to that which he says elsewhere: *The Father is greater than I*[^46]—just as we too live because of him who is greater than us: this came about from the fact that he was sent. For his being sent is the emptying of himself, and the taking of the form of a servant: which is rightly understood, the equality of the Son with the Father in nature being also preserved. For the Father is greater than the man, the son; but he has the Son as an equal God: since the same one is both God and man, the Son of God and the son of man, one Christ Jesus. In which sense, if these words are rightly taken, he so spoke: *As the living Father sent me, and I live because of the Father; and he who eats me, he too will live because of me*: as if he said: That I live because of the Father, that is, that I refer my life to him as the greater, my emptying brought it about, in which he sent me; but that anyone lives because of me, the participation brings about by which he eats me. I, then, humbled, live because of the Father; he, raised up, lives because of me. But if it was so said: *I live because of the Father*, because he is from the Father, not the Father from him; it was said without detriment to the equality. Nor yet, by saying, *and he who eats me, he too will live because of me*, did he signify that his equality and ours are the same; but he showed the grace of the mediator.  
**20.** *This is the bread that came down from heaven*: that by eating it we may live, because we cannot have eternal life of ourselves. *Not as*, he says, *your fathers ate manna, and died: he who eats this bread will live forever*[^47]. That they died, then, he wishes to be so understood, that they do not live forever. For temporally these too, who eat Christ, will surely die: but they live forever, because Christ is eternal life.
