# Tractate 95

## *And when he has come, he will convict the world concerning sin, and concerning righteousness, and concerning judgment*[^1].

## *He will convict the world.* He will pour out love in your hearts; for thus, fear being driven away, you will have the freedom to convict.

**1.** The Lord, promising that he would send the Holy Spirit, says: *When he has come, he will convict the world concerning sin, and concerning righteousness, and concerning judgment*[^2]. What is this? Does the Lord Christ not convict the world concerning sin, when he says, *If I had not come and spoken to them, they would not have sin; but now they have no excuse for their sin*[^3]? But lest perhaps anyone say that this pertains properly to the Jews, not to the world, does he not say in another place, *If you were of the world, the world would love what was its own*[^4]? Does he not convict concerning righteousness, where he says, *Righteous Father, the world has not known you*[^5]? Does he not convict concerning judgment, where he says that he will say to those on the left, *Go into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^6]? And many other places are found in the holy Gospel, where Christ convicts the world concerning these things. What is it, then, that he attributes this as it were properly to the Holy Spirit? Is it perhaps because Christ spoke only among the nation of the Jews, and so does not seem to have convicted the world — so that he is understood to be convicted who hears the one convicting? But the Holy Spirit, in his disciples scattered throughout the whole world, is understood to have convicted not one nation, but the world. For this he said to them when about to ascend into heaven: *It is not yours to know the times or the moments which the Father has placed in his own power: but you will receive the power of the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and you will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea, and in Samaria, and unto the ends of the earth*[^7]. This is to convict the world.

But who would dare to say that the Holy Spirit convicts the world through the disciples of Christ, and that Christ himself does not convict, when the Apostle cries out: *Or do you wish to receive a proof of the Christ who speaks in me*[^8]? Those, therefore, whom the Holy Spirit convicts, Christ certainly convicts also. But, as it seems to me, because love was to be poured out in their hearts through the Holy Spirit — love which casts out fear, by which they could be hindered from daring to convict the world that raged with persecutions — therefore he said, *He will convict the world*: as though he said: He will pour out love in your hearts; for thus, fear being driven away, you will have the freedom to convict. But we have often said that the works of the Trinity are inseparable; yet the persons had to be commended individually, so that not only without separation, but also without confusion, both the unity and the Trinity may be understood.

## The very incorporation of the faithful is the reproach of the unfaithful.

**2.** He then explains what he said, *concerning sin, and concerning righteousness, and concerning judgment. Concerning sin, indeed,* he says, *because they did not believe in me*[^9]. For this sin, as though it were the only one, he set before the rest; because while it remains the rest are held fast, and when it departs the rest are forgiven.

*But concerning righteousness,* he says, *because I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me*[^10]. Here first it is to be seen: if anyone is rightly convicted concerning sin, how is he rightly convicted also concerning righteousness? For if a sinner is to be convicted because he is a sinner, will anyone think that a righteous man too is to be convicted because he is righteous? Far be it. For even if a righteous man is sometimes convicted, he is rightly convicted for this reason, because, as it is written, *There is no righteous man on earth who will do good and will not sin*[^11]. Wherefore even when a righteous man is convicted, he is convicted concerning sin, not concerning righteousness. For even in that which we read said by God, *Do not become exceedingly righteous*[^12], it was not the righteousness of the wise man that was noted, but the pride of the presumptuous. He, then, who becomes *exceedingly righteous,* by that very excess becomes unrighteous. For he makes himself exceedingly righteous who says he has no sin; or who thinks he is made righteous not by the grace of God, but by his own sufficient will; and he is not righteous by living rightly, but rather puffed up, thinking himself to be what he is not.

In what way, then, is the world to be convicted concerning righteousness, except concerning the righteousness of those who believe? It is convicted, therefore, concerning sin, because it does not believe in Christ; and it is convicted concerning the righteousness of those who believe. For the very comparison of the faithful is the reproach of the unfaithful. This the very explanation also sufficiently indicates. For, wishing to open up what he had said, he says, *But concerning righteousness, because I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me.* He does not say: And they will no longer see me; of whom he had said, *because they did not believe in me.* But explaining what he called sin, he spoke of those, saying, *because they did not believe in me*; while explaining what righteousness he meant, concerning which the world is convicted, he turned to those to whom he was speaking, and said, *because I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me.* Wherefore the world is convicted concerning its own sin, but concerning another's righteousness, just as darkness is convicted concerning light: *For all things that are convicted,* says the Apostle, *are made manifest by the light*[^13]. For how great the evil is of those who do not believe can appear not only through itself, but also from the good of those who believe. And since this has been the voice of the unfaithful: How do we believe what we do not see? therefore the righteousness of believers had to be so defined: *Because I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me. For blessed are they who do not see, and believe*[^14]. For even those who saw Christ — their faith was praised in this, not because they believed what they saw, that is, the Son of man, but because they believed what they did not see, that is, the Son of God. But when even the form of a servant was withdrawn from their sight, then indeed in every part was fulfilled, *The righteous lives by faith*[^15]. For *faith is,* as it is defined in the Epistle which is to the Hebrews, *the substance of things hoped for, the conviction of things that are not seen*[^16].

**3.** But what is, *You will no longer see me*? For he does not say: I go to the Father, and you will not see me — so that it might be understood to signify an interval of time in which he would not be seen, whether short or long, yet certainly bounded: but by saying, *You will no longer see me,* the truth foretold, as it were, that thereafter they would never see Christ. Is this the righteousness — never to see Christ, and yet to believe in him — when for this reason the faith by which the righteous man lives is praised, that he believes that Christ, whom he now does not see, he will at some time see? Finally, according to this righteousness, are we going to say that the apostle Paul was not righteous, who confesses that he saw Christ after his ascension into heaven — concerning whom he had certainly already said, with reference to that time, *You will no longer see me*? According to this righteousness, was not the most glorious Stephen righteous, who, when he was being stoned, said: *Behold, I see heaven opened, and the Son of man standing at the right hand of God*[^17]?

What, then, is, *I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me*; unless it be, as I am while I am with you? For then he was still mortal, in the likeness of sinful flesh, who could hunger and thirst, be wearied and sleep: this Christ, then, that is, such a Christ, when he had passed from this world to the Father, they were no longer going to see; and this is itself the righteousness of faith, of which the Apostle says: *Even if we had known Christ according to the flesh, yet now we know him so no longer*[^18]. And so, he says, your righteousness, by which the world will be convicted, will be this: *because I go to the Father, and you will no longer see me*: since you will believe in me, whom you will not see; and when you will see me as I shall then be, you will not see me as I am now with you; you will not see me lowly, but lofty; you will not see me mortal, but everlasting; you will not see me to be judged, but to judge: and concerning this faith of yours, that is, your righteousness, the Holy Spirit will convict the unbelieving world.

**4.** He will convict also *concerning judgment, because the prince of this world has been judged*[^19]. Who is this, but he of whom he says in another place, *Behold, the prince of the world comes, and in me he will find nothing*[^20]; that is, nothing of his own right, nothing that belongs to him, no sin at all? For by this the devil is prince of the world. For the devil is not prince of heaven and earth and all that is in them, in which signification the world is understood where it is said, *And the world was made through him*[^21]: but the devil is prince of the world of which, there, he at once adds and says, *And the world did not know him*[^22] — that is, unbelieving men, of whom the world throughout the whole earth is full: among whom the faithful world groans, which he chose out of the world, the world through whom the world was made; of whom he himself says: *The Son of man did not come to judge the world, but that the world might be saved through him*[^23]. The world is condemned when he judges, the world is saved when he comes to its aid: since, just as a tree is full of leaves and fruits, just as a threshing floor of chaff and grain, so the world is full of the unfaithful and the faithful. The prince of this world, then — that is, the prince of this darkness, that is, of the unfaithful, from whom the world is rescued, to whom it is said, *You were once darkness; but now light in the Lord*[^24] — the prince of this world, of whom he says elsewhere, *Now the prince of this world has been cast out*[^25], has certainly been judged; since he has been irrevocably destined to the judgment of eternal fire. And so concerning this judgment too, by which the prince of the world has been judged, the world is convicted by the Holy Spirit; since it is judged together with its prince, whom the proud and impious man imitates. *For if God,* as the apostle Peter says, *did not spare the angels who sinned, but, thrusting them down into the prisons of the gloom of hell, handed them over to be kept for punishment in the judgment*[^26]; how is the world not convicted by the Holy Spirit concerning this judgment, when the Apostle speaks these things in the Holy Spirit? Let men, therefore, believe in Christ, lest they be convicted concerning the sin of their unbelief, by which all sins are held fast: let them pass into the number of the faithful, lest they be convicted concerning the righteousness of those whom, now justified, they do not imitate: let them beware the judgment to come, lest they be judged with the prince of the world, whom, now judged, they imitate. For lest the hard pride of mortals think itself spared, it must be terrified by the punishment of the proud angels.
