# Tractate 102

## *The Father himself loves you, because you have loved me*[^1].

## We love God because he himself first loved us. From this, then, it has come about that we love, because we have been loved. It is altogether the gift of God to love God.

**1.** These words of the Lord must now be treated: *Amen, amen, I say to you, if you ask anything of the Father in my name, he will give it to you*[^2]. It has already been said in the earlier parts of this discourse of the Lord, on account of those who ask certain things of the Father in Christ's name and do not receive — that whatever is asked against the account of salvation is not asked in the name of the Savior. For not the sound of letters and syllables, but what the sound itself signifies, and what by that sound is rightly and truly understood, is to be taken as what he says when he says, *in my name.* Whence he who thinks of Christ that which is not to be thought of the only Son of God does not ask in his name, even if he does not keep silent "Christ" in letters and syllables; since he asks in the name of him whom he thinks of when he asks. But he who thinks of him what is to be thought, he asks in his name; and receives what he asks, if he does not ask against his everlasting salvation. But he receives when he ought to receive. For some things are not denied, but deferred so that they may be given at a fitting time. So indeed is what he says, *he will give it to you,* to be understood, so that those benefits be known to be signified by these words which properly belong to those who ask. For all the saints are heard for themselves, but they are not heard for all, whether their friends or their enemies, or any others whatever: because it was not said simply, *he will give*; but, *he will give it to you.*

## Full joy is not a fleshly but a spiritual joy.

**2.** *Until now,* he says, *you have not asked anything in my name. Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be full*[^3]. This which he calls full joy is surely not a fleshly, but a spiritual joy: and when it will be so great that nothing is now to be added to it, without doubt it will then be full. Whatever, then, is asked that belongs to the obtaining of this joy, this is to be asked in the name of Christ, if we understand the divine grace, if we seek the truly blessed life. But whatever else is asked, nothing is asked: not because it is no thing at all, but because in comparison with so great a thing, whatever else is desired is nothing. For a man is not altogether no thing, of whom the Apostle says: *He who thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing*[^4]. For in comparison with the spiritual man, who knows that by the grace of God he is what he is, whoever presumes vain things is nothing. So, then, this too can be rightly understood: *Amen, amen, I say to you, if you ask anything of the Father in my name, he will give it to you*; so that this which he says, *anything,* is understood not as anything whatever, but as something that in comparison with the blessed life is not nothing.

And what follows, *Until now you have not asked anything in my name,* can be understood in two ways: either because you have not asked in my name, having not known that name as it is to be known; or because you have asked nothing, since in comparison with the thing you ought to have asked, what you have asked is to be held as nothing. Therefore, that in his name they may ask, not nothing, but full joy (since, if they ask anything else, that something is nothing), he exhorts, saying: *Ask, and you will receive, that your joy may be full*: that is, ask this in my name, that your joy may be full, and you will receive. For the divine mercy will by no means defraud his saints persevering in asking for this good.

**3.** *These things,* he says, *I have spoken to you in proverbs: the hour comes when I will no longer speak to you in proverbs, but openly will announce to you concerning my Father*[^5]. I could say that this hour of which he speaks is to be understood as the age to come, where we shall see openly, as blessed Paul says, *face to face*; so that what he says, *These things I have spoken to you in proverbs,* is what was said by the same apostle, *We see now through a mirror in an enigma*: but *I will announce to you,* because through the Son the Father will be seen, according to that which he says elsewhere, *Nor does anyone know the Father, except the Son, and he to whom the Son will reveal him*[^6]. But this sense seems to be hindered by what follows: *In that day you will ask in my name*[^7]. For in the age to come, when we shall have come to the kingdom, where we shall be like him, because we shall see him as he is, what shall we ask, when our desire will be satisfied with good things? Whence in another psalm it is said: *I shall be satisfied when your glory is made manifest*[^8]. For asking belongs to some need, which there will be none where this satiety will be.

**4.** It remains, therefore, as far as I am able to be wise, that we understand Jesus to have promised that he would make his disciples spiritual from being fleshly or animal — although not yet such as we shall be, when we shall have a spiritual body too; but such as was he who said, *We speak wisdom among the perfect*; and, *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to fleshly*; and, *We have not received the spirit of this world, but the Spirit who is from God, that we may know the things that have been given us by God; which also we speak, not in the learned words of human wisdom, but in those taught by the Spirit, comparing spiritual things with spiritual. But the animal man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God*[^9]. The animal man, then, not perceiving the things that are of the Spirit of God, so hears whatever he hears of God's nature that he cannot think of anything but a body — however vast or immense, however bright and beautiful, yet a body: therefore whatever sayings of wisdom there are concerning the incorporeal and unchangeable substance are to him proverbs; not that he reckons them as proverbs, but because he so thinks of them, as those who are wont to hear proverbs and not understand them.

But when the spiritual man has begun to judge all things, while he himself is judged by no one, even if in this life he still beholds as through a mirror in part, yet he perceives — not by any bodily sense, not by any imaginary thinking which grasps or feigns the likenesses of bodies of whatever sort, but by the most certain understanding of the mind — that God is not a body, but spirit: so that, the Son announcing openly concerning the Father, he too who announces is beheld to be of the same substance. Then those who ask, ask in his name; because in the sound of his name they understand nothing other than the very thing which is called by this name, nor do they, by the vanity or weakness of the mind, fashion as it were the Father in one place, the Son in another standing before the Father and asking on our behalf, with masses occupying their respective spaces for both, and the Word making words to him whose Word he is on our behalf, an interval being interposed between the mouth of the speaker and the little ears of the hearer; and other such things which the animal, and likewise fleshly, men build for themselves in their hearts. For whatever of this sort occurs to the spiritual when thinking of God, from the habit of bodies, by denying and rejecting they drive from their inner eyes, as importunate flies; and they rest in the sincerity of that light, by whose witness and judgment they convict these very images of bodies, rushing in upon their internal gaze, to be altogether false. These men can in some way think that our Lord Jesus Christ, insofar as he is man, intercedes with the Father for us; but insofar as he is God, hears us together with the Father. Which I think he signified where he says: *And I do not say to you that I will ask the Father concerning you*[^10]. For to this beholding — how the Son does not ask the Father, but the Father and the Son together hear those who ask — none but the spiritual eye of the mind ascends.

## God made the love by which we worship God.

**5.** *For the Father himself,* he says, *loves you, because you have loved me*[^11]. Does he love because we love; or rather, because he loves, do we therefore love? Let the same evangelist answer from his epistle: *We love,* he says, *because he himself first loved us*[^12]. From this, then, it has come about that we love, because we have been loved. It is altogether the gift of God to love God. He himself gave that he should be loved, who, unloved, loved. Displeasing, we were loved, that there might be in us that whereby we should please. For we would not love the Son, unless we also loved the Father. The Father loves us, because we love the Son; whereas from the Father and the Son we have received that we both love the Father and the Son: for the Spirit of both pours out love in our hearts, through which Spirit we love both the Father and the Son, and which Spirit we love together with the Father and the Son. God, then, made our devout love by which we worship God, and saw that it was good, and therefore he loved that which he made. But he would not make in us what he might love, unless, before he made it, he loved us.

**6.** *And you have believed,* he says, *that I came forth from God. I came forth from the Father, and came into the world: again I leave the world, and go to the Father*[^13]. Plainly we have believed. For it ought not to seem incredible on this account, that, coming to the world, he so came forth from the Father that he did not forsake the Father; and so goes to the Father, the world being left, that he does not forsake the world. For he came forth from the Father, because he is of the Father: he came into the world, because he showed to the world his body, which he took up from the Virgin. He left the world by a bodily departure, he proceeded to the Father by the ascension of the man, nor did he forsake the world by the governance of his presence.
