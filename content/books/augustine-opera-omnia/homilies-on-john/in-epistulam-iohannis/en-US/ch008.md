# Tractate 7: Now you are from God

## (1 John 4:4–12).

> *With Christ as our leader, let us drink in love, having set the world aside. Love is God: whoever has it has seen God. Love, and do what you will. Love the man, not his error.*

## If you do not want to die of thirst in this desert, drink love.

**1.** This world is to all the faithful who seek their homeland just as the desert was to the people of Israel. They were indeed still wandering, and seeking their homeland: but with God as their leader they could not go astray. The way for them was God's command. For where they went about for forty years, the journey itself is accomplished in very few stages, and it is known to all. They were delayed because they were being trained, not because they were being abandoned.

What God promises us, then, is an unspeakable sweetness and good, as Scripture says and as you have often heard us recall, *what eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has it entered into the heart of man*[^1]. But we are trained by temporal labors and instructed by the trials of the present life. Yet if you do not want to die of thirst in this desert, drink love. It is a fountain that the Lord willed to place here, so that we might not fail along the way: and we shall drink of it more abundantly when we come to our homeland.

The Gospel has just been read; so that I may speak about the very words with which the reading ended, what else did you hear about but love? For we have made a covenant with our God in prayer, that if we want him to forgive us our sins, we too should forgive the sins that have been committed against us[^2]. But nothing forgives except love.

Take love away from the heart; it holds hatred, it does not know how to pardon. Let love be there, and it pardons without anxiety, for it is not constrained. But see whether this whole Epistle, which we have undertaken to expound to you, commends anything other than this one thing, love. Nor is it to be feared that by being said often it may become hateful. For what is loved, if love itself becomes hateful? The love by which it comes about that other things are rightly loved—how is it itself to be loved? A thing, then, that ought never to depart from the heart should not depart from the mouth either.

## Be the beast of burden of the one who rides you.

**2.** *Now you,* he says, *are from God, little children, and you have overcome him*[^3]: overcome whom, if not the antichrist? For above he had said: *Everyone who dissolves Jesus Christ and denies that he has come in the flesh is not from God*[^4]. And we explained, if you remember, that all who violate love deny that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh. For there was no need for Jesus to come except for the sake of love. For that love is commended to us which he himself commends in the Gospel: *No one can have greater love than this, that he lay down his life for his friends*[^5]. How could the Son of God lay down his life for us, unless he were clothed in flesh, in which he could die?

So whoever violates love, whatever his tongue may say, his very life denies that Christ has come in the flesh; and that person is antichrist, wherever he may be, into whatever place he may enter. But what does he say to those who are citizens of that homeland for which we sigh? *You have overcome him.*

And how did they overcome? *Because he who is in you is greater than he who is in this world*[^6]*.* Lest they attribute the victory to their own strength and be overcome by the arrogance of pride (for whomever the devil makes proud, he overcomes), wishing to keep them humble, what does he say? *You have overcome him.*

Now every man who hears *You have overcome* lifts his head, lifts his neck, wants to be praised. Do not exalt yourself; see who has overcome in you. Why have you overcome? *Because he who is in you is greater than he who is in this world.* Be humble, carry your Lord; be the beast of burden of the one who rides you.

It is good for you that he himself should rule and lead you. For if you do not have him as your rider, you can lift your neck, you can kick out: but woe to you without a guide; for that liberty hands you over to the beasts to be devoured.

## Whoever loves the world neglects love.

**3.** *They are of the world.* Who? The antichrists. You have already heard who they are. And even if you are not such, you recognize them: but whoever is such does not recognize it. *They are of the world: therefore they speak of the world, and the world hears them*[^7]*.* Who are those who speak of the world? Notice those who are against love. See, you have heard the Lord saying: *If you forgive men their sins, your heavenly Father will also forgive you your sins: but if you do not forgive, neither will your Father forgive you your sins*[^8].

It is the verdict of truth: or if it is not truth that speaks, contradict it. If you are a Christian, and you believe Christ, he himself said: *I am the truth*[^9]. This verdict is true, it is firm. Now hear men speaking of the world. Are you not going to avenge yourself, and will not that man say that he has gotten the better of you? On the contrary, you feel that you have a real man to deal with.

These things are said every day. Those who say such things speak of the world; and the world hears them. Nor do any say such things except those who love the world; nor are such things heard except by those who love the world. And whoever loves the world and neglects love—you have heard that he denies that Jesus has come in the flesh.

Or did the Lord himself do that while in the flesh? When he was struck, did he want to avenge himself? When he hung on the cross, did he not say: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^10]? But if he who had the power did not threaten, why do you threaten, why do you bluster when you are set under another's power? He died because he willed it, and he did not threaten; you do not know when you will die, and you threaten?

## God is love.

**4.** *We are from God.* Let us see why: see whether it is for any reason other than love. *We are from God. Whoever knows God hears us: whoever is not from God does not hear us. By this we know the spirit of truth and of error*[^11]*.* For whoever hears us has the spirit of truth: whoever does not hear us has the spirit of error. It is clear, then, from what source we may recognize the brethren. Let us see what he warns, and let us rather hear him warning us in the spirit of truth; not the antichrists, not the lovers of the world, not the world: whether we have been born from God:

*Beloved,* as it follows above, see what: *We are from God. Whoever knows God hears us: whoever is not from God does not hear us. By this is known the spirit of truth and of error.* So now he has made us attentive: because whoever knows God hears, but whoever does not know him does not hear: and this is the distinction between the spirit of truth and of error. Let us see what he is going to warn, in which we ought to hear him. *Beloved, let us love one another.*

Why? Because a man warns us? *Because love is from God.* He has greatly commended love, because he said it *is from God*: he is going to say more, let us listen attentively. He has just said: *Love is from God: and everyone who loves is born from God, and has known God. Whoever does not love does not know God.* Why? *Because God is love*[^12]*.* What more could have been said, brethren? If nothing were said in praise of love throughout all these pages of this Epistle, if nothing at all throughout the other pages of the Scriptures, and we heard only this one thing from the voice of the Spirit of God, *that God is love*, we ought to seek nothing more.

## To act against love is to act against him.

**5.** Now see that to act against love is to act against God. Let no one say: I sin against a man when I do not love my brother (pay attention); and a sin against a man is easy, only let me not sin against God. How do you not sin against God when you sin against love? *God is love.* Do we say this? If we were to say *God is love*, perhaps some one of you would be scandalized and would say: What did he say? What did he mean to say, that *God is love*? God gave love, God granted love. *Love is from God: God is love.* See, brethren, you have the Scriptures of God: this Epistle is canonical; it is read aloud among all nations, it is held by the authority of the whole world, it has itself built up the whole world. You hear here from the Spirit of God: *God is love.* Now if you dare, act against God, and refuse to love your brother.

## Love is God, but God because it is from God.

**6.** How then, a moment ago, *Love is from God*; and now, *Love is God*? For God is Father and Son and Holy Spirit: the Son, God from God; the Holy Spirit, God from God; and these three are one God, not three gods. If the Son is God, and the Holy Spirit is God, and the one in whom the Holy Spirit dwells loves: then love is God; but God because it is from God. For you have both in the Epistle; both *Love is from God,* and *Love is God.* Of the Father alone Scripture does not know how to say that he is from God.

But when you hear *from God,* either the Son is understood, or the Holy Spirit. And because the Apostle says: *The love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^13]; let us understand that the Holy Spirit is in love. For it is the Holy Spirit himself whom the wicked cannot receive; he is that fountain of which Scripture says: *Let the fountain of your water be your own, and let no stranger share it with you*[^14].

For all who do not love God are strangers, are antichrists. And although they enter the basilicas, they cannot be numbered among the children of God; that fountain of life does not belong to them. Even a wicked man can have baptism; even a wicked man can have prophecy. We find that King Saul had prophecy: he was persecuting holy David, and he was filled with the Spirit of prophecy, and he began to prophesy[^15].

Even a wicked man can receive the sacrament of the body and blood of the Lord: for of such it was said: *Whoever eats and drinks unworthily, eats and drinks judgment to himself*[^16]. Even a wicked man can have the name of Christ; that is, even a wicked man can be called a Christian: of whom it was said: *They polluted the name of their God*[^17]. So even a wicked man can have all these sacraments; but to have love, and to be wicked, is not possible. This, then, is the proper gift; this is the singular fountain. To drink of it the Spirit of God exhorts you; to drink of himself the Spirit of God exhorts you.

## The Father handed over the Son in love, Judas handed him over in betrayal.

**7.** *In this the love of God was made manifest in us.* See, we have an exhortation to love God. Could we love him, unless he first loved us? If we were sluggish to love, let us not be sluggish to love in return. He loved us first; and even so we do not love him in return. He loved the wicked, but he dissolved their wickedness: he loved the wicked, but he did not gather them together for wickedness.

He loved the sick, but he visited them to heal them. *God,* then, *is love. In this the love of God was made manifest in us, because he sent his only-begotten Son into this world, that we might live through him*[^18]*.* Just as the Lord himself says: *No one can have greater love than this, that he lay down his life for his friends*; and there the love of Christ for us was proven, because he died for us.

How was the love of the Father proven toward us? Because he sent his only Son to die for us: so too the apostle Paul says: *He who did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all, how has he not also with him given us all things*?[^19] See, the Father handed Christ over, and Judas handed him over;

does it not appear, as it were, to be a similar deed? Judas is a betrayer: so is God the Father then a betrayer too? God forbid, you say. I do not say it, but the Apostle says it: *He who did not spare his own Son, but handed him over for us all.* Both the Father handed him over, and he handed himself over. The same apostle says: *Who loved me, and handed himself over for me*[^20]. If the Father handed over the Son, and the Son handed himself over, what did Judas do?

A handing over was done by the Father, a handing over was done by the Son, a handing over was done by Judas; one thing was done: but what is it that distinguishes the Father handing over the Son, the Son handing over himself, and Judas the disciple handing over his master?

It is that the Father and the Son did this in love; but Judas did this in betrayal. You see that it is not what a man does that must be considered, but with what mind and will he does it. In the same deed we find God the Father in which we find Judas; the Father we bless, Judas we detest. Why do we bless the Father and detest Judas?

We bless love, we detest wickedness. For how great a benefit was bestowed on the human race by the handing over of Christ? Did Judas think of this when he handed him over? God thought of our salvation, by which we have been redeemed; Judas thought of the price for which he sold the Lord. The Son himself thought of the price he gave for us; Judas thought of the price he received for selling him. A different intention, then, made the deeds different. Although it is one thing, if we measure it by the different intentions, we find one thing to be loved, the other to be condemned; one thing to be glorified, the other to be detested. So much does love avail. See that it alone makes the distinction; see that it alone sets apart the deeds of men.

## Love, and do what you will.

**8.** We have said this concerning similar deeds. In different deeds, we find a man raging out of love, and a man caressing out of wickedness. A father beats a boy, and a slave-dealer caresses him. If you set forth two things, blows and caresses, who would not choose the caresses and flee the blows? If you attend to the persons, it is love that beats, wickedness that caresses.

See what we are commending, that the deeds of men are not distinguished except by the root of love. For many things can be done that have a good appearance, and yet do not proceed from the root of love. For even thorns have flowers: some things indeed seem harsh, seem fierce; but they are done for discipline at the dictate of love. Once for all, then, a short precept is enjoined on you: Love, and do what you will: whether you keep silent, keep silent out of love; whether you cry out, cry out of love; whether you correct, correct out of love; whether you spare, spare out of love: let the root of love be within, and from this root nothing but good can come forth.

## God loved first.

**9.** *In this is love. In this the love of God was made manifest in us, because God sent his only-begotten Son into this world, that we might live through him. In this is love, not that we loved him, but that he loved us.* We did not love him first: for he loved us to this end, that we might love him. *And he sent his Son as a propitiation for our sins*: a propitiator, a sacrificer. He sacrificed for our sins. Where did he find a victim? Where did he find the offering he wanted to offer pure? He found nothing else; he offered himself. *Beloved, if God so loved us, we too ought to love one another*[^21]*. Peter,* he says, *do you love me?* And he said: *I love you. Feed my sheep*[^22].

## If you want to see God, dwell in love.

**10.** *No one has ever seen God*[^23]: he is a thing invisible; he must be sought not with the eye but with the heart. But just as, if we wanted to see this sun, we would cleanse the eye of the body by which light can be seen, so, wanting to see God, let us cleanse the eye by which God can be seen. Where is that eye? Hear the Gospel: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^24]. But let no one, out of the desire of the eyes, imagine God for himself. For he makes for himself either some enormous shape, or stretches out some immeasurable magnitude through space, as he enlarges through the fields, as much as he can, this light that he sees with these eyes; or he makes for himself some old man, as it were, of venerable form.

Think none of these things. There is something you may think, if you want to see God: *God is love.* What sort of face does love have? What sort of form does it have? What sort of stature does it have? What sort of feet does it have? What sort of hands does it have? No one can say. Yet it has feet; for they lead to the Church: it has hands; for they reach out to the poor man:

it has eyes; for by them is understood the one who is in need: *Blessed,* he says, *is he who considers the needy and the poor*[^25]. It has ears, of which the Lord says: *He who has ears to hear, let him hear*[^26]. They are not members set apart in different places, but the one who has love sees the whole at once by understanding. Dwell, and you will be dwelt in; abide, and you will be abided in.

For what, my brethren, who loves what he does not see? But why, when love is praised, do you rise up, applaud, give praise? What have I shown you? Have I brought out some colors? Have I set out gold and silver? Have I dug gems out of treasuries? What such thing have I shown to your eyes? Has my face changed as I speak?

I bear flesh, I am in the same form in which I came forth, you are in the same form in which you came: love is praised, and you cry out. Surely you see nothing. But just as it pleases you when you praise it, so let it please you to keep it in your heart.

For attend to what I say, brethren; I exhort you, as much as the Lord grants, toward a great treasure. If some small vessel were shown to you, embossed, gilded, elaborately made, and it allured your eyes and drew to itself the attention of your heart, and the craftsman's hand pleased you, and the weight of silver, and the splendor of the metal; would not each one of you say: Oh, if only I had that little vessel?

And you would say it to no purpose, for it was not in your power. Or if anyone wanted to have it, he would think of stealing it from another's house. Love is praised to you; if it pleases you, have it, possess it: there is no need to commit theft against anyone, no need to think of buying it; it costs nothing. Hold it, embrace it; nothing is sweeter than it. If it is such when it is spoken of, what is it when it is possessed?

## Love what God made, not what man himself made.

**11.** If perhaps you want to keep love, brethren, above all do not think it abject and lazy; nor that love is kept by a kind of gentleness, or rather not gentleness but laxity and negligence. It is not kept that way. Do not think that you love your slave when you do not beat him; or that you love your son when you do not give him discipline; or that you love your neighbor when you do not rebuke him: that is not love, but feebleness. Let love be fervent to correct, to amend: but if his ways are good, let them be a delight; if they are bad, let them be amended, corrected. Do not love the error in a man, but the man: for God made the man, and man himself made the error. Love that which God made, do not love that which man himself made. When you love that, you take this away; when you love that, you amend this. But even if you rage at times, it is out of love for correction.

This is why love was shown in the dove that came down upon the Lord[^27]. That appearance of a dove, in which appearance the Holy Spirit came so that love might be poured into us. Why this? The dove has no gall: yet it fights for its nest with beak and wings, it rages without bitterness. This is what a father also does; when he chastises his son, he chastises him for discipline. As I said, the seducer, in order to sell, caresses with bitterness: the father, in order to correct, chastises without gall. Be such toward all. See, brethren, a great lesson, a great rule: Each one has children, or wants to have them; or if he has altogether decided not to have children in the flesh, he desires to have them spiritually: who is there who does not correct his son? Who is there to whom a father does not give discipline[^28]? And yet he seems to rage. Love rages, charity rages: it rages in a certain way without gall, after the manner of the dove, not of the raven.

From this it comes to mind, my brethren, to say to you that those violators of love made a schism: just as they hate love itself, so they hate the dove also. But the dove convicts them: it proceeds from heaven, the heavens are opened, and it remains upon the head of the Lord. Why this? So that he might hear: *This is he who baptizes*[^29].

Withdraw, you robbers; withdraw, you invaders of the possession of Christ. In your own possessions, where you want to be masters, you have dared to fix the title-deeds of the powerful man. He recognizes his own title-deeds; he claims his possession for himself: he does not erase the title-deeds, but enters and takes possession. So for one coming to the Catholic Church, baptism is not erased, lest the title of the emperor be erased. But what happens in the Catholic Church? The title is recognized; the possessor enters under his own titles, where the robber had been entering under another's titles.
