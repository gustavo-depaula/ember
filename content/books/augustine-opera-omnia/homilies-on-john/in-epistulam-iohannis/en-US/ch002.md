# Homily 1: That which was from the beginning

## (1 John 1:1—2:11).

> *Through faith we can touch the Word joined to flesh and be enlightened by his light and have life, if we humbly confess our sins and hold fast to brotherly love.*

## Life itself was made manifest in the flesh.

**1.** *That which was from the beginning, which we have heard, which we have seen with our eyes, and our hands have handled, of the Word of life*[^1]*.* Who is it that handles the Word with his hands, unless because *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^2]? Now this Word that was made flesh, that it might be handled with hands, began to be flesh of the Virgin Mary; but the Word did not then begin, for he said *that which was from the beginning.*

See whether his Epistle does not bear witness to his Gospel, where you just now heard: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God*[^3]. Perhaps someone might take what is said about the Word of life as though it were a manner of speaking about Christ, and not the very body of Christ that was handled with hands. See what follows: *And the life was made manifest*[^4]*.* Christ, then, is the Word of life. And whence was he made manifest? For he was from the beginning, but he had not been made manifest to men; he had, however, been made manifest to the angels, who beheld him and fed upon him as upon their own bread. But what does Scripture say?

*Man ate the bread of angels*[^5]. So life itself was made manifest in the flesh; for it was set forth in manifestation, so that what can be seen by the heart alone might be seen also with the eyes, that it might heal hearts. For the Word is seen by the heart alone; but flesh is seen also with the bodily eyes. We had the means to see the flesh, but not the means to see the Word: the Word was made flesh, which we could see, so that there might be healed in us that whereby we might see the Word.

## In that virginal womb the bridegroom Word and the bride flesh were joined.

**2.** *And we have seen, and are witnesses*[^6]*.* Perhaps some of the brethren who do not know Greek do not know what *witnesses* are in Greek; yet the word is familiar to all and is held in reverence: for those whom in Latin we call witnesses are in Greek martyrs. And who has not heard of martyrs, or in whose Christian mouth does the name of the martyrs not dwell daily? And would that it dwelt likewise in the heart, so that we might imitate the sufferings of the martyrs, and not persecute them with our heels.

This, then, is what he said: *We have seen, and are witnesses*: we have seen, and are martyrs. For in bearing testimony to what they had seen, and bearing testimony to what they had heard from those who had seen, when that very testimony displeased the men against whom it was spoken, they suffered all that the martyrs suffered. The martyrs are God's witnesses. God willed to have men as witnesses, so that men too might have God as their witness.

*We have seen,* he says, *and are witnesses.* Where did they see? In the manifestation. What is meant by, in the manifestation? In the sun, that is, in this light. But how could he who made the sun be seen in the sun, unless because *in the sun he set his tabernacle, and he himself, like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber, rejoiced like a giant to run his course*[^7]?

He who is before the sun, who made the sun; he who is before the morning star, before all the stars, before all the angels, the true Creator—since *all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^8]—that he might be seen with the eyes of flesh by those who see the sun;

he set his tabernacle in the sun, that is, he showed his flesh in the manifestation of this light; and the chamber of that bridegroom was the womb of the Virgin, for in that virginal womb two were joined, bridegroom and bride, the bridegroom Word and the bride flesh;

for it is written: *And the two shall be in one flesh*[^9]; and the Lord says in the Gospel: *So they are no longer two, but one flesh*[^10]. And Isaiah remembers well that these two are one: for he speaks in the person of Christ, and says: *As on a bridegroom he set a miter upon me, and as a bride he adorned me with ornament*[^11].

One seems to be speaking, and he made himself bridegroom and made himself bride; for they are *no longer two, but one flesh*: because *the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us.* To that flesh the Church is joined, and there comes to be the whole Christ, head and body.

## Seated in heaven, we cannot handle him with the hand but can touch him by faith.

**3.** *And we are witnesses,* he says, *and we declare to you eternal life, which was with the Father, and was made manifest among us*[^12]: that is, was made manifest among us; which would be put more plainly, was made manifest to us. *What* therefore *we have seen and heard, we declare to you.* Let your Charity attend: *What* therefore *we have seen and heard, we declare to you.* They saw the Lord himself present in the flesh, and heard words from the mouth of the Lord, and declared them to us. We too, then, have heard, but we have not seen. Are we then less blessed than those who saw and heard? And how does he add: *That you also may have fellowship with us*?

They saw, we did not see, and yet we are fellows; because we hold a common faith. For one man too, by seeing, did not believe, and wished to touch, and so to believe, and said: *I will not believe unless I put my fingers into the place of the nails, and touch his scars.* And he who ever offers himself to be seen by the gaze of the angels offered himself for a time to be touched by the hands of men; and that disciple touched him and cried out: *My Lord and my God.* Because he touched the man, he confessed God.

And the Lord, consoling us who cannot now handle with the hand him who is seated in heaven, but can touch him by faith, said to that man: *Because you have seen, you have believed; blessed are they who do not see and believe*[^13]. We are described, we are designated.

Let there come to be in us, then, the blessedness that the Lord foretold would be: let us hold firmly what we do not see, because they declare it who saw. *That you also,* he says, *may have fellowship with us.* And what great thing is it to have fellowship with men?

Do not despise it; see what he adds: *And our fellowship may be with God the Father, and Jesus Christ his Son. And these things,* he says, *we write to you, that your joy may be full*[^14]*.* He calls the joy full in that very fellowship, in that very love, in that very unity.

## God is light: from him he enlightens us too, that we may be light.

**4.** *And this is the announcement which we have heard from him, and announce to you*[^15]*.* What is this? They themselves saw, they handled with their hands the Word of life: he was from the beginning, and for a time the only Son of God was made visible and able to be touched.

For what did he come, or what new thing did he announce to us? What did he wish to teach? Why did he do what he did, that the Word should be made flesh, that God should suffer from men things unworthy beyond all measure, that he should endure their blows from the hands he himself had formed? What did he wish to teach? What did he wish to show? What did he wish to announce?

Let us listen: for without the fruit of a precept, the hearing of an event—that Christ was born, and that Christ suffered—is a diversion of the mind, not its strengthening. What great thing do you hear? See with what fruit you hear it. What did he wish to teach? What to announce? Listen: *That God is light,* he says, *and in him there is no darkness at all*[^16]*.*

So far he has indeed named light, but the words are obscure: it is good for us that the very light he named should illumine our hearts, and that we may see what he said. This is what we announce, *that God is light, and in him there is no darkness at all.* For who would dare to say that there is darkness in God? Or what is this light itself? Or what is darkness? Lest perhaps he should say such things as pertain to these eyes of ours. *God is light*: someone or other says, The sun too is light, and the moon is light, and a lamp is light. It must be something far greater than these, far more excellent, far more surpassing. As far as God is above the creature, as far as the maker is above what is made, as far as wisdom is above what was made through wisdom, so far beyond all things must this light be. And perhaps we shall be near to it, if we come to know what this light is, and apply ourselves to it, that we may be enlightened from it; for in ourselves we are darkness, and enlightened from it we can be light, and not be confounded by it, since by ourselves we are confounded. Who is it that is confounded over himself? He who knows himself a sinner. Who is not confounded by it? He who is enlightened by it. What is it to be enlightened by it? He who now sees himself darkened by sins, and longs to be enlightened by it, comes to it: whence the Psalm says: *Draw near to him, and be enlightened; and your faces shall not blush*[^17]. But you will not blush over it, if, when it has shown you to yourself in your foulness, your foulness displeases you, so that you may take hold of its beauty. This is what he wishes to teach.

## Through the blood of Christ the darkness must be driven from us, that light may come to be in us.

**5.** And perhaps we say that too hastily? Let him himself make it manifest in what follows. Remember, at the beginning of our discourse, that this Epistle commends love: *God is light,* he says, *and in him there is no darkness at all.* And what had he said above? *That you may have fellowship with us, and our fellowship may be with God the Father and his Son Jesus Christ.* Further, if God is light, and in him there is no darkness at all, and we are to have fellowship with him, then the darkness must be driven from us, that light may come to be in us; for darkness cannot have fellowship with light: therefore see what follows: *But if we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in darkness, we lie*[^18]*.* You have the apostle Paul too saying: *Or what fellowship has light with darkness*?[^19] You say you have fellowship with God, and you walk in darkness; and *God is light, and in him there is no darkness at all*: how then is there fellowship between light and darkness?

So now let a man say to himself: What shall I do? Whence shall I be light? I live in sins and in iniquities. As it were, a certain despair and sadness creeps in. There is no salvation except in fellowship with God. *God is light, and in him there is no darkness at all.* But sins are darkness, as the Apostle says that the devil and his angels are the rulers of this darkness[^20].

He would not call them rulers of darkness, were they not rulers of sins, lords of the wicked. What then are we to do, my brethren? Fellowship with God must be had, there is no other hope of eternal life; but *God is light, and in him there is no darkness at all*: yet iniquities are darkness; we are weighed down by iniquities, so that we cannot have fellowship with God: what hope, then, is there? Had I not promised that I would speak something in these days that would bring joy? And if I do not present it, this is sadness. *God is light, and in him there is no darkness at all*; sins are darkness: what will become of us?

Let us listen, in case perhaps he consoles us, raises us up, gives us hope, lest we fail on the way. For we are running, and running to our homeland; and if we despair of arriving, by that very despair we fail.

But he who wills us to arrive, that he may keep us safe in the homeland, feeds us on the way. Let us listen, then: *But if we say that we have fellowship with him, and walk in darkness; we lie, and do not the truth.*

Let us not say that we have fellowship with him, if we walk in darkness. *But if we walk in the light, as he also is in the light; we have fellowship with one another*[^21]*.* Let us walk in the light, as he also is in the light, that we may be able to have fellowship with him. And what are we to do about sins?

Hear what follows: *And the blood of Jesus Christ his Son will cleanse us from every sin.* God has given great assurance.

Rightly do we celebrate the Pasch, where the blood of the Lord was poured out, by which we are cleansed from every sin. Let us be secure: the devil held the bond of our servitude against us, but it has been blotted out by the blood of Christ. *The blood,* he says, *of his Son will cleanse us from every sin.* What is, *from every sin*[^22]*.*

Attend: behold, now in the name of Christ, through his blood, which these who are called infants have now confessed, all their sins have been cleansed. They entered old, they went out new. What is, they entered old, they went out new? They entered as old men, they went out as infants. For old age is a worn-out, aged life; but the infancy of regeneration is a new life. But what are we to do? Past sins have been forgiven, not only to them, but also to us; and after the forgiveness and abolition of all sins, by living in this world amid temptations, certain ones have perhaps been contracted. Therefore let a man do what he can; let him confess what he is, that he may be cured by him who ever is what he is: for he always was and is; we were not, and are.

## Pride extinguishes love, humility strengthens it.

**6.** See, then, what he says: *But if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.* Therefore, if you confess yourself a sinner, the truth is in you: for truth itself is light. Your life has not yet shone forth perfectly, because sins are present; but nevertheless you have already begun to be enlightened, because the confession of sins is present.

See, then, what follows: *But if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all iniquity*[^23]*.* Not only past sins, but also any we have perhaps contracted from this life; for a man, so long as he bears the flesh, cannot but have at least slight sins. But do not despise these that we call slight. If you despise them when you weigh them, be terrified when you count them. Many slight things make one great one: many drops fill a river; many grains make a heap. And what hope is there? Before all things, confession: lest anyone think himself just, and the man who was not, and is, lift up his neck before the eyes of God, who sees what he is. Before all things, then, confession; then love: for what has been said about love? *Love covers a multitude of sins*[^24].

Let us now see whether he commends love itself, on account of the sins that creep in: for love alone extinguishes sins. Pride extinguishes love: humility, then, strengthens love; love extinguishes sins. Humility pertains to confession, by which we confess that we are sinners:

this is humility, not that we say it with the tongue alone—as though, on account of arrogance, lest we displease men if we say we are just. The impious and the insane do this: I know indeed that I am just, but what shall I say before men? if I say I am just, who will bear it, who will tolerate it?

before God let my righteousness be known: I, however, will call myself a sinner; not because I am, but lest I be found hateful through arrogance. Tell men what you are, tell God what you are. For if you do not tell God what you are, God condemns what he finds in you.

Do you not wish him to condemn it? Then you condemn it. Do you wish him to pardon it? Then acknowledge it, so that you can say to God: *Turn away your face from my sins.* Say to him also those words in the same Psalm: *For I acknowledge my iniquity*[^25].

*But if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all iniquity. But if we say that we have not sinned, we make him a liar, and his word is not in us*[^26]*.* If you say, I have not sinned, you make him a liar, when you wish to make yourself truthful. How then can it be that God is a liar, and man truthful, when Scripture says the contrary: *Every man is a liar, God alone is truthful*[^27]? God, therefore, is truthful through himself; you are truthful through God, for through yourself you are a liar.

## It is bad to wish to be secure: let yourself always displease you.

**7.** And lest he seem perhaps to have given impunity to sins, because he said: *He is faithful and just, to cleanse us from all iniquity*; and men should now say to themselves: Let us sin, let us securely do what we will, Christ cleanses us, he is faithful and just, he cleanses us from all iniquity—he takes from you a bad security, and instills a useful fear. You wish wrongly to be secure; be anxious.

For he is faithful and just, to forgive us our sins, if you always displease yourself, and are changed until you are made perfect. Therefore, what follows? *My little children, these things I write to you, that you may not sin.* But perhaps sin creeps in from human life: what then is to be done? What? Will there now be despair? Listen: *And if anyone,* he says, *has sinned, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the just; and he is the propitiation for our sins*[^28]*.*

He, then, is the advocate; you, take pains not to sin: if, through the weakness of life, sin should creep in, see to it at once, let it at once displease you, condemn it at once; and when you have condemned it, you will come secure to the judge. There you have an advocate; do not fear that you will lose the case of your confession. For if sometimes in this life a man entrusts himself to an eloquent tongue, and does not perish, will you entrust yourself to the Word, and be doomed to perish? *Cry out: We have an advocate with the Father.*

## Do not follow false justifiers: Christ alone justifies.

**8.** See John himself preserving humility. Surely he was a just and great man, who drank the secrets of the mysteries from the breast of the Lord; he, he who by drinking from the breast of the Lord poured forth the divinity: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God*[^29]: such a man as he did not say: You have an advocate with the Father; but: *If anyone has sinned, we have,* he says, *an advocate.* He did not say, you have; nor did he say, you have me; nor did he say, you have Christ himself: but he both set down Christ, not himself, and said *we have,* not, you have.

He preferred to place himself in the number of sinners, that he might have Christ as advocate, rather than to set himself up as advocate in place of Christ, and be found among the proud who are to be condemned. Brethren, Jesus Christ the just, him we have as advocate with the Father; he is the propitiation for our sins. Whoever held to this made no heresy; whoever held to this made no schism. For whence have schisms been made? When men say: We are just; when men say: We sanctify the unclean, we justify the impious, we ask, we obtain. But what did John say? *And if anyone has sinned, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the just.*

But someone will say: Do the saints, then, not ask for us? Do the bishops and prelates, then, not ask for the people? But attend to the Scriptures, and see that the prelates too commend themselves to the people.

For the Apostle says to the people: *Praying at the same time for us also*[^30]. The Apostle prays for the people, the people pray for the Apostle. We pray for you, brethren: but you also pray for us.

Let all the members pray in turn for one another; let the head intercede for all. Therefore it is no wonder that here he follows on, and shuts the mouths of those who divide the Church of God.

For he who said: *We have Jesus Christ the just, and he is the propitiation for our sins*: on account of those who were going to divide and to say: *Behold, here is Christ, behold, there*[^31]; and would wish to show in a part him who bought the whole and possesses the whole; he at once went on: *Not for ours only, but also for those of the whole world*[^32]*.*

What is this, brethren? Surely *we found her in the fields of the wood*[^33], we found the Church in all nations. Behold, Christ *is the propitiation for our sins; not for ours only, but also for those of the whole world.* Behold, you have the Church throughout the whole world; do not follow false justifiers and true cutters-off. Be on that mountain which filled the whole world[^34]: for Christ *is the propitiation for our sins; not for ours only, but also for those of the whole world,* which he purchased with his blood.

## Love your enemies in such a way that they may be called into your fellowship.

**9.** *And by this,* he says, *we know him, if we keep his commandments.* What commandments? *He who says that he has known him, and does not keep his commandments, is a liar, and in him there is no truth.* But you still ask: What commandments? *But he who,* he says, *keeps his word, truly in him the love of God is made perfect*[^35]*.*

Let us see whether the commandment itself be called love. For we were asking, what commandments, and he says: *But he who keeps his word, truly in him the love of God is made perfect.*

Attend to the Gospel, whether this is not the commandment: *A new commandment,* he says, *I give to you, that you love one another*[^36]. *By this we know that we are in him, if we are made perfect in him*[^37]. He calls them perfect in love: what is the perfection of love?

To love even enemies, and to love them to this end, that they may be brothers. For our love ought not to be carnal. To wish someone temporal welfare is good; but even if it is lacking, let the soul be safe. Do you wish life for some friend of yours? You do well. Do you rejoice at the death of your enemy? You do ill.

But perhaps even for your friend the life you wish him is useless, and for your enemy the death over which you rejoice was useful. It is uncertain whether this life is useful to someone or useless; but the life that is with God is, without doubt, useful.

Love your enemies in such a way that you wish them to be brothers; love your enemies in such a way that they may be called into your fellowship.

For so did he love who, hanging on the cross, said: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^38].

For he did not say: Father, let these live a long time; they indeed kill me, but let them live. But what did he say? *Forgive them, for they know not what they do.* He was driving everlasting death away from them, by a most merciful prayer and a most surpassing power.

Many of them believed, and the blood of Christ that had been shed was forgiven them. First they shed it when they raged, now they drank it when they believed. *By this we know that we are in him, if we are made perfect in him.* Admonishing us of this very perfection of loving enemies, the Lord says: *Be therefore perfect, as your heavenly Father also is perfect*[^39].

*He* therefore *who says that he abides in him, ought himself also to walk just as he walked*[^40]*.* How, brethren? Of what does he admonish us? *He who says that he abides in him,* that is, in Christ, *ought himself also to walk just as he walked.* Does he perhaps admonish us of this, that we should walk on the sea? God forbid.

This, then: that we should walk in the way of righteousness. In what way? I have already mentioned it. He was fixed on the cross, and was walking in that very way: that is the way of love: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do.* So then, if you have learned to pray for your enemy, you walk the way of the Lord.

## A new commandment, because it pertains to the new man.

**10.** *Most beloved, it is not a new commandment that I write to you, but an old commandment which you had from the beginning.* What did he call an old commandment? *Which you had,* he says, *from the beginning.* It is old, then, because you have already heard it: otherwise he will be contrary to the Lord, where he says: *A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another.* But why an old commandment? Not because it pertains to the old man. But why? *Which you had from the beginning. The old commandment is the word which you heard*[^41]*.*

It is old, then, because you have already heard it. And he shows the same to be new, saying: *Again a new commandment I write to you.* Not another, but the very same that he called old is also new. Why? *Which is true in him and in you.* Why it is old, you have already heard; because you already knew it.

But why is it new? *Because the darkness has passed away, and the true light now shines*[^42]*.* Behold whence it is new: because darkness pertains to the old man, but light to the new. What does the apostle Paul say? *Put off the old man, and put on the new*[^43]. And again, what does he say? *You were once darkness; but now you are light in the Lord*[^44].

## Whoever hates his brother walks not in the light, not in Christ.

**11.** *He who says that he is in the light*; now he makes manifest all that he has said: *He who says that he is in the light, and hates his brother, is in darkness even until now*[^45]*.* Come now, my brethren, how long shall we say to you: *Love your enemies*[^46]? See, what is worse, that you do not still hate your brothers. If you loved only your brothers, you would not yet be perfect:

but if you hate your brothers, what are you? where are you? Let each one look back upon his own heart: let him not hold hatred against a brother over some harsh word; over a contention for land, lest he himself become land. For whoever hates his brother, let him not say that he walks in the light. What did I say? Let him not say that he walks in Christ. *He who says that he is in the light, and hates his brother, is in darkness even until now.*

For someone or other has become a Christian from being a pagan; attend: behold, he was in darkness when he was a pagan; now he has already become a Christian; thanks be to God, all rejoice with him: the Apostle is read aloud, rejoicing. *For you were once darkness; but now you are light in the Lord.*

He used to worship idols, he worships God; he used to worship what he made, he worships him who made him. He has been changed; thanks be to God, all Christians rejoice with him. Why? Because he is now a worshiper of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, and a detester of demons and idols. Yet John is anxious about this man; while many rejoice with him, he is still under suspicion.

Brethren, let us gladly embrace this motherly anxiety. Not without cause is the mother anxious about us, when others rejoice: I mean the mother love; for she dwelt in the heart of John when he said these things. Why, unless because she fears something in us, even when men already rejoice with us? What is it that she fears? *He who says that he is in the light.* What is this? He who now says that he is a Christian: *and hates his brother, is in darkness even until now.* Which is not to be explained; but which is to be rejoiced over if it does not happen, or to be mourned if it does.

## If you hold fast to love, you will suffer no stumbling either in Christ or in the Church.

**12.** *He who loves his brother abides in the light, and there is no stumbling-block in him*[^47]*.* I beseech you through Christ; God feeds us, we are to refresh our bodies in the name of Christ, and they have been somewhat refreshed, and are to be refreshed; let our mind be fed. I do not say it because I am going to speak long; for behold, the reading is now coming to an end: but lest perhaps from weariness we listen less attentively to what is most necessary.

*He who loves his brother abides in the light, and there is no stumbling-block in him.* Who are they that suffer stumbling, or cause it? Those who are scandalized at Christ and at the Church. Those who are scandalized at Christ are scorched as by the sun; those who at the Church, as by the moon. But the Psalm says: *By day the sun shall not scorch you, nor the moon by night*[^48]: that is: If you hold fast to love, you will suffer no stumbling either in Christ or in the Church; you will forsake neither Christ nor the Church. For he who forsakes the Church, how is he in Christ, who is not among the members of Christ? how is he in Christ, who is not in the body of Christ? They, then, suffer stumbling who forsake either Christ or the Church. Whence do we understand that the Psalm said for this reason: *By day the sun shall not scorch you, nor the moon by night,* namely that it wishes the scorching itself to be understood as stumbling? First attend to the comparison itself. As he who is scorched says: I cannot bear it, I cannot endure it, and withdraws himself: so those who cannot endure certain things in the Church, and withdraw themselves either from the name of Christ or from the Church, suffer stumbling.

For see how those carnal men suffered stumbling, as it were from the sun, to whom Christ preached his flesh, and said: *He who does not eat the flesh of the Son of man, and drink his blood, shall not have life in him.* About seventy men said: *This is a hard saying*; and they withdrew from him: and there remained twelve. The sun scorched all those, and they withdrew, not able to bear the force of the word. There remained, then, twelve. And lest perhaps men should think that they confer a benefit on Christ by believing in Christ, and that the benefit is not conferred on them by him; when the twelve had remained, the Lord said to them: *Do you also wish to go?* That you may know that I am necessary to you, not you to me. But those whom the sun had not scorched answered through the voice of Peter: *Lord, you have the word of eternal life; to whom shall we go*?[^49]

But whom does the Church scorch, as the moon by night? Those who have made schisms. Hear the very word set down in the Apostle: *Who is weak, and I am not weak? who is made to stumble, and I am not scorched*?[^50] How, then, is there no stumbling-block in him who loves his brother? Because he who loves his brother endures all things for the sake of unity; for in the unity of love is brotherly love. Someone or other offends you, whether bad, or as you think bad, or as you imagine bad, and you desert so many good men?

What sort of brotherly love is it, what sort appeared in these men? When they accuse the Africans, they have deserted the whole world. Were there no saints in the whole world? Or could they be condemned by you unheard? But O if you loved the brethren, there would be no stumbling-block in you. Hear what the Psalm says: *Great peace have they who love your law, and there is no stumbling-block to them*[^51].

He spoke of great peace to those who love the law of God, and that for this reason there is no stumbling-block to them. They, then, who suffer stumbling lose peace. And whom did he say suffer no stumbling, or cause none? Those who love the law of God. Therefore they are set in love. But someone says: He spoke of those who love the *law* of God, not the brethren. Hear what the Lord says: *A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another.* What is the law, but a commandment? But how do they suffer no stumbling, except while they bear with one another? As Paul says: *Bearing with one another in love, eager to keep the unity of the spirit in the bond of peace*[^52]. And because this is the law of Christ, hear the same Apostle commending this very law: *Bear,* he says, *one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ*[^53].

## Those who hate the brethren have stumbled against the mountain.

**13.** *For he who hates his brother is in darkness, and walks in darkness, and does not know where he goes.* A great matter, brethren; attend, we beg you. *He who hates his brother walks in darkness, and does not know where he goes; for the darkness has blinded his eyes*[^54]*.*

What is so blind as those who hate the brethren? For, that you may know they are blind, they have stumbled against the mountain. I say the same things, lest they slip from you. Is not that stone which was cut from the mountain without hands Christ, from the kingdom of the Jews without marital work? Did not that stone shatter all the kingdoms of the earth, that is, all the dominions of idols and demons? Did not that stone grow, and become a great mountain, and fill the whole world[^55]?

Do we point out this mountain with the finger, the way the third-day moon is pointed out to men? For instance, when men wish to see the new moon, they say: Behold the moon, behold where it is: and if there are there those who cannot direct their gaze, and say: Where? the finger is pointed out to them so that they may see. Sometimes, while they blush lest they be thought blind, they say they have seen what they have not seen. Do we point out the Church in this way, my brethren? Is it not open? is it not manifest? has it not taken hold of all nations? Is not that being fulfilled which so many years ago was promised to Abraham, that in his seed all nations should be blessed[^56]?

To one believer it was promised, and with thousands of believers the world has been filled. Behold the mountain filling the whole face of the earth: behold the city of which it was said: *A city set upon a mountain cannot be hidden*[^57]. But they stumble against the mountain.

And when it is said to them: Climb up; There is no mountain, they say; and they more easily dash their face there than seek a dwelling there. Isaiah was read yesterday; whoever of you was watchful, not with the eyes only, but with the ear, and not the ear of the body, but the ear of the heart, took note: *It shall be in the last days that the mountain of the house of the Lord shall be made manifest, prepared on the top of the mountains.*

What is so manifest as a mountain? But there are also mountains that are unknown, because they are set in one part of the earth. Which of you knows Mount Olympus? Just as those who live there do not know our Giddaba. These mountains are in particular regions. But that mountain is not so, because it has filled the whole face of the earth; and of it is said: *Prepared on the top of the mountains.*

It is a mountain above the tops of all the mountains. *And there shall be gathered,* he says, *to it all nations*[^58]. Who goes astray on this mountain? Who breaks his face by stumbling against it? Who is ignorant of the city set upon the mountain? But do not wonder that it is unknown to those who hate the brethren: because they walk in darkness, and do not know where they go; because the darkness has blinded their eyes.

They do not see the mountain: do not wonder, they have no eyes. Whence have they no eyes? Because the darkness has blinded them.

Whence do we prove it? Because they hate the brethren, because, when they are offended at the Africans, they separate themselves from the whole world

because for the peace of Christ they do not bear with those whom they defame, and for the party of Donatus they bear with those whom they condemn.
