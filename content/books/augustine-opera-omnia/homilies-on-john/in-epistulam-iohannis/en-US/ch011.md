# Tractate 10: Everyone who believes

## (1 John 5:1–3).

> *He who faithfully loves the Father also loves the Son and the children of God, without toil and secure, because no one takes them away. Love is the end of the commandment, and it extends itself to the members of Christ, to his Church.*

## Faith that works through love.

**1.** I believe you remember, you who were present yesterday, to what place in the progress of this Epistle our discussion came: that is: *For he who does not love his brother whom he sees, how can he love God whom he does not see? And this commandment we have from him, that he who loves God love his brother also*[^1]*.* Up to this point we had discussed. Let us, then, see in order what follows.

*Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ is born of God*[^2]*.* Who is it that does not believe that Jesus is the Christ? He who does not live as Christ commanded. For many say: I believe; but faith without works does not save. The work of faith is love itself, as the apostle Paul says: *And faith that works through love*[^3]. Your past works, indeed, before you believed, were either nothing; or, if they seemed good, they were empty.

For if they were nothing; you were like a man without feet, or unable to walk with crippled feet: but if they seemed good; before you believed, you were indeed running, but by running off the way, you were going astray rather than arriving. We must, then, both run, and run on the way. He who runs off the way runs in vain: rather, he runs to toil. The more he runs off the way, the more he goes astray. What is the way along which we run? Christ said: *I am the way.* What is the homeland to which we run?

Christ said: *I am the truth*[^4]. Through him you run, to him you run, in him you rest. But that we might run through him, he stretched himself out all the way to us: for we were far off, and we were wandering far away.

It is little that we were wandering far away; we were also feeble and could not move ourselves. The physician came to the sick, the way was held out to the pilgrims. Let us be saved by him, let us walk through him.

This is to believe that Jesus is the Christ, in the way Christians believe, who are Christians not in name alone, but in deeds and in life: not in the way the demons believe. For *the demons also believe, and tremble*[^5]*,* as Scripture says. What more could the demons believe than to say: *We know who you are, the Son of God*? What the demons said, this Peter also said.

When the Lord asked who he was, and whom men said that he was, the disciples answered him: *Some say you are John the Baptist, others Elijah, others Jeremiah, or one of the prophets.* And he: *But who do you say that I am?* Peter answered, and said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God.* And he heard from the Lord: *Blessed are you, Simon Bar-Jonah, because flesh and blood has not revealed it to you, but my Father who is in heaven.* See what praises attend this faith: *You are Peter, and upon this rock I will build my Church*[^6].

What is it, *upon this rock I will build my Church*? Upon this faith, upon that which was said: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God. Upon this rock,* he says, *I will found my Church.* Great praise! So Peter says: *You are the Christ, the Son of the living God*: the demons too say: *We know who you are, the Son of God, the Holy One of God.* This Peter, this also the demons:

the same words, but not the same mind. And how is it established that Peter said this with love? Because the faith of a Christian is with love; but that of a demon is without love. How without love? Peter said this in order to embrace Christ: the demons said this in order that Christ might withdraw from them. For before they said: *We know who you are; you are the Son of God: What is there between us and you,* did they say? *Have you come to destroy us before the time*?[^7] So it is one thing to confess Christ in order to hold Christ, another to confess Christ in order to drive Christ away from yourself.

So you see that, in the way he says here, *He who believes,* there is a certain proper faith; not as it is with many. And so, brethren, let no one of the heretics say to you: We too believe. For I set forth the example of the demons for this reason, that you may not rejoice at the words of those who believe, but examine the deeds of those who live.

## He who loves the Father loves the Son.

**2.** Let us see, then, what it is to believe in Christ; what it is to believe that Jesus himself is the Christ. It follows: *Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ is born of God.* But what is it to believe that? *And everyone who loves him who begot, loves him who is begotten by him*[^8]*.* At once he joined love to faith; because without love faith is empty.

With love, the faith of a Christian; without love, the faith of a demon: but those who do not believe are worse than the demons, and slower than the demons. Some man or other does not want to believe in Christ; so far he does not even imitate the demons. Another now believes in Christ, but hates Christ; he has the confession of faith in fear of punishment, not in love of the crown: for those too feared to be punished.

Add love to this faith, so that it becomes the faith of which the apostle Paul speaks: *Faith that works through love*[^9]: you have found a Christian, you have found a citizen of Jerusalem, you have found a fellow citizen of the angels, you have found a pilgrim sighing on the way;

join yourself to him, he is your companion, run with him, if indeed you too are this. *Everyone who loves him who begot, loves him who is begotten by him.* Who begot? The Father. Who is begotten? The Son. What then does he say? Everyone who loves the Father loves the Son.

## He who loves the Son also loves the children of God.

**3.** *In this we know that we love the children of God*[^10]*.* What is this, brethren? A little before he was speaking of the Son of God, not of the children of God: see, Christ alone has been set forth for us to contemplate, and it was said to us: *Everyone who believes that Jesus is the Christ is born of God: and everyone who loves him who begot,* that is, the Father, *loves him who is begotten by him,* that is, the Son, our Lord Jesus Christ.

And it follows: *In this we know that we love the children of God*; as though he were going to say: In this we know that we love the Son of God: he said the children of God, who a little before was speaking of the Son of God; because the children of God are the body of the only Son of God; and since he is the head, we the members, there is one Son of God.

So he who loves the children of God loves the Son of God; and he who loves the Son of God loves the Father: nor can anyone love the Father, unless he love the Son; and he who loves the Son also loves the children of God.

What children of God? The members of the Son of God. And by loving he too becomes a member, and becomes through love part of the frame of the body of Christ; and there will be one Christ loving himself. For when the members love one another in turn, the body loves itself. *And if one member suffers, all the members suffer with it; and if one member is glorified, all the members rejoice with it.* And what does he say next? *But you are the body of Christ and members*[^11].

A little before he was speaking of brotherly love, and he says: *He who does not love his brother whom he sees, how will he be able to love God whom he does not see*?[^12] But if you love your brother, perhaps you love your brother, and do not love Christ? How can that be, when you love the members of Christ? So when you love the members of Christ, you love Christ; when you love Christ, you love the Son of God; when you love the Son of God, you love the Father also. So love cannot be separated.

Choose for yourself what to love; the rest follow you. You may say: I love God alone, God the Father. You lie: if you love, you do not love him alone; but if you love the Father, you love the Son also. See, you say, I love the Father, and I love the Son: but only this, the Father God and the Son God and our Lord Jesus Christ who ascended into heaven, and sits at the right hand of the Father, that Word through whom all things were made, and the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us[^13]; this alone I love.

You lie: for if you love the head, you love the members also; but if you do not love the members, neither do you love the head. Are you not terrified at the voice of the head crying from heaven on behalf of the members: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*?[^14] He called his persecutor the persecutor of his members: he called the one who loves him the lover of his members. Now you know what his members are, brethren; it is the Church of God.

*In this we know that we love the children of God, because we love God*[^15]*.* And how? Are the children of God not one thing, and God another? But he who loves God loves his commandments. And what are the commandments of God? *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another*[^16]. Let no one excuse himself by means of one love, from another love; this love altogether holds itself thus: just as it is itself framed together into one, so it makes one all the things that depend on it, and the fire, as it were, fuses them. It is gold, the mass is fused, and becomes one thing: but unless the heat of love kindles it, out of many it cannot be fused into one. *Because we love God, thence we know that we love the children of God.*

## God is loved without toil.

**4.** And how do we know that we love the children of God? *Because we love God, and do his commandments*[^17]*.* Here we sigh at the difficulty of doing the commandment of God. Hear what follows. Man, why do you toil in loving? In loving avarice. What you love is loved with toil: God is loved without toil.

Avarice is going to command toils, dangers, threshings, tribulations; and you are going to obey. To what end? That you may have something to fill your money chest, and lose your security. Perhaps you were more secure before you had it than when you began to have it. See what avarice commanded you: you filled your house, robbers are feared; you acquired gold, you lost sleep. See what avarice commanded you: Do it, and you did it.

What does God command you? Love me. You love gold, you are going to seek gold, and perhaps not find it: whoever seeks me, I am with him.

You are going to love honor, and perhaps not attain it: who has loved me, and not attained me? God says to you: You want to make for yourself a patron, or a powerful friend; you make your appeal through another, an inferior. Love me, God says to you: there is no appealing to me through another; love itself makes me present to you. What is sweeter than this love, brethren? Not without reason did you hear just now in the Psalm, brethren: *The unjust have told me of their delights; but not as your law, O Lord*[^18]. What is the law of God? The commandment of God. What is the commandment of God? That new commandment, which is called new for this reason, that it makes new: *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another.* Hear that this is the law of God: the Apostle says: *Bear one another's burdens, and so you will fulfill the law of Christ*[^19]. This is the consummation of all our works, love. There is the end: for this we run; to it we run; when we have come to it we shall rest.

## The end of the commandment is love, and God is love: there is your end.

**5.** You heard in the Psalm: *I have seen the end of all consummation*[^20]. He said: *I have seen the end of all consummation*: what had this man seen? Do we suppose he had climbed to the peak of some very high and very sharp mountain, and looked out, and seen the circuit of the earth and the circles of the whole world; and that therefore he said: *I have seen the end of all consummation*? If this is praiseworthy, let us ask of the Lord eyes of the flesh so keen that we may search out some most lofty mountain that is on the earth, from whose summit we may see the end of all consummation.

Do not go far: see, I tell you, climb the mountain, and see the end. Christ is the mountain; come to Christ, you see from there the end of all consummation. What is this end? Ask Paul: *But the end of the commandment is love from a pure heart, and a good conscience, and faith unfeigned*[^21]; and in another place: *But the fullness of the law is love*[^22]. What is so finished and bounded as fullness? Indeed, brethren, he sets an end in a praiseworthy sense.

Do not understand consumption, but consummation. For it is said one way: I have finished the bread; another way: I have finished the tunic. I have finished the bread by eating it; I have finished the tunic by weaving it.

And there "end" is heard, and there "end" is heard: but yet the bread is finished so as to be consumed, the tunic is finished so as to be completed; the bread is finished so as not to be, the tunic is finished so as to be perfected.

So hear "end" in this way, also when the Psalm is read, and you hear: *Unto the end, a Psalm of David.* You constantly hear this in the Psalms, and you ought to know what you hear. What is it, *unto the end*? *For Christ is the end of the Law, unto righteousness for everyone who believes*[^23].

And what is it, *Christ is the end*? Because Christ is God, and the end of the commandment is love, and God is love: because the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one. There is your end: elsewhere is the way.

Do not stick fast in the way, and not arrive at the end. Whatever else you come to, pass on until you arrive at the end. What is the end? *But for me it is good to cling to God*[^24]. You have clung to God, you have finished the way; you will abide in the homeland.

Attend. Someone seeks money; let it not be your end: pass on like a pilgrim. Seek a place to pass through, not to remain. But if you love it, you are entangled by avarice: avarice will be a chain on your feet; you cannot go further. So pass beyond this too; seek the end. You seek health of the body, do not yet remain there.

For what is this health of the body, which is destroyed by death, weakened by sickness, trifling, mortal, fleeting? Seek it, lest perhaps sickly health hinder your good works. So the end is not there; because it is sought for the sake of something else. Whatever is sought for the sake of something else, the end is not there: whatever is sought for its own sake and freely, there is the end.

You seek honors; perhaps you seek them in order to do something, to accomplish something, to please God: do not love the honor itself, lest you remain there. You seek praise?

If you seek God's praise, you do well: if you seek your own, you do badly; you remain in the way. But see, you are loved, you are praised: do not rejoice when you are praised in yourself; be praised in the Lord, so that you may sing: *In the Lord my soul shall be praised*[^25].

You speak some good discourse, and your discourse is praised? Let it not be praised as yours, the end is not there. If you place the end there, you are finished: but you are not finished as one perfected, but finished so as to be consumed.

805.0000000000000000000~So let your discourse not be praised as from you, as yours. But how should it be praised? As the Psalm says: *In God I will praise my discourse, in God I will praise my word.* From this it comes about that what follows is fulfilled in you: *In God I have hoped, I will not fear what man may do to me*[^26]. For when all that is yours is praised in God, there is no fear that your praise may perish; because God does not fail. So pass beyond this too.

## You love God, the brotherhood, the Church; no one takes them away, you are secure.

**6.** See, brethren, how many things we pass beyond, in which the end is not. We use these things as on a journey; we are refreshed as in the lodgings of an inn, and we pass on. Where, then, is the end? *Beloved, we are children of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be*; this was said here, in this Epistle.

808.0000000000000000000~So we are still in the way; still, wherever we come, we must pass on, until we arrive at some end. *We know that when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^27]. This is the end; there is perpetual praise, there is always Alleluia without ceasing.

So he named the end itself in the Psalm: *I have seen the end of all consummation.* And as though it were said to him: What is the end you have seen? *Your commandment is exceedingly broad*[^28].

This is the end, the breadth of the commandment. The breadth of the commandment is love; because where love is, there are no straits.

The Apostle was in that breadth, when he said: *Our mouth is open to you, O Corinthians; our heart is enlarged: you are not straitened in us*[^29]. Therefore *your commandment is exceedingly broad.* What is the broad commandment? *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another.*

Love, then, is not straitened. Do you want not to be straitened on earth? Dwell in what is broad. For whatever a man does to you does not straiten you; because you love that which a man cannot harm: you love God, you love the brotherhood, you love the law of God, you love the Church of God; it will be everlasting.

You toil on earth, but you will reach the promised fruit. Who takes from you what you love? If no one takes from you what you love, you sleep secure: rather, you keep watch secure, lest by sleeping you lose what you love. For it was not said in vain: *Enlighten my eyes, lest I ever sleep in death*[^30]. Those who close their eyes against love fall asleep in the desires of carnal delights. So wake up. For there are delights: to eat, to drink, to revel, to play, to hunt: all evils follow these vain shows. Do we not know that they are delights? Who would deny that they delight? But the law of God is loved more. Cry out against such persuaders: *The unjust have told me of their delights; but not as your law, O Lord.* That delight abides. It not only abides as a place to which you may come, but it also calls back the one who flees.

## Love: it cannot happen except that you do well.

815.0000000000000000000~7. *For this is the love of God, that we keep his commandments*[^31]*.* You have already heard: *On these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets.* How did he refuse to divide you among many pages? *On these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets.* On which two commandments?

*You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind*; and: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two commandments hang all the Law and the Prophets*[^32]. See, of which commandments this whole Epistle speaks. So hold to love, and be secure. Why do you fear lest you do evil to someone? Who does evil to the one he loves? Love: it cannot happen except that you do well.

But perhaps you rebuke? Love does this, not cruelty. But perhaps you beat? You do it for discipline; because the love of love itself does not permit you to neglect the undisciplined.

And it comes about in a way that the fruit is, as it were, diverse and contrary, so that sometimes hatred caresses, and love rages. Some man or other hates his enemy, and feigns friendship for him: he sees him do something evil, and praises it:

he wants him to be headlong, he wants him to go blind through the precipices of his own desires, from which perhaps he may not return; he praises him: *For the sinner is praised in the desires of his soul*[^33]; he applies to him the ointment of his flattery: see, he hates, and he praises.

Another sees his friend do some such thing, and calls him back; if he does not listen to him, he utters words even of chastisement, he chides, he quarrels: sometimes it comes to this necessity, that he quarrels. See, hatred caresses, and love quarrels. Do not attend to the words of the one who caresses, and the seeming cruelty of the one who chides; inspect the vein, seek the root from which they proceed. The one caresses in order to deceive, the other quarrels in order to correct.

So there is no need, brethren, that your heart be enlarged through us; obtain from God that you love one another.

Love all men, even your enemies: not because they are brethren, but that they may be brethren; so that you may always burn with brotherly love, whether toward one who has become a brother, or toward an enemy, that he may become a brother by your loving him.

Wherever you love a brother, you love a friend. He is now with you, he is now joined to you even in catholic unity. If you live well, you love a brother made out of an enemy. But you love someone who has not yet believed in Christ, or, if he has believed in Christ, believes as the demons believe; you reprove his vanity.

You, love him, and love him with brotherly love: he is not yet a brother, but you love him for this reason, that he may be a brother. So our whole love is brotherly, toward Christians, toward all his members.

The discipline of love, my brethren—its strength, flowers, fruit, beauty, pleasantness, food, drink, nourishment, embrace—is without satiety. If it so delights us as pilgrims, how shall we rejoice in the homeland?

## Do you adore Christ in the head, and blaspheme him in the body?

827.0000000000000000000~8. Let us run, then, my brethren, let us run, and love Christ. What Christ? Jesus Christ. Who is this? The Word of God. And how did he come to the sick? *The Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*[^34]. So what Scripture foretold was fulfilled: *Christ had to suffer, and to rise again from the dead on the third day.* Where does his body lie? Where do his members toil?

828.0000000000000000000~Where ought you to be, so that you may be under the head? *And that repentance and forgiveness of sins be preached in his name to all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^35]. There let your love be poured out. Christ says, and the Psalm, that is, the Spirit of God, says: *Your commandment is exceedingly broad*; and some man or other places the bounds of love in Africa!

Extend your love through the whole world, if you want to love Christ; because the members of Christ lie throughout the world. If you love a part, you are divided: if you are divided, you are not in the body: if you are not in the body, you are not under the head.

What does it profit that you believe, and blaspheme? You adore him in the head, you blaspheme him in the body. He loves his body. If you have cut yourself off from his body, the head has not cut itself off from its body. In vain do you honor me, the head cries to you from above; in vain do you honor me. As if someone wanted to kiss your head, and to trample your feet; perhaps with nailed boots he would crush your feet, while wanting to take hold of your head and kiss it; would you not, in the midst of his words of honor, cry out and say: What are you doing, man? You are trampling me.

You would not say: You are trampling my head; because he was honoring the head: but the head would cry out more for the trampled members than for itself, because it was being honored. Does not the head itself cry out: I do not want your honor; do not trample me? Now you say, if you can: Why have I trampled you?

Say it to the head: I wanted to kiss you, I wanted to embrace you. But do you not see, foolish man, that what you want to embrace reaches, through a certain frame of unity, to that which you trample? Above you honor me, below you trample me.

It grieves more for what you trample than it rejoices for what you honor; because that which you honor grieves for those whom you trample. How does the tongue cry out? It hurts me. It does not say: It hurts my foot; but: It hurts me, it says. O tongue, who touched you? Who struck you? Who goaded you? Who pricked you? No one, but I am joined to those who are trampled. How do you want me not to hurt, when I am not separated?

## Christ, ascending into heaven, commended his body.

**9.** So our Lord Jesus Christ, when ascending into heaven on the fortieth day, commended his body, where it was going to lie, because he saw that many would honor him because he ascended into heaven; and he saw that their honor is useless, if they trample his members on earth.

And lest anyone should err, and, while adoring the head in heaven, trample the feet on earth, he said where his members were. For when about to ascend he spoke his last words; after those words he did not speak on earth. The head, about to ascend into heaven, commended the members on earth, and departed. Now you do not find Christ speaking on earth: you find him speaking, but from heaven. And from heaven itself, why?

Because the members were being trampled on earth. For to the persecutor Saul he said from above: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me*?[^36] I have ascended into heaven, but I still lie on earth: here I sit at the right hand of the Father; there I still hunger, thirst, and am a pilgrim. How, then, did he commend his body on earth when about to ascend?

When the disciples asked him: *Lord, will you at this time present yourself, and when will the kingdom of Israel be?* he answered, about to go: *It is not yours to know the time that the Father has set in his own power: but you will receive the power of the Holy Spirit coming upon you, and you will be my witnesses.*

See where he pours out his body, see where he does not want himself to be trampled: *You will be my witnesses in Jerusalem, and in all Judea, and Samaria, and to all the earth*[^37]. See where I lie, I who ascend. For I ascend, because I am the head: my body still lies. Where does it lie? Throughout all the earth. Beware lest you strike, beware lest you violate, beware lest you trample: these are the last words of Christ, about to go into heaven.

Consider a man languishing on his bed, lying in his house, and worn away by sickness, near death, gasping, now in a way holding his soul between his teeth, who perhaps, anxious about some matter dear to him that he greatly loves, has it come into his mind, and calls his heirs and says: I beg you, do this. He in a way violently holds his soul, lest it depart before those words are confirmed. When he has dictated those last words, he breathes out his soul: the corpse is carried to the tomb. How do his heirs remember the last words of the dying man? How, if someone arises who says to them: Do not do it: what then do they say? So am I not to do what my father, breathing out his soul, last commanded me, what sounded last in my ears, as my father was departing from here? Any other words of his I can hold otherwise; the last words hold me more: I did not see him afterward, I did not hear him speaking.

Brethren, consider with Christian hearts: if to heirs the words of one about to go into the tomb are so sweet, so welcome, of so great weight; to the heirs of Christ, what ought the last words to be, of one not about to return into a tomb, but about to ascend into heaven!

842.0000000000000000000~For as for the man who lived and died, his soul is carried off to other places, his body is laid in the earth: whether those words are carried out or not, does not concern him; he now does something else, or suffers something else: either he rejoices in the bosom of Abraham, or in the eternal fire he longs for a little water[^38];

but in his tomb the corpse lies without feeling; and the last words of the dying man are kept. What do those hope for themselves who do not keep the last words of him who sits in heaven, who sees from above whether they are despised or not despised? Of him who said: *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?* who keeps for judgment whatever he sees his members suffer?

## Return to your mistress, hold to the Lord's peace.

**10.** And what have we done, they say? We have suffered persecution, we have not done it. You have done it, O wretched ones: first, because you have divided the Church. The sword of the tongue is greater than that of iron.

846.0000000000000000000~Hagar, the maidservant of Sarah, was proud; and she was afflicted by her mistress on account of her pride. That was discipline, not punishment. And so, when she had withdrawn from her mistress, what did the angel say to her? *Return to your mistress*[^39]. So then, carnal soul, like the proud maidservant, if perhaps you have suffered some troubles on account of discipline, why do you rave? Return to your mistress, hold to the Lord's peace. See, the Gospels are brought forth, we read where the Church is spread abroad: it is disputed against, and we are told: *Betrayers*.

847.0000000000000000000~Betrayers of what thing? Christ commends his Church, and you do not believe; am I going to believe you who curse my parents? Do you want me to believe you about betrayers? You first believe Christ. What is fitting? Christ is God, you are a man: which should be believed first? Christ has spread his Church throughout the whole world: I say, despise it; the Gospel speaks, beware. What does the Gospel say? *Christ had to suffer, and to rise from the dead on the third day, and that repentance and forgiveness of sins be preached in his name*[^40].

Where there is forgiveness of sins, there is the Church. How is it the Church? For to it was said: *I will give you the keys of the kingdom of heaven: and whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed also in heaven; and whatever you bind on earth shall be bound also in heaven*[^41].

849.0000000000000000000~Where is this forgiveness of sins spread abroad? *Through all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^42]. See, believe Christ. But because you understand that, if you believe Christ, you have nothing to say about betrayers; you want me to believe my parents who curse, rather than that you should believe Christ who foretells.
