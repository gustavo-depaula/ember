# Prologue

## It is John's: he said much, and nearly everything about love.

Your Holiness remembers that we are accustomed to treat the Gospel according to John in the order of its lections; but because there has now intervened the solemnity of the holy days, on which certain set lections from the Gospel must be read aloud in the Church—lections that recur each year in such a way that no others can take their place—the order that we had taken up has been, by necessity, briefly interrupted, not abandoned.

Now when I was considering what, in keeping with the gladness of these present days, I might take up with you from the Scriptures during this week—so far as the Lord deigns to grant—something that could be finished in these seven or eight days, the Epistle of blessed John came to mind: so that, having briefly set aside his Gospel, we might in treating his Epistle not depart from him; especially because in this Epistle, which is sweet enough to all whose palate of the heart is healthy, where the bread of God may have its savor, and memorable enough in the holy Church of God, it is above all love that is commended. He said much, and nearly everything about love.

Whoever has within himself the wherewithal to hear must needs rejoice at what he hears. For this reading will be to him like oil upon a flame; if there is there something to be nourished, it is nourished, and grows, and abides.

Likewise, for some it must be like flame applied to kindling: so that if it was not burning, it may be kindled by the coming of the word. For in some, what is there is nourished; in some, what is lacking is kindled—so that we may all rejoice in one love. But where there is love, there is peace; and where there is humility, there is love.

Let us now listen to him himself; and to his words, which the Lord supplies, let us speak also to you, that you may understand them well.
