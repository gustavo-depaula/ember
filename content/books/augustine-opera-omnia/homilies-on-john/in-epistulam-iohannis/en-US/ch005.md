# Homily 4: *And it is true*

## (1 John 2:27—3:9).

> *We conquer the devil if, with God speaking in the heart and faith kept, we do not defend our sin. And we are of God if, being Christians in our conduct too, we patiently await Christ who is to come, righteous by faith, imitators of God through Christ.*

## God speaks within to those who make room for him.

**1.** You remember, brethren, that yesterday's reading was ended at this point, that *you have no need that anyone teach you, but the anointing itself teaches you about all things*[^1]. But this, as I am sure you recall, we expounded to you thus: that we who speak from without to your ears are as workmen, applying cultivation from without to the tree, but cannot give the increase, nor form the fruits: but he who created you, and redeemed you, and called you, dwelling in you by faith and his Spirit, unless he speaks to you within, in vain do we make our noise.

Whence does this appear? Because, when many hear, not all are persuaded of what is said; but only those to whom God speaks within. But he speaks within to those who make room for him: and those make room for God who do not make room for the devil. For the devil wishes to inhabit the hearts of men, and to speak there all the things that avail for seduction.

But what does the Lord Jesus say? *The prince of this world has been cast out*[^2]. Whence has he been cast out? surely not outside heaven and earth? surely not outside the structure of the world? But outside the hearts of believers. The invader being cast out, let the redeemer dwell there; for he himself redeemed who created.

And the devil now assails from without, he does not conquer him who possesses within. But he assails from without, sending in various temptations: but he to whom God speaks within, and the anointing of which you have heard, does not consent.

## Do you keep faith, and God keeps his promise.

**2.** *And it is true,* he says, that same anointing; that is, the very Spirit of the Lord who teaches men cannot lie. *And it is no lie. As it has taught you, abide in it. And now, little children, abide in him, that when he is made manifest, we may have confidence in his sight, that we may not be confounded by him at his coming*[^3].

You see, brethren; we believe in Jesus whom we have not seen: those announced him who saw him, who touched him, who heard the word from his own mouth; and that they might persuade the human race of these things, they were sent by him, they did not dare to go of themselves. And whither were they sent? You heard when the Gospel was read: *Go, preach the Gospel to every creature that is under heaven*[^4].

Everywhere, then, the disciples were sent, signs and wonders bearing witness, that they might be believed, because they were saying what they had seen. And we believe in him whom we have not seen, and we await his coming. Whoever await him by faith will rejoice when he comes: those who are without faith, when he comes, what they now do not see, they will blush at.

And that confounding will not be of one day and pass away, as those are wont to be confounded who are found in some fault, and are insulted by men. That confounding will lead the confounded over to the left, to hear: *Go into eternal fire, which has been prepared for the devil and his angels*[^5].

Let us, then, abide in his words, lest we be confounded when he comes. For he himself says in the Gospel to those who had believed in him: *If you abide in my word, you are truly my disciples*[^6]*.* And as though they should say: With what fruit? *And you shall know,* he says, *the truth, and the truth has set you free*[^7].

For now our salvation is in hope, not yet in reality: for we do not yet hold what was promised, but we hope it is to come. But he who promised is faithful; he does not deceive you: only do not you fail, but await the promise. For Truth does not know how to deceive. Do not you be a liar, so as to profess one thing and do another: do you keep faith, and he keeps his promise. But if you do not keep faith, you have defrauded yourself, not he who promised.

## Do not defend your sin, be with God: you have conquered the devil.

**3.** *If you know that he is just, know that everyone who does righteousness has been born of him*[^8]*.* Our righteousness now is from faith. Perfect righteousness is only in the angels; and scarcely in the angels, if they are compared to God:

yet if there is any perfect righteousness of the souls and spirits that God created, it is in the holy angels, just, good, turned away by no lapse, falling by no pride, but abiding always in the contemplation of the Word of God, and having nothing else sweet but him by whom they were created; in them is perfect righteousness: but in us it has begun to be, from faith, according to the Spirit.

You heard when the Psalm was read: *Begin to the Lord with confession*[^9]. *Begin,* he says: the beginning of our righteousness is the confession of sins. You have begun not to defend your sin; you have already made a start at righteousness: but it will be made perfect in you, when nothing else will delight you to do, when death will be swallowed up in victory[^10], when no concupiscence will titillate, when there will be no struggle with flesh and blood, when there will be the crown of victory, the triumph over the enemy; then will righteousness be perfect.

Now we still fight: if we fight, we are in the arena; we strike, and we are struck: but he who conquers is awaited. But he conquers who, even in what he strikes, presumes not on his own strength, but on God who exhorts. The devil alone fights against us. We, if we are with God, conquer the devil: for even if you alone fight with the devil, you will be conquered. He is a practiced foe: of how many victories? Consider whence he has cast men down: that we might be born mortal, he first cast our very origin out of paradise. What, then, is to be done, since he is so practiced?

Let the Almighty be invoked against the practiced devil. Let him who cannot be conquered dwell in you, and secure you will conquer him who is wont to conquer. But whom? Those in whom God does not dwell. For, that you may know, brethren, Adam, set in paradise, despised the precept of God, and lifted up his neck as though wishing to be in his own power, and unwilling to be subject to the will of God, and fell from that immortality, from that blessedness[^11].

But a certain man, now practiced, born mortal, while he sat on a dunghill, putrid with worms, conquered the devil: Adam himself too conquered, and in Job himself; because Job is of his race. Therefore Adam, conquered in paradise, conquered on the dunghill. When he was in paradise, he listened to the persuasion of the woman, whom the devil had sent in to him:

but when he was on the dunghill, he said to Eve: *You have spoken like one of the foolish women*[^12]. There he lent his ear; here he gave an answer: when he was happy, he listened; when he was scourged, he conquered.

Therefore see what follows, brethren, in this Epistle; for he commends this to us, that we should indeed conquer the devil, but not of ourselves. *If you know that he is just,* he says, *know that everyone who does righteousness has been born of him*: of God, of Christ. And because he said: *Has been born of him,* he exhorts us. Now then, because we have been born of him, we are perfect.

## Many are called Christians, and in the realities are not found to be such.

**4.** Listen: *Behold what sort of love the Father has given us, that we should be called sons of God and should be so*[^13]*.* For those who are called and are not, what does the name profit them where the reality is not? How many are called physicians, who do not know how to heal? how many are called watchmen, who sleep all night? So many are called Christians, and in the realities are not found to be such; because they are not what they are called, that is, in their life, in their conduct, in faith, in hope, in love.

But here what did you hear, brethren? *Behold what sort of love the Father has given us, that we should be called sons of God and should be so. For this reason the world does not know us, because it did not know him*[^14]. The whole world Christian, and the whole world impious; for throughout the whole world there are the impious, and throughout the whole world the pious: the latter do not know the former. How do we suppose they do not know them? They insult those who live well.

Attend and see, for perhaps there are such even among you. Each one of you who now lives piously, who despises worldly things, who does not wish to go to the spectacles, who does not wish to make himself drunk as though it were a solemn observance, and—what is more grievous—to become unclean on the holy days, taking that as a sanction; whoever has refused to do these things, how he is insulted by those who do them? Would he be insulted if he were recognized?

But why is he not recognized? The world does not recognize him. Who is the world? Those inhabitants of the world, as a house is named from its inhabitants. These things have now often been said, and we do not repeat them to you out of hatred. Now when you hear "the world" in the bad sense, understand only the lovers of the world; because by love they inhabit it, and from that which they inhabit they have earned to have the name.

For this reason the world did not know us, because it did not know him. The Lord Jesus Christ himself too walked here, in the flesh he was God, he lay hidden in weakness. And whence was he not known? Because he reproved all sins in men. Those men, by loving the delights of sins, did not recognize God: by loving what the fever urged, they did injury to the physician.

## He will come to judge, in the way he had come to be judged.

**5.** What, then, of us? We have now been born of him; but because we are in hope: *Most beloved,* he says, *now we are sons of God.* Now already? What, then, is it that we await, if we are already sons of God? *And it has not yet,* he says, *been made manifest what we shall be.* But what shall we be other than sons of God? Hear what follows: *We know that when he appears, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is*[^15].

Let your Charity understand. A great matter: *We know that when he appears, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.* Now attend to what is meant by *is.* You know what is meant[^16]. He *is* what is meant, and not only is he called so, but he truly is, he is unchangeable; he abides always, knows not how to be changed, is in no part corrupted: he neither advances, because he is perfect; nor falls back, because he is eternal. And what is this? *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^17].

And what is this? *Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal with God*[^18]. In this way the wicked cannot see Christ in the form of God, the Word of God, the Only-begotten of the Father, equal to the Father.

But according to the fact that the Word was made flesh, the wicked too will be able to see him: for on the day of judgment the wicked too will see him; for he will come to judge in the way he had come to be judged. In that form a man, but God: for *cursed is everyone who puts his hope in man*[^19]. As a man he came to be judged, as a man he will come to judge.

And if he will not be seen, what is that which is written: *They shall look on him whom they pierced*[^20]? For it was said of the impious that they shall look on him, and be confounded. How will the impious not see him, when he sets some on the right hand, others on the left?

To those set on the right hand he will say: *Come, blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*[^21]*.* To those set on the left he will say: *Go into eternal fire*[^22]. They will see, but the form of a servant: the form of God they will not see. Why? Because they are impious; and the Lord himself says: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^23]. Therefore we are going to see a certain vision, brethren, which neither eye has seen, nor ear heard, nor has it ascended into the heart of man[^24]: a certain vision, a vision surpassing all earthly beauties, of gold, of silver, of groves and fields, the beauty of the sea and the air, the beauty of the sun and the moon, the beauty of the stars, the beauty of the angels, surpassing all things; because from it all things are beautiful.

## Let us desire: we are to be filled.

**6.** What, then, shall we be, when we shall see this? What has been promised us? *We shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.* As far as it could, my tongue has sounded it forth; let the rest be thought in the heart. For what did even John himself say, in comparison with him who is, or what can be said by us men, far unequal to his merits?

Let us return, then, to that anointing of his, let us return to that anointing which teaches within what we cannot speak; and because you cannot now see, let your office be in desire. The whole life of a good Christian is holy desire.

But what you desire, you do not yet see; but by desiring you are made capable, so that when what you are to see comes, you may be filled.

For just as, if you wish to fill some pouch, and you know how great is what will be given, you stretch the pouch, whether of a sack or a wineskin or some other thing: you know how much you are going to put in, and you see that the pouch is narrow; by stretching you make it more capacious: so God, by deferring, stretches our desire; by desiring he stretches the mind; by stretching he makes it capable.

Let us desire, then, brethren, because we are to be filled. See Paul stretching his pouch, that he may be able to hold what is to come. For he says: *Not that I have already received, or am already perfect: brethren, I do not consider that I have laid hold of it*[^25]*.* What, then, are you doing in this life, if you have not yet laid hold of it? *But one thing, forgetting the things that are behind, stretching forward to the things that are ahead, I follow according to my purpose toward the prize of the heavenly calling*[^26].

He said he was stretched forward, and said he follows according to his purpose. He felt himself too small to lay hold of what eye has not seen, nor ear heard, nor has ascended into the heart of man.

This is our life, that by desiring we be exercised. But holy desire exercises us just so far as we have cut off our desires from love of the age. We have already said at some time: Empty out what is to be filled. You are to be filled with good, pour out the evil. Suppose God wishes to fill you with honey: if you are full of vinegar, where will you put the honey? What the vessel was carrying must be poured out: the vessel itself must be cleansed; it must be cleansed, even if with labor, with scouring, that it may be made fit for a certain thing.

Let us say "honey," let us say "gold," let us say "wine"; whatever we say, that cannot be said, whatever we wish to say, is called God. And when we say "God," what have we said? Are these two syllables the whole of what we await? Whatever, then, we have been able to say is beneath it: let us stretch ourselves toward him, that when he comes, he may fill us. For *we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is.*

## Patience itself exercises desire.

**7.** *And everyone who has this hope in him*[^27]*.* You see that he has set us in hope. You see how the apostle Paul agrees with his fellow apostle: *By hope we have been saved. But hope that is seen is not hope: for what a man sees, why does he hope for it? But if we hope for what we do not see, we await it through patience*[^28].

Patience itself exercises desire. Do you abide, for he abides: and persevere in walking, that you may arrive; because that toward which you tend will not depart. See: *And everyone who has this hope in him purifies himself, just as he too is pure*[^29]*.* See how he has not taken away free choice, in saying, *he purifies himself.* Who purifies us but God? But God does not purify you against your will.

Therefore, in that you join your will to God, you purify yourself. You purify yourself, not of yourself, but of him who comes to dwell in you. Yet because you do something there by your will, for that reason something too is attributed to you. And it is attributed to you for this end, that you may say, as in the Psalm: *Be my helper, do not forsake me*[^30]. If you say: *Be my helper,* you do something: for if you do nothing, how does he help you?

## Let us not now forsake the righteousness that is from faith.

**8.** *Everyone who commits sin commits iniquity also*[^31]*.* Let no one say: Sin is one thing, iniquity another: let no one say: I am a sinful man, but I am not iniquitous. For *everyone who commits sin commits iniquity also. Sin is iniquity.* What, then, are we to do about our sins and iniquities? Hear what he says: *And you know that he was made manifest, to take away sin; and in him there is no sin*[^32]*.* He in whom there is no sin came to take away sin. For if there were sin in him too, it would have to be taken away from him, he would not himself take it away. *Everyone who abides in him does not sin.* So far as he abides in him, so far he does not sin. *Everyone who sins has not seen him, nor known him.* This is a great question: *Everyone who sins has not seen him, nor known him*[^33]*.*

It is no wonder. We have not seen him, but we are going to see him; we have not known him, but we are going to know him: we believe in him whom we have not known. Or perhaps we have known him by faith, and have not yet known him by sight? But in faith we have both seen and known him. For if faith does not yet see, why are we called enlightened? There is an enlightenment through faith, there is an enlightenment through sight.

Now, while we are on pilgrimage, we walk by faith, not by sight[^34]. Therefore our righteousness too is by faith, not by sight. Our righteousness will be perfect when we shall see by sight. Let us not now forsake that righteousness which is from faith, since *the just man lives by faith*[^35], as the Apostle says. *Everyone who abides in him does not sin.* For *everyone who sins has not seen him, nor known him.* He who sins does not believe: but if he believes, in so far as it pertains to his faith, he does not sin.

## We too have the image of God, but not as the Son has.

**9.** *Little children, let no one seduce you. He who does righteousness is just, just as he too is just*[^36]*.* When we have heard that we are just, *just as he too,* ought we to suppose ourselves equal to God? You ought to know what *just as* means: for already he said: *He purifies himself, just as he himself is pure.*

Is our chastity, then, now matched and equal to the chastity of God, and our righteousness to the righteousness of God? Who would say this? But *just as* is not always wont to be said with respect to equality. As, for example, when this spacious basilica has been seen, if someone wishes to make a smaller one, yet in proportion to its measurements, so that, for example, if this one is one unit wide and two long; he too makes one one unit wide and two long:

he seems to have made it just as this one is. But this one has, for example, a hundred cubits, that one thirty: and so it is, and it is unequal. You see that *just as* is not always referred to parity and equality.

For example, see how great a difference there is between a man's face and its image in a mirror: there is a face in the image, a face in the body; the image in imitation, the body in reality. And what do we say? For as the eyes are here, so they are there; as the ears are here, so the ears are there. The thing is unequal; but *just as* is said with respect to likeness.

Therefore we too have the image of God; but not that which the Son has, who is equal to the Father; yet we too, in our small measure, if we were not as he is, would in no part be called like him.

Therefore he purifies us just as he too is pure; but he is pure by eternity, we are pure by faith: we are just just as he too is just; but he in that very unchangeable perpetuity, we just by believing in him whom we do not see, that one day we may see. And when our righteousness shall be perfect, when we shall have been made equal to the angels; not even then will it be matched to him. How far, then, is it now from him, when not even then will it be on a par?

## You will be a son of the devil, by imitating.

**10.** *He who commits sin is of the devil; for the devil sins from the beginning*[^37]*. Is of the devil,* you know what he means: by imitating the devil. For the devil made no one, begot no one, created no one: but whoever has imitated the devil, as though born of him, becomes a son of the devil by imitating, not by being properly born.

How are you a son of Abraham? did Abraham beget you? In the way the Jews, sons of Abraham not imitating the faith of Abraham, were made sons of the devil: they were born of the flesh of Abraham, and did not imitate the faith of Abraham.

If, then, those who were born of him were disinherited, because they did not imitate him; you who were not born of him are made a son, and so you will be a son by imitating. And if you have imitated the devil, because he stood forth proud and impious against God, you will be a son of the devil, by imitating; not because he created you, or begot you.

## Adam and Christ.

**11.** *For this the Son of God was made manifest*[^38]*.* Come now, brethren, all sinners are born of the devil, in so far as they are sinners. Adam was made by God: but when he consented to the devil, he was born of the devil; and he begot all such as he himself was. With that very concupiscence we are born; and before we add our own debts, we are born of that condemnation. For if we are born with no sin, what is it that men run with infants to Baptism, that they may be loosed? Therefore mark two births, brethren, Adam and Christ: there are two men; but one of them is man, man; the other of them is man, God. Through the man, man, we are sinners: through the man, God, we are justified. That birth cast us down to death; this birth raised us up to life: that birth drags sin with it; this birth frees from sin. For Christ came as man for this, that he might loose the sins of men. *For this the Son of God was made manifest, that he might loose the works of the devil.*

## A great question about sin.

**12.** The rest I commend to your Charity, lest I weigh you down. For this is the very question in whose solving we labor, namely that we call ourselves sinners: for if anyone says he is without sin, he is a liar. And in this same Epistle of John we find: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves.* For you ought to remember what went before: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^39].

And again in what follows you hear: *He who is born of God does not sin: He who commits sin has not seen him, nor known him: Everyone who commits sin is of the devil*[^40]*.* Sin is not from God. Again he terrifies us. How are we born of God, and how do we confess ourselves sinners? Or are we to say that we are not born of God? And what do these sacraments do in infants? What did John say? *He who is born of God does not sin.* John himself again said: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.* It is a great and narrow question; and to solving it I would make your Charity attentive. In the name of the Lord, tomorrow we shall expound what he grants concerning it.
