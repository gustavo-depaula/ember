# Homily 2: *I write to you, little children*

## (1 John 2:12-17).

> *Christ died according to the Scriptures, and his Church is spread throughout the whole world. We are children, because we are born; fathers, because we know the Beginning; young men, because we fight. The love of God forbids the three concupiscences of love of the world.*

## Whatever there is in the Scriptures sounds forth Christ, but only if it find ears.

**1.** All things that are read from the holy Scriptures we ought to hear attentively, for our instruction and salvation. Yet most of all are those things to be committed to memory which avail most against the heretics; whose snares do not cease to ensnare the weaker and more negligent. Remember that our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ both died for us and rose again; he died, that is, on account of our sins, and rose again on account of our justification[^1].

As you just now heard that the eyes of the two disciples whom he found on the road were held, that they might not recognize him: and he found them despairing of the redemption that was in Christ, and supposing that he had now suffered and died, like a man, and not thinking that, as the Son of God, he ever lives; and that he had so died in the flesh as not to come to life again, but was like one of the Prophets: as you, who were attentive a little while ago, heard their words.

Then he opened to them the Scriptures, beginning from Moses through all the Prophets, showing them that all the things he had suffered had been foretold; lest they be the more disturbed if the Lord had risen, and the more disbelieve him, if these things had not been foretold of him beforehand. For the firmness of faith lies in this, that all the things that happened in Christ were foretold. The disciples, then, did not recognize him except in the breaking of the bread. And truly, whoever does not eat and drink judgment to himself recognizes Christ in the breaking of the bread.

Afterward the eleven too thought they were seeing a spirit. He who offered himself to be crucified offered himself to be touched; to be crucified by enemies, to be touched by friends: yet the physician of all, both of the impiety of the former and of the unbelief of the latter. For you heard, when the Acts of the Apostles were read, how many thousands believed from among the slayers of Christ[^2]. If those who had killed him believed afterward, were those who doubted a little not going to believe?

And yet to them too (which you ought most of all to mark, and to commit to your memory, namely that against insidious errors God willed to set a foundation in the Scriptures, against which no one dares to speak who wishes in any way to seem a Christian), when he had offered himself to be touched, it did not suffice him unless he confirmed the heart of the believers from the Scriptures: for he was looking ahead to us who were to come; in whom we do not have what we may touch, but we have what we may read.

For if they believed for this reason, that they held and touched, what shall we do? Christ has now ascended into heaven, he is not to come except at the end, to judge the living and the dead: whence are we to believe, except whence he willed even them, though they touched, to be confirmed?

For he opened to them the Scriptures, and showed them that Christ had to suffer, and that all things had to be fulfilled which are written of him in the law of Moses, and the Prophets, and the Psalms. He embraced the whole ancient text of the Scriptures. Whatever there is of those Scriptures sounds forth Christ; but only if it find ears. And he opened their understanding, that they might understand the Scriptures. Whence this too must be prayed for by us, that he himself may open our understanding.

## In the passion and resurrection the bride Church, spread throughout all nations, was joined to the bridegroom Christ.

**2.** But what did the Lord show to be written of himself in the law of Moses, and the Prophets, and the Psalms? what did he show? Let him say it himself. The Evangelist set this down briefly, so that we might know what, in so great a breadth of the Scriptures, we ought to believe and understand. Surely there are many pages, and many books, and they all have this which the Lord said briefly to his disciples.

What is this? That *Christ had to suffer, and to rise again on the third day*[^3]*.* You have now, concerning the bridegroom, that *Christ had to suffer and to rise again.* The bridegroom has been commended to us. Of the bride let us see what he says; that you, when you have come to know bridegroom and bride, may not come to the wedding without cause. For the celebration of the wedding is the whole celebration: the wedding of the Church is celebrated.

The son of the king is to take a wife, and the son of the king is himself king: and those who attend, they themselves are the bride. Not as in carnal weddings, where some attend the wedding and another is married; in the Church those who attend, if they attend well, become the bride.

For the whole Church is the bride of Christ, whose beginning and firstfruits is the flesh of Christ: there the bride was joined to the bridegroom in the flesh. Rightly, when he commended that flesh, did he break bread; and rightly in the breaking of the bread were the eyes of the disciples opened, and they recognized him.

What, then, did the Lord say was written of himself in the Law, and the Prophets, and the Psalms? That *Christ had to suffer.* If he had not added, *and to rise again,* rightly would they mourn whose eyes were held; but it was foretold also that he would *rise again.* And why this? why did Christ have to suffer and to rise again? On account of that psalm which we commended to you most of all, on the fourth day of the week, at the first station of the last week. Why did Christ have to suffer and to rise again? For this reason: *All the ends of the earth shall remember and turn to the Lord, and all the families of the nations shall worship in his sight*[^4]. For, that you may know that *Christ had to suffer and to rise again*; and here too what did he add, so that, after the commendation of the bridegroom, he might commend also the bride? *And that there be preached,* he says, *in his name repentance and the remission of sins among all nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^5].

You have heard, brethren, hold it fast. Let no one doubt of the Church, that it is among all nations; let no one doubt that it began from Jerusalem, and filled all nations. We recognize the field where the vine was planted: but when it has grown, we do not recognize it, because it has occupied the whole. Whence did it begin? *From Jerusalem.* Where did it arrive? At *all nations.*

A few remain, it will hold all. Meanwhile, while it holds all, certain useless shoots seemed to the farmer to need cutting off; and they made heresies and schisms.

Let not the things that have been cut off lead you, so that you too be cut off; rather, exhort the things that have been cut off, that they may be grafted in again.

It is manifest that Christ suffered, rose again, and ascended into heaven: manifest too is the Church, since in his name repentance and the remission of sins are preached among all nations. Whence did it begin? *Beginning from Jerusalem.* The fool hears, the empty man, and—what more shall I say than the blind man, who does not see so great a mountain; who closes his eyes against the lamp set on the lampstand?

## Where now all tongues were sounding, all tongues were shown to be about to believe.

**3.** When we say to them: If you are Catholic Christians, be in communion with that Church from which the Gospel is spread throughout the whole world; be in communion with that Jerusalem—when we say this to them, they answer us: We are not in communion with that city where our king was killed, where our Lord was killed: as though they hated the city where our Lord was killed.

The Jews killed him whom they found on earth, these men blow away with contempt him who sits in heaven. Which are worse, those who despised him because they thought him a man, or those who blow away his sacraments, whom they now confess to be God? But they hate, of course, the city in which their Lord was killed. Pious and merciful men, they grieve much that Christ was killed, and they kill Christ in men! But he loved that city, and had mercy on it: thence he said his preaching began: *Beginning from Jerusalem.* There he made the beginning of the preaching of his name: and do you shudder at communion with that city? It is no wonder if, being cut off, you have come to hate the root. What does he say to his disciples? *Be seated in the city, because I send my promise upon you*[^6].

Behold what city they hate. Perhaps they would love it, if the Jews, the slayers of Christ, dwelt in it. For it is manifest that all the slayers of Christ, that is, the Jews, were expelled from that city. The city that had men raging against Christ now has men adoring Christ. For this reason these men hate it, because there are Christians in it. There he willed his disciples to sit, and there to send them the Holy Spirit. Where was the Church begun, except where the Holy Spirit came from heaven, and filled the hundred and twenty sitting in one place?

That number twelve had been multiplied tenfold. A hundred and twenty men were sitting, and the Holy Spirit came, and filled the whole place, and there came a sound, as if a violent blast were borne along, and tongues as of fire were divided. You heard the Acts of the Apostles, that very lection was read today: *They began to speak in tongues, as the Spirit gave them to utter*[^7]*.* And all who were present there, Jews coming from various nations, each one recognized his own tongue; and they marveled that those uneducated and unskilled men had suddenly learned, not one or two tongues, but those of absolutely all nations.

Where, then, all tongues were now sounding, all tongues were shown to be about to believe. But these men, who love Christ much, and therefore are unwilling to be in communion with the city that killed Christ, so honor Christ that they say he has remained confined to two tongues, the Latin and the Punic, that is, the African. Does Christ hold only two tongues? For these two tongues alone are in the party of Donatus, they have no more.

Let us awake, brethren, let us rather see the gift of the Spirit of God, and believe the things foretold of it, and see fulfilled the things that were foretold in the Psalm: *There are no utterances nor words, whose voices are not heard*[^8]*.* And lest perhaps the tongues themselves came to one place, and not rather the gift of Christ came to all tongues, hear what follows: *Their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^9]*.*

Why this? *Because in the sun he set his tabernacle*[^10], that is, in the open. His tabernacle, his flesh; his tabernacle, his Church: it is set in the sun, it is not in the night, but in the day. But why do they not recognize it? Return to the reading at which the end was made yesterday, and see why they do not recognize it: *He who hates his brother walks in darkness, and does not know where he goes; for the darkness has blinded his eyes*[^11]*.* Let us, then, see what follows, and not be in darkness. Whence shall we not be in darkness? If we love the brethren. Whence is it proved that we love the brotherhood? Because we do not rend unity, because we hold fast to love.

## Little children, because, your sins being forgiven, you are born in the name of Christ.

**4.** *I write to you, little children, because your sins are forgiven you through his name*[^12]*.* Little children, then, because, your sins being forgiven, you are born. But through whose name are sins forgiven? Surely not through Augustine's? Then neither through the name of Donatus[^13]. You see who Augustine is, or who Donatus is: neither through the name of Paul, nor through the name of Peter.

For to those dividing the Church among themselves, and trying to make parties out of unity, the mother love, giving birth to little ones in the Apostle, lays bare her inmost being, in a manner rends open her breasts with words, weeps over the children she sees carried out, calls back to one name those who wished to make many names for themselves, drives them away from love of her so that Christ may be loved, and says: *Was Paul crucified for you? or were you baptized in the name of Paul*?[^14] What does she say? I do not wish you to be mine, but to be with me: be with me; we are all his who died for us, who was crucified for us: whence here too: *Your sins are forgiven you through his name,* not through the name of any man.

## Fathers, because you have known him who is from the beginning.

**5.** *I write to you, fathers.* Why first children? *Because your sins are forgiven you through his name,* and you are regenerated into a new life, therefore children. Why fathers? *Because you have known him who is from the beginning*[^15]: for the beginning pertains to fatherhood. Christ is new in the flesh, but ancient in his divinity. How ancient do we suppose? of how many years? Do we suppose him greater than his mother? Greater plainly than his mother: *for all things were made through him*[^16].

If all things, then the Ancient One made even the mother herself, of whom the new one would be born. Do we suppose him before the mother alone? He is ancient also before the mother's forefathers. Abraham is the forefather of his mother; and the Lord says: *Before Abraham I am*[^17]. Do we say, before Abraham?

Heaven and earth were made before man existed. Before these the Lord was, nay rather, is. For he excellently did not say: Before Abraham I was; but: *Before Abraham I am.* For what is said to have been, is not; and what is said to be going to be, is not yet: he knows nothing but to be.

Inasmuch as he is God, he knows being; he does not know to have been and to be going to be. There is there one day, but everlasting. They do not place that day in the middle between yesterday and tomorrow: for, yesterday being ended, today, beginning, will be ended when tomorrow comes. That one day is there without darkness, without night, without intervals, without measure, without hours.

Call it what you will: if you will, it is a day; if you will, it is a year; if you will, it is years. For it is said of him: *And your years shall not fail*[^18]. But when was it called a day? When it was said to the Lord: *This day I have begotten you*[^19]. Begotten of the eternal Father, begotten from eternity, begotten in eternity: with no beginning, no end, no span of breadth; for he is what he is, for he is he who is.

This name of his he told to Moses: *You shall say to them: He who is has sent me to you*[^20]. What, then, before Abraham? what before Noah? what before Adam? Hear the Scripture: *Before the morning star I begot you*[^21].

Lastly, before heaven and earth. Why? Because *all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing*[^22]. Therefore, fathers, take notice: for men become fathers by knowing what is from the beginning.

## Young men, because you have conquered the evil one.

**6.** *I write to you, young men.* They are children, they are fathers, they are young men; children, because they are born; fathers, because they know the beginning: but why young men? *Because you have conquered the evil one*[^23]*.* In children, birth; in fathers, antiquity; in young men, strength. If the evil one is conquered by young men, he fights against us.

He fights, but he does not vanquish. Why? Because we are strong, or because he is strong in us who was found weak in the hands of his persecutors? He made us strong who did not resist his persecutors. For he was crucified out of weakness, but he lives by the power of God[^24].

**7.** *I write to you, boys.* Whence boys? *Because you have known the Father*[^25]*. I write to you, fathers*: he commends this, and repeats it: *Because you have known him who is from the beginning.* Remember that you are fathers:

if you forget him who is from the beginning, you have lost your fatherhood. *I write to you, young men.* Again and again consider that you are young men: fight, that you may conquer; conquer, that you may be crowned; be humble, lest you fall in the fight. *I write to you, young men, because you are strong, and the word of God abides in you, and you have conquered the evil one*[^26]*.*

## How shall we be able to love God, if we love the world?

**8.** All these things, brethren—that we have known what is from the beginning, that we are strong, that we have known the Father: do all these commend, as it were, knowledge, and not commend love? If we have come to know, let us love: for knowledge without love has not saved. *Knowledge puffs up, love builds up*[^27].

If you wish to confess and not to love, you begin to be like the demons. The demons confessed the Son of God, and said: *What have we to do with you*?[^28] and they were driven back. Confess, and embrace. For they feared on account of their own iniquities; you, love the forgiver of your iniquities.

But how shall we be able to love God, if we love the world? He prepares us, then, to be inhabited by love. There are two loves, of the world and of God: if love of the world dwells there, there is no way for love of God to enter: let love of the world withdraw, and let love of God dwell; let the better take its place. You used to love the world, do not love the world: when you have drained your heart of earthly love, you will drink in divine love; and now love begins to dwell, from which nothing evil can proceed.

Hear, then, the words of him who is now purifying. He finds men's hearts, as it were, a field: but how does he find it? If he finds woodland, he grubs it up; if he finds the field cleared, he plants. He wishes to plant there a tree, love. And what woodland does he wish to grub up? Love of the world. Hear the grubber-up of the woodland. *Do not love the world,* for this is what follows, *nor the things that are in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him*[^29]*.*

## Let us have the root of love.

**9.** You have heard that *if anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.* Let no one say in his heart that this is false, brethren: God says it, the Holy Spirit spoke through the Apostle, nothing is truer: *If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.* Do you wish to have the love of the Father, that you may be a coheir of the Son? Do not love the world. Shut out the evil love of the world, that you may be filled with the love of God.

You are a vessel, but you are still full; pour out what you have, that you may receive what you do not have. Surely our brethren have now been reborn of water and the Spirit; and we, some years ago, were reborn of water and the Spirit. It is good for us not to love the world, lest the sacraments remain in us to condemnation, and not as supports to salvation. The support of salvation is to have the root of love, to have the power of godliness, not the form alone.

A good form, a holy form: but what does the form avail, if it does not hold the root? Is not the shoot that is cut off cast into the fire? Have the form, but in the root. But how are you to be rooted, so that you may not be uprooted? By holding fast to love, as the apostle Paul says: *Rooted and grounded in love*[^30].

How will love be rooted there, among so many thickets of love of the world? Grub up the thickets. You are about to set a great seed; let there not be in the field what may choke the seed. These are the uprooting words that he said: *Do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him.*

## Does love of the world whirl you about? hold fast to Christ.

**10.** *For all that is in the world is the desire of the flesh, and the desire of the eyes, and the ambition of the age*—three things he named—*which are not from the Father, but are from the world. And the world passes away and its desires: but he who does the will of God abides forever, just as he himself abides forever*[^31]*.*

Why should I not love what God made? What do you wish? to love temporal things, and to pass away with time; or not to love the world, and to live forever with God? The river of temporal things drags us along: but, as it were beside that river, a tree was born, our Lord Jesus Christ. He took flesh, died, rose again, ascended into heaven. He willed in a manner to plant himself beside the river of temporal things. Are you being swept headlong? hold fast to the wood.

Does love of the world whirl you about? hold fast to Christ. For your sake he was made temporal, that you might be made eternal; for he too was so made temporal that he remained eternal. Something accrued to him from time, nothing departed from his eternity. But you were born temporal, and through sin you were made temporal: you were made temporal through sin, he was made temporal through the mercy of forgiving sins. How great is the difference, when two are in prison, between the guilty man and his visitor?

For a man sometimes comes to his friend, and enters to visit him, and both are seen in the prison; but they are far apart and distinct. The former a charge weighs down, the latter kindness has brought. So in this mortality, we were held by guilt;

he came down in mercy: as redeemer he entered to the captive, not as oppressor. The Lord poured out his blood for us, redeemed us, changed our hope. Still we carry the mortality of the flesh, and presume upon a future immortality: and we toss on the sea, but we have already fixed the anchor of hope on land.

## God does not forbid you to love creatures, but not to love them for the sake of blessedness.

**11.** But let us not love the world, nor the things that are in the world. For the things that are in the world are *the desire of the flesh, and the desire of the eyes, and the ambition of the age.* These are three: lest perhaps anyone say: The things that are in the world God made, that is, heaven and earth, the sea, the sun, the moon, the stars, all the adornments of the heavens.

What are the adornments of the sea? All things that creep. What are those of the earth? Animals, trees, birds. These are in the world, God made them. Why, then, should I not love what God made? Let the Spirit of God be in you, that you may see that all these things are good: but woe to you if you love the things created, and forsake the Creator. They are beautiful to you; but how much more beautiful is he who formed them?

Let your Charity attend. For you can be instructed by comparisons: lest Satan creep upon you, saying what he is wont to say. May it be well with you in the creation of God; why did he make it, unless that it might be well with you? And they become drunk, and perish, and forget their Creator:

while they use created things not temperately, but greedily, the Creator is despised. Of such the Apostle says: *They worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator, who is blessed forever*[^32]. God does not forbid you to love these things, but not to love them for the sake of blessedness; rather, to approve and praise them, that you may love the Creator.

Just as, brethren, if a bridegroom made his bride a ring, and she, having received the ring, loved the ring more than the bridegroom who made her the ring; would not the soul be caught in adultery in the very gift of the bridegroom, although she loved what the bridegroom gave?

Surely she would love what the bridegroom gave: yet if she said: This ring is enough for me, I do not wish now to see his face; what sort of woman would she be? Who would not detest this madness? who would not convict her of an adulterous mind?

You love the gold in place of the man, you love the ring in place of the bridegroom: if this is in you, that you love the ring in place of your bridegroom, and do not wish to see your bridegroom; he gave you the pledge for this, not to pledge you to himself, but to turn you away.

For this surely the bridegroom gives a pledge, that in his pledge he himself may be loved. Therefore God gave you all these things; love him who made them. There is more that he wishes to give you, that is, himself, who made these things. But if you love these, although God made them, and neglect the Creator, and love the world; will not your love be reckoned adulterous?

## Not only this structure, but its inhabitants, are called the world.

**12.** For not only this structure which God made—heaven and earth, the sea, things visible and invisible—is called the world: but the inhabitants of the world are called the world, just as a house is called both the walls and those who dwell in it. And sometimes we praise the house, and blame the dwellers.

For we say, A good house; because it is marbled and beautifully paneled: and in another way we say, A good house; no one there suffers injury, no robberies, no oppressions are done there. Now we are not praising the walls, but the dwellers within the walls: yet it is called a house, whether the one or the other:

For all lovers of the world, because by love they inhabit the world; just as those whose heart is above inhabit heaven, and yet walk in the flesh on earth: all lovers of the world, then, are called the world. They themselves have nothing but these three, the desire of the flesh, the desire of the eyes, and the ambition of the age. For they desire to eat, to drink, to lie together, to use these pleasures. Is there not a measure in these?

Or when it is said: *Do not love these things,* is it said in this sense, that you should not eat, or not drink, or not beget children? It is not said in this sense. But let there be a measure, for the Creator's sake, so that these things may not bind you by love of them; lest you love for enjoyment what you ought to have for use.

But you are not tested, except when two things are set before you, this or that: Do you wish righteousness, or gains? I have no means to live, no means to eat, no means to drink. But what if you cannot have these except through iniquity? Do you not better love what you do not lose, than commit iniquity? You see the gain of gold, you do not see the loss of faith. This, then, he tells us, *is the desire of the flesh,* that is, the desire of those things that pertain to the flesh, such as food and intercourse, and other things of this kind.

## By the desire of the eyes he means all curiosity.

**13.** *And the desire of the eyes*: by the desire of the eyes he means all curiosity. Now how widely does curiosity extend? It is curiosity itself in spectacles, in theaters, in the sacraments of the devil, in magic arts, in sorceries. Sometimes it tempts even the servants of God, so that they wish, as it were, to work a miracle, to test whether God hears them in miracles; it is curiosity, this is the desire of the eyes; it is not from the Father. If God has granted it, do it; for he has offered it that you may do it: for those who have not done such things will not therefore fail to belong to the kingdom of God.

When the Apostles rejoiced that the demons were subject to them, what did the Lord say to them? *Do not rejoice in this; but rejoice because your names are written in heaven*[^33]. He willed the Apostles to rejoice in that wherein you too rejoice. For woe to you, if your name is not written in heaven. Is there woe to you if you do not raise the dead? is there woe to you if you do not walk on the sea? is there woe to you if you do not cast out demons?

If you have received the means to do them, use it humbly, not proudly. For the Lord said of certain false prophets too that they would work signs and wonders[^34]. Therefore let there be no ambition of the age. The ambition of the age is pride. A man wishes to vaunt himself in honors; he seems great to himself, whether from riches, or from some power.

## The Lord too was tempted by these things.

**14.** These are three, and you find nothing whereby human desire is tempted, except either by the desire of the flesh, or the desire of the eyes, or the ambition of the age. By these three the Lord was tempted by the devil. By the desire of the flesh he was tempted, when it was said to him: *If you are the Son of God, tell these stones to become loaves*[^35]; when he hungered after his fast. But how did he repel the tempter, and teach the soldier to fight? Attend to what he said to him: *Man does not live by bread alone, but by every word of God*[^36]*.*

He was tempted also by the desire of the eyes, concerning a miracle, when he said to him: *Cast yourself down, for it is written: He has given his angels charge concerning you, that they bear you up, lest at any time you dash your foot against a stone*[^37]*.* He resisted the tempter: for if he had worked a miracle, he would have seemed only either to have yielded, or to have acted from curiosity: for he worked them when he willed, as God, but as one healing the weak.

For if he had done it then, he would have been thought to have wished merely to work a miracle. But that men might not feel this, attend to what he answered; and when such a temptation befalls you, do you too say this: *Get behind me, Satan; for it is written: You shall not tempt the Lord your God*[^38]: that is: If I do this, I tempt God. He said this, which he wished you to say. When the enemy suggests to you: What sort of man, what sort of Christian are you?

have you yet worked even one miracle, or have the dead risen at your prayers, or have you healed the fevered? if you were truly of any moment, you would work some miracle: answer and say: *It is written: You shall not tempt the Lord your God*: therefore I will not tempt God, as though I belonged to God then if I worked a miracle, and did not belong to him if I did not. And where are his words: *Rejoice because your names are written in heaven*?

By the ambition of the age, how was the Lord tempted? When he raised him up on high, and said to him: *All these things I will give you, if you fall down and adore me.* By the exaltation of an earthly kingdom he wished to tempt the King of the ages:

but the Lord who made heaven and earth was trampling the devil underfoot. What great thing is it, for the devil to be conquered by the Lord? What, then, did he answer the devil, but what he taught you to answer? *It is written: You shall adore the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve*[^39].

Holding to these things, you will not have the concupiscence of the world: by not having the concupiscence of the world, neither the desire of the flesh, nor the desire of the eyes, nor the ambition of the age will subjugate you; and you will make room for love coming in, that you may love God. For if there be there love of the world, there will not be there love of God.

Hold rather to love of God, that as God is eternal, so you too may abide forever: for each one is such as his love is. Do you love the earth? you will be earth. Do you love God? what shall I say? you will be a god? I do not dare to say it of myself, let us hear the Scriptures: *I said: You are gods, and all of you sons of the Most High*[^40].

If, then, you wish to be gods and sons of the Most High, *do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him. For all the things that are in the world are the desire of the flesh, and the desire of the eyes, and the ambition of the age, which is not from the Father, but from the world*: that is, from men who are lovers of the world. *And the world passes away, and its desires: but he who does the will of God abides forever, just as God abides forever*[^41]*.*
