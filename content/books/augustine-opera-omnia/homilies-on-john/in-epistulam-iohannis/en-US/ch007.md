# Homily 6: Little children, let us not love

## (1 John 3:18—4:3).

> *Let love have an inner root, where he sees who crowns and now hears unto salvation. In love is security, the spirit of understanding, the unity of the Church. The heretics break unity: therefore they do not have love.*

## The beginning of love and its perfection.

**1.** If you remember, brethren, that yesterday we closed our discourse at this sentence, which without doubt ought to have remained and ought to remain in your heart, since you heard it last: *Little children, let us not love in word only and with the tongue, but in deed and in truth.* Then it follows: *And in this we know that we are of the truth, and before him we persuade our heart: that if our heart thinks ill, God is greater than our heart, and knows all things*[^1]. He had said: *Let us not love in word only and with the tongue, but in deed and in truth*: it is asked of us, in what deed and in what truth he who loves God, or he who loves his brother, is recognized. Already above he had said how far love is made perfect; which the Lord too says in the Gospel: *Greater love than this has no one, than to lay down his soul for his friends*[^2]: and this man had said this: *As he laid down his soul for us, we too ought to lay down our souls for the brethren*[^3]*.* This is the perfection of love; and a greater can in no way be found.

But because it is not perfect in all, and he in whom it is not perfect ought not to despair, if it has already been born so that it may be made perfect: and surely, if it has been born, it must be nourished, and by certain nourishments of its own be led to its proper perfection.

We sought whence the beginning of love begins, and there we found at once: *If anyone has the goods of the world, and sees his brother in need, and shuts his heart against him; how does the love of the Father abide in him*?[^4]

Therefore this love begins here, brethren, that one give of his superfluous things to one in need, set in some straits; that from what abounds to him for the time being, he free his brother from temporal tribulation. Here is the beginning of love.

This, thus begun, if you nourish it with the word of God and the hope of the life to come, you will come to that perfection, that you be ready to lay down your soul for your brethren.

## He has love, if the eye that penetrates the heart bears witness to him.

**2.** But because many such things are done by those who seek other things, and who do not love the brethren; let us be called back to the testimony of conscience. Whence do we prove that many such things are done by those who do not love the brethren?

How many call themselves martyrs in heresies and schisms! They seem to themselves to lay down their soul for their brethren. If they laid down their soul for the brethren, they would not separate themselves from the whole brotherhood.

Likewise, how many there are who, for the sake of ostentation, bestow much, give much; and seek there nothing but human praise and popular glory, full of winds, established on no stability! Because, then, there are such men, where will brotherly love have to be proved? Because he wished it to be proved, he says, admonishing: *Little children, let us not love in word only and with the tongue, but in deed and in truth.*

We ask: In what deed, in what truth? Can there be a more manifest deed than to give to the poor? Many do this out of ostentation, not out of love. Can there be a greater deed than to die for the brethren? And this too many wish to be thought to do, for the ostentation of winning a name, not out of the inmost marrow of love.

It remains that he loves his brother who, before God, where he alone sees, persuades his own heart, and questions his heart whether he truly does this on account of love of the brethren; and the eye that penetrates the heart, where a man cannot look, bears witness to him. Therefore the apostle Paul, because he was ready to die for the brethren, and said: *I myself will be spent for your souls*[^5]: yet, because God saw this in his heart, not the men to whom he was speaking, said to them: *But to me it is a very small thing to be judged by you, or by any human day*[^6].

And he himself shows too, in a certain place, that these things are wont to be done out of empty ostentation, not out of the firmness of love: for he says, when he was speaking of the commendation of love itself: *If I distribute all my goods to the poor, and hand over my body to be burned, but have not love, it profits me nothing*[^7].

For can anyone do this without love? He can. For those who do not have love have divided unity. Seek there, and you will see many giving much to the poor; you will see others ready to undergo death, so that, when the persecutor desists, they cast themselves down: these without doubt do this without love. Let us be called back, then, to conscience, of which the Apostle says: *For this is our glory, the testimony of our conscience*[^8].

Let us be called back to conscience, of which the same Apostle says: *But let each one prove his own work, and then he will have glory in himself, and not in another*[^9].

Let each one of us, then, prove his own work, whether it flows from the vein of love, whether from the root of love the branches of good works sprout.

*But let each one prove his own work,* he says, *and then he will have glory in himself, and not in another*: not when another's tongue bears witness to him, but when his own conscience bears witness.

## Let him see who crowns.

**3.** This, then, he commends here. *In this we know that we are of the truth,* when we love in deed and in truth, not in words and with the tongue only: *and before him we persuade our heart*[^10]*.* What is, *before him*? Where he himself sees. Whence the Lord himself in the Gospel: *Beware,* he says, *of doing your righteousness before men, to be seen by them: otherwise you will have no reward with your Father who is in heaven*[^11]*.* And what is: *Let not your left hand know what your right hand is doing*[^12]: but that the right hand is the pure conscience; the left, desire of the world?

Many, through desire of the world, do many wondrous things; the left hand works, not the right. The right hand ought to work, and the left not knowing, so that not even the desire of the age may mix itself in, when we work some good out of love. And where do we know this? You are before God, question your heart: see what you have done, and what you sought there; your salvation, or the windy praise of men. Look within: for a man cannot judge whom he cannot see. If we persuade our heart, let us persuade it before him.

*For if our heart thinks ill,* that is, accuses us within, because we do not do with the right intention what ought to be done; *God is greater than our heart, and knows all things*[^13]*.* You hide your heart from man; hide it from God if you can. How will you hide it from him, to whom it was said by a certain sinner, fearing and confessing: *Where shall I go from your spirit? and from your face whither shall I flee?*

He was seeking whither he might flee, that he might escape the judgment of God, and did not find it. For where is God not? *If I ascend,* he says, *into heaven, you are there: if I descend into hell, you are present*[^14]. Whither will you go? whither will you flee? Do you wish to hear counsel? If you wish to flee from him, flee to him. Flee to him by confessing, not by hiding from him: for you cannot hide, but you can confess.

Say to him: *You are my refuge*[^15]; and let love be nourished in you, which alone leads to life. Let your conscience bear witness to you that it is of God. If it is of God, do not wish to vaunt it before men; because neither do the praises of men lift you up to heaven, nor do their reproaches put you down from there. Let him see who crowns; let him be witness by whose judgment you are crowned. *God is greater than our heart, and knows all things.*

## He was heard unto salvation, though not according to his will.

**4.** *Most beloved, if our heart thinks not ill, we have confidence toward God*[^16]*.* What is, *the heart thinks not ill*? It has answered us truly, that we love, and there is genuine love in us: not feigned, but sincere; seeking the welfare of the brother, expecting no advantage from the brother, but his welfare. *We have confidence toward God; and whatever we ask, we shall receive from him, because we keep his commandments*[^17]*.*

Therefore not in the sight of men, but where God himself sees in the heart. *We have* therefore *confidence toward God; and whatever we ask, we shall receive from him*: but, *because we keep his commandments.* What are his commandments? Must it always be repeated? *A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another*[^18].

He speaks of love itself, he commends it. Whoever, then, has brotherly love, and has it before God, where God sees, and his heart, questioned under a just examination, answers him nothing else than that there is there the genuine root of love, whence good fruits arise; he has confidence with God, and whatever he asks, he will receive from him, because he keeps his commandments.

**5.** A certain question arises, because it is not this or that man, or you or I, of whom, if I ask something from the Lord our God, and do not receive it, each one can easily say of me: He does not have love; and of any man of this time it can easily be said; and let a man think what he wishes of a man: they do not make a greater question, except those men whom it is agreed were holy when they wrote, and now are with God.

Who has love, if Paul did not have it, who said: *Our mouth is open to you, O Corinthians, our heart is enlarged; you are not straitened in us*[^19]; who said: *I will be spent for your souls*[^20]: and so great grace was in him that it was made manifest that he had love? Yet we find that he asked, and did not receive. What do we say, brethren? It is a question; be attentive to God. This too is a great question.

Just as concerning sin, where it was said: *He who is born of God does not sin*[^21]; we found that this sin is to violate love, and that this is properly designated in this place: so now too we ask what he has said. For if you attend to the words, it seems plain; if to the examples, it is obscure. Than these words nothing is plainer: *And whatever we ask, we shall receive from him; because we keep his commandments, and do the things that please him in his sight*[^22]*. Whatever we ask,* he says, *we shall receive from him.*

He has bound us tightly. For there too it would bind, if he said every sin: but for this reason we found a way of explaining it, because he spoke of a certain sin, not of every one; but of a certain sin, which everyone who is born of God does not commit: and we found that that certain sin is the violation of love. And we have a plain example from the Gospel, when the Lord says: *If I had not come, they would have no sin*[^23].

What then? had he come to innocent Jews, that he speaks so? Then if he had not come, they would have no sin? Did the presence of the physician, then, make the sick man, not take away the fever? Who would say this, even in madness? He came only to cure and heal the sick. Why, then, did he say: *If I had not come, they would have no sin,* except because he wished a certain particular sin to be understood? For a certain sin the Jews would not have.

What sin? That whereby they did not believe in him, whereby they despised him present. As, then, there he said sin, and it does not follow that we understand every sin, but a certain sin: so here too not every sin, lest he be contrary to that place where he says: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^24]; but a certain particular sin, that is, the violation of love. But here he has bound us more: If we ask, he said, if our heart does not accuse us, and reports in the sight of God that there is true love in us; *whatever we ask, we shall receive from him.*

**6.** Now then I have said to your Charity, brethren, let no one attend to us. For what are we? Or what are you? What, but the Church of God, which is known to all? And if it pleases him, we are in it; and we who by love abide in it, let us persevere there, if we wish to show the love we have.

Nevertheless, of the apostle Paul what evil are we to think? Did he not love the brethren? Was there not in him the testimony of his conscience in the sight of God? Was there not in Paul that root of love, whence all good fruits proceeded? Who would say this in madness? Where, then, do we find that the Apostle asked and did not receive? He himself says: *Lest I be exalted by the greatness of the revelations, there was given to me a thorn in my flesh, an angel of Satan, to buffet me: for which I three times asked the Lord to take it from me; and he said to me: My grace is sufficient for you; for strength is made perfect in weakness*[^25].

Behold, he was not heard, that the angel of Satan should be taken from him. But why? Because it did not profit him. Therefore he was heard unto salvation, who was not heard according to his will. Let your Charity know a great sacrament; which for this reason we commend to you, lest it slip from you in your temptations. The saints are heard in all things unto salvation, they are always heard unto eternal salvation: this they desire; because according to this they are always heard.

**7.** But let us distinguish God's hearings. For we find some not heard according to their will, heard unto salvation:

and again we find some heard according to their will, and not heard unto salvation.

Distinguish this, hold this example of him who was not heard according to his will, but heard unto salvation.

Hear the apostle Paul; for God showed him that very hearing unto salvation: *My grace is sufficient for you,* he says; *for strength is made perfect in weakness.* You asked, you cried out, three times you cried out:

that very thing which you cried out once I heard, I did not turn away my ears from you; I know what to do: you wish the medicine by which you are cauterized to be taken away; I know the weakness by which you are weighed down. Therefore this man was heard unto salvation, he was not heard according to his will.

Whom do we find heard according to their will, not heard unto salvation? Do we find, do you suppose, some wicked man, some impious man heard by God according to his will, not heard unto salvation?

If I set down the example of some man, perhaps you will say to me: You call him iniquitous, but he was just; if he were not just, he would not be heard by God. I am going to set forth such a one of whose iniquity and impiety no one may doubt. The devil himself asked for Job, and received him[^26].

Did you not here too hear of the devil that *he who commits sin is of the devil*[^27]? Not because he created him, but because the man imitates him. Was it not said of him: *He stood not in the truth*[^28]? Is he not that ancient serpent who through the woman gave the poison to the first man to drink[^29]? Who for Job too kept the woman for this reason, that through her the husband should not be consoled, but tempted[^30].

The devil himself asked that the holy man be tempted, and received it: the Apostle asked that the thorn of the flesh be taken from him, and did not receive it.

But the Apostle was heard more than the devil. For the Apostle was heard unto salvation, though not according to his will: the devil was heard according to his will, but unto condemnation. For this man was granted to be tempted for this reason, that, he being proved, the other might be tormented. But this, brethren, we find not only in the old Books, but also in the Gospel.

The demons asked of the Lord, when he was driving them out of a man, to be permitted to go into swine. Could not the Lord have said to them, that they should not go even there? For they would not, if he were unwilling, rebel against the king of heaven and earth. Yet for the sake of a certain mystery and a certain dispensation he let the demons go into the swine[^31], to show that the devil rules over those who lead the life of swine.

The demons, then, were heard, and the Apostle not heard? Or rather let us say what is truer: nay, the Apostle was heard, the demons were not heard? Their will was done, this man's health was made perfect.

## Let love be in you, and be secure.

**8.** According to this we ought to understand that God, even if he does not give to our will, gives to our salvation. For what if you ask that which harms you, and the physician knows that it harms you?

For the physician does not fail to hear you when perhaps you ask for cold water, and if it profits, he gives it at once; if it does not profit, he does not give it. Did he not hear you, or rather did he hear you unto health, in that he contradicted your will?

Let love, then, be in you, brethren; let it be in you, and be secure: and when what you ask is not given you, you are heard; but you do not know it. Many have been given into their own hands to their own harm: of whom the Apostle says: *God handed them over to the desires of their heart*[^32].

Someone asked for great wealth; he received it to his own harm. When he did not have it, he feared little; he began to have it, he became prey to a more powerful man.

Was he not heard to his own harm, who wished to have that for which he would be sought out by a robber, whom, when poor, no one sought out?

Learn to ask God in such a way that you entrust to the physician what he himself knows to do. Do you confess the disease, let him apply the medicine.

Only hold fast to love. For he wishes to cut, he wishes to cauterize; if you cry out, and are not heard in the cutting, in the cauterizing and the affliction, he knows how far the rottenness goes.

You now wish him to draw back his hands, and he regards the depth of the wound; he knows how far he must reach. He does not hear you according to your will, but he hears you unto health.

Be sure, then, my brethren, that what the Apostle says is true: *For what we should pray for as we ought, we do not know; but the Spirit himself intercedes with unutterable groanings, for he intercedes for the saints*[^33]. What is: *The Spirit himself intercedes for the saints,* but that very love which has been made in you through the Spirit?

For the same Apostle says: *The love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^34]. Love itself groans, love itself prays; against it he who gave it does not know how to shut his ears. Be secure, let love ask; and there are the ears of God.

What you wish is not done, but what is expedient is done. Therefore *whatever we ask,* he says, *we shall receive from him.* I have already said, if you understand it unto salvation, there is no question: if not unto salvation, there is a question, and a great one, which makes you a slanderer of the apostle Paul. *Whatever we ask, we shall receive from him; because we keep his commandments, and do the things that please him in his sight. In his sight,* within where he sees.

## With love you have the Spirit of God for understanding.

**9.** And what are those commandments? *This is,* he says, *his commandment, that we believe in the name of his Son Jesus Christ, and love one another.* You see that this is the commandment, you see that whoever acts against this commandment commits the sin which everyone who is born of God lacks. *As he gave us commandment*: that we should love one another. *And he who keeps his commandment*: you see that nothing else is enjoined on us, but that we love one another:

*And he who keeps his commandment abides in him, and he in him. And in this we know that he abides in us, by the Spirit whom he gave us*[^35]*.* Is it not manifest that the Holy Spirit works this in a man, that there be in him love and charity? Is it not manifest, what the apostle Paul says: *The love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*?

For he was speaking of love, and saying that in the sight of God we ought to question our heart. *But if our heart thinks not ill*: that is, if it confesses that whatever is done in good work is done out of love of the brother. He went on further, when he spoke of the commandment, to say this: *This is his commandment, that we believe in the name of his Son Jesus Christ, and love one another. And he who does his commandment abides in him, and he in him. In this we know that he abides in us, by the Spirit whom he gave us.* For if you find that you have love, you have the Spirit of God for understanding: for it is a very necessary thing.

## In the unity of love we are joined to our brethren.

**10.** In the first times the Holy Spirit fell upon believers; and they spoke in tongues they had not learned, as the Spirit gave them to utter. These were signs suited to the time. For it was fitting that the Holy Spirit be thus signified in all tongues, because the Gospel of God was to run through all tongues throughout the whole world. That was signified, and it passed. Surely now, when hands are laid on men that they may receive the Holy Spirit, is this expected, that they should speak in tongues?

Or when we laid hands on these infants, did each one of you mark whether they spoke in tongues; and when he saw them not speaking in tongues, was any of you of so perverse a heart as to say: These have not received the Holy Spirit; for if they had received it, they would speak in tongues, as was done then? If, then, the testimony of the presence of the Holy Spirit is not given now through these miracles; whence does it come, whence does each one know that he has received the Holy Spirit?

Let him question his heart: if he loves his brother, the Spirit of God abides in him. Let him see, let him prove himself before the eyes of God; let him see whether there is in him love of peace and unity, love of the Church spread throughout the whole world. Let him not attend to loving only that brother whom he beholds before him: for many of our brethren we do not see, and in the unity of the Spirit we are joined to them. What wonder that they are not with us? We are in one body, we have one head in heaven.

Brethren, our eyes do not see one another, as though they do not know one another. Or in the love of bodily framing do they not know one another? For, that you may know that in the conjunction of love they know one another: when both are open, it is not possible that the right eye attend to anything that the left does not attend to.

Direct the ray of the right eye without the other, if you can. They come together at once, they are directed at once; the intention is one, the places are different. If, then, all who love God with you have one intention with you, do not attend to the fact that you are separated in body by place; you have together fixed the gaze of your heart on the light of truth.

Therefore, if you wish to know that you have received the Spirit, question your heart; lest perhaps you have the sacrament, and do not have the power of the sacrament. Question your heart: if there is there love of the brother, be secure. There cannot be love without the Spirit of God: for Paul cries: *The love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^36]*.*

## He who has been cut off through iniquity does not have the Spirit.

**11.** *Most beloved, do not believe every spirit.* Because he had said: *In this we know that he abides in us, by the Spirit whom he gave us.* But whence the Spirit himself is known, attend: *Most beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits whether they are of God*[^37]*.* And who is it that tests the spirits? He has set us a difficult thing, my brethren: it is good for us that he himself say whence we may discern. He is going to say it; do not dread: but first see, attend; see that from this is squeezed out that whereby the empty heretics raise their slanders. Attend, see what he says: *Most beloved, do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits whether they are of God.*

The Holy Spirit was called by the name of water in the Gospel, when the Lord cried and said: *If anyone thirsts, let him come to me, and drink: he who believes in me, rivers of living water shall flow from his belly.* But the Evangelist explained whence he said it: for he went on to say: *But this he said of the Spirit which those who were to believe in him were to receive.* Why did the Lord not baptize many? But what does he say? *For the Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*[^38].

Because, then, they had Baptism, and had not yet received the Holy Spirit, whom on the day of Pentecost the Lord sent from heaven; that the Spirit might be given, the glorification of the Lord was awaited. And before he was glorified, and before he sent him, he yet invited men to prepare themselves to receive the water, of which he said: *He who thirsts, let him come and drink*; and: *He who believes in me, rivers of living water shall flow from his belly.* What is, *rivers of living water*? what is that water? Let no one question me; question the Gospel. *But this he said,* it says, *of the Spirit which those who were to believe in him were to receive.*

One thing, then, is the water of the Sacrament, another the water that signifies the Spirit of God. The water of the Sacrament is visible; the water of the Spirit invisible. The former washes the body, and signifies what is done in the soul: through that Spirit the soul itself is cleansed and fattened.

He is the Spirit of God, whom the heretics cannot have, and whoever cut themselves off from the Church. And whoever do not openly cut themselves off, but have been cut off through iniquity, and within are tossed about like chaff, and are not grains, do not have this Spirit.

This Spirit was signified by the Lord under the name of water: and we have heard from this Epistle: *Do not believe every spirit*; and those words of Solomon bear witness: *Abstain from another's water.* What is water? The Spirit. Does water always signify the Spirit? Not always: but in certain places it signifies the Spirit, in certain places it signifies Baptism, in certain places it signifies peoples, in certain places it signifies counsel. You have it said in a certain place: *Counsel is a fountain of life to those who possess it*[^39].

Therefore through the various places of the Scriptures the name of water signifies various things. Now, however, under the name of water you have heard the Holy Spirit, not by our own interpretation, but by the Gospel testimony, where it says: *But this he said of the Spirit which those who were to believe in him were to receive.*

If, then, by the name of water the Holy Spirit is signified, and this Epistle says to us: *Do not believe every spirit, but test the spirits whether they are of God*; let us understand that it was said for this reason: *Abstain from another's water, and do not drink from another's fountain*[^40]. What is, *do not drink from another's fountain*? Do not believe another's spirit.

## The heretics confess that Christ has come in the flesh.

**12.** There remains, then, an examination, whereby it may be proved that it is the Spirit of God. He has indeed set down a sign, and this perhaps difficult; yet let us see. We are going to return to that love; it is what teaches us, because it is the anointing. Yet here what does he say? *Test the spirits whether they are of God: for many false prophets have gone out into this world.*

There already are all the heretics and all the schismatics. How, then, do I test the spirit? It follows: *In this the Spirit of God is known.* Lift up the ears of the heart. We were laboring, and saying: Who knows? who discerns? Behold, he is going to tell the sign. *In this the Spirit of God is known: every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God. And every spirit that does not confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is not of God: and this is the antichrist, of whom you have heard that he is coming; and now he is in this world*[^41].

As it were, our ears are pricked up to discern the spirits; and we have heard such a thing that nonetheless we do not discern. For what does he say? *Every spirit that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh is of God.* Then the spirit that is among the heretics is of God; because they confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh? Now here perhaps they raise themselves against us, and say: You do not have the Spirit of God; but we confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh: but this man denied that those have the Spirit of God who do not confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh. Ask the Arians; they confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh: ask the Eunomians; they confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh: ask the Macedonians; they confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh: question the Cataphrygians; they confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh: question the Novatians; they confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh. Do all these heresies, then, have the Spirit of God? Are they not, then, false prophets? is there, then, no deception there, no seduction there? Surely they are antichrists, who went out from us, but were not of us.

## They confess with the voice, they deny with the heart.

**13.** What, then, do we do? whence do we discern? Attend; let us go together in heart, and knock. Love itself keeps watch, for it is love that will knock, love that will open: now you will understand in the name of our Lord Jesus Christ. You have already heard above that it was said: *He who denies that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh, this is the antichrist*[^42].

And there we asked, who denies it; because neither do we deny it, nor do they deny it. And we found that some deny it by deeds[^43]; and we brought the testimony from the Apostle, who says: *For they confess that they know God, but by their deeds they deny him*[^44]. So, then, now too let us seek in deeds, not in the tongue.

Who is the spirit that is not of God? *He who denies that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.* And who is the spirit that is of God? *He who confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh.* Who is it that confesses that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh?

Come now, brethren, let us attend to works, not to the noise of the tongue. Let us ask why Christ came in the flesh, and we shall find those who deny that he has come in the flesh. For if you attend to tongues, you will hear many heresies confessing that Christ has come in the flesh: but the truth convicts them. Why did Christ come in the flesh? Was he not God? Was it not written of him: *In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^45]?

Was he not himself feeding the angels, and does he not still feed the angels? Did he not come here in such a way that he did not depart from there? Did he not ascend in such a way that he did not abandon us? Why then did he come in the flesh? Because the hope of resurrection had to be shown to us. He was God, and he came in the flesh; for God could not die, but the flesh could die; therefore he came in the flesh, that he might die for us.

But how did he die for us? *Greater love has no one than this, that he lay down his life for his friends*[^46]. Love, then, brought him to the flesh. So whoever does not have love denies that Christ has come in the flesh. Now go ahead and question all the heretics: has Christ come in the flesh? He has come; this I believe, this I confess. On the contrary, you deny it. How do I deny it? You hear that I say it. On the contrary, I prove that you deny it. You say it with your voice, you deny it in your heart; you say it with words, you deny it with deeds.

How, you ask, do I deny it with deeds? Because Christ came in the flesh for this reason: that he might die for us. He died for us for this reason: that he taught great love: *Greater love has no one than this, that he lay down his life for his friends.* You do not have love, because for the sake of your own honor you divide the unity. So from this understand the Spirit that is from God.

Knock, tap the earthen vessels, in case they may be cracked and ring poorly: see whether they ring sound, see whether love is there.

You take yourself away from the unity of the whole world, you divide the Church through schisms, you tear apart the body of Christ. He came in the flesh to gather together: you cry out in order to scatter.

So it is the Spirit of God who says that Jesus has come in the flesh: who says it not with the tongue but with deeds; who says it not by making a sound but by loving. But that one is not the Spirit of God who denies that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh: he too denies it not with the tongue but with his life, not with words but with deeds. It is clear, then, how we may recognize the brethren. Many who are within are, as it were, within; but no one is outside unless he is truly outside.

## They break apart the Church, which Christ gathered together.

**14.** So that you may know that he was referring to deeds: *And every spirit,* he says, *that dissolves Christ, that he has come in the flesh, is not from God.*

To dissolve is understood as something done by deeds. What does he show you? The one who denies; because he said, *dissolves.* He came to gather, you come to dissolve. You want to pull apart the members of Christ.

How do you not deny that Christ has come in the flesh, you who break apart the Church of God, which he gathered together? So you come against Christ; you are antichrist. Whether you are inside or outside, you are antichrist: but when you are inside, you are hidden, and when you are outside, you are made manifest.

You dissolve Jesus, and you deny that he has come in the flesh; you are not from God. This is why he says in the Gospel: *Whoever dissolves one of these least commandments, and teaches men so, shall be called least in the kingdom of heaven.*

What does it mean, it is dissolved? What does it mean, it is taught? It is dissolved by deeds, and it is taught, as it were, by words. *You who preach that one must not steal, do you steal?*[^47]

So the one who steals dissolves it in deed, and yet teaches in this way; he *shall be called least in the kingdom of heaven,* that is, in the Church of this present time.

Of him it was said: *Do what they say; but do not do what they do*[^48]. *But whoever does them and so teaches them shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven*[^49].

From the fact that he said here, *does them,* he set in opposition there what he said, *dissolves them,* that is, does not do them and teaches men so. So the one who does not act dissolves them. What does this teach us, except that we should question deeds and not trust words?

The obscurity of these matters compels us to say much, above all so that what the Lord deigns to reveal may reach even the slower brethren; for all have been purchased by the blood of Christ. And I fear that this Epistle may not be finished in these days, as I had promised: but since it pleases the Lord, it is better to keep something in reserve than to burden hearts with too much food.
