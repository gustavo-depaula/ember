# Homily 5: *He who is born of God*

## (1 John 3:9-17).

> *He does not sin against love, which is perfect if one is ready to die for the brethren. Love is a great mark of distinction, which makes us pass from death to life and does not envy. Its beginning is to give of one's goods.*

## We are sinners and we are born of God.

**1.** Listen attentively, I beg you, for no small matter is in question among us: and I do not doubt that, as you were present attentively yesterday, so today you have gathered more attentively still.

For it is no small question, how he says in this Epistle: *He who is born of God does not sin*[^1]: and how in the same Epistle above he said: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^2].

What is the man to do whom both statements from the same Epistle have hemmed in between them? If he confesses himself a sinner, he fears lest it be said to him: Then you are not born of God; for it is written: *He who is born of God does not sin.* But if he says he is just, and has no sin, he receives a blow from the other side from this same Epistle: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.*

A man, then, set in the middle, does not find what to say, and what to confess, or what to profess. To profess oneself to be without sin is dangerous; and not only dangerous, but also false: *We deceive ourselves,* he says, *and the truth is not in us, if we say that we have no sin.* But would that you did not have it, and said so! for then you would speak the truth, nor would you dread, in bringing forth the truth, any trace even of iniquity.

But you do ill if you say it, because you tell a lie: *The truth,* he says, *is not in us, if we say that we have no sin.* He did not say: We did not have; lest perhaps it should seem said of a past life. For this man did have sins; but from the time he was born of God, he began not to have them.

If it were so, no question would press us. For we would say: We were sinners, but now we have been justified; we had sin, but now we do not have it. He does not say this: but what does he say? *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.* After a little, again: *He who is born of God,* he says, *does not sin.* Was not John himself born of God? If John was not born of God—he of whom you heard that he reclined on the breast of the Lord—does anyone dare to promise himself a regeneration wrought in him which that man did not deserve to have, who deserved to recline on the breast of the Lord? Whom the Lord loved more than the rest[^3], him alone had he not begotten of the Spirit?

## He who is of God does not sin against love.

**2.** Attend now to these words: I still commend to you our straits, so that through your attention—which is a prayer both for us and for you—God may open it wide, and give an outcome; lest anyone find in his word an occasion of his own perdition, a word that has not been preached and written down except for healing and salvation. *Everyone,* he says, *who commits sin commits iniquity also*[^4]*.* Lest perhaps you distinguish: *Sin is iniquity.*

Do not say: I am a sinner, but I am not iniquitous: *Sin is iniquity. And you know that for this he was made manifest, to take away sins; and in him there is no sin*[^5]*.* And what does it profit us that he came without sin? *Everyone who does not sin abides in him: and everyone who sins has not seen him, nor known him. Little children, let no one seduce you. He who does righteousness is just, just as he too is just*[^6]*.* These things we have already said, that *just as* is wont to be said with respect to a certain likeness, not with respect to equality. *He who commits sin is of the devil; for the devil sins from the beginning*[^7]*.* And this we have said, that the devil created no one, nor begot anyone; but those who imitate him are, as it were, born of him. *For this the Son of God was made manifest, that he might loose the works of the devil*[^8]. Therefore, that he might loose sins, he who has no sin.

Then it follows: *Everyone who is born of God does not commit sin, because his seed abides in him; and he cannot sin, because he is born of God*[^9]*.* He has bound us tight. Perhaps he said *does not sin* with respect to some particular sin, not with respect to every sin: so that, as for his saying, *He who is born of God does not sin,* you understand a certain particular sin, which the man who is born of God cannot commit: and such is that sin that, if anyone commits it, he confirms the rest; but if anyone does not commit it, he looses the rest. What is this sin? To act against the commandment. What is the commandment? *A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another*[^10]. Attend. This commandment of Christ is called love: through this love sins are loosed. If this is not held, it is both a grave sin, and the root of all sins.

**3.** Attend, brethren; we have brought forth something in which, for those who understand well, the question has been solved. But do we walk the way only with the swifter? Those who walk more slowly are not to be left behind. Let us turn this over in what words we can, that it may reach all.

For I think, brethren, that every man is anxious for his soul who does not enter the Church without cause, who does not seek temporal things in the Church, who does not enter for this, that he may transact worldly business; but who enters for this, that he may hold to something eternal promised him, whither he may arrive:

he must needs consider how he may walk in the way, lest he remain behind, lest he go backward, lest he stray, lest by limping he fail to arrive. Whoever, then, is anxious, whether he be slow or swift, let him not depart from the way. This, then, I have said, that *he who is born of God does not sin,* he perhaps wished to be understood with respect to some particular sin: for otherwise it will be contrary to that place: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.*

So, then, the question can be solved. There is a certain sin which he who is born of God cannot commit; and which not being committed, the rest are loosed, which being committed, the rest are confirmed.

What is this sin? To act against the commandment of Christ, against the new testament. What is the new commandment? *A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another.* He who acts against love and against brotherly affection, let him not dare to boast, and say that he is born of God: but he who is established in brotherly love—there are certain sins which he cannot commit, and this most of all, that he should hate his brother. And what does he do about the other sins, of which it was said: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*? Let him hear assurance from another place of Scripture: *Love covers a multitude of sins*[^11].

## Love is perfect in him who is ready to die for the brethren.

**4.** We commend love, then; this Epistle commends love. After the resurrection, what else did the Lord ask Peter, but: *Do you love me?* And it was not enough to ask once; and a second time nothing else, and a third time nothing else. When already at the third time he was affected with grief, as though the Lord did not believe him, like one who did not know what was happening in him; yet a first, and a second, and a third time he asked this. Three times fear denied, three times love confessed[^12]. Behold, Peter loves the Lord. What is he going to render to the Lord? For he too was not unmoved in that psalm: *What shall I render to the Lord for all that he has rendered to me?*[^13]

For he who said these things in the Psalm was considering how great things had been bestowed on him by God; and he was seeking what he might render to God, and did not find it. For whatever you wish to render, you received from him in order to give back. And what did he find to render? What, as we said, brethren, he had received from him, this he found to render. *I will take the cup of salvation, and call upon the name of the Lord*[^14].

For who had given him the cup of salvation, but he to whom he wished to render? But to take the cup of salvation, and to call upon the name of the Lord, this is to be satisfied with love; and so satisfied that not only do you not hate your brother, but you are ready to die for your brother. This is perfect love, that you be ready to die for your brother.

This the Lord himself displayed in himself, dying for all, praying for those by whom he was being crucified, and saying: *Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do*[^15]. But if he alone did this, he would not be a master, if he had no disciples. The disciples followed and did this.

Stephen was being stoned, and on bended knee he said: *Lord, do not lay this sin to their charge*[^16]. He loved those by whom he was being killed, because he was even dying for them. Hear too the apostle Paul: *And I myself,* he says, *will be spent for your souls*[^17]. For he was among those for whom Stephen prayed, when he was dying by their hands.

This, then, is perfect love. If anyone has so great a love that he is ready even to die for the brethren, love is perfect in him. But is it at once, as soon as it is born, already wholly perfect? It is born that it may be made perfect: when it has been born, it is nourished; when it has been nourished, it is strengthened; when it has been strengthened, it is made perfect: when it has come to perfection, what does it say? *To me to live is Christ, and to die is gain. I desired to be dissolved and to be with Christ; for that is far better: to remain in the flesh is necessary for your sake*[^18]. For their sake he wished to live, for whom he was ready to die.

## Do for the brethren what I have done for you.

**5.** And that you may know that this is the perfect love which he who is born of God does not violate, and against which he does not sin; the Lord says this to Peter: *Peter, do you love me?* And he: *I love you*[^19]*.* He did not say: If you love me, wait upon me.

For when the Lord was in mortal flesh, he hungered, he thirsted: at the time when he hungered and thirsted, he was received as a guest; those who had means ministered to him from their substance, as we read in the Gospel[^20]. Zacchaeus received him as a guest: he was made well from his disease, the physician being received. From what disease? From avarice. For he was very rich, and a chief of the publicans. Attend to him healed of the disease of avarice. *Half,* he says, *of my goods I give to the poor; and if I have taken anything from anyone, I will restore fourfold*[^21]. Therefore he kept the other half, not that he might enjoy it, but that from it he might pay his debts.

He received the physician then as a guest, because there was the weakness of the flesh in the Lord, to whom men might offer this service: and this, because he willed to grant a benefit to those who served; for it profited those who served, not him. For whom the angels ministered to, did he require the service of these?

Nor did Elijah his servant ever have need of this, to whom he sent bread and meat by a raven; and yet, that a devout widow might be blessed, the servant of God is sent, and he who was fed by God in secret is fed by the widow[^22].

Nevertheless, although those who consider the need of these servants of God serve themselves in serving them, on account of that reward most plainly set down by the Lord in the Gospel: *He who receives a just man in the name of a just man shall receive a just man's reward; and he who receives a prophet in the name of a prophet shall receive a prophet's reward; and whoever shall give a cup of cold water to one of these least only in the name of a disciple, amen I say to you, he shall not lose his reward*[^23].

Although, then, those who do this serve themselves, yet not even this could be offered to one about to ascend into heaven. What could Peter render to him, who loved him? Hear what: *Feed my sheep*[^24]; that is: Do for the brethren what I have done for you. I have redeemed all by my blood: do not hesitate to die for the confession of the truth, that the rest may imitate you.

## Let the baptized see whether he has love.

**6.** But this, as we have said, brethren, is perfect love; he who is born of God has it. Let your Charity attend, see what I say.

Behold, a baptized man has received the sacrament of birth; he has the sacrament, and a great sacrament, divine, holy, ineffable. Consider what sort: that it may make a new man by the forgiveness of all sins. Yet let him attend in his heart, whether that has been made perfect there which has been done in his body:

let him see whether he has love, and then let him say: I am born of God. But if he does not have it, he has indeed the character imposed, but he wanders a deserter. Let him have love; otherwise let him not say he is born of God. But I have, he says, the sacrament. Hear the Apostle: *If I know all the sacraments, and have all faith, so that I remove mountains, but have not love, I am nothing*[^25].

## Love is a great mark of distinction.

**7.** This, if you remember, we commended when we began to read this Epistle, that nothing in it is so commended to us as love. And although it seems to say one thing and another, it makes its return there; and to love itself it wishes to refer all things, whatever it has said. Let us see whether here too it does this.

Attend: *Everyone who is born of God does not commit sin.* We ask, what sin: for if you understand every sin, it will be contrary to that place: *If we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us.*

Therefore let him say what sin, let him teach us; lest perhaps I have rashly said that this sin is the violation of love, because above he said: *He who hates his brother is in darkness, and walks in darkness, and does not know where he goes, for the darkness has blinded his eyes*[^26]. But perhaps he said something in what follows, and named love.

See that that circuit of words has this as its end, this as its outcome. *Everyone who is born of God does not sin; because his seed abides in him.* The seed of God, that is, the word of God: whence the Apostle says: *Through the Gospel I begot you*[^27]. *And he cannot sin, because he is born of God.* Let him say this, let us see in what he cannot sin. *In this the sons of God and the sons of the devil are made manifest. Everyone who is not just is not of God, and he who does not love his brother*[^28]*.* Surely it is now manifest whence he speaks: *And he who does not love,* he says, *his brother.*

Love alone, then, distinguishes between the sons of God and the sons of the devil. Let all sign themselves with the sign of the cross of Christ; let all answer, Amen; let all sing, Alleluia; let all be baptized, let all enter the churches, let all build the walls of basilicas:

the sons of God are not distinguished from the sons of the devil except by love. Those who have love are born of God: those who do not have it are not born of God. A great sign, a great mark of distinction.

Have whatever you will; only do not have this one thing, and nothing profits you: if you do not have the others, have this, and you have fulfilled the Law. *For he who loves another has fulfilled the Law,* says the Apostle: and: *Love is the fullness of the Law*[^29].

I think this is that pearl which the merchant man is described in the Gospel to have sought, who found one pearl, and sold all that he had, and bought it[^30]. This is the precious pearl, love, without which nothing profits you, whatever you have: which, if you have it alone, suffices you. Now you see with faith, then you will see with sight. For if we love when we do not see, how shall we embrace when we have seen? But where ought we to exercise ourselves? In brotherly love. You can say to me: I have not seen God; can you say to me: I have not seen a man? Love your brother. For if you love the brother whom you see, you will at the same time see God also; for you will see love itself, and within God dwells.

## He who envies does not love. Cain and Abel.

**8.** *He who is not just is not of God; and he who does not love his brother. For this is the announcement*: see how he confirms it: *For this is the announcement which we heard from the beginning, that we should love one another*[^31]*.* He has made manifest to us that he speaks of this: whoever acts against this commandment is in that wicked sin into which fall those who are not born of God. *Not as Cain, who was of the evil one, and killed his brother. And for what reason did he kill him? Because his works were evil, but his brother's just*[^32]*.*

Therefore where there is envy, brotherly love cannot be. Let your Charity attend. He who envies does not love. The sin of the devil is in him;

for the devil too cast men down by envying. For he fell, and envied him who stood. He did not wish to cast him down so that he himself might stand, but lest he should fall alone.

Hold in mind, from what he added, that envy cannot be in love. You have it openly, where love itself was being praised: *Love is not envious*[^33].

There was no love in Cain; and unless there were love in Abel, God would not accept his sacrifice. For when both had offered, the one from the fruits of the earth, the other from the offspring of his sheep; do you think, brethren, that God neglected the fruits of the earth, and loved the offspring of the sheep?

God did not look at the hands; but he saw into the heart: and him whom he saw offering with love, his sacrifice he regarded; him whom he saw offering with envy, from his sacrifice he turned away his eyes. By the good works of Abel, then, he means nothing but love: by the evil works of Cain he means nothing but brotherly hatred.

It is not enough that he hated his brother, and envied his good works; for he was unwilling to imitate them, and wished to slay him. And from this it appeared that he was a son of the devil, and the other from this appeared a just man of God.

From this, then, men are distinguished, my brethren. Let no one attend to tongues, but to deeds and the heart. If he does not act well for his brethren, he shows what he has in himself. By temptations men are proved.

## What the world is.

**9.** *Do not wonder, brethren, if the world hates us*[^34]*.* Must it be said to you often what the world is? Not heaven, not earth, nor those works that God made; but the lovers of the world. By saying these things often I am burdensome to some: but I say it not so vainly, since some, when asked whether I have said it, do not answer. Therefore let something, even by being driven in, cling in the hearts of the hearers.

What is the world? The world is, when it is put in the bad sense, the lovers of the world: the world, when it is put in praise, is heaven and earth, and the works of God in them; whence it is said: *And the world was made through him*[^35]. Likewise the world is the fullness of the earth, as John himself said: *And he is the propitiation not only for our sins, but also for those of the whole world*[^36]. By the world he means all the faithful scattered through the world. But the world in the bad sense are the lovers of the world. Those who love the world cannot love their brother.

## He who does not love abides in death.

**10.** *If the world hates us: we know.* What do we know? *That we have passed from death to life.* Whence do we know? *Because we love the brethren*[^37]*.* Let no one question a man; let each one return to his own heart: if there he has found brotherly love, let him be secure, because he has passed from death to life.

He is already on the right hand: let him not heed that his glory is now hidden: when the Lord comes, then he will appear in glory. For he flourishes, but still in winter; the root flourishes, but the branches are as if dry: within is the marrow that flourishes, within are the leaves of the trees, within the fruit; but they await the summer. Therefore *we know that we have passed from death to life, because we love the brethren. He who does not love abides in death.*

Do not think, brethren, that it is a light thing to hate or not to love; hear what follows: *Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer.* Now then, if anyone despised brotherly hatred, will he despise murder too in his heart? He does not move his hand to kill a man, he is already held a murderer by the Lord; the other lives, and this one is already judged a slayer. *Everyone who hates his brother is a murderer. And you know that no murderer has eternal life abiding in him*[^38]*.*

**11.** *In this we know love.* He means the perfection of love, that perfection which we have commended. *In this we know love, because he laid down his soul for us: and we ought to lay down our souls for the brethren*[^39]*.* Behold whence it came: *Peter, do you love me? feed my sheep*[^40]*.* For, that you may know that he wished his sheep to be so fed by him that he would lay down his soul for the sheep, he said this to him at once: *When you were young, you girded yourself, and went where you would; but when you are older, another will gird you, and carry you where you do not wish. But this he said,* says the Evangelist, *signifying by what death he was to glorify God*[^41]: so that to him to whom he had said: *Feed my sheep,* he might teach him to lay down his soul for his sheep.

## The beginning of love is to give to the brethren of one's goods.

**12.** Whence does love begin, brethren? Attend a little: where it is made perfect you have heard; that very end and that very measure the Lord too commended in the Gospel: *Greater love has no one,* he says, *than to lay down his soul for his friends*[^42]. Its perfection, then, he showed in the Gospel, and here its perfection has been commended: but you question yourselves, and say to yourselves: When can we have this love? Do not quickly despair of yourself: perhaps it has been born, but is not yet perfect; nourish it, lest it be choked. But you will say to me: And whence do I know? We have heard where it is made perfect; let us hear whence it begins.

It follows, and he says: *But whoever has the goods of the world, and sees his brother hungering, and shuts his heart against him: how can the love of God abide in him?*[^43] Behold whence love begins. If you are not yet fit to die for your brother, be already fit to give of your goods to your brother. Let love already strike your heart, that you may Acts not out of ostentation, but out of the inmost marrow of mercy; that you may consider him set in want. For if you cannot give your superfluous things to your brother, can you lay down your soul for your brother?

Money lies in your bosom, which thieves can take from you; and if thieves do not take it, by dying you will leave it, even if it does not leave you while you live: what will you do with it? Your brother hungers, he is set in necessity: perhaps he is hard pressed, he is straitened by a creditor; he has nothing, you have it: he is your brother, you were bought together, your price is one, both of you were redeemed by the blood of Christ:

see whether you have mercy, if you have the goods of the world. What is it to me? perhaps you say. Am I to give my money, that he may not suffer trouble? If your heart answers you this, the love of the Father does not abide in you. If the love of the Father does not abide in you, you are not born of God.

How do you boast that you are a Christian? You have the name, and you do not have the deeds. But if the deed follows the name, let anyone call you a pagan, do you show by your deeds that you are a Christian. For if you do not show by your deeds that you are a Christian, though all call you a Christian, what does the name profit you, where the reality is not found? *But whoever has the goods of the world, and sees his brother in need, and shuts his heart against him; how can the love of God abide in him?* And it follows: *Little children, let us not love in word only and with the tongue, but in deed and in truth*[^44]*.*

**13.** I think a great and necessary secret and sacrament has been made manifest to you, my brethren. How much love avails, all Scripture commends; but I do not know if anywhere it is commended more than in this Epistle. We ask and beseech you in the Lord, that you both hold in memory the things you have heard; and that to the things still to be said, until the Epistle itself is finished, you come attentive, and listen attentively. But open your heart to the good seeds: grub up the thorns, lest what is sown in you be choked, but rather let the crop grow; and let the farmer rejoice, and prepare a barn for you as for grain, not fire as for chaff.
