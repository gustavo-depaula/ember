# Tractate 9: In this love has been perfected

## (1 John 4:17–21).

> *Love, making us abide in God, extends itself to enemies. One fear is servile, another chaste, by which we fear lest we forsake God. We are made beautiful by love in Christ and in the unity of the Church.*

## We do not love the slothful in God, lest you fall.

**1.** Your Charity remembers that, of the Epistle of John the apostle, the last part remains for us to expound and to set forth to you, as far as the Lord grants. We, then, are mindful of this debt: but you ought to be mindful of exacting it. For the same love that in this Epistle is commended above all, and almost alone, both makes us most faithful debtors and makes you most sweet exactors. I said most sweet exactors for this reason: that where love is not, the exactor is bitter; but where love is, both the one who exacts is sweet, and the one from whom it is exacted, even if he takes on some labor, love itself makes that same labor almost nothing and light.

Do we not see, even in dumb and irrational animals, where there is not spiritual love but carnal and natural love, that milk is nonetheless exacted with great affection from the mother's breasts by the little ones? And although the suckling thrusts hard at the breasts; yet it is better for the mother than if it does not suck, and does not exact what is owed out of love.

We often see the udders of cows struck even by the heads of larger calves, and the bodies of the mothers almost lifted by that very thrust, and yet the calves are not driven off with a kick; but even if the offspring that would suck is absent, the cows call them to the udders by lowing.

If, then, there is in us that spiritual love of which the Apostle says: *I became a little one in your midst, like a nurse cherishing her children*[^1]; then we love you when you exact from us. We do not love the slothful; because we fear for the languishing.

But certain solemn readings for the feast days intervened, which caused us to interrupt the text of this Epistle, and which could not but be read, and themselves expounded. Now, then, let us return to the order we left aside; and let your Holiness attentively receive what remains.

I do not know whether love could be commended to us more magnificently than by saying: *God is love*[^2]*.* A short praise, and a great praise: short in words, and great in meaning. How quickly it is said: *God is love!* This too is short: if you count, it is one word;

if you weigh it, how great it is! *God is love. And he who abides,* he says, *in love abides in God, and God abides in him*[^3]. Let God be a house to you, and be a house of God; abide in God, and let God abide in you. God abides in you, to contain you: you abide in God, lest you fall; for so the Apostle says of love itself: *Love never falls*[^4]. How does he fall whom God contains?

## There are those who die with patience, and those who live with patience.

**2.** *In this love has been perfected in us, that we may have confidence on the day of judgment: because as he is, so are we also in this world*[^5]*.* He tells how each one may test himself, how far love has advanced in him: or rather how far he has advanced in love.

For if love is God, God neither advances nor falls short: love is said to advance in you in this sense, that you advance in it. Ask, then, how far you have advanced in love, and what your heart answers you, so that you may know the measure of your advance.

For he promised to show us by what we may know him, and he says: *In this love has been perfected in us.* Ask, by what? *That we may have confidence on the day of judgment.* Whoever has confidence on the day of judgment, love is perfected in him. What is it to have confidence on the day of judgment? Not to fear lest the day of judgment come.

There are men who do not believe in a day of judgment; they cannot have confidence on a day that they do not believe will come. Let us pass these by: may God arouse them, that they may live; why do we speak of the dead?

They do not believe a day of judgment is to come, and they neither fear nor desire what they do not believe. Someone has begun to believe in a day of judgment: if he has begun to believe, he has begun also to fear.

But because he still fears, he does not yet have confidence on the day of judgment, love is not yet perfected in him. Yet must one despair? In whom you see the beginning, why do you despair of the end? What beginning do I see, you ask? That very fear. Hear the Scripture: *The fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom*[^6].

He has begun, then, to fear the day of judgment: by fearing let him correct himself; let him watch against his enemies, that is, against his sins; let him begin to come to life again inwardly, and to mortify his members that are upon the earth, as the Apostle says: *Mortify your members that are upon the earth*[^7]*.*

He calls the spiritual things of wickedness members upon the earth: for he goes on and explains: *Avarice, uncleanness,* and the rest that he there enumerates. But as much as this man, who has begun to fear the day of judgment, mortifies his members upon the earth, so much do his heavenly members rise and grow strong. The heavenly members are all good works. As the heavenly members rise, he begins to desire what he feared. For he feared lest Christ should come and find an impious man whom he would condemn;

he desires him to come, because he is going to find a pious man whom he will crown. Now when the chaste soul has begun to desire the coming Christ, the soul that desires the embraces of the bridegroom, it renounces the adulterer; it becomes a virgin inwardly by that very faith, hope, and love. It now has confidence on the day of judgment: it does not fight against itself when it prays, and says: *Your kingdom come*[^8].

For he who fears lest the kingdom of God come, fears lest he be heard. How does he pray who fears lest he be heard? But he who prays with the confidence of love now wishes it to come. Of this very desire someone said in the Psalm: *And you, O Lord, how long? Turn, O Lord, and rescue my soul*[^9]. He groaned that he was being delayed. For there are men who die with patience: but there are certain perfect ones who live with patience.

What did I say? He who still desires this life, when the day of death comes to him, patiently endures death; he struggles against himself, so as to follow the will of God; and he does in his mind rather what God chooses, not what human will chooses: and out of desire for the present life there arises a struggle with death; and he applies patience and fortitude, so as to die with an even mind: this man dies patiently. But he who desires, as the Apostle says, *to be dissolved and to be with Christ,* does not die patiently; but he lives patiently, and dies with delight.

See the Apostle living patiently, that is, with patience here not loving life, but enduring it. *To be dissolved,* he says, *and to be with Christ is by far the best: but to remain in the flesh is necessary for your sake*[^10]. So, brethren, give effort, work within with yourselves, so that you may desire the day of judgment. Perfect love is not proven otherwise than when that day begins to be desired. But he desires it who has confidence in it: and he has confidence in it whose conscience does not tremble in love that is perfect and sincere.

**3.** *In this his love has been perfected in us, that we may have confidence on the day of judgment.* Why shall we have confidence? *Because as he is, so are we also in this world*[^11]*.* You have heard the cause of your confidence: *Because as he is,* he says, *so are we also in this world.* Does he not seem to have said something impossible? For can a man be as God is?

I have already explained to you that *as* is not always said with reference to equality; but it is said with reference to a certain likeness. For how do you say: As I have ears, so the image too has them? Is it entirely so? But still you say *as.* If, then, we have been made to the image of God, why are we not as God is? Not as to equality, but according to our measure.

Whence, then, is confidence given us on the day of judgment? Because *as he is, so are we also in this world.* We ought to refer this to love itself, and to understand what was said. The Lord says in the Gospel: *If you love those who love you, what reward will you have? Do not even the tax collectors do this*?[^12] What, then, does he want of us? *But I say to you, love your enemies, and pray for those who persecute you*[^13].

If, then, he commands us to love our enemies, from where does he give us the example? From God himself: for he says: *That you may be children of your Father who is in heaven.* How does God do that? He loves his enemies, *who makes his sun rise upon the good and the bad, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^14]. If, then, God invites us to this perfection, that we should love our enemies as he himself loved his; this is our confidence on the day of judgment, *that as he is, so are we also in this world*: that just as he loves his enemies, by making his sun rise upon the good and the bad, and by raining upon the just and the unjust; so we, because we cannot bestow sun and rain on our enemies, bestow tears, when we pray for them.

## Fear, as it were, prepares a place for love.

**4.** Now, then, concerning this confidence, see what he says. By what is perfect love understood? *There is no fear in love.* What, then, do we say of the one who has begun to fear the day of judgment?

If love were perfected in him, he would not fear. For perfect love would produce perfect righteousness, and he would have no reason to fear: rather he would have reason to desire that iniquity might pass away, and the kingdom of God might come.

So *there is no fear in love.* But in what love? Not in love that is only begun. In what, then? *But perfect love,* he says, *casts out fear*[^15]*.* So let fear begin; *for the fear of the Lord is the beginning of wisdom.* Fear, as it were, prepares a place for love.

But when love has begun to dwell there, the fear that prepared a place for it is driven out. For as much as love grows, fear shrinks; and as much as love becomes interior, fear is driven outside. The greater the love, the less the fear; the less the love, the greater the fear.

But if there is no fear, there is nothing by which love may enter. Just as we see linen thread drawn through by a bristle, when something is being sewn; the bristle enters first, but unless it goes out, the thread does not follow: so fear first occupies the mind, but fear does not remain there, because it entered for this reason, that it might introduce love. Once security has been established in the soul, what joy is ours, whether in this world or in the world to come? And in this world, who will harm us when we are full of love? See how the Apostle exults over this very love. *Who,* he says, *shall separate us from the love of Christ? Tribulation? Or distress? Or persecution? Or famine? Or nakedness? Or peril? Or the sword*[^16]? And Peter says: *And who can harm you, if you are zealous for what is good*?[^17]

*There is no fear in love: but perfect love casts out fear; because fear has torment.* The conscience of sins torments the heart, justification has not yet taken place. There is there something that tickles, that pricks.

This is why, in the Psalm, what does it say of the very perfection of righteousness? *You have turned my mourning into joy for me: you have torn off my sackcloth, and girded me with gladness; that my glory may sing to you, and that I may not be pricked*[^18]. What is it, *that I may not be pricked*? Let there be nothing to goad my conscience.

Fear goads: but do not fear; love enters, which heals what fear wounds. The fear of God wounds in the way the physician's instrument does; it removes the rottenness, and seems, as it were, to enlarge the wound. See, when the rottenness was in the body, the wound was smaller, but dangerous:

the physician's instrument comes; that wound hurt less than it hurts now when it is being cut. It hurts more when it is being treated than if it were not treated; but it hurts more at the coming of the medicine for this reason, that it may never hurt once health follows. So let fear occupy your heart, that it may bring in love; let the scar follow the physician's instrument.

Such is the physician that not even the scars appear: only submit yourself to his right hand. For if you are without fear, you will not be able to be justified. The verdict has been spoken from the Scriptures: *For he who is without fear will not be able to be justified*[^19]. It is necessary, then, that fear enter first, through which love may come. Fear is the medicine, love is the health. *But he who fears is not perfect in love*[^20]*.* Why? *Because fear has torment*, just as the cutting of the physician has torment.

## There is fear in love too, but a chaste fear.

**5.** But there is another saying, which seems to be contrary to this, if it does not have someone to understand it: for it is said in a certain place in the Psalm: *The fear of the Lord is chaste, abiding forever and ever*[^21]. It shows us a certain eternal fear, but a chaste one. But if it shows us an eternal fear, does this Epistle perhaps contradict it, which says: *There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear*?

Let us question both utterances of God. The Spirit is one, even if there are two books, even if there are two mouths, even if there are two tongues. For this was said through John, that was said through David; but do not think the Spirit is different. If one breath blows into two pipes, can one Spirit not fill two hearts, set in motion two tongues? But if, filled by one spirit, that is, by one breath, two pipes sound in harmony; can two tongues filled by the Spirit of God sound in discord?

There is, then, a certain harmony there, there is a certain concord, but it requires a listener. See, the Spirit of God has inspired and filled two hearts, two mouths, has moved two tongues: and we have heard from one tongue, *There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear;* we have heard from another: *The fear of the Lord is chaste, abiding forever and ever.* What is this? Do they, as it were, sound in discord?

No: clear your ears, attend to the melody. Not without reason did he add *chaste* here, and not add it there: except that there is one fear that is called chaste, and another that is not called chaste. Let us distinguish these two fears, and understand the harmony of the pipes.

How do we understand it, or how do we distinguish it? Let your Charity attend. There are men who fear God for this reason, lest they be cast into hell, lest perhaps they burn with the devil in the eternal fire. This is the fear that introduces love: but it comes in such a way that it goes out. For if you still fear God on account of punishments, you do not yet love him whom you so fear.

You do not desire good things, but you guard against evil ones. But from the fact that you guard against evil ones, you correct yourself, and you begin to desire good ones.

When you have begun to desire good things, there will be in you a chaste fear. What is the chaste fear? Lest you lose those very good things.

Attend. It is one thing to fear God, lest he cast you into hell with the devil; it is another to fear God, lest he withdraw from you. That fear by which you fear lest you be cast into hell with the devil is not yet chaste; for it does not come from love of God, but from fear of punishment: but when you fear God, lest his presence forsake you; you embrace him, you desire to enjoy him.

## Two fears like two women.

**6.** The difference between these two fears—the one that love casts out, and the other, the chaste one, that abides forever and ever—cannot be better explained than if you set forth two married women, one of whom you suppose to be willing to commit adultery, delighting in wickedness, but fearing lest she be condemned by her husband. She fears her husband, but because she still loves wickedness, that is why she fears her husband: to her the presence of her husband is not welcome, but burdensome; and if perhaps she lives wickedly, she fears lest her husband come.

Such are those who fear lest the day of judgment come. Suppose the other woman loves her husband, owes him chaste embraces, stains herself with no uncleanness of adultery; she desires the presence of her husband.

And how are these two fears distinguished? The one fears, the other fears too. Ask, and they answer you, as it were, one and the same thing: ask the one: Do you fear your husband? She answers: I fear him. Ask the other too whether she fears her husband; she answers: I fear him.

The voice is one, but the mind is different. Now then let them be asked: Why? The one says: I fear my husband, lest he come: the other says: I fear my husband, lest he depart. The one says: I fear lest I be condemned: the other says: I fear lest I be forsaken. Set this in the mind of Christians, and you find the fear that love casts out, and the other, chaste fear, abiding forever and ever.

## The unfaithful woman fears lest she be condemned by her husband.

**7.** Let us speak, then, first to those who fear God in the way that woman does whom wickedness delights; for she fears her husband, lest he condemn her: let us speak first to such people.

O soul who so fears God, lest God condemn you, in the way the woman whom wickedness delights fears; she fears her husband, lest she be condemned by her husband: just as that woman displeases you, let you too be displeased with yourself.

If perhaps you have a wife, do you want your wife to fear you in such a way, lest she be condemned by you; so that wickedness delights her, but she is restrained by the weight of fear of you, not by condemnation of wickedness?

You want her chaste, so that she may love you, not fear you. Show yourself such to God as you want to have your wife. And if you do not yet have one, and you want to have one; you want to have such a one.

And what do we say, brethren? That woman who fears her husband, lest she be condemned by her husband, perhaps does not commit adultery, lest it somehow reach her husband, and he take from her this temporal light. But that husband can also be deceived; for he is a man, just as she too is one who can deceive. She fears the one out of whose sight she can be: do you not always fear the face of your husband above you?

*But the face of the Lord is upon those who do evil*[^22]. She seizes the absence of her husband, and is perhaps incited by the delight of adultery; and yet she says to herself: I will not do it: he is indeed absent, but it is difficult for it not to reach him in some way.

She restrains herself, lest it reach a man who can also be ignorant of it, who can also be deceived, who can also suspect even a bad woman to be good, who can also suspect to be chaste one who is an adulteress: do you not fear the eyes of him whom no one can deceive? Do you not fear the presence of him who cannot be turned away from you?

Ask God to look upon you, and to turn his face away from your sins: *Turn your face away from my sins*[^23]*.* But how do you deserve that he should turn his face away from your sins? If you do not turn your own face away from your sins. For the same voice says in the Psalm: *For I acknowledge my iniquity, and my sin is always before me*[^24]. You acknowledge it, and he pardons it.

## The faithful woman fears lest she be forsaken by him.

**8.** We have addressed the woman who still has fear that does not abide forever and ever, but which love shuts out and casts forth; let us address also the woman who already has chaste fear, abiding forever and ever. Do we suppose we find her, so as to address her? Do you suppose she is among this people? Do you suppose she is in this hall? Do you suppose she is in this land? She cannot but be, but she is hidden.

It is winter; within, there is greenness in the root. Perhaps we find her ears. But wherever that soul is, would that I might find her, and that she would not lend me her ears, but that I might lend my ears to her! She would teach me something rather than learn from me.

A certain soul, holy, fiery, and desiring the kingdom of God: this soul I do not address, but God himself does, and thus he consoles her, living patiently in this land: Now you want me to come, and I know that you now want me to come: I know what sort you are, that you await my coming secure;

I know it is a trouble to you: but wait still, endure; I am coming, and I am coming quickly. But to the one who loves, it is slow. Hear her singing as a lily from among the thorns: hear her sighing, and saying: *I will sing and understand in the unspotted way; when will you come to me*?[^25]

But in the unspotted way she rightly does not fear; because *perfect love casts out fear.* And when she has come to his embrace, she fears, but securely. What does she fear? She will guard and watch herself against her iniquity, lest she sin again: not lest she be cast into the fire, but lest she be forsaken by him. And there will be in her, what? *Chaste fear, abiding forever and ever.*

We have heard two pipes sounding in harmony. The one speaks of fear, and the other of fear: but the one of the fear by which the soul fears lest it be condemned, the other of the fear by which the soul fears lest it be forsaken. The one is the fear that love shuts out: the other is the fear abiding forever and ever.

## By loving we are made beautiful.

**9.** *Let us love, because he first loved us*[^26]*.* For from what source would we love, unless he had first loved us? By loving we became friends; but he loved us as enemies, that we might be made friends. He first loved us, and granted us to love him. We did not yet love him; by loving we are made beautiful.

What does a man do, deformed and with a distorted face, if he loves a beautiful woman? Or what does a deformed and distorted and dark woman do, if she loves a beautiful man? Can she become beautiful by loving? Can he become handsome by loving? He loves a beautiful woman; and when he sees himself in the mirror, he blushes to lift his face to that beautiful one whom he loves. What will he do to be handsome?

Does he wait for beauty to come? On the contrary, by waiting, old age is added, and makes him uglier. There is, then, nothing he can do, there is no way to give him counsel, except that he restrain himself, and not dare to love unequal an unequal: or if perhaps he loves and desires to take a wife, let him love chastity in her, not the appearance of the flesh.

But our soul, my brethren, is foul through iniquity: by loving God it is made beautiful. What sort of love is it that makes the lover beautiful? But God is always beautiful, never deformed, never changeable. He who is always beautiful loved us first; and what sort did he love, except the foul and deformed? Yet not so as to leave us foul; but so as to change us, and out of deformed to make us beautiful. How shall we be beautiful? By loving him who is always beautiful. As much as love grows in you, so much does beauty grow; for love itself is the beauty of the soul.

*Let us love, because he first loved us.* Hear the apostle Paul: *But God shows his love in us, because while we were still sinners, Christ died for us*[^27], the just for the unjust, the beautiful for the foul. How do we find Jesus beautiful? *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men, grace is poured out on your lips*[^28]. How? Again, see from what source he is beautiful: *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men;* because *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^29].

But because he took on flesh, he took on, as it were, your foulness, that is, your mortality, so as to fit himself to you, and to suit himself to you, and to rouse you to love the beauty within. From what source, then, do we find that Jesus is foul and deformed, just as we find that he is beautiful and lovely in form beyond the sons of men? From what source do we find that he is also deformed? Ask Isaiah: *And we saw him, and he had no form or comeliness*[^30].

These are the two pipes, sounding, as it were, differently; but one Spirit blows into both. By this one it is said: *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men*: by this one it is said in Isaiah: *We saw him, and he had no form or comeliness.* Both pipes are filled by one Spirit, they do not sound in discord. Do not turn your ears away, apply your understanding. Let us question the apostle Paul, and let him expound to us the harmony of the two pipes. Let him sound for us: *Beautiful in form beyond the sons of men: Who, though he was in the form of God, did not count it robbery to be equal to God.* Behold, *beautiful in form beyond the sons of men.*

Let him also sound for us: *We saw him, and he had no form or comeliness: He emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in appearance as a man*[^31]. *He had no form or comeliness,* so as to give you form and comeliness. What form? What comeliness?

The love of charity; so that, loving, you may run, and, running, you may love. You are now beautiful: but do not attend to yourself, lest you lose what you have received; attend to him by whom you were made beautiful. Be beautiful for this end, that he may love you. But direct your whole attention to him, run to him, seek his embraces, fear to depart from him; so that there may be in you chaste fear, abiding forever and ever. *Let us love, because he first loved us.*

## It is necessary that whoever loves his brother love God.

**10.** *If anyone says: I love God.* What God? Why do we love? *Because he first loved us,* and granted us to love. He loved the impious, to make them pious; he loved the unjust, to make them just; he loved the sick, to make them healthy. So *let us too love, because he first loved us.* Question each one, let him tell you whether he loves God. He cries out, he confesses: *I love him,* he himself knows. There is something else about which he may be questioned. *If anyone says,* he says: *I love God, and hates his brother, he is a liar.* How do you prove that he is a liar?

Hear: *For he who does not love his brother whom he sees, how can he love God whom he does not see?*[^32] What then? He who loves his brother, does he love God too? He must necessarily love God, he must necessarily love love itself. Can he love his brother and not love love? He must necessarily love love. What then, does he who loves love thereby love God? Yes, indeed thereby. By loving love, he loves God. Or have you forgotten what you said a little before: *God is love*? If God is love, whoever loves love loves God. So love your brother, and be secure[^33]. You cannot say: I love my brother, but I do not love God. Just as you lie if you say: I love God, when you do not love your brother; so you are deceived when you say: I love my brother, if you think that you do not love God.

It is necessary that you who love your brother love love itself; but *love is God*: it is necessary, then, that whoever loves his brother love God. But if you do not love your brother whom you see, how can you love God whom you do not see? Why does he not see God? Because he does not have love itself. He does not see God for this reason, that he does not have love; he does not have love for this reason, that he does not love his brother: therefore he does not see God for this reason, that he does not have love. For if he had love, he would see God; because *God is love*: and that eye is purified more and more by love, so that it may see that unchangeable substance; in whose presence it may always rejoice, which it may enjoy fully forever, joined to the angels.

But let it run now, so that one day it may rejoice in the homeland. Let it not love the pilgrimage, let it not love the way: let everything be bitter, except him who calls, until we cling to him, and say what was said in the Psalm: *You have destroyed all who fornicate away from you.*

And who are those who fornicate? Those who depart, and love the world. But what about you? It follows and says: *But for me it is good to cling to God*[^34]. My whole good is to cling to God freely. For if you ask, and say: Why do you cling to God? And he says: That he may give to me. What should he give you? He made heaven himself, he made earth himself: what is he going to give you? You already cling to him: I have found something better, and he gives you himself.

## Let us hold the unity of the Church, Christ, love.

**11.** *For he who does not love his brother whom he sees, how can he love God whom he does not see? And this commandment we have from him, that he who loves God love his brother also*[^35]*.* You were saying magnificently: *I love God*; and you hate your brother! O murderer, how do you love God? Did you not hear earlier in this very Epistle: *He who hates his brother is a murderer*[^36]? But surely I love God, although I hate my brother. Surely you do not love God, if you hate your brother.

And now I prove it by another argument. He himself said: *He gave us a commandment, that we love one another*[^37]: how do you love him whose commandment you hate? Who is there who would say: I love the emperor, but I hate his laws? In this the emperor knows whether you love him: if his laws are observed throughout the provinces. What is the law of the emperor? *A new commandment I give you, that you love one another*[^38]. You say, then, that you love Christ; keep his commandment, and love your brother. But if you do not love your brother; how do you love him whose commandment you despise?

Brethren, I am not satisfied with speaking of love in the name of Christ. As much as you too have an avarice for this thing, so much do we hope that it grows in you, and casts out fear, so that there may remain that chaste fear abiding forever and ever. Let us endure the world, let us endure tribulations, let us endure the scandals of temptations. Let us not withdraw from the way; let us hold the unity of the Church, let us hold Christ, let us hold love. Let us not be torn from the members of his bride, let us not be torn from the faith, so that we may glory in his presence: and we will abide secure in him, now through faith, then through sight, of which we have such great pledges in the gift of the Holy Spirit.
