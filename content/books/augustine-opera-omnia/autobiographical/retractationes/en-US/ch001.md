# Prologue

**1**. For a long time now I have been thinking about and planning to do what, with the Lord's help, I now undertake, since I judge that it should no longer be put off: namely, to review my little works—whether in books, in letters, or in treatises—with a certain judicial strictness and, where something offends me, to mark it as with a censor's pen. For no one, unless imprudent, will dare to reproach me for reproaching my own errors. But if he says that I ought not to have said the things that afterward displeased even me, he speaks the truth and is on my side. He is indeed a reprover of the very things of which I too am a reprover. For I would not have to reprove them, had I had to say them.

**2**. But let each take what I am doing as he will; for my part, in this matter too I had to keep my eye on that apostolic saying where he says: *if we judged ourselves, we should not be judged by the Lord*[^1].

That too which is written, *in much talking you will not escape sin*[^2], frightens me greatly—not because I have written much, or because much also that was not dictated has nevertheless been recorded as spoken by me (far be it that I should reckon as much talking those things that are said of necessity, however great the multitude and length of the discourses in which they are said); but I fear that saying of holy Scripture for this reason, that out of so many of my disputations many things can doubtless be gathered which, if not false, at least seem, or even are shown, to be unnecessary. And whom among his faithful has Christ not frightened when he says: *for every idle word that a man speaks, he shall render account for it on the day of judgment*[^3]? Whence also his apostle James says: *let every man be quick to hear but slow to speak*[^4], and in another place he says: *do not become many teachers, my brethren, knowing that you incur the greater judgment. For in many things we all offend. If anyone does not offend in word, he is a perfect man*[^5]. I do not claim this perfection for myself even now, when I am already old—how much less when as a young man I began to write or to speak before the people; and so much was granted to me that, wherever it was necessary to speak to the people in my presence, very rarely was I permitted to keep silent and to listen to others, and to be *quick to hear but slow to speak*.

It remains, therefore, that I judge myself under one master[^6], whose judgment concerning my offenses I desire to escape. Now I reckon that there come to be many teachers when men hold differing and mutually opposed views. But when all say the same thing[^7], and say what is true, they do not depart from the teaching of the one true Master. They give offense not when they say many things that are his, but when they add their own[^8]. For in this way they fall from "much talking" into false talking as well.

**3**. It has seemed good to me to write these things, so that I may send them forth into the hands of men from whom I cannot recall for correction the things I have already published. Nor indeed do I pass over those works that I wrote when I was already a catechumen—although I had given up the hope I had borne, an earthly one, yet was still puffed up with the habit of secular letters—because these too have come into the knowledge of those who copy and read them, and they are read with profit, if some things are forgiven; or if they are not forgiven, the errors are at least not clung to.

Therefore whoever is going to read these works, let them not imitate me in erring, but in advancing for the better. For whoever reads my little works in the order in which they were written will perhaps find how I made progress in writing[^9]. That he may be able to do so, I will take care in this work, as far as I can, that he may come to know that same order.

[^1]: 1 Cor 11:31.
[^2]: Prov 10:19.
[^3]: Matt 12:36.
[^4]: Jas 1:19.
[^5]: Jas 3:1-2.
[^6]: See Matt 23:8.
[^7]: See 1 Cor 1:10.
[^8]: See John 8:44.
[^9]: See Ep. 143.2.
