# Revisions II

## Chapter 6 (33): The Thirteen Books of *The Confessions*

**1**. The thirteen books of my *Confessions* praise God, who is just and good, for my evil deeds and my good ones alike, and they rouse the human mind and heart toward him. As far as I am concerned, this is what they did in me when they were being written, and what they still do when they are read. What others think of them is for them to judge; but I know that they have given great pleasure to many of my brethren, and still do.

Books one through ten were written about myself; the other three about the holy Scriptures, from the words *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^1] down to *the Sabbath rest*[^2].

**2**. In the fourth book, when I was confessing the misery of my soul over the death of a friend, saying that our soul had in some way been made one out of two, I wrote: *and that, perhaps, is why I was afraid to die—lest he whom I had loved so dearly should wholly die*[^3]. This seems to me more a trivial piece of rhetoric than a serious confession, though this ineptitude is tempered somewhat by the word I added, *perhaps*.

And in the thirteenth book, where I said *that the firmament was made between the spiritual waters above and the bodily waters below*[^4]—this was said without sufficient thought; the matter, however, lies deeply hidden.

This work begins thus: *Great are you, O Lord*.

[^1]: Gen 1:1.
[^2]: See Gen 2:2.
[^3]: See *Confessions* 4.6.11.
[^4]: See *Confessions* 13.32.47.
