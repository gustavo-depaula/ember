# Book 13: The creation of the world allegorically considered

## From the fullness of God's goodness creation subsists

### Chapter 1: The good God is invoked

**1**. I call upon you, *my God, my mercy*[^1], who made me, and who did not forget me when I had forgotten you. I call you into my soul, which you are preparing to receive you, from the longing which you inspire in it; do not desert me now as I call upon you—you who, before I called, went before me[^2] and pressed in, growing in manifold voices, that I might hear from far off and turn and call upon you who were calling me.

For you, Lord, blotted out all my evil deserts, that you might not repay my hands[^3], with which I fell away from you; and you went before all my good deserts, that you might repay your hands, with which you made me[^4]; for even before I was, you were, nor was there anyone to whom you could grant that I should be; and yet, behold, I am, from your goodness going before all this—both what you have made me and what you have made me from.

For you did not need me, nor am I such a good as you should be helped by, *my Lord and my God*[^5]—not that I should so serve you as if you would grow weary in working, or as if your power might be lessened by lacking my obedience,

nor that I should so cultivate you as one cultivates land—that you would be uncultivated if I did not cultivate you—but that I might serve you and worship you, that from you it may be well with me, from whom it is that I am, that I should be one with whom it is well.

### Chapter 2

**2**. For from the fullness of your goodness your creature subsists—so that a good, which would profit you nothing, and was not from you equal to you[^6], yet, because it could come from you, should not be lacking. For what did heaven and earth, which you made *in the Beginning*[^7], earn from you?

Let them say what spiritual and corporeal nature earned from you, which you made *in your Wisdom*[^8], that thence might depend even things inchoate and unformed, each in its kind, spiritual or corporeal, going into immoderation and into a remote unlikeness to you—the unformed spiritual better than if it were a formed body, and the unformed corporeal better than if it were nothing at all,

and so they would hang unformed on your Word, unless through that same Word they were recalled to your unity and formed, and were, from you, the one supreme Good, *all things very good*[^9]. What had they deserved from you, that they should even be unformed—which would not even be this except from you?

**3**. What did corporeal matter earn from you, that it should even be *invisible and unformed*[^10], since it would not even be this, unless you had made it? And therefore, since it was not, it could not earn from you that it should be.

Or what did the inchoate spiritual creature earn from you, that even darksome it should float, like to an abyss[^11]—unlike to you—unless, through the same Word, it was turned back to that same one by which it was made, and, illumined by it, became light[^12]—though not equally, yet conformed to the form equal to you[^13]?

For just as for a body to be is not the same as to be beautiful (otherwise it could not be deformed), so for the created spirit to live is not the same as to live wisely; otherwise it would be wise unchangeably. But for it, *it is good to cleave to you*[^14] always, lest, by turning away, it lose the light it had gained by turning toward you, and fall back into a life like to a dark abyss.

For we too, who according to the soul are a spiritual creature, having turned away from you, our light, in that life were *once darkness*[^15], and we labor in the remnants of our obscurity, until we are *your righteousness* in your Only-begotten *like the mountains of God*: for your judgments we were like a *great abyss*[^16].

## The origin of things allegorically considered and explained

### Chapter 3

**4**. But that in the first foundings you said, *Let there be light, and there was light*[^17], I not incongruously understand this of the spiritual creature, because there was already some kind of life which you might illumine.

But just as it had not earned from you that there should be such a life as could be illumined, so, when it already was, it did not earn from you that it should be illumined. For its formlessness would not please you, if it did not become light—not by existing, but by gazing at the illuminating light and cleaving to it,

so that both that it lives in any way and that it lives happily, it might owe only to your grace—turned by a better change to that which can be changed neither into better nor into worse; which you alone are, because you alone simply are, for whom it is not one thing to live and another to live happily, since you are your own beatitude.

### Chapter 4: The Spirit over the waters, or the goodness of the Creator

**5**. What, then, would be lacking to your good, which you are to yourself, even if these things either were not at all, or remained unformed—things you made not from need, but from the fullness of your goodness, restraining and turning them toward form, not as though your joy were to be completed from them? For their imperfection displeases you, who are perfect, that they may be perfected from you and please you, not as one imperfect, as though you too were to be perfected by their perfection.

For *your good Spirit was borne over the waters*[^18]; it was not borne by them, as though it rested in them. For in whom your Spirit is said to rest[^19], those he makes to rest in himself. But your incorruptible and unchangeable will was borne over—itself sufficient in itself for itself—above that life you had made; for which to live is not the same as to live happily, since it lives even when it floats in its own obscurity; for which there remains to turn back to him by whom it was made, and more and more to live *at the fountain of life and in his light to see light*[^20], and to be perfected and illumined and made blessed.

## The Trinity described in an enigma

### Chapter 5

**6**. Behold, the Trinity appears to me *in an enigma*[^21]—which you are, my God; since you, Father, *in the Beginning* of our wisdom, which is your Wisdom born of you, equal and coeternal with you, that is, in your Son, made *heaven and earth*[^22].

And we have said much about the heaven of heaven and about the invisible and unformed earth and about the dark abyss, with reference to the wandering defection of spiritual formlessness, if it were not turned to him from whom there was some kind of life, and through illumination became beautiful life, and was the *heaven of heaven*[^23] of his, which was afterwards made between water and water[^24].

And I now held the Father in the name "God," who made these things, and the Son in the name "Beginning," in which he made these things; and, believing the Trinity to be my God, as I believed, I sought in his holy utterances, and behold, *your Spirit was borne over the waters*[^25]. Behold, the Trinity, my God: Father and Son and Holy Spirit, Creator of the whole creation.

### Chapter 6: Why the Spirit is named last

**7**. But what was the reason, O truthful Light—I bring my heart to you, that it may not teach me vain things; scatter its darkness and tell me, I beseech you through mother love, I beseech you, tell me, what was the reason that, after heaven and the invisible and unformed earth and the darkness over the abyss had been named, then at last your Scripture named your Spirit? Was it because it was fitting that he should be intimated in such a way that he should be said to be borne over? This could not be said unless something were first mentioned over which your Spirit could be understood to be borne. For he was not borne over the Father or the Son, nor would he rightly be said to be borne over, if he were borne over nothing. First, then, that had to be said over which he was to be borne, and then he, who needed to be mentioned in no other way than that he should be said to be borne over. Why, then, was he not fittingly to be intimated otherwise than that he should be said to be borne over?

### Chapter 7: The Spirit of God lifts us up

**8**. From here let him who can follow with his understanding your Apostle when he says that *your love is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^26], and when he teaches *about spiritual things*[^27] and points out[^28] *the supereminent way*[^29] of love, and bends his knees for us to you[^30], that we may know *the supereminent knowledge of the love of Christ*[^31]. And so from the beginning, supereminent, *he was borne over the waters*[^32].

To whom shall I say, how shall I say, about the weight of desire into the steep abyss, and about the lifting up of love through your Spirit, who *was borne over the waters*? To whom shall I say? How shall I say? For they are not places, in which we are submerged and rise up. What is more like, and what is more unlike?

They are affections, they are loves: the uncleanness of our spirit flowing downward with love of cares, and your holiness lifting us upward with love of security, that we may have our heart *upward* toward you[^33], where *your Spirit is borne over the waters*, and may come to the supereminent rest, when *our soul has passed through the waters that are without substance*[^34].

### Chapter 8: Spirits of creatures fallen away and restored to beatitude

**9**. The angel flowed away, the soul of man flowed away, and they made clear the abyss of all spiritual creation in a dark depth—had you not said from the beginning, *Let there be light, and there was light*[^35], and had not every obedient understanding of your heavenly city cleaved to you and rested in your Spirit, who is borne unchangeably over all that is changeable.

Otherwise the very *heaven of heaven*[^36] would be a dark abyss in itself; but now *it is light in the Lord*[^37]. For even in the very wretched restlessness of the spirits that flow away and make clear their darkness, stripped of the garment of your light, you sufficiently show how great a rational creature you have made, for whom in no way anything less than you suffices for blessed rest, and thus not even it for itself. For you, our God, *will light our darkness*[^38]; from you arise our garments, and *our darkness shall be as noonday*[^39].

Give me yourself, my God, give yourself back to me; lo, I love, and if it is little, let me love more strongly. I cannot measure, that I may know, how much love is wanting to me toward what is enough—that my life may run into your embraces and not be turned away, until it is hidden *in the hiding of your face*[^40]. This only I know, that it is ill with me apart from you, not only outside me but even within me; and every abundance that is not my God is to me poverty.

### Chapter 9: All things are borne by love

**10**. Was the Father, or the Son, not also borne over the waters? If "borne over" is meant as in place, like a body, then not even the Holy Spirit was; but if it is the eminence of unchangeable divinity above all that is changeable, then Father and Son and Holy Spirit alike *were borne over the waters*[^41]. Why, then, was this said only of your Spirit? Why is it said of him alone—as if a place, where he was—who is not a place; of whom alone it is said that he is your gift[^42]?

In your gift we rest: there we enjoy you. Our rest is our place. Love lifts us there, and *your good Spirit*[^43] exalts *our lowliness from the gates of death*[^44]. In good will is peace for us[^45]. A body by its weight strives toward its own place. Weight is not only downward, but to its own place. Fire tends upward, a stone downward. They are driven by their weights, they seek their own places. Oil poured under water rises above the water; water poured over oil sinks beneath the oil; they are driven by their weights, they seek their own places. Things less ordered are restless: they are ordered, and they rest.

My weight is my love; by it I am borne wherever I am borne. By your gift we are kindled and borne upward; we burn and we go. We ascend *ascents in the heart*[^46] and sing a song of degrees[^47]. By your fire, by your good fire we burn and we go, since we go upward *to the peace of Jerusalem*, since *I rejoiced in those who said to me: We shall go into the house of the Lord*[^48]. There a good will shall set us, that we may wish nothing other than to remain there *forever*[^49].

### Chapter 10: The blessedness of the creature immediately turned to the unfailing light

**11**. Blessed creature, which has known no other—since it itself was another—except that, by your gift which is borne over all that is changeable, it was, as soon as it was made, lifted up, with no interval of time, in that calling by which you said, *Let there be light, and there was light*[^50].

For in us it is distinguished by time—that we were *darkness* and are made *light*[^51]; but in it it was said what it would have been, had it not been illumined, and so it was said as if it had first been flowing and dark[^52], that the cause might appear by which it came to be otherwise—that is, that, turned toward the unfailing Light[^53], it might be light.

Let him who can, understand; let him ask of you. Why is he *troublesome to me*[^54], as if I were to illumine any *man coming into this world*[^55]?

### Chapter 11: The image of the Trinity adumbrated in humans

**12**. The almighty Trinity—who shall understand it? And who does not speak of it, if indeed of it? Rare is the soul which, whatever it says about it, knows what it says. And they contend, and they wrangle, and no one sees that vision without peace.

I would wish people to think these three within themselves. These three are far different from that Trinity—but I say where they may exercise themselves and test and feel how far different. I say, then, these three: to be, to know, to will. For I am and I know and I will: I am, knowing and willing; and I know that I am and that I will; and I will to be and to know.

In these three, then, how inseparable a life and one life and one mind and one essence is—how inseparable a distinction, and yet a distinction—let him see who can. It is surely before him; let him attend to himself and see[^56], and tell me.

But when he has found something in these and said it, let him not now think he has found that which is unchangeable above these—which is unchangeably and knows unchangeably and wills unchangeably; and whether on account of these three there is a Trinity also there, or whether these three are in each, so that there are three of each,

or whether both, in wondrous ways, simply and manifoldly, with an infinite end in itself to itself, by which it is and is known to itself and is sufficient to itself—the Selfsame, unchangeably—by an abundant magnitude of unity, who could easily think? Who in any way could say? Who in any way could rashly declare?

## Those who were covered by the darkness of ignorance have been illumined by the Lord's teaching

### Chapter 12

**13**. Go on in confession, my faith; say to the Lord your God: *Holy, holy, holy, Lord my God*[^57], in your name *we have been baptized*[^58], Father and Son and Holy Spirit; in your name we baptize, Father and Son and Holy Spirit[^59]; since with us too, in his Christ, *God has made heaven and earth*[^60]—the spiritual and the carnal members of his Church—and our *earth, before it received the form of teaching, was invisible and unformed*[^61], and we were covered by the darkness of ignorance, since *you have chastened a man for iniquity*[^62], and *your judgments are like a great abyss*[^63]. But because *your Spirit was borne over the water*[^64], your mercy did not abandon our misery; and you said, *Let there be light*[^65]; *Repent, for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near. Repent*[^66]; *Let there be light*. And since *our soul was troubled within ourselves*, we remembered you, Lord, *from the land of the Jordan and from the mountain* equal to you, but small for our sake[^67]; and our darkness displeased us, and *we turned to you*[^68], *and there was light*[^69]. And behold, we were *once darkness, but now light in the Lord*[^70].

### Chapter 13: The expectation of adoption and redemption

**14**. And yet still *by faith, not yet by sight*[^71]. *For by hope we are saved. But hope that is seen is not hope*[^72]. Still *deep calls to deep, but now in the voice of your waterfalls*[^73].

And still even he who says, *I could not speak to you as to spiritual, but as to carnal*[^74], even he does not yet judge that he has comprehended; and, *forgetting the things behind, he is extended toward the things that are before*[^75], and groans, weighed down[^76], and his soul *thirsts for the living God, as the harts for the fountains of water*, and says, *When shall I come?*[^77] Longing *to be clothed over with his dwelling, which is from heaven*[^78], he calls upon the lower deep, saying, *Do not be conformed to this age, but be reformed in the newness of your mind*[^79]; and, *Do not become children in mind, but in malice be little ones, that you may be perfect in mind*[^80]; and, *O foolish Galatians, who has bewitched you?*[^81]

But no longer in his own voice; for in yours, who sent *your Spirit from on high*[^82] through him who *ascended on high*[^83] and opened the floodgates of his gifts[^84], that *the river's rush might gladden your city*[^85].

For for him sighs *the bridegroom's friend*[^86], having already *the first fruits of the Spirit* with him, but groaning still within himself, *awaiting adoption, the redemption of his body*[^87]; for him he sighs (for he is a member of the bride), and for him he is jealous[^88] (for he is *the bridegroom's friend*), for him he is jealous, not for himself; because *in the voice of your waterfalls*, not in his own voice, he calls upon the other deep, for which, jealous, he fears, *lest, as the serpent deceived Eve by his craftiness*, so their *senses too be corrupted from the chastity that is* in our bridegroom[^89], your Only-begotten.

What is that light of vision? When *we shall see him as he is*[^90], and the *tears shall pass away which have become my bread day and night, while it is said to me daily: Where is your God?*[^91]

### Chapter 14: Faith and hope

**15**. And I say, "My God, where are you?" Behold where you are. *I breathe in you a little*[^92], when *I pour out my soul above me in the voice of exultation and confession, of a sound that celebrates a festival*[^93]. And still it is sad, because it falls back and becomes an abyss, or rather still feels itself to be an abyss.

My faith, which you have kindled in the night before my feet, says to it: *Why are you sad, my soul, and why do you trouble me? Hope in the Lord*[^94]; *a lamp to your feet* is his *word*[^95]. *Hope* and persevere, until the night passes, the mother of the unjust; until the wrath of the Lord passes, whose *sons we too were*[^96], *once darkness*[^97], whose remnants we drag along in *a body dead because of sin*[^98], *until the day breathe and the shadows be removed*[^99]. *Hope in the Lord: in the morning I will stand and* contemplate[^100]; *I will always confess to him*[^101].

*In the morning I will stand and see*[^102] the *salvation of my countenance, my God*[^103], *who will also give life to our mortal bodies because of the Spirit who dwells in us*[^104], because he was mercifully borne over our inner, dark and flowing depth. From this, on this pilgrimage we have received a pledge[^105], that we may already be *light*[^106], while still *we are saved by hope*[^107], and are *sons of light and sons of day, not sons of night nor of darkness*[^108]—which yet we were[^109]. Between them and us, in this still uncertain human knowledge, you alone divide[^110], you who *test our hearts*[^111] and call *light day and darkness night*[^112]. For *who discerns us but you?*[^113] And what have we that we have *not received from you*—*from the same lump*, vessels *for honor*, from which others have been made *for dishonor*[^114]?

## The firmament, or the abiding authority of Scripture

### Chapter 15

**16**. Or who but you, our God, has made for us a firmament of authority above us[^115] in your divine Scripture? For *heaven shall be rolled up like a book*[^116], and now *like a skin it is stretched out*[^117] above us. For your divine Scripture is of more sublime authority since those mortals through whom you dispensed it to us have already passed this death. *And you know, Lord, you know*[^118], how you clothed human beings in skins, when by sin they became mortal[^119]. Thus, as a skin, you have stretched out the firmament of your Book—your concordant words, which through the ministry of mortals you have set above us.

For by their very death the solid frame of authority in your utterances brought forth through them is sublimely stretched out above all that is below, which, while they lived here, was not so sublimely stretched. *You had not yet stretched out heaven like a skin*[^120]; you had not yet spread the fame of their death throughout.

**17**. Let us see, Lord, *the heavens, the works of your fingers*[^121]; clear from our eyes the cloud with which you have undergirded them. There is your *testimony, giving wisdom to little ones*[^122]. Perfect, my God, *your praise out of the mouth of infants and sucklings*[^123].

For we know no other books so destroying pride[^124], so destroying *the enemy and the defender*[^125], who resists your reconciliation by defending his sins. I know not, Lord, I know not any other such *chaste words*[^126], which so persuade me to confession, and soften my neck to your yoke[^127], and invite me to worship you freely.

Let me understand these things[^128], good Father; grant me this, I who am set beneath—because for those set beneath you have made them solid.

#### The waters above the firmament, or the peoples of the angels

**18**. There are other waters above this firmament[^129], I believe, immortal and set apart from earthly corruption. *Let them praise your name; let them praise* you, the supercelestial peoples of your angels[^130], who have no need to look up at this firmament and learn your word by reading. For they always *see your face*[^131], and there they read, without syllables of time, what your eternal will wills. They read, they choose, and they love; they always read, and what they read never passes. For by choosing and loving they read the very unchangeableness of your counsel. Their book is not closed, nor is their scroll rolled up[^132], because you yourself are this to them, and are forever[^133]—since over this firmament you have set them in order, which you have made firm over the weakness of lower peoples, where they may look up and know your mercy proclaiming you in time, you who made times. *For in heaven, Lord, your mercy, and your truth as far as the clouds*[^134].

The clouds pass[^135], but heaven abides. The preachers of your word pass from this life into another life, but your Scripture is stretched over the peoples to the end of the age. But also *heaven and earth shall pass away, but your words shall not pass away*[^136], since the skin shall be rolled up, and the grass over which it was stretched shall pass away with its splendor; but *your word abides forever*[^137]—which now *in the enigma of clouds and through the mirror*[^138] of heaven, not as it is, appears to us; since we too, although beloved by your Son, *it has not yet appeared what we shall be*[^139]. He looked through the lattice[^140] of the flesh, and he allured us and inflamed us, and we run after the fragrance of him[^141]. But *when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^142]—as he is, Lord, our seeing, which we do not yet have.

### Chapter 16: The soul thirsts and hungers

**19**. For as you simply are, you alone know—you who are unchangeably and know unchangeably and will unchangeably. And your essence knows and wills unchangeably, and your knowledge is and wills unchangeably, and your will is and knows unchangeably. Nor does it seem just before you that, as the unchangeable Light knows itself, so it should be known by the changeable illumined one. Therefore my *soul is to you as a land without water*[^143], because just as it cannot illumine itself from itself, so it cannot satisfy itself from itself. For so *with you is the fountain of life*, just as *in your light we shall see light*[^144].

## The gathering of the waters, or the society of those who live as pagans

### Chapter 17

**20**. Who has gathered the bitter into one society? For the same end of temporal and earthly happiness is theirs, for which they do all things, although they fluctuate in countless variety of cares. Who, Lord, but you, who said that the *waters be gathered into one gathering, and the dry land appear*[^145], thirsting for you? *For yours is the sea, and you made it, and your hands formed the dry land*[^146]. For not the bitterness of wills, but the gathering of waters, is called sea. For you also restrain the evil desires of souls, and fix limits, how far they may be allowed to advance, and that their waves be broken in themselves[^147]. And thus you make the sea, by the order of your rule over all things.

#### Dry land and its fruits, or souls thirsting for God and doing good

**21**. But souls thirsting for you[^148] and appearing before you, distinguished by another end from the society of the sea, you water by a hidden and sweet fountain, that the earth too may give its fruit; and it gives its fruit, and at your command, its Lord God, our soul springs up with works of mercy *according to kind*[^149], loving our neighbor[^150] in helps for carnal needs, *having in itself seed according to likeness*[^151], since out of our own weakness we have compassion to come to the aid of the needy, helping them as we would wish help to be brought to us, if we were in like need;

not only in easy things, as in a plant of seed, but also in the protection of help with strong vigor, like a fruit-bearing tree—that is, beneficent for rescuing one who suffers wrong from the hand of the powerful, and providing a shelter of protection by the strong vigor of just judgment.

## Sun, moon, and stars, or the operations of the Spirit

### Chapter 18

**22**. So, Lord, so, I beseech you, let it rise—as you make, as you give cheerfulness and capacity—let *truth rise from the earth, and righteousness look down from heaven*[^152]; and *let there be luminaries in the firmament*[^153]. Let us *break our bread for the hungry, and bring the needy without shelter into our house; let us clothe the naked, and not despise the members of our own seed. With these fruits born of the earth*, see that it is good, and *let our temporal light break forth*[^154]; and, holding the superior Word of life from this lower fruit of action into the delights of contemplation, let us appear *like luminaries in the world*[^155], cleaving to the firmament of your Scripture.

For there you confer with us, that we may divide between the intelligible and the sensible, as between day and night—or between souls given some to the intelligible, some to the sensible—so that no longer do you alone, in the hiddenness of your discerning, divide between light and darkness, as before the firmament was made[^156], but your spiritual ones too, set in that same firmament and distinguished, with your grace made manifest throughout the world, *may shine over the earth and divide between day and night, and signify times*[^157]—because *old things have passed, behold, all things have been made new*[^158], and because *our salvation is nearer than when we believed*, and because *the night is past, but the day has drawn near*[^159], and because *you bless the crown of your year*[^160], sending *workers into your harvest*[^161], in the sowing of which *others have labored*[^162], sending them also into another sowing, whose harvest is at the end.

So you give the petitioner his prayers and bless the years of the righteous; *but you yourself are the same, and in your years, which do not fail*[^163], you prepare a barn for years that pass away. For by your eternal counsel, in their own times, you give heavenly goods upon the earth.

**23**. Since indeed *to one is given through the Spirit the word of wisdom* as a greater luminary, on account of those who delight in the light of clear truth as at the beginning of day; *to another the word of knowledge according to the same Spirit* as a lesser luminary; *to another faith, to another the gift of healings, to another the workings of miracles, to another prophecy, to another the discerning of spirits, to another kinds of tongues*—and all these as stars. *For one and the same Spirit works all these, dividing to each what is his own as he wills*, and making the stars appear in manifestation *for utility*[^164].

But the *word of knowledge*, in which all *mysteries* are contained[^165]—which are varied with the times, like the moon—and the other notions of gifts, which are mentioned next, like stars, by however much they differ from that brightness of wisdom in which the aforesaid day rejoices, by so much they are at the beginning of night. For these are necessary to those to whom that most prudent servant of yours *could not speak as to spiritual, but as to carnal*[^166]—he who *speaks wisdom among the perfect*[^167]. But *the animal man*[^168], as a little one in Christ and a drinker of milk[^169], until he is strengthened for solid food[^170] and steadies his gaze for the sight of the sun, let him not have his night deserted, but be content with the light of the moon and the stars.

These things you most wisely confer with us, our God, in your book, your firmament, that we may discern all things by a wondrous contemplation—although still *in signs and in times and in days and in years*[^171].

### Chapter 19: An exhortation of the elect

**24**. But first *wash yourselves, be clean, take away wickedness from your souls and from the sight of my eyes*, that *the dry land may appear*. *Learn to do good; judge for the orphan, and justify the widow*, that the earth may bring forth a plant of fodder and a fruit-bearing tree, and *come, let us reason together, says the Lord*—that *luminaries may be made in the firmament of heaven, and shine over the earth*[^172].

That rich man was asking the good Master what he should do to obtain eternal life; let the good Master—whom he supposed to be a man and nothing more (but he is good, because he is God)—say to him that, if he wishes to come to life, he should keep the commandments, set apart from himself the bitterness of malice and wickedness, not kill, not commit adultery, not steal, not bear false witness, that *the dry land may appear* and bring forth honor for mother and father and love for his neighbor. *I have done*, he said, *all these things*.

From where, then, so many thorns, if the earth is fruit-bearing? *Go, root out the wooded thickets of greed, sell what you possess, and fill yourself with fruits by giving to the poor, and you shall have treasure in heaven; and follow the Lord, if you wish to be perfect*, joined to those among whom he speaks wisdom—he who knows what to distribute to day and to night—that you too may know, that for you too *luminaries may be made in the firmament of heaven*; which will not be, unless *your heart be there*; which likewise will not be, unless *your treasure be there*, as you have heard from the good Master. But the barren earth was saddened, and *the thorns choked the word*[^173].

**25**. But you, *chosen race*[^174], *the weak things of the world*[^175], who have left all things to follow the Lord[^176], go after him and *confound the strong*[^177]; go after him, *beautiful feet*[^178], and shine *in the firmament*[^179], that *the heavens may declare his glory*[^180]—dividing between the light of the perfect, but not yet as of angels, and the darkness of the little ones, but not the despaired-of[^181]; shine over all the earth[^182], and let day, blazing with the sun, belch forth *to day the word of wisdom*, and let night, the moon shining, announce *to night the word of knowledge*[^183]. The moon and stars shine for the night, but night does not darken them, since they illumine it according to its measure.

For behold, as if at God's saying, *Let there be luminaries in the firmament of heaven*[^184], *suddenly there came a sound from heaven, as of a violent rushing wind, and there appeared tongues divided as if of fire, which also sat upon each of them*[^185]; and luminaries were made in the firmament of heaven, having *the word of life*[^186]. Run everywhere, holy fires, beautiful fires. For *you are the light of the world, and you are not under a bushel*[^187]. He to whom you clung is exalted, and he has exalted you. Run, and make yourselves known to all the nations[^188].

## Creeping things, or the sacraments; whales, or miracles; flying creatures, or God's messengers

### Chapter 20

**26**. Let the sea conceive and bring forth your works; *and let the waters produce the creeping things of living souls*[^189]. For, *separating the precious from the vile*, you have been made *the mouth of God*[^190], through which he says: *Let the waters produce*—not the living soul which the earth will produce[^191]—but *the creeping things of living souls and the flying creatures flying over the earth*[^192]. For your sacraments crept, God, through the works of your saints, among the midst of the waves of the temptations of the age, to imbue the nations with your name in your baptism[^193].

And amid these were wrought *great things*[^194], wonderful things, like great whales, and the voices of your messengers flying over the earth alongside the firmament of your Book, that being set before them as their authority, under which they would fly wherever they went. *For there are no tongues, no speeches, whose voices are not heard, when their sound has gone forth into every land, and their words to the ends of the world*[^195]—since you, Lord, by blessing have multiplied these things[^196].

#### Waters, or the nations

**27**. Do I lie, or by mingling confuse, and not distinguish the clear knowings of these things in the firmament of heaven and the bodily works in the wavy sea and under the firmament of heaven?

For of those things whose knowings are solid and bounded, without the increments of generations, like the lights of wisdom and knowledge, of these same things the bodily operations are many and varied, and one from another, by growing, they are multiplied in your blessing, God,

you who have consoled the disgusts of mortal senses, so that in the knowing of the mind one thing may, in many ways, through bodily motions, be figured and spoken.

*The waters produced these things*[^197], but in your word. The necessities of peoples alienated from the eternity of your truth produced these things, but in your Gospel—since the waters themselves cast these forth, whose bitter sickness was the cause that, in your word, they should produce them.

**28**. And all things are beautiful as you make them; and behold, you are unspeakably more beautiful, you who have made all things. From whom, if Adam had not fallen, the saltness of the sea would not have been poured out of his womb—the human race, deeply curious and stormily swollen and unstably flowing—and thus it would not have been necessary that, in the many waters, bodily and sensibly, your dispensers should work mystical deeds and sayings.

For so the creeping things and flying creatures now occur to me, by which people, imbued and initiated, made subject to bodily sacraments, would not advance further, unless the soul came spiritually alive at another degree, and, after the word of beginning, looked forward to perfection[^198].

## The living soul, or the faithful

### Chapter 21

**29**. And by this, in your word, not the depth of the sea, but the earth distinct from the bitterness of the waters, casts forth not the creeping things of living souls and the flying creatures, but *a living soul*[^199]. For it no longer has need of baptism, of which the nations have need, as it had when it was covered with the waters: for there is no other way *to enter into the kingdom of heaven* than that you have instituted, that one should so enter[^200]; nor does it seek the *great things*[^201] of marvels, by which faith may come about; for it does not say, *Unless he see signs and wonders, he does not believe*[^202], since now the faithful land has been distinguished from the waters of the sea bitter with infidelity; and *tongues are for a sign, not to the faithful, but to the unfaithful*[^203].

Nor, then, has the earth, which *you founded above the waters*[^204], need of this flying kind which the waters produced at your word. Send into it your word[^205] through your messengers. For we recount their works, but it is you who work in them[^206], that they may work *a living soul*[^207]. The earth produces it, since the earth is the cause that these things should act in it—just as the sea was the cause that *the creeping things of living souls and the flying creatures under the firmament of heaven*[^208] should act—which the earth no longer needs, although it eats a fish[^209] raised from the deep on that table which *you have prepared in the sight* of believers[^210]; for it was raised from the deep precisely to feed the dry land.

And the birds, the sea's progeny[^211], are nevertheless multiplied above the earth[^212]. For the unbelief of human beings was the cause of the first voices preaching the gospel; yet the faithful too are exhorted and blessed by them in many ways *from day to day*[^213]. But the living soul takes its origin from the earth, since it benefits only those already faithful—to refrain from love of this age[^214], that their soul may live to you[^215], which was *dead, living in delights*[^216]—deadly delights, Lord; for you are the life-giving delights of the pure of heart[^217].

**30**. Let your ministers, then, now work in the earth, not as in the waters of unbelief by announcing and speaking through miracles and sacraments and mystical voices, where intent ignorance becomes the mother of admiration in fear of hidden signs (for such is the entrance to faith for the sons of Adam, who have forgotten you while they hide from your face[^218] and become an abyss); but let them work even as in dry land distinguished from the whirlpools of the abyss, and be *a pattern for the faithful*[^219], by living before them and rousing them to imitation.

For so they hear not only for hearing but also for doing: *Seek God, and your soul shall live*[^220], that *the earth may bring forth a living soul*[^221]. *Do not be conformed to this age*[^222]; restrain yourselves from it. By avoiding it the soul lives that, by craving, dies. Restrain yourselves from the savage ferocity of pride, from the idle voluptuousness of luxury, and from *the deceitful name of knowledge*[^223], that the beasts may be tame and the cattle broken and the serpents harmless[^224].

For these are motions of the soul in allegory; but the haughtiness of pride and the delight of lust and the venom of curiosity are motions of a dead soul: since it does not die in such a way as to lack all motion, but, by departing from the fountain of life[^225], it dies, and so is received by the passing age, and is conformed to it.

#### The beasts of the earth, or the good affections of the soul

**31**. But your Word, God[^226], is the fountain of eternal life[^227], and does not pass away[^228]; and so in your word that departure is restrained, while it is said to us: *Do not be conformed to this age*—that the earth may bring forth, at the fountain of life, *a living soul*, in your word through your evangelists—a soul restraining itself, by imitating the imitators of your Christ[^229]. For this is *according to kind*[^230], since one's emulation is of his friend: *Be as I am*, he says, *for I too am as you*[^231].

Thus in the living soul there will be good beasts in the meekness of action. For you have commanded, saying, *Carry out your works in meekness, and you shall be loved by every man*[^232]. And good cattle, who, if they eat, are not in abundance, and if they do not eat, are not in want[^233]; and good serpents, not destructive to harm, but cunning to beware[^234], and exploring temporal nature only so much as suffices that *through the things that have been made, eternity may be beheld as understood*[^235]. For these animals serve reason, when, restrained from a deadly progress, they live and are good.

## The human being made in the image of God, or renewed

### Chapter 22

**32**. For behold, Lord our God, our Creator, when the affections shall have been restrained from the love of the age, by which we were dying through ill living, and the soul shall have begun to be a living soul through good living, and your word shall have been fulfilled in which through your Apostle you said, *Do not be conformed to this age*, there will follow what you added at once and said: *But be reformed in the newness of your mind*[^236]—no longer *according to kind*[^237], as if imitating the neighbor who has gone before, nor living by the authority of a better man.

For you did not say, "Let a man be made according to kind," but, *Let us make a human being in our image and likeness*[^238]—that we may prove *what is your will*[^239]. For to this end your dispenser, begetting sons through the gospel[^240], lest he should always have little ones whom he must feed with milk[^241] and cherish as a nurse[^242], says: *Be reformed in the newness of your mind, to prove what is the will of God—what is good and well-pleasing and perfect*[^243]. Therefore you do not say, "Let a man be made," but, *Let us make*; nor do you say, "according to kind," but, *in our image and likeness*.

For one renewed in mind, and beholding your truth as understood, does not need a human demonstrator that he may imitate his own kind, but, with you demonstrating, he himself proves *what is your will—what is good and well-pleasing and perfect*; and you teach him, now capable, to see the Trinity of the Unity, or the Unity of the Trinity.

Therefore, after it has been said in the plural, *Let us make a human being*, it is brought in in the singular, *And God made a human being*; and after it has been said in the plural, *in our image*, it is brought in in the singular, *in the image of God*[^244].

Thus a human being *is renewed in the knowledge of God, according to the image of him who created him*[^245], and, made spiritual, *judges all things* which surely are to be judged; *but himself is judged by no one*[^246].

### Chapter 23: What is the power of the spiritual human

**33**. But that he *judges all things* means that he has *power over the fish of the sea and the flying creatures of heaven and all the cattle and beasts and over all the earth and over all things that creep, which creep upon the earth*[^247]. For this he does through the mind's understanding, through which *he perceives the things of the Spirit of God*[^248]. Otherwise, *a man placed in honor did not understand; he has been compared to senseless beasts of burden and has been made like them*[^249].

Therefore in your Church, our God, *according to your grace*[^250], which you have given it, since *we are your handiwork, created in good works*[^251], not only those who spiritually preside, but also those who spiritually are made subject to those who preside (for *you have made a human being male and female*[^252] in this manner in your spiritual grace, where, as to bodily sex, *there is not male and female*, since *neither Jew nor Greek, neither slave nor free*[^253])—

—the spiritual, then, whether those who preside or those who obey, spiritually judge[^254]—not concerning the spiritual cognitions which shine *in the firmament*[^255] (for it is not fitting to judge concerning so sublime an authority), nor concerning your book itself, even if something there does not shine, since we submit our understanding to it and hold for sure that even what is closed to our sight has been rightly and truly said (for so a person, even though now spiritual and *renewed in the knowledge of God, according to the image of him who created him*[^256], ought to be a *doer of the law, not its judge*[^257])—

nor does he judge concerning that distinction of spiritual and carnal human beings, who are known, our God, to your eyes, and have not yet appeared to us by any works, that *we might know them from their fruits*[^258]; but you, Lord, already know them, and you have divided them and called them in secret, before the firmament was made.

Nor does the spiritual man judge concerning the turbulent peoples of this age[^259]. For *what has he to judge concerning those who are outside*[^260], not knowing who from there will come into the sweetness of your grace, and who will remain in the perpetual bitterness of impiety?

**34**. And so the human being, whom you made *in your image*[^261], has not received power over the luminaries of heaven, nor over the hidden heaven itself, nor over day and night, which you called before the establishing of heaven, nor over the gathering of the waters which is the sea; but has received power over the fish of the sea and the flying creatures of heaven and all the cattle and all the earth and all things that creep, which creep upon the earth.

For he judges and approves what he finds right, and disapproves what he finds amiss—whether in that solemnity of the sacraments by which those are initiated whom your mercy searches out in the many waters; or in that in which the fish is set forth which, raised from the deep, the devout earth eats; or in the signs of words and voices subject to the authority of your Book, like ones flying under the firmament—by interpreting, expounding, discussing, disputing, blessing, and invoking you, with signs bursting and sounding from the mouth, so that the people may answer, *Amen*[^262].

For all these voices, which must be uttered bodily, the cause is the abyss of the age and the blindness of the flesh, through which thoughts cannot be seen, so that there is need to make a noise in the ears. Thus, although *the flying creatures are multiplied over the earth, they draw their origin from the waters*[^263].

The *spiritual* man also *judges*, approving what is right, and disapproving what he finds amiss in the works and ways of the faithful—in almsgiving, as in fruit-bearing earth, and in the affections tamed in the living soul, *in chastity, in fastings*[^264], in pious thoughts about the things perceived by the bodily sense. For he is now said to judge concerning those things in which he also has the power to correct.

## The propagation of the human race, or the fruitfulness of understanding and speech

### Chapter 24

**35**. But *what is this*[^265], and what mystery is it? Behold, you bless human beings, Lord, that they may *grow and multiply and fill the earth*[^266]. Do you hint nothing to us from this for us to understand? Why did you not so bless the light, which you called day, nor the firmament of heaven, nor the luminaries, nor the stars, nor the earth, nor the sea? I would say, our God, who created us in your image[^267], I would say that you willed this gift of blessing to be bestowed properly on the human being, were it not that you blessed the fishes and whales in the same way, that they should grow and multiply and fill the waters of the sea, and that the flying creatures should be multiplied above the earth[^268].

Likewise I would say that this blessing pertains to those kinds of things that are propagated by begetting from themselves, were I to find it in trees and bushes and in cattle of the earth. But as it is, it is not said to the plants and trees, nor to the beasts and serpents, *Grow and multiply*, even though all these too, like fish and birds and human beings, are increased by begetting and keep their kind[^269].

**36**. What, then, shall I say, my light, truth? That this is in vain, that it has been said emptily? Not at all, Father of mercy; far be it that a servant of your word should say this. And if I do not understand what you signify by this utterance, let those who are better, that is, more intelligent than I, use it better—each according to as much as you have given him to understand. But let my confession too please *before your eyes*[^270], in which I confess to you that I believe, Lord, that you have not spoken so in vain; nor will I be silent about what the occasion of this reading suggests to me. For it is true; and I see nothing that hinders me from so perceiving the figurative sayings of your Books.

For I know that what is understood by the mind in one way is signified bodily in many ways, and that what is signified bodily in one way is understood by the mind in many ways. Behold, the simple love of God and neighbor[^271]—by how many sacraments and innumerable tongues, and in each tongue by innumerable modes of speaking, is it bodily uttered! So the offspring of the waters grow and multiply.

Attend again, whoever reads this. Behold what Scripture offers in one way, and the voice sounds: *In the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^272]—is it not understood in many ways, not by the deceit of errors, but by kinds of true understandings? So grow and multiply the offspring of human beings.

**37**. And so, if we think of the very natures of things not allegorically but properly, the word fits all things that are begotten from seeds: *Grow and multiply*[^273]. But if we treat these things as set down figuratively (which I rather judge that Scripture intended, which surely did not without reason attribute this blessing only to the offspring of the waters and of human beings), we find multitudes also in spiritual and corporeal creatures, as in heaven and earth; and in souls righteous and unjust, as in light and darkness; and in the holy authors through whom the Law was administered, as in the firmament made firm between water and water; and in the society of the bitter peoples, as in the sea; and in the zeal of devout souls, as in dry land; and in the works of mercy according to the present life, as in seed-bearing plants and fruit-bearing trees; and in spiritual gifts manifested *for utility*[^274], as in the luminaries of heaven; and in the affections formed for temperance, as in the living soul.

In all these we find multitudes and abundance and increases; but that something should so grow and multiply that one thing may be uttered in many ways, and one utterance be understood in many ways, we do not find except in signs uttered bodily and in things thought out intelligibly. Signs uttered bodily we have understood as the generations of the waters, on account of the necessary causes of carnal profundity; but things thought out intelligibly we have understood as human generations, on account of the fruitfulness of reason.

And so we have believed that it has been said by you, Lord, to both these kinds: *Grow and multiply*. For in this blessing we hold that there has been granted to us from you the capacity and power both to utter in many ways what we have grasped as understood in one way, and to understand in many ways what we have read obscurely uttered in one way.

So the waters of the sea are filled, which are not moved except by various significations; so too the earth, whose dryness appears in zeal and over which reason rules, is filled with human offspring.

## Plants and trees, or the works of mercy owed to God's ministers

### Chapter 25

**38**. I wish to say too, Lord my God, what your Scripture, as it follows, reminds me of; and I will say, and I will not fear. For I shall say true things, with you inspiring me, which from these words you willed that I should say. For with no other inspiring than you do I believe that I speak truly, since you are *the Truth, but every man is a liar*[^275].

And therefore *he who speaks a lie speaks of his own*[^276]. Therefore, that I may speak the truth, I speak of yours: *behold, you have given us for food every fertile grass sowing seed, which is over all the earth, and every tree which has in it the fruit of fertile seed*[^277]. And not to us alone, but also to all the birds of heaven and the beasts of the earth and the serpents[^278]—but to the fishes and the great whales you did not give these. For we were saying that by these fruits of the earth there are signified, and figured in allegory, the works of mercy which from the fruit-bearing earth are shown forth for the necessities of this life.

Such an earth was the devout Onesiphorus, to whose house you gave *mercy, because he frequently refreshed your Paul and was not ashamed of his chain*[^279]. The brethren did this too, and bore fruit in such produce, who *supplied what was lacking to him from Macedonia*[^280].

But how he grieves over certain trees which did not give him the fruit owed, where he says: *In my first defense no one stood by me, but all forsook me: may it not be reckoned to them*[^281].

For food is owed to those who minister rational teaching through understandings of the divine mysteries; and so it is owed to them as to human beings.

And it is owed to them as to a living soul, offering themselves to be imitated in every continence.

Likewise it is owed to them as to flying creatures, on account of their blessings, which are multiplied over the earth, since *their sound has gone forth into every land*[^282].

### Chapter 26: Let the will of the giver be right

**39**. But those are fed with these foods who rejoice in them; and those do not rejoice in them *whose god is the belly*[^283]. For also in those who provide these things, the fruit is not what they give, but the spirit in which they give. And so that one who served God, not his own belly[^284]—I see plainly the source of his joy; I see, and I greatly congratulate him. For he had received from the Philippians what they had sent through Epaphroditus[^285]; but still, the source of his joy I see.

From where he rejoices, from there he is fed; since, speaking in truth, he says: *I rejoiced greatly in the Lord, that at last you have flourished again to be wise concerning me, in which you were wise; but you were weary of it*. So they had drooped through long weariness and had, as it were, dried up from this fruit of good work; and he rejoices over them because they have sprung up again, not over himself because they came to his aid in his need.

And so he continues: *I do not say this because of any want; for I have learned, in whatever state I am, to be content. I know how to have less, and I know how to abound; in all things and in every way I am instructed both to be filled and to be hungry, both to abound and to suffer want: I can do all things in him who strengthens me*[^286].

#### Let us rejoice in the right will of the giver, not in the use of the gift

**40**. From where, then, do you rejoice, great Paul? From where do you rejoice, from where are you fed, man *renewed in the knowledge of God, according to the image of him who created you*[^287], and living soul of so great continence, and flying tongue speaking mysteries[^288]? For to such living creatures this food is owed. What is it that feeds you? Joy. Let me hear what follows: *Yet*, he says, *you did well by sharing in my tribulation*[^289].

From this he rejoices, from this he is fed, because they did well, not because his distress was relaxed—he who says to you, *In tribulation you gave me room*[^290], since *both to abound and to suffer want* he knows in you, who strengthen him.

*For you yourselves know, Philippians, that at the beginning of the Gospel, when I set out from Macedonia, no Church communicated with me in the matter of giving and receiving except you alone; for to Thessalonica also you sent once and again for my needs*[^291]. He now rejoices that they have come back to these good works, and is glad that they have sprung up again, as if a field's fertility were turning green once more.

**41**. Was it because of his own uses—since he said, *you sent for my needs*—was it for that reason he rejoices? Not for that reason. And how do we know this? Because he himself goes on to say: *Not that I seek the gift, but I look for the fruit*[^292].

I have learned from you, my God, to distinguish between *the gift and the fruit*. The gift is the thing itself which he gives who imparts these necessities, such as money, food, drink, clothing, shelter, help. But the fruit is the good and right will of the giver.

For the good Master did not say only, *he who receives a prophet*, but added, *in the name of a prophet*;

nor did he say only, *he who receives a righteous man*, but added, *in the name of a righteous man*; for so the one shall receive a prophet's reward, the other a righteous man's reward.

Nor did he say only, *he who has given a cup of cold water to drink to one of these least*, but added, *only in the name of a disciple*; and so he added, *Amen I say to you, he shall not lose his reward*[^293].

The gift is to receive a prophet, to receive a righteous man, to hand a cup of cold water to a disciple; but the fruit is to do this *in the name of a prophet, in the name of a righteous man, in the name of a disciple*. By the fruit Elijah is fed by the widow who knew that she was feeding a man of God and was feeding him for that reason; but by the raven he was fed with the gift[^294]. And not the inner Elijah, but the outer was fed—he who could be corrupted even by want of such food.

### Chapter 27: Unbelievers do not perform good works with a right will

**42**. Therefore I will say what is true before you, Lord: when *uninstructed and unbelieving* human beings[^295]—to whose initiation and winning the sacraments of initiation and the *great things*[^296] of miracles are necessary, which we believe to be signified by the name of fishes and whales—take in your boys to be bodily refreshed or helped in some need of the present life, while they are ignorant of why this should be done and to what it pertains, neither do those feed these, nor are these fed by them; because they do not work these things with a holy and right will, nor do the latter rejoice in their gifts where they do not yet see fruit. For the mind is fed from where it rejoices. And so the fishes and whales do not feed on the food which the earth, already distinguished and separated from the bitterness of the sea-waves, does not bring forth[^297].

## All God's works are very good

### Chapter 28

**43**. And you saw, God, all the things which you made, *and behold, very good*[^298]—since we too see them, and behold, all *very good*. In each of the kinds of your works, when you had said that they should be made, and they were made, you saw this and that, that it was good. Seven times I counted it written that you saw that what you made was good[^299]; and this is the eighth: that you saw all the things you made, and behold, not only good but also *very good*, as it were all together.

For singly they were only good; but together all were both *good* and *very*. This is said too by all beautiful bodies: that a body composed of all beautiful members is far, far more beautiful than the members themselves singly, by whose most ordered conjunction the whole is completed, although those too, taken one by one, are beautiful.

### Chapter 29: No time accrues to God's word

**44**. And I attended to find out whether you saw seven times or eight times that your works are good, when they pleased you; and in your vision I found no times by which I might understand that so many times you saw what you made; and I said:

"O Lord, is not this Scripture of yours true, since you, *truthful*[^300] and *the Truth*[^301], brought it forth? Why, then, do you tell me that in your vision there are no times, and this Scripture of yours tells me that on each day you saw the things you made, that they are good—and when I counted them, I found how many times?"

To this you say to me, since you are *my God*[^302], and you say with a strong voice in my inner ear to your servant, breaking through my deafness and crying: "O man, surely what my Scripture says, I say. And yet it says it temporally; but to my word time does not accrue, since it stands with me in equal eternity. So the things you see by my Spirit, I see, just as the things you say by my Spirit, I say. And thus, while you see them temporally, I do not see them temporally; just as, while you say them temporally, I do not say them temporally."

### Chapter 30: The Manicheans' mad sayings about the works of God

**45**. And I heard, Lord my God, and I licked a drop of sweetness from your truth, and I understood that there are some to whom your works are displeasing, and they say that you made many of them under the compulsion of necessity, such as the fabrics of the heavens and the arrangements of the stars, and that you did not make them from your own, but they had already been created elsewhere, from another source, and you drew them together and joined and wove them, when from your conquered enemies you fashioned the walls of the world, so that, by that construction defeated, they could not rebel against you again; while others, they say, you neither made nor put together at all—such as all flesh, and the tiniest animals, and whatever holds the earth by its roots—but that a hostile mind and another nature, not founded by you and opposed to you, in the lower places of the world begets and forms these things. They are mad who say these things, since they do not see your works by your Spirit, nor do they recognize you in them.

### Chapter 31: All things that are, seen through God's Spirit, are seen as good

**46**. But those who see these things by your Spirit—you see in them. Therefore when they see that they are good, you see that they are good; and whatever pleases for your sake, you please in them; and what pleases us by your Spirit, pleases you in us.

*For who among men knows the things of a man except the spirit of the man, which is in him? So too no one knows the things of God except the Spirit of God. But we*, he says, *have received not the spirit of this world, but the Spirit who is from God, that we may know what has been given to us by God*.

And I am admonished to say: surely *no one knows the things of God except the Spirit of God*. How then do we too know *what has been given to us by God*? It is answered me that what we know through his Spirit, even thus *no one knows except the Spirit of God*[^303]. For just as it was rightly said to those who would speak in the Spirit of God, *For it is not you who speak*[^304], so it is rightly said to those who know in the Spirit of God: "It is not you who know"; and no less rightly is it said to those who see in the Spirit of God: "It is not you who see." Thus whatever they see in the Spirit of God that it is good, not they but God sees that it is good.

It is one thing, then, for someone to think evil what is good, like those mentioned above;

another, for a man to see what is good, that it is good, as your creation pleases many because it is good, to whom yet you do not please in it, so that they wish to enjoy the creation itself more than you;

and another, that, when a man sees that something is good, God in him sees that it is good, so that he may be loved in what he has made, who would not be loved except through the Spirit whom he has given, since *the love of God has been poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us*[^305], through whom we see that, in whatever way it is, it is good: for it is from him who is not in some way, but who is what he is[^306].

## Thanks are given to God for his good works

### Chapter 32

**47**. *Thanks be to you, Lord*[^307]! We see heaven and earth—whether the upper and lower bodily part, or the spiritual and the corporeal creation—and in the adornment of these parts, of which either the whole mass of the world or all creation whatsoever consists, we see light made and divided from the darkness.

We see the firmament of heaven—whether between the spiritual waters above and the corporeal below, the primary body of the world, or this space of air (for this too is called heaven), through which fly the flying creatures of heaven between the waters that are borne above it in vapor and that bedew even cloudless nights, and these heavy waters that flow on the earth.

We see the appearance of the gathered waters in the plains of the sea, and the dry land, either laid bare or formed to be visible and composed, the mother of grasses and trees.

We see the luminaries shining from above—the sun sufficient for the day, the moon and the stars consoling the night—and that by all these times are marked and signified.

We see the moist nature everywhere fruitful with fish and great beasts and birds—since the corpulence of the air, which bears the flights of birds, grows thick by the exhalation of waters.

We see the face of the earth adorned with earthly animals, and the human being—made in your image and likeness—set above all irrational living creatures by your image and likeness itself, that is, by the power of reason and understanding. And just as in the human soul there is one thing that governs by deliberation, and another that is subjected to obey, so for the man the woman was made even bodily—who would have in mind, indeed, an equal nature of rational understanding, yet in bodily sex would so be subjected to the male sex, just as the appetite of action is subjected for the conceiving of skill in right doing from the reason of the mind. We see these things—both each one good, and all of them very good.

## How all things have been made

### Chapter 33

**48**. Your works praise you[^308], that we may love you; and we love you, that your works may praise you. They have a beginning and an end in time, a rising and a setting, a progress and a defect, form and privation. They have, then, a consequent morning and evening, partly hidden, partly evident.

For from nothing, by you, they were made—not from you, not from anything not yours, nor from anything that existed before—but from concreated, that is, simultaneously created matter, since you formed its formlessness with no interposition of time.

For although the matter of heaven and earth is one thing, and the form of heaven and earth another, you made the matter indeed from utter nothing, and the form of the world from unformed matter; yet you made both together, so that form followed matter with no interval of delay.

### Chapter 34: The things figured in the first chapter of Genesis, expounded once more and in summary

**49**. We have also looked into things for whose figuration you willed these to be made in this order, or to be written in this order; and we have seen that, singly, they are good, and all very good, in your Word, in your Only-begotten—heaven and earth, the head and the body of the Church[^309], in predestination before all times, without morning and evening.

But when you began to carry out temporally the things predestined, that you might *make hidden things manifest*[^310] and put in order our unformed things (since our sins were upon us, and we had gone away from you into a dark depth, and *your good Spirit*[^311] was borne over to come to our aid *in the opportune time*[^312]), you justified the ungodly[^313] and distinguished them from the unjust, and made firm the authority of your Book between the higher who would be docile to you and the lower who would be subject to it, and gathered the society of the unfaithful into one conspiracy, that the zeal of the faithful might appear, that they might bring forth for you works of mercy, distributing even earthly possessions to the poor[^314] to acquire heavenly things.

And from there you kindled certain luminaries in the firmament—your saints, *having the word of life*[^315] and shining with sublime authority granted them through spiritual gifts; and from there you brought forth, from corporeal matter, sacraments and visible miracles and the voices of words, according to the firmament of your Book, to imbue the unfaithful nations, by which the faithful too should be blessed;

and then you formed the faithful's living soul through affections ordered by the vigor of continence; and from there you renewed in your image and likeness the mind subject to you alone and needing the authority of no human being to imitate; and to the surpassing intellect you subjected rational action, as a woman to a man; and for all your ministries necessary in this life for the perfecting of the faithful, you willed that fruitful works for the future should be provided by those same faithful for temporal uses. All these things we see, and they are very good, since you see them in us, who have given us the Spirit by which we might see them and love you in them.

### Chapter 35: May the Lord give us the rest of the eternal Sabbath

**50**. *Lord God, give us peace* (for *you have bestowed all things upon us*[^316])—the peace of rest, the peace of the Sabbath, the peace without evening[^317]. For this whole most beautiful order of things very good, when its measures have been carried out, is to pass away: for in them *there was both morning and evening*[^318].

### Chapter 36

**51**. But the seventh day is without evening and has no setting, because you sanctified it for sempiternal abiding; so that the fact that you, after your works very good—although you made them in quietness—*rested on the seventh day*[^319], this the voice of your Book foretells to us: that we too, after our works which are very good for this reason, that you gave them to us, may rest in you on the Sabbath of eternal life.

### Chapter 37

**52**. For then too you will so rest in us as you now work in us, and that rest of yours will be through us, just as these works of yours are through us. But you, Lord, always work and always rest. You neither see in time, nor are moved in time, nor rest in time; and yet you make both temporal sights and the very times, and rest from time.

### Chapter 38

**53**. We, then, see these things which you have made, because they are; but you, because you see them, they are.

And we outwardly see that they are, and inwardly that they are good; but you saw them as made where you saw them as to-be-made.

And we were moved at one time to do good, after our heart conceived from your Spirit; but at an earlier time we were moved to do evil, deserting you. But you, one good God, have never ceased to do good. And there are certain good works of ours, from your gift indeed, but not sempiternal; after them we hope to rest in your great sanctification. But you—the good needing no good—are always at rest, since your rest is yourself.

And what man shall give to man the understanding of this? What angel to an angel? What angel to a man? Let it be asked of you, sought in you, knocked for at your door; so, so shall it be received, so shall it be found, so shall it be opened[^320]. Amen.
