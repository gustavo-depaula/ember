# Prologue

## Prefatory Letter from Augustine to Aurelius, Bishop of Carthage

To the most blessed lord, to be revered with the most sincere love, the holy brother and fellow priest Pope Aurelius,[^1] Augustine sends greeting in the Lord.

I began as a young man, and have published as an old man, the books on the Trinity, who is the supreme and true God. For I had laid this work aside after I found out that it had been snatched or stolen from me before I had completed it and had polished it by revising it as my plan had been. For I had decided to publish them not one by one but all together, for the reason that the later books are linked to the earlier ones by the progress of the inquiry.

Since, then, through those men—who were able to reach some of these books before I wished them to—my plan could not be carried out, I had left the dictation interrupted, thinking to make this very complaint in some writings of mine, so that those who could might know that the same books had not been published by me but had been taken away before they seemed to me worthy of my publication.

But compelled by the most vehement demand of many brothers, and above all by your command, I took care, with the Lord's help, to bring so laborious a work to its end; and having corrected them not as I wished but as I was able, lest they should differ too greatly from those that had already gone out into men's hands by stealth, I have sent them to Your Reverence through our most dear son, my fellow deacon,[^2] and have permitted them to be heard, copied, and read by anyone.

In these books, had my plan been able to be preserved, they would surely—though holding the same opinions—have been much clearer and plainer, so far as the difficulty of explaining such great matters and my own ability would allow. But there are some who have the first four, or rather even five, without their prefaces, and the twelfth without a considerable final portion; yet if this edition can become known to them, they will correct everything, if they wish and are able. I ask, indeed, that you order this letter to be placed separately, yet at the head of these same books. Pray for me.

[^1]: The title "pope" was given widely to the bishops of the more important sees. The bishop of Carthage was the permanent president of the African synod.
[^2]: Augustine, though a bishop, will usually address or refer to a presbyter as his fellow presbyter and a deacon as his fellow deacon, because his episcopal office was seen as including and sharing in their subordinate ministries.
