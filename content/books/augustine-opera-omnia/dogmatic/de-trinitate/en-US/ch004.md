# Book II

## Preface

### Two things in the error of men are most difficult to tolerate.

**1. 1**. When men seek God, and bend their mind toward the understanding of the Trinity according to the capacity of human weakness, having experienced the laborious difficulties—whether in the very edge of the mind striving to behold *the unapproachable light*, or in the very manifold and many-sided speech of the Sacred Letters, where it seems to me that nothing happens but that Adam[^1] is worn down, so that the glorified grace of Christ may shine clear[^2]—when, all ambiguity discussed away, they have arrived at something certain, they ought most readily to pardon those who err in the investigation of so great a secret.

But there are two things that in the error of men are most difficult to tolerate: presumption, before the truth is clear; and, when it has already become clear, the defense of the presumed falsehood.[^3] From which two vices, exceedingly hostile to the finding of truth and to the handling of the divine and holy Books, if God, as I pray and hope, defends and fortifies me with the *shield of his good will* and the grace of his mercy, I shall not be slow to inquire into the substance of God, whether through his Scripture or through his creation.

Both of which are set before us to be beheld for this end: that he himself be sought, that he himself be loved, who both inspired the one and created the other.[^4]

Nor will I be fearful in bringing forth my opinion, in which I would rather be inspected by the upright than fear to be bitten by the perverse.[^5] For the most beautiful and most modest love gladly accepts the dove's eye; but the dog's tooth either the most cautious humility avoids, or the most solid truth blunts.

And I would rather be reproved by anyone than be praised either by one who errs or by one who flatters. For no reprover is to be feared by a lover of truth. For either an enemy will reprove, or a friend. If, then, an enemy insults, he must be borne; but if a friend errs, he must be taught; if he teaches, he must be heard. But a praiser who errs confirms the error, and one who flatters lures into error.[^6] *The just man, therefore, will correct me in mercy, and will reprove me; but let not the oil of the sinner anoint my head*.

### A canonical rule for understanding the expressions of the Scriptures concerning Christ.

**1. 2**. Wherefore, although we hold most firmly, as a canonical rule disseminated through the Scriptures and demonstrated by the learned Catholic expounders of those same Scriptures, how the Son of God is to be understood as both equal to the Father according to the *form of God* in which he is, and less than the Father according to the *form of a servant* which he took; in which form he was found less not only than the Father, but even than the Holy Spirit, and not only this, but even than himself—not than himself who was, but than himself who is; because, having taken the *form of a servant*, he did not lose the *form of God*, as the testimonies of the Scriptures which we recalled in the previous book[^7] have taught;

there are nevertheless certain things in the divine utterances so placed that it is ambiguous to which rule they are rather to be referred: whether to that by which we understand the Son to be less in the assumed creature, or to that by which we understand that the Son is not indeed less, but equal to the Father, yet that he is from him, God of God, light of light. For we say that the Son is God of God; but the Father, God only; not, "of God." Whence it is manifest that the Son has another from whom he is, and to whom he is Son; but the Father does not have a Son from whom he is, but only one to whom he is Father. For every son is from a father what he is, and is son to a father; but no father is from a son what he is, but is father to a son.

### The expressions are of a threefold kind.

**1. 3**. Some things, therefore, are so placed in the Scriptures concerning the Father and the Son as to indicate the unity and equality of substance, as it is, *I and the Father are one*; and, *though he was in the form of God, he did not count it robbery to be equal to God*; and whatever things are such.

But some are so placed as to show the Son to be less on account of the *form of a servant*, that is, on account of the assumed creature of changeable and human substance, as is that which he says, *for the Father is greater than I*, and, *the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*. For a little later he consequently says, *and he gave him power also to execute judgment, because he is the Son of man*.

But some, further, are so placed that the Son is then shown neither less nor *equal*, but only that he is *from the Father* is intimated, as is that, *as the Father has life in himself, so he gave to the Son to have life in himself*; and that, *for the Son can do nothing of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*. But if we take this to have been said because the Son is less in the form assumed from the creature, the consequence will be that the Father first walked upon the waters, or opened the eyes of some other man blind from birth with spittle and clay, and the rest that the Son, appearing in the flesh among men, did,[^8] so that he could do these things who said that the Son *could not do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*:[^9] but who, even though delirious, would think so?

It remains, therefore, that these things were said for this reason: that the life of the Son is unchangeable, like the Father's, and yet is from the Father; and the working of the Father and of the Son is inseparable, but yet that the Son so works comes from him from whom he himself is, that is, from the Father; and the Son so sees the Father that, by the very fact by which he sees him, he is the Son.[^10]

For to be from the Father, that is, to be born of the Father, is for him no other thing than to see the *Father*; nor is it another thing to see him *working* than to work along with him; but therefore *not of himself*, because he is not from himself. And therefore the things that he *sees the Father doing, these same things the Son also does likewise*, because he is from the Father.

For neither does he do other things *likewise*, as a painter paints other panels in the way he saw others painted by another; nor the same things unlike, as the body forms the same letters that the mind thought; but *whatever*, he says, *the Father does, these same things the Son also does likewise*. And he said both, *these same things*, and, *likewise*; and on this account the working is inseparable and equal for the Father and the Son, but it is from the Father for the Son. Therefore *the Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing*.

From this rule, therefore, by which the Scriptures so speak as to wish to show, not the one less than the other, but only[^11] who is from whom, some have conceived the sense as though the Son were said to be less.

But certain of our more unlearned brethren, and those least trained in these matters, while they try to take these things according to the *form of a servant*,[^12] and the right understanding does not follow them, are disturbed.

Lest this happen, this rule must be held, by which the Son is not less, but it is intimated that he is from the Father: by which words not his inequality, but his birth, is shown.[^13]

### Certain things concerning the Son are so placed in the Scriptures that it is ambiguous to what they are to be referred.

**2. 4**. There are, then, certain things in the Holy Books, as I had begun to say, so placed that it is ambiguous to what they are to be referred: whether to that by which the Son is less on account of the assumed creature; or to that by which, though he is equal, yet it is indicated that he is from the Father. And it indeed seems to me that, if it is ambiguous in such a way that it cannot be explained and distinguished, it can be understood by either rule without danger, as is that which he says, *my teaching is not mine, but his who sent me*. For it can be taken from the *form of a servant*, as we have already treated in the previous book; and from the *form of God*, in which he is so equal to the Father that yet he is from the Father.[^14] For *in the form of God*, just as the Son is not one thing and his life another, but the Son is that very life; so the Son is not one thing and his teaching another, but the Son is that very teaching.[^15] And on this account, as that which was said, *he gave to the Son life*, is understood as no other than, "he begot the Son who is life"; so too when it is said, "*he gave to the Son* teaching," it is rightly understood, "he begot the Son who is teaching"; so that what was said, *my teaching is not mine, but his who sent me*, may be understood as if it were said, "I am not from myself, but from him who sent me."

### The Spirit is said not to speak of himself, because he proceeds from the Father.

**3. 5**. For of the Holy Spirit too, of whom it was not said, *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*, the Lord himself nevertheless says: *but when that Spirit of truth comes, he will teach you all truth. For he will not speak of himself, but whatever he hears he will speak, and the things that are to come he will announce to you. He will glorify me; for he will receive of mine, and will announce it to you*. After these words, unless he had immediately gone on to say, *all things whatever the Father has are mine; therefore I said that he will receive of mine, and will announce it to you*, it would perhaps be believed that the Holy Spirit was thus *born* of Christ, just as the Son *of the Father*.

For of himself he had said, *my teaching is not mine, but his who sent me*; but of the Holy Spirit, *for he will not speak of himself, but whatever he hears he will speak*; and, *for he will receive of mine, and will announce it to you*. But because he gave the reason why he said, *he will receive of mine*—for he says, *all things whatever the Father has are mine; therefore I said that he will receive of mine*—it remains that the Holy Spirit too is understood to have from the Father, just as the Son also.

How, except according to that which we said above:[^16] *but when the Paraclete comes, whom I will send to you from the Father, the Spirit of truth who proceeds from the Father, he will bear witness of me*? By proceeding, therefore, *from the Father*, he is said *not* to speak *of himself*; and just as it does not follow from this that the Son is less because he said, *the Son cannot do anything of himself, except what he sees the Father doing* (for he did not say this *from the form of a servant*, but *from the form of God*, as we have already shown; and these words do not indicate that he is less, but that he is from the Father); so it is not thereby brought about that the Holy Spirit is less because it was said of him, *for he will not speak of himself, but whatever he hears, he will speak*; for this was said according to that by which he proceeds from the Father.

But since both the Son is from the Father, and the Holy Spirit proceeds from the Father, why they are not both called Sons, nor both begotten, but that one is the only-begotten Son, while this one, the Holy Spirit, is neither Son nor begotten—because if begotten, then certainly Son—we shall discuss in another place, if God grants it, and as far as he grants.

### The Son, glorified by the Father, is not less than the Father.

**4. 6**. But here let those wake up, if they can, who thought even this favored them, as though to demonstrate that the Father is greater than the Son, because the Son said, *Father, glorify me*. Behold, the Holy Spirit too glorifies him; is he then also greater than he? But further, if the Holy Spirit glorifies the Son for this reason, that he will receive of the Son's, and therefore receives of his because *all that the Father has* are his; it is manifest that when the Holy Spirit glorifies the Son, the Father glorifies the Son.

Whence it is recognized that *all that the Father has* are not only the Son's, but also the Holy Spirit's, because the Holy Spirit is able to glorify the Son, whom the Father glorifies. But if the one who glorifies is greater than the one whom he glorifies, let them allow those to be equal who glorify one another. But it is written that the Son too glorifies the Father: *I have glorified you*, he says, *upon the earth*. Of course, let them beware lest the Holy Spirit be thought greater than both, because he glorifies the Son whom the Father glorifies,[^17] while it is written that he himself is glorified neither by the Father nor by the Son.[^18]

### The Son and the Spirit are not less because they are sent.

**5. 7**. But, convicted in these things, they turn to this, to say, "He who sends is greater than he who is sent"; accordingly the Father is greater than the Son, because the Son continually recalls that he was sent by the Father; he is greater than the Holy Spirit too, because Jesus said of him, *whom the Father will send in my name*. And the Holy Spirit is less than both; because both the Father sends him, as we have recalled, and the Son, when he says, *but if I go away, I will send him to you*.

In which question I first ask, from where and to where the Son was sent. *I*, he says, *came forth from the Father, and have come into this world*. To come forth *from the Father*, then, *and* to come *into this world*, this is to be sent. What then is it that the same Evangelist says of him, *he was in this world, and the world was made through him, and the world did not know him?* Then he adds, *he came into his own*. He was assuredly sent there, to where he *came*: but if he was sent *into this world*, because he came forth *from the Father*, and came *into this world*, and *was in this world*; he was therefore sent there where he *was*.

For that too which is written in the prophet, that God says, *I fill heaven and earth*; if it was said of the Son (for some[^19] want him to be understood as having spoken either to the Prophets or in the Prophets), to where was he sent, except there where he *was*? For he was everywhere who says, *I fill heaven and earth*.

But if it was said of the Father, where could he be without his Word and without his Wisdom, which *reaches mightily from end to end, and orders all things sweetly*? But neither could he be anywhere without his Spirit. And so if God is everywhere, his Spirit too is everywhere. Therefore the Holy Spirit too was sent there where he was.

For he too who finds no place to go from the face of God, and says, *if I ascend into heaven, you are there; if I descend into hell, you are present*, wishing God to be understood as present everywhere, first named his Spirit. For he says thus: *where shall I go from your Spirit? And where shall I flee from your face*?

### The Son, in that he was born of Mary, came sent into this world.

**5. 8**. Wherefore, if both the Son and the Holy Spirit are sent there where they were, we must inquire how this sending is to be understood, whether of the Son or of the Holy Spirit. For the Father alone is nowhere read to have been sent. And of the Son the Apostle writes thus: *but when the fullness of time came, God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the Law, that he might redeem those who were under the Law*. *He sent*, he says, *his Son made of a woman*. By which name what Catholic does not know that he wished to signify, not the privation of virginity, but the difference of sex, in the Hebrew manner of speaking?[^20] Since, then, he says, *God sent his Son made of a woman*, he sufficiently shows that the *Son* was sent by that very thing by which he was made *of a woman*. *In that*, therefore, *he was born of God, he was in this world*; but *in that he was born of Mary, he came sent into this world*.[^21]

Accordingly, he could not be sent by the Father without the Holy Spirit; not only because the Father is understood, when he sent him—that is, made him of a woman—certainly not to have done so without his Spirit; but also because it is said most manifestly and most plainly in the Gospel to the Virgin Mary, when she asked of the angel, *how shall this be? The Holy Spirit will come upon you, and the power of the Most High will overshadow you*; and Matthew says, *she was found to be with child of the Holy Spirit*. Although in the prophet Isaiah too, Christ himself is understood to speak of his future coming, *and now the Lord has sent me, and his Spirit*.

### The Son was sent even by himself.

**5. 9**. Perhaps someone will press us to say that the Son was sent even by himself; because that conception and bringing forth by Mary is the working of the Trinity, by whose creating all things are created. "And how, then," he says, "did the Father send him, if he sent himself?" To whom I first answer, asking him to say, if he can, how the *Father sanctified* him, if he sanctified himself? For the same Lord says both: *whom the Father*, he says, *sanctified and sent into this world, you say, "he blasphemes"*,[^22] *because I said, I am the Son of God*. But in another place he says: *and for them I sanctify myself*.

Likewise I ask how the Father delivered him up, if he delivered himself up? For the apostle Paul says both: *who did not spare his own Son*, he says, *but delivered him up for us all*. But elsewhere he says of the Savior himself: *who loved me, and delivered himself up for me*.

I believe he will answer, if he is rightly wise, that the will of the Father and of the Son is one, and the working inseparable. So, then, let him understand that incarnation and birth from the Virgin, in which the Son is understood to have been sent, to have been done inseparably by one and the same working of the Father and of the Son, certainly not with the Holy Spirit separated from it, of whom it is plainly said, *she was found to be with child of the Holy Spirit*.

For even if we inquire thus, what we say will perhaps appear more clearly: how did *God send* his *Son*? Did he command him to come, and he, obeying the one commanding, came; or did he ask him, or did he merely admonish him?

But whichever of these it be, it was certainly done by a word, and the *Word of God* is the *Son of God* himself. Wherefore, when the Father *sent* him by a word, it was done by the Father and his Word that he should be sent. Therefore the same Son was sent by the Father and the Son, because the Son himself is the Word of the Father.

For who would clothe himself in so sacrilegious an opinion as to think that a temporal word was made by the Father, so that the eternal Son might be sent and appear *in the flesh* in time? But certainly in the Word of God itself, which *was in the beginning with God, and was God*,[^23] that is, in the very *Wisdom of God*, the time at which it behooved her to appear in the flesh[^24] was without time. And so, since without any beginning of time the *Word was in the beginning, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*; without any time, the time at which the *Word* should become *flesh, and* dwell *among us* was in the Word itself.

When this *fullness of time had come, God sent his Son, made of a woman*, that is, made in time, so that the incarnate Word might appear to men; the time at which it should be done was, in the Word itself, without time. For the order of times is, *in* the eternal *Wisdom of God*,[^25] without time.

Since, then, this was done by the Father and the Son, that the Son should appear in the flesh, he who appeared in that flesh is fittingly said to have been sent; but he who did not appear in it, to have sent. For the things that are done outwardly before bodily eyes arise from the inner apparatus of the spiritual nature, and on this account are fittingly said to be "sent." Moreover, that form of the man assumed is the person of the Son, not also of the Father.

Wherefore the invisible Father, together with the Son invisible along with him, by making the same Son visible, is said to have sent him;[^26] who, if he were made visible in such a way that he ceased to be invisible with the Father—that is, if the invisible substance of the Word were changed and, passing over, turned into a visible creature[^27]—the Son would be understood to have been sent by the Father in such a way that he would be found only sent, and not also sending together with the Father.

But since the *form of a servant* was so taken that the *form of God* remained unchangeable, it is manifest that what appeared in the Son was done by the Father and the Son not appearing—that is, that by the invisible Father, with the invisible Son, the same Son was sent visible. Why, then, does he say, *and I did not come of myself*? This was now said according to the *form of a servant*, according to which it was said, *I judge no one*.

### The sending of the Holy Spirit is an operation visibly expressed.

**5. 10**. If, then, he is said to be sent insofar as he appeared outwardly in a corporeal creature, who inwardly in his spiritual nature is always hidden from mortal eyes, it is now easy to understand of the Holy Spirit also why he too is said to be sent. For a certain appearance of a creature was made in time, in which the Holy Spirit might be visibly shown, whether when he *descended in a corporeal appearance like a dove* upon the Lord himself, or *when, ten days having passed after his ascension, on the day of Pentecost there suddenly came a sound from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind, and there appeared to them divided tongues as of fire, which also settled upon each one of them*. This operation, visibly expressed and offered to mortal eyes, was called the sending of the Holy Spirit; not that his very substance should appear, by which he too is invisible and unchangeable, like the Father and the Son; but that, the hearts of men being moved by outward sights, they might be turned from the temporal manifestation of him who comes to the hidden eternity of him who is always present.

### The creature in which the Holy Spirit appeared was not assumed as the form of a servant was by the Son. The Word in a man is one thing, the Word a man another.

**6. 11**. But for this reason it is nowhere written that God the Father is greater than the Holy Spirit, or that the Holy Spirit is less than God the Father: because the creature in which the Holy Spirit appeared was not so assumed as the Son of man was assumed, in which form the person of the Word of God itself was presented; not that he should have the Word of God, like other holy wise men, but *above his fellows*;[^28] not, indeed, because he had more of the Word, so that he was wiser than the rest by a more excellent wisdom, but because he was the Word itself.

For the Word in the flesh is one thing, the *Word flesh* another; that is, the Word in a man is one thing, the Word a man another. For *flesh* was put for "man" in that which he says, *the Word was made flesh*; just as also that, *and all flesh shall see*[^29] *the salvation of God*. For not without soul or without mind; but *all flesh* thus, as if it were said, "all men."

The creature in which the Holy Spirit appeared, therefore, was not so assumed as that flesh and that human form was assumed from the *Virgin Mary*. For the Spirit did not beatify the dove, or that wind, or that fire, and join it to himself and to his person in unity and condition forever; nor indeed is the nature of the Holy Spirit changeable and convertible, so that these things were not made out of a creature, but he himself was changeably turned into this and that, like water into ice. But these things appeared, as they ought fittingly to appear, *the creature serving the Creator*, and, *at his nod*[^30]—of him who unchangeably remains in himself—*changed and converted* to signify and demonstrate him, as it behooved him to be signified and demonstrated to mortals.[^31]

Accordingly, although that dove was called the Spirit, and although, when it was said of that fire, *there appeared to them*, he says, *divided tongues as of fire, which also settled upon each one of them, and they began to speak in tongues, as the Spirit gave them to utter*, so that he might show the Spirit demonstrated through that fire, as through the dove; yet we cannot in this way say that the Holy Spirit is both God and a dove, or both God and fire, as we say that the Son is both God and man; nor as we say that the Son is the Lamb of God, not only with John the Baptist saying, *Behold the Lamb of God*, but also with John the Evangelist seeing the *Lamb slain* in the Apocalypse.[^32]

For that prophetic vision was not presented to bodily eyes through bodily forms, but *in the spirit* through spiritual images of bodies. But that dove and fire they saw with their eyes,[^33] whoever saw them. Although concerning the fire it can be disputed whether it was seen with the eyes or in the spirit, on account of the words being placed thus. For he does not say, "they saw divided tongues as of fire"; but, *there appeared to them*. Now we are not accustomed to say, *it appeared to me*, under the same meaning as we say, *I saw*. And in those spiritual sights of bodily images it is customary to say both, "*it appeared* to me," and, *I saw*: but in those that are demonstrated to the eyes through an expressed bodily appearance, it is not customary to say, "*it appeared* to me," but, *I saw*. Of that fire, therefore, there can be a question how it was seen; whether inwardly *in the spirit* as if outwardly, or truly outwardly before the eyes of the flesh. But of that dove which was said to have descended *in a corporeal appearance*, no one ever doubted that it was seen with the eyes.

Nor, as we say that the Son is a rock (for it is written, *and the rock was Christ*), can we in that way say that the Spirit is a dove or fire. For that rock already existed in creation, and by the mode of action was named with the name of Christ, whom it signified; just as that stone which Jacob set at his head, and even with an anointing, he took up to signify the Lord;[^34] just as Isaac was Christ, when he carried the wood for his own immolation.[^35] To these, already existing, a certain significative action was added; but they did not, like that dove and fire, suddenly come into being only to signify these things. These things seem to me rather like that flame which *appeared in the bush* to Moses,[^36] and that pillar which the people followed in the desert,[^37] and the lightnings and thunders that occurred when the Law was given on the mountain.[^38] For the bodily appearance of those things came into being for this, that it might signify something and pass away.

### The Father is not said to be sent.

**7. 12**. On account, therefore, of these bodily forms, which came into being temporally to signify him and, as it behooved, to demonstrate him to human senses, the Holy Spirit too is said to be sent; yet he was not said to be less than the Father, as the Son was on account of the *form of a servant*; because that *form of a servant* clung to the unity of the person, but those bodily appearances appeared for a time to demonstrate what was needful, and afterward ceased to be.

Why, then, is the Father too not said to be sent through those bodily appearances—the fire of the bush,[^39] and the pillar of cloud[^40] or of fire, and the lightnings on the mountain,[^41] and any such things that appeared then, since we have learned, with Scripture as witness, that he spoke to the Fathers face to face—if he himself was demonstrated through those modes and forms of creation, bodily expressed and presented to human sight?

But if the Son was demonstrated through them, why is he said to be sent so long afterward, when he was made of a woman, as the Apostle says, *but when the fullness of time came, God sent his Son made of a woman*; since he was sent even before, when he appeared to the Fathers through those changeable forms of creation?

Or if he could not rightly be said to be sent except when *the Word was made flesh*; why is the Holy Spirit said to be sent, of whom no such incorporation was made?

But if through those visible things that are commended in the Law and the Prophets, neither the Father nor the Son, but the Holy Spirit was shown; why is even he now said to be sent, since he was sent by those modes even before?

### Three things inquired into.

**7. 13**. In the perplexity of this question, with the Lord's help, it must first be inquired whether it was the Father, or the Son, or the Holy Spirit; or sometimes the Father, sometimes the Son, sometimes the Holy Spirit; or without any distinction of persons, as *the one and only God* is spoken of,[^42] that is, the Trinity itself, that appeared to the Fathers through those forms of creation. Then, whichever of these is found or seen, whether the creature was formed for this work alone, in which God, as he then judged it behooved, might be shown to human sight; or whether the angels, who already existed, were so sent that they spoke in the person of God, assuming a bodily *appearance* from corporeal creation, for the use of their ministry, as each was needful; or whether they changed and turned their own body, to which they are not subjected but which they rule as subjected, into the appearances they wished, accommodated and fitted to their actions,[^43] according to the power attributed to them by the Creator. Finally, we shall see that which we had set out to inquire: whether the Son and the Holy Spirit were sent even before; and, if they were sent, what difference there is between that sending and the one we read of in the Gospel;[^44] or whether none of them was sent except when either the Son was made of the Virgin Mary, or the Holy Spirit appeared in a visible form, whether in a dove or in tongues of fire.[^45]

### The first inquiry: whether one person or the whole invisible Trinity appeared in the Old Testament.

**8. 14**. Let us, then, pass over *those who*, too carnally, thought the nature of the Word of God and the Wisdom, which *remaining in herself renews all things*, whom we call the *only Son of God*, to be not only *changeable* but even visible. For these brought a very *gross heart*[^46] to inquiring into divine things, more boldly than reverently. For the soul, since it is a spiritual substance, and since it too was made—nor could it be made by another except *by him through whom all things were made, and without whom nothing was made*—although it is changeable, is nevertheless not visible; which they believed of the Word itself and the very *Wisdom of God*, through whom the soul was made; whereas she is not only invisible, which the soul also is, but also unchangeable, which the soul is not. For this very unchangeableness of hers was recalled in saying, *remaining in herself she renews all things*.

And these, indeed, trying as it were to prop up the ruin of their error with testimonies of the divine Scriptures, bring forward a sentence of the apostle Paul; and what was said of the one and only God, in whom the Trinity itself is understood, they take as said only of the Father, and not also of the Son and of the Holy Spirit: *now to the King of the ages, immortal, invisible, the only God, be honor and glory forever and ever*; and that other passage, *the blessed and only Sovereign, the King of kings, and Lord of lords; who alone has immortality, and dwells in unapproachable light; whom no man has seen, nor can see*. How these are to be understood, I think we have already sufficiently discussed.[^47]

### Against those who believed that the Father alone is immortal and invisible.

**9. 15**. But those who want these things to be taken not of the Son nor of the Holy Spirit, but only of the Father, say that the Son is visible, not *through the flesh assumed from the Virgin*, but even before, through himself. "For he himself," they say, "appeared to the eyes of the Fathers." To whom if you say, "how, then, is the Son visible through himself, so too mortal through himself, so that it may stand for you what you want to be understood only of the Father, that which was said, *who alone has immortality*?

For if the Son is mortal on account of the flesh assumed, allow that on account of this he is also visible": they answer that they do not call the Son mortal on account of this; but, just as he was visible before, so also mortal before.

For if they say that the Son is mortal on account of the flesh, then the Father without the Son no longer *alone has immortality*; because his *Word* too, *through whom all things were made, has immortality*. For he did not, because he assumed mortal flesh, therefore lose his immortality; since not even to the human soul could it happen that it should die with the body, the Lord himself saying, *do not fear those who kill the body, but cannot kill the soul*.

Or did the Holy Spirit too assume flesh? Concerning whom, certainly, they will without doubt be troubled, if the Son is mortal on account of the flesh, as to how they may take it that the Father alone, without the Son and without the Holy Spirit, has immortality; since the Holy Spirit did not assume flesh; who, if he does not *have immortality*, then the Son is not mortal on account of the flesh; but if the Holy Spirit *has immortality*, then it was not said of the Father alone, *that*[^48] *he alone has immortality*.

Wherefore they think they can prove the Son to be mortal through himself even before the *incarnation*, because changeableness itself is not inappropriately called mortality, according to which the soul too is said to die; not because it is changed and turned into a body or into some other substance,[^49] but in its very own substance whatever now is in another mode than it was, according to that by which it ceased to be what it was, is found to be mortal.

"Since, then," they say, "before the *Son of God was born of the Virgin Mary*, he himself appeared to our Fathers, not in one and the same appearance, but in manifold forms, in one way and another, he is both visible through himself, because, the flesh not yet assumed, his substance was conspicuous to mortal eyes; and mortal, insofar as he is changeable. So too the Holy Spirit, who appeared at one time as a dove, at another as fire."[^50] "Whence," they say, "it suits not the Trinity, but singularly and properly the Father alone, that which was said, *to the immortal, invisible, the only God*; and, *who alone has immortality, and dwells in unapproachable light; whom no man has seen, nor can see*."

### The truth is to be sought with a peaceful zeal.

**9. 16**. Passing over, then, those who could not even know the invisible substance of the soul—whence it was far removed from them to know that the substance of the one and only God, that is, of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit, remains not only invisible but also unchangeable, and on this account consists in true and sincere immortality;

let us, who say that God—neither the Father, nor the Son, nor the Holy Spirit—has never appeared to bodily eyes except through a corporeal creature subjected to his power, in Catholic peace and with peaceful zeal seek out, prepared to be corrected if we are reproved in a brotherly and right way; prepared even if we are bitten by an enemy who nevertheless speaks the truth;

whether God appeared indiscriminately to our Fathers before Christ came in the flesh, or some one person of the Trinity, or each singly as it were by turns.

### Whether some one person of the Trinity spoke with Adam, or the Trinity itself indiscriminately?

**10. 17**. And first, in that which is written in Genesis, that God spoke with the man whom he had fashioned from the clay,[^51] if, apart from the figurative signification—so that the truth of the deed may be held even to the letter—we treat these things, God seems then to have spoken with the man in the appearance of a man. Not indeed is this expressly placed in the book, but the circumstance of the reading echoes it, especially in that it is written that Adam heard the *voice of God walking in paradise toward evening*, and hid *himself in the midst of the tree which was in paradise*, and to God saying, *Adam, where are you?* answered, *I heard your voice, and I hid myself from your face, because I am naked*. For how such a walking and conversing of God could be understood to the letter, except in human appearance, I do not see. For it cannot be said that only a voice was made where God was said to have walked, or that he who was walking was not visible in the place, since Adam too says that he hid himself from the face of God. Who, then, was he? Whether the Father, or the Son, or the Holy Spirit? Or was God altogether, the Trinity itself indiscriminately, speaking to the man in the form of a man?

The very context of Scripture is nowhere felt to pass from person to person; but he seems to speak to the first man who said, *let there be light*, and, *let there be a firmament*, and the rest through those several days; whom we are accustomed to take as *God the Father*, saying that *whatever he wished to make should be made*. For he made all things through his Word, which Word we know, according to the right rule of faith, to be his *only Son*.

If, then, God the Father spoke to the first man, and he himself walked *in paradise toward evening*, and the sinner had hidden himself *from his face in the midst of the tree of paradise*; why should he not now be understood to have appeared to Abraham and to Moses, and to whom he would, as he would, through a changeable and visible creature subjected to himself, while he himself remains in himself and in his substance, by which he is unchangeable and invisible?

But it could be that Scripture passed secretly from person to person, and when it had narrated that the Father said, *let there be light*, and the rest that he is recalled to have made through the Word, it now indicated that the Son spoke to the first man—not explaining this openly, but intimating it to be understood by those who could.

### The matter is hidden.

**10. 18**. Whoever, then, has the strength by which he can penetrate this secret with the edge of his mind, so that it clearly appears to him either that the Father too can, or that only the Son and the Holy Spirit can, appear to human eyes through a visible creature, let him go on to scrutinize these things, if he can, and even to express them in words and treat of them; the matter, however, so far as concerns this testimony of Scripture, where God spoke with man, is, as I judge, hidden; because even whether Adam was accustomed to see God with bodily eyes does not evidently appear; especially since it is a great question of what kind the eyes were that were opened to them when they tasted the forbidden food;[^52] for these, before they tasted, were closed.

This much only I would not rashly say: if that Scripture intimates paradise to be a certain bodily place, that God could in no way walk there except in some bodily form. For it can be said that only voices were made which the man might hear, while he saw no form; nor, because it is written, *Adam hid himself from the face of God*, does it immediately follow that he was accustomed to see his face. For what if he himself indeed could not see, but feared to be seen by him whose voice he had heard, and whose presence as he walked he had felt? For Cain too said to God, *I will hide myself from your face*; nor are we therefore compelled to confess that he was accustomed to discern the face of God with bodily eyes, in any visible form, although he had heard the voice of one questioning him about his crime and speaking with him.[^53]

But of what kind of speech God then sounded to the outer ears of men, especially speaking to the first man, is both difficult to find out and not what we have undertaken in this discourse. Nevertheless, if only voices and sounds were made, by which a certain sensible presence of God was offered to those first men, why I should not understand the person of God the Father there, I do not know; since his person is shown both in that voice, when *Jesus shining before the three disciples on the mountain*[^54] appeared; and in that, when *upon the baptized one a dove* descended;[^55] and in that where he cried to the Father about his glorification, and it was answered him, *and I have glorified it, and will glorify it again*.[^56] Not that the voice could be made without the work of the Son and of the Holy Spirit (for the Trinity works inseparably); but because that voice was made which would show the person of the Father alone; just as the Trinity wrought that human form from the Virgin Mary, but it is the person of the Son alone; for the invisible Trinity wrought the visible person of the Son alone.

Nor does anything forbid us to understand those voices made to Adam as not only made by the Trinity, but also to take them as demonstrating the person of the same Trinity. For there we are compelled to take it as the Father's alone, where it was said, *this is my beloved Son*.[^57] For Jesus can be neither believed nor understood to be also the son of the Holy Spirit, or even his own Son.

And where it sounded, *and I have glorified it, and will glorify it again*, we confess the person of the Father alone. For it is a response to that voice of the Lord by which he had said, *Father, glorify your Son*, which he could not say except to God the Father only, not also to the Holy Spirit, whose son he is not.[^58] But here, where it is written, *and the Lord God said to Adam*, why the Trinity itself should not be understood, nothing can be said.

### The vision of Abraham.

**10. 19**. Likewise also that which is written, *and the Lord said to Abraham: "go out from your land, and from your kindred, and from your father's house"*, it is not open whether only a voice was made to the ears of Abraham, or whether something also appeared to his eyes. But a little later it is said somewhat more openly, *and the Lord was seen by Abraham, and said to him: "to your seed I will give this land"*. But neither is it there expressed in what appearance the *Lord was seen by him*; or whether the Father, or the Son, or the Holy Spirit *was seen by him*.

Unless perhaps they think the Son was seen by Abraham for this reason, that it is not written, "*God was seen by him*"; but, *the Lord was seen by him*. For the Son seems properly to be called Lord, the Apostle saying, *for although there are those who are called gods, whether in heaven or on earth, as indeed there are many gods and many lords; yet for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things, and we in him; and one Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things, and we through him*.

But since God the Father too is found in many places called Lord, as is that, *the Lord said to me: you are my Son, today I have begotten you*; and that, *the Lord said to my Lord: "sit at my right hand"*.[^59]

Since the Holy Spirit too is found called Lord, where the Apostle says, *now the Lord is the Spirit*;[^60] and lest anyone should think the Son signified, and therefore "Spirit" said on account of the incorporeal substance, he went on to add, *but where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is liberty*; and that the Spirit of the Lord is the Holy Spirit, no one would doubt.

Neither here, then, does it evidently appear whether some one person of the Trinity, or God the Trinity himself, of which one God it was said, *you shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve*, *was seen by Abraham*.[^61]

But *under the oak of Mamre* he saw three men, whom, when they had been invited and received in hospitality and were feasting, he served. Yet Scripture thus began to narrate that deed, that it does not say, "three men were seen by him"; but, *the Lord was seen by him*. And from there, consequently explaining how the Lord was seen by him, it wove in the narration about the three men, whom Abraham invites in the plural number, to receive in hospitality; and afterward addresses singularly as one; and as one promises him a son by Sarah, whom Scripture calls Lord, just as in the beginning of the same narration, *the Lord*, he says, *was seen by Abraham*. He invites them, then, and washes their feet, and escorts them as they depart, as though they were men; but he speaks as though with the Lord God, whether when a son is promised him, or when the impending destruction of Sodom is announced to him.

### Since three were seen, why should we not take the equality of the Trinity to be intimated?

**11. 20**. This passage of Scripture demands no small nor passing consideration. For if one man had been seen, would not those who say that the Son was visible through his own substance even before he was born of the Virgin cry out that it was he himself? For "of the Father," they say, "it was said, *to the invisible, the only God*."

And yet I could still ask, how before he assumed flesh was he *found in fashion as a man*; since his feet were washed, and he feasted on human food? How could this be done, *while* he was still *in the form of God, not counting it robbery to be equal to God*? For had he already *emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, made in the likeness of men, and found in fashion as a man*? Since we know that he did this through the Virgin's childbearing? How, then, before he had done this, did he *appear to Abraham* as one man? Or was that form not true?

I could ask these things, if one man had appeared to Abraham, and he were believed to be the Son of God. But since three were seen, and none of them was said to be greater than the rest in form, or age, or power; why should we not here take the equality of the Trinity, visibly intimated through a visible creature, and in three persons one and the same substance?

**11. 21**. For lest anyone should think it thus intimated that one of the three was greater, and that he was to be understood as the Lord, the Son of God, but the other two as his angels—because, although three were seen, Abraham there speaks to one Lord—Holy Scripture, contradicting such future thoughts and opinions, did not fail to meet them, since a little later it says that two angels came to Lot, among whom that just man too, who deserved to be freed from the burning of Sodom, speaks to one Lord.[^62] For Scripture follows thus, saying: *but the Lord went away after he ceased speaking to Abraham, and Abraham returned to his place*.

### The vision of Lot.

**12. 21**. *But the two angels came to Sodom in the evening*. Here must be considered more attentively what I set out to show. Abraham was certainly speaking with three, and called him Lord in the singular. "Perhaps," someone says, "he recognized one of the three as the Lord; but the other two, as his angels." What, then, does it mean that Scripture consequently says, *but the Lord went away after he ceased speaking to Abraham, and Abraham returned to his place; but the two angels came to Sodom in the evening?* Or had perhaps that one departed, who was recognized as the Lord among the three, and sent the two angels who were with him to destroy Sodom? Let us, then, see what follows. *There came*, it says, *two angels to Sodom in the evening. But Lot was sitting at the gate of Sodom. And when Lot had seen them, he rose to meet them, and bowed with his face to the ground, and said: "behold, my lords, turn aside into the house of your servant*." Here it is manifest both that there were two angels, and that they were invited in the plural into hospitality, and honorably addressed as lords, although perhaps they were thought to be men.

**12. 22**. But again it troubles us, that unless they were recognized as angels of God, Lot would not have bowed with his face to the ground. Why, then, are both hospitality and food provided for them, as if they needed such human service?

But whatever lies hidden here, let us now pursue what we have undertaken. *Two* appear, both are called *angels*, they are invited in the plural, he speaks with them in the plural as with two, until they go out of Sodom.

Then Scripture follows and says: *and it came to pass, after they had led them outside, they said: 'saving, save your soul; do not look back, nor stay in all this region; go to the mountain, and there you will be saved, lest perhaps you be caught.' But Lot said to them: 'I beg, Lord, since your servant has found mercy before you.'* What is this, that he said to them, *I beg, Lord*, if he who was the Lord had already departed, and had sent the angels? Why is it said, *I beg, Lord*, and not, "I beg, lords"?

Or if he wished to address one of them, why does Scripture say, *but Lot said to them: "I beg, Lord, since your servant has found mercy before you"*? Or do we here too understand two persons in the plural number, but, when these same two are addressed as one, one Lord God of one substance? But which two persons do we here understand? Of the Father and the Son, or of the Father and the Holy Spirit, or of the Son and the Holy Spirit?

This last, which I said, is perhaps more fitting. For they said that they were sent, which we say of the Son and of the Holy Spirit. For nowhere in the Scriptures do we meet that the Father was sent.

### The vision in the bush.

**13. 23**. But when Moses was sent *to lead the people of Israel out of Egypt*, it is written that the Lord appeared to him thus: *he was pasturing*, it says, *the sheep of Jethro his father-in-law, the priest of Midian, and he drove the sheep into the desert and came to the mountain of God, Horeb. And the angel of the Lord appeared to him in a flame of fire from the bush. And he saw that the fire was burning in the bush, but the bush was not consumed. And Moses said: "I will go, and I will see this sight, which I have seen so great, that the bush is not consumed." When, then, the Lord saw that he came to see, the Lord called to him from the bush, saying: "I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob."*

And here he was first called *the angel of the Lord*, then *God*. Is the *angel*, then, the *God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*? It can, then, rightly be understood as the Savior himself, of whom the Apostle says: *whose are the Fathers, and of whom is Christ according to the flesh, who is over all, God blessed forever*. *He* therefore *who is over all, God blessed forever*, is not absurdly understood here too to be himself the *God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*.

But why was he first called *the angel of the Lord*, when he appeared from the bush in a flame of fire? Whether because he was one of the many angels, but by dispensation bore the person of his Lord; or whether something of creation was assumed, which might visibly appear for the present business, and from which voices might be sensibly uttered, by which the presence of the Lord, through a subjected creature, might be exhibited even to the bodily senses of man, as it behooved? For if he was one of the angels, who could easily affirm whether the person of the Son was imposed on him to announce, or of the Holy Spirit, or of God the Father, or of the Trinity itself altogether, which is the one and only God, so that he might say, *I am the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob*?

For we cannot say that the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, is the Son of God, and is not the Father. Or will anyone dare to deny that the Holy Spirit, or the Trinity itself, which we believe and understand to be one God, is the God of Abraham, and the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob? For he is not the God of those Fathers who is not God.

Moreover, if not only the Father is God, as all even the heretics concede; but also the Son, which, whether they will or not, they are compelled to confess, the Apostle saying, *who is over all, God blessed forever*; and the Holy Spirit, the same Apostle saying, *glorify God, therefore, in your body*; since above he had said, *do you not know that your bodies are a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God*? And these three are one God, as Catholic soundness believes—it does not sufficiently appear which person in the Trinity, and whether any, or that of the Trinity itself, that angel bore, if he was one of the other angels.

But if for the use of the present matter a creature was assumed, which both appeared to human eyes and sounded to their ears, and was called *the angel of the Lord*, and *Lord*, and *God*; here God cannot be understood as the Father, but either the Son or the Holy Spirit. Although I do not recall the Holy Spirit anywhere called an angel, yet it can be understood from his work; for it was said of him, *the things that are to come he will announce to you*; and certainly "angel" in Greek is interpreted in Latin as "messenger." But of the Lord Jesus Christ we read most evidently in the Prophet, that he was called *the angel of great counsel*; though both the Holy Spirit and the Son of God are God and Lord of the angels.

### The vision in the pillar of cloud and fire.

**14. 24**. Likewise *in the departure* of the children *of Israel from Egypt*[^63] it is written: *but God went before them, by day indeed in a pillar of cloud, and showed them the way; but by night in a pillar of fire; and the pillar of cloud did not fail by day, nor the pillar of fire by night, before the people*. Who would here too doubt that through a subjected and corporeal creature, not through his own substance, God appeared to the eyes of mortals? But whether the Father, or the Son, or the Holy Spirit, or the Trinity itself, the one God, likewise does not appear. Nor is this distinguished there, as I judge, where it is written: *and the majesty of the Lord appeared in the cloud, and the Lord spoke to Moses, saying: 'I have heard the murmuring of the children of Israel.'*

### The vision on Sinai.

**15. 25**. And now of the clouds, and voices, and lightnings, and trumpet, and smoke on Mount Sinai, when it was said: *but Mount Sinai was all smoking, because God had descended upon it in fire, and the smoke went up as the smoke of a furnace; and all the people were exceedingly confounded in mind; and there came forth the sounds of the trumpet, growing very strong. Moses spoke, and God answered him with a voice*.

And a little later, the Law having been given in the ten precepts, it is consequently said: *and all the people saw the voices, and the lamps, and the sounds of the trumpet, and the smoking mountain*.

And a little later: *and all the people*, it says, *stood afar off; but Moses entered into the mist where God was, and the Lord said to Moses*.

What shall I say of this, except that no one is so senseless as to believe that smoke, fire, clouds, and mist, and any such things, are the substance of the Word and Wisdom of God, which is Christ, or of the Holy Spirit? For of God the Father, not even the Arians ever dared to say this.

Therefore, *the creature serving the Creator*,[^64] all those things were made, and presented to human senses by a fitting dispensation. Unless perhaps, because it was said, *but Moses entered into the mist where God was*, carnal thought will reckon this: that the mist indeed was seen by the people, but that within the mist Moses saw with carnal eyes the Son of God, whom the delirious heretics want to have been seen in his own substance.

Let Moses indeed have seen him with carnal eyes—if with carnal eyes there can be seen, not now the Wisdom of God which is Christ, but even the wisdom of any man whatever and of whatever kind he be; or because it is written *of the elders of Israel*, that *they saw the place where the God of Israel had stood*, and that *under his feet was as it were a work of sapphire stone, and as the appearance of the firmament of heaven*, must we therefore believe that the Word and Wisdom of God stood through his own substance in the space of an earthly place—she who *reaches mightily from end to end, and orders all things sweetly*; and that the *Word of God, through whom all things were made*, is so changeable that now it contracts itself, now stretches out? May the Lord *cleanse the hearts of his faithful* from such thoughts.

But through a subjected creature, as we have often said, all these visible and sensible things are exhibited, to signify the *invisible* and intelligible *God*, not only the Father, but also the Son and the Holy Spirit, *from whom are all things, through whom are all things, in whom are all things*;[^65] although *the invisible things of God, from the creation of the world, are clearly seen, being understood through the things that are made, his eternal power also and divinity*.

### If here one person of the Trinity can be understood, why do we not rather understand the Holy Spirit?

**15. 26**. But as concerns what we have now undertaken, neither on Mount Sinai do I see how it appears, through all those things that were terribly shown to the senses of mortals, whether God the Trinity, or the Father, or the Son, or the Holy Spirit was speaking properly. Nevertheless, if it is permitted here, without the rashness of asserting, to conjecture modestly and hesitatingly, if one person of the Trinity can be understood, why do we not rather understand the Holy Spirit, since the Law itself which was given there is said to have been written on the stone tablets *by the finger of God*,[^66] by which name we know the Holy Spirit to be signified in the Gospel.[^67]

And fifty days are counted from the slaying of the lamb and the celebration of the Passover up to the day on which these things began to be done on Mount Sinai;[^68] just as after the Lord's passion, from his resurrection fifty days are counted, and the Holy Spirit, promised by the Son of God, came. And in his very coming, which we read of in the Acts of the Apostles, fire appeared through the division of tongues, which also *settled upon each one of them*. This agrees with Exodus, where it is written, *but Mount Sinai was all smoking, because God descended upon it in fire*; and somewhat later, *the appearance*, it says, *of the majesty of the Lord was as burning fire upon the top of the mountain before the children of Israel*.

Or if these things were done for this reason, that neither the Father nor the Son could be presented there in that mode without the Holy Spirit, by which the Law itself had to be written; we know indeed that God appeared there, not through his own substance, which remains invisible and unchangeable, but through that appearance of a creature; but some one person of the Trinity, by a certain proper sign, so far as pertains to the capacity of my understanding, we do not see.

### Moses did not see God himself.

**16. 27**. There is also that by which very many are wont to be moved, because it is written, *and the Lord spoke to Moses face to face, as one speaks to his friend*, when a little later the same Moses says, *if, then, I have found grace before you, show me yourself manifestly, that I may see you; that I may be one finding grace before you, and that I may know that this nation is your people*; and a little later again *Moses said to the Lord, "show me your majesty."*

What is this, that in all the things that were done above, God was thought to be seen through his own substance—whence by wretched men the Son of God was believed visible, not through a creature, but through himself; and that, since *Moses had entered into the mist*, he was thought to have entered for this, that to the eyes of the people indeed a misty darkness might be shown, but that he within might hear the words of God as though contemplating his face; and how it was said, *the Lord spoke to Moses face to face, as one speaks to his friend*; and behold the same Moses says, *if I have found grace in your sight*,[^69] *show me yourself manifestly*?

He certainly knew what he saw bodily, and was seeking the true vision of God spiritually. For that speech which was made in voices was so modified as though it were that of a friend speaking to a friend. But who sees God the Father with bodily eyes? And that which *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God, through whom all things were made*, who sees with bodily eyes? And the Spirit of Wisdom, who sees with bodily eyes?

But what is, *show me yourself manifestly, that I may see you*; except, "show me your substance"? But if Moses had not said this, the fools who think that through the things said or done above the substance of God was conspicuous to his eyes would somehow have to be borne; but since here it is most plainly demonstrated that this was not granted even to one desiring it,[^70] who would dare to say that through similar forms, which had appeared visibly to this man too, not a creature serving God, but that very thing which God is, appeared to the eyes of any mortal?

**16. 28**. And that, indeed, which the Lord afterward says to Moses, *"you cannot see my face, and live; for no man will see my face and live." And the Lord said: "behold, there is a place near me, and you will stand upon the rock, and as soon as my majesty passes by, I will set you in a cleft of the rock; and I will cover you with my hand over you, until I pass, and I will take away my hand, and then you will see my back parts; for my face will not appear to you."*

### The back parts of God: the flesh of Christ.

**17. 28**. It is not incongruously wont to be understood as prefigured in the person of our Lord Jesus Christ, that his back parts are taken to be his flesh, in which he was born of the Virgin, and died, and rose again; whether his *back parts*[^71] were so called on account of the posteriority of mortality, or because he deigned to assume it near the end of the age, that is, later. But his face is that form of God, in which *he did not count it robbery to be equal to God* the Father, which assuredly no one can see and live;[^72]

whether because, after this life, in which *we are away from the Lord*, and where *the body which is corrupted weighs down the soul, we shall see face to face*, as the Apostle says. For of this life it is said in the Psalms, *truly every living man is altogether vanity*; and again, *for no living man will be justified before you*. In which life also, according to John, *it has not yet appeared what we shall be. But we know*, he says, *that when he appears, we shall be like him; for we shall see him as he is*; which he certainly wished to be understood after this life, when we have paid the debt of death, and have received the promise of resurrection.

Or because even now, insofar as we spiritually understand *the Wisdom of God through whom all things were made*,[^73] to that extent we die to carnal affections, so that, reckoning this world dead to us, we ourselves too die to this world, and say what the Apostle says, *the world is crucified to me, and I to the world*.

For of this death he likewise said, *but if you have died with Christ, why do you still, as though living, make decrees about this world*[^74]? Not undeservedly, then, will no one be able to *see* the *face*, that is, the very manifestation of the Wisdom of God, *and live*.[^75]

For that is the form for the contemplating of which everyone sighs who *strives to love God with his whole heart, and with his whole soul, and with his whole mind*;[^76] for the contemplating of which he also builds up his neighbor as much as he can, who *loves his neighbor too as himself; on which two precepts the whole Law hangs, and the prophets*.

This is signified also in Moses himself. For when he had said, on account of the love of God by which he was especially aflame, *if I have found grace in your sight, show me yourself manifestly, that I may be one finding grace before you*; immediately, on account of the love of his neighbor too, he added and said, *and that I may know that this nation is your people*.

That, then, is the form which seizes every rational soul with desire of itself, the more ardent the purer it is, and the purer the more it rises again to spiritual things; but the more it rises again to spiritual things, the more it dies to carnal things. But *while we are away from the Lord, and walk by faith, not by sight*, we ought to see the back parts of Christ, that is, his flesh, through that very faith—that is, standing on the solid foundation of faith, which the rock signifies; and beholding it from such a most safe lookout, namely in the Catholic Church, of which it was said, *and upon this rock I will build my Church*. For we love the more surely than we desire to see the face of Christ, the more in his back parts[^77] we recognize how much *Christ first loved us*.[^78]

### In that very flesh the faith of his resurrection makes us safe.

**17. 29**. But in that very flesh the faith of his resurrection makes us safe and justifies us. *For if you believe*, he says, *in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved*; and again, *who was delivered up*, he says, *for our offenses, and rose again for our justification*. And therefore the merit of our faith is the resurrection of the Lord's body. For that that flesh died on the cross of the passion, even his enemies believe, but that it rose again they do not believe. Which we, believing most firmly, behold as from the solidity of a rock; whence with sure hope *we await the adoption, the redemption of our body*; because we hope this in the members of Christ, which we ourselves are, which we know by the soundness of faith to be perfected in him as in our head. Hence he does not wish his back parts to be seen except when he has passed by, so that his resurrection may be believed. For "Pasch" is said to be a Hebrew word, which is interpreted "passage." Whence John the Evangelist too says, *before the festal day of Pasch, Jesus knowing that his hour had come, that he should pass from this world to the Father*.

### In the Catholic Church alone he sees the back parts of God who believes in the resurrection of Christ.

**17. 30**. But those who believe this, and yet believe not in the Catholic Church, but in some schism or in a heresy, do not see the back parts of the Lord from the place that is near him. For what does the Lord mean when he says, *behold, there is a place near me, and you will stand upon the rock*?

What earthly place is near the Lord, except that that is near him which spiritually touches him? For what place is not near the Lord, who *reaches from end to end mightily, and orders all things sweetly*; and of whom it was said that *heaven is his seat, and earth the footstool* of his feet; and who said, *what house will you build me? Or what place of my rest? Has not my hand made all these things*?

But clearly the place near him in which one stands upon the rock is understood to be the Catholic Church itself, where he healthfully sees the *Pasch* of the Lord, *that is, the passage of the Lord*, and his back parts, that is, his body, who believes in his resurrection.

*And you will stand*, he says, *upon the rock, as soon as my majesty passes by*. For indeed, *as soon as the majesty* of the Lord *passed by*, in the glorification of the Lord by which, rising, he *ascended to the Father*, we were made solid *upon the rock*.

And Peter himself was then made solid, so that he might preach with confidence him whom, before he was made solid, he had three times through fear denied,[^79] being already indeed by predestination set in the cleft of the rock, but still with the Lord's hand placed over him lest he see. For he was to see his back parts, and that one had not yet passed by, namely *from death to life*; he had not yet been glorified by the resurrection.

### Many Israelites believed in the Lord after his resurrection.

**17. 31**. For that too which follows in Exodus, and he says, *I will cover you with my hand over you, until I pass; and I will take away my hand, and then you will see my back parts*; many Israelites, of whom Moses was then the figure, believed in him after the Lord's resurrection, as though now seeing his back parts, his hand removed from their eyes. Whence the evangelist too recalls such a prophecy of Isaiah: *make gross the heart of this people, and stop their ears, and weigh down their eyes*.

Finally, in the Psalm it is not absurdly understood to be said in their person, *for day and night your hand was heavy upon me*. By day perhaps, when he did manifest miracles, and was not recognized by them; but *by night*, when he died in the passion, when they thought more surely that, like any man whatever, he had been slain and extinguished.

But since, when he had passed by so that his back parts might be seen, the apostle Peter preaching to them *that Christ had to suffer and rise again*,[^80] they were pricked with the grief of repentance, so that there might come to pass in the baptized what is said at the head of that Psalm, *blessed are those whose iniquities are forgiven, and whose sins are covered*.

Therefore, when it had been said, *your hand was heavy upon me*, as though the Lord were passing by, that he might now remove his hand, and his back parts might be seen, there follows the voice of one grieving and confessing, and from faith in the Lord's resurrection receiving the forgiveness of sins: *I was turned*, he says, *in my distress, while the thorn was being fixed in me. I acknowledged my sin, and I did not cover my injustice. I said, "I will declare against myself my injustice to the Lord, and you forgave the impiety of my heart."*

For we ought not[^81] to be wrapped in so great a cloud of the flesh as to think that the face of the Lord is indeed invisible, but his back visible; since in the *form of a servant* both appeared visibly; but in the *form of God*, far be it that any such thing be thought. Far be it that the Word of God and the Wisdom of God should have a face on one side, and a back on the other, like a human body, or be changed at all by any appearance or motion, whether in place or in time.

### It is too rash to say that God the Father never appeared to the Fathers through any visible forms.

**17. 32**. Wherefore, if in those voices which were made in Exodus, and in all those bodily demonstrations, the Lord Jesus Christ was shown; or at one time Christ, as the consideration of this passage persuades; at another the Holy Spirit, as the things we said above admonish: this does not bring it about that God the Father never appeared to the Fathers in any such appearance. For many such sights were made in those times, without the Father, or the Son, or the Holy Spirit being evidently named and designated in them; but yet, with certain very probable significations existing as some indications, so that it is too rash to say that God the Father never appeared to the Fathers or the Prophets through any visible forms.

For this opinion those begot who could not understand, in the unity of the Trinity, what was said, *now to the King of the ages, immortal, invisible, the only God*; and, *whom no man has seen, nor can see*. Which is understood, by sound faith, of that supreme and supremely divine and unchangeable substance, where both the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are the *one* and *only God*. But those visions were made through a changeable creature subjected to the unchangeable God, showing God not properly as he is, but significatively, as it behooved according to the causes of things and the times.

### The vision of Daniel.

**18. 33**. Although I do not know how these people understand that to Daniel appeared the *Ancient of days*, from whom the Son of man—which he deigned to be for our sake—is understood to have received a kingdom: namely, from him who says to him in the Psalms, *you are my Son, today I have begotten you; ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance*; and who *subjected all things under his feet*.

If, then, both the Father giving the kingdom, and the Son receiving it, appeared to Daniel in a bodily appearance; how do these people say that the *Father* was never seen by the Prophets, and that therefore only he is to be understood as invisible *whom no man has seen, nor can see*?

For Daniel narrated thus: *I beheld*, he says, *until thrones were set, and the Ancient of days was seated; and his garment was white as snow, and the hair of his head as clean wool; his throne was a flame of fire, its wheels burning fire, and a river of fire was drawn before his sight. And a thousand thousands served him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood by him. And the judgment was set, and the books were opened*.

And a little later: *I beheld*, he says, *in the vision of the night; and behold, with the clouds of heaven, one was coming as it were the Son of man, and he came even to the Ancient of days, and was presented to him. And to him was given the dominion and honor and kingdom; and all peoples, tribes, tongues will serve him. His power is an eternal power, which will not pass away, and his kingdom will not be corrupted*.

Behold the Father giving, and the Son receiving, an everlasting kingdom, and both are before the prophesying one in a visible appearance. It is not, then, incongruously believed that God the Father too is wont to appear to mortals in that mode.

### An objection.

**18. 34**. Unless perhaps someone will say that the Father is not visible for this reason, that he appeared before the sight of one dreaming; but that the Son and the Holy Spirit are visible, because Moses saw all those things awake. As though Moses saw the Word and the *Wisdom of God* with carnal eyes, or as though the *spirit*, even the *human* one *which* gives life to *this* flesh, can be seen, or even the corporeal one which is called *wind*;[^82] how much less that Spirit of God who surpasses the minds of all men and angels by the ineffable excellence of the divine substance.

Or would anyone be hurled into such an error as to dare to say that the Son and the Holy Spirit are visible even to waking men, but the Father only to those dreaming? How, then, do they take it of the Father alone, *whom no man has seen, nor can see*? Or, when men sleep, are they then not men?

Or he who can form a likeness of a body to signify himself through the visions of those dreaming, can he not form the corporeal creature itself to signify himself to the eyes of the waking? Whereas his very substance, by which he is what he is, can be shown by no likeness of a body to one sleeping, by no "corporeal appearance" to one waking; and that not only of the Father, but also of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.

And certainly those who are moved by the sights of waking men, to believe that not the Father, but only the Son or the Holy Spirit appeared to the bodily sight of men—to pass over the great breadth of the holy pages, and their so manifold understanding, from which no one of sound head ought to affirm that the person of the Father was nowhere demonstrated through any bodily appearance to the eyes of the waking;

but to pass this over, as I said: what do they say of our father Abraham, to whom certainly, while he was awake and ministering, when Scripture had premised, saying, *the Lord was seen by Abraham*, not one or two, but *three men appeared*, of whom none was said to have stood out more loftily than the others, none to have shone more honorably, none to have acted more imperiously?

### The nature of God is invisible, but it must be believed that the three persons could give a signification of themselves in a bodily appearance.

**18. 35**. Wherefore, since in that tripartite distribution of ours we set out first to inquire whether the Father, or the Son, or the Holy Spirit; or sometimes the Father, sometimes the Son, sometimes the Holy Spirit; or without any distinction of persons, as he is called the one and only God—that is, the Trinity itself—appeared to the Fathers through those forms of creation.

Having interrogated the passages of the holy Scriptures that we could, as far as seemed sufficient,[^83] nothing else, as I judge, does the modest and cautious consideration of the divine "mysteries"[^84] persuade, except that we should not rashly say which person of the Trinity appeared to any of the Fathers or Prophets in some body or likeness of a body, unless the connection of the reading sets around it some probable indications.

For the very nature, or substance, or essence, or by whatever other name that very thing which God is, whatever it is, is to be called, cannot be seen bodily; but through a subjected creature, it must be believed that not only the Son, or the Holy Spirit, but also the Father could give a signification of himself to mortal senses in a bodily appearance or likeness.

*Since these things are so*,[^85] lest the length of this second volume proceed too immoderately, let us see the things that remain in what follows.
