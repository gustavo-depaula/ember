# Book I

## Chapter 1

### He writes against those who, abusing reason, corrupt the faith. Three errors concerning God.

**1**. Whoever is going to read what we discuss concerning the Trinity ought first to know that our pen is on guard against the slanders of those who, despising the beginning of faith,[^1] are deceived by an immature and perverse love of reason. Some of them try to transfer what they have observed of bodily things, whether learned through the bodily senses,[^2] or perceived by the nature of human talent and the keenness of diligence or with the help of skill, to incorporeal and spiritual things, wishing to measure and form opinions about the latter from the former. There are others, again, who think about God, if they think anything at all, according to the nature or disposition of the human mind; and from this error, when they argue about God, they fix distorted and deceptive rules upon their discourse.[^3]

There is, again, another kind of men, those who strive indeed to transcend the whole of creation, which is certainly changeable, so as to lift up their attention to the unchangeable substance which is God; but weighed down by the burden of mortality, since they both want to seem to know what they do not know, and cannot know what they want to know,[^4] by too boldly asserting the presumptions of their own opinions, they block for themselves the very ways of understanding, choosing rather not to correct their perverse view than to change the one they have defended.

And this indeed is the disease common to all the three kinds I have set forth:[^5] namely, of those who think of God according to body; of those who do so according to the spiritual creation, such as the soul; and of those who think falsely of God according neither to body nor to the spiritual creation,[^6] the more remote from the truth in that what they think is found neither in body, nor in a made and created spirit, nor in the Creator himself. For whoever supposes God, for example, to be white or ruddy is deceived; yet these are nevertheless found in body. Again, whoever supposes God now to forget, now to remember, or anything of this kind, is no less in error;[^7] yet these are nevertheless found in the mind. But whoever thinks God to be of such power that he begot himself errs all the more, in that not only is God not so, but neither is the spiritual nor the bodily creation: for there is nothing whatever that begets itself so as to exist.[^8]

### Holy Scripture avoided the words of no kind of thing by which our understanding might rise up to divine things.

**2**. Therefore, in order that the human mind might be purified of falsehoods of this kind, Holy Scripture, adapting itself to little ones, avoided the words of no kind of thing by which, as if step by step, our understanding might rise up, as though nourished, to divine and sublime things. For it used words taken from bodily things when it spoke of God; as when it says, *under the shadow*[^9] *of your wings protect me*[^10]. And from the spiritual creation it transferred many things, by which it might signify what was not so, but had to be spoken of as so; as it is, *I am a jealous God*[^11]; and, *it repents me that I have made man*[^12].

But from things that altogether do not exist it drew no terms by which either to figure its expressions or to compact[^13] its riddles. Hence the more perniciously and the more emptily do those vanish away who are cut off from the truth by that third kind of error, suspecting that of God which can be found neither in him nor in any creature.[^14]

For from the things that are found in creation divine Scripture is wont to fashion, as it were, childlike delights, by which the gaze of the weak might be moved, according to its small measure, as though by steps, toward seeking higher things and abandoning lower ones.[^15]

But the things that are said properly of God, and that are found in no creature, divine Scripture sets down rarely; as that which was said to Moses, *I am who am*; and, *he who is has sent me to you*[^16]. For since both body and mind are in some way said to be, unless it wished to be understood in a certain proper way, it surely would not say this.

And that which the Apostle says, *who alone has immortality*[^17]: since the soul too is said in a certain way to be immortal, and is so, he would not say *alone has* it, except that true immortality is unchangeableness, which no creature can have, since it belongs to the Creator alone. This is what James says: *every good endowment and every perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of lights, with whom there is no change, nor shadow of a moment*[^18]. This too David: *you will change them, and they will be changed; but you are the same*[^19].

### We are nourished by faith so that we may become fit to grasp divine things.

**3**. Accordingly, it is difficult to behold and fully to know the substance of God, who makes changeable things without any change of himself,[^20] and who creates temporal things without any temporal movement of himself; and therefore a purification of our mind is necessary, by which that ineffable thing may ineffably be seen;[^21] and, not yet endowed with this, we are nourished by faith, and are led by certain more bearable paths, so that we may be made apt and fit to grasp it.

Hence the Apostle says that in Christ indeed are *hidden all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge*; yet he commended him to those already reborn by his grace, but still carnal and animal, like little ones in Christ, not from the divine power in which he is equal to the Father, but from the human weakness by which he was crucified.[^22] For he says: *for I decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ, and him crucified*. Then he goes on to say: *and I was with you in weakness and in fear and in much trembling*. And a little later he says to them: *and I, brothers, could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to carnal men. As to little ones in Christ, I gave you milk to drink, not solid food; for you were not yet able to receive it; nor indeed are you able even now*.

When this is said to certain people, they grow angry, and think it is said to them insultingly; and for the most part they prefer to believe that those from whom they hear this have nothing to say, rather than that they themselves cannot grasp what has been said. And sometimes we offer them a reason—not the one they ask for when they inquire about God, since neither are they able to take it in, nor are we perhaps able even to apprehend or set it forth—but one by which it may be shown to them how unfit and least suited they are to perceive what they demand. But because they do not hear what they want, they think either that we are acting craftily so as to hide our own ignorance, or maliciously, because we begrudge them the knowledge; and so they go away indignant and disturbed.

### The aim and plan of the work.

**4**. Therefore, with the help of the Lord our God, we will undertake to render, as far as we can, the very reason they demand: that the Trinity[^23] is the one and only and true God, and how rightly the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are said, believed, and understood to be of one and the same substance or essence;[^24]

so that they may not be mocked, as it were, by our excuses,[^25] but may experience in fact both that there is that supreme good which is discerned by the most purified minds, and that it cannot on that account be discerned and comprehended by them, because the human mind's vision, too weak, is not fixed upon so excellent a light unless it is nourished and invigorated *by the righteousness of faith*.[^26]

But first it must be shown, on the authority of the Holy Scriptures, whether the faith is indeed so. Then, if God wills it and helps, we shall perhaps so serve these garrulous reasoners—more lofty than capacious, and therefore laboring under a more dangerous disease—that they may find something they cannot doubt, and on this account, in what they have been unable to find, may complain of their own minds rather than of the truth itself or of our discussions.[^27]

And so, if they have any love or fear toward God, let them return to the beginning and order of faith, now perceiving how healthfully the medicine of the faithful has been established in the holy Church, so that observed piety may heal the feeble mind for the perception of unchangeable truth,[^28] lest disordered rashness should cast it down into the opinion of harmful falsehood.[^29] It will not irk me, however, to inquire wherever I hesitate; nor will it shame me to learn wherever I err.

### A pious agreement to be entered into by Augustine with all who read what he writes.

**5**. Accordingly, whoever reads this, where he is equally certain, let him go on with me; where he equally hesitates, let him inquire with me; where he recognizes his own error, let him return to me; where mine, let him call me back. Thus let us together enter the way of love, reaching out toward him of whom it is said, *seek his face* always.[^30] And this pious and safe agreement I would enter, before the Lord our God, with all who read what I write, both in all my writings and especially in these where the unity of the Trinity—of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit—is sought; for nowhere is error more dangerous, nor is anything sought with more labor, nor is anything found with more fruit.[^31]

Whoever, then, when he reads, says, "This is not well stated, because I do not understand it," censures my manner of speaking, not the faith; and perhaps it truly could have been said more plainly; yet no one among men has ever spoken in such a way as to be understood in all things by all. Let him, then, who is displeased by this in my discourse see whether he understands others who are versed in such matters and questions, when he does not understand me; and if it is so, let him put down my book, or even, if it seems good, throw it away, and rather spend his labor and time on those whom he does understand.

Yet it does not follow on that account that I ought to have kept silent, because I could not speak as readily and as lucidly as those whom he understands. For not all the things that are written by all come into the hands of all; and it may happen that some who are able to understand even these writings of ours do not find those plainer books, and at least fall upon these.

And therefore it is useful that many works be made by many writers in a different style, though not in a different faith, even on the same questions, so that the matter itself may reach the greatest number: some in this way, others in that.[^32] But if the one who complains that he has not understood these things has never been able to understand anything carefully and acutely discussed about such matters, let him deal with himself by prayers and efforts so that he may make progress, not with me by complaints and reproaches so that I may keep silent.

But whoever, reading these things, says, "I do understand what has been said, but it is not stated truly," let him, if he pleases, assert his own opinion, and refute mine if he can. And if he does this with love and truth, and also takes care (if I remain in this life) to make it known to me, I shall reap the most abundant fruit of this labor of mine. And if he cannot do this for me, may he be willing and glad to do it for those for whom he can.

I, however, will *meditate on the law of the Lord*, if not *day and night*, at least in whatever little portions of time I can; and I bind my meditations to the pen, lest they flee away through forgetfulness; hoping in the mercy of God that he will make me persevere in all the true things that are certain to me; *but if in anything* I think *otherwise, this too* he himself will *reveal* to me,[^33] whether through hidden inspirations and admonitions, or through his manifest utterances, or through the conversations of the brothers.

This I pray, and this my deposit and my desire I hold in his keeping, who is sufficiently *able* for me to guard what he has given, and *to render what he has promised*.[^34]

### Augustine prefers to be censured by one who reproves falsehood than to be praised by one who praises it.

**6**. I think, indeed, that some of the slower will suppose, in certain passages of my books, that I meant what I did not mean, or did not mean what I meant. Who does not know that their error ought not to be charged to me, if, as though following me but not grasping me, they have strayed into some falsehood while I am forced to pick my way through certain dense and shadowy places—seeing that no one would rightly charge to the holy authorities of the divine Books themselves the many and varied errors of the heretics, when all of them try to defend their false and deceptive opinions out of these same Scriptures?[^35]

Plainly the law of Christ—that is, love[^36]—admonishes me and commands me with its most gentle authority that, when men think I meant something false in my books which I did not mean, and that very falsehood displeases one and pleases another,[^37] I should prefer to be censured by one who reproves the falsehood than to be praised by one who praises it. For by the former, although I am not rightly censured, since I did not mean this, the error itself is nevertheless rightly blamed; but by the latter neither am I rightly praised by one who reckons that I meant what the truth blames, nor is the very opinion that the truth blames. Therefore let us set about the work undertaken in the name of the Lord.

## Chapter 2

### The teaching of the Catholic faith concerning the Trinity.

**7**. All those I have been able to read who have written before me about the Trinity, who is God—Catholic expounders of the divine Books, old and new—have intended to teach, according to the Scriptures, this: that the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, of *one and the same substance*, suggest the divine unity in an inseparable equality; and therefore there are *not three gods, but one God*; although the Father begot the Son, and therefore the Son is not he who is the Father; and the Son was *begotten of the Father*, and therefore the *Father is not* he who is the *Son*; and the *Holy Spirit* is neither Father nor Son, but only the Spirit of the Father and of the Son, himself also coequal with the Father and the Son, and belonging to the unity of the Trinity.[^38]

Yet it was not the same Trinity that was born *of the Virgin Mary*, and crucified and buried *under Pontius Pilate*, and *rose on the third day* and *ascended into heaven*,[^39] but only the Son. Nor was it the same Trinity that descended *in the form of a dove* upon Jesus when he was baptized;[^40] or that, *on the day of Pentecost after the Lord's ascension*, when a sound was made *from heaven as of a rushing mighty wind*, and there were divided tongues *as of fire*,[^41] but only the Holy Spirit.[^42] Nor was it the same Trinity that said from heaven, *you are my Son*, whether when he was baptized by John, or on the mountain when the three disciples were with him; or when the voice sounded, saying, *and I have glorified it, and will glorify it again*: but it was only the Father's voice made to the Son; although the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, just as they are inseparable, so they work inseparably.[^43] This is also my faith, since this is the Catholic faith.

### Three questions by which some are troubled.

**8**. But in this some are troubled, when they hear of God the Father, and God the Son, and God the Holy Spirit, and yet that this Trinity is not three gods but one God; and they ask how they are to understand this; especially since it is said that the Trinity works inseparably in everything that God works, and yet that a certain voice of the Father sounded[^44] which was not the voice of the Son; that it was only the Son who was *born in the flesh*, and *suffered*, and *rose again*, and *ascended*[^45]; and that it was only the Holy Spirit who came *in the form of a dove*.[^46]

They want to understand how the Trinity made both that voice which was the Father's alone, and how the same Trinity created that flesh in which the Son alone *was born of the Virgin*,[^47] and how that very Trinity wrought that form of a dove in which the Holy Spirit alone appeared. Otherwise the Trinity does not work inseparably, but the Father does some things, the Son others, the Holy Spirit others; or if they do some things together, others separately, then the Trinity is no longer inseparable.

It also troubles them how the Holy Spirit is in the Trinity, whom neither the Father nor the Son nor both begot, since he is the Spirit both of the Father and of the Son. Since, then, men inquire about these things, and they are wearisome to us, if our weakness has any taste of this from the gift of God, let us set it forth to them as we are able, *and let us not journey with consuming envy*.

If we say that we are not accustomed to think about such things, we lie; but if we confess that these things dwell in our thoughts, since we are carried away by the love of searching out the truth, they demand, by the right of love, that we tell them what we have been able to think out about this.

*Not that I have already obtained, or am already perfect*[^48] (for if Paul the apostle was not, how much more do I, lying far below his feet, not think myself to have grasped it?);[^49] but according to my small measure, if I *forget what lies behind*, and *strain forward to what lies ahead*, and *according to my aim press on to the prize of the upward call*,[^50] it is desired of me that I disclose how much of that same road I have traveled, and where I have arrived from which the remaining course is left for me to the end—it being desired by those whom free love compels me to serve.

And it is fitting, and God will grant, that in ministering to them things for them to read, I myself may also make progress; and that, wishing to answer them as they ask, I myself may also find what I was seeking.[^51] Therefore I have undertaken these things, at the command and with the help of the Lord our God, not so much to discuss with authority what is known, as to come to know it by discussing it with piety.

### The Son is true God, of the same substance as the Father.

**9**. Those who have said that our Lord Jesus Christ is not God, or is not true God, or is not one and the only God with the Father, or is not truly immortal because changeable, have been refuted by the most manifest and harmonious voice of the divine testimonies; whence come those words: *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*. For it is manifest that we take the Word of God to be the only Son of God, of whom he says afterward, *and the Word was made flesh, and dwelt among us*, on account of the birth of his incarnation, which took place in time *of the Virgin*. But in this he declares that he is not only God, but also of the same substance as the Father, since, after he had said, *and the Word was God*, he says, *this was in the beginning with God; all things were made through him, and without him was nothing made*. For he says *all things* only of those that were made, that is, of the whole creation. Whence it clearly appears that he himself was not made, *through whom all things were made*. And if he was not made, he is not a creature; but if he is not a creature, he is *of the same substance as the Father*. For every substance that is not God is a creature; and what is not a creature is God. And if the Son is not of the same substance as the Father, then he is a made substance; if he is a made substance, then *all things were not made through him*; but *all things were made through him*; therefore he is of one and the same substance as the Father. And so he is not only God, but also true God. The same John says this most plainly in his Epistle: *we know that the Son of God has come, and has given us understanding, to know the true God, and to be in his true Son Jesus Christ*. *This is the true God, and eternal life*.

### The one and only God, who is the Trinity itself, has immortality.

**10**. Hence it is also consequently understood that the apostle Paul said, *who alone has immortality*,[^52] not of the Father alone, but of the one and only God, who is the Trinity itself. For *eternal life* itself is not mortal according to any changeableness; and on this account the *Son of God*, since he is *eternal life*, is himself also understood together with the Father, where it is said, *who alone has immortality*. For made *partakers* of that eternal life, we too are made immortal according to our small measure. But it is one thing, the *eternal life* of which we are made *partakers*; another thing, we who shall live *forever* by partaking of it.

For if he had said, *which* the blessed *and only Sovereign, the King of kings and Lord of lords, who alone has immortality*, the Father *will manifest*[^53] *in his own times*, not even thus would it be right to understand the Son as separated from this.

For neither, because the Son himself, speaking elsewhere in the voice of Wisdom (for he is the *Wisdom of God*), says, *I alone have gone round the circuit of heaven*, did he separate the Father from himself; how much more, then, is it not necessary that what is said—*who alone has immortality*—be understood only of the Father apart from the Son, when it is said thus: *that you keep*, he says, *the commandment unstained, free from reproach*,[^54] *until the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ; which the blessed and only Sovereign will manifest in his own times, the King of kings and Lord of lords; who alone has immortality, and dwells in unapproachable light; whom no man has seen, nor can see; to whom be honor and glory forever and ever. Amen*. In which words neither the Father is named properly, nor the Son, nor the Holy Spirit; but the *blessed and only Sovereign*, the *King of kings and Lord of lords*, which is the one and only and true God, the Trinity itself.

### The Son and the Trinity are invisible.

**11**. Unless perhaps what follows will trouble this understanding, because he said, *whom no man has seen, nor can see*; since this too is taken to pertain to Christ according to his divinity, which the Jews did not see, though they saw and crucified the flesh. But the divinity can in no way be seen by human sight; rather it is seen with that sight by which those who already see are not men, but beyond men. Rightly, therefore, is the very God of the Trinity understood as the *blessed and only Sovereign*, manifesting *the appearing of our Lord Jesus Christ in his own times*. For it is said, *alone has immortality*, just as it is said, *who alone does wondrous things*. Of whom I would like to know of whom they take this to be said. If of the Father only, then how is it true that the Son himself says, *for whatever the Father does, these same things the Son also does likewise*? Or is there anything among wonders more wonderful than to raise and bring the dead to life? But the same Son says, *as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, so also the Son gives life to whom he will*. How, then, does the Father *alone do wondrous things*, when these words allow us to understand neither the Father only, nor the Son only, but assuredly the one true and only God, that is, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit?

### All things were made through the Son.

**12**. Likewise, when the same Apostle says, *for us there is one God, the Father, from whom are all things, and we in him; and one Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things, and we through him*. Who doubts that by *all things* he means those that were created, just as John says, *all things were made through him*? I ask, therefore, of whom he speaks in another place, *for from him, and through him, and in him are all things; to him be glory forever and ever. Amen*.

For if it is of the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, so that single things are attributed to single persons—*from him*, from the Father; *through him*, through the Son; *in him*, in the Holy Spirit—it is manifest that *the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are one God*, since he added in the singular, *to him be glory forever and ever*.

For where he began this sense, he did not say, *O depth of the riches of the wisdom and knowledge* of the Father, or of the Son, or of the Holy Spirit; but, *of the wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways! For who has known the mind of the Lord? Or who has been his counselor? Or who has first given to him, that he should be repaid? For from him, and through him, and in him are all things; to him be glory forever and ever. Amen*.

But if they want this to be understood of the Father only, then how are all things through the Father, as is said here; and all things through the Son, as in the letter to the Corinthians where he says, *and one Lord Jesus Christ, through whom are all things*; and as in the Gospel of John, *all things were made through him*? For if some things are through the Father, others through the Son, then all things are not through the Father, nor all things through the Son. But if all things are through the Father, and all things through the Son, then the same things are through the Father which are through the Son.

The Son, therefore, is *equal to the Father*, and the working of the Father and of the Son is inseparable. For if the Father even made the Son, whom the Son himself did not make, then *all things were not made through* the Son; but *all things were made through* the Son. He himself, therefore, was not made, so that he might make together with the Father *all things that were made*. And yet the Apostle did not keep silent even about the word itself, but said most plainly of all, *who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped*,[^55] here calling the Father properly God, as elsewhere, *but the head of Christ is God*.

### The Holy Spirit is true God, wholly equal to the Father and the Son.

**13**. Likewise, testimonies have been gathered *concerning the Holy Spirit*, which those who discussed these things before us used more abundantly, because he too is *God* and not a creature.[^56] And if not a creature, then not only God (for men too have been called gods), but also *true God*. Therefore he is wholly equal to the Father and the Son, and in the unity of the Trinity consubstantial and coeternal. But most of all it is clear enough that the Holy Spirit is not a creature in that place where we are commanded to serve not the *creature* but the *Creator*, not in the way in which we are commanded *through love* to serve *one another*, which in Greek is δουλεύειν, but in the way in which God alone is served, which in Greek is λατρεύειν. Hence those are called idolaters who render to images that service which is owed to God. For according to this service it was said, *you shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve*.[^57]

For this too is found more distinctly in the Greek Scripture, for it has λατρεύσεις. Now if by such service we are forbidden to serve the *creature*, since it was said, *you shall worship the Lord your God, and him only shall you serve*—whence also the Apostle detests those who *worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator*—then the Holy Spirit is certainly not a *creature*, to whom such service is rendered by all the saints, the Apostle saying, *for we are the circumcision, serving the Spirit of God*, which in Greek is λατρεύοντες. For many Latin codices also have it thus: *who serve the Spirit of God*; but all the Greek ones, or nearly all. In some Latin copies, however, we find not *we serve the Spirit of God*, but *in the spirit we serve God*.[^58]

But those who err in this, and decline to yield to the weightier authority, do they not also find that other passage varying in the codices: *do you not know that your bodies are a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God*? But what is more insane and more sacrilegious than that anyone should dare to say that the *members of Christ* are the *temple* of a creature lesser, according to these people,[^59] than Christ is? For in another place he says, *your bodies are members of Christ*. But if the things that *are members of Christ* are the *temple of the Holy Spirit*, then the Holy Spirit is *not a creature*, since to him to whom we render our body as a *temple* we must necessarily owe that service by which God alone is to be served, which in Greek is called λατρεία. Whence he consequently says, *glorify God, therefore, in your body*.

## Chapter 3

### The Son, in the form of a servant, is less than the Father and than himself.

**14**. By these and similar testimonies of the divine Scriptures, which, as I said, our predecessors used more copiously when they overthrew such slanders or errors of the heretics, the unity and equality of the Trinity is suggested to our faith. But because many things are said in the holy Books on account of the incarnation of the Word of God—which took place for the restoring of our salvation, that *the man Christ Jesus* might be the *mediator between God and men*—in such a way as to signify, or even most plainly to show, that the Father is greater than the Son; men have erred who scrutinized or considered less diligently the whole series of the Scriptures, and have tried to transfer the things that were said of Christ Jesus according to the man to that substance of his which before the incarnation was everlasting, and is everlasting. And they indeed say that the Son is less than the Father is, because it is written, the Lord himself saying, *the Father is greater than I*.[^60] But the truth shows that according to this manner the Son is less even than himself. For how is he not made less even than himself, who *emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*?

For he did not so take the *form of a servant* as to lose the *form of God*, in which he was *equal to the Father*. If, then, the *form of a servant* was so taken that the *form of God* was not lost—since in both the *form of a servant* and the *form of God* he is the same *only-begotten Son of God* the Father, *in the form of God equal to the Father*,[^61] in the *form of a servant the mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^62]—who does not understand that in the *form of God* he is greater even than himself, but in the *form of a servant* he is less even than himself?

Not undeservedly, therefore, does Scripture say both: that the Son is equal to the Father, and that the Father is greater than the Son. For the one is understood on account of the *form of God*, the other on account of the *form of a servant*, without any confusion.

And this rule for resolving this question throughout all the sacred Scriptures is drawn from one little chapter of the Epistle of the apostle Paul, where this distinction is more plainly commended. For he says: *who, though he was in the form of God, did not count equality with God a thing to be grasped; but emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, being made in the likeness*[^63] *of men, and found in fashion as a man*. The *Son of God*, therefore, is *equal* to *God the Father* by nature, *less* in fashion. For in the *form of a servant* which he took, he is *less than the Father*; but in the *form of God* in which he was even before he took this, he is *equal to the Father*. *In the form of God, the Word through whom all things were made*; but in the *form of a servant, made of a woman, made under the law, that he might redeem those who were under the law*. Accordingly, *in the form of God he made man*; *in the form of a servant he was made man*. For if the Father only, without the Son, had made man, it would not have been written, *let us make man to our image and likeness*.[^64] Therefore, because the form of God took the form of a servant, he is both God and man; but he is both God on account of the God who takes, and both man on account of the man who is taken. For by that assumption neither of the two was turned and changed into the other; neither was the divinity changed into the creature, so as to cease to be divinity; nor the creature into the divinity, so as to cease to be a creature.[^65]

### The Son is subject to the Father by reason of the human nature he assumed.

**15**. But that which the Apostle says, *when all things shall have been subjected to him, then the Son himself also will be subjected to him who subjected all things to him*—either this was said lest anyone should think that the fashion of Christ, which was assumed from the human creature, will afterward be turned into the very divinity, or, that I may express it more surely, into the deity,[^66] which is not a creature, but is the unity of the Trinity, an incorporeal and unchangeable nature, consubstantial with itself and coeternal.

Or if anyone contends,[^67] as some have held, that it was said—*and the Son himself will be subjected to him who subjected all things to him*—in such a way as to believe that this very subjection will be a changing and turning of the creature into the very substance or essence of the Creator, that is, so that what had been the substance of the creature becomes the substance of the Creator; he certainly grants at least this, which admits of no doubt, that this had not yet been done when the Lord said, *the Father is greater than I*. For he said this not only before he had ascended into heaven, but even before he had suffered and risen from the dead.

But those who think that the human nature in him is changed and turned into the substance of the deity, and that it was thus said—*then the Son himself also will be subjected to him who subjected all things to him*—as if it were said, "*then the Son* of man too, the human nature assumed by the Word of God, will be changed into the nature of him *who subjected all things to him*"; they think this will happen *when*, after the day of judgment, *he delivers the kingdom to God and the Father*. And on this account, even according to this opinion, the *Father is* still *greater* than that *form of a servant which was taken of the Virgin*.[^68]

But if some affirm even this, that *the man Christ Jesus* has already[^69] been changed into the substance of God, they certainly cannot deny that the nature of man still remained when, before the Passion, he said, *for the Father is greater than I*. Whence there is no hesitation that, according to this, it was said that *the Father is greater than the form of a servant*, to whom the Son is *equal in the form of God*.

Nor let anyone, when he hears what the Apostle says, *but when he says that all things are subjected to him, it is manifest that he is excepted who subjected all things to him*,[^70] so think it is to be understood of the Father, that the Father subjected all things to the Son in such a way that he should not think the Son himself subjected all things to himself. This the Apostle shows to the Philippians, saying: *but our commonwealth is in heaven; whence also we await the Savior, the Lord Jesus Christ, who will transform the body of our lowliness, that it may be made conformed to the body of his glory, according to his working by which he is able even to subject all things to himself*.

For the working of the Father and of the Son is inseparable. Otherwise not even the Father himself subjected all things to himself, but the Son subjected them to him who delivers the kingdom to him, and abolishes *every rule and every authority and power*.

For these things were said of the Son: *when he delivers*, he says, *the kingdom to God and the Father, when he abolishes every rule and every authority and power*. For he himself subjects who abolishes.

### The Son will not deliver the kingdom to the Father in such a way as to take it from himself.

**16**. Nor let us think that Christ will deliver *the kingdom to God and the Father* in such a way as to take it from himself. For some empty talkers have believed this too. For when it is said, *he delivers the kingdom to God and the Father*, he himself is not separated; since together with the Father *he is one God*.

But the word that is placed there, "until," deceives those who are careless of the divine Scriptures and eager for contentions. For it follows thus, *for he must reign until he puts all his enemies under his feet*, as though, when he has put them there, he will not reign. Nor do they understand that it is said just as that is: *his heart is steadfast; he will not be moved, until he looks down upon his enemies*. For not when he has looked upon them will he then *be moved*.

What, then, is *when he delivers the kingdom to God and the Father*, as though God and the Father did not now have the kingdom? But because *the mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus*,[^71] in whom he now reigns over all the just who live *by faith*, is going to bring them to the *sight*—which the same Apostle calls the vision *face to face*[^72]—it was said, *when he delivers the kingdom to God and the Father*, as if it were said, "When he has brought believers to the contemplation of God and the Father."

For he says thus: *all things have been delivered to me by my Father; and no one knows the Son except the Father; and no one knows the Father except the Son, and anyone to whom the Son wishes to reveal him*.[^73] Then the Father will be revealed by the Son, *when he abolishes every rule and every authority and power*;[^74] that is, so that the dispensation of likenesses through the angelic principalities and authorities and powers will no longer be necessary.

In whose person it is not inappropriately understood to be said to the bride in the Song of Songs: *we will make you likenesses of gold with markings of silver, while the king is on his couch*, that is, while Christ is in his secret place; for *your life is hidden with Christ in God. When Christ your life appears, then you too will appear with him in glory*. Before this comes to pass, *we now see through a mirror in an enigma*, that is, in likenesses; but then, face to face.

### The contemplation of God is promised to us as the end of all actions.

**17**. For this contemplation is promised to us as the end of all actions and the eternal perfection of joys. For *we are children of God*, *and it has not yet appeared what we shall be; we know that, when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*.

For what he said to his servant Moses, *I am who am*. *And so*[^75] *you shall say to the children of Israel: he who is has sent me to you*; this we shall contemplate when we shall live forever. For he says thus: *and this is eternal life, that they may know you, the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*.

This will come to pass when the Lord comes and illuminates *the hidden things of darkness*, when the darkness of this mortality and *corruption shall have passed away*.[^76] Then will be our morning, of which it is said in the Psalm: *in the morning I will stand before you, and I will contemplate*. Of this contemplation I understand to be said, *when he delivers the kingdom to God and the Father*; that is, when he has brought the just—in whom, now living *by faith*, the *mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus*, reigns—to the contemplation of God and the Father. If I am foolish here, let him who is wiser correct me; to me nothing else seems true.

For we shall seek nothing else when we have arrived at the contemplation of him, which now is not, as long as our joy is in hope. *But hope that is seen is not hope; for who hopes for what he sees? But if we hope for what we do not see, we wait for it with patience*, *while the king is on his couch*. Then will be what is written: *you will fill me with gladness with your countenance*. In that gladness nothing more will be required; for there will be nothing more to require. For the Father will be shown to us, and it will suffice us. This Philip had well understood, so as to say, *Lord, show us the Father, and it suffices us*. But he had not yet understood that in that same way he could have said the same thing: "Lord, show us yourself, and it suffices us." For, that he might understand this, it was answered him by the Lord, *have I been with you so long, and you have not known me? Philip, he who has seen me has seen the Father also*. But because he wanted him to live by faith before he could see that, he went on and said: *do you not believe that I am in the Father, and the Father in me*? For *as long as we are in the body, we are away from the Lord. For we walk by faith, not by sight*.

For contemplation is the reward of faith, for which reward hearts are cleansed through faith; as it is written, *cleansing their hearts by faith*. But that hearts are cleansed for that contemplation is proved especially by that sentence: *blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*. And because *this is eternal life*, God says in the Psalm: *with length of days will I fill him, and I will show him my salvation*. Whether, then, we hear, "show us the Son," or whether we hear, *show us the Father*, it amounts to the same; for neither can be shown without the other. For they are one, as he himself says: *I and the Father are one*. Finally, on account of this very inseparability, sometimes either the Father alone or the Son alone is sufficiently named as the one who will fill us *with gladness with his countenance*.

### The Spirit alone suffices for our blessedness, because he cannot be separated from the Father and the Son.

**18**. Nor is the Spirit of both separated from this—that is, the Spirit of the Father and of the Son. This Holy Spirit is properly called the *Spirit of truth, whom this world cannot receive*. For this is our *full joy*, than *which there is nothing greater*: to enjoy the Trinity, God, to whose *image we were made*.[^77]

On this account he sometimes speaks of the Holy Spirit as if he alone sufficed for our blessedness; and he alone suffices for this reason, that he cannot be separated from the Father and the Son; just as the Father alone suffices, because he cannot be separated from the Son and the Holy Spirit; and the Son alone suffices for this reason, that he cannot be separated from the Father and the Holy Spirit.

For what does he mean when he says, *if you love me, keep my commandments; and I will ask the Father, and he will give you another advocate, to be with you forever, the Spirit of truth, whom this world cannot receive*—that is, the lovers of the world? *For the animal man does not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God*.

But it can still seem to have been said—*and I will ask the Father, and he will give you another advocate*[^78]—as if the Son alone did not suffice. But in that place it is said of him as though he alone wholly sufficed: *when that Spirit of truth comes, he will teach you all truth*. Is the Son, then, separated from this, as though he himself does not teach *all truth*, or as though the Holy Spirit fulfills what the Son was less able to teach? Let them say, then, if they please, that the Holy Spirit, whom they are wont to call less than the Son, is greater than the Son.

Or is it because it was not said, "he alone," or, "no one but he, *will teach you all truth*," that they therefore allow it to be believed that the Son too teaches together with him? The Apostle, then, separated the Son from the knowing of those *things that are of God*, where he says: *so also no one knows the things that are of God except the Spirit of God*. So that now these perverse people can say from this that not even the Son knows the things that are of God except the Holy Spirit, as the greater the lesser; to whom the Son himself ascribed so much as to say, *because I have said these things to you, sorrow has filled your heart. But I tell you the truth: it is expedient for you that I go; for if I do not go away, the advocate will not come to you*.

### Sometimes all are understood in one person.

But he said this, not on account of any inequality of the Word of God and the Holy Spirit, but as though the presence of the Son of man among them were an impediment to the coming of him who was not less, because he *did not empty himself, taking the form of a servant*, as the Son did.

It was necessary, therefore, that the form of a servant be taken from their eyes, since, gazing upon it, they thought Christ to be only what they saw. Hence too is that which he says, *if you loved me, you would surely rejoice, because I go to the Father; for the Father is greater than I*; that is, for this reason I must go to the Father, because, while you see me thus, from what you see you reckon that I am less than the Father, and so, occupied about the creature and the fashion I have assumed, you do not understand the equality I have with the Father.

Hence too is that other saying: *do not touch me; for I have not yet ascended to the Father*. For touch, as it were, makes an end of acquaintance. And therefore he did not want there to be in him the end of the heart's straining toward him, so that he might be thought to be only what was seen. But the ascension to the Father was to be seen as he is equal to the Father, so that there might be the end of the vision which suffices us.

Likewise, sometimes it is said of the Son alone that he himself suffices, and in his vision the whole reward of our love and desire is promised. For he says thus: *he who has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me. And he who loves me will be loved by my Father; and I will love him, and will show myself to him*. Did he here, because he did not say, "I will show him the Father also," therefore separate the Father? But because it is true, *I and the Father are one*, when the Father is shown, the Son too is shown, who is in him; and when the Son is shown, the Father too is shown, who is in him.

As, therefore, when he says, *and I will show myself to him*, it is understood that he shows the Father also; so too in that which is said, *when he delivers the kingdom to God and the Father*, it is understood that he does not take it from himself. For when he brings believers to the contemplation of God and the Father, he assuredly brings them to his own contemplation, who said, *and I will show myself to him*. And therefore, consequently, when Judas had said to him, *Lord, what has happened, that you are going to show yourself to us, and not to this world? Jesus answered and said to him: if anyone loves me, he will keep my word; and my Father will love him, and we will come to him, and will make our dwelling with him*. Behold, he does not show only himself to the one by whom he is loved, since he comes to him together with the Father, *and makes his dwelling with him*.

**19**. Or will it perhaps be thought that, since the Father and the Son make their dwelling in the one who loves him, the Holy Spirit is excluded from this dwelling? What, then, is that which he says above of the Holy Spirit: *whom this world cannot receive, because it does not see him; but you know him, because he abides with you, and is in you*? He is not, therefore, separated from this dwelling, of whom it is said, *he abides with you and is in you*. Unless perhaps someone is so absurd as to think that, when the Father and the Son come to make their dwelling with the one who loves him, the Holy Spirit will depart thence, and as it were give place to the greater. But Scripture meets this carnal thought; for a little above it says, *and I will ask the Father, and he will give you another advocate, to be with you forever*. He will not, then, depart when the Father and the Son come, but will be in the same dwelling with them *forever*; for neither does he come without them, nor they without him. But on account of the suggestion of the Trinity, certain things are said with the single persons even named singly;[^79] yet they are not understood with the others separated, on account of the unity of the same Trinity and the one substance and deity of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit.

### The contemplation of God.

**20**. *And so our Lord Jesus Christ will deliver the kingdom to God and the Father*, without separating himself from it, nor the Holy Spirit, since[^80] he will bring believers to the contemplation of God, where is the end of all good actions, and everlasting rest, and the joy that will never be taken from us. For this he signifies in that which he says: *I will see you again, and your heart will rejoice, and your joy no one will take from you*.

A likeness of this joy was prefigured by Mary *sitting at the Lord's feet, and intent upon his word*; resting, that is, from all action, and intent upon the truth according to a certain manner of which this life is capable, by which, nevertheless, she might prefigure that which is to be forever. For while her sister Martha was busy in the action of necessity—good and useful, yet, when rest succeeds, destined to pass away—she herself rested in the word of the Lord.

And therefore the Lord, when Martha complained that her sister did not help her, answered: *Mary has chosen the best part, which will not be taken from her*. He did not call what Martha was doing a bad part, but this *best part which will not be taken away*. For that which is in the ministry of need will be taken away when the need itself has passed. For the reward of a good work that is to pass away is rest that will abide.

In that contemplation, therefore, *God* will be *all in all*, because nothing else will be required of him, but it will suffice to be illumined by him alone and to enjoy him alone. And therefore he in whom *the Spirit makes intercession with unutterable groanings* says, *one thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek: that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, that I may contemplate the delight of the Lord*.

For we shall contemplate God the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, *when the mediator between God and men, the man Christ Jesus, delivers the kingdom to God and the Father*,[^81] so that our *mediator* and *priest*,[^82] the *Son of God and Son of man*, may no longer *intercede for us*; but he himself too, insofar as he is *priest* by the *form of a servant* assumed for our sake, may be *subjected to him who subjected all things to him*, and to whom *he subjected all things*;

so that, insofar as he is God, he may have us subjected together with himself, and, insofar as he is *priest*, he may be *subjected with us to him*. Wherefore, since the Son is both *God and man*, the man in the Son is a different substance rather than the Son in the Father; just as the flesh of my soul is a different substance with respect to my soul, though in one man, than the soul of another man is with respect to my soul.

### When he has brought believers to contemplation, the Son will no longer intercede for us.

**21**. *When*, therefore, *he delivers the kingdom to God and the Father*—that is, when he has brought believers and those who live by faith, for whom *the mediator now intercedes*,[^83] to the contemplation for the receiving of which we sigh and *groan, and when the labor and the groaning have passed*—he will no longer intercede for us, the kingdom having been delivered to God and the Father. Signifying this, he says, *these things I have spoken to you in likenesses; the hour will come when I shall no longer speak to you in likenesses, but will tell you plainly of the Father*; that is, there will no longer be likenesses, when the vision shall be *face to face*.

For this is what he says, *but I will tell you plainly of the Father*; as if he said, "I will show you the Father plainly." For he says *I will tell*, because he is his Word. For he goes on and says: *in that day you will ask in my name, and I do not tell you that I will ask the Father; for the Father himself loves you, because you love me*,[^84] *and have believed that I came forth from God. I came forth from the Father, and have come into this world; again I leave the world, and go to the Father*.

What is *I came forth from the Father*, except, "not in that form in which I am equal to the Father, but otherwise, that is, in the assumed creature, I appeared less"? And what is *I have come into this world*, except, "the form of a servant, which I took in emptying myself, I showed even to the eyes of sinners who love this world"? And what is *again I leave the world*, except, "from the sight of the lovers of the world I take away what they saw"? And what is *I go to the Father*, except, "I teach that I am to be understood by my faithful as I am equal to the Father"?

Those who believe this will be held worthy to be brought from faith to sight, that is, to the vision itself, to which, in bringing them, he is said *to deliver the kingdom to God and the Father*. For his faithful, whom he redeemed by his blood, are called his kingdom, for whom he now intercedes; but then, making them cleave to him there where he is equal to the Father, he will no longer ask the Father for them. *For the Father himself*, he says, *loves you*.

For he asks in that by which he is less than the Father; but in that by which he is equal, he hears together with the Father. Whence he certainly does not separate himself from what he said, *for the Father himself loves you*; but he makes it understood according to the things I recalled above and sufficiently suggested—that very often each person in the Trinity is so named that the others too are understood there. So, then, it was said, *for the Father himself loves you*, that consequently the Son too and the Holy Spirit may be understood.

Not that he does not now love us, *who did not spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us all*; but God loves us such as we shall be, not such as we are. For such as he loves, he preserves *forever*; which will then be, *when he delivers the kingdom to God and the Father, who now intercedes for us*, so that he no longer asks the Father, because *the Father himself loves* us.

But by what merit, except that of faith, by which we believe before we see what is promised? For through this we shall arrive at *sight*, so that he may love us such as he loves us to be; not such as he hates because we are, and exhorts and grants that we may not always wish to be such.

## Chapter 4

### The rule by which the Son is understood in the Scriptures now as equal, now as less.

**22**. Wherefore, this rule for understanding the Scriptures concerning the Son of God being known—so that we may distinguish what in them sounds according to the *form* of God, in which he is and[^85] is *equal to the Father*, and what according to the *form of a servant* which he took, and[^86] is *less than the Father*—we shall not be disturbed, as though the sentences of the Holy Books were contrary to and at odds with one another.

For according to the *form of God*, both the *Son* and the Holy Spirit are *equal to the Father*, because neither of them is a creature, as we have already shown.

But according to the form of a servant, he is less than the Father, because he himself said, *the Father is greater than I*; he is less than himself, because it was said of him, *he emptied himself*;[^87] he is less than the Holy Spirit, because he himself says: *whoever speaks a blasphemy against the Son of man, it will be forgiven him; but whoever speaks against the Holy Spirit, it will not be forgiven him*.

And in him he worked miracles, saying: *if I by the Spirit of God cast out demons, then surely the kingdom of God has come upon you*.

And in Isaiah he says—a reading which he himself recited in the synagogue, and showed to be fulfilled in himself without a scruple of doubt—*the Spirit*, he says, *of the Lord is upon me; because of this he has anointed me, he has sent me to preach good news to the poor, to proclaim release to the captives*, and the rest; and he says that he was sent to do these things for this reason, because the *Spirit of the Lord* is upon him.[^88]

According to the form of God, *all things were made through him*;[^89] according to the form of a servant, he himself *was made of a woman, made under the Law*. According to the form of God, he *and the Father are one*; according to the form of a servant, *he came not to do his own will, but the will of him who sent him*. According to the form of God, *as the Father has life in himself, so he gave to the Son also to have life in himself*; according to the form of a servant, his *soul is sorrowful even unto death*; and, *Father*, he says, *if it can be, let*[^90] *this cup pass*.[^91] According to the form of God, *he is the true God and eternal life*; according to the form of a servant, *he became obedient unto death, even death on a cross*.

**11. 23**. According to the *form of God, all that the Father has* are *his*; and, *all mine are yours*, he says, *and yours mine*; according to the *form of a servant*, the *teaching is not* his own, *but his who sent* him.

### The Son does not know the day of judgment, because he did not know it in such a way as then to tell the disciples.

**12. 23**. And: *of the day and the hour no one knows, neither the angels in heaven, nor the Son, but the Father*. For he does not know this in such a way as makes others know—that is, he did not know it in such a way as then to tell the disciples. Just as it was said to Abraham, *now I know that you fear God*, that is, "now I have made you know it," since he too became known to himself, proved in that trial.

For he was certainly going to tell the disciples that too at an opportune time, of which, speaking of the future as though of the past, he says, *I will no longer call you servants, but friends. For the servant does not know the will of his lord; but I have called you friends, because all that I have heard from my Father I have made known to you*. This he had not yet done, but because he was certainly going to do it, he spoke as though he had already done it. For he says to them: *I have many things to say to you, but you cannot bear them now*. Among which is understood also, *of the day and the hour*.

For the Apostle too says: *for I decided to know nothing among you except Christ Jesus, and him crucified*. For he was speaking to those who could not grasp the higher things concerning Christ's deity. To whom also a little later he says: *I could not speak to you as to spiritual men, but as to carnal men*. This, then, he did not know among them, because they could not know it through him. And this alone he said he knew, because they needed to know it through him.

Finally, he knew among the perfect what he did not know among the little ones; for there he says, *we speak wisdom among the perfect*. For by that manner of speaking a person is said not to know what he conceals, just as a ditch is called blind which is hidden.[^92] For the Scriptures speak by no manner of speech that is not found in human custom; since they certainly speak to men.[^93]

### In the Scriptures some things are said of Christ according to the form of God, others according to the form of a servant.

**12. 24**. According to the *form of God* it was said, *before all the hills he begot me*, that is, before all the heights of creatures; and, *before the morning star I begot you*, that is, before all times and temporal things; but according to the *form of a servant* it was said, *the Lord created me in the beginning of his ways*.

For according to the *form of God* he said, *I am the truth*; and according to the *form of a servant, I am the way*. For because he himself, the *firstborn from the dead*, made a road for his Church to the kingdom of God, to eternal life—to which he is the head, even unto the immortality of the body—therefore he was created in the beginning of God's ways for his works.

For according to the *form of God*, he is the *beginning who also speaks to us*; *in which beginning God made heaven and earth*; but according to the *form of a servant*, he is a *bridegroom coming forth from his chamber*.

According to the *form of God*, he is the *firstborn of all creation, and he is before all, and all things hold together in him*; according to the *form of a servant, he is the head of the body, the Church*.

According to the *form of God, he is the Lord of glory*. Whence it is manifest that he himself glorifies his saints. For *those whom he predestined, them he also called; and those whom he called, them he also justified; and those whom he justified, them he also glorified*. For of him it was said that he *justifies the ungodly*; of him it was said that he *is just and the justifier*. If, then, those whom he justified, them he also glorified, he who justifies himself also glorifies, who is, as I said, the Lord of glory. Yet according to the *form of a servant*, when the disciples were busy about his glorification, he answered: *to sit at my right hand or at my left is not mine to give to you, but to those for whom it has been prepared by my Father*.

### The Son himself prepares glory for the elect.

**12. 25**. But what was prepared by his Father was also prepared by the Son himself, because *he and the Father are one*. For we have already shown that in this Trinity, by many modes of the divine expressions, what belongs to all is said even of single persons, on account of the inseparable working of *one and the same substance*.[^94]

Just as he also says of the Holy Spirit, *when I go, I will send him to you*. He did not say, "we will send," but as though the Son alone were to send him, and not the Father; whereas in another place he says, *these things I have spoken to you while abiding with you; but that advocate, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, he will declare all things to you*.

Here, again, it was said as though the Son too were not to send him, but only the Father. As, therefore, these sayings, so also that which he says, *but to those for whom it has been prepared by my Father*—he wished it to be understood that, together with the Father, he prepares the seats of glory for whom he would.

But someone says: "There, when he spoke of the Holy Spirit, he said that he would send him in such a way as not to deny that the Father would send him; and in another place he said the Father would, in such a way as not to deny that he himself would send him. But here he plainly says, *it is not mine to give*, and so going on, he said these things were prepared by the Father."

But this is what we laid down beforehand, that it was said according to the *form of a servant*; so that we should understand *it is not mine to give* as if it were said, "it is not in human power to give this"; so that through that by which God is equal to the Father, it may be understood that he gives this. *It is not mine*, he says, *to give*, that is, "I do not give these things by human power," *but to those for whom it has been prepared by my Father*; "but now do you understand that if *all that the Father has are mine*, this too is certainly mine, and I prepared these things together with the Father."

### How the Son both will not judge and will judge.

**12. 26**. For I also inquire how it was said, *if anyone does not hear my words, I will not judge him*. For perhaps he said here, *I will not judge him*, in the same way as there, *it is not mine to give*.

But what follows here? *For I have not come*, he says, *to judge the world, but to save the world*; then he adds and says, *he who despises me and does not receive my words has one who judges him*.[^95] Here we would now understand the Father, unless he added and said: *the word that I have spoken, that will judge him on the last day*. What then—will the Son not now judge, because he said, *I will not judge him*, nor the Father, but *the word that* the Son *has spoken*?

Nay, hear still what follows: *for I*, he says, *have not spoken of myself, but the Father who sent me, he gave me a commandment what I should say, and what I should speak; and I know that his commandment is eternal life. What I speak, therefore, as the Father has told me, so I speak*.

If, then, the Son does not judge, but *the word that* the Son *has spoken*; and the *word that* the Son *has spoken* judges for this reason, that the Son did not speak of himself, *but the Father who sent* him *gave* him *a commandment* what he should say and what he should speak; then the Father certainly judges, whose *Word* is *that which* the Son *has spoken*, and that very Word of the Father is the Son himself. For the commandment of the Father is not one thing and the Word of the Father another; for he called this both "word" and "commandment."

Let us see, then, whether perhaps what he says, *I have not spoken of myself*, he wished to be understood as, "I was not born of myself." For if he speaks the Word of the Father, he speaks himself, because he himself is the Word of the Father. For he often says, *the Father has given to me*, in which he wishes it to be understood that the Father begot him; not that he gave something to one as it were already existing and not having it; but that his very giving him to have was begetting him to exist.

For the *Son* of God is not, like a creature, one thing before the *incarnation* and before the assumed creature—the *only-begotten through whom all things were made*[^96]—and another thing that he has; but this very thing is what is that which he has. This is said more plainly in that place, if anyone is fit to grasp it, where he says: *as the Father has life in himself, so he gave to the Son to have life in himself*.

For he did not give to one already existing and not having life that he should have *life in himself*, since by the very fact that he is, he is life. This, then, is, *he gave to the Son to have life in himself*: he begot the Son to be unchangeable life, which is eternal life.

Since, then, the Son of God is the Word of God, and the Son of God is the *true God and eternal life*, as John says in his Epistle; here too what else do we recognize, when the Lord says, *the Word that I have spoken, that will judge him on the last day*; and says that the word itself is the Father's word and the commandment of the Father, and that very commandment is eternal life. *And I know*, he says, *that his commandment is eternal life*.

### The sense of the words: *My teaching is not mine*.

**12. 27**. I inquire, therefore, how we are to understand, *I will not judge, but the word that I have spoken will judge*, which from what follows appears to be said as if he said, "*I will not judge*, but the Word of the Father will judge." But the Word of the Father is the Son of God himself. Is it thus to be understood, "*I will not judge*, but I will judge"? How can this be true, except thus: "*I*," that is, "*will not judge* by human power, *because I am the Son of man*; but I will judge by the power of the Word, *since I am the Son of God*"?

Or if they seem contrary and at odds, "*I will not judge*, but I will judge," what shall we say there, where he says, *my teaching is not mine*? How is it *mine*, how is it *not mine*? For he did not say, "this *teaching is not mine*," but, *my teaching is not mine*; the same teaching that he called his, he called not his. How is this true, except that in one respect he called it his; in another, not his; according to the *form of God*, his; according to the *form of a servant*, not his? For when he says, *it is not mine, but his who sent me*, he makes us run back to the Word itself. For the teaching of the Father is the Word of the Father, who is the *only Son*.

What too does this mean, *he who believes in me does not believe in me*? How is it in him, how is it not in him? How can a thing so contrary and adverse to itself be understood—*he who believes in me*, he says, *does not believe in me but in him who sent me*—unless you understand it thus: "*he who believes in me* does not believe in this which he sees," lest our hope be in a creature; but in him who assumed the creature, in which he might appear to human eyes, and so might cleanse hearts through faith to contemplate him equal to the Father?

And therefore, referring the attention of believers to the Father, and saying, *he does not believe in me, but in him who sent me*, he certainly did not wish to be separated from the Father, that is, from him who sent him; but rather that he be believed in just as the Father is, to whom he is equal. This he plainly says in another place: *believe in God, and believe in me*; that is, as *you believe in God*, so *also in me*, because *I and the Father are one God*.

As, therefore, here he as it were took men's faith away from himself and transferred it to the Father, by saying, *he does not believe in me, but in him who sent me*—from whom, nevertheless, he certainly did not separate himself—so too that which he says, *it is not mine to give, but to those for whom it has been prepared by my Father*, I think it is clear in what respect each is to be taken. For such too is that, *I will not judge*; though he himself is to *judge the living and the dead*, but because it is not by human power, therefore, running back to the deity, he lifts up the hearts[^97] of men, for the lifting of which he descended.

### Rightly is it said: God was crucified.

**13. 28**. Unless, however, he who is the *Son of God* on account of the *form of God* in which he is were the same as the *Son of man* on account of the *form of a servant* which he took, the apostle Paul would not say of the rulers of this age: *for if they had known, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory*. For he *was crucified* from the *form of a servant*, and yet *the Lord of glory was crucified*. For such was that "assumption" which made God man, and man God.[^98] Yet what is said on account of what, and what is said in what respect, the prudent and diligent and pious reader, with the Lord's help, understands.

For behold, we said[^99] that according to that which he is God, he glorifies his own, according to this very thing that he is *Lord of glory*; and yet *the Lord of glory was crucified*, because it is rightly said that even God *was crucified*, not from the *power of the divinity*, but *from the weakness of the flesh*;[^100] just as we say that according to that which he is God he judges, that is, by divine power, not by human; and yet he himself, as *man, is to judge*, just as *the Lord of glory was crucified*.

For he plainly says, *when the Son of man comes in his glory, and all the angels with him, then will be gathered before him all the nations*; and the rest that is foretold of the future judgment in that place up to the final sentence. And the Jews, indeed, who persevering in malice are to be punished in that judgment, as is written elsewhere, *will look on him whom they have pierced*.

For since both the good and the wicked are to see *the judge of the living and the dead*,[^101] without doubt the wicked will not be able to see him except according to the form by which he is the *Son of man*; but yet in the *splendor* in which he *will judge*, not in the *lowliness* in which he was judged. Moreover, that *form of God* in which he is *equal to the Father*, without doubt the ungodly will not see. For they are not *pure in heart: for blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*.

And that vision is *face to face*,[^102] which is promised to the just as the highest reward; and it will come to pass *when he delivers the kingdom to God and the Father*; in which he wishes the vision of his own form too to be understood, the whole creation being subjected to God, including that very creation in which the Son of God was made the Son of man. For according to this, *the Son himself too will then be subjected to him who subjected all things to him, that God may be all in all*.

Otherwise, if the *Son of God*, as *judge*, in the form in which he is *equal to the Father*, will *appear* even to the ungodly when he is *to judge*, what is it that he promises as a great thing to the one who loves him, saying: *and I will love him, and will show myself to him*? Wherefore the *Son of man is to judge*, yet not by human power, but by that by which he is the *Son of God*; and again the *Son of God is to judge*, yet not appearing in that form in which he is *God equal to the Father*, but in that by which he is the *Son of man*.

### How the Son of man will both judge and not judge.

**13. 29**. And so both can be said: both "the Son of man will judge," and "the Son of man will not judge"; for the Son of man will judge, so that what he says is true, *when the Son of man comes, then will be gathered before him all the nations*; and the Son of man will not judge, so that what he says is true, *I will not judge*; and, *I do not seek my own glory; there is one who seeks it and judges*. For according to the fact that in the judgment not the form of God, but the form of the Son of man will appear, not even the Father himself will judge. For according to this it was said, *the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*. Whether this was said from that expression which we recalled above, where he says, *so he gave to the Son to have life in himself*, to signify that he thus begot the Son; or from that by which the Apostle speaks, saying, *wherefore he raised him up, and gave him the name which is above every name*.

For this was said of the Son of man, according to whom the Son of God was raised from the dead. For he who in the form of God is equal to the Father, from which he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant, in that very form of a servant both acts, and suffers, and receives the things that the Apostle consequently weaves together: *he humbled himself, becoming obedient unto death, even death on a cross; wherefore God also exalted him, and gave him the name which is above every name, that at the name of Jesus every knee should bow, of things in heaven and on earth and under the earth, and every tongue confess that the Lord Jesus Christ is in the glory of God the Father*.

Whether, then, it was said from that or from this expression, *he gave all judgment to the Son*, sufficiently appears from this: that if it were said according to that by which it was said, *he gave to the Son to have life in himself*, it certainly would not be said, *the Father judges no one*. For according to that by which the Father begot the Son equal, he judges with him. According to this, therefore, it was said, that *in the judgment*, not the *form of God*, but the form of the Son of man *will appear*.

Not that he who gave all judgment to the Son will not judge, since the Son says of him, *there is one who seeks it and judges*; but it was said thus, *the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*, as if it were said, "No one will see the Father in the judgment of the living and the dead, but all will see the Son"; for he is also the Son of man, that he may be seen even by the ungodly, when they too *will look on him whom they have pierced*.

### The vision of the Son of man will be granted even to the wicked, the vision of the form of God only to the pure in heart.

**13. 30**. And lest we seem to conjecture rather than plainly to demonstrate this, let us bring forward a sure and manifest sentence of the same Lord, by which we may show that this was the very reason why he said, *the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son*: because the *judge* will appear in the form of the Son of man, which form is not the Father's but the Son's; nor that of the Son in which he is *equal to the Father*, but that in which he is *less than the Father*; so that in the judgment he may be conspicuous both to the good and to the wicked.

For a little later he says: *amen I say to you, that he who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life, and will not come into judgment, but will pass from death to life*. This *eternal life* is that vision which does not pertain to the wicked.

Then it follows: *amen, amen I say to you, that the hour is coming, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live*. And this is proper to the pious, who so hear of his incarnation that they believe *that he is the Son of God*; that is, they so receive him as having been made for their sake *less than the Father* in the *form of a servant*, that they believe that he is *equal to the Father in the form of God*.

And therefore it follows, and commending this very thing he says: *for as the Father has life in himself, so he gave to the Son also to have life in himself*. Then he comes to the vision of his splendor in which he is to come to judgment, which vision will be common both to the ungodly and to the just. For it follows and he says: *and he gave him power also to execute judgment, because he is the Son of man*.

I think nothing is more manifest. For *because he is the Son of God he is equal to the Father*, he does not receive this power of executing judgment, but has it with the Father in secret; but he receives it so that the good and the wicked may see him judging, *because he is the Son of man*. For the vision of the Son of man will be granted even to the wicked; but the vision of the *form of God* only to the *pure in heart, for they shall see God*; that is, to the pious alone, to whose love he promises this very thing, that he will show himself to them.

And therefore see what follows: *do not marvel at this*,[^103] he says. What forbids us to marvel, except that at which everyone marvels who does not understand—so that he should say that the Father gave *him power and to execute judgment, because he is the Son of man*, when rather it would as it were be expected that he should say, *because he is the Son of God*?

But because the wicked cannot see the Son of God according to that by which he is *equal to the Father in the form of God*; and yet it is necessary that, as the judge *of the living and the dead*, when they are judged before him, both the just and the wicked see him. *Do not*, he says, *marvel at this, for the hour is coming in which all who are in the tombs will hear his voice; and those who have done good will come forth to the resurrection of life; but those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment*.

For this, then, it was necessary that he should receive that power, *because he is the Son of man*, so that all who rise might see him in the form in which he can be seen by all, but *some to damnation, others to eternal life*.[^104] But what *is eternal life* except that vision which is not granted to the ungodly? *That they may know you*, he says, *the one true God, and him whom you have sent, Jesus Christ*. How will they also know *Jesus Christ* himself, except as the *one true God*, who will show himself to them, not as he will show himself even to those to be punished in the form of the Son of man?

### No one is good but God.

**13. 31**. According to that vision he is good, according to which vision God appears to the pure in heart; for, *how good is the God of Israel to the upright in heart*! But when the wicked see the judge, he will not seem good to them; for they will not rejoice in him with their heart, but then *all the tribes of the earth will mourn for themselves*, in the number, that is, of all the wicked and unbelievers. On account of this, to that man too who had called him a good teacher, when he asked of him counsel for attaining eternal life, he answered: *why do you ask me about the good? No one is good but God alone*. Although in another place the Lord himself calls even a man good: *the good man*, he says, *out of the good treasure of his heart brings forth good things; and the evil man out of the evil treasure of his heart brings forth evil things*.

But because that man was seeking eternal life, and eternal life is in that contemplation in which God is seen not unto punishment but unto everlasting joy; and he did not understand with whom he was speaking, since he reckoned him to be only the *Son of man*: *why do you ask me*, he says, *about the good?* That is, "this form which you see, *why do you ask about the good*, and call me, according to what you see, a good teacher? This is the form of the Son of man, this is the form that was assumed, this form will appear in the judgment, not only to the just but also to the ungodly; and the vision of this form will not be for good to those who act wickedly. But there is the vision of my form, in which, when I was in it, I *did not count it robbery to be equal to God*, but, that I might take this form, I *emptied* myself."

That *one God, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit*, therefore, will not appear except unto the joy which will not be taken from the just; for which future joy he sighs who says, *one thing I have asked of the Lord, this will I seek: that I may dwell in the house of the Lord all the days of my life, that I may contemplate the delight of the Lord*; the *one God*, therefore, is himself the *only good*, in this respect, that no one sees him unto grief and mourning, but only *unto salvation* and true gladness. "If you understand me according to that form, I am good; but if according to this alone, *why do you ask me about the good*, if you will be among those who *will look on him whom they have pierced*; and that very vision will be an evil for them, because it will be penal."

That the Lord said, *why do you ask me about the good? No one is good but God alone*, from this consideration, is probable by the proofs I have recalled: namely, that that vision of God by which we shall contemplate the unchangeable substance of God, invisible to human eyes, which is promised to the saints alone, which the apostle Paul calls *face to face*; and of which the apostle John says, *we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*; and of which it is said, *one thing I have asked of the Lord, that I may contemplate the delight of the Lord*; and of which the Lord himself says, *and I will love him, and will show myself to him*; and for the sake of which alone we cleanse our hearts by faith, that we may be *blessed pure in heart, for they shall see God*; and whatever other things have been said of this vision, which anyone finds most abundantly scattered through all the Scriptures who bends the eye of love to seeking it; this alone is our supreme good, for the obtaining of which we are commanded to do whatever we rightly do.

But that vision of the Son of man which was foretold, when all the nations will be gathered before him, and they will say to him, *Lord, when did we see you hungering and thirsting*? and the rest, will be neither a good for the ungodly who will be sent into eternal fire, nor the supreme good for the just. For he still calls them to the kingdom, which has been prepared for them from the beginning of the world. For as he will say to those, *go into eternal fire*; so to these, *come, blessed of my Father, possess the kingdom prepared for you. And as those will go into eternal burning, so the just into eternal life*.

But what is eternal life, except, *that they may know you*, he says, *the one true God, and him whom you have sent, Jesus Christ*? But now in that splendor of which he says to the Father, *which I had with you before the world* was made. For then he *will deliver the kingdom to God and the Father*, that the *good servant* may enter *into the joy of his lord*, and may *hide those* whom God possesses in the *hiding of his countenance from the disturbance of men*—of those, that is, who will then be disturbed when they hear that sentence; at which *evil hearing the just man will not fear*, if only he is protected *in the tabernacle*, that is, in the right faith of the Catholic Church, *from the contradiction of tongues*, that is, from the slanders of heretics.

But if there is another understanding of the words of the Lord by which he says, *why do you ask me about the good? No one is good but God alone*;[^105] provided, however, that it not be believed on this account that the substance of the Father is of greater goodness than that of the Son, according to which he is the *Word through whom all things were made*, and provided it does not recoil from sound doctrine, let us use it without anxiety—not one only, but as many as can be found. For heretics are convicted the more strongly, the more exits lie open for avoiding their snares.
