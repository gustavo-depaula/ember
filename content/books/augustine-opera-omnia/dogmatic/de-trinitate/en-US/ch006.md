# Book IV

## Proem

### Those are better who set knowing themselves above the knowledge of earthly and heavenly things. The essence of God has nothing changeable.

**1.** 1. The human race is wont to set great store by the knowledge of earthly and heavenly things.[^1] In which matter assuredly those are better who set knowing themselves above this knowledge;[^2] and more praiseworthy is the mind to which even its own weakness is known, than the one which, with no regard for that, scrutinizes the courses of the stars—even though it will come to know them—or which already holds them known, while itself ignorant of the way by which it should enter[^3] upon its own salvation and stability.

But one who has now awakened toward God, roused by the warmth of the Holy Spirit, and in his love has become cheap in his own sight; who, wishing to enter to him yet unable, and with God shining upon him attends to himself, and finds himself, and recognizes that his own sickness cannot be reconciled to that purity; finds it sweet to weep, and to beseech him to have mercy again and again until he puts off all his misery, and to pray with confidence, having now received the free pledge of salvation through his only Savior and Enlightener of man;

such a man, thus needy[^4] and grieving, *knowledge* does not *puff up*, because *love builds up*; for he has set one knowledge above another—he has set knowing his own weakness above knowing *the walls of the world*[^5], the foundations of the earth, and the heights of the heavens; and *by adding this knowledge*, he has added *sorrow*—the sorrow of his pilgrimage, from the longing for his homeland and for its founder, his blessed God.[^6]

Among this kind of men, in the family of your Christ,[^7] O Lord my God, if I groan among your poor, grant me from your bread to answer the men who do not *hunger and thirst for righteousness*, but are sated and abound. For their own phantasm has sated them, not your truth, which by rejecting it they recoil from, and fall into their own emptiness. I, for my part, perceive how many fictions the human heart brings forth. And what is my heart but a human heart?

But this I pray of the *God of my heart*, that I may belch forth none of those fictions into these writings as though it were solid truth, but that whatever can come through me into them may come from that source whence, although cast off *from before his eyes* and trying to return from *far away*,[^8] by the way which he has paved with the humanity of the divinity of his Only-begotten, the breath of his truth is sprinkled upon me. This breath, changeable though I am, I drink in just so far as I see nothing changeable in it—neither by places and times, as with bodies; nor by times alone and as it were by places, as with the thoughts of our spirits;[^9] nor by times alone and by no image of places, as with certain reasonings of our minds.

For the essence of God, by which he is, has nothing changeable, neither in eternity, nor in truth, nor in will; because there truth is eternal, love eternal; and there love is true, eternity true; and there eternity is dear, and truth dear.[^10]

### We had to be persuaded how much, and what we were like, God loved us.

**1.** 2. But since we have gone into exile from unchangeable joy, and yet have not been so cut off and broken away from it that we should not seek even in these changeable and temporal things eternity, truth, and blessedness (for we wish neither to die, nor to be deceived, nor to be troubled), there were sent to us divinely the visions suited to our pilgrimage[^11] by which we might be warned that what we seek is not here, but that we must return from this place to there—from which, did we not hang upon it, we would not seek these things here.[^12]

And first we had to be persuaded how much God loved us, lest out of despair we should not dare to be lifted up to him.[^13] But it had to be shown what we were like when he loved us, lest, being proud as though over our own merits, we should recoil from him all the more and fail all the more in our own strength; and through this he dealt with us so that we might rather make progress through his strength, and so the power of love might be perfected in the weakness of humility.

This he signifies in the Psalm, where he says: *setting apart a voluntary rain, O God, for your inheritance, and it was weakened; but you made it perfect*. By a *voluntary rain* he wishes nothing else to be understood than grace, not rendered for merits but given freely, whence it is also named grace; for he gave it, not because we were worthy, but because he willed it. Recognizing this, we will not be trusting in ourselves; and this is to be weakened.

But he himself perfects us, who also said to the apostle Paul: *my grace is sufficient for you; for power is made perfect in weakness*. Therefore man had to be persuaded how much God loved us, and what we were like when he loved us: how much, that we might not despair; what we were like, that we might not be proud.

The Apostle explains this most necessary point thus: *but God*, he says, *commends his love toward us, in that, while we were still sinners, Christ died for us; much more then, being now justified by his blood, shall we be saved from wrath through him. For if, while we were enemies, we were reconciled to God through the death of his Son, much more, being reconciled, shall we be saved by his life*. Likewise in another place: *what then*, he says, *shall we say to these things? If God is for us, who is against us? He who did not spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us all, how has he not also with him given us all things?* But what is announced to us as already done was shown to be future also to the righteous men of old, so that through the same faith they too, being humbled, might be made weak, and being made weak, might be made perfect.

### The Word of God, through whom all things were made, is the light of rational minds.

**1.** 3. Since, therefore, there is one Word of God, *through whom all things were made*, which is unchangeable truth, where[^14] all things are principally and unchangeably together at once—not only those that now are in this whole creation, but also those that "have been" and those that "are to be." But there they have neither been nor are to be, but only are; and all things are life, and all things are one, and rather is it "one," and is one life. For *all things were made through him* in such a way that whatever *was made* in these things *was life in him*; and it was not made, because *in the beginning* the Word was not made, but *the Word was, and the Word was*[^15] *with God, and the Word was God*, and *all things were made through him*; nor would *all things have been made through him*, unless he himself were before all things and were not made.

But among the things that *were made through him*, even a body, which is not life, would not be made through him unless in him, before it was made, it were life. For *what was made* was already *life in him*, and not just any sort of life. For the soul too is the life of the body, but this also was made, because it is changeable; and through what was it made, except through the unchangeable Word of God? For *all things were made through him, and without him was made nothing. What* therefore *was made* was already *life in him*, and not just any sort of life, but *the life was the light of men*—the light, that is, of rational minds, by which men differ from beasts, and on that account are men.[^16] Not, then, corporeal light, which is the light of flesh, whether it shine from heaven or be kindled by earthly fires—and not only of human flesh, but also of the beasts'[^17] flesh, and down to the tiniest little worms; for all these see that light. But that *life was the light of men, and not placed far from each one of us, for in it we live and move and have our being*.

### Through the incarnate Word we are made fit to perceive the Truth.

**2.** 4. But *the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it*. Now the darkness is the foolish minds of men, blinded by depraved desire and unbelief.

That he might cure and heal these, *the Word, through whom all things were made, was made flesh and dwelt among us*. For our enlightenment is a participation in the Word, that is, in that life which *is the light of men*. But for this participation we were altogether unfit and insufficiently suited, because of the uncleanness of our sins. Therefore we had to be cleansed. Now there is one cleansing for the wicked and the proud: the *blood of the Just One*, and the humility of God; so that, to contemplate God, which by nature we are not, we might be cleansed through him who was made what by nature we are, and what by sin we are not. For by nature we are not God; by nature we are men, by sin we are not righteous. And so God, made a righteous man, interceded with God for sinful man. For a sinner is not in keeping with a righteous one, but a man is in keeping with a man. Joining to us, then, the likeness of his humanity, he took away the unlikeness of our iniquity, and made a partaker of our mortality, he made us partakers of his divinity.[^18] Rightly indeed the death of the sinner, coming from the necessity of condemnation, was loosed by the death of the Just One, coming from the will of mercy, while his single matches our double.

For this congruence, or fitting, or concord, or consonance, or whatever may be more suitably called the relation of one to two, has the greatest force in every joining—or, if it is better so called, in the fitting together[^19] of a creation. For I wished to call it this fitting together, as it now occurs to me, which the Greeks call ἁρμονία.[^20] Nor is this now the place to show how much the consonance of the single to the double avails, which is found greatest in us, and is so naturally implanted in us (by whom, indeed, but by him who created us?) that even the untrained cannot fail to perceive it, whether singing themselves or hearing others. For through it the higher and lower notes are in concord, so that whoever sounds out of tune with it gravely offends, not knowledge, of which very many are without, but the very sense of our hearing. But to demonstrate this requires a long discourse; it can, however, be presented to the ears themselves by one who knows how, on a single regulated monochord.

### The single of Jesus Christ chimes with our double unto salvation.

**3.** 5. But what presses upon us at present, so far as God grants, is to set forth how the single of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ corresponds to our double, and in a certain way chimes with it unto salvation. We certainly—as no Christian doubts—are dead both in soul and in body: in soul because of sin, in body because of the punishment of sin, and through this also *in body because of sin*.[^21] But each of these things of ours, that is, both soul and body, had need of healing and of resurrection, so that what had been changed for the worse might be renewed for the better.

Now the death of the soul is ungodliness, and the death of the body is corruptibility, through which there comes also the soul's departure from the body. For just as the soul dies when God forsakes it, so the body dies when the soul forsakes it. Whence the one becomes foolish, the other lifeless. The soul, then, is raised again through repentance, and in the body, still mortal, the renewal of life is begun by faith, by which one believes *in him who justifies the ungodly*, and it is increased and strengthened by good conduct *from day to day*, as more and more *the inner man is renewed*.

But the body, as the *outer man*, the longer this life lasts, is more and more *corrupted*, whether by age, or by disease, or by various afflictions, until it comes to that last one which by all is called death. But its resurrection is deferred to the end, when our justification too will be perfected in an ineffable way.[^22] For then *we shall be like him, because we shall see him as he is*. But now, as long as *the body that is corruptible weighs down the soul*, and: *human life upon earth is wholly a trial, nor is any living man justified in his sight*—in comparison with that righteousness by which we shall be made equal to the angels, and with that glory *which shall be revealed in us*.

But as for distinguishing the death of the soul from the death of the body, why should I recall further proofs, when the Lord, in a single sentence of the Gospel, has set both deaths so that anyone may easily discern them, where he says: *let the dead bury their dead*? For the dead body was to be buried; but its buriers, through the unbelief of ungodliness,[^23] he wished to be understood as dead in soul—the kind who are roused when it is said, *rise, you who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ shall enlighten you*. But the Apostle detests a certain kind of death, saying of the widow, *but she who lives in pleasures is dead while living*. Therefore the soul that is now godly, which had been ungodly, is said, because of the *righteousness of faith*, to have come back to life from death and to live.

But the body, as destined to die not only because of the soul's departure that is to come, but because of the great weakness of flesh and blood, is in a certain place in the Scriptures even called dead, when the Apostle says, *the body indeed*, he says, *is dead because of sin, but the spirit is life because of righteousness*. This life is brought about by faith, since *the righteous lives by faith*.[^24] But what follows? *And if the Spirit of him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, he who raised Jesus Christ from the dead will give life also to your mortal bodies through his Spirit dwelling in you*.

### Our Savior laid out his single death for our twofold death.

**3.** 6. For this twofold death of ours, then, our Savior laid out his single one, and to bring about both our raisings, he set before us in sacrament and example his single one. For he was neither a sinner nor ungodly, that he should have need to be renewed in the inner man as though dead in spirit,[^25] and as though by repenting to be recalled to the life of righteousness; but clothed in mortal flesh, and dying in that alone, rising again in that alone, in that alone he chimed with us for both, since in it there was made the sacrament of the inner man and the example of the outer.

For to the sacrament of our inner man was given that cry which pertains to signifying the death of our soul, not only in the Psalm but also on the cross: *my God, my God, why have you forsaken me?* With this cry the Apostle agrees, saying, *knowing that our old man was crucified together with him, that the body of sin might be done away with, that we may no longer be slaves to sin*. For by the crucifixion of the inner man are understood the pains of repentance, and a certain healthful torment of continence, by which death the death of ungodliness is destroyed, in which death God does not forsake us.

And therefore through such a cross *the body of sin is done away with*, so that we may no longer present our *members as weapons of iniquity for sin*. For the *inner man* too, if indeed he *is renewed from day to day*, is assuredly *old* before he is renewed. For *within* is done what the same Apostle says, *put off the old man, and put on the new*. Which he goes on to expound thus: *wherefore, putting away falsehood, speak the truth*. But where is falsehood put away, if not *within*, that he who *speaks truth in his heart* may dwell *in* God's *holy mountain*?

But the resurrection of the Lord's body is shown to pertain to the sacrament of our inner resurrection, where, after he had risen, he says to the woman, *do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father*. With this mystery the Apostle agrees, saying, *but if you have risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; mind the things that are above*. For this is not to touch Christ until he has ascended to the Father: not to be wise about Christ in a carnal way.[^26]

But now to the example of the death of our outer man[^27] pertains the death of the Lord's flesh, because by such a passion he especially exhorted his servants not to fear *those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul*. On account of which the Apostle says, *that I may supply what is lacking of the afflictions of Christ in my flesh*. And the resurrection of the Lord's body is found to pertain to the example of the resurrection of our outer man, because he says to the disciples, *handle me and see, for a spirit does not have flesh and bones, as you see that I have*. And *one of the disciples*, even touching his scars, cried out, saying, *my Lord and my God!* And since the whole integrity of that flesh appeared, there was demonstrated in it what he had said in exhorting his own, *a hair of your head shall not perish*.

For why at first, *do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to my Father*, and why, before he ascends to the Father, is he touched by the disciples, unless because there the sacrament of the inner man was being intimated, and here the example of the outer was being offered? Or is anyone perhaps so absurd and so averse from the truth as to dare to say that he was touched by men before he ascended, but by women after he had ascended?[^28] On account of this example of our future resurrection in the body, which went before in the Lord, the Apostle says, *Christ the beginning, then those who are Christ's*. For in that passage he was treating of the resurrection of the body, on account of which he also says, *he will transfigure*[^29] *the body of our humility, conformed to the body of his glory*.

Therefore the one death of our Savior was for the salvation of our two deaths. And his one resurrection bestowed on us two resurrections, since his body, in each matter—that is, both in death and in resurrection—was ministered, by a certain medicinal fittingness, both to the sacrament of our inner man and to the example of the outer.

### The ratio of the single to the double from the perfection of the number six.

**4.** 7. Now this ratio of the single to the double arises indeed from the number three; for one to two is three. But all this that I have said reaches as far as six; for one and two and three make six. This number is called perfect for the reason that it is completed by its own parts; for it has three of them—a sixth, a third, a half—and no other part can be found in it that could be said to be a fraction of it. Its sixth, then, is one; its third, two; its half, three. But one and two and three add up to that same six. Its perfection holy Scripture commends to us, especially in this, that *God in six days perfected his works*,[^30] and on the sixth day *man was made to the image of God*.[^31] And in the sixth age of the human race, *the Son of God came* and was made *the Son of man*, that he might reform us *to the image of God*.[^32] For it is this age that is now in course, whether we distribute a thousand years to each age, or trace in the divine writings memorable and notable points of the times as it were as joints, so that the first age is found from Adam to Noah, then the second to Abraham, and thereafter, as the Evangelist Matthew distinguished, *from Abraham to David, from David to the deportation to Babylon*,[^33] and from there to the Virgin's childbearing. These three ages, joined to those two, make five. Accordingly the birth of the Lord began the sixth, which is now in course until the hidden end of time.

That this number six bears a certain figure of time, we recognize also in that division into three parts, by which we reckon one time *before the Law*, another *under the Law*, a third *under grace*.[^34] In this time we receive the sacrament of renewal, so that at the end of time, renewed in every part even by the resurrection of the flesh, we may be healed of every weakness, not only of soul but also of body.[^35] Hence is understood that woman, as a type of the Church, healed and set upright by the Lord, whom an infirmity had bent over while Satan bound her;[^36] for it is of such hidden enemies that the voice of the Psalm laments, *they have bent down my soul*. Now this woman had been eighteen years in her infirmity, which is three sixes.[^37] But the months of eighteen years are found in the number of the solid square of six, which is six times six, and this six times. For nearby, in the same place of the Gospel, is also that fig tree, whose wretched barrenness even a third year reproached. But intercession was made for it, that it be left that year, so that if it bore fruit, well; but if not, that it be cut down.[^38] For the three years too pertain to the same threefold division,[^39] and the months of three years make the square of six, which is six times six.

### In the course of a year the number six avails greatly.

**4.** 8. A single year too, if its twelve whole months be considered, which thirty days complete (for the ancients observed such a month, which the lunar circuit shows), is strong in the number six. For what six avails in the first rank of numbers, which consists of units up to ten, that sixty avails in the second rank, which consists of tens up to a hundred. The number of sixty days, then, is a sixth part of the year. Accordingly it is multiplied by the six of the first row, as it were the six of the second row, and there come six times sixty, three hundred and sixty days, which are twelve whole months.

But since, just as the circuit of the moon shows men the month, so the year is observed by the circuit of the sun, there remain five days and a quarter of a day for the sun to complete its course and conclude the year. For four quarters make one day, which must needs be intercalated when a four-year period has run out, which they call the bissextile,[^40] lest the order of the seasons be disturbed. Even in those five days and a quarter themselves, if we consider them, the number six avails greatly. First, because, as it usually happens that the whole is reckoned from a part, they are now not five days but rather six, the quarter being taken for a day; next, because in those five days themselves there is a sixth part of a month, while the quarter itself has six hours. For a whole day, that is, with its night, is twenty-four hours, of which the fourth part, which is a quarter of a day, is found to be six hours. Thus in the course of a year the number six avails greatly.

### The number six in the building of the body of Christ.

**5.** 9. And not without reason is the number six itself, set for a year, understood in the building of the Lord's body, in figure of which he said that the temple destroyed by the Jews he would raise up in three days. For they said, *this temple was built in forty-six years*. And forty-six times six make two hundred and seventy-six. This number of days completes nine months and six days, which are reckoned as ten months for women giving birth—not because all come to the sixth day after the ninth month, but because the very perfecting of the Lord's body is found to have been brought to birth in just so many days, as the authority of the Church, receiving it handed down by our forefathers, keeps. For he is believed to have been conceived on the eighth day before the Kalends of April, on which he also suffered. Thus to the *new tomb* in which he was buried, *where no one of the dead had been laid*[^41]—neither before nor afterward—corresponds the Virgin's womb in which he was conceived, where no one of mortals was sown.[^42] But he is recorded to have been born on the eighth day before the Kalends of January. Reckoned, then, from that day to this, two hundred and seventy-six days are found, which contain the number six forty-six times. By which number of years *the temple was built*, because by that number of sixes the body of the Lord was perfected, which, destroyed by the suffering of death, he raised up in three days. *For he said this of the temple of his body*, as is declared by the most evident and most robust testimony of the Gospel, in which he says, *as Jonah was in the belly of the whale three days and three nights, so shall the Son of man be in the heart of the earth three days and three nights*.

### The three days of the resurrection, in which the ratio of the single to the double also appears.

**6.** 10.[^43] But that those three days themselves were not whole and complete, Scripture is witness. But the first day was reckoned whole from its last part, while the third day was reckoned from its first part, and it too whole; the one in the middle between them, however, that is, the second day, was absolutely whole with its twenty-four hours, twelve nocturnal and twelve diurnal.

For he was first crucified by the voices of the Jews at the *third hour*, when it was the sixth day of the week;[^44] then he was hung upon the cross itself at the *sixth hour*, and gave up his spirit at the *ninth hour*;[^45] but he was buried *when it was already late*,[^46] as the words of the Gospel have it, which is understood as at the end of the day.

Wherever, then, you may begin—even if another account can be given of how it is not against the Gospel of John, so that he be understood to have been hung on the wood at the third hour[^47]—you do not include the whole first day. Therefore it will be reckoned whole from its last part, as the third from its first part. For the night up to the dawn, in which the Lord's resurrection was declared, pertains to the third day; *because God, who said that light should shine out of darkness*, that through the grace of the New Testament and a participation in Christ's resurrection we might hear, *for you were once darkness, but now you are light in the Lord*, intimates to us in a certain way that the day takes its beginning from night.

For just as the first days, on account of man's future fall, are reckoned from light to night,[^48] so these, on account of man's restoration, are reckoned from darkness to light. From the hour of his death, then, to the dawn of the resurrection there are forty hours, counting in that ninth hour itself. With which number agrees also his life upon earth after the resurrection, in forty days. And this number is most frequent in the Scriptures for intimating the mystery of perfection in the four-part world. For ten has a certain perfection, and multiplied four times it makes forty.

But from the evening of the burial to the dawn of the resurrection there are thirty-six hours, which is the square of six. And it is referred to that ratio of the single to the double, where there is the greatest consonance of "fitting together." For twelve agrees with twenty-four as the single with the double, and they make thirty-six—a whole night with a whole day and a whole night; nor is this without that sacrament which I mentioned above.[^49] For not absurdly do we compare the spirit to day, but the body to night. For the Lord's body in death and resurrection bore both a figure of our spirit and an example of our body. So in this way too that ratio of the single to the double appears in the thirty-six hours, when twelve are compared with twenty-four.

And of these numbers, indeed, why they are set in the holy Scriptures, one person may trace out some causes, another others—causes either to be preferred to those I have given, or equally probable, or even more probable than these. Yet that they are set in the Scriptures in vain, and that there are no mystical causes why these numbers are recorded there, no one so foolish and inept would contend. But the causes I have given I have gathered either from the authority of the Church handed down by our forefathers, or from the testimony of the divine Scriptures, or from the rationale of the numbers and likenesses. Against reason let no sober person think, against the Scriptures let no Christian, against the Church let no peaceable person.

### Having vanished away into many things, we are gathered into One through the one Mediator.

**7.** 11. This sacrament, this sacrifice, this priest, this God, before he came as one sent, *made of a woman*—all the things that appeared in a sacred and mystical way to our fathers through angelic miracles, or that were done through them,[^50] were likenesses of him, so that every creature in a certain way spoke by its deeds of the one who was to come, in whom would be the salvation of all who were to be restored from death.

For because, recoiling and sounding out of tune through the iniquity of ungodliness, we had flowed away from the one true and most high God, and had vanished away into many things, torn asunder through many things and clinging to many things, it was fitting, by the nod and command of the merciful God, that the very many things should cry aloud the one who was to come, and that the one who was cried aloud by the many should come, and that the many should testify that the one had come, and that, unburdened of the many, we should come to the one;

and that we, *dead* in soul through many *sins*, and destined to die in flesh *because of sin*, might love the one who without sin died in the flesh for us, and, believing in him raised again, and rising again with him in spirit *through faith*, might be justified in the one righteous one,[^51] made one; and might not despair of rising again in the flesh itself,[^52] since we behold many *members* have gone before us as *one head*, in whom, *now* cleansed *through faith*, and *then* restored whole *through sight*,[^53] and reconciled to God through the Mediator, we may cleave to the One,[^54] enjoy the One, remain One.

### That they may be one in us.

**8.** 12. Thus the *Son of God* himself, *the Word of God, and the same one the Mediator of God and men, the Son of man, equal to the Father*[^55] through the unity of divinity, and our partner through the assumption of humanity, interceding with the Father for us through that by which he was man, yet not keeping silent about the fact that as God he was one with the Father, speaks among other things thus: *but I pray not for these*, he says, *only, but also for those who will believe in me through their word, that they all may be one, as you, Father, are in me, and I in you, that they too may be one in us; that the world may believe that you sent me. And the glory which you gave me I have given them, that they may be one, as we also are one*.

### One also through the same fellowship of love.

**9.** 12. He did not say, "I and they are one," although, in respect of the fact that he *is the head of the Church and the Church his body*,[^56] he could have said, "I and they," not "one thing," but "one person," because the *head* and the *body are one Christ*.[^57] But showing his divinity consubstantial with the Father (on account of which he also says in another place, *I and the Father are one*), in its own kind, that is, in the consubstantial parity of the same nature, he wishes his own to be *one*, but in himself; because in themselves they could not be one, sundered from one another through diverse wills[^58] and desires and the uncleanness of sins; whence they are cleansed through the Mediator, *that they may be one* in him; not only through the same nature by which all from mortal men *are made equal to the angels*,[^59] but also through the same will, conspiring most harmoniously toward the same blessedness, fused as it were into one spirit by the fire of love.[^60]

For to this avails what he says, *that they may be one, as we also are one*: that just as the Father and the Son are *one* not only by equality of substance but also by will, so these too, between whom and God the Son is *Mediator*, may be *one* not only through the fact that they are of the same nature, but also through the same fellowship of love. Then that he himself is the *Mediator* through whom we are reconciled to God, he indicates thus: *I*, he says, *in them, and you in me, that they may be perfected into one*.

### The devil held man subject.

**10.** 13. This is true peace, and a firm connection with our Creator for us, purified and reconciled through the Mediator of life, just as, stained and estranged, we had withdrawn from him through the mediator of death. For just as the proud devil led proud man down to death, so the humble Christ led obedient man back to life. Because, just as that one, being exalted, fell and cast down the one who consented to him, so this one, being humbled, rose and raised up the one who believed.[^61]

For because the devil had not reached where he himself had led man (for he bore the death of the spirit in ungodliness, but had not undergone the death of the flesh, since he had not taken on any garment of flesh), he seemed to man a great prince among the legions of demons, through whom he wields his kingdom of deceptions. Thus, puffing man up the more through the smoke of self-exaltation—more eager for power than for righteousness—either through false philosophy, or ensnaring him through sacrilegious rites, in which there are also magical deceptions, casting headlong the more curious and proud souls, deceived and deluded, he holds him subject;[^62]

promising even a purification of the soul through what they call τελεταί,[^63] by transfiguring *himself into an angel of light* through a manifold contrivance *in lying signs and wonders*.

### The miracles of demons are to be scorned.

**11.** 14. For it is easy for the most wicked spirits to do, through their airy bodies, many things that souls weighed down by earthly bodies marvel at, even souls of better disposition.[^64] For if those very earthly bodies, modified by certain arts and exercises, exhibit to men in theatrical spectacles such marvels that those who have never seen such things scarcely believe them when told, what great matter is it for the devil and his angels, out of the corporeal elements through their airy bodies, to do things that the flesh marvels at, or even, by hidden inspirations, to contrive phantasms of images for deluding human senses, by which he may deceive men waking or sleeping, or drive the frenzied to and fro?

But just as it can happen that a man better in life and conduct watches most wicked men walking on a rope, or doing many incredible things by manifold movements of their bodies, and in no way desires to do such things, nor on that account thinks them to be preferred to himself, so the faithful and godly soul, even if it sees, and even if, on account of the frailty of the flesh, it shudders at the miracles of demons, will not on that account either grieve that it cannot do such things, or judge them to be better on this account. Especially since it is in the fellowship of the saints, who through the power of God, to whom all things are subject, have done things in no way deceptive and far greater—whether men or good angels.[^65]

### The devil the mediator of death.

**12.** 15. By no means, therefore, are souls purified and reconciled to God through sacrilegious likenesses and ungodly curiosities and magical consecrations; for the false mediator does not carry one across to the higher things, but rather, besieging, blocks the way through the affections, which he inspires the more malignant the prouder his fellowship is. These cannot nourish the wings of the virtues for flying up, but rather heap up the weights of the vices for sinking down, the more heavily as the soul, doomed to fall, seems to itself to be borne up the more loftily.

Accordingly, just as the Magi did when divinely warned—whom a star led to adore the humility of the Lord—so we too ought to return to our homeland not by the way we came, but *by another way*, which the humble King taught, and which the proud king, the adversary of the humble King, cannot besiege. For to us also, that we may adore the humble Christ, the heavens have declared the glory of God, when *their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*.

There was a way for us to death through sin in Adam; for *through one man sin entered into the world, and through sin death, and so it passed to all men, in whom all sinned*. The mediator of this way was the devil, the persuader to sin and the one who casts headlong into death;[^66] for he too, to work our twofold death, brought his single one. For through ungodliness he died in spirit, though he certainly did not die in flesh; but to us he both persuaded ungodliness, and through this brought it about that we should deserve to come to the death of the flesh. The one, then, we sought through his wicked persuasion; the other followed us through just condemnation.

For this reason indeed it is written, *God did not make death*; because he was not himself the cause of death, yet through his retribution a most just death was inflicted on the sinner. Just as a judge inflicts punishment on a guilty man, yet the cause of the punishment is not the justice of the judge, but the desert of the crime. Where, then, the mediator of death sent us across, and where he himself did not come—that is, to the death of the flesh—there the Lord our God inserted for us the medicine of amendment, which that one did not deserve, by a hidden and exceedingly secret ordering of divine and lofty justice.

So that, just as *through one man came death, so through one man* might come *the resurrection of the dead*—because men shunned more what they could not escape, the death of the flesh, than the death of the spirit; that is, more the punishment than the desert of punishment (for not to sin is either not cared for, or little cared for, but not to die, though it cannot be obtained, is vehemently striven after)—the Mediator of life, showing how little death is to be feared, which through the human condition can now no longer be evaded, but rather ungodliness, which through faith can be guarded against, met us at the end to which we came, but not by the way we came. For we came to death through sin, he through righteousness; and therefore, since our death is the punishment of sin, his death was made a sacrifice for sin.[^67]

### Christ died because he willed it.

**13.** 16. Wherefore, since the spirit is set above the body, and the death of the spirit is to be forsaken by God, but the death of the body is to be forsaken by the spirit, and this is the punishment in the death of the body, that the spirit, because it willingly forsook God, forsakes the body unwillingly; so that, when the spirit has forsaken God because it willed it, it forsakes the body even though it would not; and does not forsake it when it would, unless it has brought upon itself some force by which the body itself is destroyed—the spirit of the Mediator demonstrated how no punishment of sin reached him even to the death of the flesh, because he did not forsake it unwillingly, but because he willed it, when he willed it, in the way he willed it.

Indeed, compounded into unity with the Word of God,[^68] he says of this, *I have power to lay down my soul, and I have power to take it up again*. *No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of myself, and I take it up again*. And at this above all those who were present marveled, as the Gospel tells, when, after that cry in which he uttered the figure of our sin, he straightway *gave up his spirit*.[^69] For those hung upon the wood were tormented by a long death. Whence the legs of the thieves were broken, that they might now die and be taken down from the wood before the Sabbath. But he, because he was found already dead, was a marvel.[^70] This too we read that Pilate marveled at, when the body of the Lord was asked of him for burial.[^71]

### The true Mediator of life cast the mediator of death out from the spirits of those who believe in him.

**13.** 17. That deceiver, then, who was man's mediator to death, and falsely sets himself against him for life under the name of purification through sacrilegious rites and sacrifices, by which the proud are seduced—because he could have neither a participation in our death nor a resurrection of his own—was able indeed to bring his single death against our double; but a single resurrection, in which there would be both a sacrament of our renewal and an example of that awakening which is to be at the end, this assuredly he could not. He, accordingly, who, *living in spirit*, raised up his own dead flesh, the true Mediator of life, cast that one, dead in spirit and the mediator of death, out from the spirits of those who believe in him, so that he might not reign within, but might assail from without, yet not overcome.

To whom he also offered himself to be tempted, that he might be Mediator for overcoming his temptations too, not only by help but also by example. But that one, when at first, striving to creep in through all the approaches to the interior, he was driven out, after the baptism, in the desert, when every alluring temptation had been completed—because, dead in spirit, he did not invade the living spirit[^72]—greedy in whatever way for human death, turned himself to bring about the death he could, and was permitted into that mortal which the living Mediator had taken from us. And where he could do something, there he was utterly vanquished on every side; and from where he received outwardly the power of killing the Lord's flesh, thence his inner power, by which he held us, was slain.[^73]

For it came about that the bonds of the many sins of many men in their many deaths were loosed through the one death of the one whom no sin had gone before.[^74] Which death the Lord on this account paid for us, though not owed, so that the death owed by us might not harm us. For he was not stripped of his flesh by the right of anyone's power, but he stripped himself.[^75] For he who could not die if he would not, beyond doubt died because he willed it; and therefore *he made an example of the principalities and powers, confidently triumphing over them in himself*.

For by his death, the one most true sacrifice offered for us, he purified, abolished, extinguished whatever faults there were on account of which the principalities and powers by right held us fast to pay the penalties; and by his resurrection he *called* us, *predestined* to a new life, *and those whom he called he justified, and those whom he justified he glorified*.[^76]

Thus the devil lost, in the very death of the flesh, the man whom, seduced through consent, he possessed as though by full right—he who, himself hedged about by no corruption of flesh and blood, lorded it through this frailty of the mortal body over one exceedingly needy and weak, the prouder for being as it were richer and stronger, over one as it were ragged and wretched. For where he drove on the falling sinner whom he did not follow, thither he followed and compelled the descending Redeemer.

Thus in the fellowship of death the Son of God deigned to become our friend, where the enemy, by not arriving, thought himself better and greater than we. For our Redeemer says: *greater love has no one than this, that he lay down his soul for his friends*. Wherefore the devil believed himself superior even to the Lord himself, insofar as the Lord yielded to him in his sufferings; for of him too was understood what is read in the Psalm, *you made him a little less than the angels*: so that, while the wicked one acted against us as though by equal right, the innocent one, being slain, might overcome him by a most equitable right, and so might lead captive the captivity made on account of sin, and free us from the captivity that was just on account of sin, by his just blood unjustly shed *blotting out the bond* of death, and redeeming sinners to be justified.

### The most exalted wisdom of God uses the devil for the salvation of his faithful.

**13.** 18. Here too the devil still deludes his own, to whom, as if to be purified—but rather to be entangled and submerged—the false mediator sets himself in opposition through his rites, in that he most easily persuades the proud to mock and despise the death of Christ, from which the more estranged he himself is, the more he is believed by them to be holy and divine. Yet very few have remained with him, since the nations now acknowledge and with godly humility drink their price, and in confidence in it forsake their enemy and run together to their Redeemer.

For the devil does not know how the most exalted wisdom of God uses him, both lying in wait and raging, for the salvation of his faithful, *reaching mightily from the higher end*, which is the beginning of spiritual creation, *to the lower end*, which is the death of the body, *and ordering all things sweetly. For it attains everywhere because of its purity, and nothing defiled enters into it*. But for the devil, estranged from the death of the flesh, on which account he walks exceedingly proud, a death of another kind is prepared in the eternal fire of Tartarus, by which spirits can be tormented not only with earthly but also with airy bodies.

But proud men, to whom Christ has become cheap because he died—where he bought us at so great a price[^77]—both render that death back, along with men, to the wretched condition of a nature that is drawn from the first sin, and will be cast headlong into that condition together with him. Him they have preferred to Christ for this reason, that he cast them down into this state, into which he himself, through his distant nature, did not fall, and into which Christ, on their account, through his immense mercy, descended; and yet they do not hesitate to believe that they are better than the demons, and they do not cease to assail and detest with every curse those whom they certainly know to be estranged from the suffering of this death—on account of which they despise Christ.

Nor are they willing to consider in this way how it could come about that the Word of God, remaining in itself and in no part changeable through itself, could nonetheless, through the assumption of a lower nature, suffer something lower, which an unclean demon, because it does not have an earthly body, cannot suffer. Thus, although they themselves are better than the demons, yet because they bear flesh, they can die in the way that demons, because they do not bear it, certainly cannot die.

And although they presume much upon the deaths of their sacrifices, which they do not perceive that they are immolating to deceitful and proud spirits—or if they now do perceive it, they suppose that the friendship of the treacherous and envious profits them something, whose intention has no business but the hindering of our return.[^78]

### The one Mediator reconciles us to God through a sacrifice of peace.

**14.** 19. They do not understand that not even those most proud spirits themselves could have rejoiced in the honors of sacrifices, unless a true sacrifice were owed to the one true God, for whom they wish to be worshiped. Nor can it be duly offered except through a holy and righteous priest; nor unless what is offered be accepted from those for whom it is offered, and that without fault, so that it can be offered for the cleansing of the faulty. This certainly all desire who wish a sacrifice to be offered to God for themselves.

Who, then, is so righteous and holy a priest as the only Son of God, who had no need to purify his own sins by sacrifice[^79]—neither original ones nor those that are added from human life? And what could be taken from men so fittingly to be offered for them as human flesh? And what is so apt for this immolation as mortal flesh? And what so clean for the cleansing of the faults of mortals as flesh, born in a virgin's womb and from a virgin's womb without any contagion of carnal desire? And what could be so gratefully offered and accepted as the flesh of our sacrifice, made the body of our priest? So that—since four things are considered in every sacrifice: to whom it is offered, by whom it is offered, what is offered, for whom it is offered—the same one and true Mediator, reconciling us to God through a sacrifice of peace,[^80] might remain one with him to whom he was offering, might make one in himself those for whom he was offering, might himself be one who was offering and what he was offering.

### Those who think that they can be purified to contemplate God by their own power, pride itself most stains.

**15.** 20. But there are some who think that they can be purified by their own power to contemplate God and to cleave to God, whom pride itself most stains. For there is no vice that is more resisted by the divine Law, and against which that most proud spirit—the mediator to the depths, the blocker to the heights—receives a greater right of lording it: unless either, secretly lying in wait, he be evaded by another way, or, through the "failing people," which is interpreted Amalek, openly raging and denying passage by fighting against the way to the land of promise, he be overcome through the cross of the Lord, which was prefigured by the outstretched hands of Moses.[^81]

For these promise themselves a purification by their own power because some of them have been able to send the edge of their mind beyond every creature, and to touch, in however small a part, the light of unchangeable truth; at which they deride the fact that many Christians, *living* for the time being by faith alone, have not yet been able to do so.[^82] But what does it profit one who is proud, and on this account ashamed to mount the wood, to gaze from afar upon the homeland across the sea?[^83] Or what does it harm the humble man not to see it across so great an interval, when he comes to it on that wood by which the other disdains to be carried?

### The philosophers could not see in the eternal reasons the things that pertain to history.

**16.** 21. These also reprehend us for believing the resurrection of the flesh, and wish themselves rather to be believed even about these matters; as though, because they were able to understand the most lofty and unchangeable substance through the things that were made, they were on that account to be consulted about the turning of changeable things, or about the interwoven order of the ages. For because they argue most truly, and persuade by most certain proofs, that all temporal things come to be by eternal reasons, were they on that account able to perceive in those very reasons, or to gather from them, how many kinds of animals there are, what the seeds of each are at their beginnings, what their measure in growth, what numbers run through conceptions, through births, through ages, through deaths, what motions there are in seeking the things that are according to nature and fleeing their contraries? Did they not seek out all these things, not through that unchangeable wisdom, but through the history of places and times, and believe them as experienced and recorded by others?

So much the less is it to be wondered at that they were in no way able to trace out the series of the more extended ages, and a certain goal of this course by which, as by a river, the human race runs down, and then the turning of each to its own due end.

For these things neither could the historians write—things far in the future and experienced and narrated by no one—nor did these philosophers, better than the rest, contemplate such things by understanding in those highest and eternal reasons; otherwise they would not inquire into past things of the same kind which the historians were able to, but would rather also foreknow future things; and those who could do this were called by them seers, by ours prophets. Although the name of prophets too is not altogether foreign to their writings.

### The foreknowledge of things to come.

**17.** 22. But it makes a great difference whether future things are conjectured from experience of past ones—as physicians, by foreseeing many things, have even committed to writing what they themselves had noted from experience; as, in short, farmers or even sailors foretell many things (for such things, if they happen at long intervals of time, are thought to be divinations);

or whether things now to come have gone before, and, seen from afar as coming, are announced according to the keen sense of those who see—which, when the airy powers do it, they are believed to divine; as if someone from a mountaintop should see someone coming from afar, and announce it beforehand to those dwelling nearby on the plain;

or whether they are foretold by the holy angels, to whom God indicates them through his Word and Wisdom, where both future and past things stand—whether they be foretold to certain men, or, heard from the angels, be in turn passed on to other men;

or whether the minds of certain men themselves are so far raised up by the Holy Spirit that they behold, not through angels but through themselves, the standing causes of future things in the very highest citadel of things. For the airy powers also hear these things, whether the angels announce them, or men; and they hear only so much as he judges to be needful, to whom all things are subject.

Many things too are foretold by a certain instinct and impulse of spirit by those who do not know: as *Caiaphas* did not know what he said, but, *being high priest, he prophesied*.

### About the successions of the ages and about the resurrection of the dead the philosophers are not to be consulted.

**17.** 23. Therefore about the successions of the ages and about the resurrection of the dead we ought not to consult even those philosophers who understood, so far as they were able, the eternity of the Creator *in whom we live, move, and have our being*, *because, knowing God through the things that were made, they did not glorify him as God, nor give thanks, but, saying that they were wise, they were made fools*.

And since they were not fit to fix the edge of their mind so steadily upon the eternity of the spiritual and unchangeable nature as to see, in the very wisdom of the Creator and Ruler of the universe, the volumes of the ages, which were already there and always were, but here were future so as not yet to be; and as to see there the conversions to the better, not only of souls but also of human bodies, even to the perfection of their proper measure;

since, then, they were in no way fit to see these things there, they were not even held worthy that these things should be announced to them through the holy angels—whether outwardly through the senses of the body, or by inner revelations expressed in the spirit; as these things were demonstrated to our fathers, endowed with true piety, who, foretelling them, by making good faith—through present signs or through near things, which came to pass just as they had foretold—deserved to have authority for being believed about things far in the future, even to the end of the age.

But the proud and deceitful airy powers, even if they are found to have said through their seers certain things about the fellowship and city of the saints and about the true Mediator, heard from the holy prophets or angels, did this in order that, through these alien truths, they might lead astray, if they could, even the faithful of God to their own falsehoods.[^84] But God, through those who did not know, brought it about that the truth should resound on every side, for the help of the faithful, for the testimony against the ungodly.

### The Son of God came and himself took up into himself our faith, by which he might lead us to his truth.

**18.** 24. Since, therefore, we were not fit to lay hold of eternal things, and the filth of sins weighed us down—filth contracted by the love of temporal things, and as it were naturally ingrained from the propagation of mortality—we had to be purified. But we could not be purified, so as to be attuned to eternal things, except through temporal things, by which, attuned to them, we were already held. For health differs greatly from disease; but the intermediate cure, unless it accords with the disease, does not lead to health. Useless temporal things deceive the sick; useful temporal things take up those to be healed, and carry across to eternal things those who are healed.

But the rational mind, just as, when purified, it owes contemplation to eternal things, so, when to be purified, it owes faith to temporal things. One even of those who once among the Greeks were held to be wise said: *as much as eternity avails for that which has arisen, so much does truth avail for faith*.[^85] And assuredly it is a true saying. For what we call temporal, he called *that which has arisen*. Of which kind we too are, not only according to the body, but also according to the changeableness of the mind. For that is not properly called eternal which is in some part changed. In so far, then, as we are changeable, in so far do we stand apart from eternity.

But eternal life is promised us through truth, from whose clarity our faith again stands as far apart as mortality from eternity. Now, therefore, we apply faith to things done in time for our sake, and through it we are cleansed;[^86] so that when we come to sight, just as truth succeeds to faith, so eternity may succeed to mortality. Wherefore, since our faith will become truth when we have come to that which is promised to us who believe—and eternal life is promised to us; and the Truth said, not that which will come to be as our faith is future, but which always is Truth, because there is eternity—the Truth, then, said, *but this is eternal life, that they may know you the one true God, and Jesus Christ whom you have sent*; when our faith, by seeing, becomes truth, then eternity will hold our mortality changed.[^87]

Until this comes about, and that it may come about, since we accommodate the faith of believing to things that have arisen, just as in eternal things we hope for the truth of contemplation, lest the faith of mortal life should sound out of tune with the truth of eternal life, the very Truth, coeternal with the Father, *arose from the earth*, when the Son of God came so as to become the son of man, and himself took up into himself our faith, by which he might lead us to his truth—he who so took up our mortality that he did not lose his eternity. For *as much as eternity avails for that which has arisen, so much does truth avail for faith*.[^88]

Thus, then, we had to be purified in such a way that he should become for us "one who has arisen" who yet remained eternal, lest there be for us one in faith and another in truth. Nor could we pass from that by which we have arisen to eternal things, unless, the eternal one being joined to us through our arising, we were carried across to his eternity.

Now, therefore, our faith has in a certain way followed thither, whither he ascended in whom we have believed: "arisen," dead, raised again, taken up. Of these four, the two former we knew in ourselves; for we know that men both arise and die: but the two remaining, that is, to be raised again and to be taken up, we justly hope will be in us, because we have believed they were done in him. And so, in him, because that which had arisen also passed over to eternity, our own will pass over too, when faith has reached truth.

For to those now believing, that they might remain in the word of faith, and from there be led to truth, and through this to eternity, and be freed from death, he speaks thus: *if you remain in my word, you are truly my disciples*.[^89] And as though they were asking, "with what fruit?" he goes on to say: *and you shall know the truth*. And again, as though they were saying, "what does truth profit mortals?" *and the truth*, he says, *shall set you free*. From what, if not from death, from corruption, from changeableness? For truth remains immortal, incorrupt, unchangeable. But true immortality, true incorruptibility, true unchangeableness, this is eternity itself.[^90]

### The testimonies of the Son's mission.

**19.** 25. Behold that for which the Son of God was sent; nay rather, behold what it is for the Son of God to have been sent. Whatever things, for the producing of the faith by which we might be cleansed to contemplate the truth, were done in time among things "arisen"—brought forth from eternity and referred back to eternity—were either testimonies of this mission, or the very mission of the Son of God.

But some testimonies foretold that he would come, some bore witness that he had come. For it was fitting that he through whom every creature was made, made a creature, should have every creature as witness. For unless the one were proclaimed by the sending of many, the one would not be held fast by the dismissing of many.[^91] And unless the testimonies were such that to the little they would seem great, it would not be believed that the great one, sent little to the little, might make them great.

For incomparably greater works of the Son of God are *heaven and earth and all things that are in them, made*, since *all things were made through him*, than the signs and wonders that burst forth in testimony of him. But yet men, that they might, being little, believe these great things to have been made through him, trembled at those little things as though they were great.

### Christ made manifest as less than the Father, unknown as equal to the Father.

**19.** 26. *When*, therefore, *the fullness of time came, God sent his Son, made of a woman, made under the Law*; so far little, that he was *made*; sent, therefore, in that he was *made*. If, then, the greater sends the lesser, we too confess that he was made lesser, and lesser in so far as he was made, and made in so far as he was sent. For *he sent his Son made of a woman*, through whom nonetheless, because *all things were made*, not only before he was sent as made, but before all things were, we confess the same one equal to the sender, whom we call lesser as sent.

How, then, before that *fullness of time* in which he had to be sent, before he was sent, could he be seen by the fathers, when certain angelic visions were shown to them,[^92] when not even, now sent, was he seen as he is equal to the Father?

For why does he say to Philip, by whom certainly, as by the rest, and by the very men by whom he was crucified, he was seen in the flesh, *so long a time I am with you, and you have not known me? Philip, he who has seen me has seen the Father also*—unless because he was seen, and was not seen? He was seen as he had been made, sent; he was not seen as *all things were made through him*.

Or why does he also say that, *he who has my commandments and keeps them, he it is who loves me; and he who loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will love him, and will manifest myself to him*—when he was manifest before the eyes of men—unless because he was holding out to our faith the flesh, which the Word had become in the fullness of time,[^93] to be taken up,[^94] but was keeping the *Word himself, through whom all things were made*, to be contemplated in eternity by the mind purified through faith?

### The Son consubstantial with the Father, and yet sent.

**20.** 27. But if the Son is said to be sent by the Father for this reason, that the one is the Father, the other the Son, it in no way hinders us from believing that the Son is equal to the Father and consubstantial and coeternal, and yet that the Son was sent by the Father. Not because the one is greater, the other lesser, but because the one is Father, the other Son; the one Begetter, the other Begotten; the one he from whom is he who is sent, the other he who is from him who sends. For the Son is from the Father, not the Father from the Son.

According to this it can now be understood that the Son is said to be sent not only because *the Word was made flesh*, but because he was sent that the Word might be made flesh, and through his bodily presence might work the things that are written; that is, that not only is the man understood to be sent, which the Word became, but also the Word sent that he might become man: because he was not sent according to an unequal power or substance, or anything in him that is not equal to the Father, but according to that by which the Son is from the Father, not the Father from the Son.

For the Son is the Word of the Father, who is also called his Wisdom. What wonder, then, if he is sent, not because he is unequal to the Father, but because he *is a certain pure emanation of the glory of almighty God*? But there what emanates and that from which it emanates is of one and the same substance. For it does not emanate as water from a hole in the earth or a rock, but as light from light.

For what was said, *for it is the brightness of eternal light*—what else was said than, It is the light *of eternal light*? For the brightness of light, what is it but light? And therefore coeternal with the light of which it is the light. But he preferred to say, *the brightness of light*, rather than "Light of light," lest this that emanates be thought darker than that from which it emanates. For when one hears that this is its *brightness*, it is easier that through this one believe that to shine, than that this one shine less.

But because there was no need to take care lest that light which begot this one be thought lesser (for this no heretic ever dared to say, nor is it to be believed that anyone will dare), Scripture met that thought by which this light that emanates might seem darker than that from which it emanates: which suspicion it removed when it said, *it is the brightness of that*, that is, *of eternal light*; and so it showed it to be equal. For if this is lesser, it is the darkness of that, not the *brightness* of that. But if it is greater, it does not emanate from that: for it would not surpass that from which it was begotten. Because, then, it emanates from that, it is not greater than that; but because it is not the darkness of that, but the *brightness* of that, it is not lesser; it is therefore equal.

Nor ought this to disturb us, that it was called *a certain pure emanation of the glory of almighty God*: as though it were not itself almighty, but the emanation of the Almighty. For soon it is said of it, *and being one, it can do all things*. But who is almighty except him who can do all things? From him, therefore, it is sent, from whom it emanates. For thus it is sought from him by the one who loved it and desired it. *Send it forth*, he says, *from your holy heavens, and send it from the seat of your greatness, that it may be with me and labor with me*—that is, "let it teach me to labor, lest I labor."[^95] For its labors are virtues. But it is sent in one way that it may be with man, in another it was sent that it may itself be man. For *it transfers itself into holy souls, and makes them friends of God and prophets*, just as it also fills the holy angels, and works through them all things suited to such ministries. *But when the fullness of time came*, it was sent, not to fill angels, nor to be an angel, except insofar as it announced the counsel of the Father,[^96] which was also its own; nor to be with men or in men, for this it had been even before in the fathers and prophets; but that the Word itself might become flesh, that is, might become man: in whom, this future sacrament being revealed, there should be salvation even of those wise and holy ones who, before he himself was born of the Virgin, were born of women; and in whom, this being done and proclaimed, there is salvation of all who believe, hope, and love. For this is the *great sacrament of piety, which was manifested in the flesh, justified in the spirit, appeared to angels, preached among the nations, believed in the world, taken up in glory*.

### The Son sent as born in the flesh and as perceived in the mind.

**20.** 28. By him, therefore, the Word of God is sent, whose Word it is; by him it is sent from whom it is born: he who begot sends, that which is begotten is sent. And it is sent to each one when it is known and perceived by anyone, as far as it can be known and perceived according to the capacity of the rational soul, whether making progress toward God or perfected in God. Not, then, by the very fact that he was born of the Father is the Son said to be sent; but either by the fact that the *Word made flesh* appeared to this world—whence he says, *I came forth from the Father, and came into this world*—or by the fact that he is perceived in time by anyone's mind, as it was said, *send it, that it may be with me and labor with me*. That, then, which is born from eternity is eternal:[^97] for *it is the brightness of eternal light*. But that which is sent in time is known by someone.

But when the Son of God was manifested in the flesh, *he was sent into this world, in the fullness of time, made of a woman*. *For because in the wisdom of God the world could not know God through wisdom*, since *the light shines in the darkness, and the darkness did not comprehend it*, *it pleased God through the foolishness of preaching to save those who believe*, that the Word might become flesh and dwell among us. But when, in time, he is perceived by the mind of someone making progress, he is indeed said to be sent, but not into this world: for he does not appear sensibly, that is, he is not present to the bodily senses. For we too, according as we grasp something eternal with the mind, as far as we can, are not in this world; and the spirits of all the righteous, even those still living in this flesh, insofar as they are wise about divine things, are not in this world.

But the Father, when he is known in time by anyone, is not said to be sent: for he has no one from whom he is, or from whom he proceeds. For *Wisdom* says, *I came forth from the mouth of the Most High*; and of the Holy Spirit it is said, *he proceeds from the Father*: but the Father from no one.

### For the Holy Spirit, to be sent is to be known to proceed from the Father.

**20.** 29. As, therefore, the Father begot, the Son was begotten, so the Father sent, the Son was sent. But just as he who begot and he who was begotten, so also he who sent and he who was sent are one, because the Father and the Son *are one*.[^98] So too the Holy Spirit is one with them, because *these three are one*.[^99] For just as, for the Son, to have been born is to be from the Father, so, for the Son, to be sent is to be known to be from him. And just as, for the Holy Spirit, to be the gift of God is to proceed from the Father, so to be sent is to be known to proceed from him. Nor can we say that the Holy Spirit does not also proceed from the Son: for not in vain is the same Spirit called the Spirit both of the Father and of the Son.[^100]

Nor do I see what else he meant to signify, when, breathing into the face of the disciples, he said: *receive the Holy Spirit*. For that corporeal breath, proceeding from the body with the sense of bodily touching, was not the substance of the Holy Spirit; but a demonstration, through a fitting signification, that the Holy Spirit proceeds not only from the Father but also from the Son. For who would be so very mad as to say that the Spirit whom he gave by breathing was one, and the one whom he sent after his ascension another? For the Spirit of God is one, the Spirit of the Father and the Son, the Holy Spirit, *who works all things in all*. But that he was given twice was certainly a dispensation of signification, of which in its own place, so far as the Lord shall grant, we shall treat.

What, then, the Lord says, *whom I will send to you from the Father*, shows the Spirit to be of both the Father and the Son. For also, when he had said, *whom the Father will send*, he added, *in my name*; yet he did not say, "whom the Father will send from me," as he said, *whom I will send to you from the Father*—evidently showing that the Father is the principle of the whole divinity, or, if it is better so called, the deity. He, then, who proceeds from the Father and the Son is referred to him from whom the Son was born.

And what the Evangelist says, *the Spirit was not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*—how is it understood, except that that certain giving or sending of the Holy Spirit was to be after the glorification of Christ, such as had never been before? For it was not that there had been none before, but that there had not been such a one. For if the Holy Spirit was not given before, by whom, when filled, did the prophets speak?

Since Scripture says openly, and shows in many places, that they spoke by the Holy Spirit: since also of John the Baptist it was said, *he shall be filled with the Holy Spirit even from his mother's womb*; and Zechariah his father is found filled with the Holy Spirit, that he might say such things of him; and Mary by the Holy Spirit,[^101] that she might proclaim such things of the Lord whom she bore in her womb;[^102] Simeon and Anna by the Holy Spirit, that they might recognize the greatness of the infant Christ:[^103]

how, then, was *the Spirit not yet given, because Jesus was not yet glorified*, except that that giving, or bestowal, or sending of the Holy Spirit was to have a certain property of its own in his very coming, such as had never been before? For nowhere do we read that men spoke in tongues they did not know when the Holy Spirit came upon them, as happened then, when it was fitting that his coming be demonstrated by sensible signs, that it might be shown that the whole world and all the nations, set in their various tongues, would believe in Christ through the gift of the Holy Spirit; that there might be fulfilled what is sung in the Psalm, *there are no utterances nor words whose voices are not heard; their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*.

**20.** 30. Man, therefore, was coupled to the Word of God into a unity of person, and in a certain way compounded with him, when, the *fullness of time* coming, *the Son of God was sent into this world, made of a woman*, that he might be also the Son of man on account of the sons of men.[^104] This person the angelic nature could before figure, so as to foretell it; not appropriate to itself, so as to be it.

### The Trinity works inseparably, but cannot be demonstrated inseparably through a creature.

**21.** 30. But as for the sensible demonstration of the Holy Spirit, whether through the form of a dove[^105] or through the fiery tongues,[^106] when a subject and serving creature[^107] showed by temporal motions and forms his substance, coeternal with the Father and the Son and equally unchangeable—since to the unity of his person, as the *flesh* which *the Word became*, it was not coupled—I do not dare to say that nothing such had been done before. But plainly I would say with confidence that the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, of one and the same substance, the creator God, the almighty Trinity, work inseparably; but that thus they cannot be inseparably demonstrated through a creature far unequal and especially corporeal; just as, through our voices, which certainly sound corporeally, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit cannot be named except by their own and proper intervals of time, distinguished by a fixed separation, which the syllables of each one's name occupy. For in their substance by which they are, *the three are one*, the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, by no temporal motion above every creature, the selfsame without any intervals of times or places, and at once one and the same from eternity to eternity, as eternity itself, which is not without truth and love: but in my voices the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are separated, nor could they be said at once, and in visible letters they held separately their spaces of place. And just as, when I name my memory and understanding and will, the single names are indeed referred to single things, yet each was made by all three; for there is none of these three names which my memory and understanding and will did not together work: so the Trinity together worked the voice of the Father, and the flesh of the Son, and the dove of the Holy Spirit, although these single things are referred to the single persons. By which likeness it is somehow recognized that the Trinity, inseparable in itself, is separably demonstrated through the form of a visible creature, and that the inseparable working of the Trinity is also in single things which are said to pertain properly to demonstrating either the Father, or the Son, or the Holy Spirit.

### The Incarnation is distinguished from the other missions.

**21.** 31. If, then, it is asked of me how the voices or sensible forms and appearances were made before the incarnation of the Word of God, which prefigured this that was to come: I answer that God worked them through angels; which I have also shown sufficiently, as I think, by the testimonies of the holy Scriptures.[^108] But if it is asked how the incarnation itself was made: I say that the Word of God itself was made flesh, that is, was made man,[^109] yet not converted and changed into that which it was made;[^110] made so, indeed, that there be there not only the Word of God and the flesh of man, but also the rational soul of man, and this whole be called both God on account of God and man on account of man. But if this is hard to understand, let the mind be purified by faith, more and more abstaining from sins, and working well, and praying with the groaning of holy desires, that, making progress through divine help, it may both understand and love.

But if it is asked, after the incarnation of the Word, how the voice of the Father was made, or the corporeal form by which the Holy Spirit was demonstrated: that these were made through a creature I do not doubt; but whether through one only corporeal and sensible, or with a spirit also rational or intellectual applied (for some have been pleased so to call what the Greeks call νοερός)—not indeed to the unity of person (for who would say this, that whatever it is of creature through which the voice of the Father sounded is so God the Father, or whatever it is of creature in which through the form of a dove[^111] or through the fiery tongues[^112] the Holy Spirit was demonstrated is so the Holy Spirit, as the Son of God is that man who was made of the Virgin?), but only to the ministry of carrying through the signification, as God judged it should be—or whether something else is to be understood, is difficult to find, and to affirm rashly is not expedient. Yet how these things could have been done without a rational or intellectual creature, I do not see. Nor is this yet the place to explain why I think so, so far as the Lord shall give strength. For first the arguments of the heretics must be discussed and refuted, which they bring forth not from the divine books but from their own reasonings, by which they think they vehemently compel that the testimonies of the Scriptures which are about the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit must be understood as they themselves wish.[^113]

### The Son and the Holy Spirit are not lesser because sent.

**21.** 32. But now it has been sufficiently demonstrated, as I think, that the Son is not lesser because he was sent by the Father, nor the Holy Spirit lesser because both the Father and the Son sent him.

For whether on account of the visible creature, or rather on account of the commendation of the principle, these things are understood to be set in the Scriptures not on account of inequality or disparity or unlikeness of substance: because, even if God the Father had willed to appear visibly through a subject creature, it would yet be most absurd to say that he was sent by the Son whom he begot, or by the Holy Spirit who proceeds from him.[^114]

Let this, then, be the limit of this volume: hereafter, in the rest, with the Lord helping, we shall see what those most cunning arguments of the heretics are, and how they are refuted.
