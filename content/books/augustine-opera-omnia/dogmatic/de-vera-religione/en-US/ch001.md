# Revisions I

## Chapter 13 (12): True Religion, one book[^1]

**1**. At that time I also wrote the book *True Religion*, in which it is argued at length and most fully that the one true God, that is, the Trinity, Father and Son and Holy Spirit, is to be worshipped with true religion, and with how great a mercy of his the Christian religion, which is the true religion, has been granted to human beings through the dispensation in time, and how a person is to be fitted by a certain sweetness of his own life[^2] to that same worship of God. This book speaks above all, however, against the two natures of the Manichees.

**2**. In a certain place in this book I say: *let it be clear to you and grasped that no error could have arisen in religion if the soul did not worship, in place of its God, a soul or a body or its own phantasms*.[^3] Here I used *soul* for the whole of incorporeal creation, not speaking in the manner of the Scriptures, which, when they use the word "soul" in no transferred sense, mean by it, I think, only that by which mortal animals live, among which human beings too are included, so long as they are mortal. A little later, however, I expressed the same sense better and more briefly, where I said: *let us not, then, serve the creature rather than the Creator*[^4] *nor become vain in our thoughts,*[^5] and our religion is perfect.[^6] For by the single word "creature" I signified both, that is, the spiritual and the corporeal.[^7] There remains what I said there: *or its own phantasms;* on account of which I said here: *nor become vain in our thoughts.*

**3**. Likewise, when I said: *this is, in our times, the Christian religion, which it is the most secure and most certain salvation to know and follow*,[^8] this was said with respect to the name, not with respect to the very reality of which this is the name. For the very reality that is now called the Christian religion existed even among the ancients and was not absent from the beginning of the human race, until Christ himself came in the flesh,[^9] from which point the true religion, which already existed, began to be called Christian. For when the apostles had begun to preach him after the resurrection and ascension into heaven, and very many believed, *at Antioch first*, as it is written, *the disciples were called Christians*.[^10] For this reason I said: *this is, in our times, the Christian religion,* not because it did not exist in earlier times, but because it received this name in later times.

**4**. In another place I say: *attend, then, to what follows, carefully and devoutly, as far as you can, for such people God helps*.[^11] This is not to be understood as though he helps only such people, since he also helps those who are not such, that they may become such, that is, that they may seek carefully and devoutly; but such people he helps to find.[^12]

And again elsewhere I say: *then it will follow that, after the bodily death which we owe to the first sin, this body will, in its own time and its own order, be restored to its former stability*.[^13] This is to be taken to mean that even the former stability of the body, which we lost by sinning, had such great happiness that it did not incline toward the decline of old age. To this former stability, then, this body will be restored in the resurrection of the dead. But it will have more, so that it is not sustained by bodily nourishment, but is sufficiently *made alive by the spirit alone, when it has risen as a life-giving spirit*,[^14] for which reason it will also be spiritual. But that first body, although it would not have died had man not sinned, was nevertheless made animal, that is, *a living soul*.[^15]

**5**. And elsewhere I say: *sin is so far a voluntary evil that it is in no way sin if it is not voluntary*.[^16] This definition can seem false; but if it is examined carefully, it will be found to be most true. For one must think of that sin which is only sin, not that which is also the punishment of sin, as I showed above when I recalled certain things from the third book of *On Free Will*.[^17] And yet even those sins which are not without reason called involuntary, because they are committed either by people who are ignorant or by people who are coerced, cannot be committed altogether without the will, since the one who sins in ignorance does indeed sin by his will,[^18] in that he thinks something is to be done that ought not to be done; and the one who, *while the flesh lusts against the spirit*,[^19] does not do the things he wills, lusts indeed unwillingly, and in this does not do what he wills, but if he is overcome, he consents to the lust willingly, and in this does nothing but what he wills, being free with regard to righteousness and a slave of sin.[^20] And that which in little children is called original sin, although they do not yet use the choice of the will, is not absurdly called "voluntary" too, because, contracted from the first evil will of man, it has become in a way hereditary. And so it is not false that I said: *sin is so far a voluntary evil that it is in no way sin if it is not voluntary*.[^21] Therefore by the grace of God not only is the guilt of all past sins released in all who are baptized in Christ, which happens by the Spirit of regeneration, but in grown people the will itself is also healed and prepared by the Lord, which happens by the spirit of faith and love.

**6**. In another place, in what I said about the Lord Jesus Christ, *he did nothing by force, but everything by persuading and admonishing*,[^22] it had not occurred to me that he drove the sellers and buyers out of the Temple by scourging them.[^23] But what is this, or how great is it? Even though he also cast out demons unwilling from human beings, not by the word of persuasion but by the force of power?[^24]

Likewise in another place I say: *these are first to be followed, who say that the one highest, only, and true God is alone to be worshipped.*[^25] If among these the truth does not shine forth, then at last one must move on.[^26] This can seem to have been said as though I had doubted the truth of this religion. But I said it as suited the person to whom I was writing.[^27] For I said: *if among these the truth does not shine forth,* in no way doubting that it would shine forth among them, just as the Apostle says: *if Christ has not risen,* in no way doubting that he has risen.

**7**. Likewise, when I said: *nor have those miracles been allowed to endure into our times, lest the soul should always seek visible things, and the human race should grow cold through familiarity with the very things by whose novelty it had been set aflame*,[^28] it is indeed true; for it is not the case that even now, when the hand is laid on the baptized, they so receive the Holy Spirit that they speak in the tongues of all nations,[^29] nor even now are the sick healed by the shadow of the preachers of Christ as they pass by,[^30] and if any such things were done then, it is clear that they later ceased.

But what I said is not to be taken to mean that no miracles are now believed to be done in the name of Christ. For I myself, when I wrote this very book, already knew that a blind man had been given sight at the bodies of the martyrs of Milan in that same city,[^31] and several other things, of which so many happen even in these times that we can neither know them all nor enumerate those that we do know.[^32]

**8**. And in another place, what I said, *as the Apostle says: 'all order is from God'*,[^33] the Apostle did not say in these very words, although the sense seems to be the same. For he says: *the things that are, are ordained by God*.

And elsewhere I say: *let no one at all deceive us: whatever is rightly blamed is rejected in comparison with something better*.[^34] This was said of substances and natures, for the discussion was about them, not about good actions and sins.

And again elsewhere I say: *but not even thus is a man to be loved by a man as carnal brothers or sons or spouses or any kinsfolk, or relations by marriage, or fellow citizens are loved, for that love too is temporal. For we would have no such ties, which come about by being born and dying, if our nature, remaining in the precepts and image of God, were not banished into this corruption*.[^35]

This sense I wholly disapprove, as I already disapproved it above too in the first book of *On Genesis Against the Manichees*.[^36] For it leads to the belief that those first spouses would not have begotten human descendants had they not sinned, as though it were necessary that mortals be begotten if they were to be begotten from the intercourse of male and female.

For I had not yet seen that it could have been the case that immortals be born of immortals, if human nature had not been changed for the worse by that great sin, and that, on this account, if fruitfulness and happiness had remained both in the parents and in the children, human beings would have been born, up to the fixed number of the saints whom *God has predestined*,[^37] not to succeed dying parents but to reign with the living. There would therefore be these kinships and relations by marriage as well, if no one sinned and no one died.

**9**. Likewise in another place I say: *tending toward the one God and binding our souls to him alone, from which "religion" is believed to be named, let us be free of all superstition*.[^38] In these words of mine, the account that was given of where "religion" gets its name pleased me more. For it does not escape me that authors writing in Latin have set forth another origin of this name, namely that it is called "religion" from being "gathered together again,"[^39] which word is compounded from "gathering," that is, from "selecting," so that "religo" would seem to be Latin in the way that "eligo" is.[^40]

This book begins thus: *since the whole way of a good and blessed life.*
