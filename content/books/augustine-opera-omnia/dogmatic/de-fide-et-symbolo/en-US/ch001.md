# Revisions I

## Chapter 17 (16): Faith and the Creed, one book

Around the same time, in the presence of the bishops, who at their bidding were holding a plenary council of all Africa at Hippo Regius, I, then a priest, delivered a discourse *On Faith and the Creed*. At the most earnest urging of some of those who loved us more intimately, I put that discourse into a book, in which the subjects themselves are treated in such a way that the connected sequence of words handed down to be memorized by those preparing for baptism is nevertheless not produced.

In this book, when the resurrection of the flesh was being discussed, I said: *the body will rise again according to the Christian faith, which cannot deceive. To anyone who finds this incredible, he is attending to what the flesh is now and is not considering what it will be, because at that time of the angelic change it will no longer be flesh and blood but only a body*, and the rest, where I discussed the changing of earthly bodies into heavenly bodies, since the Apostle said, when speaking of this matter: *flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God*[^1]. But whoever takes these words so as to suppose that the earthly body such as we now have is changed by the resurrection into a heavenly body in such a way that neither these members nor the substance of the flesh will remain, is without doubt to be corrected, being reminded of the body of the Lord, who after the resurrection appeared in the same members not only to be seen with the eyes but even to be handled with the hands, and who confirmed in words too that he had flesh, saying: *handle me and see, for a spirit does not have bones and flesh, as you see that I have*[^2].

From this it is clear that the Apostle did not deny that the substance of the flesh would be in the kingdom of God, but that he used the name *flesh and blood* to refer either to people who live according to the flesh or to the very corruption of the flesh, which then certainly will not exist at all. For when he had said: *flesh and blood will not possess the kingdom of God*, it is rightly understood that he immediately added, as though explaining what he had said: *nor will corruption possess incorruption*[^3]. On this matter, which is difficult to argue persuasively to unbelievers, whoever reads the last book of *The City of God* will find that I have treated it as carefully as I could.

This book begins thus: *Since it is written.*

[^1]: 1 Cor 15:50
[^2]: Luke 24:39
[^3]: 1 Cor 15:50
