# Book XX: What the Sacred Books Say of the Last Judgment

**1. 1.** Being about to speak, by what he himself shall grant, of the day of God's last judgment, and to assert it against the impious and unbelieving, we ought first, as on the foundation of a building, to lay down the divine testimonies; and those who refuse to believe them try to oppose them with petty human reasonings, false and deceptive, to this end: that they may either contend that the testimony adduced from the sacred letters means something else, or deny altogether that it was spoken by God.

For I think there is no mortal who, when he has understood these things as they were spoken and has believed them to have been spoken by the supreme and true God through holy souls, would not yield and consent to them—whether he also confesses this with his mouth, or whether by some fault he blushes or is afraid to confess it, or even, with an obstinacy very like madness, strives most contentiously to defend what he knows or believes to be false against what he knows or believes to be true.

## In this book the last judgment is treated.

**1. 2.** That which the whole Church of the true God holds in confession and profession, namely that Christ is to come from heaven to judge the living and the dead[^1], this we call the last day of the divine judgment, that is, the last time. For through how many days this judgment is to extend is uncertain; but that in the manner of the holy Scriptures "day" is usually put for "time" no one is unaware who has read those letters even at all carelessly.

But for this reason, when we speak of the day of God's judgment, we add "last" or "final," because he both judges now and has judged from the beginning of the human race when he dismissed from paradise and separated from the tree of life the first human beings, the perpetrators of a great sin[^2]; nay more, even when he did not spare the sinning angels[^3], whose prince, having overthrown himself, overthrew men by envy, he without doubt judged; nor without his deep and just judgment is the most wretched life of demons and men, in this airy heaven and on earth, most full of errors and hardships. Indeed, even if no one had sinned, it would not be without good and right judgment that he would keep the whole rational creation, most steadfastly cleaving to him its Lord, in eternal blessedness.

He also judges not only universally concerning the race of demons and men, that they should be wretched on account of the desert of the first sins, but also concerning the proper works of individuals, which they do by the choice of the will. For the demons too pray that they may not be tormented[^4], and surely they are neither unjustly spared nor unjustly tormented, each for his own wickedness; and men for the most part openly, always secretly, pay penalties divinely appointed for their deeds, whether in this life or after death—although no man acts rightly unless he is aided by divine help, and no demon or man acts unjustly unless it is permitted by the same most just divine judgment. For as the Apostle says: *there is no injustice with God*[^5]; and as he himself says elsewhere: *his judgments are inscrutable and his ways past finding out*[^6].

Not, therefore, of those first judgments of God, nor of these intermediate ones, but of the last judgment itself shall I argue in this book, as much as he himself shall grant, when Christ is to come from heaven to judge the living and the dead[^7]. For that day is now properly called the day of judgment, because there will be no place there for the ignorant complaint, why this unjust man is happy and why that just man is unhappy. For then the true and full felicity of none but the good, and the deserved and supreme infelicity of none but the wicked, will appear.

## What is the relation of this life to the judgment of God.

**2.** But now we learn both to bear with equanimity the evils that the good too suffer, and not to set great store by the goods that the wicked too attain; and through this, even in those matters in which the divine justice does not appear, the divine teaching is salutary. For we do not know by what judgment of God that good man is poor, that wicked man is rich; that man rejoices whom we suppose ought to be tormented with sorrows for his ruined morals, that man is saddened whom a praiseworthy life persuades us ought to rejoice;

an innocent man leaves the court not only unavenged but even condemned, oppressed by the iniquity of the judge or overwhelmed by false testimony, while on the contrary his criminal adversary not only goes unpunished but even, vindicated, exults over him;

the impious enjoys excellent health, the devout wastes away in sickness; the most healthy young men turn robbers, and infants who could not harm anyone even by a word are afflicted with the various severity of diseases;

one useful to human affairs is snatched away by an untimely death, and one who seems as though he ought not even to have been born nevertheless lives on a very long time; one full of crimes is exalted with honors, while obscure ignominy hides a man without reproach—and other things of this kind, which who can collect, who can enumerate?

If these things had a kind of consistency in their very absurdity—so that in this life, in which, as the sacred Psalm declares, *man has been made like to vanity, and his days pass away like a shadow*[^8], none but the wicked attained these transitory and earthly goods, and none but the good suffered such evils—this could be referred to a judgment of God that is just, or even kind,

so that those who were not going to attain the eternal goods which make men blessed would either be deceived by temporal goods for their malice, or be consoled by them through the mercy of God; and those who were not going to suffer eternal torments would either be afflicted by temporal evils for whatever and however small their sins were, or be exercised by evils for the perfecting of their virtues.

But as it is, since not only are the good in evil and the wicked in good—which seems unjust—but very often also evils befall the wicked and goods come to the good, the judgments of God become the more *inscrutable, and his ways past finding out*[^9].

Although, then, we do not know by what judgment God either does or permits these things—he with whom is the highest virtue, the highest wisdom, the highest justice, no weakness, no rashness, no iniquity—nevertheless we learn salutarily not to set great store either by the goods or the evils which we see to be common to the good and the wicked, and to seek those goods which are proper to the good, and most of all to flee those evils which are proper to the wicked.

But when we have come to that judgment of God, the time of which is now properly called the *day of judgment* and sometimes the *day of the Lord*, then not only whatever shall then be judged, but also whatever has been judged from the beginning and whatever is still to be judged up to that time, will appear to be most just. There this too will be made manifest: by how just a judgment of God it comes about that now so many—indeed almost all—the just judgments of God lie hidden from the senses and minds of mortals, although in this matter it does not lie hidden from the faith of the devout that what lies hidden is just.

**3.** Surely Solomon, the wisest king of Israel, who reigned in Jerusalem, thus began the book which is called *Ecclesiastes* and is held by the Jews too in the canon of the sacred letters: *vanity of vanities, said Ecclesiastes; vanity of vanities, all is vanity. What abundance has a man in all his labor with which he labors under the sun?*[^10]. And when he had connected the rest with this sentence, recalling the hardships and errors of this life and meanwhile the vanishing lapses of time, in which nothing solid, nothing stable is retained, in that vanity of things under the sun he also deplores in a certain way this: that, though there is an abundance of *wisdom over folly, as there is an abundance of light over darkness, and the eyes of the wise man are in his head while the fool walks in darkness*, *yet one event happens to all*—namely in this life that is lived under the sun—plainly signifying those evils which we see to be common to the good and the wicked[^11]. He also says this, that both the good suffer evils as though they were wicked, and the wicked attain goods as though they were good, speaking thus: *there is*, he says, *a vanity which is done upon the earth, that there are just men to whom it happens according to the work of the impious, and there are impious men to whom it happens according to the work of the just. I said that this also is vanity*[^12].

To setting forth this vanity, as much as seemed enough, the most wise man devoted that whole book (surely for no other reason than that we might desire that life which has no vanity under this sun, but truth under him who made this sun); in this vanity, then, would man not, by the just and right judgment of God, become like to that same vanity and vanish away[^13]? Yet in the days of his vanity it makes a very great difference whether he resists or yields to the truth, and whether he is without true piety or a partaker of it—not for the sake of acquiring the goods or avoiding the evils of this life that pass away in vanishing, but for the sake of the future judgment, through which both goods will remain for the good and evils for the wicked without end.

Finally, this wise man so concluded the book that he said: *fear God and keep his commandments, for this is the whole man; for God will bring every work into judgment for every thing despised, whether good or evil*[^14]. What briefer, truer, more salutary thing could be said? *Fear God*, he says, *and keep his commandments, for this is the whole man*.

For whoever exists is this, namely a keeper of the commandments of God; since he who is not this is nothing, for he is not reformed to the image of truth, remaining in the likeness of vanity. *For every work*, that is, whatever is done by man in this life, *whether good or evil, God will bring into judgment for every thing despised*, that is, for every man who here seems contemptible and is therefore not even seen; for God both sees him and does not despise him, nor does he pass him by when he judges.

## First the new, then the old proclamations will be adduced.

**4.** Of this last judgment of God, then, the testimonies from the holy Scriptures which I have undertaken to set down are first to be chosen from the books of the New Testament, afterward from the Old. For although the old are prior in time, the new are nevertheless to be preferred in dignity, since those old things are the proclamations of the new. The new, then, will be set down first; and that we may prove them more firmly, the old too will be taken up. In the old are the Law and the Prophets; in the new, the Gospel and the apostolic letters.

But the Apostle says: *for through the law comes the knowledge of sin. But now the righteousness of God has been manifested apart from law, attested by the law and the prophets; the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe*[^15]. This righteousness of God belongs to the New Testament and has its testimony from the old books, that is, the Law and the Prophets.

First, then, the case itself is to be set down, and afterward the witnesses are to be brought in. Christ Jesus himself, showing that this order is to be kept, says: *a scribe instructed in the kingdom of God is like a householder bringing forth out of his treasure new things and old*[^16]. He did not say, "old and new," which he certainly would have said, had he not preferred to keep the order of merit rather than of times.

## What Jesus said in Matthew about the judgment of the good and the wicked.

**5. 1.** Therefore the Savior himself, when he was rebuking the cities in which he had done great works and which had not believed, and was setting foreigners before them, said: *nevertheless I say to you, it shall be more tolerable for Tyre and Sidon on the day of judgment than for you*[^17]; and a little after, to another city: *amen, I say to you, it shall be more tolerable for the land of Sodom on the day of judgment than for you*[^18] (here he most evidently proclaims that the day of judgment is to come);

and in another place: *the men of Nineveh*, he says, *will rise up at the judgment with this generation and will condemn it; for they repented at the preaching of Jonah, and behold, something more than Jonah is here. The queen of the South will rise up at the judgment with this generation and will condemn it; for she came from the ends of the earth to hear the wisdom of Solomon, and behold, something more than Solomon is here*[^19].

In this place we learn two things: both that judgment is to come and that it is to come with the resurrection of the dead. For when he said these things of the Ninevites and the queen of the South, he was without doubt speaking of the dead, whom nevertheless he foretold would rise on the day of judgment. Nor did he say *will condemn* because they themselves will judge, but because by comparison with them these will deservedly be condemned.

**5. 2.** Again, in another place, when he was speaking of the present mingling of good and wicked men and their later separation—which will surely take place on the day of judgment—he employed a likeness of the wheat sown and the tares sown over it,

and explaining it to his disciples he said: *he who sows the good seed is the Son of man; the field is the world; the good seed are the sons of the kingdom; the tares are the sons of the evil one; the enemy who sowed them is the devil; the harvest is the end of the age, and the reapers are angels. Just as, then, the tares are gathered and burned with fire, so it will be at the end of the age. The Son of man will send his angels, and they will gather out of his kingdom all causes of scandal and those who do iniquity, and will cast them into the furnace of fire; there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth. Then the just will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father. He who has ears, let him hear*[^20].

Here indeed he did not name the judgment or the day of judgment, but he expressed it much more clearly by the very things, and foretold that it would come at the end of the age.

**5. 3.** Likewise to his disciples: *amen, I say to you that you who have followed me, in the regeneration, when the Son of man shall sit on the throne of his majesty, you also will sit on twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel*[^21]. Here we learn that Jesus will judge together with his disciples. Hence too he said elsewhere to the Jews: *if I cast out demons by Beelzebub, by whom do your sons cast them out? Therefore they will be your judges*[^22]. Nor, because he says they will sit on twelve thrones, ought we to think that only twelve men will judge with him. For by the number twelve a certain universality of those who judge is signified, on account of the two parts of the number seven, by which universality is for the most part signified; which two parts, that is, three and four, multiplied one by the other make twelve—for both four times three and three times four are twelve; and if any other account of this number twelve is found that avails for this.

Otherwise, since we read that the apostle Matthias was ordained in the place of the traitor Judas[^23], the apostle Paul, who labored more than all of them[^24], would have nowhere to sit for judging; he who surely shows that he belongs to the number of the judges along with the other saints when he says: *do you not know that we shall judge angels?*[^25]. Concerning those also who are to be judged the case is similar in this number twelve. For not because it is said, *judging the twelve tribes of Israel*, will the tribe of Levi, which is the thirteenth, not be judged by them, or will they judge that people alone and not also the other nations. And as for his saying *in the regeneration*[^26], he without doubt meant that the resurrection of the dead should be understood by the name "regeneration." For our flesh will be regenerated through incorruption just as our soul has been regenerated through faith.

**5. 4.** I pass over many things which seem to be said of the last judgment in such a way that, carefully considered, they are found to be ambiguous or rather to pertain to something else—namely either to that coming of the Savior by which throughout all this time he comes in his Church, that is, in his members, part by part and little by little, since the whole is his body; or to the destruction of earthly Jerusalem, since when he speaks of that too he often speaks as though he were speaking of the end of the age and that last and great day of judgment, so that it cannot be distinguished at all unless those things which are similarly said about this matter in the three Evangelists Matthew, Mark, and Luke[^27] are all compared with one another. For some things one sets forth more obscurely, another more plainly, so that the things said pertaining to one matter make it apparent from what they are said. This I have tried to do as best I could in a certain letter, which I wrote in reply to a man of blessed memory, Hesychius, bishop of the city of Salona, the title of which letter is *On the End of the Age*[^28].

**5. 5.** Accordingly I shall now say here what is read in the Gospel according to Matthew concerning the separation of the good and the wicked by the most present and last judgment of Christ. *But when the Son of man comes*, he says, *in his majesty, and all the angels with him, then he will sit on the throne of his majesty, and all the nations will be gathered before him, and he will separate them one from another, as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats; and he will set the sheep on his right hand, but the goats on the left. Then the King will say to those who will be on his right hand: come, blessed of my Father, possess the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat; I was thirsty, and you gave me to drink; I was a stranger, and you took me in; naked, and you covered me; sick, and you visited me; I was in prison, and you came to me. Then the just will answer him, saying: Lord, when did we see you hungry, and fed you; or thirsty, and gave you drink? And when did we see you a stranger, and took you in; or naked, and covered you? Or when did we see you sick or in prison, and came to you? And the King, answering, will say to them: amen, I say to you, as long as you did it to one of these least of my brothers, you did it to me. Then he will say*, he says, *also to those who will be on the left hand: depart from me, you cursed, into the eternal fire, which is prepared for the devil and his angels*[^29]. Then he likewise enumerates to these that they did not do those things which he recounted that the right-hand ones did. And when they likewise ask when they saw him placed in want of these things, he answers that what was not done to his least ones was not done to himself; and concluding his discourse he says: *and these will go away into eternal punishment, but the just into eternal life*[^30].

## [The testimonies of John and the first and second resurrection]

But John the Evangelist most plainly narrates that he foretold judgment at the resurrection of the dead. For when he had said: *for the Father judges no one, but has given all judgment to the Son, that all may honor the Son as they honor the Father; he who does not honor the Son does not honor the Father who sent him*, he at once added: *amen, amen, I say to you, he who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life, and does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life*[^31].

Behold, here he said that his faithful do not come into judgment. How then will they be separated from the wicked by judgment and stand at his right hand, unless in this place he put *judgment* for condemnation? For into such judgment they will not come who hear his word and believe him who sent him.

**6. 1.** Then he goes on and says: *amen, amen, I say to you, the hour is coming, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live. For as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son also to have life in himself*[^32]. He is not yet speaking of the second resurrection, that is, of bodies, which will take place at the end, but of the first, which is now. For to distinguish this he said: *the hour is coming, and now is*.

But this is not of bodies but of souls. For souls too have their death in impiety and sins, according to which death they are dead of whom the same Lord says: *let the dead bury their dead*[^33]; namely that the dead in soul should bury the dead in body. For these, then, who are dead in soul through impiety and iniquity, *the hour is coming*, he says, *and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God; and those who hear will live*. By *those who hear* he meant those who obey, who believe and persevere to the end. And here he made no difference between the good and the wicked. For it is good for all to hear his voice and to live, by passing from the death of impiety to the life of piety.

Of this death the apostle Paul says: *therefore all have died, and he died for all, that those who live may no longer live for themselves, but for him who died for them and rose again*[^34]. All, then, have died in sins, no one at all excepted, whether in original sins or in those added also by will, whether by being ignorant or by knowing and not doing what is just; and for all the dead one died who was living, that is, having no sin at all; so that those who live through the remission of sins may *no longer live for themselves, but for him who died for all* on account of our sins and rose again on account of our justification[^35], that we, believing in him who justifies the impious[^36], being justified from impiety, made alive as if from death, might be able to belong to the first resurrection, which *now is*.

For to this first resurrection none belong but those who will be blessed forever; but to the second, of which he is about to speak presently, he will teach that both the blessed and the wretched belong. The one is of mercy, the other of judgment. Wherefore it is written in the Psalm: *mercy and judgment I will sing to you, O Lord*[^37].

**6. 2.** Concerning this judgment he consequently added and said: *and he has given him power to do judgment, because he is the Son of man*[^38]. Here he shows that he will come to judge in that flesh in which he had come to be judged. For to this end he says: *because he is the Son of man*. And then, subjoining the matter we are treating: *do not marvel at this*, he says, *for the hour is coming in which all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and will come forth: those who have done good, to the resurrection of life; but those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment*[^39].

This is that *judgment* which a little before, as now, he had put for condemnation when he said: *he who hears my word and believes him who sent me has eternal life and does not come into judgment, but has passed from death to life*[^40]—that is, by belonging to the first resurrection, by which one now passes from death to life, he will not come into condemnation, which he signified by the term *judgment*, just as also in this place where he says: *those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment*, that is, of condemnation.

Let him, then, rise in the first who does not wish to be condemned in the second resurrection. For *the hour is coming, and now is, when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live*[^41], that is, will not come into condemnation, which is called the second death; into which death, after the second resurrection, which will be of bodies, those will be cast headlong who do not rise in the first, which is of souls. For *the hour is coming* (where he does not say "and now is," because it will be at the end of the age, that is, at the last and greatest judgment of God), *when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and will come forth*[^42].

into which death, after the second resurrection—that of bodies—those will be cast who do not rise in the first resurrection, that of souls. *For the hour will come*—here he does not say “and now is,” because it will be at the end of the age, that is, at God’s last and greatest judgment—*when all who are in the tombs will hear his voice and come forth*[^43].

He did not say, as in the first, "and those who hear will live." For not all will live—that life, namely, which, because it is blessed, alone deserves to be called life. For surely they could not hear and come forth from the tombs with the flesh rising again without some kind of life.

But why not all will live, he teaches in what follows: *those who have done good*, he says, *to the resurrection of life*—these are they who will live; *but those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment*[^44]—these are they who will not live, because they will die the second death. For they have done evil because they lived ill; and they lived ill because in the first resurrection, which now is, the resurrection of souls, they did not come back to life, or, in that which they had come back to life, they did not remain to the end.

As, then, there are two regenerations, of which I have already spoken above—one according to faith, which now takes place through baptism; the other according to the flesh, which will take place in its incorruption and immortality through the great and last judgment—so there are also two resurrections: one the first, which both now is and is of souls, which does not permit one to come into the second death; the other the second, which is not now but will be at the end of the age, and is not of souls but of bodies, which through the last judgment sends some into the second death, others into that life which has no death.

## How the millenarians expound certain things from the Apocalypse.

**7. 1.** Of these two resurrections the same John the Evangelist, in the book which is called the *Apocalypse*, has spoken in such a way that the first of them, not understood by some of our people, has even been turned into certain ridiculous fables.

For the apostle John says in the book mentioned: *and I saw an angel coming down from heaven, having the key of the abyss and a chain in his hand. And he laid hold of the dragon, that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan, and bound him for a thousand years and cast him into the abyss; and he shut and sealed it over him, that he should no longer deceive the nations, until the thousand years should be finished; after these things he must be loosed for a short time. And I saw thrones and those who sat upon them, and judgment was given. And the souls of those slain for the testimony of Jesus and for the word of God, and whoever had not adored the beast nor its image, nor had received its inscription on the forehead or on the hand—and they reigned with Jesus for a thousand years; the rest of them did not live until the thousand years should be finished. This is the first resurrection. Blessed and holy is he who has a part in this first resurrection. Over these the second death has no power; but they will be priests of God and of Christ, and will reign with him for a thousand years*[^45].

Those who, on account of these words of this book, have supposed that the first resurrection will be bodily, have been moved, among other things, chiefly by the number of a thousand years, as though it were fitting that there should be in the saints a kind of sabbath-keeping for so great a span of time—a holy rest, that is, after the labors of the six thousand years from the time man was created and, for the desert of that great sin, was dismissed from the felicity of paradise into the hardships of this mortality;

so that, since it is written, *one day with the Lord is as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day*[^46], the six thousand years being completed as it were six days, there should follow as it were a seventh of sabbath in the last thousand years—for the saints to rise, that is, for the keeping of this sabbath.

This opinion would be in some measure tolerable, if it were believed that some spiritual delights would be present to the saints in that sabbath through the presence of the Lord. For we too once held this opinion. But since they say that those who then rise again will be occupied with the most immoderate carnal feasts, in which there will be such food and drink that they not only keep no moderation but exceed even the measure of belief itself, these things can in no way be believed except by carnal men. But those who are spiritual call these who believe such things by the Greek word χιλιαστάς, whom, expressing the word by a word, we might call "millenarians." But to refute them point by point would be long; rather, we ought now to show how this Scripture is to be taken.

**7. 2.** The Lord Jesus Christ himself says: *no one can enter the house of a strong man and carry off his goods unless he first binds the strong man*[^47], meaning that the *strong man* be understood as the devil, because he was able to hold the human race captive; but his *goods*, which he was about to carry off, his future faithful, whom that one possessed in various sins and impieties.

In order, then, that this strong man might be bound, this Apostle saw in the *Apocalypse an angel coming down from heaven, having the key of the abyss and a chain in his hand. And he laid hold*, he says, *of the dragon, that ancient serpent, who is called the devil and Satan, and bound him for a thousand years*[^48]; that is, he restrained and curbed his power to deceive and possess those who were to be set free.

But the thousand years can, as far as occurs to me, be understood in two ways: either because this takes place in the last thousand years, that is, in the sixth millennium as it were the sixth day, the later spaces of which are now turning, with a sabbath to follow that has no evening—namely the rest of the saints, which has no end—so that he called the last part of this millennium, as it were of this day, which remained until the end of the age, a thousand years, by that manner of speaking by which the part is signified by the whole; or else he certainly put a thousand years for all the years of this age, so that by the perfect number the very fullness of time might be marked.

For the number a thousand renders the solid square of the number ten. For ten times ten is a hundred, which is now a square figure, but a plane one; but that it may rise into height and become solid, again a hundred is multiplied ten times, and they are a thousand. Further, if a hundred is sometimes put for universality—as is that which the Lord promised to one who left all his own and followed him, saying: *he will receive a hundredfold in this age*[^49], which the Apostle in a certain way expounds when he says: *as having nothing, yet possessing all things*[^50]; for it had already been said before: *the whole world of riches belongs to the faithful man*[^51]—

how much more is a thousand put for universality, where there is the solidity of that very squaring of ten? Whence too that is better understood which is read in the Psalm: *he was mindful of his covenant forever, the word which he commanded for a thousand generations*[^52], that is, "for all."

**7. 3.** *And he cast him*, he says, *into the abyss*; surely he cast the devil into the abyss, by which name is signified the innumerable multitude of the impious, whose hearts are very deep in malice against the Church of God; not because the devil was not there before, but he is said to have been cast there for this reason: because, excluded from believers, he began to possess the impious all the more. For he is the more possessed by the devil who is not only estranged from God but also gratuitously hates those who serve God.

*And he shut*, he says, *and sealed it over him, that he should no longer deceive the nations, until the thousand years should be finished*[^53]. *He shut over him* means "he forbade him to be able to go out," that is, to transgress what was forbidden.

But what he added, *he sealed*, seems to me to have signified that he willed it to be hidden who belong to the part of the devil and who do not. For in this present age this lies entirely hidden, since it is uncertain both whether he who seems to stand is going to fall, and whether he who seems to lie is going to rise[^54].

But from deceiving those nations the devil is prohibited and restrained by the bond and bar of this prohibition—nations which, belonging to Christ, he was deceiving or holding before. For God chose these *before the foundation of the world*[^55] to rescue them from the power of darkness and to transfer them into the kingdom of the Son of his love[^56], as the Apostle says.

For that he deceives the nations even now and drags them with him into eternal punishment—those not predestined to eternal life—what faithful person is unaware? Nor let it disturb anyone that the devil often deceives even those who, already regenerated in Christ, are entering on the ways of God. For *the Lord knows who are his*[^57]; of these he deceives no one into eternal damnation.

For the Lord knows them as God, from whom nothing even of future things is hidden, not as a man, who sees a man as he is at present (if indeed he sees him whose heart he does not see), but what sort he will afterward be he does not even see in himself.

To this end, then, the devil is bound and shut up in the abyss, that he should no longer deceive the nations of which the Church consists, which he was holding deceived before they were the Church. For it was not said "that he should deceive no one," but *that he should no longer deceive*, he says, *the nations*, in which he without doubt meant the Church to be understood, *until*, he says, *the thousand years should be finished*, that is, either what remains of the sixth day, which consists of a thousand years, or all the years through which thereafter this age is to be carried on.

**7. 4.** Nor is what he says, *that he should not deceive the nations, until the thousand years should be finished*[^58], to be taken as though afterward he is to deceive those very nations from which the predestined Church consists, from deceiving which he is prohibited by that bond and bar. But it is said either by that mode of speech which is found a number of times in the Scriptures, such as is in the Psalm: *so are our eyes toward the Lord our God, until he have mercy on us*[^59]—for not, when he has had mercy, will the eyes of his servants not be toward the Lord their God;

or else the order of the words is this: *and he shut and sealed it over him, until the thousand years should be finished*; and what he interposed, *that he should no longer deceive the nations*, stands so as to be free from the connection of this order and to be understood separately, as though it were added afterward, so that the whole sentence would run thus: *and he shut and sealed it over him until the thousand years should be finished, so that he should no longer deceive the nations*; that is, he shut him up until the thousand years should be finished for this purpose, that he should no longer deceive the nations.

**8. 1.** *After these things*, he says, *he must be loosed for a short time*[^60]. If for the devil to be bound and shut up is to be unable to deceive the Church, will then this be his loosing, that he is able to? Far from it; for never will the Church, predestined and chosen before the foundation of the world[^61], of which it is said, *the Lord knows who are his*[^62], be deceived by him.

And yet it will be here even at that time when the devil is to be loosed, just as, from the time it was established, it has been here and will be at every time, namely in those of its members who succeed by being born to those who die. For a little later he says that the loosed devil will drag the deceived nations throughout the whole world into war against it, the number of which enemies will be as the sand of the sea.

*And they went up*, he says, *over the breadth of the earth, and surrounded the camp of the saints and the beloved city; and fire came down from heaven from God and devoured them; and the devil, who deceived them, was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where also the beast and the false prophet are; and they will be tormented day and night forever and ever*[^63]. But this already pertains to the last judgment, which I thought should now be mentioned, lest anyone suppose that, during that very short time in which the devil will be loosed, the Church will not be on this earth—either because he does not find it here when he has been loosed, or because he consumes it when he has persecuted it in every way.

Thus throughout this whole time which this book embraces—from the first coming of Christ, that is, to the end of the age, which will be his second coming—the devil is not so bound that this very binding of his is, throughout this interval which is called by the number *a thousand years*, an inability to deceive the Church, since not even when loosed will he deceive it. For surely, if for him to be bound is to be unable to deceive or not to be permitted, what will it be to be loosed except to be able to deceive or to be permitted? Far be it that this should happen;

but the binding of the devil is his not being permitted to exert the whole power of temptation that he has, either by force or by guile, for seducing men—drawing them to his party by violent compulsion or fraudulently deceiving them. For if he were permitted, over so long a time and amid such weakness of many, he would cast down and hinder from believing very many such as God does not will to suffer this, both faithful ones and those who would believe; and lest he should do this, he is bound.

## What is to be said about God and the loosed devil.

**8. 2.** But then he will be loosed, when both the *short time* will come (for we read that for three years and six months he will rage with all his own strength and that of his followers[^64]), and those will be such, with whom he must wage war, that they cannot be conquered by so great an onset and snares of his. But if he were never loosed, his malicious power would be less apparent, the most faithful endurance of the holy city would be less proved, and finally it would be less perceived how well the Almighty made use of his great evil—he who did not altogether take him away from the temptation of the saints, although cast out from their inner selves, where God is believed in, to the outside, that they might profit by his attack from without;

and in those who are of his party he bound him, lest by pouring out and exercising as much malice as he could, he should deter or break from the faith of piety the innumerable weak ones—of whom the Church had to be multiplied and filled—some who were going to believe, others already believing; and he will loose him at the end, so that the city of God may behold, with great glory of its redeemer, helper, and liberator, how strong an adversary it overcame.

Indeed, in comparison with those saints and faithful who will then exist, what are we? Seeing that, to prove them, so great an enemy will be loosed, with whom we struggle, while he is bound, amid such great dangers. Although it is not doubtful that even in this interval of time some soldiers of Christ have been and are so prudent and brave that, even if they were living in this mortality when he is loosed, they would most wisely beware of all his snares and assaults and most patiently endure them.

**8. 3.** But this binding of the devil was not only done from the time the Church began to be spread, beyond the land of Judea, into nation after nation; but it is also done now and will be done until the end of the age, when he is to be loosed. For even now men are converted to the faith from the unbelief in which he held them, and without doubt will be converted up to that end; and surely for each one this strong man is then bound, when he is snatched away from him as his vessel. And the abyss in which he is shut up was not consumed in those who were dead, who existed when he began to be shut up; but others have succeeded them by being born and do succeed, until this age is finished, who hate Christians, in whose blind and deep hearts, as in an abyss, he is daily shut up.

But whether, even in those last three years and six months, when, loosed, he is to rage with all his strength, anyone is to come to the faith who was not in it before, is no small question. For how will that stand which was said, *who enters the house of a strong man to carry off his goods unless he first binds the strong man*[^65], if they are carried off even when he is loosed? And through this this sentence seems to compel us to believe that in that brief time no one will be added to the Christian people, but the devil will fight with those who are already found Christians; of whom, even if some are conquered and follow him, they do not belong to the predestined number of the sons of God. For it is not in vain that the same apostle John, who also wrote this *Apocalypse*, says in his epistle of certain men: *they went out from us, but they were not of us; for if they had been of us, they would surely have remained with us*[^66].

But what will become of the little ones? For it is exceedingly incredible that at that time there will be no infants already born and not yet baptized, the children of Christians, none also being born in those very days; or, if there will be, that they will not be brought by their parents to the laver of regeneration in whatever way. But if this will be, in what manner, with the devil now loosed, will those vessels be carried off, into whose house no one enters to carry off his goods unless he first binds him?

Nay rather, this is to be believed: that at that time neither those who fall from the Church nor those who join the Church will be lacking; but surely both the parents on behalf of their little ones to be baptized, and those who will then first believe, will be so strong that they will conquer that strong man even when he is not bound—that is, that they will both vigilantly understand and patiently bear his snares and his pressing force in all forms such as he never used before, and so even from the unbound one they will be carried off.

Nor for this reason will that gospel sentence be false: *who enters the house of a strong man to carry off his goods unless he first binds the strong man?* For according to the truth of that sentence this order has been kept: that first the strong man should be bound, and, his goods being carried off, the Church should be so multiplied far and wide among all the nations, from the strong and the weak, that, by its most robust faith in things divinely foretold and fulfilled, it could carry off his goods even from the unbound one.

For just as it must be confessed that the love of many will grow cold[^67] when iniquity abounds, and that by the unaccustomed and greatest persecutions and deceits of the now-loosed devil many of those who are not written in the book of life will give way, so it must be thought that not only will that time find some good faithful, but also that some who will still be outside, with the help of God's grace, through consideration of the Scriptures—in which both other things and the very end was foretold, which they perceive to be now coming—will be the firmer for believing what they did not believe, and the stronger for conquering even the unbound devil.

And if it will be so, then his binding must be said to have preceded, so that the despoiling both of the bound and of the loosed one might follow; since concerning this matter it was said: *who will enter the house of a strong man to carry off his goods unless he first binds the strong man?*

## What the kingdom of God is now and then.

**9. 1.** Meanwhile, while the devil is bound for a thousand years, the saints reign with Christ also for a thousand years—the same, no doubt, and to be understood in the same way, that is, of this present time of his first coming. For setting aside that kingdom of which he is to speak at the end: *come, blessed of my Father, possess the kingdom prepared for you*[^68]—unless in some other way, far inferior indeed, his saints already reign with him now, to whom he says: *behold, I am with you even to the consummation of the age*[^69]—surely the Church would not even now be called his kingdom or the kingdom of heaven.

For surely at this time that scribe is instructed in the kingdom of God, *who brings forth out of his treasure new things and old*, of whom we spoke above[^70];

and from the Church those reapers are to gather the tares which he permitted to grow together with the wheat until the harvest; which expounding he says: *the harvest is the end of the age, and the reapers are angels. Just as, then, the tares are gathered and burned with fire, so it will be at the consummation of the age; the Son of man will send his angels, and they will gather out of his kingdom all causes of scandal*[^71]. Surely not from that kingdom where there are no scandals? Therefore they will be gathered from this kingdom of his, which is here the Church.

Likewise he says: *whoever shall loose one of these least commandments and shall so teach men, he shall be called least in the kingdom of heaven; but whoever shall do and so teach, he shall be called great in the kingdom of heaven*[^72]. He says that both are in the kingdom of heaven: both he who does not do the commandments which he teaches (for this is what *to loose* means: "not to keep, not to do") and he who does and so teaches; but the one *least*, the other *great*.

And immediately following he adds: *for I say to you that unless your righteousness shall abound more than that of the scribes and Pharisees*[^73]—that is, more than those who loose what they teach (for of the scribes and Pharisees he says elsewhere: *for they say, and do not do*[^74])—unless, then, your righteousness shall abound more than these, that is, so that you do not loose but rather do what you teach, *you will not enter*, he says, *into the kingdom of heaven*[^75]. The kingdom of heaven, then, must be understood in one way, where both are—both he who looses what he teaches and he who does it, but the one least, the other great; in another way the kingdom of heaven is spoken of, into which none enters but he who does. And through this, where both kinds are, it is the Church such as it is now; but where that one alone will be, it is the Church such as it will be then, when there will be no wicked man in it.

Therefore even now the Church is the kingdom of Christ and the kingdom of heaven. Accordingly his saints reign with him even now, in another way indeed than they will reign then; yet the tares do not reign with him, although they grow in the Church together with the wheat[^76]. For those reign with him who do what the Apostle says: *if you have risen with Christ, savor the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; seek the things that are above, not the things that are on earth*[^77]; of such he likewise says that their conversation is in heaven[^78].

Finally, those reign with him who are in his kingdom in such a way that they themselves too are his kingdom. But how are those his kingdom who—to say nothing else—although they are there until all the scandals are gathered out of his kingdom at the end of the age, nevertheless there seek their own things, not the things of Jesus Christ?

**9. 2.** Of this kingdom of warfare, then, in which the conflict with the enemy still goes on, and sometimes there is resistance to the fighting vices, sometimes command over them as they yield, until one comes to that most peaceful kingdom where one will reign without an enemy; and of this first resurrection, which now is, this book speaks thus.

For when he had said that the devil was bound for a thousand years and afterward loosed for a short time, then, recapitulating what the Church does in these thousand years, or what is done in it: *and I saw*, he says, *thrones and those who sat upon them, and judgment was given*[^79]. This is not to be thought said of the last judgment; but the thrones of those set over the Church and the very ones set over it are to be understood, through whom the Church is now governed.

And the judgment given seems best taken as that which was said: *whatever you bind on earth shall be bound also in heaven; and whatever you loose on earth shall be loosed also in heaven*[^80]. Whence the Apostle says: *for what have I to do with judging those who are outside? Do you not judge those who are within?*[^81].

*And the souls*, he says, *of those slain for the testimony of Jesus and for the word of God*[^82]; there is understood what he is afterward to say: *they reigned with Jesus for a thousand years*; namely the souls of the martyrs, their bodies not yet restored to them. For the souls of the devout dead are not separated from the Church, which even now is the kingdom of Christ. Otherwise no memory of them would be made at the altar of God in the communication of the body of Christ; nor would it profit anything to run to his baptism in dangers, lest this life end without it; nor to reconciliation, if perhaps through penance or an evil conscience someone has been separated from that same body. For why are these things done, except because the faithful, even the deceased, are his members? Although, then, with their bodies not yet, yet their souls already reign with him, while these thousand years run on.

Whence in this same book it is read also elsewhere: *blessed are the dead who die in the Lord. From now on, says the Spirit, that they may rest from their labors; for their works follow them*[^83]. Therefore the Church now first reigns with Christ in the living and the dead. For *for this reason*, as the Apostle says, *Christ died, that he might be Lord both of the living and of the dead*[^84]. But he mentioned only the souls of the martyrs because they especially reign who fought even to death for the truth, when dead. But by the part we understand the whole, namely the other dead also who belong to the Church, which is the kingdom of Christ.

**9. 3.** But what follows—*and whoever had not adored the beast nor its image, nor had received its inscription on the forehead or on the hand*[^85]—we ought to take of the living and the dead together. Moreover, what this beast is, although it must be inquired into more diligently, is nevertheless not at variance with right faith for it to be understood as that impious city itself and the people of the unbelievers, opposed to the faithful people and the city of God. But its *image* seems to me to be its pretense, namely in those men who profess the faith as it were and live unfaithfully. For they pretend to be what they are not, and are called Christians not by a true likeness but by a deceptive image. For to the same beast belong not only the open enemies of the name of Christ and of his most glorious city, but also the tares which are to be gathered out of his kingdom, which is the Church, at the end of the age[^86].

And who are those who do not adore the beast nor its image, except those who do what the Apostle says: *do not be yoked together with unbelievers*[^87]? For *they do not adore* means they do not consent, they are not subjected; *nor do they receive the inscription*, namely the mark of crime, *on the forehead* on account of profession, *on the hand* on account of activity. Estranged, then, from these evils, whether still living in this mortal flesh or deceased, they reign with Christ even now in a certain way suited to this time, throughout this whole interval which is signified by the number of a thousand years.

**9. 4.** *The rest of them*, he says, *did not live*[^88]. For *the hour now is, when the dead hear the voice of the Son of God, and those who hear will live*[^89]; therefore the rest of them will not live. But what he added, *until the thousand years should be finished*, is to be understood that they did not live at the time at which they ought to have lived, namely by passing from death to life.

And therefore, when the day comes on which the resurrection of bodies too takes place, they will come forth from the tombs not to life but to judgment, namely to the condemnation which is called the second death. For until the thousand years are finished, whoever has not lived—that is, throughout all this time in which the first resurrection takes place has not heard the voice of the Son of God and passed from death to life—will surely, in the second resurrection, which is of the flesh, pass with that same flesh into the second death.

For he goes on and says: *this is the first resurrection. Blessed and holy is he who has a part in this first resurrection*[^90], that is, who is a partaker of it. But he is a partaker of it who not only comes back to life from the death which is in sins, but also remains in that to which he has come back to life. *Over these*, he says, *the second death has no power*[^91]. It has power, then, over the rest, of whom he said above: *the rest of them did not live until the thousand years should be finished*[^92]; since throughout this whole interval of time which he calls a thousand years, however long each of them lived in the body, he did not come back to life from the death in which impiety held him, so as, by thus coming back to life, to become a partaker of the first resurrection and that the second death should have no power over him.

**10.** There are those who think that resurrection cannot be spoken of except of bodies, and therefore contend that this first resurrection too will be of bodies. For, they say, it belongs to those to rise who fall. But bodies fall by dying; for indeed from falling they are called cadavers. Resurrection, they say, then, cannot be of souls but of bodies. But what do they say against the Apostle[^93], who calls it a resurrection? For it was according to the inner, not according to the outer man, that they had surely risen to whom he says: *if you have risen with Christ, savor the things that are above*[^94]. The same meaning he set down in other words elsewhere, saying: *that, just as Christ rose from the dead through the glory of the Father, so we too may walk in newness of life*[^95]. Hence too is that: *rise, you who sleep, and arise from the dead, and Christ will enlighten you*[^96].

But as for their saying that none can rise except those who fall, and therefore their thinking that resurrection pertains to bodies, not to souls, because it belongs to bodies to fall—why do they not hear: *do not depart from him, lest you fall*[^97]; and: *to his own Lord he stands or falls*[^98]; and: *he who thinks he stands, let him beware lest he fall*[^99]? For I think that this falling is to be guarded against in the soul, not in the body. If, then, resurrection belongs to those who fall, and souls too fall, surely it must be confessed that souls too rise.

But that, when he had said, *over these the second death has no power*, he subjoined and said, *but they will be priests of God and of Christ, and will reign with him for a thousand years*[^100], was certainly not said of bishops and presbyters alone, who are now properly called priests in the Church; but just as we call all "anointed" on account of the mystical chrism, so all "priests," since they are members of the one priest; of whom the apostle Peter says: *a holy people, a royal priesthood*[^101]. Indeed, briefly and in passing, he intimated that Christ is God, by saying: *priests of God and of Christ*, that is, of the Father and of the Son; although on account of the form of a servant[^102], as he became the Son of man, so also Christ was made a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek[^103]. Of this matter we have spoken more than once in this work[^104].

## What Gog, Magog, and the last persecution are.

**11.** *And when the thousand years*, he says, *shall be finished, Satan will be loosed from his prison, and will go out to deceive the nations which are in the four corners of the earth, Gog and Magog, and will draw them into war, whose number is as the sand of the sea*[^105]. To this end, then, he will deceive them: to draw them into this war. For before, too, in whatever ways he could, he was deceiving them by many and various evils.

But *he will go out* means "he will break forth into open persecution out of the lurking-places of his hatreds." For this will be the last persecution, when the last judgment is at hand, which the holy Church will suffer throughout the whole world—the whole city of Christ, that is, from the whole city of the devil, as great as each will be upon the earth. For these nations which he calls Gog and Magog are not to be taken as though they were some barbarians established in some part of the earth, whether those whom some suspect to be the Getae and Massagetae on account of the first letters of these names, or some other foreigners separated from Roman law. For they are signified to be throughout the whole world when it is said: *the nations which are in the four corners of the earth*, and he subjoined that these are *Gog and Magog*.

The interpretation of which names we find to be: Gog, "roof"; Magog, "from the roof"; as it were, "house" and "he who comes forth from the house." They are, then, the nations in which we understood the devil to be shut up above as in an abyss, and he himself in a certain way lifting himself up and coming forth from them; so that they are the roof, and he is "from the roof."

But if we refer both to the nations, not one of them to the nations and the other to the devil: then both they themselves are the roof, because in them now the ancient enemy is shut up and in a certain way covered; and they themselves will be "from the roof," when they are about to break forth into open hatred from concealment.

But what he says—*and they went up over the breadth of the earth and surrounded the camp of the saints and the beloved city*[^106]—does not mean that they came or are to come to one place, as though the camp of the saints and the beloved city were to be in some one place, since this is none other than the Church of Christ spread throughout the whole world;

and through this, wherever it will then be, since it will be among all the nations, which is signified by the name *the breadth of the earth*, there will be the camp of the saints, there will be his beloved city, there it will be surrounded by all its enemies—since they too will be with it among all the nations—by the enormity of that persecution; that is, it will be hemmed in by the straits of tribulation, hard pressed, shut in; nor will it forsake its warfare, which is named by the word "camp."

**12.** But what he says—*and fire came down from heaven and devoured them*[^107]—is not to be thought that final punishment which there will be when it shall be said: *depart from me, you cursed, into the eternal fire!*[^108] For then they themselves will be cast into the fire; the fire will not come from heaven upon them. But here *fire from heaven* is well understood of the very firmness of the saints, by which they will not yield to the raging ones so as to do their will.

For the firmament is heaven, by whose firmness those will be tortured with most burning zeal, since they will not be able to draw the saints of Christ over to the parts of Antichrist. And it will be this fire which will devour them, and this *from God*, because by the gift of God the saints are made insuperable, whence the enemies are tormented. For as it is set down in a good sense, *the zeal of your house has devoured me*[^109], so, on the contrary: *zeal has seized the untaught people, and now fire will devour the adversaries*[^110].

And this will itself be the fire that devours them, and it will be *from God*, because it is by God’s gift that the saints become unconquerable, and this torments their enemies.

For just as it is said in a good sense, *zeal for your house has consumed me*[^111], so, conversely, *jealousy has seized an untaught people, and now fire will devour the adversaries*[^112].

*And now*, surely, apart that is from the fire of that last judgment. Or if that blow, by which the persecutors of the Church are to be struck when Christ is now coming—those whom he will find living upon the earth, when he will slay Antichrist with the breath of his mouth[^113]—he called fire coming down from heaven and devouring them; neither will this be the final punishment of the impious, but that which they are to suffer when the resurrection of bodies has taken place.

**13.** This last persecution, which will come from Antichrist (as we have already said[^114], because it is set down both earlier in this book[^115] and in the prophet Daniel[^116]), will be for three years and six months. Whether this time, though small, belongs to the thousand years during which he says both that the devil is bound and that the saints reign with Christ, or whether this small span is added to those same years and is outside them, is deservedly doubted; because, if we should say that it belongs to those same years, then the kingdom of the saints with Christ will be found to extend over not so great but a longer time than the devil is bound. For surely the saints, with their king, will reign especially in that very persecution, overcoming such great evils, when the devil will no longer be bound, so that he can persecute them with all his strength. How then does this Scripture mark off both by the same thousand years—the binding of the devil, namely, and the kingdom of the saints—when, by the interval of three years and six months, the binding of the devil ends before the kingdom of the saints with Christ in these thousand years?

But if we say that this small span of this persecution is not to be reckoned in the thousand years, but rather added to them when they are completed, so that it may properly be understood that, when he had said, *the priests of God and of Christ will reign with him for a thousand years*, he added: *and when the thousand years shall be finished, Satan will be loosed from his prison*[^117]; for in this way he signifies that both the kingdom of the saints and the chain of the devil are to cease together, so that thereafter the time of that persecution is to be believed to belong neither to the kingdom of the saints nor to the imprisonment of Satan, both of which are within the thousand years, but to be superadded and reckoned outside;

for in this way he indicates that the saints’ reign and the devil’s imprisonment will cease together, so that the time of that persecution is understood as belonging neither to the saints’ reign nor to Satan’s confinement, both of which last a thousand years, but as added to them and counted separately;

we shall be forced to confess that the saints will not reign with Christ in that persecution. But who would bear that his members will not then reign with him, when they will cleave to him most and most strongly, and at a time when, the more fierce the onset of the war, the greater the glory of not yielding, the thicker the crown of martyrdom?

Or if, on account of the tribulations they are to suffer, they are not to be said to reign, it will follow that even in the earlier days within those same thousand years, whoever of the saints were being tribulated, at the very time of their tribulation are to be said not to have reigned with Christ; and through this even those whose souls the author of this book writes that he saw, *of those slain for the testimony of Jesus and for the word of God*, were not reigning with Christ when they were suffering persecution, and they themselves were not the kingdom of Christ, whom Christ possessed in a more excellent way.

This indeed is most absurd and in every way to be rejected. But surely the victorious souls of the most glorious martyrs, all pains and labors overcome and finished, after they have laid aside their mortal members, have certainly reigned and reign with Christ until the thousand years are finished, so that afterward, their bodies too being received now immortal, they may reign.

Accordingly, in those three years and a half, the souls of those slain for his martyrdom—both those which went out from their bodies before and those which are to go out in that last persecution—will reign with him until the mortal age is finished and there is a passing to that kingdom where there will be no death. Wherefore the years of the saints reigning with Christ will be more than the years of the devil's chain and imprisonment, because they, with their king the Son of God, will reign through those three years and a half also, when the devil is no longer bound.

It remains, then, that when we hear, *the priests of God and of Christ will reign with him for a thousand years, and when the thousand years shall be finished, Satan will be loosed from his prison*, we either understand that the thousand years of this kingdom of the saints are not finished, but those of the devil's chain and imprisonment—so that the thousand years, that is, all the years of each, every part to be finished by its own diverse lengths, the kingdom of the saints being the larger, the chain of the devil the shorter;

or else certainly, since the span of three years and six months is very short, it is to be believed that he did not wish to reckon it, whether because it seems to make less of Satan's chain or more of the kingdom of the saints—just as concerning the four hundred years I argued in the sixteenth volume of this work[^118], since they were something more, and yet were called four hundred; and such things are often found in the sacred letters, if anyone attends.

## What the book of life is in the judgment.

**14.** But after this mention of the last persecution, he briefly comprehends the whole of what, in the last judgment, the devil and the city hostile to God, along with its prince, are to suffer. For he says: *and the devil, who deceived them, was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone, where also the beast and the false prophet are; and they will be tormented day and night forever and ever*[^119]. The beast we have said above is well understood as that impious city itself[^120]. But its false prophet is either Antichrist or that image, that is, the figment, of which we spoke there.

After these things, narrating by recapitulation the last judgment itself, which will be at the second resurrection of the dead, which is of bodies, in the manner in which it was revealed to him: *and I saw*, he says, *a great and white throne and one seated upon it, from whose face heaven and earth fled, and their place was not found*[^121]. He did not say, "I saw a great and white throne and one seated upon it, and from his face heaven and earth fled," because this did not happen then, that is, before the living and the dead had been judged; but he said that he saw him seated on the throne, from whose face heaven and earth would flee, but afterward. For when the judgment is finished, then this heaven and this earth will cease to be, when there will begin to be a new heaven and a new earth[^122]. For it is by a change of things, not by an utter destruction, that this world will pass away. Whence the Apostle too says: *for the figure of this world is passing away; I wish you to be without anxiety*[^123]. The figure, then, passes away, not the nature.

When, then, John had said that he saw one seated upon the throne, from whose face—what is to be afterward—heaven and earth fled: *and I saw*, he says, *the dead, great and small, and books were opened; and another book was opened, which is the book of life of each one; and the dead were judged out of those things written in the books, according to their deeds*[^124]. He said that books were opened, and a book; but what sort of book he did not pass over in silence: *which is*, he says, *the book of life of each one*.

Therefore those books which he put in the first place are to be understood as holy ones, both old and new, that in them might be shown what things God had commanded to be done; but in that one *which is the book of life of each one*, which of these each one had not done or had done. And if this book be thought of in a carnal way, who could estimate its size or length? Or in how long a time could a book be read in which the whole lives of all are written? Or will there be present as great a number of angels as there will be of men, and will each one hear his life recited by the angel assigned to him? There will not, then, be one book of all, but single books of single persons.

But this Scripture, wishing one to be understood, says: *and another book was opened*. A certain divine power, then, is to be understood, by which it will come about that to each one all his works, whether good or evil, are recalled to memory and discerned by the gaze of the mind with marvelous swiftness, so that knowledge may accuse or excuse conscience, and thus both all and each are judged at once. This divine power, no doubt, received the name of "book." For in it, in a certain way, is read whatever, by its working, is recalled.

But to show who the dead are who are to be judged, small and great, he says by recapitulation, as though returning to what he had passed over or rather deferred: *and the sea gave up the dead which were in it, and death and hell gave back the dead which they had in them*[^125]. This without doubt happened before the dead were judged; and yet it was said afterward. This, then, is what I said: that by recapitulation he returned to what he had left out. But now he kept the order, and, that the order itself might be set forth, he more fittingly repeated in its own place, concerning the dead judged, what he had already said. For when he had said: *and the sea gave up the dead which were in it, and death and hell gave back the dead which they had in them*, he at once added what he had set down a little before: *and each one was judged according to his deeds*[^126]. For this is what he had said above: *and the dead were judged according to their deeds*.

**15.** But who are the dead which the sea gave up, which were in it? For neither are those who die in the sea not in hell, nor are their bodies kept in the sea; or—what is more absurd—the sea had the good dead and hell the wicked. Who would suppose this? But surely some fittingly take the sea here put for this present age. Since, then, he meant that those too whom Christ will find here established in the body are to be judged together with those who are to rise again, he called these too "dead," both the good, to whom it is said, *for you have died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God*[^127], and the wicked, of whom it is said, *let the dead bury their dead*[^128]. They can be called "dead" also for this reason, that they bear mortal bodies; whence the Apostle says: *the body indeed is dead because of sin, but the spirit is life because of righteousness*[^129], showing that both are in a living man and one established in this body—both a dead body and a spirit that is life. Yet he did not say a mortal body, but a dead one, although a little after he calls the same bodies mortal[^130] too, as they are more usually called. *These dead, then, the sea gave up*, which were in it, that is, this present age gave up the men, whoever were in it, because they had not yet died.

*And death and hell*, he says, *gave back the dead which they had in them*[^131]. The sea *gave up*, because, as they were found, they were present; but death and hell *gave back*, because they called them back to the life from which they had already gone out. Nor perhaps was it without purpose that it was not enough to say *death* or *hell*, but both were said: death on account of the good, who could suffer only death and not also hell; but hell on account of the wicked, who pay penalties also in the lower regions.

For if it does not seem absurd to believe that the ancient saints too, who held the faith of the Christ to come, were in places indeed most remote from the torments of the impious, but nevertheless in the lower regions, until the blood of Christ and his descent to those places rescued them thence, surely thereafter the good faithful, now redeemed by the shedding of that price, do not know the lower regions at all, until, their bodies too being received, they receive the goods which they merit.

But when he had said, *and each one was judged according to his deeds*, he briefly subjoined how they had been judged: *and death and hell*, he says, *were cast into the lake of fire*[^132], signifying by these names the devil, since he is the author of death and of the punishments of hell, and the whole society of demons together. For this is what above, by anticipation, he had already said more evidently: *and the devil, who deceived them, was cast into the lake of fire and brimstone*[^133]. But what there he had added more obscurely, saying, *where also the beast and the false prophet are*[^134], here he says more openly: *and whoever were not found written in the book of life were cast into the lake of fire*[^135].

This book does not remind God, lest he be deceived by forgetfulness; but it signifies the predestination of those to whom eternal life will be given. For neither is God ignorant of them and reads them in this book so as to know them; but rather his very foreknowledge concerning them, which cannot err, is the book of life in which they are written, that is, foreknown beforehand.

**16.** But the judgment being finished, in which he foretold that the wicked are to be judged, it remains for him to speak also of the good. For he has now set forth what was briefly said by the Lord: *so these will go away into eternal punishment*; it follows that he set forth what is also connected there: *but the just into eternal life*[^136]. *And I saw*, he says, *a new heaven and a new earth. For the first heaven and earth had passed away, and the sea is no more*[^137]. This will take place in the order which above, by anticipation, he had already stated—that he saw one seated upon the throne, from whose face heaven and earth fled[^138].

For when those who are not written in the book of life have been judged and cast into the eternal fire (of what sort that fire is, and in what part of the world or of things it will be, I think no man knows, unless perhaps to one the divine Spirit has shown it[^139]), then the figure of this world will pass away in a conflagration of worldly fires, just as the flood happened in an inundation of worldly waters. And so, as I said, in that worldly conflagration the qualities of the corruptible elements which suited our corruptible bodies will perish utterly by burning, and the very substance will have those qualities which by a marvelous change are suited to immortal bodies; so that, namely, the world, renewed for the better, may be fittingly accommodated to men too renewed for the better even in their flesh.

But what he says, *and the sea is no more*—whether by that greatest heat it is dried up, or whether it too is turned into something better—I could not easily say. For we read that there will be a new heaven and a new earth, but I do not recall having read anywhere anything of a new sea; except what is found in this same book: *as it were a sea of glass like crystal*[^140]. But there he was not speaking of this end of the age, nor does he seem to have spoken properly of the sea, but *as it were a sea*.

Although even now, as prophetic speech loves to mingle metaphorical with proper words and so in a certain way to veil what is said, he could have said of that sea, *and the sea is no more*, of which above he had said, *and the sea gave up the dead which were in it*[^141]. For then there will no longer be this present age, the life of mortals, turbulent and stormy, which he figured by the name of the sea.

## In that Jerusalem there will be no mourning or grief.

**17.** *And the great city*, he says, *the new Jerusalem, I saw coming down from heaven from God, made ready as a new bride adorned for her husband. And I heard a great voice from the throne saying: behold the tabernacle of God with men, and he will dwell with them, and they will be his people, and God himself will be with them. And he will wipe away every tear from their eyes; and death will be no more, nor mourning, nor crying, nor any pain, for the former things have passed away. And he who sat on the throne said: behold, I make all things new*[^142]. This city is said to come down from heaven, because heavenly is the grace by which God made it. Wherefore he also says to it through Isaiah: *I am the Lord who makes you*[^143].

And it comes down from heaven indeed from the beginning of itself, since throughout the time of this age the grace of God, coming from above through the laver of regeneration in the Holy Spirit sent from heaven, continually increases its citizens. But through the judgment of God, which will be the last, through his Son Jesus Christ, so great and so new a brightness from the gift of God will appear in it, that no traces of the old will remain; seeing that bodies too will pass from old corruption and mortality to new incorruption and immortality.

For to take this of the present time, when it reigns with its king for a thousand years, seems to me a mark of excessive impudence, since he says most plainly: *he will wipe away every tear from their eyes; and death will be no more, nor mourning, nor crying, nor any pain*. But who would be so absurd, and raging with most obstinate contentiousness, as to dare affirm that, amid the hardships of this mortality, not, I say, a holy people, but any single one of the saints—who either is leading or will lead or has led this life—has no tears and pains, when rather, the holier and the fuller of holy longing each one is, the more abundant is his weeping in prayer? Or is it not the voice of a citizen of the heavenly Jerusalem: *my tears have been my bread day and night*[^144]; and: *I will wash my bed every night, I will water my couch with my tears*[^145]; and: *my groaning is not hidden from you*[^146], and *my sorrow is ever renewed*[^147]?

Or are not his sons those who groan, being burdened, in that they do not wish to be unclothed but clothed upon, that what is mortal may be swallowed up by life[^148]? Are they not the very ones who, having the firstfruits of the Spirit, groan within themselves, awaiting adoption, the redemption of their body[^149]? Or was the apostle Paul himself not a citizen of the heavenly Jerusalem—or rather was he not much more so—when he had great sorrow and continual grief in his heart for the Israelites, his brothers according to the flesh[^150]? But when will death not be in this city, except when it shall be said: *where, O death, is your contention? Where, O death, is your sting? Now the sting of death is sin*[^151]? Which surely will not be then, when it shall be said: *where is it?* But now not just any weak citizen of that city, but that same John in his epistle cries out: *if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves and the truth is not in us*[^152].

And indeed in this book, whose name is the *Apocalypse*, many things are said obscurely, to exercise the mind of the reader, and there are few things in it from the clarity of which the rest may be traced out with labor—chiefly because it so repeats the same things in many ways that it seems to be saying different and different things, when in different and different ways it is found to be saying these very same things. But in these words, where he says, *he will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death will be no more, nor mourning, nor crying, nor any pain*, the things are said with so great a light concerning the age to come and concerning the immortality and eternity of the saints (for then alone and there alone will these things not be) that we ought to seek or read nothing manifest in the sacred letters, if we have thought these things obscure.

## What Peter says about the last conflagration.

**18.** Now let us see what the apostle Peter too wrote of this judgment: *there will come*, he says, *in the last of the days scoffers, walking according to their own desires and saying: where is the promise of his coming? For since the fathers fell asleep, all things continue thus from the beginning of creation. For this escapes them, willingly, that the heavens were of old, and the earth out of water and established by water through the word of God, by which the world that then was, being flooded with water, perished. But the heavens and earth which now are, by the same word are stored up, reserved for fire unto the day of judgment and of the perdition of impious men. But this one thing let it not escape you, beloved, that one day with the Lord is as a thousand years, and a thousand years as one day. The Lord does not delay his promise, as some count delay; but he bears patiently for your sake, not willing that anyone perish, but that all turn to repentance. But the day of the Lord will come as a thief, in which the heavens will pass away with a great rush, and the elements, burning, will be dissolved, and the earth and the works that are in it will be burned up. Since, then, all these things are to perish, what manner of men ought you to be in holy conversations, awaiting and hastening to the coming of the day of the Lord, through which the burning heavens will be dissolved and the elements melt with the heat of fire? But we await new heavens and a new earth according to his promises, in which righteousness dwells*[^153].

Here he said nothing of the resurrection of the dead, but enough indeed of the perdition of this world. Where also, recalling what was done before the flood, he seems in a certain way to have admonished us to what extent we should believe that this world is to perish at the end of this age. For he said that at that time too the world which then was perished; and not only the globe of the earth, but also the heavens, by which we surely understand these airy ones, whose place and space the water then overtopped by rising.

Therefore the whole, or nearly the whole, of this windy air (which he calls heaven, or rather heavens, but surely these lowest, not those highest, where the sun and moon and stars are established) had been turned into a watery quality and in this way had perished together with the earth, whose former face had certainly been blotted out by the flood. *But the heavens and earth which now are*, he says, *by the same word are stored up, reserved for fire unto the day of judgment and of the perdition of impious men*[^154].

Accordingly the heavens which, and the earth which—that is, the world which—in place of that world which perished in the flood, has been stored up out of the same water, is itself reserved for the last fire unto the day of judgment and of the perdition of impious men. For he does not hesitate to say that there will be a perdition of men too, on account of a certain great change, although nevertheless their nature, even in eternal punishments, is to remain.

Perhaps someone may ask: if, after the judgment is done, this world will burn before the new heaven and new earth are stored up in its place, where will the saints be at the very time of its conflagration, since, having bodies, they must of necessity be in some bodily place? We can answer that they will be in the upper regions, where the flame of that fire will not so ascend, just as neither did the wave of the flood. For they will have such bodies that they will be where they will to be. But neither will they fear the fire of that conflagration, being made immortal and incorruptible, if the corruptible and mortal bodies of the three men were able to live unharmed in the burning furnace[^155].

## What Paul said about Antichrist.

**19. 1.** I see that many gospel and apostolic sentences about this divine last judgment must be passed over by me, lest this volume be drawn out to too great a length; but the apostle Paul is in no way to be passed over, who, writing to the Thessalonians, says: *we beseech you, brothers, by the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ and our gathering together to him, that you be not quickly moved in mind nor terrified, neither by spirit nor by word nor by letter as though from us, as if the day of the Lord were at hand; let no one deceive you in any way; for unless the apostasy comes first and the man of sin be revealed, the son of perdition, who opposes and exalts himself above everything that is called God or that is worshiped, so that he sits in the temple of God, displaying himself as though he were God. Do you not hold in memory that, when I was still with you, these things were said to you? And now you know what restrains him, that he may be revealed in his own time.*

*For already the mystery of iniquity is at work. Only let him who now holds, hold, until he be taken out of the way; and then the lawless one will be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus will slay with the breath of his mouth and destroy by the brightness of his coming—him whose coming is according to the working of Satan, in all power and signs and lying wonders, and in every seduction of iniquity for those who perish, because they did not receive the love of the truth, that they might be saved. And therefore God will send them a working of error, that they may believe a lie, and that all may be judged who did not believe the truth but consented to iniquity*[^156].

**19. 2.** No one doubts that he said these things of Antichrist, and that the day of judgment (for this he calls the *day of the Lord*[^157]) will not come unless that one comes first, whom he calls the *apostate*, namely from the Lord God. And if this can deservedly be said of all the impious, how much more of this one! But in what temple of God he is to sit is uncertain—whether in that ruin of the temple which was built by King Solomon, or rather in the Church. For the Apostle would not call the temple of some idol or demon the *temple of God*.

Whence some wish to understand by Antichrist in this place not the prince himself but, in a certain way, his whole body, that is, the multitude of men belonging to him, together with their prince himself; and they think it is more correctly said even in Latin, as it is in the Greek, not *in the temple of God*, but "that he sit into the temple of God," as though he himself were the temple of God, which is the Church; just as we say, "he sits as a friend," that is, "as it were a friend," or whatever else is usually said by this kind of expression.

But what he says, *and now you know what restrains*—that is, what causes the delay, what is the reason of his postponement, *that he may be revealed in his own time*—you know: since he said that they knew, he did not wish to say it openly.

And therefore we, who do not know what they knew, desire with labor to arrive at what the Apostle meant, and are unable—especially because the things which he added make this meaning more obscure. For what is: *for already the mystery of iniquity is at work. Only let him who now holds, hold, until he be taken out of the way; and then the lawless one will be revealed*[^158]? I confess outright that I do not know what he meant. Yet I will not pass over in silence the conjectures of men which I have been able to hear or read.

**19. 3.** Some think this was said of the Roman empire, and that for this reason the apostle Paul did not wish to write it openly, lest, namely, he incur the charge of having wished ill to the Roman empire, when it was hoped to be eternal; so that what he said, *for already the mystery of iniquity is at work*[^159], he meant to be understood of Nero, whose deeds already seemed to be as it were those of Antichrist[^160].

Whence some suspect that he will rise again and will be the future Antichrist; others, however, think that he was not even slain but rather withdrawn, so that he might be thought slain, and that, alive, he is hidden in the vigor of that age in which he was when he was believed to be dead, *until in his own time* he is revealed and restored to the kingdom[^161].

But the great presumption of those who suppose such things is exceedingly strange to me.

Nevertheless, what the Apostle says, *only let him who now holds, hold, until he be taken out of the way*[^162], is not absurdly believed to have been said of the Roman empire itself, as though it were said: "only let him who now reigns, reign, until he be taken out of the way," that is, removed from the midst. *And then the lawless one will be revealed*: no one doubts that Antichrist is signified.

But others think that what he says, *you know what restrains* and *the mystery of iniquity is at work*[^163], was said only of the wicked and false who are in the Church, until they come to so great a number as to make a great people for Antichrist; and that this is the *mystery of iniquity*, because it seems hidden; and that the Apostle exhorts the faithful to persevere tenaciously in the faith they hold, by saying: *only let him who now holds, hold, until he be taken out of the way*; that is, until the mystery of iniquity, which is now hidden, goes out of the midst of the Church. For they judge that to this mystery belongs what the Evangelist John says in his epistle of certain men: *little children, it is the last hour; and as you have heard that Antichrist is coming, now many Antichrists have arisen; whence we know that it is the last hour. They went out from us, but they were not of us. For if they had been of us, they would surely have remained with us*[^164]. As, then, before the *end*, in this *hour*, they say, which John calls the last, many heretics went out from the midst of the Church, whom he calls many Antichrists, so all those will then go out from there who will belong not to Christ but to that last Antichrist, and then he will be revealed.

**19. 4.** One man, then, conjectures the Apostle's obscure words in one way, another in another; but what he undoubtedly said is this: that Christ will not come to judge the living and the dead[^165] unless first his adversary Antichrist comes to deceive those dead in soul—although that they are deceived by him belongs already to the hidden judgment of God. For *his coming*, as it was said, *will be according to the working of Satan, in all power and signs and lying wonders, and in every seduction of iniquity for those who perish*[^166]. For then Satan will be loosed, and through that Antichrist will work in all his power, marvelously indeed, but mendaciously.

It is usually doubted whether *signs and lying wonders* were called such because he is going to deceive mortal senses by phantasms, so that he may seem to do what he does not do, or because those very things, even if they will be true wonders, will draw to a lie those who will believe that they could not have been done except divinely, not knowing the power of the devil—especially when he will have received so great a power as he never had. For when fire fell from heaven and consumed at one onset the great household with the great flocks of cattle of holy Job, and the whirlwind, rushing in and throwing down the house, killed his sons, these were not phantasms; yet they were works of Satan, to whom God had given this power[^167].

On account of which of these, then, they were called wonders and signs of a lie, will rather appear at that time. But for whichever of these it was said, those will be seduced by these signs and wonders who will deserve to be seduced: *because they did not receive*, he says, *the love of the truth, that they might be saved*. Nor did the Apostle hesitate to add and say: *therefore God will send them a working of error, that they may believe a lie*. For God will send, because God will permit the devil to do these things by his own just judgment, although that one does them by a wicked and malignant design.

Nor did the Apostle hesitate to add, *therefore God will send them the working of error, so that they may believe a lie*. For God will send it because God will permit the devil to do these things: God does so by a just judgment, although the devil acts with an unjust and malicious purpose.

*That they may be judged*, he says, *all who did not believe the truth but consented to iniquity*[^168]. Accordingly, being judged they will be seduced, and being seduced they will be judged.

But being judged they will be seduced by those judgments of God secretly just, justly secret, by which from the beginning of the sin of the rational creature he has never ceased to judge; but being seduced they will be judged by that last and manifest judgment through Christ Jesus, who will judge most justly, having been most unjustly judged.

## In the first letter to the Thessalonians Paul speaks of the resurrection.

**20. 1.** But here the Apostle was silent about the resurrection of the dead; writing, however, to the same people in his first letter, he says: *we do not wish you, brothers, to be ignorant concerning those who sleep, that you may not be saddened, as the rest also who have no hope. For if we believe that Jesus died and rose again, so also God will bring with him those who have fallen asleep through Jesus.*

*For this we say to you in the word of the Lord, that we who are alive, who remain unto the coming of the Lord, will not precede those who have fallen asleep before; for the Lord himself, with a command and with the voice of an archangel and with the trumpet of God, will descend from heaven, and the dead in Christ will rise first; then we who are alive, who remain, will together with them be caught up in the clouds to meet Christ in the air, and so we shall always be with the Lord*[^169]. These apostolic words most clearly show the future resurrection of the dead, when Christ will come, surely to judge the living and the dead.

**20. 2.** But it is usually asked whether those whom Christ will find living here—whose person the Apostle was transferring to himself and to those who were then living with him—will never die at all, or whether at that very point of time in which, with those rising again, they will be caught up in the clouds to meet Christ in the air, they will, through death, pass to immortality with marvelous swiftness. For it is not to be said that it cannot happen that, while they are being carried aloft through the air, they should in that space both die and come back to life.

For what he says, *and so we shall always be with the Lord*[^170], is not to be taken as though he said that we shall remain always with the Lord in the air; for neither will he himself surely remain there, since, coming, he is to pass on; for he is met as he comes, not as he remains. But *so we shall be with the Lord*, that is, we shall be thus, having everlasting bodies, wherever we shall be with him.

And to this meaning—by which we might suppose that even those whom the Lord will find alive here will, in that very small span, both suffer death and receive immortality—the Apostle himself seems to press us, where he says: *in Christ all will be made alive*[^171]; since in another place, speaking of this resurrection of bodies, he says: *what you sow is not made alive unless it dies*[^172]. How then will those whom Christ will find living here be made alive through immortality in him, even if they do not die, when we see that on this account it was said: *what you sow is not made alive unless it dies*? Or if we cannot rightly say that any bodies of men are "sown" except those which by dying in some way return to the earth (as is also that sentence divinely uttered against the father of the human race, the transgressor: *you are earth, and into earth you shall go*[^173]), it must be confessed that those whom, not yet gone out of their bodies, Christ will find when he comes, are bound neither by these words of the Apostle nor by those of Genesis; since, caught up aloft in the clouds, they are surely not "sown," because they neither go into the earth nor return, whether they experience no death at all or die for a little while in the air.

**20. 3.** But again something else occurs which the same Apostle said, when he was speaking to the Corinthians of the resurrection of bodies: *we shall all rise again*, or, as other manuscripts have it, *we shall all sleep*[^174]. Since, then, neither can resurrection take place unless death has preceded, nor can we understand "falling asleep" in that place except as "death," how will "all" either sleep or rise again, if so many, whom Christ will find in the body, will neither sleep nor rise again?

If, then, we believe that the saints who will be found living when Christ comes, and who will be caught up to meet him, will in that same catching-up go out of their mortal bodies and immediately return to those same bodies made immortal, we shall suffer no difficulties in the Apostle's words—whether where he says, *what you sow is not made alive unless it dies*, or where he says, *we shall all rise again* or *we shall all sleep*; because they too will not be made alive through immortality unless, however little, they nevertheless first die, and through this they will not be alien from a resurrection which they precede by a falling-asleep, however brief, yet not none at all.

But why should it seem incredible to us that that multitude of bodies should in a certain way be "sown" in the air and there forthwith come back to life immortally and incorruptibly, since we believe—what that same Apostle most plainly says—that the resurrection will be *in the twinkling of an eye*[^175], and that the dust of the most ancient corpses will return into members destined to live without end with so great ease and so inestimable swiftness?

Nor let us think those saints exempt from that sentence by which it was said to man, *you are earth, and into earth you shall go*[^176], if their bodies, when they die, do not fall back into the earth, but, as they die in the very catching-up, so they also rise again while they are carried into the air. For *into the earth you shall go* means: "into this you shall go, your life lost, which you were before you took on life"; that is, you shall be this, when deprived of soul, which you were before you were animated (for into the face of the earth God breathed the breath of life, when man was made into a living soul);

as though it were said: "you are animated earth, which you were not; you shall be earth without soul, as you were"; which all the bodies of the dead are even before they decay; which those too will be, if they die, wherever they die, when they will lack the life which they are immediately to receive again.

Thus, then, they shall go into the earth, because from living men they shall be earth, just as that goes into ash which becomes ash, that goes into old age which becomes old, that goes into a potsherd which from clay becomes a potsherd; and a thousand other things we speak of in this way. But in what manner that will be which we now in some way conjecture, as best we can, by the powers of our poor reasoning, will rather be then, that we may be able to know it. For the resurrection of the dead, to come and in the flesh, when Christ is to come to judge the living and the dead, we ought to believe, if we wish to be Christians; but not for that reason is our faith in this matter empty, if we are unable to comprehend perfectly in what way it will be.

But now, as we promised above[^177], we ought to show what the old prophetic books too have foretold of this last judgment of God, as much as shall seem enough; which, as I think, it will not be necessary to treat and set forth at such great length, if the reader takes care to be aided by those things which we have set down before.

## What blessedness Isaiah describes in the resurrection.

**21. 1.** The prophet Isaiah says: *the dead will rise, and those who were in the tombs will rise, and all who are on the earth will rejoice; for the dew which is from you is health to them; but the earth of the impious will fall*[^178]. All that earlier part pertains to the resurrection of the blessed. But what he says, *but the earth of the impious will fall*, is well understood as said: "but the ruin of damnation will receive the bodies of the impious." And further, if we wish to look more diligently and distinctly at what is said of the resurrection of the good, what was said, *the dead will rise*, must be referred to the first; but what follows, *and those who were in the tombs will rise*, to the second. And if we inquire also into those saints whom the Lord will find living here, to them will fittingly be assigned what he added: *and all who are on the earth will rejoice; for the dew which is from you is health to them*. By *health* in this place we most rightly take "immortality"; for that is the fullest health, which is not restored by foods as though by daily medicines.

Likewise, concerning the day of judgment, first giving hope to the good and then terrifying the wicked, the same prophet speaks thus: *thus says the Lord: behold, I incline upon them as a river of peace and as a torrent overflowing the glory of the nations. Their sons will be carried on shoulders and comforted upon knees. As one whom his mother comforts, so will I comfort you; and in Jerusalem you will be comforted, and you will see, and your heart will rejoice, and your bones will spring up like grass. And the hand of the Lord will be known to those who worship him, and he will threaten the defiant. For behold, the Lord will come like fire, and like a tempest his chariots, to render vengeance in indignation and devastation in a flame of fire. For in the fire of the Lord all the earth will be judged, and in his sword all flesh; many will be wounded by the Lord*[^179].

In the promise to the good, by *a river of peace* we ought surely to understand the abundance of that peace than which none can be greater. By this we shall be watered at the end; of which we spoke abundantly in the preceding book.

He says that this river inclines upon those to whom he promises so great a blessedness, that we may understand that in that region of felicity, which is in the heavens, all things are satisfied by this river; but because the peace of incorruption and immortality flows thence even into earthly bodies, therefore he said that this river inclines itself, to flood as it were even the lower things from the higher and to make men equal to the angels.

By *Jerusalem* too let us understand, not that one which serves with her children, but our free mother, according to the Apostle, eternal in the heavens[^180]. There, after the labors of mortal hardships and cares, we shall be comforted, as her little ones carried on shoulders and knees. For that blessedness, unaccustomed to us, will receive us, raw and new, with most gentle aids. There we shall see, and our heart will rejoice.

Nor did he express what we shall see; but what except God? that there may be fulfilled in us the gospel promise: *blessed are the pure in heart, for they will see God*[^181], and all those things which we now do not see, but, believing, think of them—according to the small measure of human capacity—far less than they are, and incomparably so. *And you will see*, he says, *and your heart will rejoice*. Here you believe, there you will see.

**21. 2.** But since he said, *and your heart will rejoice*, lest we should suppose that those good things of Jerusalem pertain only to our spirit, *and your bones*, he says, *will spring up like grass*; where he touched on the resurrection of bodies, as though restoring what he had not said; for not when we shall see will it take place, but when it has taken place we shall see. For he had already said above of the new heaven and the new earth, since he spoke often and in manifold ways of those things which are promised to the saints at the end.

*There will be*, he says, *a new heaven and a new earth, and they will not be mindful of the former things, nor will it come up into their heart, but they will find joy and exultation in it. Behold, I will make Jerusalem an exultation and my people a joy; and I will exult in Jerusalem and rejoice in my people; and no more will the voice of weeping be heard in her*[^182], and the rest, which some try to refer to those carnal thousand years.

For figurative expressions are, in the prophetic manner, mingled with proper ones, so that a sober attention may arrive at the spiritual understanding with a certain useful and salutary labor; but carnal sloth, or the slowness of an untaught and unexercised mind, content with the surface of the letter, thinks that nothing within is to be sought. Let this much suffice to have said about the prophetic words which are written before this place.

But in this place, from which we digressed to those things, when he had said, *and your bones will spring up like grass*, to show that he was now recalling the resurrection of the flesh indeed, but of the good, he added: *and the hand of the Lord will be known to those who worship him*. What is this but the hand of one distinguishing his worshipers from his despisers?

Of whom, weaving in what follows: *and he will threaten*, he says, *the defiant*, or, as another translator has it, *the unbelievers*[^183]. Nor will he threaten then; but those things which are now said threateningly will then be effectively fulfilled. *For behold, the Lord*, he says, *will come like fire, and like a tempest his chariots, to render vengeance in indignation and devastation in a flame of fire. For in the fire of the Lord all the earth will be judged, and in his sword all flesh; many will be wounded by the Lord*[^184].

Whether by *fire* or by *tempest* or by *sword*, he signifies the punishment of the judgment; seeing that he says the Lord himself will come as it were a fire, surely for those for whom his coming will be penal. But his *chariots* (for they are spoken of in the plural) we not unfittingly take as the angelic ministries.

But that he says all the earth and all flesh are judged in his fire and sword, let us not understand here too the spiritual and the saints, but the earthly and carnal, of such as it was said: *who savor earthly things*[^185]; and: *to savor according to the flesh is death*[^186]; and such as are altogether called flesh by the Lord, where he says: *my spirit will not remain in these men, because they are flesh*[^187].

But what is set down here, *many will be wounded by the Lord*, by this wound the second death will be wrought. *Fire* indeed and *sword* and *wound* can also be taken in a good sense. For the Lord said he wished to send fire into the world[^188], and divided tongues as it were of fire were seen by them when the Holy Spirit came[^189]; and: *I came not*, says the same Lord, *to send peace upon the earth, but a sword*[^190]; and Scripture calls the word of God a two-edged sword on account of the twofold edge of the two Testaments[^191]; and in the *Song of Songs*[^192] the holy Church says that she is wounded by love, as though pierced by the arrow of love. But here, when we read or hear that the Lord is to come as an avenger, it is clear how these things are to be understood.

**21. 3.** Then, having briefly mentioned those who will be consumed through this judgment—signifying sinners and the impious under the figure of the foods forbidden in the old Law, from which they did not abstain—he recapitulates from the beginning the grace of the New Testament, leading and ending his discourse from the first coming of the Savior up to the last judgment, of which we are now treating. For he narrates that the Lord says he is coming to gather all the nations, and that they will come and see his glory[^193]. *For all*, as the Apostle says, *have sinned and lack the glory of God*[^194].

And he says that he will leave signs upon them, by which, marveling, they may believe in him; and that he will send forth from them the saved into nations diverse and far-off islands which have not heard his name nor seen his glory; and that they will announce his glory among the nations and bring the brothers of those to whom he was speaking, that is, in faith brothers of the elect Israelites under God the Father;

and that they will bring from all nations a gift to the Lord on beasts of burden and in vehicles[^195] (which beasts and vehicles are well understood to be the divine helps through the ministries of God of every kind, whether angelic or human) into the holy city Jerusalem, which is now spread through the lands in the holy faithful. For where they are divinely helped, there they believe, and where they believe, there they come.

But the Lord compared them, by a likeness as it were, to the sons of Israel offering him their sacrifices with Psalms in his house, which the Church now does everywhere; and he promised that from them he would receive for himself priests and Levites, which we see being done none the less now. For not from the race of flesh and blood, as it was first according to the order of Aaron, but, as was fitting in the New Testament, where the high priest is Christ according to the order of Melchizedek[^196], we see priests and Levites now chosen for each one's merit, by what the divine grace has conferred on him—who are to be weighed not by this name, which the unworthy often attain, but by that holiness which is not common to the good and the wicked.

**21. 4.** When he had said these things of that mercy of God, which is now imparted to the Church, clear and most known to us, he also promised the ends to which, through the last judgment, with the separation of the good and the wicked accomplished, things will come, saying through the prophet, or, of the Lord, the prophet himself saying: *for as the new heaven and the new earth will remain before me, says the Lord, so will your seed and your name stand; and there will be month from month and sabbath from sabbath. All flesh will come into my sight to adore in Jerusalem, says the Lord; and they will go out and will see the members of the men who transgressed against me. Their worm will not die, and their fire will not be extinguished, and they will be a sight to all flesh*[^197].

At this point this prophet ended his book, at the point at which the age will be ended. Some indeed did not translate *the members of the men*, but "the cadavers of the men," by "cadavers" signifying the evident punishment of bodies; although "cadaver" is not usually called anything but flesh without soul, whereas those bodies will be animated—otherwise they will be able to feel no torments; unless perhaps, because they will be the bodies of the dead, that is, of those who fall into the second death, they may not absurdly be called "cadavers" too.

Whence is also that which, said by the same prophet, I set down above: *but the earth of the impious will fall*[^198]. But who does not see that "cadavers" are named from "falling"? But that those translators put *of the men* for what is "of human beings" is manifest. For no one will say that transgressing women will not be in that punishment; but from the more important one—especially the one from whom woman was made[^199]—both sexes are understood.

But, what most pertains to the matter, since both in the case of the good it is said, *all flesh will come*, because that people will consist of every kind of men (for not all men will be there, since in punishments there will be more), but, as I had begun to say, since both in the case of the good "flesh" and in the case of the wicked "members" or "cadavers" are named, surely it is declared that, after the resurrection of the flesh—the faith of which is wholly confirmed by these terms of things—that judgment will take place by which the good and the wicked will be separated to their own ends.

**22.** But how will the good *go out* to see the punishments of the wicked? Will they by a movement of the body leave those blessed seats and proceed to the penal places, to behold the torments of the wicked by bodily presence? Far from it; but they will *go out* through knowledge. For by this word it is signified that those who will be tormented will be without.

Wherefore the Lord too calls those places *outer darkness*[^200], to which is contrary that "entering in," of which it is said to the good servant: *enter into the joy of your Lord*[^201]; lest the wicked be thought to enter there, that they may be known, but rather that the good, as it were, go out to them by the knowledge with which they will know them, because they will know that which is without.

For those who will be in punishments will not know what is done within in the joy of the Lord; but those who will be in that joy will know what is done without in those outer darknesses. Therefore it was said, *they will go out*[^202], because those things too which will be outside them will surely not be hidden from them. For if the prophets could know things not yet done, through this, that God was—as much as he was—in the minds of those mortals, how will the immortal saints then not know things already done, when God will be all in all[^203]?

There will stand, then, in that blessedness of the saints both the seed and the name; the seed, namely, of which John says: *and his seed remains in him*[^204]; the name, of which it was said through this Isaiah: *I will give them an eternal name*[^205]. *There will be for them month from month and sabbath from sabbath*[^206], as it were "moon from moon and rest from rest"; both of which they themselves will be, when from these old and temporal shadows they pass into those new and everlasting lights.

But in the punishments of the wicked, both the inextinguishable fire and the most living worm[^207] have been expounded by some this way and by others that. For some referred both to the body, others both to the soul; others properly the fire to the body, figuratively the worm to the soul, which seems more credible. But now there is no time to argue this difference. For we have undertaken to fill this volume with the last judgment, by which the separation of the good and the wicked will take place; but of the rewards and punishments themselves we must treat more diligently elsewhere.

## What Daniel said of the four beasts and ten kings.

**23. 1.** Daniel prophesies of this last judgment in such a way that he also foretells that Antichrist will come first, and brings his narration up to the eternal kingdom of the saints. For when, in a prophetic vision, he had seen four beasts signifying four kingdoms, and the fourth itself overcome by a certain king, who is recognized as Antichrist, and after these things the eternal kingdom of the Son of man, who is understood to be Christ: *my spirit shuddered*, he says, *I, Daniel, in my frame, and the visions of my head troubled me. And I drew near*, he says, *to one of those who stood by, and I sought the truth from him concerning all these things, and he told me the truth*[^208].

Then, as though the other were expounding to him, he speaks thus of what he heard from the one of whom he asked about all these things: *these four great beasts are four kingdoms which will arise on the earth, which will be taken away, and the saints of the Most High will receive the kingdom and will possess it forever and for ever and ever. And I sought*, he says, *diligently concerning the fourth beast, which was different from every beast, more terrible (its teeth were of iron and its claws of bronze, devouring and crushing and trampling the rest with its feet), and concerning its ten horns which were in its head, and concerning the other, which came up and shook out three of the former ones; that horn in which were eyes and a mouth speaking great things, and its appearance was greater than the rest. I watched, and that horn made war with the saints and prevailed against them, until the ancient of days came, and gave the kingdom to the saints of the Most High; and the time came, and the saints obtained the kingdom*[^209].

These things Daniel said he had asked. Then, immediately subjoining what he heard: *and he said*, he says (that is, the one of whom he had asked answered and said): *the fourth beast will be a fourth kingdom on the earth, which will prevail over all kingdoms; and it will devour the whole earth, and will trample it down and cut it in pieces. And its ten horns are ten kings who will arise; and after them another will arise, who will surpass in evils all who were before him; and he will humble three kings and will speak words against the Most High and will wear down the saints of the Most High and will suppose that he can change times and the law; and it will be given into his hand until a time and times and half a time. And the judgment will sit, and they will remove the dominion, to exterminate and destroy it unto the end; and the kingdom and the power and the greatness of the kings who are under all heaven was given to the saints of the Most High. And his kingdom is an everlasting kingdom; and all dominions will serve and obey him. Thus far*, he says, *is the end of the discourse. I, Daniel; my thoughts greatly troubled me, and my appearance was changed upon me, and I kept the word in my heart*[^210].

Those four kingdoms some have expounded as those of the Assyrians, the Persians, the Macedonians, and the Romans. But how fittingly they did this, those who wish to know may read the book of the presbyter Jerome on Daniel, written with sufficient learning and diligence[^211]. But that the most savage kingdom of Antichrist against the Church—though it must be endured for a small span of time—is to be borne until the saints receive the everlasting kingdom by the last judgment of God, no one who reads this even half-asleep is allowed to doubt.

For that *a time and times and half a time* is one year and two and a half—and through this three years and a half—becomes clear from the number of days set down afterward[^212], and is sometimes in the Scriptures also declared by the number of months. For *times* seems to be said here indefinitely in the Latin tongue; but it is said by the dual number, which the Latins do not have. But as the Greeks have it, so the Hebrews too are said to have it. Thus, then, *times* was said, as though "two times" were said. I confess that I am indeed afraid lest in the *ten kings*, whom Antichrist seems about to find as it were ten men, we may perhaps be deceived, and so that one may come unexpectedly, there not being so many kings in the Roman world. For what if by this number ten the universality of kings was signified, after whom that one is to come?—just as by a thousand, a hundred, a seven, universality is for the most part signified, and by other and other numbers, which it is not now necessary to mention.

**23. 2.** In another place the same Daniel says: *and there will be a time of tribulation such as has not been since a nation was born upon the earth up to that time. And in that time your whole people will be saved, whoever is found written in the book. And many of those sleeping in the mound of the earth will rise: these to eternal life, and these to reproach and to eternal confusion. And those who understand will shine like the brightness of the firmament, and of the just many like stars forever and beyond*[^213]. This place is very like that gospel sentence, at least concerning the resurrection of the bodies of the dead.

For those who there were said to be *in the tombs*[^214], the same here are *those sleeping in the mound of the earth*, or, as others translated, *in the dust of the earth*; and as there it was said, *they will come forth*, so here, *they will rise*; as there, *those who have done good, to the resurrection of life; but those who have done evil, to the resurrection of judgment*[^215], so also in this place: *these to eternal life, and these to reproach and to eternal confusion*.

Nor let it be thought a discrepancy that, whereas there it was set down, *all who are in the tombs*, here the prophet does not say *all*, but *many of those sleeping in the mound of the earth*. For Scripture sometimes puts "many" for "all." Therefore it was said to Abraham too: *I have made you a father of many nations*[^216], to whom nevertheless it is said in another place: *in your seed*, he says, *all nations will be blessed*[^217].

And of such a resurrection it is said also to this same prophet Daniel a little after: *and you, come and rest; for there are yet days unto the fulfilling of the consummation, and you will rest and will rise in your lot at the end of the days*[^218].

## What Psalm 102 (101) said of the end of the world.

**24. 1.** Many things about the last judgment are said in the Psalms, but most of them in passing and briefly. Yet what is there most plainly said of the end of this age I shall by no means pass over in silence. *In the beginning you founded the earth, O Lord, and the heavens are the works of your hands. They will perish, but you remain; and all will grow old like a garment, and like a covering you will change them, and they will be changed; but you are the same, and your years will not fail*[^219]. Why is it that Porphyry, when he praises the piety of the Hebrews, by which the great and true God, terrible even to the deities themselves, is worshiped by them, charges the Christians on this account with the greatest folly, even from the oracles of his gods, because they say this world is to perish? Behold, in the letters of the piety of the Hebrews it is said to the God whom—as so great a philosopher confesses—the deities themselves shudder at: *the heavens are the works of your hands; they will perish*.

When the heavens perish, will the world, of which these same heavens are the higher and safer part, not perish? If this sentence displeases Jove, by whose oracle—as this philosopher writes—of as it were weightier authority the Christians are blamed in their belief, why does he not likewise blame the wisdom of the Hebrews as folly, in whose most devout books it is found?

Further, if in that wisdom which pleases Porphyry so much that he proclaims it even by the voices of his own gods it is read that the heavens are to perish, why is that fallacy so empty that, in the faith of the Christians, whether among other things or above other things, they detest this: that in it the world is believed to be going to perish—which surely the heavens cannot perish unless it perishes? And in the sacred letters indeed which are properly ours, not common to the Hebrews and us—that is, in the gospel and apostolic books—it is read: *the figure of this world is passing away*[^220]; it is read: *the world passes away*[^221]; it is read: *heaven and earth will pass away*[^222]. But I think that *passes away, passes, will pass away* are said somewhat more mildly than "will perish."

In the epistle too of the apostle Peter, where the world that then was is said to have perished, flooded with water[^223], it is clear enough both what part of the world is signified by the whole, and in what sense it is said to have perished, and which heavens are stored up to be reserved for fire unto the day of judgment and the perdition of impious men; and in that which a little after he says: *the day of the Lord will come as a thief, in which the heavens will pass away with a great rush, and the elements, burning, will be dissolved, and the earth and the works that are in it will be burned up*; and then he subjoined: *since all these things are to perish, what manner of men ought you to be?*[^224]

those heavens can be understood as to perish which he said were stored up to be reserved for fire, and those elements taken as to burn which subsist in this lowest part of the world, stormy and turbulent, in which he said those same heavens were stored up—the higher ones being safe and remaining in their integrity, in whose firmament the stars are established.

For even that which is written, *the stars will fall from heaven*[^225]—besides that it can be understood much more probably and otherwise—rather shows that those heavens will remain, if indeed the stars are to fall from them;

since either it is a figurative expression, which is more credible, or it will take place in this lowest heaven, surely more marvelously than now happens. Whence too is that Virgilian star which *ran on drawing a torch with much light*[^226], and hid itself in the Idaean wood.

But this which I mentioned from the Psalm seems to leave none of the heavens which it did not say is to perish. For where it is said, *the heavens are the works of your hands; they will perish*[^227], just as none of them is separated from the work of God, so none of them is separated from perdition.

For they will not deign, from the expression of the apostle Peter, whom they vehemently hate, to defend the piety of the Hebrews, approved by the oracles of their own gods,

so that at least, lest the whole world be believed to be going to perish, the part might be taken from the whole in what was said, *they will perish*, since only the lowest heavens are to perish—just as in that apostolic epistle the part is taken from the whole, when the world is said to have perished in the flood, although only its lowest part with its heavens perished.

But because, as I said, they will not deign to do this—lest they either approve the meaning of the apostle Peter, or concede to the last conflagration as much as we say the flood availed, they who contend that the whole human race cannot perish by any waters or any flames—it remains for them to say that their gods praised the Hebrew wisdom for this reason: because they had not read this Psalm.

**24. 2.** In the fiftieth Psalm too it is understood to be said of the last judgment of God: *God will come manifest, our God, and will not keep silence. A fire will burn in his sight, and round about him a mighty tempest. He will summon the heaven above and the earth to separate his people. Gather to him his just ones, who arrange his covenant over sacrifices*[^228]. This we understand of the Lord Jesus Christ, whom we hope is to come from heaven to judge the living and the dead. For he will come manifest, to judge the just and the unjust justly, who first came hidden, to be judged by the unjust unjustly. He, I say, *will come manifest and will not keep silence*, that is, will appear evident in the voice of the judge, who, when he first had come hidden, was silent before the judge, when *he was led as a sheep to the slaughter, and as a lamb before its shearer was without voice*, as we read prophesied of him through Isaiah[^229] and see fulfilled in the Gospel[^230].

But of the *fire* and the *tempest*, when we were treating some such thing in the prophecy of Isaiah, we have already said how this is to be understood[^231]. But what was said, *he will summon the heaven above*—since the saints and the just are rightly called *heaven*—is surely that which the Apostle says: *together with them we shall be caught up in the clouds to meet Christ in the air*[^232]. For according to the surface of the letter, how is heaven summoned above, as though it could be anywhere but above?

But what was added, *and the earth, to separate his people*—if only *he will summon* is to be understood, that is, "he will summon the earth too," and *above* is not understood—this seems to have this sense according to right faith: that *heaven* is understood in those who will judge with him, and *earth* in those who are to be judged; so that by *he will summon the heaven above* we understand here not "he will catch up into the air," but "he will raise to the seats of judgment."

That too can be understood: *he will summon the heaven above*, "he will summon the angels in the supreme and exalted places, with whom he descends to do judgment"; "he will summon *the earth* too," that is, men on the earth, surely to be judged. But if both are to be understood when it is said *and the earth*—that is, both *he will summon* and *above*—so that this is the sense, "he will summon the heaven above, and the earth he will summon above"; I think nothing better is to be understood than all who will be caught up to meet Christ in the air, but *heaven* said on account of the souls, *earth* on account of the bodies.

Further, *to separate his people*—what is this but by judgment to separate the good from the wicked, as the sheep from the goats? Then a turning of the discourse to the angels is made: *gather to him his just ones*; for surely so great a matter is to be accomplished through angelic ministry. But if we ask which just ones the angels are to gather to him: *who arrange*, he says, *his covenant over sacrifices*. This is the whole life of the just: to arrange the covenant of God over sacrifices. For either works of mercy are *over sacrifices*, that is, to be preferred to sacrifices, according to the sentence of God, who says: *I desire mercy rather than sacrifice*[^233]; or, if *over sacrifices* is understood as said for "in sacrifices," in the way it is said that something is done over the earth which surely is done in the earth—surely the very works of mercy are the sacrifices by which God is pleased, as I remember having argued in the tenth book of this work[^234]; in which works the just arrange the covenant of God, because they do these things on account of the promises which are contained in his New Testament.

Whence, when his just ones have been gathered to him and established at his right hand, in the last judgment Christ will surely say: *come, blessed of my Father, possess the kingdom prepared for you from the foundation of the world. For I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^235], and the rest which are there brought forward concerning the good works of the good and concerning their everlasting rewards by the last sentence of the one judging.

## Malachi says something about purgatorial punishments.

**25.** The prophet Malachiel, or Malachi, who was also called "angel," and who is believed by some to be also Ezra the priest, some of whose other writings were received into the canon (for Jerome says that this is the opinion of the Hebrews about him[^236]), prophesies of the last judgment, saying: *behold, he comes, says the Lord almighty; and who will endure the day of his entering, or who will be able to bear to look upon him? For he enters like the fire of the refiner and like the herb of those who wash; and he will sit refining and cleansing like silver and like gold, and he will cleanse the sons of Levi and will pour them out like gold and silver, and they will be offering sacrifices to the Lord in righteousness, and the sacrifice of Judah and Jerusalem will please the Lord, as in the former days and as in the earlier years. And I will draw near to you in judgment, and I will be a swift witness against the doers of evil and against adulterers and against those who swear falsely in my name, and who defraud the hired man of his wages and oppress widows by power and strike orphans and pervert the judgment of the stranger, and who do not fear me, says the Lord almighty; for I am the Lord your God, and I am not changed*[^237].

From what has been said it seems to appear more evidently that in that judgment there will be certain purgatorial punishments of certain persons. For where it is said, *who will endure the day of his entering, or who will be able to bear to look upon him? For he enters like the fire of the refiner and like the herb of those who wash; and he will sit refining and cleansing like silver and like gold, and will cleanse the sons of Levi and will pour them out like gold and silver*, what else is to be understood? Isaiah too says something of this kind: *the Lord will wash away the filth of the sons and daughters of Sion, and will cleanse the blood from their midst by a spirit of judgment and a spirit of burning*[^238]. Unless perhaps it must be said that they are cleansed from filth and in a certain way refined when the wicked are separated from them by a penal judgment, so that the separation and condemnation of such is the purification of these, because for the rest they are to live without the mixture of such men.

But when he says, *and he will cleanse the sons of Levi and will pour them out like gold and silver; and they will be offering sacrifices to the Lord in righteousness, and the sacrifice of Judah and Jerusalem will please the Lord*, he surely shows that those very ones who will be cleansed will thereafter please the Lord in the sacrifices of righteousness, and through this that they themselves will be cleansed from their own unrighteousness, in which they displeased the Lord.

Furthermore, they themselves, in full and perfect righteousness, when they have been cleansed, will be the victims. For what do such men offer to God more acceptable than themselves? But this question concerning purgatorial punishments, that it may be treated more diligently, must be deferred to another time.

But by *the sons of Levi* and *Judah* and *Jerusalem* we ought to understand the Church of God itself, gathered not from the Hebrews only but also from other nations; nor such as it is now, where, *if we say that we have no sin, we deceive ourselves, and the truth is not in us*[^239]; but such as it will be then, purified by the last judgment as a threshing-floor by winnowing[^240], those too being cleansed by fire to whom such cleansing is necessary, so that there is no one at all who offers sacrifice for his own sins. For all who so offer are surely in the sins for the forgiveness of which they offer, so that, when they have offered and it has been accepted by God, then they may be forgiven.

**26. 1.** But God, wishing to show that his city will then not be in this custom, said that the sons of Levi will offer sacrifices in righteousness; not, then, *in* sin, and through this not *for* sin. Whence it can be understood, in what he went on to add and say, *and the sacrifice of Judah and Jerusalem will please the Lord, as in the former days and as in the earlier years*[^241], that the Jews promise themselves in vain, according to the law of the Old Testament, the past times of their sacrifices.

For at that time they did not offer their victims in righteousness, but in sins, when chiefly and first they offered for sins—to such a degree that the priest himself, whom we ought surely to believe was more just than the rest, was accustomed, according to God's command, to offer first for his own sins, then for the people's[^242]. Wherefore we ought to explain how that is to be taken which was said, *as in the former days and as in the earlier years*. For perhaps it recalls that time when the first human beings were in paradise. For then, pure and whole, free from all filth and stain of sin, they offered themselves to God as most clean victims; but from the time when, on account of the transgression committed, they were dismissed from there and human nature was condemned in them, except for the one Mediator and—after the laver of regeneration—any infants as yet, *no one is clean from filth*, as it is written, *not even the infant whose life upon the earth is of one day*[^243].

But if it is answered that even those can deservedly be said to offer sacrifices in righteousness who offer in faith (for *the just man lives by faith*[^244]; although he deceives himself if he says he has no sin[^245], and therefore let him not say it, since he lives by faith): will anyone say that this time of faith is to be made equal to that end, when, by the fire of the last judgment, those will be cleansed who offer sacrifices in righteousness? And through this, since after such a cleansing the just are to be believed to have no sin, surely that time, as far as having no sin is concerned, is to be compared to no time except when the first human beings lived in paradise, before the transgression, in most innocent felicity. Rightly, then, is this understood to be signified when it was said, *as in the former days and as in the earlier years*.

For through Isaiah too, after a new heaven and a new earth had been promised, among other things which he there sets forth of the blessedness of the saints by allegories and enigmas, to which the care of avoiding length has forbidden us to render a fitting exposition, he says: *according to the days of the tree of life will be the days of my people*[^246]. But who has touched the sacred letters and is ignorant where God planted the tree of life, from the food of which those men being separated, when their iniquity cast them out of paradise, a fiery and terrible guard was set around that same tree[^247]?

**26. 2.** But if anyone contends that those *days of the tree of life* which the prophet Isaiah mentioned are these days of the Church of Christ which are now passing, and that Christ himself is prophetically called the *tree of life*, because he is the Wisdom of God, of which Solomon says, *it is a tree of life to all who embrace it*[^248]; and that those first human beings did not pass any years in paradise, whence they were cast out so quickly that they begot no son there, and therefore that time cannot be understood in what was said, *as in the former days and as in the earlier years*; I pass over this question, lest I be compelled to discuss everything, which is a lengthy matter, so that the manifested truth of one of these may confirm it.

For I see another sense, lest we believe that the *former days* and *earlier years* of carnal sacrifices were promised us by the prophet as a great gift. For those victims of the old Law were commanded to be offered, in whatever cattle, unblemished and without any fault at all[^249], and they signified holy men, such as Christ alone was found to be, without any sin at all. Accordingly, because after the judgment, when those too will have been cleansed by fire who are worthy of such cleansing, no sin at all will be found in any of the saints, and so they will offer themselves in righteousness, so that such victims will be in every way unblemished and without any fault, they will surely be *as in the former days* and *as in the earlier years*, when, in the shadow of this future thing, most clean victims were offered. For this will be the cleanness then in the immortal flesh and mind of the saints, which was figured in the bodies of those victims.

**26. 3.** Then, on account of those who are worthy not of cleansing but of damnation: *and I will draw near*, he says, *to you in judgment, and I will be a swift witness against the doers of evil and against adulterers*[^250], and the rest; to which damnable crimes, having enumerated them, he added: *for I am the Lord your God, and I am not changed*[^251]; as though he said: "when your fault has changed you for the worse and my grace for the better, I am not changed." And he says he will be a witness, because in his judgment he needs no witnesses, and a *swift* one, either because he is to come suddenly and the judgment will be most swift by his very unexpected coming, which seemed slowest, or because he will convict the consciences themselves without any length of speech.

*For in the thoughts*, as it is written, *of the impious there will be an examination*[^252]; and the Apostle says: *their thoughts accusing or even excusing them in the day when God will judge the hidden things of men, according to my gospel, through Jesus Christ*[^253]. So also, then, the Lord is to be understood as a future *witness* who is *swift*, when, without delay, he will recall to memory that whereby he will convict and punish the conscience.

## The good are separated from the reprobate.

**27.** That too which, treating of something else in the eighteenth book, I set down from this prophet[^254], pertains to the last judgment, where he says: *they will be mine, says the Lord almighty, in the day in which I make a possession, and I will choose them as a man chooses his son who serves him; and I will turn, and you will see what is between the just and the unjust, and between him who serves God and him who does not serve him. For behold, the day comes burning like an oven and will burn them up, and all the foreigners and all who do iniquity will be stubble, and the coming day will kindle them, says the Lord almighty, and there will be left in them neither root nor branch. And to you who fear my name the sun of righteousness will arise, and health in his wings, and you will go forth and leap like calves loosed from bonds; and you will trample down the unjust, and they will be ashes under your feet, says the Lord almighty*[^255].

This difference of rewards and punishments, separating the just from the unjust—which under this sun, in the vanity of this life, is not discerned[^256]—when, under that *sun of righteousness*, in the manifestation of that life, it will shine forth, then surely there will be a judgment such as never was.

**28.** But what the same prophet subjoins: *remember the law of Moses my servant, which I commanded him in Horeb for all Israel*[^257], he opportunely recalls the precepts and judgments after declaring the future so great difference between the observers of the law and its despisers; at the same time also that they may learn to understand the law spiritually and may find Christ in it, through whom, as judge, the very separation between good and wicked is to be made. For not in vain does the same Lord say to the Jews: *if you believed Moses, you would believe me too; for he wrote of me*[^258]. For by taking the law carnally, and not knowing that its earthly promises were figures of heavenly things, they fell into those murmurings, so that they dared to say: *vain is he who serves God, and what gain is it that we have kept his commandments and that we have walked as suppliants before the face of the Lord almighty? And now we call strangers blessed, and all who do iniquity are built up*[^259].

For it was not without reason that the same Lord said to the Jews, *if you believed Moses, you would believe me also, for he wrote about me*[^260]. By understanding the law in a fleshly way and not knowing that its earthly promises were figures of heavenly realities, they fell into such murmuring that they dared to say, *he who serves God is vain, and what have we gained by keeping his commandments and walking as suppliants before the face of the almighty Lord? And now we call foreigners blessed, and all who do iniquity are established*[^261].

By which words of theirs the prophet was in a certain way compelled to foretell the last judgment, where the wicked will not even be falsely blessed but will most evidently appear most wretched, and the good will labor under no even temporal misery, but will enjoy a clear and everlasting blessedness. For he had set down some such words of theirs also saying above: *everyone who does evil is good in the sight of the Lord, and such please him*[^262]. To these murmurings, I say, against God they came by taking the law of Moses carnally.

Whence also that man in the seventy-second Psalm says that his feet were almost moved and his steps poured out[^263], surely into a slip, because he was envious of sinners, beholding the peace of sinners; so that among other things he said: *how does God know, and is there knowledge in the Most High?*[^264] he said too: *have I then justified my heart in vain and washed my hands among the innocent?*[^265].

But to solve this most difficult question, which arises when the good seem to be wretched and the wicked happy: *this*, he says, *is labor before me, until I enter into the sanctuary of God and understand in the last things*[^266]. For in the last judgment it will not be so; but in the open misery of the unjust and the open felicity of the just something far other than now is will appear.

**29.** But when he had admonished them to remember the law of Moses (since he foresaw that for a long time still they would not, as they ought, receive it spiritually), he at once subjoined: *and behold, I will send you Elijah the Tishbite, before the great and illustrious day of the Lord comes, who will turn the heart of the father to the son and the heart of the man to his neighbor, lest, perhaps coming, I strike the earth utterly*[^267]. That through this great and marvelous prophet Elijah, the law being expounded to them, the Jews in the last time before the judgment will believe in the true Christ, that is, in our Christ, is most widely held in the discourses and hearts of the faithful. For not without reason is he hoped to come before the coming of the judge, the Savior, who not without reason is believed even now to be living. For he was caught up from human affairs in a fiery chariot, which the holy Scripture most evidently attests[^268].

When he comes, then, expounding the law spiritually—which the Jews now savor carnally—*he will turn the heart of the father to the son*, that is, the heart of the fathers to the sons; for the Septuagint translators put the singular for the plural number; and the sense is that the sons too, that is, the Jews, may understand the law as their fathers understood it, that is, the prophets, among whom was Moses himself; for in this way the heart of the fathers will be turned to the sons, when the understanding of the fathers is brought to the understanding of the sons; *and the heart of the sons to their fathers*, while these too consent to what those perceived; where the Septuagint said, *and the heart of the man to his neighbor*. For fathers and sons are very near to one another.

Although in the words of the Septuagint translators, who translated prophetically, another sense, and one more select, can be found: that Elijah is understood to be about to turn the heart of God the Father to the Son—not, of course, by bringing it about that the Father love the Son, but by teaching that the Father loves the Son; so that the Jews too may love the same Christ, who is ours, whom they hated before. For with the Jews God now has a heart turned away from our Christ, since this is what they think. To them, then, his heart will then be turned to the Son, when they themselves, their heart turned, will have learned the Father's love for the Son.

But what follows, *and the heart of the man to his neighbor*, that is, Elijah will turn the heart of the man too to his neighbor—what is better understood than the heart of man to the man Christ? For although in the form of God he is our God, accepting the form of a servant[^269] he deigned to be also our neighbor. This, then, Elijah will do. *Lest perhaps*, he says, *I come and strike the earth utterly*. For *earth* are those who savor earthly things, as do the carnal Jews up to now; from which fault came those murmurings against God: *that the wicked please him*[^270]; and: *vain is he who serves God*[^271].

## In the Prophets too God is to come as Christ.

**30. 1.** There are many other testimonies of the divine Scriptures concerning the last judgment of God, which, if I should gather them all, it would be too long. Let it suffice, then, that we have proved that this was foretold both in the new and in the old sacred letters. But that the judgment is to be through Christ—that is, that Christ the judge is to come from heaven—is not so evidently expressed in the old letters as in the new, for this reason: because, when there the Lord God says he is to come, or the Lord God is said to be to come, it is not consequently understood to be Christ. For the *Lord God* is both the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit; yet we ought not to leave this unattested.

First, then, it must be shown how Jesus Christ speaks as the Lord God in the prophetic books, and yet Jesus Christ evidently appears, so that, where it is not so apparent, and yet the Lord God is said to be to come to that last judgment, Jesus Christ may be understood. There is a place in the prophet Isaiah which evidently shows what I say. For God says through the prophet: *hear me, Jacob and Israel whom I call. I am the first, and I am forever, and my hand founded the earth and my right hand established the heaven. I will call them, and they will stand together, and all will be gathered and will hear. Who has announced these things to them? Loving you, I did your will against Babylon, that I might take away the seed of the Chaldeans. I spoke and I called; I led him and made his way prosperous. Draw near to me and hear these things. From the beginning I did not speak in secret; when they were being done, there I was. And now the Lord God has sent me, and his Spirit*[^272].

Surely it is he who was speaking as the Lord God; and yet he would not be understood to be Jesus Christ, had he not added: *and now the Lord God has sent me, and his Spirit*. For he said this according to the form of a servant, using of a future thing a word of past time, just as is read in the same prophet: *he was led as a sheep to the slaughter*[^273]. For he did not say "he will be led," but for that which was future he put a word of past time. And prophecy regularly speaks thus.

**30. 2.** There is another place in Zechariah which evidently shows this, that the Almighty sent the Almighty: who whom, except God the Father God the Son? For it is so written: *thus says the Lord almighty: after the glory he sent me upon the nations which spoiled you; for he who touches you touches as it were the pupil of his eye. Behold, I will bring my hand upon them, and they will be spoils to those who had served them; and you will know that the Lord almighty has sent me*[^274]. Behold, the Lord almighty says that he was sent by the Lord almighty. Who here would dare to understand any but Christ speaking, namely to the sheep that had perished of the house of Israel? For he says in the Gospel: *I was not sent except to the sheep that have perished of the house of Israel*[^275]; which here he compared to the pupil of God's eye on account of the most excellent affection of love; of which kind of sheep the Apostles themselves too were.

But after the glory, namely of his own resurrection (before which took place the Evangelist says: *Jesus was not yet glorified*[^276]), he was sent also upon the nations in his Apostles, and so was fulfilled what is read in the Psalm: *you will rescue me from the contradictions of the people, you will set me at the head of the nations*[^277];

so that those who had spoiled the Israelites and to whom the Israelites had been in servitude, when they are made subject to the nations, would not in turn be spoiled in the same way, but would themselves become the spoils of the Israelites (for this he had promised the Apostles, saying: *I will make you fishers of men*[^278]; and to one of them: *from now on*, he says, *you will be catching men*[^279]); they would become spoils, then, but for good, as vessels snatched from that strong man, but more strongly bound[^280].

**30. 3.** Likewise through the same prophet the Lord, speaking, says: *and it will be*, he says, *in that day, I will seek to take away all the nations which come against Jerusalem, and I will pour out upon the house of David and upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem a Spirit of grace and mercy; and they will look to me because they insulted, and they will mourn over him a mourning as over one most dear, and they will grieve with grief as over an only-begotten*[^281]. Surely it belongs only to God to take away all the nations hostile to the holy city Jerusalem, which *come against* it, that is, are contrary to it, or, as others have translated, "*come upon* it," that is, to subject it to themselves; or to pour out *upon the house of David and upon the inhabitants* of that same city a Spirit of grace and mercy. This surely belongs to God, and is said in the person of God through the prophet; and yet Christ shows himself to be this God doing such great and divine things, by adding and saying: *and they will look to me because they insulted, and they will mourn over him a mourning as over one most dear* (or *beloved*) *and they will grieve with grief as over an only-begotten*.

For in that day the Jews will repent—even those who are to receive the Spirit of grace and mercy—that in his passion they insulted Christ, when they will look to him coming in his majesty and will recognize that he is the one whom, humble, they had mocked in their forefathers; although their very forefathers, the authors of so great an impiety, will, when they rise again, see him, but now to be punished, not still to be corrected. Not, then, in this place are they themselves to be understood, where it was said, *and I will pour out upon the house of David and upon the inhabitants of Jerusalem a Spirit of grace and mercy; and they will look to me because they insulted*; but those coming from their stock who at that time, through Elijah, are to believe.

But just as we say to the Jews, "you killed Christ," although their forefathers did this, so these too will grieve that they, in a certain way, did what those did from whose stock they descend. Although, then, having received the *Spirit of grace and mercy*, the faithful will not now be condemned with their impious forefathers, they will nevertheless grieve as though they themselves had done what was done by them. They will grieve, therefore, not from the guilt of the crime, but from the affection of devotion.

Indeed, where the Septuagint translators said, *and they will look to me because they insulted*, it was thus translated from the Hebrew: *and they will look to me whom they pierced*; by which word indeed Christ crucified appears more evidently. But that insulting which the Septuagint preferred to put was not lacking to his whole passion.

For when he was seized and bound and adjudged and clad in the reproach of an ignominious garment and crowned with thorns and struck on the head with a reed and mockingly adored on bended knees, and when he carried his cross and hung now on the wood, they surely insulted him.

Accordingly, following not one translation but joining both, since we read both *they insulted* and *they pierced*, we recognize more fully the truth of the Lord's passion.

**30. 4.** When, then, in the prophetic letters God is read to be coming to make the last judgment, even if no other distinction be added, by the very judgment alone Christ ought to be understood; because, although the Father will judge, he will judge through the coming of the Son of man. For he himself, by the manifestation of his presence, *does not judge anyone, but has given all judgment to the Son*[^282], who will be manifested as a man about to judge, just as as a man he was judged.

For who else is he, of whom likewise God speaks through Isaiah under the name of Jacob and Israel, of whose seed he took a body? as it is so written: *Jacob is my servant, I will uphold him; Israel my chosen, my soul received him. I have given my Spirit upon him, he will bring forth judgment to the nations. He will not cry out nor cease nor will his voice be heard outside. He will not break a bruised reed nor quench smoking flax; but in truth he will bring forth judgment. He will shine forth and will not be broken, until he set judgment on the earth; and in his name the nations will hope*[^283].

In the Hebrew it is not read *Jacob* and *Israel*; but where there is read *my servant*, surely the Septuagint translators, wishing to admonish in what sense it is to be taken—namely that it was said on account of the form of a servant, in which the Most High showed himself most lowly—put the name of that very man to signify him, of whose race that same form of a servant was taken.

The Holy Spirit was given upon him, which was shown also by the appearance of a dove, the Gospel attesting[^284]; he brought forth judgment to the nations, because he foretold what was to be, which was hidden from the nations; in gentleness he did not cry out, nor yet did he cease from preaching the truth; but his voice was not heard outside, nor is it heard, since indeed by those who are cut off outside his body he is not obeyed; and his very persecutors the Jews, who are compared to a bruised reed with their integrity lost, to smoking flax with its light gone, he did not break, did not quench, because he spared them, he who had not yet come to judge them, but to be judged by them. In truth indeed he brought forth judgment, foretelling them when they were to be punished, if they persisted in their malignity. His face shone forth on the mountain[^285], his fame in the world; nor was he broken or bruised, because neither in himself nor in his Church did he yield to the persecutors so as to cease to be; and therefore it has not happened nor will it happen, what his enemies said or say: *when will he die and his name perish?*[^286].

*Until he set judgment on the earth*. Behold, that hidden thing which we were seeking is made manifest; for this is the last judgment, which he will set on the earth, when he himself comes from heaven; of which we already see fulfilled what is here set last: *and in his name the nations will hope*[^287]. Through this, surely, which cannot be denied, let that too be believed which is impudently denied.

For who would hope that even those who as yet refuse to believe in Christ already see together with us and—because they cannot deny it—gnash their teeth and waste away? Who, I say, would hope that the nations would hope in the name of Christ, when he was being held, bound, beaten, mocked, crucified; when even the disciples themselves had lost the hope which they had already begun to have in him? What then scarcely one robber hoped on the cross, now the nations spread far and wide hope[^288], and, that they may not die forever, are signed with the very cross on which he died.

## What things will come to pass in the last judgment.

**30. 5.** No one, then, either denies or doubts that, through Christ Jesus, such a last judgment will be as is foretold in these sacred letters, except one who, by some incredible animosity or blindness, does not believe these letters, which have now demonstrated their truth to the world. And so in that judgment, or about that judgment, we have learned that these things will come: Elijah the Tishbite, the faith of the Jews, Antichrist persecuting, Christ judging, the resurrection of the dead, the separation of the good and the wicked, the conflagration of the world and its renewal. That all these will come we must believe; but in what ways and in what order they come, experience of the things themselves will then teach better than the understanding of men is now able to attain to perfection. Yet I think they will come in the order in which I have mentioned them.

**30. 6.** Two books pertaining to this work remain to us, that, with the Lord's help, we may fulfill our promises; one of these will be on the punishment of the wicked, the other on the felicity of the just; in which especially, as God shall grant, the human arguments will be refuted which the wretched seem to themselves wisely to gnaw against the divine predictions and promises, and which despise the nourishments of saving faith as though false and to be laughed at. But those who are wise according to God hold, as the greatest argument for all things which seem incredible to men and yet are contained in the holy Scriptures—whose truth has now been asserted in many ways—the truthful omnipotence of God, whom they hold for certain could in no way have lied in them, and is able to do what is impossible to the unbeliever.
