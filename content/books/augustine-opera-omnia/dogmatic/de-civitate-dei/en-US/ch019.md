# Book XVII: The Prophets from Samuel to Jeremiah Are Treated

**1.** How the promises of God which were made to Abraham—to whose seed we have learned that both the Israelite nation according to the flesh and all nations according to faith are owed, by God's pledge—are fulfilled, the city of God, running through the order of the times, will indicate. Since, then, the end of the previous book was made down to the kingdom of David, now from that same kingdom, as far as seems sufficient for the work undertaken, we take up the rest that follows.

## [The Time of the Prophets]

This time, then, from when the holy Samuel began to prophesy, and thenceforth, until the people of Israel was led captive into Babylon, and from there, after seventy years, according to the prophecy of the holy Jeremiah[^1], the Israelites having returned, the house of God was restored—this whole time is the time of the Prophets.

For although we can not unreasonably call Prophets the patriarch Noah himself, in whose days the whole earth was destroyed by the flood, and others before and after, down to this time when kings began to be among the people of God—on account of certain things to come, which through them were either in some way signified or foretold, which pertained to the city of God and the kingdom of heaven—especially since we read that some of them were more expressly so named, as Abraham[^2] and Moses[^3]: yet "the days of the Prophets" were chiefly and most of all these, from when Samuel began to prophesy[^4], who, at God's command, anointed as king first Saul[^5] and, when he was rejected, David himself[^6], from whose stock the rest were to succeed, as long as it was fitting for them so to succeed.

If, then, I should wish to mention all the things foretold by the Prophets concerning Christ, while the city of God ran through these times, its members dying and departing and being born and succeeding, the undertaking would extend without measure; first, because Scripture itself, which, arranging in order the kings and their deeds and outcomes, seems, as by historical diligence, to be occupied with narrating events, if it is treated with consideration and the help of the Spirit of God, will be found intent on foretelling things to come either more than, or certainly not less than, on declaring things past. And how laborious and lengthy a thing it is to track this down by scrutiny and to show it by discussion, and how many volumes it requires, who does not know, who considers these things even moderately?

Who, on giving these matters even moderate thought, does not know how laborious and lengthy it would be to investigate them by research and demonstrate them by discussion, and how many volumes it would require?

Then, because those very things which are not doubted to pertain to prophecy are so many, concerning Christ and the kingdom of heaven, which is the city of God, that a greater discussion is necessary to open this up than the measure of this work demands. Accordingly I will so moderate my pen, if I can, that in carrying out this work, in the will of God, I may neither say the things that are superfluous nor pass over the things that are sufficient.

**2.** In the preceding book we said that from the beginning of God's promises to Abraham two things were promised: one, namely, that his seed should possess the land of Canaan, which is signified where it was said, *go into the land which I will show you, and I will make you into a great nation*[^7]; but the other far more excellent, concerning not the carnal but the spiritual seed, through which he is the father not of the one Israelite nation, but of all the nations which follow the footsteps of his faith, which began to be promised in these words, *and in you shall all the tribes of the earth be blessed*[^8]; and thereafter, by very many other testimonies, we showed that these two things were promised. The seed of Abraham, then—that is, the people of Israel according to the flesh—was already in the land of promise, and there it had already begun to reign, not only by holding and possessing the cities of its adversaries, but also by having kings, the promises of God concerning that people being now in great part fulfilled—not only those which had been made to those three fathers, Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and whatever others in their times, but also those made through Moses himself, through whom that same people was delivered from Egyptian slavery and through whom all things past were revealed, in his times, when he led the people through the wilderness.

And neither through the notable leader Joshua the son of Nun—through whom that people was led into the land of promise, and, the nations being conquered, he divided it among the twelve tribes as God had commanded, and died—nor after him, throughout the whole time of the judges, had God's promise concerning the land of Canaan, from a certain river of Egypt to the great river Euphrates[^9], been fulfilled; nor yet was it as yet prophesied as future, but awaited as to be fulfilled. But it was fulfilled through David and his son Solomon, whose kingdom was extended over as great a space as had been promised; for they subdued all those nations and made them tributary[^10].

So, then, in the land of promise according to the flesh—that is, in the land of Canaan—under these kings the seed of Abraham had been so established that nothing thereafter remained for that earthly promise of God to be fulfilled, except that in that same land, as far as temporal prosperity is concerned, the Hebrew nation should remain, through the succession of posterity, in an unshaken state down to the end of this mortal age, if it obeyed the laws of the Lord its God. But since God knew that it would not do this, he used even its temporal punishments to exercise in it his few faithful and to admonish those who were afterward to be in all the nations, of what they needed to be admonished, in whom he was about to fulfill the other promise, the New Testament being revealed through the incarnation of Christ.

**3.** 1. Wherefore, just as those divine oracles to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob, and whatever other prophetic signs or sayings were made in the sacred Scriptures preceding, so too the other prophecies from this time of the kings pertain partly to the nation of Abraham's flesh, but partly to that seed of his in which all the nations are blessed as fellow heirs of Christ through the New Testament, to possess eternal life and the kingdom of heaven;

partly, then, to the maidservant who bears into slavery, that is, the earthly Jerusalem, which is in slavery with her children, but partly to the free city of God, that is, the true eternal Jerusalem in the heavens, whose children are the men living according to God who sojourn on earth[^11]; but there are some among them which are understood to pertain to both—to the maidservant properly, to the free woman figuratively.

## Prophecy Is Made Properly, Figuratively, or in Both Ways

**3.** 2. The utterances of the Prophets, then, are found to be threefold, since some look to the earthly Jerusalem, some to the heavenly, and some to both. By examples I see I must prove what I say. Nathan the prophet was sent to accuse King David of a grave sin and to foretell to him the evils that followed[^12]. These things, and things of this kind, whether publicly—that is, for the welfare or advantage of the people—or privately, when each one obtained for his own affairs divine utterances by which something future might be known for the use of temporal life, who would doubt that they pertained to the earthly city?

But where it is read, *behold, the days are coming, says the Lord, and I will accomplish for the house of Israel and the house of Judah a new covenant, not according to the covenant which I made with their fathers in the day when I took their hand to lead them out of the land of Egypt, since they themselves did not remain in my covenant, and I disregarded them, says the Lord. For this is the covenant which I will establish with the house of Israel after those days, says the Lord: I will give my laws into their mind and write them upon their hearts, and I will look upon them, and I will be to them a God, and they shall be to me a people*[^13]; without doubt the supernal Jerusalem is prophesied, whose reward is God himself, and to have him and to be his is there the highest and the whole good.

But to both pertains this very thing, that Jerusalem is called the city of God, and in it the future house of God is prophesied, and that prophecy seems to be fulfilled when King Solomon builds that most noble temple[^14]. For these things both happened in the earthly Jerusalem according to history, and were figures of the heavenly Jerusalem.

This kind of prophecy, as it were put together and mingled out of both, in the old canonical books, which contain the narratives of events, avails very much and has greatly exercised and exercises the minds of those who search the sacred Scriptures, so that what is read as historically foretold and fulfilled in the seed of Abraham according to the flesh is inquired into as to what it allegorically signifies to be fulfilled also in the seed of Abraham according to faith; so much so that to some it has seemed that there is nothing in those same books either foretold and effected, or effected though not foretold, that does not intimate something to be referred, by a figurative signification, to the supernal city of God and her children who are pilgrims in this life[^15].

But if this is so, then the utterances of the Prophets—or rather of all those Scriptures which are reckoned under the name of the old instrument—will be twofold, not threefold. For there will be nothing there which pertains only to the earthly Jerusalem, if whatever is there said and fulfilled concerning her or on her account signifies something which is also to be referred, by an allegorical prefiguring, to the heavenly Jerusalem; but there will be only two kinds, one which pertains to the free Jerusalem, the other which pertains to both.

But to me, just as those seem to err much who think that no events in that kind of literature signify anything other than what they were done in that way, so those seem to dare much who contend that absolutely everything there is wrapped up in allegorical significations. For this reason I said they are threefold, not twofold. For this is what I think; yet not blaming those who have been able to carve out there, from any event whatever, a sense of spiritual understanding, the truth of the history being at least preserved first.

But as for those things which are so said that they cannot fit with things done or to be done humanly or divinely, what believer would doubt that they were not said to no purpose? Who would not recall them to a spiritual understanding, if he can, or confess that they are to be recalled by one who can?

**4.** 1. When, then, the course of the city of God reached the times of the Kings, when David, Saul being rejected, first so obtained the kingdom that his posterity thereafter reigned in the earthly Jerusalem in a long succession[^16], it gave a figure—signifying and foretelling by an event, what is not to be passed over in silence—of the change of things to come, as far as concerns the two Testaments, the Old and the New, where the priesthood and the kingdom were changed through a new and everlasting priest who is also king, who is Christ Jesus.

For both Samuel, substituted into the service of God when the priest Eli was rejected, performing at once the office of priest and judge[^17], and David, founded in the kingdom when Saul was cast off[^18], figured what I am saying.

## [The Prophecy of Hannah, Mother of Samuel]

Samuel's mother herself too, Hannah, who was at first barren and rejoiced in a later fruitfulness, seems to prophesy nothing other, when she pours out her thanksgiving to the Lord, exulting, when she gives back to God that same boy, born and weaned, with the same piety with which she had vowed.

For she says, *my heart is strengthened in the Lord, my horn is exalted in my God. My mouth is enlarged over my enemies, I have rejoiced in your salvation; for there is none holy as the Lord, and there is none just as our God; there is none holy besides you. Do not glory and do not speak lofty things, nor let great speech go forth from your mouth. For the Lord is a God of knowledge, and a God preparing his own designs. He has made the bow of the mighty weak, and the weak are girded with strength; those full of bread have been brought low, and the hungry have passed over the earth; because the barren has borne seven, and she who had many sons has been made weak. The Lord kills and makes alive, he leads down to hell and brings back. The Lord makes poor and enriches, he humbles and exalts. He raises up the poor from the earth and lifts the needy from the dunghill, to set him with the mighty of the people, giving them a throne of glory as an inheritance; granting a vow to the one who vows, and he has blessed the years of the just, for not in his own strength is a man mighty.*

*The Lord will make his adversary weak, the Lord is holy. Let not the prudent glory in his prudence, and let not the mighty glory in his might, and let not the rich glory in his riches; but in this let him who glories glory, to understand and to know the Lord and to do judgment and justice in the midst of the earth. The Lord has ascended into the heavens and has thundered; he himself will judge the ends of the earth, for he is just; and he gives strength to our kings, and he will exalt the horn of his anointed*[^19].

**4.** 2. Will these words, then, be thought to be those of one little woman giving thanks for a son born to her? Is the mind of men so turned away from the light of truth that it does not perceive that the words this woman poured out go beyond the measure of a woman?

Furthermore, one who is fittingly moved by the very things which have already begun to be fulfilled even in this earthly pilgrimage—does he not direct his attention and look and recognize, through this woman, whose very name, that is, Hannah, is interpreted "her grace"[^20], the Christian religion itself, the city of God itself, whose king and founder is Christ, and finally the grace of God itself, speaking thus in a prophetic spirit—the grace from which the proud are estranged, so that they fall, by which the humble are filled, so that they rise—which this hymn especially proclaimed?

Unless perhaps someone is going to say that this woman prophesied nothing, but only praised God, with an exulting proclamation, on account of the son whom she obtained by her prayer. What, then, does she mean by saying, *he has made the bow of the mighty weak, and the weak are girded with strength; those full of bread have been brought low, and the hungry have passed over the earth; because the barren has borne seven, and she who had many sons has been made weak*[^21]?

Had she herself borne seven, though she had been barren? She had a single child when she said these things; but neither did she afterward bear seven, or six, so that the seventh would be Samuel himself, but three males and two females[^22]. Then, when as yet no one reigned among that people, what she set at the end, *he gives strength to our kings, and he will exalt the horn of his anointed*[^23]—whence did she speak it, if she did not prophesy?

**4.** 3. Let the Church of Christ, then, *the city of the great king*[^24], *full of grace*, fruitful in offspring, let her say what she recognizes was so long before prophesied of her through the mouth of this pious mother: *my heart is strengthened in the Lord, my horn is exalted in my God*.

Truly the heart is strengthened and the horn truly exalted, because not in itself but in the Lord its God. *My mouth is enlarged over my enemies*[^25]; because even in the straits of afflictions *the word of God is not bound*[^26], nor in the bound heralds. *I have rejoiced*, she says, *in your salvation*.

This salvation is that Jesus, whom the aged Simeon, as is read in the Gospel, embracing him as a little one, recognizing him as great, said, *now, Lord, you dismiss your servant in peace, for my eyes have seen your salvation*[^27]. So let the Church say, *I have rejoiced in your salvation, for there is none holy as the Lord, and there is none just as our God*; as holy and sanctifying, just and justifying. There is none holy besides you, because no one becomes so except from you. Finally it follows, *do not glory and do not speak lofty things, nor let great speech go forth from your mouth; for the Lord is a God of knowledge*[^28]. He knows you, even where no one knows; for *he who thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing, deceives himself*[^29].

These things are said against the adversaries of the city of God, who belong to Babylon, presuming on their own strength, glorying in themselves, not in the Lord; among whom are also the carnal Israelites, earthborn citizens of the earthly Jerusalem, who, as the Apostle says, *being ignorant of the righteousness of God*—that is, that which God gives to man, who alone is just and justifying—*and wishing to establish their own*—that is, as though gained by themselves for themselves, not imparted by him—*are not subject to the righteousness of God*[^30],

surely because, being proud, thinking that they can please God by their own power, not by God's, they are not subject—God, who is the *God of knowledge* and therefore also the judge of consciences, seeing there the thoughts of men, that they are vain[^31], if they are of men and are not from him.

*And preparing*, she says, *his own designs*. What designs do we think, but that the proud fall and the humble rise? For she pursues these designs, saying, *the bow of the mighty has been made weak, and the weak are girded with strength*[^32]. The bow has been made weak—that is, the purpose of those who seem to themselves so mighty that, without the gift and help of God, they can fulfill the divine commands by human sufficiency—and they are girded with strength whose inner voice is, *have mercy on me, Lord, for I am weak*[^33].

**4.** 4. *Those full of bread*, she says, *have been brought low, and the hungry have passed over the earth*[^34]. Who are to be understood as full of bread, but those same ones as it were mighty—that is, the Israelites, to whom the utterances of God were entrusted[^35]?

But in that people the sons of the maidservant *have been brought low*; by which word, though in less correct Latin, it is yet well expressed that from greater they were made lesser, because even in those very loaves—that is, the divine utterances, which the Israelites alone of all nations then received—they have an earthly taste.

But the nations, to whom that law had not been given, after they came through the New Testament to those utterances, much hungering passed over the earth, because in them they tasted not earthly but heavenly things. And, as though the cause were sought why this came about, she says, *because the barren has borne seven, and she who had many sons has been made weak*[^36]. Here all that was being prophesied shone forth to those who recognize the number seven, by which the perfection of the whole Church was signified.

On account of which the apostle John too writes to seven churches, thereby showing that he writes to the fullness of one[^37]; and in the *Proverbs* of Solomon, Wisdom, prefiguring this beforehand, *built herself a house and propped it with seven columns*[^38]. For the city of God was barren in all the nations before this offspring, which we behold, arose. We behold too the earthly Jerusalem, which had many sons, now made weak; for whatever sons of the free woman were in her were her strength; but now, since there is the letter and not the spirit, having lost her strength she has been made weak.

**4.** 5. *The Lord kills and makes alive*; he killed her who had many sons, and made alive this barren one who has borne seven. Although it can more suitably be understood that he makes alive those same ones whom he has killed. For he as it were repeated this by adding, *he leads down to hell and brings back*.

For those to whom the Apostle says, *if you have died with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God*, are surely killed wholesomely by the Lord; to whom he adds, *savor the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth*; that they may be those who, *hungering, have passed over the earth*.

*For you have died*, he says; behold how God wholesomely kills; then it follows, *and your life is hidden with Christ in God*; behold how God makes alive those same ones.

But did he lead the same ones down to hell and bring them back? This both, without controversy among the faithful, we see fulfilled rather in him, namely our head, with whom the Apostle said our life is hidden in God. For he who *did not spare his own Son, but delivered him up for us all*, in this way surely killed him; and because he raised him from the dead, he made the same one alive again. And because his voice is recognized in the prophecy, *you will not leave my soul in hell*, he led the same one down to hell and brought him back.

By this poverty of his we have been enriched. For *the Lord makes poor and enriches*. For that we may know what this is, let us hear what follows: *he humbles and exalts*; surely he humbles the proud and exalts the humble. For what is read elsewhere, *God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble*, this the whole discourse of this woman holds, whose name is interpreted "her grace."

**4.** 6. Now indeed what is added, *he raises up the poor from the earth*, I understand of no one better than of him who *for our sake became poor, though he was rich, that by his poverty*, as was said a little before, *we might be enriched*[^39]. For him he raised up from the earth so quickly that his flesh did not see corruption.

Nor will I make foreign to him what was added, *and from the dunghill he lifts the needy*. For the *needy* is the same as the *poor*; but the *dunghill* from which he was lifted is most rightly understood as the persecuting Jews, in whose number, when the Apostle had said he persecuted the Church, he says, *the things which were gains to me, these I counted as losses for the sake of Christ; and not only as detriments, but I even esteemed them to be dung, that I might gain Christ*[^40]. From the earth, then, that poor one was raised up above all the rich, and from that dunghill that needy one was lifted above all the wealthy, *to sit with the mighty of the people*, to whom he says, *you shall sit upon twelve thrones*[^41]. *And giving them a throne of glory as an inheritance*[^42]; for those mighty ones had said, *behold, we have left all and followed you*[^43]. This vow they had most mightily vowed.

Thus that poor man was raised from the earth above all the rich, and that needy man was lifted from that dungheap above all the wealthy, *to sit with the powerful of the people*, to whom he says, *you will sit on twelve thrones*[^44]. *And giving them a throne of glory as their inheritance*[^45]; for those powerful men had said, *behold, we have left everything and followed you*[^46]. They had made this vow with the greatest resolve.

**4.** 7. But whence was this to them, but from him of whom it was said here immediately, *granting a vow to the one who vows*[^47]? Otherwise they would be of those mighty ones whose bow has been made weak. *Granting*, she says, *a vow to the one who vows*. For no one would vow anything right to the Lord unless he received from him what he should vow.

It follows, *and he has blessed the years of the just*, that he may live without end, namely, with him to whom it was said, *and your years shall not fail*[^48]. For there the years stand, but here they pass, or rather perish; for before they come, they are not; and when they have come, they will not be, because they come with their own end.

But of these two—that is, *granting a vow to the one who vows* and *he has blessed the years of the just*—one is what we do, the other what we receive. But this latter is not received, God being the giver, unless the former is first effected, with him as helper: *for not in his own strength is a man mighty. The Lord will make his adversary weak*; namely, the one who envies the man who vows and resists him, lest he be able to fulfill what he vowed.

It can also, from the Greek ambiguity, be understood as *his own adversary*. For when the Lord has begun to possess us, surely the adversary who had been ours becomes his and will be overcome by us, but not by our strength, *for not in his own strength is a man mighty. The Lord*, then, *will make his adversary weak, the Lord is holy*[^49]; that he may be overcome by the saints, whom the Lord, holy of holies, makes holy.

**4.** 8. And for this reason *let not the prudent glory in his prudence, and let not the mighty glory in his might, and let not the rich glory in his riches; but in this let him who glories glory, to understand and to know the Lord and to do judgment and justice in the midst of the earth*[^50]. In no small part does he understand and know the Lord who understands and knows that this too is given him by the Lord—to understand and to know the Lord. *For what have you*, says the Apostle, *that you did not receive? But if you also received it, why do you glory as though you had not received it?*[^51] that is, as though it were from yourself to yourself, whence you glory.

But he does judgment and justice who lives rightly. And he lives rightly who obeys God's command; and *the end of the commandment*—that is, that to which the commandment is referred—*is love from a pure heart and a good conscience and faith unfeigned*[^52]. Furthermore this *love*, as the apostle John testifies, *is from God*[^53]. To do judgment and justice, then, is from God.

But what is *in the midst of the earth*? For surely those who dwell at the ends of the earth ought not to fail to do judgment and justice. Who would say this? Why, then, was it added, *in the midst of the earth*? For if it were not added, and it were only said, *to do judgment and justice*[^54], this precept would pertain more to both kinds of men, both inland and maritime.

But lest anyone should think that after the end of the life which is lived in this body there remains time for doing judgment and justice, which he did not do while he was in the flesh, and that thus the divine judgment can be escaped, *in the midst of the earth* seems to me to be said, "while each one lives in the body." For in this life each one carries about his own earth, which, when the man dies, the common earth receives, to be restored, of course, to him when he rises again.

Accordingly, *in the midst of the earth*—that is, while our soul is enclosed in this earthly body—judgment and justice must be done, which will profit us hereafter, when *each one receives according to the things he has done through the body, whether good or evil*[^55].

For by *through the body* the Apostle there meant "through the time in which one lived in the body." For if anyone blasphemes with a malicious mind and impious thought, and does not work it by any members of the body, he will not on that account be guiltless, because he did not do it by a motion of the body, since he did this through that time in which the body too acted. In this way that too which is read in the Psalm can be fittingly understood: *but God our king before the ages has worked salvation in the midst of the earth*[^56]; so that the Lord Jesus is taken as *our God*, who is *before the ages*, because through him the ages were made, having worked our salvation *in the midst of the earth*, when *the Word was made flesh* and dwelt in an earthly body[^57].

**4.** 9. Then, after it was prophesied in these words of Hannah how he ought to glory who glories—not in himself, of course, but in the Lord[^58]—on account of the recompense that will be at the day of judgment, she says, *the Lord has ascended into the heavens and has thundered; he himself will judge the ends of the earth, for he is just*[^59]. She altogether kept the order of the confession of the faithful. For the Lord Christ ascended into heaven, and from there is to come to judge the living and the dead. For *who ascended*, as the Apostle says, *except him who also descended into the lower parts of the earth? He who descended is the same who also ascended above all the heavens, that he might fill all things*[^60].

Through his clouds, then, he thundered, which, when he had ascended, he filled with the Holy Spirit. Concerning which he threatened, through the prophet Isaiah, against the maidservant Jerusalem—that is, the ungrateful vineyard—that they should not rain rain upon it[^61].

But it was said, *he himself will judge the ends of the earth*, as if it were said, "even the ends of the earth." For he will not fail to judge the other parts, who without doubt will judge all men. But the *ends of the earth* are better understood as the ends of man; since those things will not be judged which in the meantime are changed for better or worse, but the ends in which he who is to be judged shall be found. On account of which it was said, *he who perseveres to the end, he shall be saved*[^62]. He, then, who perseveringly does judgment and justice in the midst of the earth will not be condemned when the ends of the earth are judged.

*And he gives*, she says, *strength to our kings*; that, in judging them, he may not condemn them. He gives them strength by which they may rule the flesh like kings, and may conquer in him the world which shed its blood on their account. *And he will exalt the horn of his anointed*[^63]. How will Christ exalt the horn of his anointed? For of him of whom it was said above, *the Lord has ascended into the heavens*, and who was understood as the Lord Christ—he himself, as it is said here, *will exalt the horn of his anointed*. Who, then, is the anointed of the Christ? Or will he exalt the horn of each one of his faithful, as this same woman says at the beginning of this hymn, *my horn is exalted in my God*[^64]? For all who are anointed with his chrism we can rightly call anointed; yet the whole body together with its head is one Christ.

These things Hannah prophesied, the mother of Samuel, a holy and much-praised man; in whom indeed there was then figured the change of the old priesthood, now fulfilled, when *she who had many sons was made weak*, so that *the barren, who bore seven*[^65], might have a new priesthood in Christ.

## What Is Revealed to Eli

**5.** 1. But this is spoken more evidently to the priest Eli himself by a man of God who was sent—whose name is indeed passed over in silence, but who is understood, by his office and ministry, to be without doubt a prophet. For it is written thus: *and a man of God came to Eli and said: thus says the Lord: revealing I revealed myself to the house of your father, when they were in the land of Egypt servants in Pharaoh's house; and I chose the house of your father out of all the scepters of Israel to exercise the priesthood for me, to go up to my altar and burn incense and bear the Ephod; and I gave to the house of your father all the things that are of the fire of the sons of Israel, for food. And why have you looked upon my incense and upon my sacrifice with a shameless eye, and have glorified your sons above me, to bless the firstfruits of every sacrifice in Israel in my sight?*

*Therefore thus says the Lord God of Israel: I said: Your house and the house of your father shall pass before me forever. And now the Lord says: by no means, but those who glorify me I will glorify, and he who despises me shall be despised. Behold, the days are coming, and I will destroy your seed and the seed of your father's house, and there shall not be for you an old man in my house all the days, and I will destroy from my altar a man of yours, so that his eyes shall fail and his soul shall waste away; and everyone who survives of your house shall fall by the sword of men. And this shall be a sign to you, which shall come upon these two sons of yours, Hophni and Phinehas: in one day they shall both die.*

*And I will raise up for myself a faithful priest, who shall do all that is in my heart and in my soul; and I will build him a faithful house, and he shall pass before my Christ all the days. And it shall be that whoever survives in your house shall come to adore him for an obol of silver, saying: cast me into one part of your priesthood, that I may eat bread*[^66].

**5.** 2. It cannot be said that this prophecy, where the change of the old priesthood is foretold with such great manifestation, was fulfilled in Samuel. For although Samuel was not of another tribe than the one that had been appointed by the Lord to serve the altar, yet he was not of the sons of Aaron, whose offspring had been assigned to be the source of priests[^67];

and for this reason in that event too the same change was foreshadowed which was to be through Christ Jesus, and it pertained properly to the Old Testament, but figuratively to the New—the prophecy itself being also of a deed, not of a word, signifying, that is, by a deed what was said by word to the priest Eli through the prophet. For there were afterward priests of the race of Aaron, such as Zadok and Abiathar[^68], during David's reign, and others thereafter, before the time came in which these things, foretold so long before about the changing of the priesthood, had to be effected through Christ. But who now, looking on these things with a faithful eye, does not see that they are fulfilled? Since indeed no tabernacle, no temple, no altar, no sacrifice, and therefore no priest, has remained to the Jews, to whom it had been commanded in the law of God that he should be ordained from the seed of Aaron.

And this too was mentioned here, when the prophet said, *thus says the Lord God of Israel: I said: Your house and the house of your father shall pass before me forever. And now the Lord says: by no means, but those who glorify me I will glorify, and he who despises me shall be despised*. For that by *the house of his father* he does not mean his immediate father, but that Aaron who was first appointed priest, from whose offspring the rest would follow, the earlier words show, where he says, *I revealed myself to the house of your father, when they were in the land of Egypt servants in Pharaoh's house, and I chose the house of your father out of all the scepters of Israel to exercise the priesthood for me*. Which of his fathers was in that Egyptian slavery, from which, when they had been delivered, he was chosen for the priesthood, but Aaron?

Of his stock, then, he said in this place that it would come to pass that there should be no longer priests; which we now see fulfilled. Let faith be watchful; the facts are at hand, they are seen, they are held, and they are thrust upon the eyes even of those unwilling to see. *Behold*, he says, *the days are coming, and I will destroy your seed and the seed of your father's house, and there shall not be for you an old man in my house all the days, and I will destroy from my altar a man of yours, so that his eyes shall fail and his soul shall waste away*[^69].

Behold, the days that were foretold have now come. There is no priest according to the order of Aaron; and whoever is a man of his race, when he sees the sacrifice of the Christians flourishing throughout the whole world, but that great honor taken from himself, his eyes fail and his soul wastes away with the decay of grief.

**5.** 3. But what follows pertains properly to the house of this Eli, to whom these things were said: *and everyone who survives of your house shall fall by the sword of men. And this shall be a sign to you, which shall come upon these two sons of yours, Hophni and Phinehas: in one day they shall both die*. This sign, then, of the changing of the priesthood from this man's house, was made; by which sign it was signified that the priesthood of the house of Aaron was to be changed.

For the death of this man's sons signified the death not of men, but of the priesthood itself of the sons of Aaron. But what follows now pertains to that priest whose figure Samuel bore in succeeding this man. Accordingly, what follows is said of Christ Jesus, the true priest of the New Testament: *and I will raise up for myself a faithful priest, who shall do all that is in my heart and in my soul; and I will build him a faithful house*. This is the eternal and supernal Jerusalem.

*And he shall pass*, he says, *before my Christ all the days. He shall pass*, he said, means "he shall conduct himself"; as he had said above of the house of Aaron, *I said: your house and the house of your father shall pass before me forever*[^70]. But what he says, *he shall pass before my Christ*, is surely to be understood of *that house*, not of that priest, who is Christ himself, the mediator and Savior. His house, then, shall pass before him.

*He shall pass* can also be understood of passing from death to life, *all the days* during which this mortality "is carried on" until the end of this age. But what God says, *who shall do all that is in my heart and in my soul*—let us not suppose that God has a soul, since he is the creator of the soul; but this is said of God tropically, not properly, just as hand and foot and other members of the body are.

And lest, on this account, man be believed to have been made in the image of God in the form of this flesh, wings too are added, which man surely does not have, and it is said to God, *under the shadow of your wings you will protect me*[^71]; that men may understand that these things are said of that ineffable nature not by proper, but by transferred names of things.

**5.** 4. But what is added, *and it shall be that whoever survives in your house shall come to adore him*, is said properly not of the house of this Eli, but of that of Aaron, of which men remained down to the coming of Jesus Christ, and of which race there are even now still some.

For of that house of this Eli it had already been said above, *and everyone who survives of your house shall fall by the sword of men*[^72]. How, then, could it here truly be said, *and it shall be that whoever survives in your house shall come to adore him*, if that is true, that no one survived from there by the avenging sword? unless because he wished those to be understood who belong to the stock, but of that whole priesthood according to the order of Aaron.

Therefore, if he is of those predestined remnants, of whom another prophet said, *the remnants shall be saved*[^73]; whence the Apostle too says, *so then, even at this time, remnants have been made by the election of grace*[^74]; because he is well understood to be of such remnants, of whom it was said, *whoever survives in your house*: surely he believes in Christ;

as in the times of the Apostles very many of that nation believed, nor are there now lacking those who, though very rarely, yet believe; and there is fulfilled in him what this man of God here went on immediately to add, *he shall come to adore him for an obol of silver*[^75]. To adore whom, but that high priest who is also God? For in that priesthood according to the order of Aaron men did not come to the temple or the altar of God for this purpose, to adore the priest. But what is it that he says, *for an obol of silver*, but the brevity of the word of faith, of which the Apostle recalls it was said, *the Lord will make a consummating and abbreviating word upon the earth*[^76]? But that silver is put for an utterance the Psalm is witness, where it is sung, *the utterances of the Lord are chaste utterances, silver tried by fire*[^77].

**5.** 5. What, then, does he say who comes to adore the priest of God and the priest who is God? *Cast me into a part of your priesthood, that I may eat bread*[^78]. I do not wish to be placed in the honor of my fathers, which is nothing; cast me into a part of your priesthood. For *I have chosen to be cast off in the house of God*[^79]; I desire to be a member, however poor and however small, of your priesthood.

For by *priesthood* he here means the very people, of which people that priest is the *mediator of God and men, the man Christ* Jesus[^80]. To which people the apostle Peter says, *a holy people, a royal priesthood*[^81]. Although some have translated *of your sacrifice*, not *of your priesthood*; which nonetheless signifies the same Christian people.

Whence the apostle Paul says, *we, though many, are one bread, one body*[^82]. And what he added, *that I may eat bread*, elegantly expressed the very kind of sacrifice, of which the priest himself says, *the bread which I shall give is my flesh for the life of the world*[^83]. This is the sacrifice; not according to the order of Aaron, but according to the order of Melchizedek[^84]; let him who reads understand.

This confession, then, is brief and wholesomely humble, by which it is said, *cast me into a part of your priesthood, that I may eat bread*; this is the *obol of silver*, because it is both brief and is the utterance of the Lord dwelling in the heart of the believer. For since he had said above that he had given foods to the house of Aaron from the victims of the Old Testament, where he says, *I gave to the house of your father all the things that are of the fire of the sons of Israel, for food*[^85]—for these had been the sacrifices of the Jews—therefore he here said, *that I may eat bread*, which in the New Testament is the sacrifice of the Christians.

**6.** 1. Since, then, these things were foretold of old with such loftiness, and now shine clear with such manifestation, yet not without reason can someone be moved and say: how do we trust that all the things foretold in those books as to come will come, if this very thing which was there said divinely, *your house and the house of your father shall pass before me forever*[^86], could not have its effect? Since we see that that priesthood was changed, and what was promised to that house can never be hoped to be fulfilled, because that which succeeds it, rejected and changed, is rather proclaimed eternal.

He who says this does not yet understand, or does not recall, that this very priesthood according to the order of Aaron was established as a shadow of the future eternal priesthood; and for this reason, when eternity was promised to it, it was promised not to the shadow and figure itself, but to that which was foreshadowed and figured through it. But lest the shadow itself should be thought to be destined to remain, therefore its change too had to be prophesied.

## Likewise the Kingdom of Saul

**6.** 2. In this way too the kingdom of Saul himself, who was certainly rejected and cast off, was a shadow of the future kingdom that would remain in eternity. For that oil with which he was anointed, and from which chrism he was called anointed[^87], is to be taken mystically and understood as a great sacrament; which David himself so venerated in him that, his heart smitten, he trembled when, hidden in a dark cave which Saul too had entered under the urging of a natural necessity, he secretly cut off behind a small piece of his garment, that he might have something by which to show how he had spared him when he could have killed him, and thus might take away from Saul's mind the suspicion by which, thinking the holy David his enemy, he was vehemently persecuting him.

And so, lest he should be guilty of so great a sacrament violated in Saul, because he had so handled even his garment, he was afraid. For it is written thus: *and David's heart smote him, because he had taken away the border of his cloak*. But to the men who were with him and were urging that he kill Saul, delivered into his hands, he said, *let it not befall me, from the Lord, if I do this thing to my lord, the Lord's anointed, to lay my hand upon him; for he is the Lord's anointed*[^88]. To this shadow of the future, then, such great veneration was shown, not on its own account, but on account of that which it prefigured.

Whence too what Samuel said to Saul, *since you have not kept my commandment, which the Lord commanded you, whereas the Lord would now have prepared your kingdom over Israel forever: but now your kingdom shall not stand for you, and the Lord will seek for himself a man after his own heart, and the Lord will command him to be prince over his people; because you have not kept what the Lord commanded you*[^89], is not to be taken as though God had prepared Saul himself to reign forever, and afterward was unwilling to keep this for him when he sinned; for he was not ignorant that he would sin; but he had prepared his kingdom, that in it there might be a figure of the eternal kingdom. Therefore he added, *but now your kingdom shall not stand for you*.

It has stood, then, and will stand, that which was signified in him; but it will not stand for him, because he himself was not to reign forever, nor his offspring, so that at least through posterity, succeeding one another, what was said, *forever*, might seem to be fulfilled. *And the Lord will seek*, he says, *for himself a man*; signifying either David or the *mediator of the New Testament* himself, who was figured also in the chrism with which David himself and his offspring were anointed.

But God seeks for himself a man not as though he did not know where he is; rather, through a man he speaks in the manner of men, because even by speaking in this way he seeks us. For not only to God the Father, but to his Only-begotten too, who came to seek what had perished[^90], we were already so well known that we were chosen in him before the foundation of the world[^91]. *He will seek for himself*, then, means "he will have for his own." Whence in the Latin language this word "receives" the preposition and is called "acquires"; what this signifies is plain enough. Although even without the addition of the preposition "to seek" is understood as "to acquire"; from which gains too are called "quests."

**7.** 1. Again Saul sinned through disobedience, and again Samuel, in the word of the Lord, said to him, *because you have despised the word of the Lord, the Lord has despised you, that you should not be king over Israel*[^92]. And again, for the same sin, when Saul confessed it and begged pardon and asked Samuel to return with him to appease God, *I will not return*, he says, *with you; because you have despised the word of the Lord, and the Lord will despise you, that you may not be king over Israel. And Samuel turned his face to depart; and Saul seized the border of his mantle and tore it. And Samuel said to him: the Lord has torn the kingdom from Israel out of your hand today and will give it to your neighbor, a good man, above you, and Israel shall be divided in two; and he will not turn nor repent; for he is not as a man, that he should repent; he threatens, and does not abide by it*[^93].

This man, to whom it is said, *the Lord will despise you, that you may not be king over Israel*, and, *the Lord has torn the kingdom from Israel out of your hand today*, reigned forty years over Israel[^94]—for as long a space of time, that is, as David himself—and heard this at the first time of his reign; that we may understand it was said for this reason, that no one of his stock would reign, and that we may look to the stock of David, whence arose according to the flesh the *mediator of God and men, the man Christ Jesus*[^95].

But Scripture does not have what is read in very many Latin codices, *the Lord has torn the kingdom of Israel out of your hand*; but, as it was set down by us, found in the Greek, *the Lord has torn the kingdom from Israel out of your hand*; so that this *out of your hand* may be understood as *from Israel*.

This man, then, figuratively bore the person of the people of Israel, which people was to lose the kingdom, Christ Jesus our Lord being about to reign through the New Testament not carnally but spiritually. And when it is said of him, *and he will give it to your neighbor*, this is referred to the kinship of the flesh; for from Israel is Christ according to the flesh, whence also was Saul.

But what was added, *a good man, above you*[^96], can indeed be understood as "better than you"; for some have so translated it; but it is better taken thus, *a good man, above you*, so that, because that one is *good*, therefore he is *above you*, according to that other prophetic saying, *until I make all your enemies the footstool of your feet*[^97]; among whom is also Israel, from whom, his persecutor, Christ took away the kingdom; although there too was an Israel in whom there was no guile[^98], as it were a kind of grain of those husks; for from there surely were the Apostles, from there so many martyrs, of whom Stephen was the first; from there so many churches, which the apostle Paul mentions[^99], magnifying God at his conversion.

About which matter I do not doubt that what follows is to be understood: *and Israel shall be divided in two*[^100]; namely, into the Israel that is an enemy to Christ and the Israel that cleaves to Christ; into the Israel belonging to the maidservant and the Israel belonging to the free woman[^101]. For these two kinds were at first together, as Abraham still cleaved to the maidservant, until the barren woman, made fruitful by the grace of Christ, cried out, *cast out the maidservant and her son*[^102]. We know, indeed, that on account of Solomon's sin, in the reign of his son Rehoboam, Israel was divided in two and so persisted, each part having its own kings, until that whole nation was overthrown and carried away by the Chaldeans in a vast devastation.

But what is this to Saul, since, if any such thing was to be threatened, it should rather have been threatened to David himself, whose son was Solomon? Finally, the Hebrew nation is not now divided among itself, but is indifferently scattered through the lands in the fellowship of the same error. But that division which God, under the person of Saul, who bore the figure of that kingdom and people, threatened to that same kingdom and people, was signified to be eternal and unchangeable by what was added, *and he will not turn nor repent; for he is not as a man, that he should repent; he threatens, and does not abide by it*[^103]; that is, man threatens, and does not abide by it; but not God, who does not repent as a man does. For where it is read that he repents, a change of things is signified, the divine foreknowledge remaining unchangeable. Where, then, he is said not to repent, he is understood not to change.

**7.** 4. We see that through these words a wholly insoluble and altogether perpetual sentence was divinely uttered concerning this division of the people of Israel. For whoever have passed over to Christ, or are passing over, or will pass over from there, were not of it according to God's foreknowledge, though they were according to the one and the same nature of the human race. Whoever of the Israelites, cleaving to Christ, persevere in him, will never be with those Israelites who persist in being his enemies to the end of this life; but in the division which is here foretold they will remain perpetually. For the Old Testament *from Mount Sinai, bearing into slavery*[^104], profits nothing, except that it bears witness to the New Testament. Otherwise, as long as Moses is read, a veil is placed over their hearts; but when anyone passes over from there to Christ, the veil will be taken away[^105]. For the very intention of those who pass over is changed from the Old to the New, so that each now intends to receive not carnal but spiritual happiness. On account of which the great prophet Samuel himself, before he anointed Saul king, when he cried out to the Lord for Israel, and he heard him, and, when he was offering a holocaust, as foreigners drew near to fight against the people of God, the Lord thundered upon them, and they were confounded and stumbled before Israel and were overcome: he took one stone and set it between the new Mizpah and the old, and called its name Ebenezer, which in Latin is "the stone of the helper," and said, *thus far the Lord has helped us*[^106]. Mizpah is interpreted "intention"[^107]. That "stone of the helper" is the mediation of the Savior, through whom one must pass over from the old Mizpah to the new—that is, from the intention by which a false carnal blessedness was expected in a carnal kingdom, to the intention by which, through the New Testament, the truest spiritual blessedness is expected in the kingdom of heaven; and since there is nothing better than this, God helps *thus far*.

## An Eternal Kingdom Is Promised to David

**8.** 1. Now I see that it must be shown what God promised to David himself, who succeeded Saul in the kingdom, by whose change that final change was figured, on account of which all things were divinely said and all things written—what God promised, that is, which pertains to the matter we are treating.

When many prosperous things had come to King David, he thought to make a house for God, namely that most excellently renowned temple which was afterward built by his son King Solomon. As he was thinking this, the word of the Lord came to the prophet Nathan, that he should carry it to the king. There, when God had said that the house should not be built for him by David himself, nor that for so long a time had he commanded anyone among his people to make him a house of cedar, *and now*, he says, *thus you shall say to my servant David: thus says the Lord almighty: I took you from the sheepfold, that you might be leader over my people, over Israel, and I was with you in all the things into which you went, and I destroyed all your enemies from before your face, and I made you renowned according to the name of the great ones who are upon the earth; and I will set a place for my people Israel, and plant them, and they shall dwell apart and shall be troubled no more; and the son of iniquity shall not add to humble them, as from the beginning, from the days when I appointed judges over my people Israel; and I will give you rest from all your enemies, and the Lord will tell you that you shall build him a house. And it shall be, when your days are fulfilled, and you sleep with your fathers, that I will raise up your seed after you, who shall be of your belly, and I will prepare his kingdom.*

*He shall build me a house for my name, and I will direct his throne forever. I will be to him a father, and he shall be to me a son. And if his iniquity comes, I will reprove him with the rod of men and with the touches of the sons of men; but my mercy I will not remove from him, as I removed it from those from whom I removed it from my face; and his house shall be faithful and his kingdom forever before me, and his throne shall be set up forever*[^108].

**8.** 2. He who thinks that this so great promise was fulfilled in Solomon errs much. For he attends to what was said, *he shall build me a house*, because Solomon built that most noble temple, and does not attend to, *his house shall be faithful and his kingdom forever before me*[^109]. Let him, then, attend and look upon Solomon's house full of foreign women worshiping false gods, and that once wise king himself seduced by them and cast down into the same idolatry[^110]; and let him not dare to suppose that God either promised this falsely or could not foreknow that Solomon and his house would be such. But we ought not to be in doubt about this, not even if we did not already see these things fulfilled in Christ our Lord, who was made of the seed of David according to the flesh[^111], lest we should here vainly and emptily look for some other, like the carnal Jews. For they too so far understand that the son who is promised to King David in this place was not Solomon, that, though the one who was promised has been declared with such great manifestation, they yet, with a wondrous blindness, say that they hope for another.

There was indeed some image of the future thing even in Solomon, in that he built the temple and had peace according to his name (for Solomon in Latin is "peaceable") and at the beginning of his reign was wonderfully praiseworthy; but he in his own person, through a shadow of the future, also foretold Christ the Lord, he did not present him. Whence certain things are written of him as though these were foretold of him, while Holy Scripture, prophesying even by deeds, in a certain way sketches in him a figure of things to come.

For besides the books of divine history, where he is narrated to have reigned, the seventy-second Psalm too is inscribed with the title of his name; in which so many things are said that cannot at all fit him, but fit the Lord Christ with the most evident clarity, that it appears plainly that in Solomon a figure of some sort was foreshadowed, but in Christ the very truth was presented. For it is known within what bounds the kingdom of Solomon was confined; and yet in that Psalm it is read, to pass over other things, *he shall have dominion from sea to sea and from the river to the ends of the earth*[^112], which we see fulfilled in Christ. For from the river he took the beginning of his dominion, where, baptized by John and pointed out by him, he began to be recognized by the disciples, who called him not only "teacher," but also "Lord"[^113].

**8.** 3. Nor for any other reason did Solomon begin to reign while his father David was still living—which befell none of those kings—except that from this too it might sufficiently shine forth that he is not the one whom this prophecy foreshadows, which speaks to his father, saying, *and it shall be, when your days are fulfilled, and you sleep with your fathers, that I will raise up your seed after you, who shall be of your belly, and I will prepare his kingdom*[^114]. How, then, on account of what follows, *he shall build me a house*, will this Solomon be thought to have been prophesied, and not rather, on account of what precedes, *when your days are fulfilled and you sleep with your fathers, I will raise up your seed after you*, is another peaceable one understood to be promised, who was foretold to be raised up not before, like Solomon, but after the death of David?

For however long an interval of time intervened before Jesus Christ came, without doubt he had to come after the death of King David, to whom he was thus promised, who would build a house for God, not of wood and stones, but of men, such as we rejoice that he builds. For to this house the Apostle says, that is, to the faithful of Christ, *for the temple of God is holy, which you are*[^115].

**9.** On account of which, in the eighty-ninth Psalm too, whose title is, *An Instruction of Ethan the Israelite*, the promises of God made to King David are mentioned, and to those which are set down in the book of *Kingdoms* some similar things are there said, such as, *I have sworn to David my servant: forever I will prepare your seed*[^116]; and again, *then you spoke in a vision to your sons and said: I have set help upon a mighty one, I have exalted a chosen one from my people. I have found David my servant, with my holy oil I have anointed him. For my hand will help him and my arm will strengthen him. The enemy shall not prevail against him, and the son of iniquity shall not harm him. And I will cut down his enemies from before his face, and those who hate him I will put to flight. And my truth and my mercy shall be with him, and in my name his horn shall be exalted. And I will set his hand in the sea and his right hand in the rivers. He himself will call upon me: you are my father, my God and the supporter of my salvation. And I will make him the firstborn, high above the kings of the earth. Forever I will keep my mercy for him, and my covenant faithful with him. And I will set his seed for age upon age, and his throne as the days of heaven*[^117]. All which are understood of the Lord Jesus, when they are rightly understood, under the name of David, on account of the form of a servant which the same mediator took of the seed of David from a virgin.

Immediately too something is said of the sins of his sons such as is set down in the book of *Kingdoms* and is rather readily taken of Solomon. For there, that is, in the book of *Kingdoms*, *and if his iniquity comes*, he says, *I will reprove him with the rod of men and with the touches of the sons of men; but my mercy I will not remove from him*[^118]; by *touches* signifying the strokes of correction. Whence is that, *touch not my anointed ones*[^119]. And what else is this than, "harm them not"? But in the Psalm, when he was treating as it were of David, that he might say there too something of this kind, *if his sons*, he says, *forsake my law and walk not in my judgments; if they profane my ordinances and keep not my commandments: I will visit their iniquities with the rod and their sins with stripes; but my mercy I will not scatter from him*[^120]. He did not say, "from them," though he was speaking of his sons, not of him; but he said, *from him*, which, well understood, means just as much. For of Christ himself, who is the head of the Church, no sins could be found which it would be necessary to restrain by human correction, mercy being preserved divinely; but in his body and members, which are his people.

Therefore in the book of *Kingdoms* it is said, *his iniquity*; but in the Psalm, *of his sons*; that we may understand that to be said in a certain way of him which is said of his body. On account of which he himself too, from heaven, when Saul was persecuting his body, which is his faithful, said, *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^121]. Then in what follows in the Psalm, *but I will not harm*, he says, *in my truth, nor will I profane my covenant, and the things that proceed from my lips I will not make void. Once I have sworn by my holy one, if I lie to David*[^122]; that is, by no means will I lie to David. For Scripture is wont to speak so. But what he will not lie about, he adds and says, *his seed shall abide forever; and his throne as the sun in my sight, and as the moon perfected forever, and a faithful witness in heaven*[^123].

**10.** After these most firm assurances of so great a promise, lest they be thought fulfilled in Solomon, as though this were hoped for and not found, *but you*, he says, *have rejected and brought to nothing, Lord*[^124]. For this happened to the kingdom of Solomon in his posterity, down to the overthrow of that earthly Jerusalem, which was the seat of that same kingdom, and especially to the ruin of that very temple which had been built by Solomon. But lest on this account God should be thought to have acted against his promises, he immediately subjoined, *you have deferred your anointed*[^125].

It is not Solomon, then, nor even David himself, if the Lord's anointed was deferred. For although all the kings consecrated with that mystical chrism were called his anointed—not only from King David onward, but even from that Saul who was anointed the first king for that same people; for David himself calls him *the Lord's anointed*[^126]—yet there was one true Christ, whose figure they bore by the prophetic anointing; who, according to the opinion of the men who thought he was to be understood in David or in Solomon, was deferred far off; but according to the disposition of God was being prepared to come in his own time.

Meanwhile, while he is deferred, what happened to the kingdom of the earthly Jerusalem, where surely he was hoped to reign, this Psalm went on and said, *you have overthrown the covenant of your servant, you have profaned his sanctity on the earth; you have destroyed all his walls, you have made his strongholds a terror; all who pass by the way have plundered him, he has become a reproach to his neighbors; you have exalted the right hand of his enemies, you have made all his enemies rejoice; you have turned away the help of his sword and have not aided him in war; you have loosed him from cleansing, you have dashed his throne to the ground; you have shortened the days of his throne, you have poured confusion over him*[^127]. All these things came upon the maidservant Jerusalem, in which some sons of the free woman too reigned, holding that kingdom in a temporary dispensation, but having the kingdom of the heavenly Jerusalem, of which they were sons, in true faith, and hoping in the true Christ. But how these things came upon that kingdom, history, the indicator of events, will show, if it is read.

**11.** After these things are prophesied, the prophet turns to praying to God; but that prayer itself too is a prophesying. *How long, Lord, do you turn away to the end?*[^128] there is understood *your face*, as elsewhere it is said, *how long do you turn your face from me?*[^129]. For on this account some codices here have not *do you turn away*, but *will you be turned away*; though it can be understood, "do you turn away your mercy, which you promised to David."

But what he said, *to the end*, what is it but, "until the end"? Which end is to be understood as the last time, when that nation too will believe in Christ Jesus, before which end those things had to come to pass which he lamented above as full of distress.

On account of which here too it follows, *your anger burns like fire: remember what is my substance*. Nothing here is better understood than Jesus himself as the substance of his people, of which is the nature of his flesh. *For not in vain*, he says, *have you established all the sons of men*[^130]. For unless there were one son of man, the substance of Israel, through whom that son of man should deliver many sons of men, surely all the sons of men would have been established in vain.

But now indeed all human nature, through the sin of the first man, has fallen from truth into vanity, on account of which another Psalm says, *man has become like vanity, his days pass away like a shadow*[^131]; yet not in vain did God establish all the sons of men, because he both delivers many from vanity through the mediator Jesus, and those whom he foreknew were not to be delivered he established, surely not in vain, for the profit of those to be delivered and for the comparison of the two cities, contrary to each other, in the most beautiful and most just ordering of the whole rational creation.

Then it follows, *who is the man that shall live and not see death, that shall rescue his soul from the hand of hell?*[^132]. Who is this, but that substance of Israel from the seed of David, Christ Jesus? Of whom the Apostle says that *rising from the dead he dies no more, and death shall no longer have dominion over him*[^133]. For he will so live and not see death, that yet he was dead, but rescued his soul from the hand of hell, where he had descended in order to loose the infernal chains of certain ones; and he rescued it by that power of which he says in the Gospel, *I have power to lay down my life, and I have power to take it up again*[^134].

**12.** But the rest of this Psalm, which runs thus, *where are your ancient mercies, Lord, which you swore to David in your truth? Remember, Lord, the reproach of your servants, which I have held in my bosom, of many nations; with which your enemies have reproached, Lord; with which they have reproached the change of your anointed*[^135]—whether these things were said in the person of those Israelites who desired the promise made to David to be rendered to them, or rather of the Christians, who are Israelites not according to the flesh but according to the spirit, may with reason be asked. For these things were said or written at the time when Ethan lived, from whose name this Psalm received its title; and that time was the time of David's kingdom; and for this reason it would not be said, *where are your ancient mercies, Lord, which you swore to David in your truth?* unless the prophecy transfigured into itself the person of those who would be long afterward, to whom this time would be ancient, when these things were promised to King David.

But it can be understood that many nations, when they persecuted the Christians, reproached them with the passion of Christ, which Scripture calls a change, since he was made immortal. The change of Christ can also be taken, according to this, as reproached to the Israelites, because, when he was hoped to be theirs in the future, he became the nations', and this many nations now reproach them with, who believed in him through the New Testament, while they remain in their oldness; so that for this reason it is said, *remember, Lord, the reproach of your servants*, because, the Lord not forgetting but rather pitying them, they too after this reproach will believe.

But the sense which I set first seems to me more fitting. For to the enemies of Christ, who are reproached because Christ, passing over to the nations, left them, this voice is incongruously fitted, *remember, Lord, the reproach of your servants*; for such Jews are not to be called servants of God.

But these words befit those who, when they suffered the grievous humiliations of persecutions for the name of Christ, could remember that an exalted kingdom had been promised to the seed of David, and out of desire for it could say, not in despair, but in asking, seeking, knocking, *where are your ancient mercies, Lord, which you swore to David in your truth? Remember, Lord, the reproach of your servants, which I have held in my bosom, of many nations*; that is, in my inner self I have patiently borne it; *with which your enemies have reproached, Lord; with which they have reproached the change of your anointed*; not thinking it to be a change, but a consumption. But what is, *remember, Lord*, but "that you may have mercy and, for my humiliation patiently endured, render the loftiness which you swore to David in your truth"?

But if we assign these words to the Jews, those servants of God could say such things who, when the earthly Jerusalem was conquered, before Jesus Christ was born in human fashion, were led into captivity, understanding the change of Christ, namely that through him an earthly and carnal happiness, such as appeared for a few years in the reign of King Solomon, was not to be faithfully expected, but a heavenly and spiritual one;

which the unbelief of the nations, then ignorant of it, when it exulted and insulted that the people of God was captive, what else was it reproaching but the change of Christ—unknowing, to those who knew?

And therefore what follows, where this Psalm is concluded, *blessed be the Lord forever: so be it, so be it*[^136], fits well enough the whole people of God belonging to the heavenly Jerusalem, whether in those who lay hidden in the Old Testament before the New was revealed, or in those who, the New Testament being now revealed, are seen manifestly to belong to Christ. For the blessing of the Lord in the seed of David is to be hoped for not for some time, such as appeared in the days of Solomon, but forever, and of it, in most certain hope, it is said, *so be it, so be it*. For the repetition of this word is the confirmation of that hope.

Understanding this, then, David says in the second book of *Kingdoms*, from which we digressed to this Psalm, *and you have spoken concerning the house of your servant for a long while to come*[^137]. And therefore a little after he says, *now begin and bless the house of your servant forever*[^138], and so forth; because he was then about to beget a son from whom his offspring would be led down to Christ, through whom his house would be eternal and the same the house of God.

For it is the house of David on account of the race of David; but the same is the house of God on account of the temple of God made of men, not of stones, where the people shall dwell forever with God and in their God, and God with the people and in his people; so that God shall be filling his people, and the people full of their God, when God shall be all in all[^139], he himself the reward in peace who is strength in war.

Therefore, since in the words of Nathan it was said, *and the Lord will tell you that you shall build him a house*[^140], afterward it was said in the words of David, *for you, Lord almighty, God of Israel, have revealed to the ear of your servant, saying: I will build you a house*[^141]. For this house we too build by living well, and God by helping us to live well; because *unless the Lord build the house, in vain have they labored who build it*[^142]. And when the final dedication of this house comes, then will come to pass what God here spoke through Nathan, saying, *and I will set a place for my people Israel, and plant them, and they shall dwell apart and shall be troubled no more, and the son of iniquity shall not add to humble them, as from the beginning, from the days when I appointed judges over my people Israel*[^143].

**13.** Whoever hopes for this so great good in this age and on this earth is foolishly wise. Or will anyone think it fulfilled in the peace of Solomon's reign? For Scripture commends that peace with excellent praise as a shadow of the future. But this supposition was watchfully met, when, after it was said, *and the son of iniquity shall not add to humble them*, it was immediately subjoined, *as from the beginning, from the days when I appointed judges over my people Israel*[^144]. For judges had been appointed over that people, before kings began to be there, from the time when it received the land of promise.

And surely the son of iniquity—that is, the foreign enemy—humbled them through intervals of times, in which peace is read to have alternated with wars[^145]; and there are found there times of peace longer than Solomon had, who reigned forty years[^146]; for under that judge who was called Ehud there were eighty years of peace[^147]. Far be it, then, that the times of Solomon should be believed to be foretold in this promise; much less, therefore, those of any other king. For none of them reigned in so great a peace as he; nor did that nation ever hold a kingdom in such a way that it was not anxious lest it be subjected to enemies; because in the great mutability of human affairs such great security was never granted to any people that it should not dread hostile incursions in this life.

That place, then, which is promised, of so peaceful and secure a habitation, is eternal and is owed to the eternal in the free mother Jerusalem, where the people of Israel shall truly be; for this name is interpreted "seeing God"[^148]; out of desire for whose reward a pious life through faith must be led in this distressful pilgrimage.

## Whether David Was the Author of All the Psalms

**14.** As the city of God ran on through the ages, then, David first reigned in the earthly Jerusalem, in the shadow of the future. Now David was a man learned in songs, who loved musical harmony not with a vulgar pleasure but with a faithful will, and by it served his God, who is the true God, by the mystical figuring of a great matter. For the rational and measured concord of diverse sounds, by a harmonious variety, intimates the compacted unity of a well-ordered city. Finally, nearly all his prophecy is in the Psalms, which the book we call the *Psalms* contains, a hundred and fifty of them. In which some wish that those alone were composed by David which are inscribed with his name. There are likewise those who think that none were composed by him except those which are headed *of David himself*; but that those which have in their titles *for David himself* were composed by others and fitted to his person. Which opinion is refuted by the evangelical voice of the Savior himself, where he says that David himself in the spirit called Christ his Lord[^149]; since the hundred and tenth Psalm begins thus, *the Lord said to my Lord: sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies the footstool of your feet*[^150]. And indeed that Psalm has not in its title "of David himself," but *for David himself*, like very many.

But to me those seem to think more credibly who attribute all those hundred and fifty Psalms to his work, and hold that he prefixed to some even the names of others, figuring something that pertains to the matter, but willed the rest to have no man's name in their titles, as the Lord inspired in him the arrangement of this variety, hidden indeed, yet not empty. Nor ought it to move us to disbelieve this, that the names of certain Prophets who lived long after the times of King David are read inscribed on certain Psalms in that book, and the things said there seem to be said as it were by them[^151]. For the prophetic spirit could indeed reveal to the prophesying King David even these names of future prophets, so that something fitting their person might be prophetically sung; just as King Josiah, who was to arise and reign more than three hundred years later, was revealed by his own name to a certain prophet, who also foretold his future deeds[^152].

**15.** Now I see it is expected of me that at this point in this book I should open up what David prophesied in the Psalms concerning the Lord Jesus Christ or his Church. But that I should not do this, as the expectation itself seems to demand (although I have already done so for one), I am hindered more by abundance than by lack. For, to avoid prolixity, I am forbidden to set down all; but I fear lest, when I have chosen some, I may seem to many who know them to have passed over the more necessary.

Then, because the testimony which is brought forward ought to have the support of the context of the whole Psalm, so that there is certainly nothing which contradicts it, even if not everything supports it, lest, after the manner of centos, we should seem to pluck out little verses for the matter we wish, as from a great poem which is found to have been written not about that matter but about another and far different one.

But that this can be shown in any given Psalm, the whole must be expounded; and how great a work this is both the volumes of others and our own, in which we have done this, sufficiently indicate. Let him read those, then, who wishes and is able; he will find how many and how great things King David, who is also a prophet, prophesied concerning Christ and his Church—that is, concerning the king and the city which he founded.

**16.** 1. For however proper and manifest the prophetic utterances about any matter may be, it is necessary that figurative ones too be mingled with them; which, chiefly on account of the slower readers, impose on teachers the laborious business of disputing and expounding. Some, however, show Christ and the Church at the very first sight, as soon as they are said; even though there remain to be expounded at leisure the things in them that are less understood;

such as that in the same book of *Psalms*: *my heart has poured forth a good word, I tell my works to the king. My tongue is the pen of a scribe writing swiftly. Beautiful in form above the sons of men; grace is poured out on your lips, therefore God has blessed you forever. Gird your sword upon your thigh, most mighty one. In your beauty and your loveliness, bend, advance prosperously and reign, on account of truth and meekness and justice, and your right hand shall lead you wonderfully. Your arrows are sharp, most mighty one, the peoples shall fall under you, in the hearts of the king's enemies. Your throne, O God, is forever and ever, a rod of direction is the rod of your kingdom. You have loved justice and hated iniquity; therefore God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness above your fellows. Myrrh and drops and cassia from your garments, from the ivory houses; from which the daughters of kings have delighted you in your honor*[^153].

Who would not here recognize Christ, whom we preach and in whom we believe, however slow he be, when he hears of a God whose throne is forever and ever, and of one anointed by God—surely as God anoints, not with a visible, but with a spiritual and intelligible chrism? For who is so untaught in this religion, or so deaf against its fame spread far and wide, as not to know that Christ is named from "chrism," that is, from anointing? But the king Christ being recognized, let him now inquire at leisure into the rest, which are here said figuratively—how he is beautiful in form above the sons of men, with a certain beauty the more to be loved and admired the less it is corporeal; what is his sword, what his arrows, and the rest set down in this way not properly but figuratively—he who is now subject to him who reigns on account of truth and meekness and justice.

**16.** 2. Then let him look upon his Church, joined to so great a husband by a spiritual marriage and divine love, of which it is said in what follows: *the queen stood at your right hand in gilded clothing, clothed about with variety. Hear, daughter, and see and incline your ear, and forget your people and your father's house; for the king has desired your beauty, for he himself is your God. And the daughters of Tyre shall adore him with gifts; the rich of the people shall entreat your face. All the glory of the king's daughter is within, in golden fringes, clothed about with variety. Virgins shall be brought to the king after her, her companions shall be brought to you. They shall be brought in gladness and exultation; they shall be led into the temple of the king. In place of your fathers, sons are born to you; you shall appoint them princes over all the earth. They shall be mindful of your name in every generation and generation. Therefore the peoples shall confess to you forever and unto age upon age*[^154].

I do not suppose that anyone is so foolish as to believe that here some little woman is praised and described; the spouse, namely, of him to whom it was said, *your throne, O God, is forever and ever; a rod of direction is the rod of your kingdom. You have loved justice and hated iniquity; therefore God, your God, has anointed you with the oil of gladness above your fellows*; Christ, surely, above the Christians. For these are his fellows, out of whose unity and concord in all the nations is made that queen, as is said of her in another Psalm, *the city of the great king*[^155].

She is Zion spiritually; which name interpreted in Latin is "watching"[^156]; for she watches for the great good of the age to come, since thither her intention is directed. She is also Jerusalem in the same way spiritually, of which we have already said much[^157]. Her enemy is the city of the devil, Babylon, which is interpreted "confusion"; out of which Babylon, nevertheless, this queen is delivered in all the nations by regeneration, and passes over from the worst king to the best king—that is, from the devil to Christ. On account of which it is said to her, *forget your people and your father's house*. Of this impious city the Israelites by flesh alone, not by faith, are a portion; enemies, even they, of this great king and his queen. For he, coming to them and being slain by them, became rather the king of others, whom he did not see in the flesh.

Whence, through the prophecy of a certain Psalm, our king himself says, *you will rescue me from the contradictions of the people, you will make me head of the nations. A people whom I have not known has served me; in the hearing of the ear it has obeyed me*[^158]. This people of the nations, then, whom Christ did not know by bodily presence, yet which believed in Christ announced to it, so that it was deservedly said of it, *in the hearing of the ear it has obeyed me*, because faith is from hearing[^159];

this people, I say, added to the true Israelites both by flesh and by faith, is the city of God, which also bore Christ himself according to the flesh, when it was in those Israelites alone. For from there was the Virgin Mary, in whom Christ took flesh, that he might be man.

Of which city another Psalm says, *mother Zion, a man shall say, and a man is born in her, and the Most High himself has founded her*[^160]. Who is this Most High but God? And for this reason Christ, God, before he became man in that city through Mary, himself founded her in the Patriarchs and Prophets. Since, then, it was said so long before, by prophecy, to this queenly city of God—which we now see fulfilled—*in place of your fathers, sons are born to you; you shall appoint them princes over all the earth*[^161]: for of her sons through all the earth are her rulers and fathers, when the peoples, running together to her, confess to her with that confession of eternal praise unto age upon age: without doubt whatever is here said somewhat obscurely in figurative expressions, however it is understood, ought to agree with these most manifest realities.

**17.** As also in that Psalm where Christ as priest, just as here as king, is most openly proclaimed: *the Lord said to my Lord: sit at my right hand, until I make your enemies the footstool of your feet*[^162], that Christ sits at the right hand of the Father is believed, not seen; that his enemies too are put under his feet does not yet appear; this is being done, it will appear at the end; this too is now believed, afterward it will be seen. But what follows, *the Lord will send forth the rod of your power out of Zion, and rule in the midst of your enemies*[^163], is so clear that it is denied not only faithlessly and unhappily, but even shamelessly. For even the enemies themselves confess that the law of Christ, which we call the Gospel, was sent forth from Zion, and we recognize it as the rod of his power. But that he rules in the midst of his enemies, those very ones among whom he rules testify, gnashing their teeth and wasting away and availing nothing against him.

Then, as for what is said a little after, *the Lord has sworn, and he will not repent*, by which words he signifies that to be immutable which he adds, *you are a priest forever according to the order of Melchizedek*[^164]—from the fact that nowhere now is there a priesthood and sacrifice according to the order of Aaron, and everywhere there is offered, under the priest Christ, that which Melchizedek brought forth when he blessed Abraham[^165], who is permitted to doubt of whom these things are said? To these manifest things, then, are referred those which are set down a little more obscurely in the same Psalm, when they are rightly understood; which we have already done in our popular sermons.

So too in that one where Christ, through prophecy, utters the humiliation of his passion, saying, *they have dug my hands and feet, they have numbered all my bones; but they have considered and gazed upon me*[^166]. By which words surely he signified his body stretched out on the cross, with hands and feet fixed and pierced through by the transfixing of nails, and that in this way he offered himself a spectacle to those considering and gazing.

Adding too, *they have divided my garments among themselves and over my garment they have cast lots*[^167], how this prophecy was fulfilled the evangelical history narrates[^168]; then surely the other things too are rightly understood which are there said less openly, when they agree with these which have shone forth with such great manifestation;

especially since those things too, which we believe not as past but behold as present, just as they are read in the same Psalm foretold so long before, are now seen exhibited throughout the whole world. For a little after, it is said there, *all the ends of the earth shall remember and turn to the Lord, and all the families of the nations shall adore in his sight; for the kingdom is the Lord's, and he shall have dominion over the nations*[^169].

**18.** 1. Concerning his resurrection too the oracles of the Psalms were by no means silent. For what else is that which is sung in the third Psalm in his person, *I slept and took my rest; I rose up, because the Lord will sustain me*[^170]? Or does anyone perhaps so play the fool as to believe that the prophet wished to indicate to us, as something great, that he slept and rose up, unless this sleep were death and the waking resurrection, which had to be thus prophesied of Christ?

For in the fortieth too this is shown much more manifestly, where, in the person of the same Mediator, things to come were prophesied, narrated, after the usual manner, as though past; since the things that were to come were already, in the predestination and foreknowledge of God, as it were done, because they were certain. *My enemies*, he says, *have spoken evil things to me: when will he die and his name perish? And if he came in to see me, his heart spoke vain things, he gathered iniquity to himself. He went out and spoke at once together. All my enemies whispered against me, against me they devised evils for me. They appointed an iniquitous word against me: shall not he who sleeps add that he shall rise again?*[^171] Here surely these words are so set down that nothing else is understood to have been said than if he had said, "Shall not he who dies add that he shall come to life again?" For the foregoing words show that his enemies thought and contrived his death, and that this was done through him who came in to see and went out to betray. To whom here does not Judas occur, made from his disciple a traitor?

Since, then, they were to do what they were plotting—that is, they were to kill him—showing that in their vain malice they would kill him in vain, since he would rise again, he added this verse, as though he said, "What are you doing, vain ones? what is your crime will be my sleep. Shall not he who sleeps add that he shall rise again?" And yet that they would not do so great a wickedness with impunity he indicates in the following verses, saying, *for even the man of my peace, in whom I hoped, who ate my bread, has enlarged the heel against me*, that is, has trampled me down. *But you*, he says, *Lord, have mercy on me and raise me up, and I will repay them*[^172].

Who now would deny this, who sees the Jews, after the passion and resurrection of Christ, utterly rooted out of their dwellings by the slaughter and destruction of war? For, slain by them, he rose again and repaid them in the meantime a temporal discipline, besides what he keeps for those not corrected, when he shall judge the living and the dead.

For the Lord Jesus himself, pointing out that very traitor of his by the bread offered to the Apostles, recalled this same verse of this Psalm and said it was fulfilled in himself: *he who ate my bread has enlarged the heel against me*[^173]. But what he says, *in whom I hoped*, does not fit the head, but the body. For the Savior himself was not ignorant of him, of whom he had already said before, *one of you shall betray me* and, *one of you is a devil*[^174].

But he is wont to transfer to himself the person of his members and to attribute to himself what is theirs, because the head and the body are one Christ; whence is that in the Gospel, *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^175], which expounding he says, *when you did it to one of the least of mine, you did it to me*[^176]. So then he said that he had hoped, what his disciples had then hoped of Judas, when he was numbered among the Apostles.

**18.** 2. But the Jews do not hope that the Christ whom they hope for is to die[^177]. And therefore they do not think that the one whom the Law and the Prophets announced is ours, but some other of their own, whom they fashion for themselves as foreign to the suffering of death. And therefore, with a wondrous vanity and blindness, they contend that the words we have set down signified not death and resurrection, but sleep and waking.

But the fifteenth Psalm too cries out to them, *therefore my heart is glad and my tongue has exulted; moreover my flesh too shall rest in hope; for you will not leave my soul in hell, nor will you give your holy one to see corruption*[^178]. Who would say that his flesh rested in this hope, that, his soul not being left in hell but quickly returning to it, it should come to life again, lest it be corrupted, as corpses are wont to be corrupted, except one who rose again on the third day? Which they certainly cannot say of the prophet and king David.

The sixty-seventh too cries out, *our God is a God of saving, and the issues of death are the Lord's*[^179]. What could be said more openly? For the God of saving is the Lord Jesus, which is interpreted "savior" or "salvific"[^180]. For the reason for this name was given when, before he was born of the virgin, it was said, *she shall bear a son, and you shall call his name Jesus. For he himself shall save his people from their sins*[^181]. For the remission of which sins his blood was shed; and therefore it was fitting that he should have from this life no other issues than those of death.

Therefore, when it had been said, *our God is a God of saving*, immediately it was subjoined, *and the issues of death are the Lord's*, that it might be shown that he would save by dying. But it was said with wonder, *and the Lord's*, as though it were said, "Such is the life of mortals, that not even the Lord himself departed from it otherwise than through death."

**19.** But that the Jews, even by such manifest testimonies of this prophecy, even with the events brought to so clear and certain an effect, yield not at all, surely there is fulfilled in them what is written in the Psalm that follows this one. For when there too, in the person of Christ, the things that pertain to his passion were prophetically said, that was mentioned which is plain in the Gospel, *they gave gall for my food, and in my thirst they gave me vinegar to drink*[^182]. And as though after such a banquet and feasts of this kind set before him, he soon added, *let their table become before them a snare and a recompense and a stumbling block; let their eyes be darkened that they see not, and bow down their back always*[^183], and the rest, which were said not by way of wishing, but foretold by prophecy under the appearance of wishing. What wonder, then, if they do not see these manifest things, whose eyes have been darkened that they see not? What wonder if they do not look up to heavenly things, whose back, that they may be bent down to earthly things, is always bowed? For by these words, transferred from the body, the vices of minds are understood.

Let these things said about the Psalms—that is, about the prophecy of King David—be enough, that there may be some measure. But let those who read these things and know all of them pardon me, and let them not complain about those things which, perhaps stronger, they either understand or suppose that I have passed over.

## Christ's Enemies Are Called in Wisdom and Sirach

**20.** 1. David, then, reigned in the earthly Jerusalem, a son of the heavenly Jerusalem, much proclaimed by divine testimony, because even his sins were overcome by such great piety, through the most wholesome humility of repenting, that he is surely among those of whom he himself says, *blessed are those whose iniquities are forgiven and whose sins are covered*[^184]. After him there reigned over that whole same people his son Solomon, who, as was said above[^185], began to reign while his father was still living. He had good beginnings but bad endings.

For "prosperity, which wearies the minds of the wise," harmed him more than wisdom itself profited him—wisdom memorable even now and hereafter, and then praised far and wide[^186]. He too is found to have prophesied in his books, three of which are received into canonical authority: *Proverbs*, *Ecclesiastes*, and the *Song of Songs*. But the other two, one of which is called *Wisdom*, the other *Sirach*, custom has obtained to be called Solomon's, on account of a certain similarity of style; but that they are not his the more learned do not doubt; yet the Church, especially the Western, received them of old into authority.

In one of which, which is called the *Wisdom* of Solomon, the passion of Christ is most openly prophesied. For his impious slayers are mentioned, saying, *let us surround the just man, because he is displeasing to us and is contrary to our works, and he reproaches us with sins against the law and decries against us sins against our discipline. He promises that he has knowledge of God and names himself the son of God. He has become for us a reproof of our thoughts. He is grievous for us even to look upon, because his life is unlike that of others and his ways are changed. We were esteemed by him as triflers, and he abstains from our ways as from uncleannesses; he prefers the last things of the just and glories that he has God as his father. Let us see, then, whether his words are true, and let us test what shall befall him, and we shall know what shall be his last things. For if the just man is the son of God, he will receive him and deliver him from the hand of his adversaries. Let us interrogate him with insult and torment, that we may know his reverence and prove his patience. Let us condemn him with a most shameful death; for there shall be respect for him from his words. These things they thought, and they erred; for their malice blinded them*[^187].

But in *Sirach* the future faith of the nations is foretold in this way: *have mercy on us, ruler God of all, and send your fear upon all the nations; lift up your hand over the foreign nations, and let them see your power. As before them you were sanctified in us, so before us may you be magnified in them, and let them acknowledge you, as we too have acknowledged you, that there is no God besides you, Lord*[^188]. This prophecy, under the appearance of wishing and praying, we see fulfilled through Jesus Christ. But against gainsayers the things that are not written in the canon of the Jews are brought forward with not so great firmness.

**20.** 2. But in those three which it is agreed are Solomon's and which the Jews hold canonical, to show that what is found in them of this sort pertains to Christ and the Church, a laborious disputation is necessary, which would extend us beyond what is fitting if it were now applied. Yet what is read in *Proverbs*, that impious men say, *let us hide the just man in the earth unjustly, let us swallow him up alive like hell, and let us take away his memory from the earth, let us seize his precious possession*[^189], is not so obscure that it cannot be understood, without laborious exposition, of Christ and his possession, the Church. For something of this kind the Lord Jesus himself too showed, through an evangelical parable, that the wicked husbandmen said, *this is the heir, come, let us kill him, and the inheritance will be ours*[^190].

Likewise that in the same book, which we touched on before, when we were treating of the barren woman who bore seven[^191], is wont to be understood of nothing but Christ and the Church, as soon as it is uttered, by those who have known Christ to be the wisdom of God: *Wisdom has built herself a house and propped up seven columns; she has sacrificed her victims, she has mingled her wine in the bowl and prepared her table. She has sent her servants, calling together with lofty proclamation to the bowl, saying: who is foolish? Let him turn aside to me. And to those lacking in sense she said: come, eat of my loaves and drink the wine which I have mingled for you*[^192].

Here surely we recognize the Wisdom of God—that is, the Word coeternal with the Father—having built herself a house in the virginal womb, a human body, and having joined to it, as members to a head, the Church, having sacrificed the victims of the martyrs, having prepared a table in wine and loaves, where appears also the priesthood according to the order of Melchizedek, having called the foolish and those lacking in sense, because, as the Apostle says, *he chose the weak things of this world to confound the strong*[^193]. To which weak ones, nevertheless, she says what follows, *leave foolishness, that you may live, and seek prudence, that you may have life*[^194]. But to become a partaker of that table is to begin to have life.

For in the other book too, which is called *Ecclesiastes*, where he says, *there is no good for a man, except that he eat and drink*[^195], what more credible is he understood to say than what pertains to the participation of this table, which the priest, the mediator of the New Testament himself[^196], sets forth according to the order of Melchizedek, of his body and blood? For that sacrifice succeeded all those sacrifices of the Old Testament, which were offered in the shadow of the future; on account of which we also recognize that voice in the thirty-ninth Psalm of the same Mediator speaking through prophecy, *sacrifice and offering you did not will, but a body you have perfected for me*[^197]; because in place of all those sacrifices and offerings his body is offered and ministered to those who partake. For that this *Ecclesiastes*, in this sentence of eating and drinking, which he often repeats and greatly commends, does not savor the carnal feasts of pleasure, that sufficiently shows where he says, *it is better to go into the house of mourning than to go into the house of drinking*; and a little after, *the heart*, he says, *of the wise is in the house of mourning, and the heart of fools in the house of feasting*[^198].

But I think it should rather be mentioned from this book what pertains to the two cities, one of the devil, the other of Christ, and their kings, the devil and Christ: *woe to you, land*, he says, *whose king is a youth, and your princes eat in the morning. Blessed are you, land, whose king is the son of free men, and your princes eat in due time, in strength, and not in confusion*[^199]. He called the devil a youth on account of folly and pride and rashness and petulance and the other vices which are wont to abound in this age; but Christ the son of free men, namely of the holy Patriarchs, belonging to the free city, from whom he was begotten in the flesh[^200].

The princes of that city eating in the morning—that is, before the fitting hour, because they do not await the opportune happiness, which is the true one, in the age to come, desiring to be made blessed in haste by the celebrity of this age; but the princes of the city of Christ patiently await the time of a happiness that does not deceive. This he says, *in strength, and not in confusion*, because the hope does not deceive them, of which the Apostle says, *but hope does not confound*[^201]; and the Psalm says, *for those who wait for you shall not be confounded*[^202].

Now indeed the *Song of Songs* is a certain spiritual pleasure of holy minds in the marriage of that king and queen of the city, which is Christ and the Church. But this pleasure is wrapped in allegorical coverings, that it may be more ardently desired and more delightfully laid bare, and that the bridegroom may appear, to whom it is said in the same *Song*, *equity has loved you*[^203], and the bride, who there hears, *love is in your delights*[^204]. We pass over many things in silence, in our care to bring this work to an end.

## What the Kingdoms of Judah and Israel Were

**21.** The rest of the Hebrew kings after Solomon are scarcely found to have prophesied, through any riddles of their sayings or deeds, anything that pertains to Christ and the Church, whether in Judah or in Israel. For so were the parts of that people called, from the time when, on account of Solomon's offense, in the time of his son Rehoboam, who succeeded his father in the kingdom, it was divided by God's avenging. Accordingly the ten tribes which Jeroboam, Solomon's servant, received, set as king over them in Samaria, were properly called Israel, although this was the name of that whole people.

But the two tribes—Judah, namely, and Benjamin—which, for David's sake, lest the kingdom of his stock should be utterly rooted out, remained subject to the city of Jerusalem, had the name of Judah, because that was the tribe from which David came. But Benjamin was the other tribe belonging, as I said, to the same kingdom, from which was Saul, king before David. But these two tribes together, as was said, were called Judah, and by this name were distinguished from Israel, which was the proper name of the ten tribes having their own king. For the tribe of Levi, since it was the priestly tribe, bound to the service of God, not of kings, was reckoned the thirteenth. For Joseph, one of the twelve sons of Israel, made not one tribe, as the rest each made one, but two—Ephraim and Manasseh. Yet even the tribe of Levi belonged rather to the kingdom of Jerusalem, where was the temple of God, which it served.

The people, then, being divided, there first reigned in Jerusalem Rehoboam, king of Judah, son of Solomon; and in Samaria Jeroboam, king of Israel, Solomon's servant. And when Rehoboam wished to pursue with war, as a tyranny, that divided part, the people were forbidden to fight against their brothers, God saying through a prophet that he had done this.

Whence it appeared that in that matter there was no sin either of the king of Israel or of the people, but the will of the avenging God was fulfilled[^205]. When this was known, both parts rested, at peace with each other; for the division had been made not of religion, but of the kingdom.

**22.** But the king of Israel, Jeroboam, with a perverse mind, not believing God, whom he had proved truthful by the kingdom promised and given to him, feared lest, by coming to the temple of God which was in Jerusalem, where, according to the divine law, that whole nation had to come for the sake of sacrificing, the people should be seduced from him and restored to the stock of David as to royal seed[^206], and he set up idolatry in his kingdom and, with nefarious impiety, deceived the people of God, bound together with himself in the worship of images.

Nor yet did God altogether cease to reprove, through the Prophets, not only that king but also his successors and imitators of impiety, and the people itself. For there too arose those great and notable Prophets, who also did many wonders—Elijah and Elisha his disciple; there too, when Elijah said, *Lord, they have killed your prophets, they have dug down your altars, and I am left alone, and they seek my life*, it was answered that there were there seven thousand men who had not bent their knees against Baal[^207].

**23.** And again in the kingdom of Judah, belonging to Jerusalem, even in the times of the succeeding kings, Prophets were not lacking; as it pleased God to send them either to foretell what was needful, or to rebuke sins and enjoin justice. For there too, although far less than in Israel, yet there arose kings who by their impieties grievously offended God and were chastised with moderate scourges together with a like people. The merits of the pious kings there are indeed praised as not small; but in Israel we read that the kings, some more, some less, but all, were reprobate.

Each part, then, as divine providence commanded or permitted, was both raised up by various prosperities and pressed down by adversities, and was so afflicted not only by external wars but even by civil wars among themselves, that, certain causes existing, the mercy of God or his wrath might be made plain, until, his indignation growing, the whole nation was subdued by the conquering Chaldeans, and not only overthrown in its dwellings, but even, for the greatest part of itself, carried away into the lands of the Assyrians; first that part which was called Israel in the ten tribes[^208]; but afterward Judah too, Jerusalem and that most noble temple being overthrown; in which lands it spent a captive idleness for seventy years[^209].

After these years, released from there, it restored the temple which had been overthrown[^210]; and although very many of them lived in the lands of foreigners, it yet did not thereafter have two parts of the kingdom and two different kings in the several parts; but in Jerusalem there was one prince of theirs, and to the temple of God which was there all came from everywhere, wherever they were and whencesoever they could, at fixed times. But not even then were enemies and conquerors from other nations lacking to them; for Christ found them already tributaries even of the Romans.

**24.** But throughout all that time, from when they returned from Babylon, after Malachi, Haggai, and Zechariah, who then prophesied, and Ezra, they had no Prophets down to the coming of the Savior, except another Zechariah, the father of John[^211], and Elizabeth his wife[^212], the nativity of Christ being now at hand, and, when he was now born, the aged Simeon[^213] and the widow Anna, now far advanced in age[^214], and John himself, the last[^215]; who, being young, foretold of the now young Christ not indeed that he was to come, but yet, while unknown, showed him by prophetic knowledge[^216]; on account of which the Lord himself says, *the law and the prophets were until John*[^217].

But the prophesying of these five is known to us from the Gospel, where also the Virgin Mother of the Lord herself[^218] is found to have prophesied before John. But this prophecy of theirs the reprobate Jews do not accept; but those innumerable ones of them who believed the Gospel did accept it. For then indeed Israel was divided in two by that division which was foretold as unchangeable to King Saul through the prophet Samuel[^219]. But Malachi, Haggai, Zechariah, and Ezra the reprobate Jews too hold as received into divine authority as the last. For there are writings of theirs too, like those of others who, in the great multitude of Prophets, very few wrote down things that obtained the authority of the canon. Of whose predictions, those that pertain to Christ and his Church, I see that some are to be set down by me in this work; which will be done more conveniently, with the Lord's help, in the following book, lest we burden this one, already so long, any further.
