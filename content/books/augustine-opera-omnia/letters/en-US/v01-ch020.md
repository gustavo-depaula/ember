# Letter 20

Augustine to Antoninus

**1.** Since writings were owed to you by two of us, the part has been more abundantly repaid, in that you see one of us present, from whose mouth, since you receive me too, I could have not written back, had I not done so at his bidding—he, on whose departure, what I have done seemed superfluous. And so I perhaps converse with you more fruitfully than if I were present in person, since you both read my letter and hear him in whose breast you know very well that I dwell. With great joy I have considered and pondered the letter of your Holiness, because it displays both your Christian mind, without any disguise of the wicked time, and your most friendly disposition toward us.

**2.** I congratulate you, and give thanks to God and our Lord for your hope and faith and love, and for yourself before him, because you think so well of us as to believe that we are faithful servants of God, and because you love that very thing in us with a most pure heart: although for this your goodwill is rather to be congratulated than thanks to be given to you. For it profits you to direct goodness itself, which surely he loves who, in loving one whom he believes good, loves him whether the man be such or otherwise than he is believed to be. Only one error in this matter is to be guarded against: that no one think of the good of man itself—not of the man—otherwise than the truth requires. But you, most beloved brother, who in no way err in believing or knowing that it is a great good to serve God freely and chastely, when you love any man whatever because you believe him a sharer in this good, your fruit is with you, even if he be not such. Wherefore you are to be congratulated about this matter; but he is to be congratulated, not if he is loved on this account, but if he is such as the one who loves him on this account judges him to be. Therefore, what sort of men we are, and how far we have advanced toward God, let him see whose judgment cannot err either about the good of man or about the man himself. For you, as far as the reward of blessedness in this matter is concerned, it is enough that, believing us to be such as the servants of God ought to be, you embrace us with the whole bosom of your heart. We give you abundant thanks, however, because, when you praise us as though we were such, you wondrously exhort us to desire to be such; and still more abundant thanks, if you not only commend yourself to our prayers, but also do not fail to pray for us. For prayer for a brother is more pleasing to God, where the sacrifice of love is offered.

**3.** I greet your little one most warmly, and, according to the salutary precepts of the Lord, I wish him to grow up. For your household too I desire and pray that the one faith and true devotion, which alone is catholic, may come about: in which matter, if you should perhaps think any other help of ours necessary, do not hesitate to claim it for yourself, relying both on the common Lord and on the right of love. This indeed I would urge upon your most religious prudence: that you implant or nourish in your weaker vessel a fear of God that is not unreasonable, by divine reading and grave conversation. For no one who is rightly anxious about the state of his soul, and on this account is intent without obstinacy on inquiring into the will of the Lord, fails—when he has used a good demonstrator—to discern what difference there is between any schism whatever and the one Catholic Church.
