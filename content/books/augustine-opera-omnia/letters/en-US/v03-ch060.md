# Letter 210

TO FELICITAS, MOST BELOVED AND MOST HOLY MOTHER, AND TO BROTHER RUSTICUS, AND TO THE SISTERS WHO ARE WITH YOU, AUGUSTINE, AND THOSE WHO ARE WITH ME, SEND GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** Good is the Lord, and his mercy is everywhere spread abroad, which consoles us, in his own bowels, concerning your charity. For how much he loves those who believe and hope in him, and who love both him and one another, and what he reserves for them hereafter, he shows especially from this, that on the unbelieving, and the despairing, and the perverse—to whom, persevering in evil will unto the end, he threatens eternal fire with the devil—he nevertheless, in this age, bestows so great goods; *who makes his sun rise over the good and the bad, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^1]: for something has been said briefly, that more may be thought. For how many gifts and gratuitous favors the impious have in this life from him whom they despise, who can enumerate? Among which is that great one, that by the examples of interposed tribulations, which, like a good physician, he mixes with the sweetness of this age, he admonishes them, if they will attend, to flee from the wrath to come; and, while they are on the way—that is, in this life—to be reconciled with the word of God, which they have made their adversary by living badly. What, then, is not mercifully bestowed on men by the Lord God, from whom even tribulation is a benefit? For prosperity is the gift of God consoling; but adversity is the gift of God admonishing: and if he bestows these things, as I said, even on the bad, what does he prepare for those who endure for him? In whose number rejoice that you have, by his grace, been gathered, *bearing with one another in love, being eager to keep the unity of the spirit in the bond of peace*[^2]. For there will not be lacking what you may bear with one another in turn, until the Lord shall so have purified you, *death being swallowed up in victory*[^3], that *God may be all in all*[^4].

**2.** But dissensions ought never to be loved; yet sometimes they either are born of charity, or prove charity. For who is easily found who is willing to be reprehended? And where is that wise man of whom it was said, *Rebuke a wise man, and he will love you*[^5]? But ought we therefore not to reprehend and rebuke a brother, lest, secure, he tend toward death? For it is wont to happen, and frequently it comes about, that for an hour he is saddened when he is reprehended, and resists, and contends; and yet afterward considers with himself in silence, where there is no one but God and himself, and does not fear to displease men because he is rebuked, but fears to displease God because he is not corrected, and thenceforth does not do that for which he was justly reprehended, and, as much as he hates his own sin, so much loves the brother whom he felt to be the enemy of his sin. But if he is of that number of whom it was said, *Rebuke a fool, and he will add to hate you*[^6], the dissension is not born of his charity, but yet it exercises and proves the charity of his reprover: because hatred is not repaid him; but the love which compels reproof endures untroubled, even when he who was reprehended hates. But if he who rebukes wishes to render evil for evil to the one who is indignant at the rebuker, he was not worthy to rebuke, but plainly worthy that he himself too ought to be rebuked. Do these things, that among you either indignations may not arise, or, having arisen, may at once be destroyed by the swiftest peace. Give greater effort to being reconciled among yourselves than to refuting one another. For just as vinegar corrupts a vessel if it remains there too long, so anger corrupts the heart, if it endures into another day. Do these things, then, and the God of peace will be with you, praying together also for us, that we may eagerly fulfill the things we well admonish.

[^1]: Matt 5:45
[^2]: Eph 4:2-3
[^3]: 1 Cor 15:54
[^4]: 1 Cor 15:28
[^5]: Prov 9:8
[^6]: See Prov 9:8
