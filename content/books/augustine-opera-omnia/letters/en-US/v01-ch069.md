# Letter 69

To the deservedly most beloved lord, and worthily honorable and to-be-received son Castorius, Alypius and Augustine send greeting in the Lord

**1.** The adversary of Christians has indeed striven, through our most dear and most sweet son, your brother, to stir up a most perilous scandal against the Catholic mother, who received you into her pious bosom as you fled from the disinherited cutting-off into the inheritance of Christ; desiring, of course, to becloud with a foul sadness the serenity of our joy, which arose to us from the good of your conduct. But the Lord our God, merciful and compassionate[^1], consoling the afflicted, nourishing the little ones, healing the weak, permitted him to be able to do something to this end, that we should rejoice over the matter corrected much more than we grieved over it afflicted. For it is far more glorious to have laid down the burden of the episcopate for the sake of avoiding the perils of the Church, than to have taken it up for the sake of governing the helm. For he shows that he could worthily have received the honor, if the reasoning of peace allowed it, who, having received it, does not unworthily defend it. God therefore willed, even through your brother, our son Maximian, to show the enemies of his Church that there are in her bowels those who seek not their own things, but the things of Jesus Christ[^2]. For he did not, overcome by some worldly desire, desert that ministry of the dispensation of the mysteries of God[^3], but, moved by a peaceful piety, laid it down, lest on account of his honor a foul and perilous, or perhaps even ruinous, dissension should arise in the members of Christ. For what would be blinder and worthier of all execration than, for the sake of the peace of the catholic Church, to desert a schism, and to disturb that very catholic peace by the question of one's own honor? For what is more praiseworthy, and more conducive to Christian love, than, the mad pride of the Donatists left behind, so to cleave to the inheritance of Christ that the testimony of humility be proved by love of unity? And so, as far as concerns him, just as we rejoice to have found him such that the tempest of this temptation in no way overturned what the divine word built up in his heart: so we wish and beseech of the Lord, that by his subsequent life and morals he may more and more declare how well he would have administered that which he would surely administer, if it had been necessary. Let eternal peace, which has been promised to the Church, be repaid to him, who understood that it was not expedient for him which was not expedient for the peace of the Church.

**2.** But you, most dear son—no mean joy of ours, who are hindered by no such necessity from taking up the episcopate—it befits your character to dedicate to Christ in you what he gave. For your talent, prudence, eloquence, gravity, sobriety, and the rest by which your morals are adorned, are gifts of God. Whom do they better serve than him by whom they were bestowed, that they may be both guarded, and increased, and perfected, and rewarded? Let them not serve this age, lest they vanish with it and perish. We know that it must not be long dealt with you in this matter—with how great ease you consider the hopes of empty men, and insatiable desires, and uncertain life. Cast away, then, from your mind whatever it had conceived in the expectation of earthly and false happiness: work in the field of God[^4], where the fruit is certain, where so many promises have been fulfilled so long beforehand, that those which remain are most insanely despaired of. We beseech you, by the divinity and humanity of Christ, by the peace of that heavenly city, whence we, on pilgrimage, by temporal labor procure eternal rest, that you succeed to the episcopate of the Church of Bagai for your brother, not falling ingloriously, but yielding gloriously. Let that people, to which, through your mind and tongue made fruitful and adorned by the gifts of God, we hope for the most abundant increases, understand in you that your brother did what he did not for his own sloth, but for its peace. We have ordered that this letter not be read to you, except when those to whom you are necessary already hold you. For we hold you by the bond of spiritual love, because you are very necessary also to our college. But why we have not displayed also our bodily presence, you will learn afterward.

[^1]: See Ps 103:8 (102:8)
[^2]: See Phil 2:21
[^3]: See 1 Cor 4:1
[^4]: See 1 Cor 3:9
