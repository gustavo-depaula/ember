# Letter 2

Augustine to Zenobius

**1.** We agree well enough between us, I think, that all the things the bodily senses reach cannot remain the same for even a moment of time, but slip away, flow off, and possess nothing present—that is, to put it in Latin, they do not exist. And so true and divine philosophy warns us to curb and lull to sleep the love of these things, a love most ruinous and most full of punishments, so that the mind, even while it drives this body, may with its whole self be carried toward and yearn for the things that are always of the same kind and that do not please by a borrowed beauty. Since this is so, and since the mind sees you in yourself as true and simple, such as you can be loved without any anxiety, we confess nonetheless that we seek this meeting and sight of you, and long for it while we may, when you depart from us in body and are separated by distance. And this fault, if I know you well, you love in us; and though you wish all good things for your dearest and closest friends, you are afraid to have them cured of this. But if you are of so powerful a mind that you can both recognize this snare and laugh at those caught in it, then truly you are great and quite another sort of man. As for me, so long as I miss someone who is absent, I want to be missed in turn. Yet I keep watch, as much as I can, and strive to love nothing that can be taken from me against my will. With which duty in mind I urge you meanwhile, whatever sort of man you are, that the discussion begun with you must be completed, if we have any care for ourselves. For I would by no means allow it to be completed with Alypius, even if he were willing. But he is not willing. For it is not in keeping with his kindness now to lend me his help, so that in as many letters as we could we might keep you here with us, who flee, I know not by what necessity.
