# Letter 245

TO POSSIDIUS, A MOST BELOVED LORD AND VENERABLE BROTHER AND FELLOW PRIEST, AND TO THE BROTHERS WHO ARE WITH YOU, AUGUSTINE, AND THE BROTHERS WHO ARE WITH ME, SEND GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** What you are to do with those who are unwilling to obey is rather to be considered, than how you may show them that what they do is not lawful. But now the letter of Your Holiness both found me most occupied, and the most swift return of the bearer permitted me neither not to write back to you, nor to answer the things you consulted me about as it ought to be done. Yet I would not have you hold a too hasty opinion in forbidding the ornaments of gold or of dress, except against those who, being neither married nor desiring to marry, ought to think how they may please God. But those think of the things that are of the world, how they may please, whether men their wives, or women their husbands[^1]. Except that it does not befit even married women to bare their hair, whom the Apostle even commands to veil the head[^2]. But to paint with pigments, so that one may appear either ruddier or whiter, is an adulterous falsehood, by which I do not doubt that even the husbands themselves are unwilling to be deceived, to whom alone women are to be permitted to be adorned, according to indulgence, not according to command. For the true adornment, especially of Christian men and women, is not only no lying paint, but not even the pomp of gold and dress, but good morals.

**2.** But the execrable superstition of amulets, among which the earrings of men too, hung from the tops of the ears on one side, are reckoned, is applied not for pleasing men, but for serving demons. But who could find special prohibitions of wicked superstitions in the Scriptures, when the Apostle says generally: *I do not want you to become associates of demons*[^3]; and again: *For what concord has Christ with Belial?*[^4] Unless perhaps, because he named Belial, and forbade the general fellowship of demons, it is lawful for Christians to sacrifice to Neptune, because we read nothing forbidden specifically about Neptune! Let the wretches in the meantime be admonished that, if they are unwilling to obey the more wholesome precepts, they at least not defend their sacrileges, lest they entangle themselves in a greater crime. But what is to be done with them, if they fear to loose their earrings, and do not fear to receive the body of Christ with the sign of the devil? But concerning ordaining one who was baptized in the party of Donatus, I cannot be an adviser to you: for it is one thing to do it if you are compelled, another to counsel that you do it.

[^1]: See 1 Cor 7:32-34
[^2]: See 1 Cor 11:5-6
[^3]: 1 Cor 10:20
[^4]: 2 Cor 6:15
