# Letter 198

TO AUGUSTINE, MOST BLESSED LORD, AND BROTHER AND FELLOW BISHOP TO BE VENERATED WITH MOST SINCERE LOVE, HESYCHIUS SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** While I was desiring and awaiting it, our holy fellow presbyter Cornutus brought me the letter of your Blessedness which you sent, which gladdened me, because you deigned to have a good remembrance of us—you who also, in a few words, in the proper speech of your holy mind, signified to me in passing about the things I had asked. And you added some things from the little works of the holy fellow presbyter Jerome, that I might be able to fulfill my question by reading his work on the holy Scriptures. And because you deigned to ask of us that we should intimate by letter to your most sincere Charity what we thought about these very questions, I have written below, regarding the things about which I read in your writing, as far as the slight understanding of my mediocrity was able to think or understand.

**2.** Since all things are carried on by the will and power of God almighty, the Creator of all creation—both the things that have been done, and those too that are to be, are made known by the voices of the holy Prophets, who, before the things that were to be came about, spoke to men by the divine will. Whence it is full enough of wonder, if God established that those things which he willed to be foretold could in no way reach men's perception, according to that passage which the Lord spoke to the blessed Apostles, saying: *No one can know the times which the Father has placed in his own power*[^1]. First, because in the most ancient books of the Churches it is not written thus, "No one can"; but it is written, *It is not for you to know the times or the moments which the Father has placed in his own power*[^2]: which sense of the saying is rightly completed by the following word, when it says, *But you shall be witnesses to me in Jerusalem, and in Judea, and in Samaria, and to the uttermost part of the earth*[^3]. He willed, then, that the Apostles be understood as witnesses not of the consummation of the world, but of his name and resurrection.

**3.** For about knowing the times the Lord himself admonishes: *Who, then, is the faithful and prudent servant, whom the Lord has set over his household, to give them food in due time? Blessed is that servant whom his Lord, when he comes, shall find so doing*[^4]. The household of Christ is fed by the word of preaching, and he is found a faithful servant who, in due time, furnishes the necessary food to those who await the Lord, to those who believe. For the evil servant is thus rebuked: *But if that evil servant shall say, My lord delays his coming; the lord of that servant will come in a day when he does not look for him, and in an hour that he does not know*[^5]; and the rest. Likewise he shows why the time is not recognized, saying: *Hypocrites, you know how to discern the face of the sky; why do you not discern this time?*[^6] Likewise the Apostle: *In the last days perilous times shall be at hand*[^7]; and the rest. Likewise the Apostle: *But of the times and the moments we have no need to write to you: for you yourselves know diligently that the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night. For when they shall say, Peace and security, then sudden destruction shall come upon them, as the pangs upon a woman in travail, and they shall not escape*[^8]. Likewise the Apostle: *Do you not remember that, when I was with you, I told you these things? And now you know what restrains, that he may be revealed in his own time. For the mystery of iniquity already works; only that he who now holds, let him hold, until he be taken out of the way: and then shall that wicked one be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus shall slay with the spirit of his mouth*[^9]. Likewise the Lord in the Gospel rebukes the Jews: *And you, if you had known the time of your visitation, perhaps you would have remained; but now they are hidden from your eyes*[^10]. And the Lord thus preaches to the Jews: *Repent; the times are fulfilled; believe the Gospel*[^11]. And to the Jews indeed he rightly said "fulfilled," because their times ended thirty-five or forty years after his preaching. And in Daniel: *Until the beast was slain, and perished, and its body was given to be burned; and the kingdom of the other beasts was taken away, and length of life was given to them until a time and a time*[^12]; which is said in Greek, *heos chronou kai kairou*. And there follows: *And behold, one coming with the clouds of heaven like the Son of man*[^13]. The mystery of the beast, from the removal of the other beasts, is manifest to those who understand the Scripture.

**4.** The coming of the Lord must be loved and awaited. For there is great blessedness for those who love his coming, as the blessed apostle Paul gives witness: *For the rest*, he says, *there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the just judge, will render to me in that day: and not to me only, but to those also who love the coming of the Lord*[^14]. And the Lord in the Gospel: *Then the righteous shall shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father*[^15]. Likewise the prophet: *For behold, darkness and gloom shall cover the earth over the nations; but in you the Lord shall appear, and his majesty shall be seen in you*[^16]. Likewise the prophet: *But those who wait for the Lord shall exult with strength; they shall put forth wings like eagles; they shall run and not be weary, they shall walk and not hunger*[^17]. And very many such things are found which pertain to the blessedness of those who love the coming of the Lord.

**5.** But that no one can gather the measures of the times is manifest. The Gospel indeed says: *Of that day and hour no one knows*[^18]; but I, on account of the incapacity of my understanding, say that neither the day, nor the month, nor the year of his coming can be known; but it befits me, by seeing and believing the signs which are of his coming, both to await it and to render this food to those who believe, so that, awaiting it, they may love the coming of him who said, *When you see all these things, know that it is near, at the doors*[^19]. The evangelical and prophetic signs, then, which are fulfilled in us, manifest the coming of the Lord. For in vain do those who either inquire or who cavil seek to comprehend in a reckoning the days and years, since it is written, *And unless those days had been shortened, no flesh would be saved; but for the sake of the elect those days shall be shortened*[^20]. It is certain that the time lacks reckoning which is to be shortened by the Lord, who appointed the times; but that his coming has drawn near, some of whose signs of coming we see, out of those that have been done, to be fulfilled; and again he says: *But when these things begin to come to pass, breathe again and lift up your heads, for your redemption shall draw near*[^21]. But what signs he said must be seen is manifest in the Gospel of holy Luke: *And Jerusalem shall be trodden down by the Gentiles, until the times of the Gentiles be fulfilled*[^22]. This has been done, and no one doubts that it is being done. And there follows: *And there shall be signs in the sun, and the moon, and in the stars; and on the earth distress of nations*[^23]. The things that we suffer even punishment compels us to confess, if perhaps the will does not heed it: for it is manifest that, at one time, both signs in heaven and the distress of nations on earth are seen and endured by men. And there follows: *Men withering away for fear, and expectation of the things which shall come upon the whole world*[^24]. It is certain that no country, no place, in our times is not afflicted or humbled, as it was said, *For fear, and expectation of the things which shall come upon the whole world*: and all the signs which the Gospel above manifests to those who read it are for the greatest part fulfilled.

**6.** But as for what was said, *And this Gospel shall be preached in the whole world, and then the end will come*[^25]—first . . . since the Lord's own promise was such, that his Apostles themselves should become witnesses of his name and resurrection in Jerusalem, and in Judea, and in Samaria, and to the uttermost part of the earth, and the Apostle teaches by this authority: *But I say, Have they not heard? Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^26]. Likewise: *Because of the hope which is laid up for you, which you heard before in the word of the truth of the Gospel, which has come to you, as it is also in all the world, bearing fruit and increasing*[^27]. But the faith announced by the Apostles among the nations had many persecutors, so that, being held back, it grew strong more slowly, that this might be fulfilled: *Before all these things, they shall lay their hands on you, and persecute you, delivering you up to the synagogues and to prisons, bringing you before kings and governors, for my name's sake*[^28]: so that this might be fulfilled which was written, *And you shall be speedily rebuilt by those by whom you were destroyed*[^29]. For from the time when, by the will of God, the most clement emperors began to be Christian—although the faith, by reason of persecution, was growing little by little in the world—once the kings were made Christian, the Gospel of Christ penetrated everywhere in a short time.

**7.** The exposition of the blessed Daniel concerning the Weeks, although our holy fellow presbyter Jerome expounded it as the teachers of the Churches had handed it down, leaves the reader in suspense. For if that most learned man, our fellow presbyter, says it is dangerous to judge of the opinions of the masters of the Churches and to prefer one to another, how much more cannot the reader do this, which the master doubted to do! But we believe what the Lord says: *For heaven and earth shall pass away; but one jot or one tittle shall not pass from the Law, until all things are accomplished*[^30]. How, then, the mystery of the Weeks has been fulfilled up to the nativity and passion of Christ, I wonder; since the Prophet spoke that in the half of a week, saying, *In the half of the week my sacrifice and supplication shall be taken away, and the abomination of desolations shall be unto sacrifice*[^31]. If, then, this abomination had already been fulfilled, how does the Lord admonish and say, *When you see the abomination of desolation, which was spoken of by Daniel the prophet, standing in the holy place—let him who reads understand*[^32]? But, lest I should be a despiser of the petition of your Blessedness, I have written to your Charity these things as I have thought them. But deign to instruct and gladden us more fully by writing back the word of your grace.
