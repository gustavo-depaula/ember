# Letter 197

TO HESYCHIUS, MOST BLESSED LORD, FROM AUGUSTINE.

**1.** Now that your son, our fellow presbyter Cornutus, is returning to your Holiness—the one through whom I received the letter of your Veneration, by which you deigned to visit my littleness—I at last render my reply, and the owed duty of returning your greeting, much commending myself to your prayers most acceptable to the Lord, most blessed lord and brother. But concerning the prophetic sayings often foretold, about which you wished that I should write something, it seemed better to me to direct to your Blessedness the expositions of those same words by the most learned man, holy Jerome (lest perhaps you do not have them), excerpted from his little works. But if you already had them, and they did not satisfy your inquiry, I ask that you not be reluctant to write back to me what you think of these matters, and how you yourself understand those prophetic oracles. For I think that passage about the Weeks of Daniel must be understood especially according to the time that has already passed: for concerning the coming of the Savior, which is awaited at the end, I do not dare to number the times; nor do I think that any prophet has fixed beforehand the number of years concerning this matter, but that there rather prevails what the Lord himself says, *No one can know the times which the Father has placed in his own power*[^1].

**2.** For as to what he says in another place, *But of that day and hour no one knows*[^2], there are those who so take it as to think that they can compute the times, but that only the very day and hour no one knows: where I forbear to say how the Scriptures are accustomed to put "day" or "hour" even for a period of time. But certainly that saying about the ignorance of the times was uttered most plainly. For when on this point the Lord was questioned by his disciples, *No one*, he says, *can know the times which the Father has placed in his own power*[^3]. For he did not say "the day" or "the hour," but "the times," which are not usually said in a short span, like a day or an hour—especially if we look at the Greek text, from which language we know that same book, where this is written, was translated into ours, although it could not be sufficiently expressed in Latin. For there it is read in Greek, *chronous e kairous*. But our translators call both of these words "times," whether *chronous* or *kairous*, although these two have between themselves a difference not to be neglected. For the Greeks call *kairoi* indeed times, yet not those that pass in the rolls of intervals, but those that are perceived in things opportune or inopportune for something—as harvest, vintage, heat, cold, peace, war, and the like; but they call *chronoi* the very spans of times.

**3.** And this certainly the Apostles themselves did not so inquire after, as though they wished to know one last day or hour—that is, a small part of a day; but whether the time was now opportune in which the kingdom should be restored to Israel. Then they heard: *No one can know the times which the Father has placed in his own power*[^4]—that is, *chronous e kairous*: which, if it were said in Latin, "times or opportunities," not even thus would what was said be expressed; because whether times are opportune or inopportune, they are called *kairoi*. To compute the times, then, that is, the *kairoi*, so that we may know when the end of this age or the coming of the Lord will be, seems to me nothing other than to wish to know what he himself says no one can know.

**4.** But the opportunity of that time will surely not be before the Gospel is preached *in the whole world for a testimony to all nations*. For a most plain sentence is read on this matter from the Savior, who says: *And this Gospel of the kingdom shall be preached in the whole world for a testimony to all nations, and then the end will come*[^5]. "Then it will come"—what is this, if not "before, it will not come"? How long after, then, it may come is uncertain to us; but that it will not come before, we surely ought not to doubt. If, therefore, the servants of God were to undertake this labor, that, traversing the world, they might gather as best they could what nations remain where the Gospel has not yet been preached, from this we could in some way perceive how far this time is from the end of the age. But if, on account of certain inaccessible and inhospitable places, it is not believed possible that the world be traversed by the servants of God, and that it be faithfully reported how many and how great nations are still without the Gospel of Christ, much less, I think, can it be comprehended in the Scriptures how great are the times to be until the end, since in them we read, *No one can know the times which the Father has placed in his own power*[^6]. Hence, even if it were now most certainly announced to us that the Gospel is preached among all nations, not even thus could we say how much time remains until the end; but we would deservedly say that it more and more draws near. Unless perhaps someone answer that, with the Gospel preached with such speed, the Roman nations and very many barbarian ones have been occupied, and thus some have been converted to the faith of Christ not gradually but suddenly, so that it is not incredible that in a few years—if not of our life, who have already grown old, then surely of the young who will come to old age—absolutely all the remaining nations could be filled with the Gospel. But if it will be so, it can more easily be proved by experience, when it has been done, than be found by reading, before it is done.

**5.** This the opinion of a certain man, whom the presbyter Jerome too marks for rashness, has compelled me to say—because he dared to expound the Weeks of Daniel concerning the future coming of Christ, not the past. But if for your better merits the Lord has revealed or shall reveal something better to the holy humility of your heart, I ask that you deign to share it with us, and to receive this reply of ours as that of a man who would indeed prefer to have knowledge rather than ignorance of those things you inquired of me; but because I have not yet been able to attain it, I rather choose to confess a cautious ignorance than to profess a false knowledge.

[^1]: Acts 1:7
[^2]: Matt 24:36
[^3]: Acts 1:7
[^4]: Acts 1:7
