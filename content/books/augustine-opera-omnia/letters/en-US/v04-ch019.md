# Letter 227

AUGUSTINE, TO ALYPIUS

**1.** Brother Paul is here safe and sound; he brings the second round of cares for his affairs; the Lord will grant that these too be the last. He greets you much, and tells of joys about Gabinianus, that, freed by the mercy of God from that cause of his, he is not only a Christian but even a faithful one, very good, baptized recently at Easter, having in his heart and in his mouth the grace he has received. How much I long for him—when shall I unfold it? But you know how I love him. The chief physician Dioscorus too is a faithful Christian, having obtained grace at the same time; hear, too, in what manner: for that little neck of his, or that tongue, would not have been subdued except by some prodigy. His daughter, in whom alone he found rest, was sick, and came even to the utter despair of temporal safety, the father himself reporting it. It is said, then—and it is established, since both before brother Paul's return Count Peregrinus, a praiseworthy man and a good Christian, who was baptized with them at the same time, told me this—it is said, then, that that old man, at last converted to imploring the mercy of Christ, bound himself by a vow that he would be a Christian, if he saw her safe. It came about. But he dissembled to discharge what he had vowed: but the hand was still upraised[^1]. For he is suddenly suffused with blindness: and at once it came to his mind whence that was; he cried out, confessing, and again vowed that, his sight recovered, he would fulfill what he had vowed. He recovered it, fulfilled it: and the hand was still upraised. He had not held the Creed, or had perhaps refused to hold it, and had excused himself that he could not: God had seen. Then, after all the festivities of his reception, he is dissolved into paralysis in many, indeed nearly all, his members, and even his tongue. Then, admonished by a dream, he confesses in writing that this had been said to him to have happened on this account, that he had not rendered the Creed. After that confession, the functions of all his members are restored, except of the tongue alone; yet that he had learned the Creed, and therefore now held it by memory, he nonetheless confessed in writing, in that same trial: and so all that frivolity was deleted from him which, as you know, much discolored a certain natural kindness of his, and made him, insulting Christians, very sacrilegious. What shall I say, but, to the Lord *let us sing a hymn*[^2], and exalt him above all forever, Amen?

[^1]: See Isa 5:25
[^2]: See Dan 3:57 LXX
