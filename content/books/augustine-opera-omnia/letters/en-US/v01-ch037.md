# Letter 37

To the most blessed lord and father, to be embraced with venerably most sincere love, Simplician, Augustine sends greeting in the Lord

**1.** A letter full of good joys, that you are mindful of me, and love me as you are wont, and that whatever of his gifts the Lord has deigned to confer on me by his mercy, not by my merits, is to you a matter of great congratulation—a letter sent by the gift of your Holiness, I have received: in which I have drawn the fatherly affection toward me from your most kindly heart not as something sudden and new, but have revisited it as something surely experienced and known, most blessed lord, and to be embraced with venerably most sincere love.

**2.** But whence has so great a felicity arisen for our literary labor, in which we have sweated in the writing of certain books, that they should be read by your Dignity? except because the Lord, to whom my soul is subject, wished to console my cares, and to refresh me from the fear by which it is necessary that I be anxious in such works, lest perhaps somewhere, more unlearned or more incautious, I should stumble, although on the most level field of truth. For when what I write pleases you, I know whom it pleases; since I know who dwells in you. For the same distributor and bestower of all spiritual gifts has, through your verdict, confirmed my obedience. For whatever those writings have worthy of your delight, in my ministry God said, Let it be made, and it was made: but in your approval *God saw that it is good*[^1].

**3.** The little questions, indeed, which you deigned to bid me unravel, even if, entangled by my own slowness, I did not understand them, yet, helped by your merits, I would open up. Only this I ask, that for my weakness you beseech God, and that, whether in those matters in which you kindly and fatherly wished to exercise me, or in others, whatever of ours may by chance come into your holy hands—since I recognize both the things given by God and my own errors—you not only bestow the care of a reader, but also assume the censure of a corrector. Farewell.

[^1]: See Gen 1:31
