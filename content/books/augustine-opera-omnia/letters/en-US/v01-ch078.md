# Letter 78

TO HIS MOST BELOVED BROTHERS, THE CLERGY, THE ELDERS, AND THE WHOLE PEOPLE OF THE CHURCH OF HIPPO, WHOM I SERVE IN THE LOVE OF CHRIST, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** Would that, attending to the Scriptures of God with anxious mind, you had no need of the help of my words in any scandals, and that rather he consoled you who consoles us also: he who foretold beforehand not only the good things that he will render to his saints and faithful, but also the evil things with which this world was to abound, and took care that they be written down beforehand, so that we might more surely await the good things to follow after the end of the age than feel the evil things, likewise foretold beforehand, going before the end of the age. Hence the Apostle also says: *For whatever was written beforehand was written for our instruction, so that through the patience and consolation of the Scriptures we might have hope toward God*[^1]. But what need was there that the Lord Jesus himself should not only say: *Then the righteous will shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father*[^2], which will come to pass after the end of the age, but should also cry out: *Woe to the world because of scandals*[^3]—except so that we might not flatter ourselves that we can come to the seats of eternal happiness unless, exercised by temporal evils, we have not failed? What need was there for him to say: *Because iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold*—except so that those of whom he immediately went on to speak, when he added: *He who perseveres to the end will be saved*[^4], when they saw, with this abundance of iniquity, love growing cold, might not be disturbed, might not be terrified, might not, as if at things unhoped-for and unexpected, fail in grief; but rather, seeing come to pass the things foretold to be before the end, might patiently persevere to the end, so that, secure, they might deserve to reign after the end in that life which has no end?

**2.** Accordingly, dearly beloved, in this scandal by which some are disturbed concerning the priest Boniface, I do not tell you not to grieve—for those who do not grieve at these things have not in them the love of Christ; while those who even rejoice at such things have abounding in them the malignity of the devil. Not that anything appeared in the said priest that would be judged worthy of condemnation, but because two from our house have such a case that one of them is without doubt held to be ruined, and the reputation of the other is, in the eyes of some, bad, in the eyes of some, doubtful, even if his conscience is not stained. Grieve at these things, since they are to be grieved at; yet not so that by that grief your love should grow cold from living well, but rather should burn for beseeching the Lord, so that if your priest is innocent—which I rather believe, because when he perceived the impure and unclean motion of the other, he neither wished to consent nor to be silent—the divine sentence may quickly present him, vindicated, to his own ministry; but if he was evilly conscious to himself—which I do not dare to suspect—and wished to wound the reputation of the other, since he could not defile his chastity, as the one with whom he has the case says, may God not permit him to hide his own wickedness, so that what men cannot find out may be revealed by the divine judgment concerning whichever of them.

**3.** For when this case had long tormented me, and I could not find how one of the two could be convicted—though I had rather believed the priest—I had first thought to leave both of them thus to God, until in the one who was suspected by me something should arise by which he might be cast out of our dwelling not without a just and manifest cause. But when he most vehemently strove to be promoted to the clerical state—either there through me, or elsewhere through my letters—while I could by no means be brought to lay the hands of ordination upon a man of whom I thought so much evil, or to slip him in by my commendation to some brother of mine, he began to act more turbulently, so that if he himself were not promoted to the clerical state, neither would the priest Boniface be permitted to remain in his rank. In this challenge of his, when I saw that Boniface was unwilling that, to any of the weak and those inclined to suspicion, a scandal should arise from a doubt about his life, and that he was prepared to suffer the loss of his honor among men rather than, in that contention—in which he could not demonstrate his conscience to the ignorant and the doubting, or to those more prone to suspect evil—to proceed vainly even to the disturbance of the Church, I chose something in between: that by a fixed agreement they should both bind themselves to go to a holy place, where the more terrible works of God might far more easily lay open the unsound conscience of either, and by punishment or fear compel them to confession. God is indeed everywhere, and is contained or enclosed in no place, he who founded all things, and he must be worshiped by true worshipers in spirit and in truth[^5], so that, hearing in secret, he may also in secret justify and crown. Nevertheless, as for these things that are visibly known to men, who can search out his counsel—why in some places these miracles happen, in others they do not happen? For the holiness of the place where the body of blessed Felix of Nola is buried is most well known to many—to which I wished them to go, because from there whatever may have been divinely revealed in either of them can more easily and faithfully be written to us. For we too know that at Milan, at the memorial of the saints—where the demons confess in a marvelous and terrible manner—a certain thief, who had come to that place to deceive by swearing falsely, was compelled to confess the theft, and to restore what he had taken away. Is not Africa too full of the bodies of holy martyrs? And yet nowhere here do we know such things to happen. For just as, as the Apostle says, *not all* the saints *have gifts of healing, nor do all have the discernment of spirits*[^6], so neither at all the memorials of the saints has he willed these things to happen, who *distributes to each one his own as he wills*[^7].

**4.** Therefore, although I had not wished this most grievous grief of my heart to be brought to your notice, lest I disturb you by saddening you terribly and vainly, perhaps for this reason God did not wish it to be hidden from you, so that you might join with us in prayers, that what he himself knows in this case—but we cannot know—he may deign to make manifest even to us. As for the name of the priest, I did not dare either to suppress or delete it from the number of his colleagues, lest I should seem to do an injury to the divine power, under whose examination the case still hangs, if I wished to anticipate his judgment by my prejudgment—which not even judges do in secular affairs, when the doubtful matter of a case is referred to a higher authority, so as to dare to change anything while the referral is pending. And in a council of bishops it was established that no cleric who has not yet been convicted ought to be suspended from communion, unless he fails to present himself to have his case examined. Boniface, however, has taken on this humility, that he would not even accept letters by which he might seek his honor in his pilgrimage, so that in that place where both are unknown, equality might be preserved toward both. And now, if it pleases you that his name not be recited, lest, as the Apostle says, we give an occasion to those who seek an occasion[^8], who are unwilling to come to the Church, this deed will not be ours, but theirs for whose sake it shall have been done. For what harm is it to a man that human ignorance does not wish him to be recited from that tablet, if an iniquitous conscience does not delete him from the book of the living?

**5.** Accordingly, my brothers, you who fear God, remember what the apostle Peter says: *Because your adversary the devil, like a roaring lion, goes about seeking someone to devour*[^9]. Whomever he cannot devour by seducing to wickedness, he tries to befoul his reputation, so that, if it can be done, the man may fail through the reproaches of men and the disparagement of evil tongues, and so rush into his jaws. But if he cannot stain even the reputation of the innocent, he tries to persuade him to judge his brother by malevolent suspicions, and so, entangled, to be swallowed by him. And who could either grasp or enumerate all his snares and entrapments? Against these three things, however, which pertain to the present case, God addresses you through the Apostle—first, lest by imitating evil examples you be seduced into wickedness, thus: *Do not be yoked together with unbelievers; for what partnership has righteousness with iniquity, or what fellowship has light with darkness*[^10]? Likewise in another place he says: *Do not be deceived: bad company corrupts good morals. Be sober, righteous, and do not sin*[^11]. And, so that you may not fail before the tongues of disparagers, he says thus through the Prophet: *Hear me, you who know judgment, my people, in whose heart is my law: do not fear the reproach of men, and do not be overcome by their disparagement, nor count it a great thing that they despise you. For as a garment, so will they be consumed by time, and as wool they will be eaten by the moth; but my righteousness abides forever*[^12]. And further, lest with malevolent mind you perish by falsely suspecting the servants of God, recall that word of the Apostle, where he says: *Do not judge anything before the time, until the Lord comes, and illuminates the hidden things of darkness, and will make manifest the thoughts of the heart, and then each one will have praise from God*[^13]. And likewise that which is written: *The things that are manifest are for you; but the things that are hidden are for the Lord your God*[^14].

**6.** It is indeed manifest that these things do not happen in the Church without grave sorrow of the holy and faithful; nevertheless, let him console us who foretold all things, and warned us that, with the abundance of iniquity, we should not grow cold, but persevere to the end, so that we may be saved[^15]. For as far as it concerns me, if there is in me even the least love of Christ, *who among you is weak, and I am not weak? Who is scandalized, and I do not burn*[^16]? Do not, then, increase my torments by failing either in false suspicions or in the sins of others. Do not, I beg you, let me say of you: *And to the pain of my wounds they added more*[^17]. For those who rejoice at these sorrows of ours—of whom it was foretold so long beforehand, in the person of the body of Christ: *They insulted me who sat in the gate, and they sang of me who drank wine*[^18]—are borne with far more tolerably; for whom, nevertheless, even for them, we have learned to pray and to wish them good. For what else do these men sit for, and what else do they aim at, except that, whenever any bishop, or cleric, or monk, or nun has fallen, they may believe, boast, and contend that all such are alike, but that not all can be made manifest? And yet they themselves, when some married woman is found to be an adulteress, neither cast out their own wives, nor accuse their own mothers. But when, of some who profess the holy name, some charge has either falsely sounded forth or truly lain open, they press on, busy themselves, and go about to have it believed of all. These men, then, who seize on the sweetness of their evil tongue from our sorrows, are easily compared—if perhaps they are to be understood in the bad sense—to those dogs that licked the wounds of that poor man who lay before the rich man's door, and who, until he came to the rest of Abraham's bosom, endured all things laborious and unworthy[^19].

**7.** Do not torment me further, you who have any hope toward God; do not multiply those very wounds which they lick; you for whom we are in peril every hour, having *fightings without, fears within, perils in the city, perils in the wilderness, perils from the nations, perils from false brothers*[^20]. I know that you grieve; but is it more keenly than I? I know that you are troubled, and I fear lest, amid the tongues of the slanderous, the weak one fail and perish, for whom Christ died[^21]. Let not our sorrow be increased from you, since your sorrow was not made by our fault. For this is what I tried to forestall: that, if it could be done, this evil should neither be neglected as something not to be avoided, nor be brought to your notice, where the firm would be fruitlessly tormented, and the weak dangerously disturbed. But may he who, knowing this, permitted you to be tempted, give you strength to endure, and instruct you out of his law; may he teach you and soothe you from the days of evil, until a pit be dug for the sinner[^22].

**8.** I hear that some of you are saddened by this more than by the fall of those two deacons who had come over from the party of Donatus, as though they had insulted the discipline of Proculeianus, boasting, as it were, about us, that out of our discipline nothing of the kind had appeared among the clergy. Whoever of you did this, I confess to you, you did not do well. Behold, God has taught you, *that he who boasts should boast in the Lord*[^23]; nor should you reproach heretics, except that they are not Catholics, lest you be like them who, having nothing to defend in the cause of their division, aim only to gather the crimes of men, and boast of these very things, mostly most falsely; so that, because they cannot accuse and obscure the very truth of the divine Scripture, by which the Church of Christ, spread everywhere, is commended, they may bring into hatred the men through whom it is preached—about whom they can also invent whatever comes into their mind. But you have not so learned Christ, if indeed you have heard him, and have been taught in him[^24]. For he himself made his faithful secure even concerning evil stewards, doing their own evil deeds and speaking his good words, where he says: *Do what they say; but what they do, do not do; for they say, and do not do*[^25]. Pray indeed for me, lest perhaps, preaching to others, I myself be found reprobate[^26]; nevertheless, when you boast, boast not in me, but in the Lord. For however much the discipline of my house may keep watch, I am a man, and I live among men, nor do I dare to arrogate to myself that my house should be better than the ark of Noah, where nevertheless among eight men one reprobate was found[^27]; or better than the house of Abraham, where it was said: *cast out the slave woman and her son*[^28]; or better than the house of Isaac, of whose two twins it was said: *Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated*[^29]; or better than the house of Jacob himself, where the son defiled his father's bed[^30]; or better than the house of David, whose son lay with his sister[^31], and whose other son rebelled against the father's so holy gentleness[^32]; or better than the cohabitation of the apostle Paul, who nevertheless, if he had dwelt among all good men, would not have said what I mentioned above, *fightings without, fears within*[^33]; nor would he have said, when he spoke of the holiness and faith of Timothy: *I have no one who is genuinely concerned for you. For all seek their own, not the things of Jesus Christ*[^34]; or better than the cohabitation of the Lord Christ himself, in which eleven good men tolerated the treacherous Judas, a thief; or better, finally, than heaven, from which the angels fell.

**9.** I confess plainly to your Charity, before the Lord our God, who is witness over my soul, that from the time I began to serve God: just as I have found it difficult to find any better than those who have advanced in monasteries, so I have not found any worse than those who have fallen in monasteries—so that from this I think it is written in the Apocalypse: *Let the righteous become more righteous, and let the filthy become filthier still*[^35]. Therefore, even if we are saddened concerning certain refuse, we are nevertheless consoled concerning the more numerous ornaments. Do not, then, on account of the lees by which your eyes are offended, detest the wine-presses from which the storehouses of the Lord are filled with the fruit of a more shining oil. May the mercy of the Lord our God guard you in his peace against all the snares of the enemy, most beloved brothers.
