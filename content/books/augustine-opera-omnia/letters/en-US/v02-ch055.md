# Letter 154

TO MY LORD, DESERVEDLY VENERABLE AND TRULY TO BE LOOKED UP TO, FATHER BISHOP AUGUSTINE, FROM MACEDONIUS.

**1.** I am affected in a marvelous way by your wisdom, both in those things which you have published, and in these which, interceding for the wicked, you do not find it burdensome to send. For the former have so much acumen, knowledge, sanctity, that there is nothing above it; and these have so much modesty that, unless I do what you bid, I judge the fault remains with me, not in the matter, my deservedly venerable and truly to be looked up to father. For you do not press (as very many men of this rank do) to extort whatever, solicitous, you wish; but what seemed to you askable from a judge bound by so many cares, you admonish with a serving modesty, which is the greatest efficacy in difficult matters among the good. Accordingly, I at once granted the effect of his desire to those commended: for I had already opened beforehand the way of hoping.

**2.** I have unrolled your books; for they were not so listless or inert as to allow me to care for anything other than themselves: they laid a hand on me, and, snatching me from other causes of solicitude, bound me with their chains (so may God be gracious to me) in such a way that I am undecided what in them I more admire—the perfection of the priesthood, the dogmas of philosophy, the full knowledge of history, or the pleasantness of eloquence, which can so entice even the unskilled that they do not desist until they have unrolled them, and when they have unrolled them, still ask for more. For the shamelessly obstinate are convicted of error: that, already from the good ages which they recall onward, worse times have befallen by the obscurity of the nature of things, and that all are deceived by their felicities daubed with a certain sweetness, through which they have been led not to blessedness but to precipices. But these our precepts, and the mysteries of the simple and true God, besides the perpetual life which they promise to the purest virtues, mitigate even these worldly things, which must of necessity befall us who are born. And you used a most powerful example of a recent calamity, by which, although you have most firmly fortified your case, yet, if either had been permitted, I would have wished it not to support you. But since a complaint had arisen from those whose folly was to be convicted, it was necessary thence to fetch the arguments of truth.

**3.** These things, meanwhile, occupied with other cares—which, even if they are vain, when we consider the outcome of things, yet are as it were necessary by the condition of being born—I have written back. If there shall be leisure and surviving life, I will write back also concerning Italy, that the work of so great learning may be requited by due, even if not satisfying, services. May almighty God keep your Holiness safe and glad for a most ample age, my deservedly desirable and truly to be looked up to father.
