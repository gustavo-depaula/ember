# Letter 76

**1.** To you, Donatists, the Catholic Church says: *O sons of men, how long will you be heavy of heart? Why do you love vanity, and seek after a lie?*[^1] Why have you divided yourselves from the unity of the whole world by the wicked sacrilege of schism? You attend to the false things told to you by men—men either lying or in error—about the handing over of the divine books, so that you may die in a heretical separation; and you do not attend to what the books themselves tell you, so that you may live in Catholic peace. Why do you open your ears to the speech of men who say what they could never prove, and are deaf to the speech of God, who says: *The Lord said to me, You are my son, today I have begotten you. Ask of me, and I will give you the nations for your inheritance, and the ends of the earth for your possession*[^2]? *The promises were made to Abraham and to his offspring. It does not say, And to offsprings, as of many, but as of one; And to your offspring, which is Christ*[^3]. *In your offspring,* he says, *shall all the nations be blessed*[^4]. Lift up the eyes of your heart, and consider the whole world, how in the offspring of Abraham all the nations are blessed. Then it was believed by one what was not yet seen; now you see it, and still you envy. The passion of the Lord is the price of the whole world; he redeemed the whole world; and you do not agree with the whole world for your gain, but rather contend in a part to your loss, so that you lose the whole. Hear in the Psalm at what price we were redeemed: *They have dug,* he says, *my hands and feet, they have numbered all my bones. They themselves looked and gazed upon me; they divided my garments among them, and over my garment they cast lots*[^5]. Why do you wish to be dividers of the Lord's garments, and do not wish to hold with the whole world that tunic of love, woven from above, which not even his persecutors divided? In the Psalm itself it is read that the whole world holds it: *All the ends of the earth,* he says, *shall remember and turn to the Lord, and all the families of the nations shall worship in his sight, for the kingdom is the Lord's, and he shall rule over the nations*[^6]. Open the ears of your heart and hear that *the Lord, the God of gods, has spoken, and has called the earth from the rising of the sun to its setting; out of Zion is the form of his beauty*[^7]. If you are unwilling to understand this, hear the Gospel, with the Lord himself now speaking through his own mouth and saying: *That it was necessary that everything written about Christ in the Law and the Prophets and the Psalms should be fulfilled, and I preached in his name repentance and the forgiveness of sins among all the nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^8]. What he said in the Psalm, *he called the earth from the rising of the sun to its setting,* this in the Gospel is, *among all the nations;* and what he said in the Psalm, *out of Zion is the form of his beauty,* this in the Gospel he said, *beginning from Jerusalem.*

**2.** You pretend that you flee the intermingled weeds before the time of harvest, because you yourselves are the only weeds. For if you were wheat, you would tolerate the intermingled weeds, and would not divide yourselves from the crop of Christ. Of the weeds indeed it was said: *Because iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold;* but of the wheat also it was said: *He who perseveres to the end will be saved*[^9]. Why do you believe that the weeds have grown and filled the world, but that the wheat has dwindled and remained in Africa alone? You call yourselves Christians, and you contradict Christ. He himself said: *Let both grow until the harvest;* he did not say: Let the weeds grow, let the wheat dwindle. He himself said: *The field is this world;* he did not say: The field is Africa. He himself said: *The harvest is the end of the age;* he did not say, the harvest is the time of Donatus. He himself said: *The reapers are angels*[^10]; he did not say: The reapers are the chiefs of the Circumcellions. But because you have accused the wheat in place of the weeds, you have shown yourselves to be the weeds; and, what is graver, you have separated yourselves from the wheat before the time. For your forebears—in whose sacrilegious cutting-off you persevere—some of them, by the municipal records, handed over the holy books and the instruments of the Church to the persecutors; some, confessing it, let them go and communicated with them; and both parties, in a frenzied faction, gathered at Carthage and, concerning the crime of handing over—on which they had already agreed among themselves—condemned men unheard, ordained bishop against bishop, raised altar against altar. Afterward they sent letters to the emperor Constantine, that bishops from overseas might judge among the Africans; and when judges had been given, as they had requested, and were judging at Rome, they did not obey, but accused the bishops before the emperor of having judged badly. Appealing again to other bishops sent to Arles, they appealed to the emperor himself; and when they had been heard by him and found to be slanderers, they remained in the same crime. Wake up to your salvation, love peace, return to unity. How these things were done, whenever you wish, we will recite to you in full.

**3.** He communicates with the wicked who consents to the deeds of the wicked, not he who tolerates the weeds in the Lord's field until the harvest, or the chaff until the final winnowing. If you hate the wicked, change yourselves from the crime of schism. If you feared intermingling with the wicked, you would not have kept Optatus among you, living in the most open iniquity for so many years. Since you now call him a martyr, it remains for you to call Christ him for whom he died. Finally, what has the Christian world done to offend you, from which you have cut yourselves off in wicked madness? And what have the Maximianists deserved from you, whom—condemned by you and driven out of the basilicas by public judgments—you have again received back into their honor? What has the peace of Christ done to offend you, against which you divide yourselves from those whom you defame? And what has the peace of Donatus deserved from you, for which you take up those whom you condemn? Felicianus of Musti is now with you. We read that he was first condemned in your council, and afterward accused by you before the proconsul, and attacked in the city of Musti by the municipal records.

**4.** If the handing over of the books is a wicked thing, which God avenged with death in war upon the king who burned the book of Jeremiah[^11], how much more wicked is the sacrilege of schism, whose authors—to whom you have compared the Maximianists—the earth, opening up, swallowed alive[^12]! How, then, do you cast at us the crime of handing over, which you do not prove, while you both condemn and accept your own schismatics? If you are righteous for this reason, that you have suffered persecution through the emperors, then the Maximianists themselves are more righteous than you, whom you yourselves persecuted through judges sent by the Catholic emperors. If you alone have Baptism, what is the baptism of the Maximianists doing among you, in those whom Felicianus—condemned, and afterward recalled to you—baptized? Let your bishops answer these things to you laymen, if they are unwilling to speak with us; and consider, for your salvation, what this very thing means, that they are unwilling to speak with us. If the wolves have held a council not to answer the shepherds, why have the sheep lost their reason, so as to approach the dens of the wolves?
