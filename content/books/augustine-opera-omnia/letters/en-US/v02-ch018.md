# Letter 117

**1.** To make a preface with you is not only superfluous but even troublesome, you who desire the matter, not words. And so hear me simply. Old Alypius, asked by me, had more than once promised to answer, with you, a few little questions of the dialogues; and since you are said to be even today in Mauretania, I ask with all my strength and beg that you alone deign to answer what, even in the presence of your own brother, you were without doubt going to do. It is not money, it is not gold, which you would without doubt give for anyone, if you had it; but now, without labor, you speak what I require. I could entreat you more, and through many dear to you; but I know your mind, which does not desire to be asked, but to grant to all, provided only that what is unseemly be absent—of which in this matter there is utterly nothing unseemly: yet, whatever it is, I ask that you grant it to one about to sail. You know how most troublesome it is to me to be a burden, I do not say to your Sincerity, but to anyone. But God alone knows how, impelled by extreme necessity, I have done this. For, you being safe and God favoring, I am about to sail: and you are not ignorant of the characters of men, who are prone to disparage, and how, if anyone questioned does not answer, he will be thought unlearned and dull, you see. Therefore, I beseech you, answer everything without hesitation; do not send me away sad. So may I see my parents; for on account of this alone I have sent Cerdo, and await him alone. Brother Zenobius has been made Master of Records, and has sent us a travel-warrant with provisions. If I am not worthy that you should answer my little questions, let the provisions at least be feared. May the supreme Divinity preserve you safe for us to a long age. Papas greets your Dignation most warmly.
