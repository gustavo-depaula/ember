# Letter 1A\*

TO THE DISTINGUISHED LORD AND DESERVEDLY HONORABLE SON, TO BE RECEIVED WITH ALL HONOR, FIRMUS, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** The books *The City of God*, which you most eagerly demanded of me, I have sent, reread by me too, as I had promised; and that this might be done, with God's help, my son Cyprian, your own brother, truly pressed me in just the way I wanted to be pressed. There are twenty-two quires, which it is a great deal to bring together into a single volume; and if you wish two books to be made, they should be so divided that one has ten books, the other twelve. For in those ten the vanities of the impious are refuted, while in the rest our religion is demonstrated and defended—although this was also done in the former where it was more opportune, and that in the latter. But if you should prefer the volumes to be more than two, then you ought to make five books: of these let the first contain the first five books, in which the argument is made against those who contend that the worship not properly of gods but of demons profits the happiness of this life; let the second contain the other five that follow, which hold that gods of this sort, or of any sort whatever, however many, are to be worshiped by rites and sacrifices for the sake of the life that is to be after death. Now the three other volumes that follow ought to have four books each; for we have so divided that same part that four set forth the rise of that city, the same number its course—or, as we prefer to say, its progress—and the last four its due ends.

**2.** If you are as diligent in reading these books as you were in obtaining them, you will learn how much they help more by your own experience than by my promise. Yet the books pertaining to this work, which is *The City of God,* which our brothers there at Carthage do not yet have, I ask that you graciously and gladly grant to those who request them for copying. For you will not be giving to many, but scarcely to one or two, and they will then give to the rest; but as for your friends, whether they desire to be instructed in the Christian people, or are held by some superstition from which they will seem able, by God's grace, to be freed through this labor of ours, you yourself will see how to share it.

**3.** I, by my writings, if the Lord wills, will frequently take care to inquire how far you have advanced in reading; and it does not escape you, as an educated man, how much a repeated reading helps toward understanding what is read; for there is either no difficulty in understanding, or certainly very little, where there is ease in reading, which becomes the greater the more it is repeated, so that by diligence [what] had been unripe [may ripen], distinguished and deservedly honorable lord and son to be received. As for those books which I composed *On the Academics*[^1] at the time of our recent conversion, since you have shown that they became known to Your Excellence in your earlier letters to me, I ask you to write back how you came by them. And how much the composition of twenty-two books has gathered together, the brief summary I have sent will show.

[^1]: Against the Skeptics
