# Letter 239

AUGUSTINE, TO PASCENTIUS

**1.** If you say that you told me your faith, and that I was unwilling to tell you my faith, which I hear you do not keep silent about; recall, I beseech you, how each is false. For you neither wished to tell me your faith, nor was I unwilling to tell you my faith; but I wished to tell it in such a way that no one could say either that I had said something which I had not said, or had not said what I had said. But you would tell me your faith, if you said that wherein you dissent from us; if you said: "I believe in God the Father, who made the Son the first creature before all the other creatures; and in the Son himself, neither equal to the Father, nor like, nor true God; and in the Holy Spirit, made through the Son after the Son": for these things I hear you say. Or if it is perhaps false that you say these things, this I rather wish to know from you. But if it is true that you say these things, I wish to know how you defend them from the holy Scriptures. But now you said that you believe "in God the Father almighty, invisible, immortal, not begotten by anyone, and from whom are all things. And in his Son Jesus Christ, God born before the ages, through whom all things were made. And in the Holy Spirit." This faith is not yours, but of us both; just as if you added that the Virgin Mary too bore the same Son of God, Jesus Christ, which we equally believe, and whatever else we confess in common. If, then, you had wished to tell your own, you would not tell this which is common to us, but rather that in which we dissent from you.

**2.** These things I would say even in person, if, as had pleased, our words were taken down. But because you were unwilling for this, saying that you feared calumny from us, and withdrew after the meal from the agreement to which you had consented in the morning; why should I say what you would narrate that I had said, in whatever way you wished, and I would have nothing whence to show what or how I had said? Do not, then, boast further that you told your faith, and I did not tell mine; because there are men who consider that I rather had confidence in my faith, who wished it to be written; but you did not have it, while you, as it were, dread calumny. You were ready, then, to deny, if it were objected to you that you had said something against my faith. See, then, what you have caused to be thought about yourself. But if you were not going to deny what was objected, why were you unwilling for what was said to be written; especially since you had greatly wished that even honorable men be present at our discourse? Why, then, wishing to avoid calumny, did you fear the pen of the notaries, and not fear the testimony of most illustrious men?

**3.** But if you so wish that I too tell my faith, as you say you told yours, I too can say it more briefly: that I believe in the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit. But if you wish to hear something proper, whence you dissent from me: I believe in the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, calling neither the Son the Father, nor the Father the Son, nor either the Spirit—either the Father or the Son; and yet the Father God, and the Son God, and the Holy Spirit God, alone eternal and immortal by his own substance, just as God alone is eternal and immortal, by that divinity which is before the ages. If this displeases you, and it pleases you to hear from me how it is asserted from the holy Scriptures, read that too which I sent, written more at length, to your Kindness. But if you have no leisure to read that, neither have I leisure to bandy words in vain. Yet I can, as far as the Lord has given me the means, whether of dictating or of writing to you, I too, whether by dictating or by writing, answer whatever you wish. *To this writing dictated and reread by me, I, Augustine, have subscribed.*
