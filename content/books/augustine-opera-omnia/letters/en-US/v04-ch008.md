# Letter 216

TO THE TRULY HOLY LORD, TO BE VENERABLY SET ABOVE ALL FOR US, AND TO BE HONORED WITH DEVOUT EXULTATION, THE MOST BLESSED POPE AUGUSTINE, VALENTINUS, A SERVANT OF YOUR HOLINESS, AND THE WHOLE COMMUNITY THAT HOPES WITH ME THROUGH YOUR PRAYERS, SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** *Your venerable writings, and the book of Your Holiness, we received with a heart so trembling that, just as blessed Elijah, when he stood at the mouth of the cave*[^1]*, veiled his face as the glory of the Lord passed by, so we covered our rebuked eyes, because in our own judgment we blushed at the boorishness of our brothers, whose disorderly departure made us fear to greet Your Beatitude;* for there is a time to speak and a time to keep silence[^2]*, lest, while we wrote through men who doubt and waver about the truth, we should seem to doubt, along with the doubters, about the sayings of your wisdom, which is like an angel of God*[^3]*. For we had no need to inquire about your beatitude and your wisdom, which is known to us by the grace of God. For at the book of Your most sweet Holiness we were so eagerly gladdened that, just as the Apostles, when the Lord was dining with them after the resurrection, did not dare to ask him who he was (for they knew that it was Jesus*)[^4]: *so we too neither wished nor dared to ask about the book, whether it was Your Holiness's; since the very faithful grace that is generously commended in it bears witness to us, holy lord pope, by its most lively words, that it is yours.*

**2.** *But let us begin, most blessed lord pope, to set forth the course of the disturbance itself. Our dearest brother Florus, the servant of Your Paternity, when at the prompting of love he had set out for his homeland of Uzalis, brought us, during his stay in his own city, blessings from the monastery, from the little works of Your Holiness—one brother, Felix, who is known to have come to Your Holiness late, after his companions, devoutly dictating that same book. They came to the monastery with that same little book, brother Florus having set out for Carthage from the city of Uzalis. Without the book being shown to me, they began to recite it to inexperienced brothers; and it stirred up the hearts of certain ones who did not understand, because, when the Lord said,* Whoever does not eat the flesh of the Son of man and drink his blood will not have life in himself[^5], *those departed who understood it impiously—not through any fault of the Lord who spoke, but through the hardness of a most impious heart.*

**3.** *The aforementioned brothers, who overturned everything, began to stir up the souls of the innocent, while my lowliness was entirely unaware; so far did their gathering of grumblers escape my notice that, had not brother Florus, returning from Carthage and learning of their disturbances, anxiously reported it to me... there was among them a furtive and, as it were, servile quarreling over a truth they would not understand. I proposed, in order to remove these impious questions, that we should send to the holy lord and father Evodius, so that he himself might write us back something more certain about this most sacred book, for the sake of the ignorant. But they were unwilling to accept even this with any patience; instead they seized upon a departure so unwished-for by us—brother Florus being almost thrown into confusion by their fury, against whom they raged because he, as they supposed, had brought them the wounds of this book, in which the sick could not recognize their medicine. Hence we asked the holy priest Sabinus, for greater authority, and His Holiness too read the book with clear interpretations; yet not even so was the wounded soul healed. We gave them, for the sake of piety itself, provisions for the journey, lest we heap up their wounds, which the grace of this book—in which your holy presence shines forth—could heal. When they had departed, quiet and peace exulted in the Lord among all the brothers. For this quarreling sprang from the animosity of five brothers, or a few more.*

**4.** *But because sometimes, lord pope, joy comes forth out of sadness, we are not greatly saddened, since through the ignorant and the meddlesome we have been made worthy to be enlightened by the most sweet admonitions of Your Holiness. For the doubt of the blessed apostle Thomas, seeking the marks of the nails*[^6]*, was the confirmation of the universal Church. We have received, therefore, lord pope, the medicine of your devout healing with the grace of your letters, and we have beaten our breasts*[^7]*, that at least in this way our conscience may be healed—which grace heals and gives life through our free choice, that the mercy of God bestows; but in this time, when we still sing of mercy with delay*[^8]*. For when we begin to sing of judgment to the Lord, we shall carry back the wage for our work, because the Lord is merciful and just, compassionate and upright*[^9]*. For, as Your Holiness teaches us,* we must be presented before the judgment seat of Christ, that each one of us may receive the proper things of the body, according as he has done, whether good or evil[^10]: *for* the Lord will come, and his reward with him[^11]: *for a man and his work will stand before him: for the Lord will come like a burning oven, to set the impious ablaze like stubble*[^12]; *and for those who fear the name of the Lord the sun of righteousness will rise*[^13], *when the impious are punished by the judgment of righteousness. This the righteous man, whose friend you are, lord pope, cries out, and trembles, and says as a suppliant:* Lord, do not enter into judgment with your servant[^14]. *If grace were a matter of one who rewards, the righteous man would not fear the secret judgment of the majesty. This is the faith of your servant Florus, father; not as these brothers have spoken. In our presence they heard him say that the gift of devotion is given not according to our merits, but through the grace of the Redeemer: for as to that day, who would doubt that grace is far off, when righteousness has begun to be angry? This we cry out, father; this we sing, with you teaching us, not secure but trembling:* Lord, do not rebuke me in your fury, nor chasten me in your anger[^15]. *This we say:* "*Amend us, Lord, and instruct us out of your law, that you may give us rest from evil days*"[^16]. *This we believe, with you teaching us, venerable father, that God questions the righteous and the impious, that, with the good set on the right and the evil on the left, he imputes the works of devotion to be rewarded and counts up the obstinacy of impiety to be punished. Where will grace be, when works will be discharged according to their quality, whether good or evil?*

**5.** *But why does an undirected lie not fear to be brought forth? We do not deny that free choice is healed by the grace of God; but we believe that it makes progress through the daily grace of Christ, and we trust that it is helped. What do men mean when they say, "It is in my power to do good"?—if only men did do good. O empty boasting of wretches! Daily they convict their own sins, and yet they take to themselves bare free choice as their boast, not examining their own conscience, which cannot be healed except through grace, so that they might say:* Have mercy on me; heal my soul, for I have sinned against you[^17]. *What would they do, who glory in their own free choice (which is not denied, with at least the help of God), if death were already swallowed up in victory, if our mortal already put on immortality, and our corruptible put on incorruption*[^18]*? Behold, their wounds stink, and proudly is the medicine sought. They do not say, as the righteous man does:* Unless the Lord had helped me, my soul would almost have dwelt in hell[^19]: *they do not say, as the holy man does:* Unless the Lord guards the city, in vain does he keep watch who guards it[^20].

**6.** *But pray, most devout father, that we may now have no care except to expiate our sin with tears, and to commend the grace of God. Pray, lord father, that the pit may not close its mouth over us*[^21]*, that we may be saved from those going down into the lake*[^22]*, that our soul may not perish with the impious*[^23] *through our pride, but be healed through the grace of the Lord. As you have commanded, then, lord pope, our brother Florus, the servant of Your Holiness, has gone forth with all eagerness—one for whom fatigue is no hindrance but a help, so that an enlightened instruction of heart may come to him; whom we humbly commend to Your Sanctity, and likewise beg that you commend the ignorant too in your prayers to the Lord, to be most gently set in order. Pray, lord and most sweet father, that the devil may flee from our community, and that, with every storm of alien questions removed, the ship of our purpose, laden with passengers of quiet, may stand securely within the station of a most safe harbor while it sails through this great and immense sea, and that in that harbor, within which the vessel of life will no longer have anything to fear, it may receive the undiminished price of its pleasing wares. This we trust to obtain by the help of Your Holiness, through the grace that is in Christ Jesus our Lord. We beg all the sons of your apostolate, our lords the clergy, and the holy ones serving in the community of our purpose, that you deign to greet them on our behalf, that with Your Beatitude they may all deign to pray for us. May the undivided Trinity of the Lord our God preserve for us in his Church your apostolate, which he chose through grace, and crown it, mindful of us, in the great Church; which is our wish, lord. But if your servant, brother Florus, should suggest anything regarding the rule of the monastery, we ask, father, that you deign to receive it gladly and to instruct us, the weak, in all things.*
