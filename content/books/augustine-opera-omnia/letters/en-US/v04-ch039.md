# Letter 247

TO ROMULUS, A MOST BELOVED LORD AND SON, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Truth is both sweet and bitter. When it is sweet, it spares; and when it is bitter, it heals. If you do not refuse to drink what I offer in this letter, you will prove what I have said. Would that, as whatever contumelies you inflict on me do me no harm, so they did you no harm: and would that the iniquity which you do to the wretched and the poor harmed you at least as much as it harms those very ones to whom you do it. For they labor for a time, but you, see what you treasure up for yourself *in the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God, who will repay each one according to his works*[^1]. Whose mercy I beseech, that he correct you here as he himself knows how, rather than reserve it for that day when there will no longer be any place for correction; so that he who has given you his fear[^2], on account of which I do not despair of you, may open your understanding, that you may see what you do, and shudder, and amend it. For things seem small to you, and as it were nothing, which are such great evils that, when subdued desire permits you to consider them, you would wet the earth with tears, that God may have mercy on you. Or if I am wicked, who deal with you in this so that wretched and needy men may not twice repay what they owe, when the tenants have given it to your agent, serving the deputy set over them and commanding, and he could not deny that he had received it: if, then, I am wicked, because it seems to me unjust that those be twice required who scarcely suffice to pay once; do what you wish. But if you yourself see that it is unjust; do what is fitting, do what God commands, and what I ask.

**2.** Not for them more (he knows whom I fear), but for yourself I ask you yourself, that, as it is written, *You may have mercy on your own soul, pleasing God*[^3]. And now indeed you are not to be asked, but to be rebuked: for this too is written: *I rebuke and chastise those whom I love*[^4]. Yet if you were to be asked for me, perhaps I would not ask you: but because you are to be asked for yourself, I ask you, though angry, to spare yourself, to be pleasing to yourself, that he whom you ask may be pleased with you. I sent to you on the Sabbath day, when you were still at your meal, that you should not set out unless you had seen me: you reported that you would do so. You rose on the Lord's day, and, as I heard, came to church, and prayed, and set out, and were unwilling to see me. May God pardon you. For what else shall I say to you, but what he himself knows that I desire? but I know that, unless you correct yourself, he is just. But when you spare yourself, you spare me too: for I am not so wretched and alien from the heart of Christ that my heart is not struck with a most grievous wound, when those whom I have begotten in his Gospel[^5] act so.

**3.** Again you will say: "I did not order them to give to Ponticanus." It is answered you: "But you ordered them to serve Ponticanus"; nor could they distinguish how far they should serve, and how far not serve, especially when he demanded the things they knew they owed. But they ought to have had your letters, which they would produce to the agent, if he exacted against your will, and would read back to him that they ought not to give unless they had received your letters. For if you ever by word ordered that they give nothing to the agent, it is much that they should remember; it is much that you yourself should remember whether you truly ordered, or ordered them, or others, or all: especially because even now you heard that the same money was given and was safe with another agent, and it did not displease you that they gave it. But when I had said: "What if this one too had embezzled, would it have to be exacted from them again?" again it began to displease you that they gave it: and when you had often said to me that you had never enjoined your duties either on Valerius or on Aginesus, suddenly, when it was about the wine, because they ought to have shown whether it had begun to turn sour, and it was said to you that he was absent; I believe it slipped your mind what you had so often said to me, and you said that they had Aginesus to show it to, and to act at his discretion. There, when I had said: "Surely you are not accustomed to enjoin your duties on these men"; you answered: "But Aginesus had my letters": as though always those on whom you enjoin something recite your letters to country men, that they may believe that you ordered. But because they see them so enjoined by you, they surely do not believe that those dare to presume anything rashly, unless power had been given by you. And therefore amid these uncertainties it does not appear what you command, nor can they hold anything firm, unless they have your letters, which they may produce to all, and obey only when your letters are produced, when something is to be given them.

**4.** But what need is there to dispute with you so long, and to burden your occupations with words, so that perhaps, when you are angry at my words, you may wish to rage against wretched men? It will be imputed to them for good merit, that for your salvation, on account of which I say so much to you, they bear you angry: but to you I do not wish to say anything heavier, lest you think I say any such thing not from fear, but from wishing you ill. Fear God if you do not wish to be deceived: I *call him to witness against my soul*[^6] that I fear more for you when I say these things, than for those for whom I seem to intercede with you. If you believe, thanks be to God: but if you do not believe, it consoles me that the Lord says: *Say: "Peace to this house"; and if there is a son of peace there, your peace will rest upon him; if not, it will return to you*[^7]. May the mercy of God protect you, most beloved lord and son.

[^1]: See Rom 2:5-6
[^2]: See Ps 86:11 (85:11)
[^3]: See Sir 30:23
[^4]: See Rev 3:19
[^5]: See 1 Cor 4:15
[^6]: See 2 Cor 1:23
[^7]: See Luke 10:5-6
