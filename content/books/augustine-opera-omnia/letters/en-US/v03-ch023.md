# Letter 176

To the most blessed and deservedly venerable lord, and in Christ to be honored, pope Innocent, from Silvanus the elder, Valentinus, Aurelius, Donatus, Restitutus, Lucianus, Alypius, Augustine, Placentius, Severus, Fortunatus, Possidius, Novatus, Secundus, Maurentius, Leo, Faustinianus, Cresconius, Malcus, Litorius, Fortunatus, Donatus, Ponticanus, Saturninus, Cresconius, Honorius, Cresconius, Lucius, Adeodatus, Processus, Secundus, Felix, Asiaticus, Rufinus, Faustinus, Servus, Terentius, Cresconius, Sperantius, Quadratus, Lucillus, Sabinus, Faustinus, Cresconius, Victor, Gignantius, Possidonius, Antoninus, Innocentius, Felix, Antonius, Victor, Honoratus, Donatus, Petrus, Praesidius, Cresconius, Lampadius, Delphinus, from the council of Mileve, greetings in the Lord.

**1.** Because the Lord, by a special gift of his grace, has placed you in the apostolic See, and has bestowed you such on our times, that it would rather count to us as the fault of negligence, if we kept silent before your veneration the things that are to be suggested for the Church, than that you could receive them either disdainfully or negligently; we beg that you deign to apply pastoral diligence to the great dangers of the weak members of Christ.

**2.** For a new and too pernicious heresy of the enemies of the grace of Christ tries to rise up, who try by impious disputations to take away from us even the Lord's Prayer. For although the Lord taught that we should say, *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*[^1], these people say that a man can, in this life, the commandments of God being known, come to so great a perfection of righteousness without the help of the grace of the Savior, through free choice of the will alone, that it is no longer necessary for him to say: *Forgive us our debts*. But that which follows, *Lead us not into temptation*, is not to be understood as though we ought to ask divine help that, being tempted, we may not fall into sin; but that this is placed in our power, and for fulfilling this the will of man alone suffices: as though the Apostle said in vain, *It is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who has mercy*[^2]; and: *God is faithful, who will not permit you to be tempted beyond what you are able; but will make with the temptation also a way out, that you may be able to endure it*[^3]. The Lord too would have said in vain to the apostle Peter, *I have prayed for you, that your faith fail not*[^4]; and to all his own, *Watch and pray, that you enter not into temptation*[^5]; if this whole is of human power. They also contend, with a wicked presumption, that little children too, even if they are renewed by no sacraments of Christian grace, will have eternal life, voiding what the Apostle says: *Through one man sin entered into the world, and through sin death, and so it passed to all men, in whom all sinned*[^6]; and in another place: *As in Adam all die, so also in Christ all will be made alive*[^7].

**3.** So that, to omit the other things which they discuss in great number against the holy Scriptures, these two for now—by which they strive to overturn altogether the whole of what we are as Christians—what faithful hearts can endure: that God is not to be asked to be our helper against the evil of sin and for working righteousness, and that the sacrament of Christian grace does not aid little ones to attain eternal life: intimating these things to your apostolic breast, we have no need to say much, and to exaggerate so great an impiety in words, since without doubt they so move you that you can in no way dissimulate about correcting them, lest they creep further and contaminate, or rather slay, many, while under the name of Christ they make them utterly estranged from the grace of Christ.

**4.** But the authors of this most pernicious error are reported to be Pelagius and Celestius, whom indeed we prefer to be healed in the Church, rather than, salvation being despaired of, cut off from the Church, if no necessity compels it. One of whom, that is, Celestius, is said also to have attained the presbyterate in Asia: about whom, what was transacted a few years ago, your holiness is better instructed by the Church of Carthage. But Pelagius, as letters sent by certain of our brothers say, established at Jerusalem, is asserted to deceive some: yet many more, who could more diligently trace out his views, contend against him for the grace of Christ and the truth of the catholic faith; but especially your holy son, our brother and fellow presbyter Jerome.

**5.** But we judge that, with the help of the mercy of the Lord our God, who deigns both to direct you consulting and to hear you praying, those who hold such perverse and pernicious views will more easily yield to the authority of your holiness, drawn from the authority of the holy Scriptures, so that we may rather congratulate ourselves on their correction than be saddened by their destruction. But whatever they themselves choose, surely your venerability sees that it must be instantly and quickly provided against, that they not entangle others, of whom they can entangle very many in their snares, if it be dissimulated. These things we have directed to your holiness, written from the council of Numidia, imitating the Church of Carthage and our fellow bishops of the province of Carthage, whom we have learned to have written to the apostolic See, which you, blessed one, illustrate, about this cause. And in another hand: Mindful of us, may you be increased in the grace of God, most blessed and deservedly venerable lord, and in Christ to be honored, holy pope.
