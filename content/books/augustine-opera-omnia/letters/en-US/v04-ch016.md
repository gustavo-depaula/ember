# Letter 224

TO QUODVULTDEUS, A LORD SINCERELY MOST BELOVED, MY BROTHER AND FELLOW DEACON, AUGUSTINE, A BISHOP.

**1.** When this occasion for writing was offered me through the priest of Fussala, whom I commend to your Charity, I reviewed your letter, in which you ask that I write something about the heresies that could have arisen from the time the coming of the Lord in the flesh began to be announced. This I did, to see whether I ought now to undertake the work itself, and to send you something of it, where you might consider that it is the more difficult the more you wish it to be made brief. But I could not do even this, hindered by such cares supervening, from which I could in no way pretend to be free: for they turned me away even from what I had in hand.

**2.** This is where I am answering the books of Julian, which he published—eight of them, after those four to which I previously responded. For when brother Alypius had received them at Rome, he had not yet copied them all when he was unwilling to let pass the occasion offered, through which he sent me five; promising that he would soon send the other three, and urging much that I not delay to answer. Compelled by his urging, I have been carrying on more remissly the work I was doing; so that I might not fail either work, the one by day, the other by night, when I was spared by other occupations that do not cease to come upon me from this side and that. But I was carrying on a most necessary matter: for I was revising my little works; and if anything in them offended me, or could offend others, I was at work, partly by reproving, partly by defending what ought to and could be read. And I had already finished two volumes, all my books having been revised, whose number I did not know: and I learned that they were two hundred and thirty-two. There remained the letters, then the popular discourses, which the Greeks call *homilies*. And I had already read very many of the letters, but had as yet dictated nothing from them, when these books of Julian too began to occupy me, the fourth of which I have now begun to answer. When, then, I have unfolded this, and have answered the fifth, if the three do not supervene, I plan (if God wills) to begin also what you ask, doing both at once—this, namely, and that revision of my little works—at nighttime and daytime hours distributed among the several tasks.

**3.** This I have intimated to Your Holiness for this reason, that, as great as your desire is to receive what you ask, so much the more ardently you may ask of the Lord help for me, by which I may serve your praiseworthy zeal, and the advantage of those whom you judge it will profit, lord sincerely most beloved brother. I commend again the bearer, and the business for which he has gone there; if you learn before whom it must be conducted, I ask that you not be reluctant to help. For we cannot abandon the necessities of men of this kind, who are not our tenants, but—what is greater—our brothers, and in the love of Christ pertain to our care. May you live for God.
