# Letter 255

TO RUSTICUS, A MOST BELOVED AND DESERVEDLY PRAISEWORTHY AND ESTEEMED LORD AND SON, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Although I wish all good things for you and your whole household—not only those pertaining to the happiness of the present world, but also those pertaining to the future and eternal life, which you have not yet been persuaded to believe—yet the things that move me, so that I dare to promise nothing as yet about the girl you ask for, I have written back to my holy brother and fellow bishop Benenatus, as far as seemed sufficient, most beloved lord and venerable son. For if you, although you most certainly know that, even if it were in our absolute power to hand over any girl in marriage, a Christian woman cannot be given by us except to a Christian man, nevertheless wished to promise me nothing of the kind about your son, whom I hear is still a pagan: how much more ought I not, on account of those things which you can read in the letter of my aforesaid brother, to pledge anything about the marriage of that girl, even if I held what I said about your son not only as promised, but rejoiced that it had already been done?
