# Letter 61

To his most beloved brother Theodore, Augustine

**1.** When your Benevolence spoke with me, how we should receive clergy from the party of Donatus, if they should wish to be catholics, it pleased me to express also in this letter given to you that which I answered you, so that, if anyone should question you about this matter, you may show, even brought forth in my hand, what we feel or do about this matter. Know, then, that we detest in them nothing but their dissension itself, through which they have become schismatics or heretics, because they do not hold the unity and truth of the catholic Church, in that they do not have peace with the people of God, which is spread throughout the whole world of lands, and in that in men they do not recognize the Baptism of Christ. We disapprove, then, the evil error of theirs which they have; but the good name of God which they have, and his sacrament, we recognize in them, and venerate, and embrace. But for this reason we grieve over them as they err, and desire to gain them for God through the love of Christ, that the holy sacrament which outside the Church they have for ruin, in the peace of the Church they may have for salvation. If, then, the evils of men be taken out of the way, and the good things of God be honored in men; there will be brotherly concord, and lovable peace, that in the hearts of men the love of Christ may conquer the persuasion of the devil.

**2.** And so, when they come to us from the party of Donatus, we do not receive their evils, that is, dissension and error, but these are taken out of the way as impediments to concord, and we embrace our brothers, standing with them, as the Apostle says, *in the unity of the spirit, in the bond of peace*[^1], and recognizing in them the good things of God, whether holy Baptism, or the blessing of Ordination, or the profession of continence, or the consecration of virginity, or the faith of the Trinity, and whatever else there is: all which, even if they were present, yet profited nothing, when love was not there[^2]. But who truly says that he has the love of Christ, when he does not embrace his unity? When, then, they come to the Catholic Church, they do not here receive what they had; but that what they had may begin to profit them, they receive here what they did not have. For here they receive the root of love in the bond of peace, and in the fellowship of unity; that all the sacraments of truth which they have may avail them not for damnation, but for liberation. For the branches ought not to boast, because they are not the wood of thorns, but of the vine[^3]. For if they do not live in the root, they will be cast into the fire with all their appearance. But about certain broken branches the Apostle said that *God is able to graft them in again*[^4]. And therefore, most beloved brother, whomever of them you see perhaps doubting in what order they are received by us, show them this my hand which you know well, and if they should wish to have it with them, let them have it; because I make God a witness upon my soul, that I will so receive them, that they may not only have the Baptism of Christ which they received, but also the honor of consecration and of continence.

[^1]: See Eph 4:3
[^2]: See 1 Cor 13:1–3
[^3]: See John 15:5
[^4]: Rom 11:23
