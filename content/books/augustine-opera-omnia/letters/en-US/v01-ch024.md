# Letter 24

To the deservedly honorable lord and most blessed father Alypius, Paulinus and Therasia, sinners

**1.** *This is true love, this is perfect affection, which you have taught us is in you toward our lowliness, truly holy lord, and deservedly most blessed and desirable. For we have received, through our man Julian returning from Carthage, a letter bringing us so great a light of your Holiness that we seemed not to recognize your love, but to recognize it again. Because, of course, from him who predestined us to himself from the origin of the world*[^1], that love has flowed, in whom we were made before we were born, because he made us, and not we ourselves[^2]*, who made the things that are to be. Formed, therefore, by his foreknowledge and work into a likeness of wills and a unity of faith—or rather, joined by a love that anticipates the knowledge of the unity of faith—we have come to know one another, before any bodily sight, the Spirit revealing it. We therefore rejoice and glory in the Lord, who, one and the same everywhere on earth, works in his own his own love through the Holy Spirit, whom he poured out upon all flesh*[^3], gladdening his city with the rush of a river[^4]. Among whose citizens he has deservedly placed you among the chief, with the princes of his people[^5], in the apostolic see: and us too, whom he raised up when cast down and lifted up the needy from the earth, he willed to be numbered in your lot. But we rejoice still more in that gift of the Lord by which he set us in the dwelling of your breast; and by which he deigned so to insinuate us into your innermost being that we claim for ourselves a special confidence in your love, drawn on by these services and gifts, so that we may not be permitted to love you distrustfully or lightly.

**2.** *For we have received the special token of your affection and solicitude, the work of a man holy and perfect in Christ the Lord, our brother Augustine, composed in five books, which we so admire and look up to that we believe the words to be divinely dictated. And so, with the confidence of looking up to your unanimity, we have dared to write to him as well, presuming to be excused to him through you for our inexperience, and commended to his love; as we shall doubtless take care, with equal affection, to greet through your Holiness all the holy ones, by whose services, though absent, you have deigned to keep us well, both your companions in the clergy of your Holiness and the emulators of your faith and virtue in the monasteries. For although you act among the peoples and over the people, ruling the sheep of the Lord's pasture, a watchful shepherd with anxious vigils*[^6], yet by renouncing the world and rejecting flesh and blood, you have made a desert for yourself, set apart from the many, called among the few.

**3.** *Indeed, by a service that is to some extent a vicarious one—though, as you had bidden, everything I send is unequal to you—I have provided that history of all times by the venerable bishop Eusebius of Constantinople. But in this there was a delay in obeying, because, at your prompting, since I myself did not have this book, I found it at Rome with our truly most holy kinsman Domnio, who without doubt complied with me the more readily in this favor, which I indicated should be conveyed to you. Nevertheless, because you deigned to indicate your whereabouts to me, as you yourself advised, we have written to the venerable partner of your crown, our father Aurelius, in such a way that, if you are now living at Hippo Regius, he might deign to send you our letter and the parchment copied at Carthage. And we have asked the holy men Comes and Evodius, whom by the token of their own love we came to know through your discourse, to take care to write themselves, lest either the kinsman Domnio be longer without his book, or, once conveyed to you, it remain with you without any necessity of returning it.*

**4.** *But this I ask of you especially, since you have filled me, unworthy and unexpecting, with great love of you: that in return for this history of the times you relate to me the whole history of your Holiness: namely, that you set forth what your descent is, from what home*[^7] you, called by the Lord, by what beginnings, set apart from your mother's womb, you passed over—the stock of flesh and blood forsworn—to the mother of the sons of God rejoicing in her offspring, and were transferred into a royal and priestly race[^8]. For what you indicated, that you had already learned of our lowliness's name at Milan when you were being initiated there, I confess I wish to learn the more curiously, so that I may know you in every part, the more to rejoice if you were either invited to the faith or consecrated to the priesthood by our father Ambrose, whom I received, so that we may both seem to have the same author. For although I was baptized by Delphinus at Bordeaux, and consecrated by Lampius at Barcelona in Spain, through the force of a people suddenly inflamed; yet I was always nourished, by Ambrose's love, toward the faith, and now I am cherished in the order of the priesthood. Indeed, he wished to claim me for his own clergy, so that, even though I live in various places, I may be reckoned his presbyter.

**5.** *But about me, that you may know what you do not know: know that a most ancient sinner, not so long ago led out from darkness and the shadow of death*[^9], has drunk the breath of the air of life, and not so long ago has put his hand to the plow[^10] and taken up the cross of the Lord; that we may be able to carry it to the end, may we be helped by your prayers. This reward will be heaped upon your merits, if by your intervention you relieve our burdens. For a holy man helping one who labors (since we do not dare to say "a brother") shall be exalted like a great city[^11]. And you indeed are a city built upon a mountain, or a lamp kindled upon a lampstand[^12], shining with sevenfold brightness; we lie hidden under the bushel of sins: visit us with your letters, and bring us forth into the light in which you yourself dwell, conspicuous upon the golden lampstands. Your words will be a light to our paths[^13], and with the oil of your lamp our head will be made fat[^14]. And our faith will be kindled, when from the breath of your mouth we take food for the mind and light for the soul.

**6.** *The peace and grace of God be with you, and may a crown of righteousness remain for you on that day*[^15], lord and father, deservedly most beloved and venerable and most longed-for. We ask, with much affection and devoted service, that the blessed companions and emulators of your Holiness, our brothers in the Lord, if they deign, both in the churches and in the monasteries at Carthage, Thagaste, Hippo Regius, and in all your parishes and all the places known to you throughout Africa, who serve the Lord in the catholic way, be greeted. If you receive the parchment of holy Domnio, you will deign to send it back to us when copied. And I ask that you write to me which hymn of mine you have come to know. We have sent your Holiness one loaf, as a token of the grace of unity, in which also the solidity of the Trinity is contained. You will make this loaf a blessing by deigning to receive it.
