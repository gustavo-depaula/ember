# Letter 97

**TO THE DISTINGUISHED LORD, AND DESERVEDLY MOST EXCELLENT, AND MUCH IN THE LOVE OF CHRIST TO BE HONORED SON OLYMPIUS, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.**

**1.** Although, as soon as we heard that you had been deservedly raised up—when the fame itself was not yet most certain to us—we believed nothing else of your mind toward the Church of God, of which we rejoice that you are truly a son, than what you soon opened in your letter; yet even after reading those letters which, of your own accord, you deigned to send, we write to you with greater confidence, even if we were sluggish and hesitant to send a most benevolent exhortation that, our humility instructing, through your religious obedience, the Lord, by whose gift you are such, may now at once come to the aid of his Church, distinguished lord, and deservedly most excellent, and much in the love of Christ to be honored son.

**2.** And indeed many of my holy brothers and colleagues, the Church being gravely disturbed, set out, almost fleeing, to the most glorious court—whom you have either already seen, or whose letters you have received from the city of Rome on the occasion of some opportunity: I, nevertheless, although I could share no counsel with them, could not omit, through this my brother and fellow-priest—who, urgent necessity pressing, for the salvation of his fellow-citizen, was compelled to come somehow to those parts even in mid-winter—both to greet and to admonish your Charity which you have in Christ Jesus our Lord, that your good work be hastened with most diligent insistence, by which the enemies of the Church may know that those laws which were sent into Africa, while Stilicho was living, about breaking idols and correcting heretics, were established by the will of the most pious and most faithful Emperor; which they boast was done either with him not knowing or unwilling—whether they boast it deceitfully, or willingly think it so—and hence render the minds of the unskilled most turbulent, and dangerously and vehemently hostile to us.

**3.** But this which, by asking or suggesting, I admonish your Excellency, I do not doubt is done by the will of all my colleagues throughout Africa; and I judge that, on whatever occasion first arising, it can and ought most easily to be hastened, so that, as I said, vain men—whose salvation, even of those opposing us, we seek—may know that the laws which were sent for the Church of Christ, the son of Theodosius rather than Stilicho took care to be sent. For this reason, indeed, the said priest, the bearer of these letters, since he is from the region of Mileve, was ordered by his bishop, my venerable brother Severus—who greets your most sincere Love much with me—to pass through Hippo Regius, where I am; because, when we happened to be together in great tribulations and disturbances of the Church, we were seeking an occasion of writing to your Distinction, and were not finding it. I had already indeed sent one letter in the business of my holy brother and colleague Boniface, bishop of Cataqua; but there had not yet come to us the graver things which agitate us more vehemently: for the suppressing or correcting of which, how by a better counsel, according to the way of Christ, aid may be brought, the bishops who for this reason sailed will more conveniently act, with so great a benignity of your heart—who could, by common counsel, bring something more diligently deliberated, as much as the narrowness of time permitted. Yet that by which the province may know the mind of the most clement and most religious prince toward the Church must in no way be deferred; but even before you see the bishops who set out, as soon as your most excellent vigilance, for the members of Christ established in the greatest tribulation, can, I suggest, ask, beseech, demand that it be hastened. For the Lord has offered no small consolation in these evils, in that he willed you to be able much more than you were able, when we were already rejoicing over your many and great good works.

**4.** We greatly rejoice, indeed, over the firm and stable faith of certain men—and not few—who, on the occasion of those very laws, were converted to the Christian religion or to Catholic peace; for whose eternal salvation it delights us to be in peril even in this temporal life. For especially for this reason do we now sustain graver assaults of enmities from men exceedingly and harshly perverse, which some of them sustain with us most patiently: but we fear most for the weakness, until they learn and are able, with the most merciful grace of the Lord helping, to despise the present age and the day of men with a stronger strength of heart. The memorandum which I sent to the brother bishops, if, as I think, they are not yet there, let it be handed to them by your Distinction when they come. For we have so great a confidence in your most sincere breast that, with the Lord our God helping, we wish you to be not only an imparter of aid, but also a partaker of counsel.
