# Letter 58

To the excellent lord and deservedly to-be-received and, in the bowels of Christ, most beloved son Pammachius, Augustine sends greeting in the Lord

**1.** Your good works, germinating by the grace of Christ, have made you to be honored by us in his members, and plainly most known and most beloved. For if I saw your face daily, you would not be more known to me than when I beheld your inner self, beautiful with the comeliness of peace and radiant with the light of truth, in the brightness of one deed of yours; I beheld and recognized it, recognized and loved it. To this self I now speak, to this I write, to my beloved friend, who, though absent, became known to me, body absent. Yet now we were together at once, and joined under one head we lived, in whose love unless you were rooted, catholic unity would not be so dear to you, nor would you admonish your African tenants—settled in that land of the earth whence the fury of the Donatists arose, that is, established in the midst of consular Numidia—with such an address, and animate them with so great a fervor of spirit, that with the readiest devotion they chose to follow that which they considered that they should follow such and so great a man as you only when the truth was recognized, and, so far removed from you by intervals of place, should go under the same head, and in his members be reckoned forever with you, by whose precept they serve you temporally.

**2.** In this deed of yours, then, embracing you, recognized, I exulted, that I might congratulate you in Christ Jesus our Lord, and send you this congratulatory letter as some token of my heart and love toward you; for I could not do more. But I ask that you not measure by this alone how much I love you: when you have read it through, pass over this letter by that invisible passing-over which is made within, and proceed, by thinking, into my breast, and discern what is being done there about you. For the chamber of love will lie open to the eye of love, which we shut against the tumultuous trifles of the age, when there we adore God; and you will see there the delights of my gladness over so good a deed of yours, which I am not able to utter by tongue nor express by pen, glowing and blazing in the sacrifice of his praise, by whose inspiring you willed this, and by whose aid you were able. *Thanks be to God for his unspeakable gift*[^1].

**3.** O of how many senators together with you, and likewise sons of the holy Church, do we desire such a work in Africa, of the kind we rejoice over in yours! But it is dangerous to exhort them, secure to congratulate you. For they perhaps will not do it, and, as though the enemies of the Church had conquered us in their mind, will lie in wait to deceive the weak. But you have already done that whence the enemies of the Church, the weak being freed, are confounded. Accordingly it has seemed sufficient that you yourself, with a friendly confidence, read this letter to those to whom you can by Christian right. For thus, from your deed, they will believe that it can be done in Africa, which perhaps, while they think it cannot be done, they grow sluggish over. But the ambushes which the heretics themselves with distorted heart plot, since I laughed that they thought they availed anything in the possession of Christ, I did not wish even to write to your mind. Yet you will hear these things from my brothers, whom I much commend to your Excellence, lest in so great and so unhoped-for a salvation of men, over whom, through you, the Catholic mother exults, you spurn even those fearing superfluous things.

[^1]: 2 Cor 9:15
