# Letter 233

AUGUSTINE, TO LONGINIANUS

**1.** They say that a certain one of the ancients was wont to say that, for those who were sufficiently persuaded to prefer nothing to being good men, the rest of learning was easy for them. This sentence (for, if I rightly recall, it is Socratic) the prophetic one, far more ancient, had already preceded, enjoining a man briefly and at once, not only to prefer nothing to being good, but also whence he becomes good: *You shall love,* it says, *the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind*[^1]; and: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^2]. For one persuaded of this, the rest of learning is not merely easy, but that is the whole of learning—the useful and wholesome, at least. For there are many learnings, if indeed they are to be called learnings, that are either superfluous or harmful. Christ, attesting to the Books of the ancients, says: *On these two precepts the whole law and the Prophets depend*[^3]. Accordingly, because I seem to myself to have seen, as in a mirror of your conversation with me, that you prefer nothing to being a good man, I dare to ask in what manner you believe God—than whom nothing is better, and from whom the human mind draws that it may be good—is to be worshiped: for that you believe he is to be worshiped I already hold. I ask too what you think about Christ. For I noticed that you do not make little of him: but whether you think that the way which was shown by him, and that way alone, can arrive at the blessed life, and from some cause neglect not to go by it, but defer it; or whether you think there is another way, or other ways, to so rich a possession, to be desired before all, and believe you are already entering upon one of them, I desire to know, as I think, not shamelessly. For I love you on account of what I said above, and I do not rashly suppose that I am loved by you; nor on any other matter, among those who know one another benevolently, is discourse more fruitful, whether expended, or demanded, or accepted, or received, than that whence we are good and blessed.

[^1]: Deut 6:5; Matt 22:37
[^2]: Lev 19:18; Matt 22:39
[^3]: Matt 22:40
