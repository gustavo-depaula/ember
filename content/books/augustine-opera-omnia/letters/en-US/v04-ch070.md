# Letter 6\*

TO ATTICUS, A MOST BLESSED LORD, AND A BROTHER AND FELLOW PRIEST TO BE RECEIVED WITH DUE VENERATION, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Although I did not receive the letter of Your Holiness through the religious brother and fellow priest Innocentius, through whom I had presumed I would receive it, yet, the cause why this was so having been learned from him, I have written these things as though I had received the writing of Your Reverence and discharged a reply, safe and sound, by the favor of God and the help of your prayers. For when rumor had spread something else, as the aforesaid brother reported to me, you believed it of a man; for what is so credible as, when it is said that a mortal has died, which without doubt is at some time going to come to anyone living in the flesh? But when, from other more recent messengers, you had heard that I was alive, and had indicated it to your Love, he narrated that you had greatly rejoiced and given thanks to God, although it was still uncertain to you.

**2.** Accordingly, lord, I ought not to doubt that you joyfully receive my letter, but I more confidently and more avidly demand, by the right of love, the owed reply that was omitted, although I have reckoned the letter of Your Beatitude sent to my brother of one mind as if it had been sent to us both; in which, rejoicing, I learned that Your Holiness had acted with pastoral care, both that the perversity of certain Pelagians be corrected and their cunning be guarded against.

**3.** But as for the fact that they even slander Catholics, it is no wonder, if in this way they strive to repress the things that are said for refuting their poisoned dogmas. For what Catholic so defends the right faith against them as to disapprove marriage, which the Maker and Founder of the world blessed? What Catholic would say that the concupiscence of marriage is of diabolical operation, since through it the human race would surely have been propagated, even if no one had sinned, so that that blessing might be fulfilled: *Increase and multiply*[^1]; which blessing not even the sin of man, in whom all have sinned[^2], deprived of the effect of its goodness, in so clear and so wonderful and praiseworthy a fecundity of nature, which is beheld by all? What Catholic does not, in every creature, proclaim the divine works of every soul and every flesh, and, by the consideration of them, pour forth a hymn to the Creator, who not only then, before sin, made, but even now makes all things very good[^3]?

**4.** But they, now with a perverse mind, confound, with contentious blindness, the evils which befell nature by the desert of fault with natural goods, so praising the Creator of men that they deny that little children, as having nothing evil, need a Savior, which is their damnable dogma; and they think they can strengthen this wicked error by the praise of marriage, saying that marriage too is condemned, if that which is born of it is condemned unless it be reborn. For they do not see that the conjugal good is one thing, from which good marriage did not fail even after sin, but original evil is another, which marriage did not make and does not now make, but finds already made, and uses it well, when out of it they do not whatever they please, but only what is lawful. This these people refuse to consider, since an error has anticipated them which they prefer to defend than to guard against.

**5.** From this error they do not distinguish the concupiscence of marriage—that is, the concupiscence of conjugal chastity, the concupiscence of lawfully propagating offspring, the concupiscence of the social bond by which each sex is bound to the other—from the concupiscence of the flesh, which burns indifferently for the lawful and the unlawful, and is, through the concupiscence of marriage, which uses it well, bridled from the unlawful and relaxed to the lawful alone; against whose impulse, resisting the law of the mind, all chastity fights, both that of the married, that they may use it well, and that of the continent and of holy virgins, that they may better and more gloriously not use it. This concupiscence of the flesh, then—in which there is only the desire of intercourse—not distinguishing from the concupiscence of marriage—in which there is the office of begetting—they most shamelessly praise; that concupiscence of which the first men were ashamed, when they covered with fig leaves those members which before sin had not been shameful; for they were naked and were not confounded[^4], so that we may understand that this movement, of which they were confounded, was conceived in human nature along with death. For then they had cause to blush, when they also began to have the necessity of dying. This concupiscence of the flesh, therefore, prudently and soberly to be distinguished from the concupiscence of marriage, they proclaim with such great praises that they think that, even if no one had sinned in paradise, children could not have been procreated without it in the body of that life, just as now they are not procreated without it in the body of this death, from which the Apostle longs to be freed through Jesus Christ.

**6.** Whence it comes about that so great an absurdity follows this opinion of theirs, coming from inconsiderate inexperience, that not even they themselves—however much human brow be hardened in them with shamelessness—could at all bear it. For if before sin there was in paradise this concupiscence of the flesh, which we perceive to have so disordered a movement that chastity must either restrain it with reins from all use, or reduce it to good use, although it is in itself evil, by the goodness of marriage; surely, in a place of such great happiness, either it would be shamefully served—if, as often as it were moved, so often there were intercourse with the spouse, by no necessity of begetting, but that the appetite of lust might fill itself, even if the wife came already pregnant—or it would be fought against, with the powers of abstinence, lest it drag one to those foul things. Of these two, then, let them choose which pleases; for if the concupiscence of the flesh were served, lest it be resisted, there was no honest liberty there; but if it were resisted, lest it be served, there was no peaceful happiness there; either of these the blessed honor of paradise, or the honorable happiness, turns away from.

**7.** Who does not see this? Who, except by most shameless obstinacy, would contradict this most manifest truth? It remains, then, either that this concupiscence of the flesh—which we perceive to be moved with a turbid and disordered appetite against our will, even when there is no need—was not there, although there was there the concupiscence of marriage, preserving the tranquil love of the spouses and, just as the will's nod commands the hands and feet to fitting works, so commanding the genital members for begetting, so that offspring in paradise would be sown in a wondrous way without the pains of the concupiscence of the flesh, just as also in a wondrous way it would be born without the pains of childbearing; or, if there was there that concupiscence of the flesh, it was surely not such as those now perceive it, most troublesome and most hateful, who fight against it whether by conjugal or widowed or virginal chastity. For it thrusts itself in where it is not necessary, and with importunate or even wicked desires solicits even the hearts of the faithful and the holy; with whose restless movements, even if we acquiesce by no nod of consent, but rather resist, yet by a holier desire we would altogether, if it could be done, be unwilling that they be in us, just as someday they will not be. For that is the perfection of good which the Apostle signified to be still lacking to the saints in this life, saying: *To will lies near to me, but to accomplish the good, no*[^5]; for he does not say "to do," but *to accomplish*, since a man does good by not consenting to such desires, but does not accomplish it by having them. *For I do not do the good that I will,* he says, *but the evil that I do not will, this I do*[^6]. He surely was not doing evil by presenting his members to fulfilling evil desires, but he was saying this of those very concupiscential movements, to which, even if he did not consent nor perpetrate what they provoked, yet by having them he was doing what he did not will to have. Finally he adds: *But if I do that which I do not will,* that is, although I do not consent to concupiscence (yet I do not will to lust, and I lust), *it is now not I who work it, but the sin that dwells in me*[^7]. The guilt of this sin is drawn by generation, drawn back by regeneration, when there is made the remission of all sins. Yet some force of it, and a certain pestilent affection of contagion, remains in the corruptible and mortal body even with the guilt loosed, against which let the regenerate, if he makes progress, fight. For even if he holds not entire continence but conjugal chastity, he too will fight against this concupiscence of the flesh, lest he commit adultery, lest he fornicate, lest he be defiled by any deadly and wicked shamefulness, finally lest he use even his own wife intemperately, with whom too, even by consent, he ought for a time to abstain from the work of joining the flesh, that they may have leisure for prayers and again return to the same, *lest Satan tempt them on account of their incontinence*, which the Apostle says to them *according to indulgence, not according to command*[^8]. Some, considering this too little, thought that marriage itself was conceded according to indulgence; but it is not so; otherwise—which far be it—marriage would be a sin. For where indulgence is given, surely it is acknowledged that a fault is pardoned; but the intercourse to which the care of propagating offspring does not lead, but the incontinence of satiating lust drags, the Apostle concedes to spouses between themselves according to indulgence, lest damnable sins be committed, while indulgence does not relax. Nevertheless, even if some married people so excel in the virtue of conjugal chastity that for the sole cause of begetting each sex is joined, and, baptized and reborn, so live, whatever offspring is born of them through that concupiscence of the flesh—which, not being good, they themselves use well through the good concupiscence of marriage—draws original sin. For that which is in no way removed except by regeneration alone follows without doubt the one begotten, unless he too be regenerated; just as the foreskin, which is removed only by circumcision, follows the son of the circumcised, unless he too be circumcised[^9].

**8.** This concupiscence of the flesh, then, if it was in paradise, that through it children might be begotten to fulfill the blessing of marriage by the multiplication of men, was surely not such as it now is, whose movement gapes indifferently both for the lawful and the unlawful; it would be carried off into many most foul things, if, wherever it were moved, it were allowed to arrive, against which one would have to fight, that chastity be preserved; but it would be such (if indeed any were there) by which the flesh would never lust against the spirit[^10], but would not, by a wonderful peace, exceed the nod of the will, so that it would never be present except when there was need, would never thrust itself, by a disordered or unlawful delight, upon the mind of one thinking, would have nothing to be disapproved that should be bridled by the reins of temperance or fought off by the labor of virtue, but would follow the will of the one using it, when it was necessary, by an easy and concordant obedience. Now, then, since it is not such, and chastity must struggle against its adversity, let them confess that it has been vitiated by sin, so that of its movement those should be confounded who before were naked and were not confounded[^11]; nor let them wonder that the Son of a virgin alone, whom they cannot say to have been conceived through it, did not draw original sin. Grant pardon that I have been burdensome to your holy senses by the length of the letter, not to make you more learned, but to refute their slanders before you.
