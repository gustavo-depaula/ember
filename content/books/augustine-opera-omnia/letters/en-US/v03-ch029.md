# Letter 182

Innocent, to Silvanus the elder, Valentinus, and the rest who took part in the synod of Mileve, his most beloved brothers, greetings in the Lord.

**1.** Among the other cares of the Roman Church, and the occupations of the apostolic See, by which we treat the consultations of various people with faithful and modest discussion, our brother and fellow bishop Julius unexpectedly brought to me the letters of your charity, which you sent from the council of Mileve with a more attentive care for the faith, subjoining also the writings of a like complaint of the synod of Carthage. Surely the Church exults that the shepherds show so great solicitude to the flocks committed to them, that they not only permit none of these to err, but, if the grass of perverse delight has rather seduced any of the sheep, and if they remain in error, either wish them to be utterly segregated, or, avoiding the things that were once illicitly sought, to be guarded by the circumspection of the former custody, consulting, namely, in both directions: lest either, by receiving such people, the rest be seduced by a like example; or, by spurning those returning, they seem to be thrown to the bites of wolves. A most prudent consultation, and full of catholic faith. For who could either tolerate one erring, or not receive one correcting himself? For as I judge it hard to show connivance to those sinning, so I judge it impious to deny the hand to those converted.

**2.** Diligently and fittingly, then, you consult the secrets of the apostolic honor (the honor, I say, of him whom, besides the things which are without, there awaits the solicitude of all the Churches) about anxious matters, what opinion is to be held, following, namely, the form of the ancient rule, which you know to have been always kept with me throughout the whole world. But these things I let pass; for I do not believe this is hidden from your prudence: for why did you confirm it by your action, except knowing that through all the provinces answers always flow from the apostolic fount to those asking? Especially as often as a matter of faith is sifted, I think all our brothers and fellow bishops ought to refer it only to Peter, that is, to the author of their name and honor, just as now your charity has referred it—what can profit all the Churches throughout the whole world in common. For they must necessarily be made more cautious, when the inventors of evils, at the report of the double synod, shall see themselves separated from ecclesiastical communion by the statutes of our sentence.

**3.** Your charity, then, will enjoy a twofold good. For you will both gain the grace of the canons kept, and the whole world will use your benefit. For what catholic man would wish to mingle speech further with the adversaries of Christ? who even to share the very light of life by communion? Surely let the authors of the new heresy be fled. For what more bitter thing could they fashion against God, than when they made void the divine helps, and took away the cause of daily prayer? This is to say, What need have I of God? Deservedly may the hymn-singer say against these: *Behold the men who did not set God as their helper*[^1]. Denying, then, the help of God, they say that a man can suffice himself, and does not need divine grace, deprived of which he must necessarily, ensnared in the snares of the devil, fall, while for fulfilling all the commandments of life he strives by liberty alone. O perverse doctrine of most depraved minds! Let him at last attend to this: that liberty itself so deceived the first man, that, while he used its reins too indulgently, he fell into transgression by presumption; nor could he be rescued from this, unless the providence of regeneration, by the coming of Christ the Lord, had reformed the state of the former liberty. Let him hear David saying, *Our help is in the name of the Lord*[^2]; and: *Be my helper, do not forsake me, nor despise me, O God of my salvation*[^3]: which he would have said in vain, if that which he asked of the Lord with tearful discourse was placed only in his own will.

**4.** Since these things are so, and since in all the divine pages we read that nothing but the help of God is to be knit to the free will, and that it, destitute of heavenly aids, can do nothing; in what way do Pelagius and Celestius, pertinaciously, as you assert, defending this possibility for it alone, persuade themselves—nay, what is more worthy of common grief, already very many? Indeed we could use manifold examples to destroy such teaching, were we not aware that your holiness is fully versed in all the divine Scriptures; especially since your report is filled with so many and such testimonies, that by these alone the present dogma can be rescinded; and there is no need of recondite ones, since they dare neither to oppose, nor are able, even those things which, easily occurring to you, you have set down. Therefore they try to take away the grace of God, which it is necessary that, even with the liberty of our former state restored to us, we seek: since we can avoid the other machinations of the devil too only by that same grace helping.

**5.** But that which your fraternity asserts they preach—that little ones can be granted the rewards of eternal life even without the grace of baptism—is exceedingly foolish. For unless *they eat the flesh of the Son of man, and drink his blood, they will not have life in themselves*[^4]. But those who defend that they have this without regeneration seem to me to wish to make void baptism itself, when they preach that these have what is believed to be conferred on them only by baptism. If, then, they wish that not to be reborn is no hindrance, they must necessarily confess that not even the sacred streams of regeneration profit. But that the depraved doctrine of these superfluous men may be cut apart by the swift reasoning of truth, the Lord proclaims this in the Gospel, saying: *Let the children come to me, and do not forbid them to come to me*[^5].

**6.** Wherefore we decree, by the authority of apostolic vigor, that Pelagius and Celestius, that is, the inventors of new words, which, as the Apostle said, are wont to produce nothing of edification, but rather most vain questions, be deprived of ecclesiastical communion, until they come to their senses from the snares of the devil, by whom they are held captive according to his will; and that meanwhile they not be received into the Lord's fold, which they themselves, following the path of the perverse way, wished to desert: for *they are to be cut off who disturb you, and wish to turn the Gospel of Christ*[^6]. But at the same time we enjoin that whoever try, with like pertinacity, to defend this, an equal punishment constrain them. For not only those who do, but also those who consent to those doing; because I judge there is not much difference between the mind of the one committing and the favor of the one consenting. And I add still more: very often he unlearns to err, to whom no one consents. Let this sentence, then, dearest brothers, remain fixed against the aforesaid: let them be far from the courts of the Lord, let them lack pastoral custody, lest the dire contagions of two sheep creep perhaps through the incautious crowd, and the wolf with rapacious heart rejoice within the Lord's fold over so great companies of poured-out sheep, while the wound of two is dissemblingly disregarded by the guardians. We must take care, then, lest, by permitting wolves, we seem to be hirelings rather than shepherds.

**7.** We surely command, since Christ the Lord signified by his own voice that he does not wish the death of the dying, but only that he turn back and live, that if ever they shall come to their senses to soundness, laying aside the error of the depraved dogma, and condemn those things by whose transgression they themselves condemned themselves, the accustomed medicine, that is, their reception, be not denied them by the Church: lest, while perhaps we forbid them returning, truly remaining outside the fold and awaiting, they be gulped down by the rabid jaws of the enemy, which they have armed against themselves with the darts of evil disputation. Fare well, brothers. Given on the sixth day before the kalends of February, in the consulship of the most illustrious men Honorius and Constantius.

[^2]: Ps 124:8 (123:8)
[^3]: Ps 27:9 (26:9)
