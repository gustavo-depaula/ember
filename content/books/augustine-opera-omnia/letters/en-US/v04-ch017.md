# Letter 225

TO AUGUSTINE, A MOST BLESSED LORD POPE, INEFFABLY WONDERFUL, INCOMPARABLY TO BE HONORED, A MOST EXCELLENT PATRON, PROSPER.

**1.** *Unknown indeed to you by face, but already to some extent, if you recall, known to you in mind and speech—for through my holy brother the deacon Leontius I sent letters and received them—now too I dare to write to Your Beatitude, not only out of zeal for greeting, as then, but also out of affection for the faith by which the Church lives. For since your most vigilant industry keeps watch over all the members of the body of Christ*[^1]*, and fights against the snares of heretical doctrines by the power of truth, I thought I need in no way fear to be burdensome or importunate to you in that which pertains to the salvation of many, and accordingly to your piety: since I would rather believe myself to be guilty in the future, if I did not refer to the special patron of the faith those things which I understand to be very pernicious.*

**2.** *Many servants of Christ, then, who reside in the city of Marseilles, think that, in the writings of Your Holiness which you composed against the Pelagian heretics, whatever you argued in them about the calling of the elect according to the purpose of God is contrary to the opinion of the fathers and to the ecclesiastical understanding. And when for some time they had preferred to blame their own slowness rather than to reprehend things they did not understand, and certain of them had wished to ask for a clearer and more open exposition of Your Beatitude on this matter, it came about, by the dispensation of God's mercy, that, when certain people within Africa had stirred up similar things, you published the book* On Rebuke and Grace*, full of divine authority. When this had been brought to our notice by an unhoped-for opportunity, we thought all the complaints of those resisting would be put to rest; because to all the questions about which Your Holiness was to be consulted, it was there so fully and completely answered, as if you had specially striven to compose what was disturbed among us. But when this book of Your Beatitude had been reviewed, just as those who previously followed the holy and apostolic authority of your teaching were made much more intelligent and instructed, so those who were hindered by the obscurity of their own conviction departed more averse than they had been. Their so abrupt dissent is first to be feared on their own account, lest the spirit of Pelagian impiety should mock men so renowned and so distinguished in the pursuit of all virtues: then, lest the simpler folk—among whom there is great reverence for these men from the contemplation of their probity—should think this safest for themselves, that they hear those whose authority they follow without judgment to assert it.*

**3.** *For this is their definition and profession: that every man indeed sinned when Adam sinned; and that no one is saved by his own works, but by the grace of God through regeneration: that nevertheless the propitiation which is in the Sacrament of the blood of Christ is set forth without exception for all men, so that whoever wish to come to faith and to baptism can be saved. But that those who will believe, and those who will remain in that faith which thereafter is to be helped by grace, God foreknew before the foundation of the world, and predestined to his kingdom*[^2] *those whom, called freely, he foresaw would be worthy of election and would depart this life with a good end. And therefore every man is admonished to believe and to act by the divine institutions, so that no one may despair of laying hold of eternal life, since a reward is prepared for voluntary devotion. But this purpose of God's calling, by which the distinction of those to be chosen and those to be rejected is said to have been made either before the beginning of the world or in the very founding of the human race, so that, according as it pleased the Creator, some were created vessels of honor, others vessels of dishonor*[^3]*, both removes from the fallen the care of rising again and brings to the saints an occasion of lukewarmness; for the reason that on both sides the labor is superfluous, if neither the rejected can by any industry enter, nor the elect by any negligence fall away: for in whatever way they conduct themselves, nothing can happen to them other than what God has determined; and under an uncertain hope the course cannot be steady, since, if the election of the one predestining holds otherwise, the intention of the one striving is empty. Thus all industry is removed, and virtues are taken away, if God's decree anticipates human wills: and under this name of predestination a kind of fatal necessity is introduced; or else the Lord is called the founder of diverse natures, if no one can be anything other than he was made. And, to set forth more briefly and fully what they think: whatever in this book Your Holiness set against yourself from the understanding of opponents, whatever too in the books against Julian was objected by him under this question, you most powerfully overthrew; all this is most contentiously cried out by these holy men. And when we bring forth the writings of Your Beatitude against them, furnished with most powerful and innumerable testimonies of the divine Scriptures, and according to the form of your disputations also construct something by which they may be concluded, they defend their obstinacy by antiquity: and they affirm that those things which are brought forth from the Letter of the apostle Paul writing to the Romans*[^4]*, to manifest the divine grace anticipating the merits of the elect, were never so understood by any of the ecclesiastics as they are now perceived. And when we demand that they themselves expound these passages according to whatever sense they wish, they profess to have found nothing that pleases, and require that silence be kept about those things whose depth no one has reached. At last the whole obstinacy comes down to this: that they define our faith to be contrary to the edification of hearers, and so, even if it is true, it is not to be brought forth: because both things perniciously not to be received are handed down; and with no danger are those things kept silent which cannot be understood.*

**4.** *But certain of these so far do not turn aside from the Pelagian paths that, when they are compelled to confess that grace of Christ which anticipates all human merits—lest, if it is repaid to merits, grace be named in vain—they want this to pertain to the condition of each man, in which the grace of the Creator establishes him, meriting nothing prior (since he does not even yet exist), as endowed with free choice and rational, so that through the discernment of good and evil*[^5] *he may be able both to direct his will to the knowledge of God and to the obedience of his commandments, and to arrive at this grace by which we are reborn in Christ—namely, by the natural faculty, asking, seeking, knocking*[^6]: *so that for this reason he receives, for this reason he finds, for this reason he enters, because, having used the good of nature well, he has merited, by the help of an initial grace, to arrive at that saving grace. But the purpose of the calling grace they define altogether thus: that God has determined to take no one into his kingdom except through the Sacrament of regeneration, and that to this gift of salvation all men universally are called, whether through the natural, or the written law, or evangelical preaching: so that both those who are willing may become sons of God, and those who have been unwilling to be faithful may be inexcusable; because the justice of God is in this, that those who have not believed perish*[^7]*; his goodness appears in this, if he repels no one from life, but indifferently wills all to* be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth[^8]. *Here now they bring forth testimonies by which the exhortation of the divine Scriptures incites the wills of men to obey, who out of free choice either do what is commanded or neglect it: and they think it follows that, because the transgressor is said not to have obeyed for the reason that he was unwilling, so too the faithful man is not to be doubted to have been devout for the reason that he was willing; and that each has as much faculty for good as for evil; and that with equal weight the mind moves itself either to vices or to virtues—the mind which, desiring good things, the grace of God fosters, and, pursuing evil things, a just damnation receives.*

**5.** *And when, among these things, the multitude of innumerable little children is set against them, who surely—apart from original sin, under which all men are alike born in the damnation of the first man—having as yet no wills, no proper actions, are not separated without the judgment of God; so that, to be taken from the use of this life before the discernment of good and evil, some, through regeneration*[^9]*, are received among the heirs of the heavenly kingdom, others, without baptism, pass over among the debtors of perpetual death: they say that such are lost, and such are saved, as the divine knowledge has foreseen they would be in their later years, if they were preserved to the active age. Nor do they consider that they subject the grace of God—which they want to be the companion, not the forerunner, of human merits—even to those wills which, according to their fancy, they do not deny to have been anticipated by it. But to such an extent do they subject God's election to any sort of fabricated merits, that, because past ones do not exist, they invent future ones that will not be, and, by a new kind of absurdity among them, both things not to be done are foreknown, and things foreknown are not done. This foreknowledge of God concerning human merits, according to which the grace of the one calling operates, they seem to themselves to construct much more reasonably, when one comes to the contemplation of those nations which* either in past ages were allowed to walk in their own ways[^10]*, or even now still perish in the impiety of their old ignorance, and on whom no illumination of either the Law or the Gospel has flashed; while nevertheless, insofar as a door has been opened to the preachers and a way made, the people of the nations, who* sat in darkness and in the shadow of death[^11]*, have seen a great light; and those who once were not a people are now the people of God; and those on whom he once had no mercy he now has mercy*[^12]: *they say that these were foreseen by the Lord as about to believe, and that the times and ministries of teachers were so dispensed to each nation as the belief of good wills was about to arise: and that it does not waver, that* God wills all men to be saved, and to come to the knowledge of the truth[^13]; *since those are inexcusable who could have been instructed by natural intelligence to the worship of the one true God, and did not hear the Gospel for this reason, that they were not going to receive it.*

**6.** *But that our Lord Jesus Christ died for the whole human race, and that absolutely no one is excepted from the redemption of his blood, even if he passes through all this life with a mind most alien to him; because the Sacrament of divine mercy pertains to all men: and that very many are for this reason not renewed, because they are foreknown as those who will not even be willing to be renewed. And so, as far as pertains to God, eternal life is prepared for all; but as far as pertains to the freedom of choice, it is laid hold of by those who have voluntarily believed in God, and have received the help of grace by the merit of their belief. But into this preaching of such a grace those whose contradiction offends us, although they previously thought better things, have for this reason most of all betaken themselves, because, if they professed that all good merits are anticipated by it, and that by it they are granted the power even to exist, they would by necessity grant that God, according to the purpose and counsel of his will, by a hidden judgment and a manifest work,* fashions one vessel unto honor, another unto dishonor[^14]; *because no one is justified except by grace, and no one is born except in transgression*[^15]*. But they shrink from confessing this, and dread to ascribe the merits of the saints to the divine work; nor do they acquiesce that the predestined number of the elect can neither be increased nor diminished, lest the incitements of those exhorting should have no place among the unbelieving and negligent, and the injunction of industry and labor should be superfluous, whose zeal, with election ceasing, would have to be frustrated: for thus at last, they say, can each one be called to correction or to advancement, if he knows that by his own diligence he can be good, and that his liberty is for this reason to be helped by God's aid, if he chooses what God commands. And so, since in those who have received the time of free will there are two things that work human salvation, namely the grace of God and the obedience of man, they want obedience to be prior to grace, so that the beginning of salvation is to be believed to stand from him who is saved, not from him who saves, and the will of man begets for itself the help of divine grace, rather than grace subjecting to itself the human will.*

**7.** *Since we have come to know, by the revelation of God's mercy and the instruction of Your Beatitude, that this is most perverse, we can indeed be steadfast in not believing it, but we are not equal to the authority of those who think such things: because they far excel us both in the merits of their life, and some of them, having lately attained the honor of the highest priesthood, are eminent above us; nor has anyone easily, except a few intrepid lovers of perfect grace, dared to go against the disputations of such great superiors. From which not only to those who hear them, but even to those very ones who are heard, the danger has grown along with their dignities; while reverence for them either restrains many in useless silence or leads them to incautious assent; and what is reprehended by the contradiction of almost no one seems to them most wholesome. Hence, because in these remnants of Pelagian depravity a fiber of no slight virulence is nourished, if the beginning of salvation is wrongly placed in man; if the human will is impiously preferred to the divine will, so that one is helped for the reason that he was willing, not is willing for the reason that he is helped; if one originally evil is wrongly believed to begin the reception of good not from the highest good but from himself; if God is pleased from any other source than from that which he himself has given: grant us in this cause, most blessed pope, best father, as much as you can with the Lord's help, the diligence of your piety, so that the things which in these questions are more obscure and more difficult to grasp, you may deign to open with the clearest expositions.*

**8.** *And first, because very many do not think the Christian faith is violated by this dissension, make plain how much danger there is in their conviction. Then, how through this grace that works beforehand and works together, free choice is not hindered. Then, whether the foreknowledge of God so remains according to the purpose that the very things which are purposed are to be taken as foreknown: or whether these things are varied through the kinds of causes and the species of persons; so that, because the callings are diverse in those who are saved though they will do nothing, the purpose of God alone seems to exist; but in those who will do some good, the purpose can stand through foreknowledge: or rather, uniformly—although foreknowledge cannot be divided from the purpose by a temporal distinction—foreknowledge is nevertheless in a certain order supported on the purpose; and just as there is nothing of any affairs whatever which the divine knowledge has not anticipated, so there is nothing of good which has not flowed into our participation with God as its author. Finally, how, through this preaching of God's purpose—by which those become faithful who are preordained to eternal life—none of those who are to be exhorted is hindered, nor do they have an occasion of negligence, if they despair of being predestined. We ask too that you demonstrate, by patiently bearing our foolishness, how that is to be dissolved which, when the opinions of earlier writers on this matter are reviewed, is found to be the like and one sentence of almost all, by which they received the purpose and predestination of God according to foreknowledge; so that for this reason God made some vessels of honor, others of dishonor*[^16]*, because he foresaw the end of each one, and foreknew under that very help of grace in what will and action he would be.*

**9.** *All these things being unraveled, and many besides—pertaining to this cause, which with a deeper insight you can better see—being discussed, we believe and hope that not only our slightness will be strengthened by the support of your disputations, but that even those whom the mist of this opinion, men renowned for merits and honors, obscures, will receive the most purified light of grace. For let Your Beatitude know that one of them, a man of especial authority and of spiritual pursuits, the holy Hilary, bishop of Arles, is an admirer and follower in all other things of your teaching; and that concerning this which he draws into complaint, he has for some time wished to convey his own view to Your Holiness by letter. But because it is uncertain whether he will do this, or with what end he will do it, and the weariness of us all, by the grace of God providing this for the present age, breathes again in the vigor of your love and knowledge; add learning to the humble, add rebuke to the proud. It is necessary and useful to write even the things that have been written, lest that be thought light which is not frequently rebuked. For they think that to be sound which does not hurt, nor do they feel a wound covered over by skin: but let them understand that it will come to the lancing, which has had a persistent swelling. May the grace of God and the peace of our Lord Jesus Christ crown you at all times, and glorify you forever as you walk from virtue to virtue*[^17]*, most blessed lord pope, ineffably wonderful, incomparably to be honored, most excellent patron.*
