# Letter 121

**1. 1.** Of the few things which have now come into my mind, the bearer of the letter running to the ship, and I myself hurrying in his haste, I shall set forth some, so that you may not write back to me without a little garland to crown it: which, if perhaps they are clear, and seem obscure to me, let no one of your prudent sons, who perhaps of our people may be standing about you in the hour of this little reading, laugh at my unwisdom, but rather let him favor it with the benevolence of brotherly love by instructing me, that I may be made a partaker of those who see, and, from your teaching, with minds illuminated, of those who consider the marvels of the law of the Lord.

**1. 2.** Tell me, then, blessed teacher of Israel, what it is that is said in the fifteenth psalm: *To the saints who are in his land he made all his wills marvelous among them. For their infirmities were multiplied; afterward they made haste*[^1]. Whom does he call saints, the saints who are in the land? Surely not those Jews who, being sons of Abraham's flesh and not sons of the promise, are excluded from the seed which was called in Isaac, saints in the land for this reason, that they are holy by carnal stock, but in life and sense earthly, who relish earthly things, and grow old in the oldness of the letter by carnal observance; not reborn into a new creature, because they did not receive him through whom the old things passed away and all things were made new? For perhaps he so calls them saints in this psalm in the way that in the Gospel he calls them just, where he says, *I came not to call the just, but sinners*[^2]; that is, those just men who glory in the holiness of stock and in the letter of the Law: to whom it is said, *Do not glory in your father Abraham; for God is able to raise up from these stones sons to Abraham*[^3]. Whose figure is set forth in that Pharisee who, recounting his own justices to the Lord as though he did not know them, proclaimed them in the temple; not praying that he might be heard, but exacting them as a debt of merit for his deeds, good indeed, but ungrateful to God, because what justice had built, pride was destroying: and that not in silence, but with a voice he cried out, so that it might appear that he spoke not to the divine ears, because he wished also to be heard by men; and therefore he did not please God, because he pleased himself: *For the Lord has scattered the bones of those who please themselves. They have been put to confusion,* he says, *because he has spurned them*[^4]—he who does not spurn a humble and contrite heart.

**1. 3.** Finally, in the very Gospel parable in which the person of the Pharisee and of the Publican is compared, the Lord himself evidently shows what he receives in a man, what he rejects, as it is written that *God resists the proud, but gives grace to the humble*[^5]. Therefore he attests that the Publican went away from the temple more justified for the confession of his sins than the Pharisee for the imputation of his justices. But deservedly that praiser of himself, repudiated, departed from the face of God—who, though by his very name he displayed expertness in the Law, had forgotten the Lord saying in the prophet, *Upon whom shall I dwell, but upon the humble, and quiet, and trembling at my words?*[^6] But the other, his own accuser in a contrite heart, is received, and obtains pardon for his confessed sins on account of the grace of humility, that holy Pharisee (such as the Jewish saints are) carrying back the burden of his sins from the boasting of his holiness. Of him surely are the figure those Jews of whom the Apostle says that, *desiring to establish their own justice, which is of the Law, they have not been subject to the justice of God*[^7], which is of faith, which was reputed to our father Abraham for justice, not from works, because according to the omnipotence of God he believed God: with whom he is truly just who lives from faith; nor is he a saint in the land, but in heaven, because he walks not in the flesh but in the spirit; whose conversation is in the heavens; not glorying in the circumcision of the flesh, but in the circumcision of the heart, which is done invisibly not by the letter but by the spirit; whence his praise is not from men, but from God.

**1. 4.** Then, what he joins in the same verse, *He made his wills marvelous among them*, I believe he says from this, that he first kindled for them the lamp of the Law and gave them the precepts of living. For *he made known,* he says, *his ways to Moses, and his wills to the sons of Israel*[^8]. Then he worked in them the very sacrament of his piety, God being born in the flesh from a virgin among their nation, and from their flesh made man from the seed of David: then the virtues of healings, which he accomplished in them and before them. Who, through these, was not only not believed, but even blasphemed by them, when they said, *This man, if he were from God, would not heal on the sabbath*[^9]; and, *He does not cast out demons except by Beelzebub, the prince of demons*[^10]. On account of this mind, blinded by hardened impiety, their infirmities and darknesses were multiplied.

**1. 5.** But what is it that he says, *Afterward they made haste*? Whether to repentance, like those in the Acts of the Apostles who, pricked by the preaching of blessed Peter, believed in him whom they had crucified, and, hastening to be cleansed of so great a sin, ran to the gift of grace? Or rather, because the virtues of the soul are strengthened by faith and the love of God, for those impious men, void of both, the infirmities of the soul were multiplied, occupied by the deadly languors of the impiety of crimes? For Christ is the light and life of believers, and there is healing under his wings: whence it is no wonder if both the darknesses and the infirmities of those were multiplied to destruction, who did not receive the life and the light, nor wished to remain under his wings; whom, as he himself weeping attests in his Gospel, he often wished to gather under his wings, as a hen gathers her chicks, and they would not. Their infirmities being multiplied, then, whither did they make haste? Perhaps to clamor for the cross of the Lord, and, Pilate being unwilling, to extort it by their wicked voices, so that they might fill up the measure of their fathers, so that these might kill the Lord of the Prophets, whose fathers killed the Prophets themselves, by whom it was announced that this Savior of the world would come. *Afterward they made haste: for their feet are swift to shed blood. Destruction and unhappiness are in their ways, and the way of peace they have not known*[^11]—that is, Christ, who says, *I am the way*[^12].

**1. 6.** In the following psalm I desire this to be expounded to me, what it says: *From your hidden things their belly was filled. They were sated with swine's flesh*, or, as I hear it written in some psalters, *they were sated with sons, and left what was over to their little ones*[^13].

**1. 7.** Again in another psalm I am wont to marvel, understanding the Son to speak to the Father, in the fifty-eighth psalm, where, about the enemy Jews, of whom he had said above, *Behold, they themselves will speak with their mouth, and a sword is in their lips*[^14], he says a little below, *Do not kill them, lest at any time they forget your Law. Scatter them in your power, and bring them down, O Lord*[^15]. Which we see fulfilled in them even to this day: for they are brought down from their ancient glory, living without temple and without sacrifices, and without Prophets, in the dispersion of all nations. But why do we marvel that he was already asking through the prophet that they not be killed, on whose behalf, even at the very time of the passion, while they were leading him to the cross, he prayed, saying, *Father, forgive them; for they do not know what they do*[^16]? But as for what he added, *lest at any time they forget your Law*—as though for this reason their life were necessary even without the faith of the Gospel—I confess this is obscure to me. For what does it profit them toward salvation, which is sought by faith alone, to be occupied in the memory and meditation of the Law; unless perhaps for the honor of the Law itself, or of the stock of Abraham, so that even in the earthly part of his carnal seed, which seems to be reckoned according to the sand of the sea, the letter of the ancient Law may persevere, lest perhaps some, by reading the Law, be enlightened to the faith of Christ, who is the end both of the Law and of the Prophets, and shines forth prefigured and prophesied in all their books? Or because from those impious men themselves a generation of the elect is to come, who, chosen from the single tribes, are designated by twelves of thousands; to whom the very revelation of blessed John, by the voice of the Angel foretelling it, bears this testimony, that they will cleave more familiarly to the train of the eternal king, being utterly immaculate and free of human conjunction? Of whom he says specially, *They follow the Lamb wherever he goes, because they did not defile themselves with women; for they are virgins*[^17].

**1. 8.** In the sixty-seventh, besides other things, this is most obscure to me, what it says: *But God has shattered the heads of his enemies, the hairy crown of those walking in their offenses*[^18]; what it is to walk in offenses with a hairy crown. For he did not say "the crown of the head," but "the crown of the hair," which is without sense. Or does he wish to show a man filled with sins? It is written, *Every heart in grief, from the feet to the head*[^19]. And a little below, what he says, *That the tongue of your dogs may be from the enemies, from him*[^20]. From which "him"? And can the Gentiles be called the dogs of God, whom he himself in the Gospel names dogs? Or does he perhaps call those the dogs of God who can be so esteemed, if any live in a gentile manner under the Christian name; whose part is set with the unbelievers, because the God whom they worship with words they deny by deeds?

**2. 9.** These things meanwhile about the Psalms; now I shall set forth something also about the Apostle. He says to the Ephesians what he had said in another Letter about the grades or orders of the dispositions of God, the Holy Spirit working the divisions of graces: *And some indeed he gave as apostles, but some as prophets, others as evangelists, but others as pastors and teachers, for the perfecting of the saints*[^21], and the rest. This I wish you to distinguish for me in this diversity of names: what is the property of offices or graces proper to each name? What is proper to Apostles, what to Prophets, what to Evangelists, what to Pastors, or what to Teachers? For in all these different names I see that a similar and almost one office of teaching was carried on. But these Prophets, whom he set after the Apostles, I do not think are those who in order of times were before the Apostles, but those to whom now, under the Apostles, was given through grace either the interpretation of Scriptures and the inspection of minds, or the prediction of time to come; as Agabus discerned, who both foretold the imminent famine and what the blessed Paul would suffer in Jerusalem, and both announced it by word and showed it by the sign of his girdle. Between Pastors specially and Teachers I wish to discern what difference there is, because both names are wont to be ascribed to the rulers of the Church.

**2. 10.** Likewise, what he says to Timothy: *I beseech, then, first of all, that supplications, prayers, petitions, thanksgivings be made for all men*[^22], I ask you to expound to me, what distinction there is in this diversity of words, since all the things he said are to be done seem to me to fit the office of prayer.

**2. 11.** Likewise, what he says to the Romans I ask and beg that you set forth to me; for I confess that I am much in the dark in this opinion of the Apostle about the Jews, where he says: *According to the Gospel indeed they are enemies for your sake; but according to election they are most dear for the sake of the fathers*[^23]: how are the same both enemies for our sake, who have believed from the Gentiles—as though the Gentiles could not have believed unless the Jews had not believed; or as though the one creator God of all, who wishes all men to be saved and to come to the knowledge of the truth, were not capable of the acquisition of both, unless he possessed the one in place of the other—then most dear for the sake of the fathers? How, or whence, most dear, if they do not believe, and persist in being enemies to God? *Did I not hate those who hated you, O God, and pine away over your enemies? With a perfect hatred I hated them*[^24]. Certainly, I think, this is the Father's voice speaking to the Son through the prophet in the same psalm, where above, about the part of believers, he had said, *But to me your friends are exceedingly honored, O God; their principality is exceedingly strengthened*[^25]. But what does it profit them toward salvation—which is grasped only through faith and the grace of Christ—if they are most dear to God for the sake of the fathers? For whose good are those loved whom it is necessary to condemn for this, that, dissenting through their unbelief from the faith of their parents the Prophets and patriarchs, they are enemies to the Gospel of Christ? If, then, most dear to God, how will they perish? And if they do not believe, how will they not perish? If for the sake of the fathers they are loved without their own merit, how will they not also for the sake of the fathers be saved; but even if Noah, Daniel, and Job were in the midst of them, they will not save their impious sons, but they alone will be saved?

**2. 12.** Still draw out for me another, more obscure thing from the deep, and bring it to the shallows. In the Letter to the Colossians I cannot understand at all what he says: *Let no one seduce you, willing in humility and the religion of angels, walking in things he has not seen, vainly puffed up by the sense of his flesh, and not holding the head*[^26]; of what angels does he speak? If of the enemies and the bad, what is their religion, or what is their humility, and who is the master of this seduction, who, under the pretext of I know not what angelic religion, as though things seen and ascertained, teaches things he has not seen? Without doubt the heretics, who both follow and bring forth the doctrines of demons, with inventions conceived from their spirit—phantasms which they have not seen, feigning them as though seen, and sowing them by pestilent disputations into hearts ill-disposed to believe—these are they who do not hold the head, that is, Christ the fount of truth, whose doctrine whatever opposes is insane. And these are blind leaders of the blind, of whom I think it is said: *They have forsaken me, the fount of living water, and have dug for themselves broken cisterns, which hold no water*[^27].

**2. 13.** Then in the following chapter he added: *Do not touch, nor taste, nor handle, which are all unto destruction by the very use, according to the precepts and doctrines of men, having indeed a show of wisdom in superstition and humility, and in not sparing the body, not in any honor unto the satisfying of the flesh*[^28]. What are these things, in which the master of truth attests that there is even a show of wisdom, and yet denies that the very truth of religion is in them? Or does he perhaps speak of such men as those of whom he says to Timothy, *having indeed an appearance of piety, but denying its power*[^29]? I ask, then, specially that you resolve for me these two chapters of the Letter to the Colossians word by word, because he has mingled execrable things with praiseworthy ones. For what is so praiseworthy as a show of wisdom, and what so execrable as the superstition of error? Humility too, both pleasing to God and especially praiseworthy in true religion, is given with a show of wisdom to those of whose doctrines and acts it is said to us, *Do not touch, nor taste, which are unto destruction*[^30], because they are not of God: and *everything that is not of faith is sin*[^31]. But God has scattered the counsels of the wise, who are foolish to God through the prudence of the flesh, which cannot be subject to the law of God: for he knows the thoughts of men, that they are vain. What sort of humility, and what sort of show of wisdom, he says is in a superstition coming from the doctrines of men, I inquire. And as for what he says, *in not sparing the body, not in any honor unto the satisfying of the flesh*—these things I understand too little, because in the same sentence there seems to me to be a great distinction: for I think he says this about any feigned or useless abstinence, such as is wont to be affected by heretics, "in not sparing the body"; but what he added, "not in any honor," because, exercising the appearance of a holy work, not in the faith of truth, they act with the honor or fruit of no glory, which they accomplish in great reproach of perverse error, transfiguring themselves into ministers of justice. But what he added, "unto the satisfying of the flesh," seems to me contrary to that which he says, "in not sparing the body": for he seems to me not to spare the body who tames the flesh by fastings, as the Apostle says, *I make my body livid, and reduce it to servitude*[^32]; from which work the satisfying of the flesh is foreign: unless perhaps he called even the very care of satisfying the flesh, which is most shameful for those who profess the observance of religion, "not to spare the body," according to that precept of honorableness which elsewhere he gives, that each one know how to possess his own vessel honorably, that he present his body as a living victim pleasing to God, not unto the satisfying of the flesh, because the distension of the body kills the sobriety of the soul, and is hostile to chastity.

**3. 14.** It remains that I suggest something also about Gospel passages to your Beatitude: not indeed as many as are wont to occur to me reading at leisure (for there is now no leisure to seek things scattered through the books, or to fan the memory in recalling them), but at least a few which come into mind at the hour of this dictation, I shall inquire about. About the form of the resurrection, that letter—not large, but full of the instruction of faith—by which you replied to my second consultation while I was wintering at Carthage, if you have it copied in your papers, I ask that you send it, or at least reweave it for me; which is easy for you. For even if the writing does not survive, because perhaps, as a brief letter, it was disdained to be kept as a hasty thing among your books, renew it for me, ready in the same sense from the treasure of your heart, and send it to me among the other answers which you will render me, as I hope—Christ granting a passage of days to me and to you—so that I may receive those things whereby your labor may bear fruit in me, according to these chapters of the Scriptures, about which I have questioned you, who see as though through God, that I may hear what God speaks to me in you or from you.

**3. 15.** But I ask that you make this shine for me: in what way, or by what reason, the Lord after the resurrection was both not recognized and recognized—whether by the women who first came to the sepulcher, or afterward by those two on the way, then by his Disciples. For he rose in the same body in which he also suffered. And how was the form of the same body not the same which it had been? Or, if it was the same, how was it not recognized by those who had known it? But that, I believe, is of a sacrament, that he who had not been recognized by those walking on the way was revealed in the breaking of bread. Yet this same thing I wish to hold by your sense, not my own.

**3. 16.** And as for what he said to Mary, *Do not touch me, for I have not yet ascended to the Father*[^33]: if she was not permitted to touch him standing near, how would she touch him when he had ascended to the Father, unless perhaps by the progress of faith and the ascent of mind, by which God becomes far or near to a man? And had she doubted about Christ, whom she had thought to be a gardener? Therefore perhaps she deserved to hear, "Do not touch me." For she was judged unworthy to touch Christ with her hand, whom she had not yet apprehended by faith, nor understood to be God, when she had thought him a gardener, about whom a little before she had heard from the Angels, *Why do you seek the living among the dead?*[^34] "Do not, then, touch me, because to you I have not yet ascended to the Father, I who still seem to be only a man; afterward you will touch me, when you have ascended, by believing, to recognizing me."

**3. 17.** About those words too of most blessed Simeon, set forth to me what you think, that I may follow your sense: when, having come into the temple by the working of the Spirit to see Christ from the oracle of God, and having taken the infant Lord into his bosom and blessed him, he said to Mary, *Behold, this one is set for the ruin and resurrection of many in Israel, and for a sign that will be contradicted; and a sword will pass through your own soul, that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed*[^35]. Is he to be believed to have prophesied this about the passion of Mary, which is nowhere written? Or rather about her maternal affection, by which afterward, at the time of the passion, standing by the cross on which was fixed that which she herself had borne, she was pierced through with the grief of maternal vitals; and the spear of the cross penetrated her soul—the soul of her who, looking on, had had her son according to the flesh pierced through? For I see that in the Psalms too it is said of Joseph, *They humbled his feet in fetters; iron passed through his soul*[^36], just as in the Gospel Simeon said, *And a sword will pass through your own soul*[^37]. He did not say "flesh," but "soul," in which the affection of piety is contained, and the sting of grief works as a sword; whether one is afflicted with some injury of one's flesh, like Joseph, who suffered not the sufferings of death but of injuries, sold into a slave, and bound as a guilty man, and given to prison; or whether one is tortured by the sadness or grief of inner affection, like Mary, whom surely the maternal mind had led to the cross of the Lord—on which she then thought only of the son of her own body—so that, when she had seen him dead, she should, by human weakness, mourn, and gather him to be buried, presuming nothing for herself about his resurrection, because the penalty of the passion set before her eyes blinded the faith of the wonder that was to follow. Although the Lord, standing by his cross, consoled her—not trembling with the weakness of one dying, but, holding in his power the death by which he died willingly, full of the strength of one living, and of the constancy of one about to rise, admonishes her from the cross, saying of the blessed apostle John, *Woman, behold your son*; and likewise to him standing there, *Behold your mother*[^38]. Now indeed, departing from the human frailty by which he was born of a woman, through the death of the cross into the eternity of God, that he might be in the glory of God the Father, he assigns to a man the rights of human piety, and from his disciples chooses the younger, so as fittingly to assign a virgin mother to a virgin apostle, teaching two things at once in the same sentence: leaving us a model of piety, when he is solicitous about his mother, that her whom he was leaving in body he might not leave in care—and yet not about to leave her in body either, because him whom she saw dying she was soon to see alive again; and that other thing which pertained to the faith of all, sealing the saving sacrament of his piety, by the secret reason of the divine counsel, under this voice, so that to one he assigned a mother to be had in place of a mother, and to be consoled in his stead, and to her in turn handed over a new son in place of his own body—nay, so to say, begot one: by which he showed that she, besides him who was born of her, a virgin, had had and had no son; because the Savior would not have had so great a care of her consolation, had he not been her only one.

**3. 18.** But let us return to the words of Simeon, in whose close I confess my understanding grows dark: *And a sword,* he says, *will pass through your soul, that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed*[^39]. According to the letter this is utterly obscure to me; because we nowhere read that most blessed Mary was killed, so that that holy man should seem to have prophesied to her a future suffering by a corporeal sword. But also what he subjoined, "that the thoughts of many hearts may be revealed." For *God searches the hearts and reins*[^40]. And about the future judgment the Apostle says that *then God will make manifest the hidden things of hearts and the secrets of darkness*[^41]. Likewise the Apostle, spiritually expressing the heavenly arms with which we ought to be armed in our inner self, calls the sword of the spirit the word of God; of which to the Hebrews he says, *The word of God is living and effective, and more penetrating than any two-edged sword; reaching,* he says, *even to the division of soul and spirit*[^42], and the rest which you know. What wonder, then, if the fiery force of this word, and the more penetrating edge of the two-edged sword, passed through the soul of holy Joseph once, and afterward of blessed Mary? For we have learned that iron passed through neither his nor her body. And so that it may be the more plain that the Prophet there set "iron" for the sword of the word, immediately in the following verse he says, *The word of the Lord set him on fire*[^43]. For the word of God is both fire and sword, the Word himself, God, saying both of himself: *I came,* he says, *to send fire on the earth; and what do I will but that it already be kindled?*[^44] Likewise elsewhere he says, *I came not to send peace, but a sword*[^45]. You see that he designated one force of his teaching by the diverse names of fire and sword. Or how would the suffering or tribulation brought upon Mary by a sword endure? And so I desire to know this: what it would pertain to Mary that the thoughts of many hearts should be revealed; or where it appeared that, from the fact that a sword—whether a carnal one in iron, or a spiritual one in the word of God—passed through her soul, thereupon the thoughts of many hearts were revealed. Expound, then, this close of the words of Simeon especially to me, because I do not doubt that it shines for your holy soul, which from the purity of the inner eye has deserved the illumination of the Holy Spirit, through whom it can search and inspect even the deep things of God. May God have mercy on me through your prayers, and illuminate his countenance over me through the lamp of your word, holy lord, most blessed brother of one soul in the Lord Christ, my master in the faith of truth, and my receiver in the bowels of the love of Christ.
