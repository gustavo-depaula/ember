# Letter 57

To the most beloved lord and deservedly honorable and to-be-received son Celer, Augustine sends greeting in the Lord

**1.** That there was no just cause why the party of Donatus should cut itself off from the world of lands, throughout which the catholic Church is spread according to the prophetic and evangelical promises, I believe that your prudence, considering it the more, most easily understands. About which matter, if a more diligent disputation is necessary, I remember that I gave to your benevolence a book to read, when my most dear—your son, my Caecilius—had intimated that you had asked for it; which book was with you for not a few days. Which, if from zeal for knowing this matter, or amid your occupations, you either wished or were able to read, I do not doubt that your prudence has found that they have nothing they can probably contradict. And if anything perhaps still moves you, as far as God grants and allows, perhaps we shall be able to answer your asking, or likewise to give something to read, most beloved lord and deservedly honorable and to-be-received son.

**2.** Wherefore I ask that you more diligently commend the catholic unity to the region of Hippo, to your men, especially to Paternus and Maurusius. I know the vigilance of your heart, nor is there need, I think, to write more; since, if you wish, you can most easily learn both what others take care of and guard against on your estates, and what is done in your affairs. That there is in your affairs—it has been very strongly affirmed to me—a friend with whom I desire to be in concord; I ask that you favor this matter, so that you may have both great praise among men and a great reward with God: for already he had sent me word, through a certain Carus, a go-between of us both, that he feared lest some violent men of his prevent him from doing it, whom in your affairs and with your favor he will not be able to fear; nor ought you yourself to love in him not constancy, but plainly obstinacy. For it is shameful to change an opinion, but a true and right one; for a foolish and harmful one, it is both praiseworthy and wholesome. But just as constancy does not allow a man to be depraved, so obstinacy does not allow him to be corrected: accordingly, just as the former is to be praised, so the latter is to be amended. The presbyter whom I have sent will more plainly intimate the rest to your prudence. May the mercy of God keep you unharmed and happy, most beloved lord and deservedly honorable and to-be-received son.
