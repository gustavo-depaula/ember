# Letter 215

TO THE MOST BELOVED LORD AND BROTHER VALENTINUS, TO BE HONORED IN THE MEMBERS OF CHRIST, AND TO THE BROTHERS WHO ARE WITH YOU, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Let your Charity know that Cresconius, Felix, and another Felix, servants of God who came to us from your community, kept Easter with us. We held them somewhat longer, so that they might return to you better instructed against the new heretics, the Pelagians, into whose error he falls who thinks that the grace of God—which alone sets man free through our Lord Jesus Christ—is given according to any human merits. But again, he who thinks that, when the Lord comes to judgment, a man is not judged according to his works—a man who through his age has already been able to use free choice of the will—is nonetheless in error. For only little children, who do not yet have works of their own, whether good or evil, will be condemned according to original sin alone, those for whom the grace of the Savior does not come to aid *through the washing of regeneration*[^1]. But all the rest, who, already using free choice, have added their own sins on top of original sin, if they are not rescued from the power of darkness by the grace of God and transferred to the kingdom of Christ[^2], will bring back judgment not only according to the merits of their origin but also according to the merits of their own will. But the good too will indeed obtain a reward according to the merits of their good will, but they have obtained that good will itself through the grace of God. And thus is fulfilled what is written: *Wrath and indignation, tribulation and anguish, upon every soul of man that works evil, the Jew first and the Greek; but glory and honor and peace to everyone who works good, to the Jew first and the Greek*[^3].

**2.** About this most difficult question, that is, about the will and grace, I had no need to dispute at greater length even in this letter, since I had already given them another, as they were to return rather soon. And I have also written you a book, which, if with the Lord's help you read it diligently and grasp it keenly, I think there will no longer be any dissensions among you about this matter. They carry with them other things too, which we believed should be sent to you, by which you may know how the Catholic Church, in the mercy of God, has repelled the poisons of the Pelagian heresy. For what was written to Pope Innocent, bishop of the city of Rome, from the council of the province of Carthage and from the council of Numidia, and somewhat more carefully by five bishops, and what he wrote back to these three; likewise what was written to Pope Zosimus from the African council, and his rescript sent to all the bishops of the whole world; and what we briefly determined against that error in a later plenary council of all Africa; and the book of mine mentioned above, which I have just now written to you: all these we have both read in the present with them, and have sent to you through them.

**3.** We also read to them the book of the most blessed martyr Cyprian on the Lord's Prayer[^4], and we showed how he taught that all the things that pertain to our conduct, by which we live rightly, are to be sought from our Father who is in heaven, lest, presuming on free choice, we fall away from divine grace. There too we demonstrated how that same most glorious martyr admonished that we ought to pray even for our enemies, who have not yet believed in Christ, that they may believe—which would surely be done in vain unless the Church believed that even the evil and unbelieving wills of men can, through the grace of God, be converted to good. But this book of holy Cyprian we did not send, because they said it was also there among you. We also read with them my letter given to Sixtus, a priest of the Roman Church, which they brought with them to us; and we showed that it was written against those who say that the grace of God is given according to our merits, that is, against the same Pelagians.

**4.** As far, then, as we could, we dealt with these brothers, both yours and ours, that they might persevere in the sound Catholic faith—which neither denies free choice, whether to an evil life or to a good one, nor attributes so much to it that it can do anything without the grace of God, whether to be converted from evil to good, or to make progress perseveringly in good, or to attain to the everlasting good where it no longer fears that it may fail. You too, dearest ones, I exhort even in this letter, as the Apostle exhorts us all, *not to be wiser than you ought to be wise, but to be wise unto moderation, as God has apportioned to each the measure of faith*[^5].

**5.** Attend to what the Holy Spirit warns through Solomon: *Make straight courses,* he says, *for your feet, and direct your ways; turn not aside to the right hand nor to the left; but turn your foot away from the evil way. For the ways that are on the right hand the Lord knows; but those on the left are perverse. And he himself will make your courses straight; and your journeys he will bring forth in peace*[^6]. In these words of holy Scripture consider, brothers, that if there were no free choice, it would not be said: *Make straight courses for your feet, and direct your ways; turn not aside to the right hand nor to the left.* And yet, if this could be done without the grace of God, it would not afterward be said: *And he himself will make your courses straight, and will bring forth your journeys in peace.*

**6.** Do not, therefore, turn aside to the right hand nor to the left; even though the ways that are on the right hand are praised, and those on the left are blamed. For this is why he added: *But turn your foot away from the evil way,* that is, from the left—which he makes plain in what follows, saying: *For the ways that are on the right hand the Lord knows; but those on the left are perverse.* We ought surely to walk in those ways that the Lord knows, of which it is read in the Psalm: *The Lord knows the way of the righteous, and the way of the impious will perish*[^7]. For this latter the Lord does not know, because it is on the left: just as he is also to say to those set on the left: *I do not know you*[^8]. But what is it that he does not know, who surely knows all things, whether the good of men or the evil? But what is *I do not know you,* except "such as you I did not make"? Just as that which was said of the Lord Jesus Christ himself, that *he knew no sin*[^9]—what is *he knew not,* except "he had not done it"? And so what was said—*The ways that are on the right hand the Lord knows*—how is it to be understood, except that he himself made the right ways, that is, the ways of the righteous, which are surely the good works *that God has prepared,* as the Apostle says, *that we should walk in them*[^10]? But the perverse ways on the left, that is, the ways of the impious, he does not know, because he himself did not make them for man, but man made them for himself; on which account he says: *But I have hated the perverse ways of the wicked*[^11]—these are the ones on the left.

**7.** But it is answered to us: "Why then did he say, *Turn not aside to the right hand nor to the left,* when he seems rather to have ought to say, Hold to the right hand and turn not aside to the left, if the ways that are on the right hand are good?" Why, do we suppose, except that the ways on the right hand are good in such a way that nevertheless to turn aside to the right hand is not good? For he is to be understood as turning aside to the right hand who wishes to assign to himself, and not to God, those good works themselves which belong to the right-hand ways. And therefore, when he had said, *For the ways that are on the right hand the Lord knows; but those on the left are perverse,* as though it were said to him, "How then do you not want us to turn aside to the right hand?" he went on to add: *And he himself will make your courses straight; and your journeys he will bring forth in peace.* Understand, then, in this way what is commanded you—*Make straight courses for your feet, and direct your ways*—so that you may know that, when you do this, it is granted you by the Lord God that you do it; and you will not turn aside to the right hand, even though you walk in the right-hand ways, by not trusting in your own strength: and he himself will be your strength, who *will make your courses straight, and will bring forth your journeys in peace.*

**8.** Wherefore, most beloved, whoever says, "My will suffices for me to do good works," turns aside to the right hand. But again, those who think that a good life is to be abandoned, when they hear the grace of God so preached that it is believed and understood that grace itself makes men's wills good out of evil, and itself keeps even those it has made, and therefore say, *Let us do evil, that good may come*[^12], turn aside to the left. For this reason he said to you: *Turn not aside to the right hand nor to the left,* that is, do not so defend free choice that you attribute good works to it without the grace of God; nor so defend grace that, as though secure on account of it, you love evil works—which may the grace of God itself turn away from you. Indeed, the Apostle, setting before himself the words of such people, says: *What then shall we say? Shall we remain in sin, that grace may abound?* And to these words of erring men, who do not understand the grace of God, he replied as he ought, saying: *God forbid. For if we have died to sin, how shall we live in it?*[^13] Nothing could be said more briefly and better. For what does the grace of God confer on us more usefully in this present evil world than that we should die to sin? And therefore he will be found ungrateful to that very grace who, on account of it, wishes to live in the sin to which through it we die. But *God, who is rich in mercy*[^14], grant you both to be soundly wise, and to persevere progressively to the end in your good purpose. This for you, this for us, this for all who love you, and for those who hate you, pray earnestly in brotherly peace and watchfully. May you live for God. If I deserve anything of you, let brother Florus come to me.
