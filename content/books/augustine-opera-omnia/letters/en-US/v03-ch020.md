# Letter 173/A

To the most beloved lords, holy brothers and fellow presbyters Deogratias and Theodorus, and the fellow deacons Titianus and brother Comes, from Augustine.

**1.** Although not by your letter, yet by a most certain and most faithful messenger, I have learned that you desire me to write something by which, without any doubt and without any obscurity which cannot be penetrated by the slower-witted, the Holy Spirit may be proved to be God. Accordingly, let your brotherhood know—so far as I can recall from the holy Scriptures on this matter—that one whom what the Apostle says does not satisfy, *Do you not know that your bodies are the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God, and you are not your own? For you were bought with a great price; glorify and bear God in your body*[^1], is wholly ignorant whence it can be persuaded to him that the Holy Spirit is God, as far as concerns the authority of the divine words: but as far as concerns reason, which can be undertaken either by a man or by such a man as we are, this matter is disputed with the greatest labor. But whoever yields to the most eminent authority of the divine Scriptures, let him first look at what is written: *You shall adore the Lord your God, and him alone shall you serve*[^2], which in Greek is so set down that there is understood not that service which is owed even to human masters, but that which is to be shown to God only, which is called *latreia*; whence idolatry too is rightly condemned, when *latreia* is shown to idols, while *latreia* is to be shown to the true God only. But he does not say, You shall adore the Lord your God alone, but, him alone, he says, shall you serve. There he said alone, where he said you shall serve, namely with that service which is called *latreia*; to this service pertain temple, sacrifice, priest, and whatever is of this kind. And through this the Apostle would in no way say that our body is the temple of the Holy Spirit, unless that service were owed to him which is called *latreia*. Further, such a service would not be owed unless he to whom it is owed were God, especially since he says that our bodies are members of Christ, whom they do not deny to be God—even those who deny the Holy Spirit to be God and who contend that Christ is greater than the Holy Spirit. How, then, would the members of a greater be the temple of a lesser? Whence not only through this is the Holy Spirit without doubt understood to be God—because a temple cannot piously and rightly be attributed except to God—but he is also necessarily found to be one God with the Father and the Son, because the Trinity is one God. For since the attribution of a temple pertains to that service which is called *latreia*, and it is written, *You shall adore the Lord your God, and him alone shall you serve*, that is, to him alone shall you show *latreia*, surely, since *latreia*, when it is rightly shown, is shown to God, and to him to whom a temple is shown *latreia* is shown, and there is one God alone to whom *latreia* is to be shown, and the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit are without doubt one God; and this is what he says, *Glorify and bear God in your body*, of whom he had said, *Your bodies are the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God*. These things, as I could, I preferred to dictate most hastily, rather than to defer the desire of your charity by any excuse. If you think this too little, keep yourselves for reading the books on the Trinity, which in the name of the Lord I am preparing now to publish, lest perhaps that one persuade what a brief letter cannot.

[^1]: 1 Cor 6:19-20
[^2]: Deut 6:13
