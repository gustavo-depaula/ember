# Letter 38

Augustine to brother Profuturus

**1.** According to the spirit, as far as it pleases the Lord and he himself deigns to grant strength, we are well; but in body, I am in bed. For I can neither walk, nor stand, nor sit, from the pain and swelling of fissures or hemorrhoids. But even so, since it pleases the Lord, what else is to be said except that we are well? For rather, if we do not want what he wants, we are to be blamed, than that he is to be thought to do or to permit anything not rightly. You know all this: but because you are to me another self, what would I more gladly speak with you than what I speak with myself? We commend, then, to your holy prayers our days and our nights, that you may pray for us, that we not use the days intemperately, that we may bear the nights with an even mind, that even if *we walk in the midst of the shadow of death*, the Lord may be with us *that we fear no evils*[^1].

**2.** That old Megalius has died, who could doubt that you have already heard? For there were, from the laying down of his body, when I wrote this, almost twenty-four days. Whether you have already seen—for you were arranging it—the successor to his primacy, we wish, if it can be done, to know. There is no lack of scandals, but neither of refuge; no lack of sorrows, but neither of consolations. And amid these, how one must keep watch lest hatred of anyone hold the inmost parts of the heart, and not allow us to *pray to God in our chamber with the door shut*[^2], but shut the door against God himself, you know very well, very good brother: but it creeps in, since to no one who is angry does his own anger seem unjust. For thus, growing old, anger becomes hatred, while a sweetness, as it were, mixed with just grief, holds it longer in the vessel, until the whole grows sour and corrupts the vessel. Wherefore it is much better that we not be angry at anyone even justly, than that, by being angry as if justly, we slip into hatred of someone by the hidden facility of anger. For in receiving unknown guests, we are wont to say that it is much better to endure an evil man than perhaps through ignorance to exclude a good one, while we guard against an evil one being received: but in the affections of the mind it is the contrary. For it is incomparably more wholesome not to open the inner chamber of the heart even to anger justly knocking, than to admit it, not easily to depart, and about to advance from a twig to a beam. For it dares shamelessly even to grow more quickly than is supposed. For it does not blush in the darkness, when the sun has set upon it[^3]. You surely recall with what care and how great solicitude I have written these things, if you recall what you lately said with me on a certain journey.

**3.** Brother Severus, and those who are with him, we greet. Perhaps we would even write to them, if the haste of the bearer permitted. But I ask that, with the same brother of ours Victor—to whom I give thanks even before your Holiness, because he indicated, when he was going to Constantina—you help by asking, on account of the business which he himself knows, about which I bear a most grievous weight from the elder Nectarius much beseeching for that matter, that he not be reluctant to return by way of Calama: for so he promised me. Farewell.

[^1]: See Ps 23:4 (22:4)
[^2]: See Matt 6:6
[^3]: See Eph 4:26
