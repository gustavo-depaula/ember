# Letter 264

TO MAXIMA, AN HONORABLE AND DISTINGUISHED AND, IN THE MEMBERS OF CHRIST, PRAISEWORTHY HANDMAID OF GOD, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** As much as your holy zeal gladdens us, so much in turn it saddens us that you signify your province to be in too great peril through noxious and most pernicious errors. But because these things were foretold as about to be, it is not to be wondered at that they arise, but to be watched lest they harm. But God, our deliverer, would not permit these things to arise, unless it were expedient that his saints be instructed even through temptations of this kind. They indeed procure for themselves, by their own perverse will, both the desert of present blindness and of future eternal punishment, if through obstinacy they are unteachable, and neglect, while they are in this life, to correct and amend themselves. Nevertheless, just as they themselves badly use the good things of God, *who makes his sun rise upon the good and the evil, and rains upon the just and the unjust*[^1]; who calls them through his patience to repentance, while they treasure up for themselves wrath in the day of wrath and of the revelation of the just judgment of God[^2]: just as, then, they themselves badly use the kindness and patience, that is, the good things of God, while they are not corrected; so, on the contrary, God uses even their evils well, not only for his justice, which will repay them worthily in the end, but also for the exercise and advancement of his saints, so that from the very perversity of the evil the good may make progress, and be tested, and be made manifest; as the Apostle says: *There must be heresies, that the approved may be made manifest among you*[^3].

**2.** For if there were no good use even of the evil, for God, toward the advantage of his elect—he who, even from the evil of Judas, conferred so great a good on us, that we might be redeemed by the blood of Christ—he could either not permit those to be born whom he foreknew would be evil, or extinguish them in the very beginning of their malignity: but he permits them to be only as much as he knows to be expedient and sufficient for admonishing and exercising his holy household. Therefore he consoles our sadness about them: because the very sadness which we have for them relieves us; but it weighs down those persevering in their perversity. But the joy which we receive when some of them, corrected, are changed for the better, and coupled to the fellowship of the saints, can be compared to no joy in this life. For this reason it is written: *Son, if you are wise, you will be wise for yourself and for your neighbors; but if you turn out evil, you alone will drink in the evils*[^4]. For when we rejoice over the faithful and the just, their good profits both them and us; but when we are saddened over the unbelieving and the wicked, it harms only them—both their malice and our sadness: but us this too helps very much with God, that we are mercifully saddened for them, and groan and pray for that very sadness. Hence, honorable and in Christ praiseworthy handmaid of God, both your sadness over such people, and the vigilance and caution against such people, which you expressed in your letter, I much approve and praise: and that you may walk perseveringly in this way, with my strength, since you demand this, I both exhort and admonish you to have mercy on them as simple as a dove, but to beware of them as cunning as a serpent[^5]; and to give effort, as much as you can, that those who cling to you may remain with you in the right faith, or, if perhaps any have been depraved in anything, be corrected to the right faith.

**3.** But about the man whom the *Word of God assumed, when he was made flesh and dwelt among us*[^6], I would amend something, if in what you believe I found anything false or perverse. Believe, then, this which you believe, that in that man the Son of God assumed our whole nature, that is, both a rational soul and mortal flesh without sin. For he was made a partaker of our weakness, not of iniquity, that through the common weakness he might dissolve our iniquity, and lead us to his righteousness, drinking death from what is ours, and offering to drink life from what is his. But if you have any writing of theirs, in which they assert what is contrary to this faith, deign to send it, that we may not only state our faith, but also refute their faithlessness, as much as we can. For without doubt this very thing which they perversely and impiously think, they try to construct from some testimonies of the divine Scriptures: in which it is to be shown them how incorrectly they understand the holy Letters written for the salvation of the faithful; just as if anyone should gravely vex himself with medicinal instruments, which surely were instituted not for wounding but for healing. But we have labored much, and labor, as far as the Lord grants, to refute diverse errors. But if you perhaps desire to have the little works of our labors, send those who may copy them for you. For God willed that you could do this most easily, who gave you the means by which you can.

[^1]: Matt 5:45
[^2]: See Rom 2:4-5
[^3]: See 1 Cor 11:19
[^4]: See Prov 9:12
[^5]: See Matt 10:16
[^6]: See John 1:14
