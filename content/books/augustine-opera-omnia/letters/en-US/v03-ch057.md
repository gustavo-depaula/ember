# Letter 207

TO CLAUDIUS, MOST BLESSED BROTHER AND FELLOW BISHOP, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** Since, stirred by brotherly affection, you yourself, before I asked, sent me the four books of Julian which he wrote against one of mine, I thought nothing more fitting to be done than that you especially, by reading the things I answer, should judge whether I have answered rightly and fittingly. For from these four books someone—I know not who—had written some excerpts to the illustrious and religious man Count Valerius, against this same book of mine, which he had learned I had written to him: and when these had come into my hands by the care of that same exalted man, I did not delay to add to that one first a second volume, in which I refuted all those things, as I could. But now, when I was inspecting those same books more diligently, I found that the one who had selected some things from them had not set down everything as it is read in these books: whence it could seem to Julian, or to any of his party, that I had rather lied, because, of course, those things which were excerpted from these books and sent to the said Count could not have become known just as these four books did. Whoever, then, reads also that second book of mine, written to Count Valerius just as the first, let him know that in certain places I answered not Julian, but rather the one who selected those things from his books, and did not set them down as he found them, but thought somewhat must be changed—perhaps that in that way he might make as it were his own what was clearly another's. But now, believing more true the copies which your Holiness sent, I see that I must answer the author himself, who boasts that he has refuted one book of mine with his four, and ceases not everywhere to scatter the venoms of the faith. I have undertaken, then, this work in the help of the Savior of the little and the great; and I know that you have prayed for me that I might fulfill it, and for those whom we believe and wish our labors of this kind will profit. Attend, then, now to my reply, the beginning of which will follow this letter. Farewell in the Lord, mindful of us, most blessed brother.
