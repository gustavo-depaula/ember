# Letter 105

Augustine, the Catholic bishop, to the Donatists

**1. 1.** The love of Christ, for whom we wish to win over every man, as far as pertains to our will, does not permit us to be silent. If you hate us for this reason, that we preach Catholic peace to you, we serve the Lord who says, *Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called sons of God*[^1]; and in the Psalm it is written, *With those who hated peace I was peaceable; when I spoke to them, they fought against me without cause*[^2]. For this reason certain priests of your party have given us a command, saying, "Withdraw from our flocks, if you do not want us to kill you." How much more justly do we say to them: No, do not withdraw, but draw near in peace to the flocks—not ours, but his whose we all are; or, if you are unwilling and are at odds, withdraw yourselves rather from the flocks for which Christ shed his blood: which you therefore wish to make your own so that they may not be Christ's, although you try to possess them under his name; just as if a slave were to steal sheep from his master's flock, and were to stamp the mark of his own master upon whatever was born from them, lest his theft could be recognized. For thus did your forefathers: they separated from the Church of Christ peoples having the baptism of Christ, and whoever grew up among them, they baptized with the baptism of Christ. But the Lord both punishes thieves, if they do not correct themselves, and recalls the sheep from error to the flock, nor does he wipe out his own mark in them.

**1. 2.** You call us traitors; which neither could your forefathers prove against our forefathers, nor can you in any way prove against us. What do you want us to do for you, who, when we tell you to listen patiently to our cause and yours, know nothing but to be proud and to rage? For surely we would show you that they were rather the traitors who condemned Caecilian and his associates, as it were, on the charge of treachery. And you say, "Withdraw from our flocks"; flocks whom you teach to believe you and not to believe Christ. For you tell them that, on account of traitors whom you do not point out, the Church of Christ has remained in Africa alone, in the party of Donatus: which you recite not from the Law, not from the Prophet, not from the Psalm, not from the Apostle, not from the Gospel, but from your own heart and from the calumnies of your parents. But Christ says that *repentance and remission of sins* are to be preached *in his name through all nations, beginning from Jerusalem*[^3]: with which Church, manifested from the mouth of Christ, you do not communicate, and, dragging others to your perdition, you are unwilling to be set free.

**2. 3.** But if we displease you for this reason, that you are compelled to unity by the commands of the Emperors, you yourselves did this, who never permitted us, wherever we wished to preach the truth—so that each might hear it secure and willing, and choose it—but always hindered us by your violences and terrors. Do not gnash and disturb your souls; consider patiently, if it can be done, what we say, and recall the deeds of your Circumcellions and of the clerics who were always their leaders, and you will see what cause has stirred this up for you. Whence you unjustly complain, because you yourselves compelled all these things to be commanded. For—not to repeat things long past and many—at least think on your recent deeds. Marcus, a priest of Casphalia, compelled by no one, of his own will became a Catholic. Why did your people persecute him and nearly kill him, had not the hand of God, through men coming up, checked their violences? Restitutus of Victoriana transferred himself to the Catholic Church with no one compelling him. Why was he seized from his house, beaten, rolled in water, clothed in reeds, and held in captivity I know not how many days, nor would perhaps have been restored to his own liberty, had not Proculeianus already seen that, on account of that very cause, an arraignment was hanging over him? Marcianus of Urga chose Catholic unity of his own will. Why did your clerics—when he himself had fled—beat his subdeacon nearly to death and overwhelm him with stones, clerics whose houses were overturned for their crime?

**2. 4.** What more shall we say? Just now you sent a herald to cry out at Sinitis: "Whoever communicates with Maximinus, his house will be burned": Maximinus, who, before he himself was converted to the Catholic Church, and had not yet returned from overseas—for what else had we sent the priest of Sinitis, except that, troublesome to none, he might visit our own, and, placed in a house of his own right, preach Catholic peace to the willing? Whom you cast out from there with grave injury. What else were we doing when one of us, Possidius, bishop of Calama, went to the estate of Figulina, except that our people, however few were there, might be visited, and, having heard the word of God, those who wished might be converted to the unity of Christ? Who, as he walked his way, was ambushed in the manner of robbers, and because he could not fall into their ambushes, they had nearly burned him alive by open violence on the estate of Liveta, with the house to which he had fled, had not the tenants of that same estate, for the danger to their own safety, a third time put out the flames laid beneath. And yet, when Crispinus was on this account convicted in the proconsular court as a heretic, by the intercession of that same bishop Possidius the ten pounds of gold were not exacted. Ungrateful to this benevolence and gentleness, he dared to appeal to the Catholic emperors. Whence he provoked upon you this wrath of God, of which you murmur, much more unseasonably and vehemently.

**2. 5.** You see that you rise up violently against the peace of Christ, and that you suffer not for him but for your own iniquities. What madness is this, that, when you live badly, you do the deeds of robbers; and when you are rightly punished, you demand the glory of martyrs? If, then, you by your own private audacity so violently compel men either to go into error or to remain in error, how much more ought we, through the most well-ordered powers which God, according to his prophecy, subjected to Christ, to resist your furies, so that wretched souls, freed from your domination, may be rescued from the most ancient falsehood, and grow accustomed to the most open truth? For as to what you say, that men are compelled by us against their will, many even wish to be compelled; which they confess to us both before and after, so that even thus they may escape your oppressions.

**2. 6.** And yet which is better—to bring forward true commands of the Emperors for unity, or false indulgences for perversity, which you did, and with your lie suddenly filled all Africa? In which deed you showed nothing else than that the party of Donatus, always presuming on a lie, is tossed and carried about by every wind, as it is written, *He who trusts in falsehoods feeds on winds*[^4]. For just as that indulgence was true, so are the crimes of Caecilian true, and the treachery of Felix of Aptunga, by whom he was ordained, and whatever else you are accustomed to say against Catholics, so that you may separate the unhappy from the peace of the Church of Christ, and be unhappily separated. We presume on the power of no man, although it is surely far more honorable to presume on Emperors than to presume on Circumcellions, to presume on laws than to presume on seditions. But we remember that it is written, *Cursed is everyone who places his hope in man*[^5]. Whence, then, we presume—if you wish to know—think of him of whom the prophet foretold, saying, *All the kings of the earth will adore him, and all the nations will serve him*[^6]. And therefore we use this power of the Church, which the Lord both promised and gave to her.

**2. 7.** For if the Emperors were in error—which God forbid—they would give laws on behalf of their error against the truth, by which the just would be both tested and crowned, by not doing what those Emperors commanded, because God forbade it. As Nebuchadnezzar had commanded that the golden statue be adored; and those who were unwilling to do this pleased God, who forbade such things. But when the Emperors hold the truth, they command on behalf of that very truth against error; and whoever despises this acquires judgment for himself. For he both pays penalties among men, and will have no countenance before God, who was unwilling to do what the very truth, through the heart of the king, commanded him. As Nebuchadnezzar himself afterward, moved and changed by the miracle of the deliverance of the three young men, set forth an edict on behalf of the truth against error, that whoever blasphemed the God of Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego should go to destruction, and their houses to ruin: and do you not wish that the Christian emperors should command some such thing against you, when they know that by you, in those whom you rebaptize, Christ is blown away in derision? If the commands of kings do not pertain to the preaching of religion and the prohibiting of sacrileges, why do you yourselves even sign yourselves at the edict of a king commanding such things? Or do you not know that the words of the king are: *The signs and wonders which the Lord the most high God has done for me, it has pleased me to announce in my sight, how great and mighty is his kingdom, an everlasting kingdom, and his power for ages of ages*[^7]? And when you have heard this, do you not answer "Amen"; and, this said with a clear voice at the edict of the king, do you not sign yourselves in holy solemnity? But now, because you can do nothing with the Emperors, you wish to make us odious for it. But if you could do anything, how much would you do; when you can do nothing, even so you do not cease!

**2. 8.** Know that your first forefathers brought the cause of Caecilian to the emperor Constantine. Demand this proof of us, and we will prove it to you; and if we do not prove it, do with us whatever you can. But because Constantine did not dare to judge the cause of a bishop, he delegated it to bishops to be examined and concluded. And this was done in the city of Rome, Melchiades, bishop of that Church, presiding with many of his colleagues. And when they had pronounced Caecilian innocent, and had struck with their sentence Donatus, who had made the schism at Carthage, again your people came to the Emperor, murmured about the judgment of the bishops, in which they had been defeated. For how can a bad litigant praise the judges by whose judgment he was defeated? Yet again the most merciful Emperor gave other bishops as judges at Arles, a city of Gaul, and from them your people appealed to the Emperor himself, until he too should examine the cause, and pronounce Caecilian innocent and them calumniators. Nor, defeated so often, did they thus rest, but about Felix of Aptunga, by whom Caecilian had been ordained, they wearied the Emperor himself with daily appeals, saying that he was a traitor, and that therefore Caecilian could not be a bishop, since he had been ordained by a traitor; until Felix himself too, by the Emperor's command, his cause examined by the proconsul Aelianus, was proved innocent.

**2. 9.** Then Constantine was the first to give a most severe law against the party of Donatus. His sons, imitating him, ordained such things. Succeeding them, Julian, deserter of Christ and enemy, at the supplication of your Rogatianus and Pontius, permitted the liberty of perdition to the party of Donatus: in short, he then gave back the basilicas to heretics when he gave the temples to demons, thinking that in this way the Christian name could perish from the earth, if he favored the unity of the Church from which he had lapsed with envy, and permitted sacrilegious dissensions to be free. This was that justice of his to be proclaimed, which the supplicating Rogatianus and Pontius praised, saying to the apostate man that with him alone justice had a place. To him succeeded Jovian, who, because he died quickly, commanded nothing about such matters. Then Valentinian; read what he commanded against you. Then Gratian and Theodosius; read, when you will, what they enacted about you. Why, then, do you wonder at the sons of Theodosius, as though they ought to follow in this cause anything other than the judgment of Constantine, most firmly kept through so many Christian emperors?

**2. 10.** But to Constantine, as we have said—as we show you whenever you wish, if indeed you are ignorant of it—your forefathers brought the cause of Caecilian of their own accord. Constantine is dead, but the judgment of Constantine lives against you: to whom your people sent their cause, before whom they reproached the bishops who were judges, to whom they appealed from the bishops who were judges, whom they most wearisomely importuned about Felix of Aptunga, from whom, so often convicted and confounded, they returned, and did not withdraw from the ruin of their fury and animosity; and they left it to you, their successors, as a hereditary thing, so impudently to make odious the commands of Christian emperors—when, if it were permitted you, you would now importune against us, not indeed Constantine the Christian, because he favored truth, but you would rouse the apostate Julian from the dead; as though, indeed, if anything of the sort happened, it would be a great evil except to you. For what is a worse death of the soul than the liberty of error?

**3. 11.** But now let us take all these things out of the way; let us love peace, which everyone learned and unlearned understands is to be preferred to discord; let us love and hold unity. This the emperors command, which Christ also commands; for when they command good, it is none but Christ who commands through them. And he beseeches us also through the Apostle, that we all say the same thing, and that there be no schisms among us, and that we not say, *I indeed am of Paul, but I of Apollos, and I of Cephas, but I of Christ*[^8]; but that all of us together be none but Christ's, since *neither is Christ divided, nor was Paul crucified for us*[^9]; how much less Donatus? Nor were we baptized in the name of Paul; how much less in that of Donatus? This the emperors too say, because they are Catholic Christians, not servants of idols, like your Julian; not heretics, like certain ones who were such and persecuted the Catholic Church, when the true Christians endured, not most just penalties for heretical error, as you do, but most glorious sufferings for Catholic truth.

**3. 12.** Attend with how most manifest a truth, through the heart of the king, which is in the hand of God, God himself has spoken in this very law which you say was brought forth against you; but it is, if you understand, brought forth for you. Attend to what the words of the prince contain: "For if, in those who were first initiated, the religion of Baptism is for that reason judged weak, that those from whom it is received are thought to be sinners, the Sacrament that has been handed on will have to be renewed as often as the administrator of the conferred Baptism is found unworthy, and our faith will depend not on the choice of our own will, nor on the grace of the divine gift, but on the merits of priests and the quality of clerics." Let your bishops make a thousand councils; let them answer this one sentence, and we agree with you on whatever you wish. For see how perversely and impiously is said what you are accustomed to say, that if the man is good, he himself sanctifies the one he baptizes; but if he is bad, and the one who is baptized does not know it, then God sanctifies. If this is true, then men ought to wish to be baptized by unknown bad men rather than by known good ones, so that they may be sanctified by God rather than by man: but far be this madness from us. Why, then, do we not speak the truth and think rightly—that that grace is always God's and the Sacrament God's, but the man's is only the ministry; who, if he is good, cleaves to God and works with God; but if he is bad, God works the visible form of the Sacrament through him, while he himself gives the invisible grace? This let us all think, and let there be no schisms among us.

**4. 13.** Be of one accord with us, brethren; we love you, we wish for you what we wish for ourselves. If you hate us the more gravely for this, that we do not permit you to err and perish, say this to God, whom we fear threatening the bad shepherds and saying, *What had strayed you did not recall, and what had perished you did not seek out*[^10]. This God himself does for you through us, whether by entreating, or by threatening, or by rebuking, or by losses, or by labors, or through his hidden admonitions or visitations, or through the laws of the temporal powers. Understand what is being done with you; God does not wish you to perish, estranged from your Catholic mother in sacrilegious discord. You could never prove anything against us; your bishops, when met by us, never wished to confer with us peaceably, as though fleeing to speak with sinners. Who could bear this pride? As though the apostle Paul did not confer with sinners and with the very sacrilegious: read the Acts of the Apostles, and see. As though the Lord himself did not have discourses about the Law with the Jews, by whom he was crucified, and answer them fittingly. Finally, the devil is the first of all sinners, who can never be turned to righteousness, and yet not even the Lord himself disdained to answer him about the Law; that you may understand that they refuse to confer with us for this reason, that they know their cause is lost.

**4. 14.** We do not know what men boast against themselves who rejoice in calumnious dissensions. In the Scriptures we learn Christ, in the Scriptures we learn the Church. These Scriptures we hold in common; why do we not hold in them both Christ and the Church in common? Where we have recognized him of whom the Apostle says, *The promises were made to Abraham and to his seed. He does not say, And to seeds, as of many, but as of one, And to your seed, which is Christ*[^11]—there we have recognized the Church, of which God says to Abraham, *In your seed all nations will be blessed*[^12]. Where we have recognized Christ prophesying of himself in the Psalm, *The Lord said to me, You are my Son; today I have begotten you*[^13]—there we have recognized the Church, in that which follows, *Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and the ends of the earth as your possession*[^14]. Where we have recognized Christ in that which is written, *The God of gods, the Lord, has spoken*—there we have recognized the Church too, in that which follows, *and has called the earth from the rising of the sun to its setting*[^15]. Where we have recognized Christ in that which is written, *And he, like a bridegroom coming forth from his chamber, has exulted like a giant to run his course*[^16]—there we have recognized the Church too, in that which is said a little above, *Their sound went out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world. In the sun he has set his tabernacle*[^17]. This is the Church set in the sun, that is, in a manifestation known to all, to the ends of the earth. Where we have recognized Christ in that which is written, *They pierced my hands and feet, they numbered all my bones; but they themselves considered and looked upon me, they divided my garments among themselves, and over my clothing they cast lots*[^18]—there we have recognized the Church too, in that which is said a little after in the same psalm, *All the ends of the earth will remember and be turned to the Lord, and all the families of the nations will worship in his sight; for the kingdom is the Lord's, and he will rule over the nations*[^19]. Where we have recognized Christ in that which is written, *Be exalted above the heavens, O God*—there we have recognized the Church too, in that which follows, *and over all the earth be your glory*[^20]. Where we have recognized Christ in that which is written, *O God, give your judgment to the king, and your justice to the king's son*[^21]—there we have recognized the Church too, in that which is said of him in the same psalm, *And he will rule from sea to sea, and from the river to the ends of the world. Before him the Ethiopians will fall down, and his enemies will lick the earth. The kings of Tarshish and the islands will offer gifts; the kings of the Arabs and of Saba will bring presents; and all the kings of the earth will adore him, all nations will serve him*[^22].

**4. 15.** Where we have recognized Christ in that which is written, that a stone cut from the mountain without hands broke all the kingdoms of the earth—those, namely, which presumed on the worship of demons—there we have recognized the Church too, in that which was said, that the stone itself grew and became a great mountain and filled the whole earth[^23]. Where we have recognized Christ in that which is written, *The Lord will prevail against them, and will destroy all the gods of the nations of the earth*—there we have recognized the Church too, in that which there follows, *And they will worship in his sight, each one from his own place, all the islands of the nations*[^24]. Where we have recognized Christ in that which is written, *God will come from the south, and the holy one from the shady mountain; his power will cover the heavens*—there we have recognized the Church, in that which follows, *and the earth is full of his praise*[^25]. For Jerusalem is set to the south, as is read in the book of Joshua son of Nun, whence the name of Christ was spread abroad; and there is the shady mountain, the Mount of Olives, whence he ascended into heaven so that his power might cover the heavens, and the Church be filled with his praise throughout all the earth. Where we have recognized Christ in that which is written, *Like a sheep he was led to be slaughtered, and like a lamb before its shearer he was without voice, so he opened not his mouth*[^26], and the rest which is said there of his passion—there we have recognized the Church too, in that which is said there, *Rejoice, O barren one who do not bear; break forth and cry out, you who do not travail; for many are the children of the desolate, more than of her who has a husband. For the Lord has said: Enlarge the place of your tabernacle, and fix your curtains, do not spare. Stretch out your cords farther, and make firm strong stakes; again and again to the right and to the left extend them. For your seed will inherit the nations, and you will inhabit the cities which were desolate. Do not fear, for you will prevail; and do not blush that you were detestable. For you will forget your confusion forever, you will not be mindful of the disgrace of your widowhood; for I am the Lord who made you, the Lord is his name; and he who rescued you, he, the God of Israel, will be called the God of all the earth*[^27].

**5. 16.** We do not know what you say about traitors, whom you could never convict, never point out. I do not say that your own people are rather shown to be detected and confessed in such a crime: what does it pertain to us about the burdens of others? Except that we should correct those whom we can, whether by rebuke or by any discipline in the spirit of gentleness and the diligence of love; but those whom we cannot correct, even if necessity compels, for the salvation of the rest, that they share the Sacraments of God with us, yet let us not share in their sins, which happens only by consenting and favoring. For thus, in this world in which the Catholic Church is spread through all nations—which the Lord calls his field—we tolerate them as tares among the wheat, or in this threshing floor of unity as chaff mixed with the grain, or within the nets of the word and Sacrament as bad fish enclosed with the good, until the time of harvest, or of winnowing, or of the shore; lest on their account we uproot the wheat too, or, separating the bare grains from the threshing floor before the time—grains not to be sent into the granary—we cast them out to be gathered by the birds; or, the nets burst by schisms, while we beware, as it were, of the bad fish, we go out into the sea of pernicious liberty. For on this account, by these and other likenesses, the Lord confirmed the tolerance of his servants, lest, while the good think themselves blamed by the admixture of the bad, they either, through human and rash dissensions, lose the little ones, or the little ones perish. And the heavenly Master warned so far that this is to be guarded against that he made the people secure even about bad rulers, lest on their account the chair of wholesome teaching be deserted, in which even the bad are compelled to say good things. For what they say is not their own but God's, who set in the chair of unity the teaching of truth. Accordingly, that truthful one, and truth itself, says of rulers who do their own evil things and say God's good things: *Do what they say; but what they do, do not do: for they say and do not do*[^28]. He surely would not say, "What they do, do not do," unless the things they did were manifestly evil.

**5. 17.** Let us not, then, on account of the bad, perish in bad dissension: although we could show, if you wish, that your forefathers were not execrators of the bad but accusers of the innocent. But whoever and of whatever sort they were, let them bear their own burdens. Behold the Scriptures held in common; behold where we know Christ; behold where we know the Church. If you hold Christ himself, why do you not hold the Church herself? If you believe in Christ himself, whom you read of and do not see, yet on account of the truth of the Scriptures, why do you deny the Church, which you both read of and see? By saying these things to you, and by compelling you to this good of peace and unity and love, we have become enemies to you; and you command that you will kill us, who tell you the truth and do not, as far as we can, permit you to perish in error. May God avenge us upon you, so that he may kill that very error of yours within you, and you may rejoice with us in the truth. Amen.
