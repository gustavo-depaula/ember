# Letter 218

TO THE MOST BELOVED AND MOST LONGED-FOR SON PALATINUS, AUGUSTINE.

**1.** Your manner of life, stronger and more fruitful toward the Lord our God, has brought us great joy. For you have chosen learning from your youth, that you may find wisdom even to gray hairs[^1]. Gray hairs, then, are the understanding of a man, and the age of old age is an immaculate life[^2]. May the Lord grant it to you, who ask, seek, and knock[^3], who knows how to give good gifts to his sons[^4]. For although you abound in exhorters and exhortations to the way of salvation and everlasting glory—above all the grace of Christ itself, which has spoken to you so wholesomely in your heart—nevertheless we too, for the duty of love which we owe you, bring some exhortation in this return greeting of ours, by which we may rouse you not as one slothful or sleeping, but provoke and incite you as one running[^5].

**2.** You ought to be wise, son, that you may persevere, because you have been wise to choose. Let this very thing belong to your wisdom: to know whose gift this is. *Reveal your way to the Lord, and hope in him; and he will act, and will bring forth your righteousness like light, and your judgment like noonday*[^6]. *He will make your courses straight, and will bring forth your journeys in peace*[^7]. As you scorned what you were hoping for in the world, lest you should glory in the abundance of riches, which after the manner of the sons of this world you had begun to covet, so now, to take up the yoke of the Lord and his burden, do not trust in your own strength; and that will be gentle, and this light[^8]. For in the Psalm both *those who trust in their own strength,* and *those who glory in the abundance of their riches,* are alike reproved[^9]. Therefore you did not yet have the glory of riches, but you most prudently despised the glory you were desiring to have. Beware lest it creep upon you to trust in your own strength; for you are a man, *and cursed is everyone who places his hope in man*[^10]. But plainly trust in your God with all your heart, and he will be your strength, in which devoutly and gratefully you may trust, to whom you may humbly and faithfully say, *I will love you, O Lord, my strength*[^11]: for the *love of God* itself, which when *perfect casts out fear*[^12], is poured out in our hearts not through our own, that is, human powers, but, as the Apostle says, *through the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^13].

**3.** Watch, therefore, and pray, that you may not enter into temptation[^14]. For that very prayer admonishes you that you have need of the help of your Lord, lest you place your hope of living well in yourself. For you pray now not to receive riches and honors of the present world, or any human vanity, but not to enter into temptation: which surely, if a man could grant it to himself by willing, would not be asked for in prayer. Wherefore, that we may not enter into temptation, if the will were enough, we would not pray; yet if the will were lacking, neither could we pray. Let it be present, then, that we may will; but let us pray that we may be able to do what we have willed, when by his gift we have been rightly wise. And since you have already begun this good, here is something for which to give thanks. For *what have you that you have not received? But if you have received it, beware lest you glory as if you had not received it*[^15], that is, as if you could have had it from yourself. But knowing from whom you have received it, ask of him that it may be perfected, by whom it was given that it might be begun. *With fear,* therefore, *and trembling* work out your *salvation. For it is God who works* in you both *to will and to accomplish, for the good will*[^16]: since *the will is prepared by the Lord*[^17], and by him *the steps of a man are directed, and he will desire his way*[^18]. This holy thought will keep you, so that your wisdom may be devotion[^19]; that is, that you may be good from God, and not ungrateful to the grace of Christ.

**4.** Your parents long for you, faithfully congratulating you on the better hope you have begun to have in the Lord[^20]. But we desire to have you, whether absent in body or present, in one spirit, through which *love is poured out in our hearts*[^21], so that wherever our flesh may be, our soul can in no way be separated. We received most gratefully the haircloth garments you sent, by which you yourself first admonished us about the frequenting and keeping of the humility of prayers.
