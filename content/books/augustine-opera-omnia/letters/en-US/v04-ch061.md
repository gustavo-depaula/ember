# Letter 268

TO MY MOST BELOVED AND MOST LONGED-FOR LORDS, THE HOLY PEOPLE TO WHOM I MINISTER, THE MEMBERS OF CHRIST, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** The most well-known and most well-proven devotion of Your Holiness in our Lord Jesus Christ gave me confidence, that even absent I might presume on that whence, present, I have been wont to rejoice: I who am always with you in spirit; not only because the grace of our Lord Jesus Christ does not cease to breathe forth such great sweetness, but also because you do not permit me myself, who serve you in the Gospel, to suffer straits. For when our brother Fascius was urged by the tax collectors to pay a debt of seventeen solidi, which at present he did not find means to discharge; lest he suffer bodily injury, he flew to the help of the holy church: those collectors too, since they were compelled to set out, and therefore could grant no delay, burdened me with the gravest complaints, so that I should either hand him over to them, or provide whence they might receive what they showed was owed to them. And when I had offered Fascius to address Your Holiness about his necessities; deterred by shame, he begged me not to do it. So I, constrained by a greater necessity, received seventeen solidi from our brother Macedonius, which I at once gave for his cause, he promising that by a certain day he could meet them with the means to repay them; and consenting that, if he could not meet it, the matter about him would be brought to your mercy, which you are wont to show to brothers as fraternal.

**2.** Now, then, since he is absent, it remains that you come to the aid, not of him, whom no one presses while absent, but of my promise, the reputation of which is always present to you. For the day on which he had promised he would meet it has now passed; and I do not find what to answer him who entrusted his solidi to my faith, except that I do what I promised I would do. But because I was not reminded of this matter, so as to make a discourse about it on the holy day of Pentecost, when your greater throng was present; I ask that you deign to have this letter in place of my present tongue, with God and our Lord—in whom you have believed, who never departs from you who fear and honor his name—admonishing and exhorting you in your hearts. In whom we too are always joined to you, although we seem to have set out from you in body; who promises you, from this seed of good works, a harvest of eternal life, the Apostle saying: *But let us not fail in doing good; for in its own time we shall reap, untiring. And so, while we have time, let us work good to all, but most of all to the household of faith*[^1]. Since, therefore, a member of the household of faith, a faithful Christian, a Catholic, is our brother, for the supplying of whose necessity I ask you to do what the Lord commands; without sadness, without murmuring, and with gladness and cheerfulness do it; for you believe God, not a man; because he promises that you will lose none of the things you mercifully do, but will receive them on that day with immortal interest[^2]: and since the Apostle himself says: *But this I say: He who sows sparingly will also reap sparingly*[^3]; you ought to understand that it is the time, while we are still in this life, to procure hastily and eagerly the gift of eternal life: because, when the end of the age comes, it will not be given except to those who, by faith, bought this for themselves before they could see it.

**3.** I have also written to the priests, that if anything be lacking after the contribution of Your Holiness, they make it up from what the church has, provided only you offer cheerfully according to what pleases: because whether it be given from yours or from the church, all things are God's, and your devotion will be sweeter than the treasures of the church; as the Apostle says: *Not that I seek the gift, but I seek the fruit*[^4]. Gladden, therefore, my heart, because I desire to rejoice over your fruits: for you are the trees of God, which he deigns to water with assiduous showers, even through our ministry. May the Lord protect you from all evil, both here and in the future world, most beloved and most longed-for lords and brothers.

[^1]: Gal 6:9-10
[^2]: See 2 Cor 9:6-7
[^3]: 2 Cor 9:6
[^4]: Phil 4:17
