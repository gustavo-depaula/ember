# Letter 70

To the most beloved and honorable lord, brother Naucelio, Alypius and Augustine

**1.** When you had reported to us what was answered by your bishop Clarentius—that is, that about Felicianus of Musti he did not deny that he was both condemned by them, and afterward received into his honor, but that he was condemned innocent, because he was absent, and proved that he was absent; we say this, that he may answer to it, because it was not permitted to condemn unheard one whom they themselves now say to have been innocent, who condemned him. Either, then, an innocent man ought not to have been condemned, or a guilty man, condemned, ought not to have been received. If an innocent man was received, an innocent man was condemned; if a guilty man was condemned, a guilty man was received. If those who condemned him did not know whether he was innocent, they are to be charged with rashness, because they dared to condemn an unheard, innocent man, about whom they did not know; and from the present deed we understand that with the same rashness they condemned also the earlier men whom they defamed with the crime of betrayal. For if an innocent man could be condemned by them, those too could be called traitors by them who were not traitors.

**2.** Then the same Felicianus, condemned by them, communicated for a long time with Maximian; if he was innocent when he was condemned, why, at a later time, communicating with the wicked Maximian, did he baptize many outside their communion? They themselves are witnesses, who acted before the proconsul that the same Felicianus, as one with Maximian, should be excluded from the basilica. It was too little, then, to have condemned an absent man, to have condemned an unheard man, to have condemned, as they say, an innocent man; moreover, the proconsul too was approached against him, that he might be expelled from the church. Even then, when they were expelling him from the church, they confess that they reckoned him among the condemned and wicked and the Maximianists. When, then, that man baptized men, communicating with Maximian, did he give true baptism or false? If he who communicated with Maximian gave true baptism, why is the baptism of the world of lands accused? But if he gave false baptism, when he communicated with Maximian, why were those whom he baptized in the schism of Maximian received with him, and no one in your party rebaptized them?
