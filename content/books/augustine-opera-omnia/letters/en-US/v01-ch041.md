# Letter 41

To the most blessed lord, venerably to be received, and most sincerely most dear brother and fellow priest, pope Aurelius, Alypius and Augustine send greeting in the Lord

**1.** *Our mouth has been filled with joy, and our tongue with exultation*[^1], your letter announcing that your holy thought, with the help of the Lord who inspired it, has been brought to effect, concerning all our ordained brothers, and especially concerning the discourse of the presbyters, which is poured forth in your presence before the people; through whose tongues your love cries out with a greater voice in the hearts of men than they in their ears: thanks be to God. For what better thing should we both bear in mind, and bring forth with the mouth, and express with the pen, than, thanks be to God? This can neither be said more briefly, nor heard more joyfully, nor understood more grandly, nor done more fruitfully. Thanks be to God, who both enriched you with so faithful a heart toward your sons, and brought out into the light that which you had in the inmost part of your soul, where the human eye does not penetrate, granting you not only to will well, but also things in which what you willed could appear. So plainly let it be, let it be: *let these works shine before men, that they may see*, rejoice, *glorify the Father who is in heaven*[^2]. May you delight in such things in the Lord: may he deign to hear you praying for them, whom you do not disdain to hear speaking through them. Let us go, let us walk, let us run in the way of the Lord; let the little ones be blessed with the great, *made glad among those who say to them: we will go into the house of the Lord*[^3]: let those go before, and let these follow, having become *imitators* of them, *as they too of Christ*[^4]. Let the journey of the holy ants grow fervent, let the works of the holy bees be fragrant, let fruit be borne with endurance and with safety, persevering even to the end. *And may the Lord not allow us to be tempted beyond what we can bear, but with the temptation may he also make a way out, that we may be able to endure*[^5].

**2.** Pray for us, you who are worthy to be heard; since with so great a sacrifice you approach God, of most sincere love and of his praise in your works: pray that these things may shine in us too; since he whom you pray knows with how great a joy of ours they shine in you. These are our prayers, by these the multitudes of consolations *according to the multitude of our sorrows in our heart gladden our soul*[^6]. So it is, because so it was promised; so it will be, what remains, as it was promised. We beseech you, by him who granted you these things, and flooded the people whom you serve with this blessing through you, that you order the individual discourses of theirs which you wish, written down and corrected, to be sent to us. For I too do not neglect what you ordered, and about the seven rules or keys of Tyconius, as I have often already written, I await to learn what seems to you. We much commend brother Hilarinus, archiater and chief man of Hippo. For about brother Romanus, what you are busy about we know, and nothing is to be asked except that the Lord help you on his behalf. Amen.

[^1]: See Ps 126:2 (125:2)
[^2]: See Matt 5:16
[^3]: See Ps 122:1 (121:1)
[^4]: See 1 Cor 11:1
[^5]: See 1 Cor 10:13
[^6]: See Ps 94:19 (93:19)
