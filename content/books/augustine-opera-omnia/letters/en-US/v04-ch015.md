# Letter 223

TO AUGUSTINE, A LORD DESERVEDLY VENERABLE AND TRULY MOST BLESSED HOLY FATHER, QUODVULTDEUS, A DEACON.

**1.** *One reminder, indeed, of Your Reverence, which you deigned to send by an ecclesiastic, I have received. For the one which Your Beatitude indicated was sent earlier, through the honorable man Philocalus, has not yet reached me. But although I have always been conscious of my own sins, now I have plainly recognized, in obtaining that gift which I begged for, that my person is an impediment to the whole Church. But I entirely trust that he who, through the grace of his Only-begotten, deigned to blot out the crimes of the human race, will not permit my person to prevail to the ruin of all, but rather will make grace overflow where sin abounded*[^1]*, lord deservedly venerable and truly most blessed holy father. I myself, not ignorant, foretold the difficulty of the work which I suppliantly begged to be supplied through your beneficence for the instruction of us most unskilled; but from the abundance of the divine fountain which the Lord has granted you, I presumed with a truthful heart.*

**2.** *For even if Philastrius and Epiphanius, venerable bishops, are recorded to have written something of this kind—which without doubt escapes me among other things, indeed like everything—I do not think, nevertheless, that they applied this care and diligence, namely to add to each opinion the contrary opinions as well, and to subjoin the rites; and then, finally, that work of either of them, of whatever kind it may be, does not perhaps have the brevity I desire. In vain, too, is Greek eloquence assigned to a man who has not learned Latin; because I asked not only for counsel but also for help. But why should I admonish Your Reverence about the difficulty, and even the obscurity, of translators, when you yourself judge this better and fully? There is added to the case that some heresies too are taught to have emerged even after their death, of none of which they made any mention.*

**3.** *Wherefore I flee to the special patronage of your piety, and with my own voice, but with a universal desire, I appeal to the sacred breast of your piety, ready for mercy; setting aside foreign flavors, with the text of my former letter considered, do not deny African bread—which our province is accustomed to have as something special—seasoned even with heavenly manna, to one knocking late*[^2] *and suffering hunger. For indeed I too will not cease to knock until you yourself grant it; so that what privilege does not obtain—which is nothing of merits—at least unwearied importunity may merit.*

[^1]: See Rom 5:20
[^2]: See Luke 11:5-8
