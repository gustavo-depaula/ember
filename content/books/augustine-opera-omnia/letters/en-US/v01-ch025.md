# Letter 25

To the lord, brother of one mind and venerable, Augustine, Paulinus and Therasia, sinners

**1.** *The love of Christ which urges us on*[^1], and binds together those who are absent through the unity of faith, has itself, shame put aside, given me the confidence to write to you: and has brought you, through your letters, into my inmost being—letters both overflowing with scholarly riches and sweet with heavenly honeycombs, which, as medicine and nourishment to my soul, I keep meanwhile in the five books that we received as the gift of our blessed and venerable bishop Alypius, not only for our instruction but also for the benefit of the Church of many cities. These books, then, I now have for reading; in these I delight myself; from these I take food, not that which perishes[^2], but that which works the substance of eternal life through our faith, by which we are incorporated in Christ Jesus our Lord: since our faith, which, neglecting the visible, gapes for the invisible[^3], is strengthened, through love, believing all things according to the truth of almighty God, by the writings and examples of the faithful. O truly the salt of the earth[^4], with which our hearts are seasoned, lest they vanish away in the error of the age! O lamp worthily set above the lampstand[^5] of the Church, which, pouring forth far and wide over the catholic cities a light fed from the sevenfold wick with the oil of gladness, scatters the dense fogs of the heretics, and clears the light of truth from the confusion of darkness by the splendor of your enlightening discourse!

**2.** *You see, brother of one mind, admirable and to be received in Christ the Lord, how familiarly I have come to know you, with what amazement I admire you, with how great a love I embrace you—I who daily enjoy the conversation of your letters and feed on the breath of your mouth. For I might deservedly call your mouth a pipe of living water and a vein of the eternal fountain, because in you Christ has been made a fountain of water leaping up into eternal life*[^6]. With desire of him my soul has thirsted in you[^7], and my land has longed to be inebriated with the abundance of your river[^8]. And so, since with this Pentateuch of yours against the Manichees you have armed me sufficiently, if you have prepared any defenses against the other enemies of the catholic faith too (since our enemy, who has a thousand arts of harming, must be assailed with weapons as various as the ambushes by which he attacks), I ask you to bring out for me from your armory, and not to refuse to confer on me the weapons of righteousness. For I am a toiling sinner, even now under a great burden, a veteran in the number of sinners, but to the eternal king a new recruit in incorporeal warfare. Wretched, until now I have admired the wisdom of the world, and through useless letters and a reprobate prudence I have been foolish and mute before God. After I had grown old among my enemies[^9] and become vain in my thoughts[^10], I lifted up my eyes to the mountains, looking to the precepts of the law and the gifts of grace: whence help came to me from the Lord[^11], who, not repaying according to our iniquities[^12], enlightened the blind, loosed the fettered[^13], humbled one wrongly raised up, that he might raise up one piously humbled.

**3.** *I follow, then, with steps not yet even, the great footsteps of the just, if by your prayers I may be able to lay hold of that in which I have been laid hold of by the mercies of God. Guide, then, a little child creeping on the ground, and teach him to walk in your steps. For I do not want you to consider me by the age of my bodily birth rather than of my spiritual emergence. For my age according to the flesh is now that which his was who was healed by the Apostles in the Beautiful Gate, by the power of the word*[^14]. But in the births of the soul, my time is still that of that infancy which, slain by the wounds aimed at Christ[^15], with worthy blood ran before the victim of the Lamb, and gave the first omen of the Lord's passion. And therefore, as one still an infant in the word of God and a suckling in spiritual age, nourish me with your words, gaping for the breasts of faith, wisdom, and love. If you consider our common office, you are a brother; if the maturity of your talent and senses, you are a father to me, even though you are perhaps younger in age, because a hoary prudence has advanced you, young as you are, to the maturity of merit and the honor of elders. Cherish, then, and strengthen me in the sacred Scriptures and in spiritual studies—me who am, as I said, new in this season, and on this account, after long perils, after many shipwrecks, unskilled in practice, scarcely yet emerging from the waves of the age—you who, already set on the solid shore, receive me into a safe bosom, that in the harbor of salvation, if you think it worthy, we may sail together. Meanwhile, as I strive to escape from the perils of this life and the deep of sins, sustain me by your prayers as if by a plank, that I may escape from this world as if naked from a shipwreck.

**4.** *For I have taken care to relieve myself of my burdens, and to strip off the garments that weigh me down, so that, unencumbered, at Christ's bidding and with his help, I may swim across this billowy sea of the present life, which separates us from God by the sins that bark between, with every covering of the flesh and care for the morrow*[^16]. Nor do I boast that I have accomplished this, for even if I could boast, I would boast in the Lord[^17], whose it is to perfect what it is given us to will[^18]: but my soul still longs to desire the judgments of the Lord[^19]. See when he attains the will of God in effect, who still desires to desire it. Yet, as far as is in me, I have loved the beauty of the holy house[^20], and, as far as was in me, I had chosen to be lowly in the house of the Lord[^21]. But he to whom it was pleasing to set me apart from my mother's womb[^22] and to draw me from the friendship of flesh and blood to his grace, was also pleased to raise me, destitute of all good merit, up from the earth[^23] and to lead me out from the pit of miseries and from the mire of dregs[^24], that he might place me with the princes of his people[^25] and set my portion in your lot, that, you supplying my merits, I might be made equal, joined to you in office.

**5.** *By a presumption, then, not my own, but by the good pleasure and ordinance of the Lord, claiming for myself the bond of your Fraternity, I, unworthy of so great an honor, deem myself worthy of it; because I know for certain, by your holiness, that you are wise with truth—not to be high-minded, but to consort with the lowly*[^26]. And therefore I hope that you will receive promptly and inwardly the love of our lowliness, which indeed I trust you have already received through the most blessed priest Alypius, our father (since he deigns to be such). For he without doubt gave you an example, in himself, of loving us before acquaintance and beyond our merit, who, by the spirit of true love, which everywhere both penetrates and is poured out, was able both to see us, unknown to him and separated by a long interval of land or sea, by directing his gaze, and to reach us by addressing us. He gave us the first tokens of his affection, and the pledges of your love, in the aforesaid worthy gift of books. And with how great expense of zeal he strove that we might not be able to love your Holiness moderately, having come to know it not only by his words but more fully by your own eloquence and faith, we believe the same man took such pains, so that in turn, by imitation of him, you might love us very much. The grace of God be with you, as it is, forever, we wish, brother in Christ the Lord of one mind, venerable, most longed-for: we greet with much affection of unanimous brotherhood your whole household, and every companion and emulator of your holiness in the Lord. The one loaf, which as a token of unanimity we have sent to your Charity, we ask that you bless by receiving it.
