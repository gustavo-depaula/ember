# Letter 21

To the most blessed and venerable lord, in the sight of the Lord most dear by sincere love, his father Bishop Valerius, Augustine the presbyter sends greeting in the Lord

**1.** Before all things I ask that your religious prudence consider that there is nothing in this life, and especially at this time, easier, more pleasant, and more acceptable to men than the office of bishop, or presbyter, or deacon, if the matter is handled perfunctorily and with flattery; but nothing before God more wretched, more sorrowful, and more damnable. And again, that there is nothing in this life, and especially at this time, more difficult, more laborious, more dangerous than the office of bishop, or presbyter, or deacon; but before God nothing more blessed, if one serves as a soldier in the way our commander bids. But what that way is, I learned neither in my boyhood nor in my youth: and at the very time when I had begun to learn, force was done to me—deservedly for my sins (for what else am I to think, I do not know)—so that the second place at the helm was handed over to me, who did not know how to hold an oar.

**2.** But I think my Lord wished to correct me in this way for this reason: that, before I had experienced what is done there, I dared, as though more learned and better, to reprove the sins of many sailors. And so, after I was sent into the midst of it, then I began to feel the rashness of my reproofs; although even before I judged this ministry most dangerous. And from this came those tears which some brothers noticed me shedding in the city at the time of my ordination; and not knowing the causes of my grief, they consoled me, with what words they could—words that did not at all touch my wound—yet with good intent. But I experienced far more, and much more amply, than I had thought: not because I saw any new waves or storms which I had not known before, or had not heard of, or had not read of, or had not thought of; but because I did not at all know my own skill and strength for avoiding or enduring them, and judged them to be of some account. But the Lord laughed at me, and willed by the very facts to show me to myself.

**3.** But if he did this not by condemning but by pitying—for this at least I hope, now that my sickness has become known—I ought to search through all the medicines of his Scriptures, and by praying and reading to act so that fitting health may be granted to my soul for such dangerous affairs; which I did not do before, because I did not even have the time. For I was ordained at the very time when we were thinking about that very time of leisure for coming to know the divine Scriptures, and wished so to arrange ourselves that we might have leisure for this business. And, what is true, I did not yet know what I lacked for such a work as now tortures and crushes me. But if for this reason I have learned in the matter itself what is necessary for a man who ministers the sacrament and the word of God to the people, so that I am now not permitted to attain what I have recognized I do not have: do you then bid me perish, father Valerius? Where is your love? Surely you love me? Surely you love the very Church to which you wished me thus to minister? And yet I am certain that you love both me and her. But you think me fit, while I know myself better—I who would not even have known myself, had I not learned by experience.

**4.** But perhaps your Holiness will say: I would like to know what is lacking to your training. But there are so many things that I could more easily enumerate what I have than what I desire to have. For I would dare to say that I know, and hold with full faith, what pertains to our salvation. But this very thing—how am I to minister it for the salvation of others, not seeking what is useful to me, but what is useful to many, that they may be saved[^1]? And there are perhaps some counsels—indeed, it is not to be doubted that there are—written in the holy Books, by which, when they are known and grasped, the man of God may minister in ecclesiastical affairs in a more orderly way, or at least, amid the hands of the wicked, may either live with a healthier conscience or die so that that life is not lost, for which alone humble and meek Christian hearts sigh. But how can this come about, except, as the Lord himself says, *by asking, seeking, knocking*[^2]—that is, by praying, reading, lamenting? For this business I wanted to obtain, through the brothers, a little time, until Easter as it were, from your most sincere and venerable Charity, and now through these prayers I ask it.

**5.** For what shall I answer to the Lord my judge? I could not now seek these things, because I was hindered by ecclesiastical affairs? If then he should say to me: *Wicked servant*[^3], if some estate of the Church were to suffer a slanderer, on the gathering of whose fruits great labor is spent—if, the field I have watered with my blood being neglected, you could do something for it before the judge of the earth, would you not, with everyone consenting and some even bidding and compelling, set out, and, if it were judged against you, even cross the sea? And in this way no complaint would call back your absence of a year or even more, lest another possess land necessary not for the soul but for the body of the poor, whose hunger nevertheless my living trees would much more easily, and to me more gratefully, satisfy if they were diligently cultivated? Why, then, do you plead that you lacked leisure of time for learning my agriculture? Tell me what I am to answer, I beg you. Or do you perhaps want me to say: Old Valerius, while he believed me to be furnished with all things, the more he loved me, the less did he permit me to learn these things?

**6.** Attend to all these things, old Valerius, I beseech you by the goodness and severity of Christ, by his mercy and judgment, by him who inspired in you so great a love toward us that we dare not offend you even for the gain of our soul. And yet you make God and Christ a witness to me of your innocence and love, and of the sincere affection you have toward us, as though I could not swear to all these things. I therefore implore that very love and affection, that you have mercy on me and grant me, for what I have asked, as much time as I have asked, and help me with your prayers, that my desire be not empty, nor my absence unfruitful to the Church of Christ and to the benefit of my brothers and fellow servants. I know that the Lord does not despise that love praying for me, especially in such a cause; and, accepting it as a sacrifice of sweetness, he will perhaps, in a shorter time than I have asked, give me back furnished with most salutary counsels from his Scriptures.

[^1]: 1 Cor 10:33
[^2]: Matt 7:7
[^3]: See Matt 25:26
