# Letter 88

TO JANUARIUS, OF THE CATHOLIC CLERGY OF THE REGION OF HIPPO REGIUS.

**1.** Your clergy and Circumcellions rage against us with a persecution of a new kind and unheard-of cruelty. Who, if they were rendering evil for evil, would even so act against the law of Christ. But now, all our deeds and yours being considered, we are found to suffer what is written: *They repaid me evil for good*[^1]; and in another psalm: *With those who hated peace I was peaceable; when I spoke to them, they fought against me without cause*[^2]. For since you are established at so great an age, we judge that you know very well that the party of Donatus, which was first at Carthage called the party of Majorinus, of its own accord accused Caecilianus, then bishop of the Church of Carthage, before that ancient emperor Constantine. But lest perhaps either your gravity has forgotten this, or you dissemble that you know it, or even—which we do not think—you perhaps do not know it, we insert in these our letters a copy of the report of the proconsul Anulinus, whom the party of Majorinus then petitioned, that those crimes which it charged against Caecilianus might be sent by the same proconsul to the said emperor.  
**TO OUR LORDS THE THREE AUGUSTI AND THE THREE CAESARS. ANULINUS, MAN OF DISTINCTION, PROCONSUL OF AFRICA.**

**2.** *The heavenly writings* *of your Majesty, received and adored, my devotion took care to make known, in the records* *of my smallness, to Caecilianus and to those who act under him and are called clergy; and it exhorted them that, unity having been made by the consent of all, since they appear to have been freed by the indulgence of your Majesty from every public burden whatsoever, they should, with the holiness of the Catholic law preserved, serve divine things with the reverence due. But after a few days there arose certain men who, having gathered to themselves a multitude of the people, thought that Caecilianus must be contradicted, and who offered to my judgment a bundle sealed in leather and a document without seal, and earnestly demanded that I direct them to the sacred and venerable court of your Divinity; which my smallness took care to direct, Caecilianus remaining in his state, with their acts subjoined, so that your Majesty may be able to discern all things. Two documents have been transmitted, the one inscribed on leather thus: "The document of the Catholic Church, of the crimes of Caecilianus, handed over by the party of Majorinus"; likewise another, without seal, attached to the same leather, given on the seventeenth day before the calends of May, at Carthage, our lord Constantine Augustus being consul for the third time.*

**3.** After this report was sent to him, the Emperor ordered the parties to come to an episcopal judgment to be held in the city of Rome; where, how the case was pleaded and finished, and Caecilianus judged innocent, the ecclesiastical records indicate. Now surely, after the peaceful conduct of the episcopal judgment, all the obstinacy of contention and animosity ought to have been extinguished. But again your forebears returned to the Emperor, and complained that it had not been rightly judged, nor the whole case heard. Hence he gave another episcopal judgment to be held in the city of Arles in Gaul, where many of yours, having condemned the vain and diabolical dissension, returned to concord with Caecilianus; but others, most obstinate and most litigious, appealed to the same emperor. Afterward he too, compelled, terminated the episcopal case, examined between the parties, and was the first to establish a law against your party, that the places of your assemblies should be claimed for the treasury: of all which matters, if we wished to insert the documents, we would make the letter exceedingly long. Yet this must in no way be passed over: how the case of Felix of Aptungi—whom, in the council of Carthage, your fathers said to be the fount of all evils, on the authority of Secundus, primate of Tigisis—was, with your people pressing before the Emperor, discussed and finished by a public judgment. For the said Emperor testifies in his letters that your people were before him as accusers and assiduous petitioners in this very case, a copy of which we have written below.  
**THE EMPERORS AND CAESARS, FLAVIUS CONSTANTINUS MAXIMUS, AND VALERIUS LICINIANUS LICINIUS, TO PROBIANUS, PROCONSUL OF AFRICA.**

**4.** *Aelianus, your predecessor, rightly—while the most perfect man, Verus, vicar of the prefects, was then held by an unfavorable state of health throughout our Africa, and he discharged the duties of that office—believed that, among other things, this business too, or rather the ill will which seems to have been stirred up concerning Caecilianus, bishop of the Catholic Church, must be recalled to his examination and decision. For when he had already caused to be present Superius the centurion, and Caecilianus the magistrate of the people of Aptungi, and Saturninus the ex-curator, and Calibius the younger, curator of the same city, and Solus the public slave of the above-written city, he gave a competent hearing: so that, when it had been objected against Caecilianus that the episcopate seemed to have been conferred on him by Felix, against whom the betrayal and burning of the divine Scriptures seemed to be charged, it was established that Felix was innocent of it. Finally, when Maximus contended that Ingentius, a decurion of the city of Ziqua, had falsified a letter of Caecilianus from the duumvir, we saw that same Ingentius, suspended on the basis of the acts which were at hand, and therefore by no means tortured, because he had asserted himself to be a decurion of the city of Ziqua. Wherefore we wish that you send that same Ingentius, under a suitable escort, to my court of Constantine Augustus, so that, to those who are now acting and do not cease to petition day by day, hearing and standing by in presence, it may appear and be made known that they wished in vain to procure ill will against the bishop Caecilianus, and to rise up violently against him. For thus it will come about that, with contentions of this kind set aside, as is fitting, the people may, without any dissension, serve their own religion with the veneration due.*

**5.** Since you see that these things are so, what is the reason that you stir up ill will against us concerning the commands of the emperors which are established against you, when you yourselves rather did all this beforehand? If the emperors ought to command nothing in these cases, if this care ought not to pertain to Christian emperors, who was it that pressed your forebears to send the case of Caecilianus to the Emperor through the proconsul, and to accuse again before the Emperor a bishop against whom, in his absence, you had already passed sentences in some fashion—so that, with him pronounced innocent, they might contrive other slanders against his ordainer Felix before the same emperor? And now what else lives but the very judgment of the elder Constantine himself against your party, which your forebears chose, which they extorted by assiduous petitions, which they preferred to the episcopal judgment? If the imperial judgments displease you, who first compelled the emperors to be roused to them for you? For you now clamor against the Catholic Church, in those things which are decreed against you by the emperors, just as if those who were sent to be consumed by the same lions—by which they themselves first wished him to be consumed—wished to clamor against Daniel, who had been freed[^3]: for it is written: *There is no difference between the threats of a king and the wrath of a lion*[^4]. The slanderous enemies compelled Daniel to be sent into the lions' den; his innocence overcame their malice; he was lifted out from there unharmed; they themselves, sent there, perished. Likewise your forebears cast Caecilianus and his fellowship to be consumed by the royal wrath, whose innocence having been freed, you suffer from the same kings the same things which yours wished those men to suffer; since it is written: *He who prepares a pit for his neighbor will himself fall into it*[^5].

**6.** You have, then, no ground to complain about us; and yet the gentleness of the Catholic Church had altogether rested even from these commands of the emperors, had not your clergy and Circumcellions—disturbing and devastating our peace by their most monstrous wickednesses and frenzied violences—compelled these things to be recalled and set in motion against you. For before those more recent laws, of which you now complain, had come into Africa, they laid ambushes for our bishops on the roads, battered our fellow-clergy with most monstrous blows, inflicted most grievous blows on laymen also, and set fires to their buildings. A certain priest too—because by his own free will he chose the unity of our communion—they seized from his house, and, having most savagely beaten him at their pleasure, rolled him even in a muddy pool, clothed him with reeds, and, in the pomp of their outrage, displayed him as a thing to be grieved over by some, to be laughed at by others; then, leading him off wherever they wished, they scarcely released him after twelve days. Wherefore, when Proculeianus was summoned by the municipal records by our bishop, and had dissembled about investigating the case, and was again immediately summoned, he stated in the records that he would say nothing further. And today those who did this are your priests, still terrifying us besides, and persecuting us as they can.

**7.** Yet about these injuries and persecutions which the Catholic Church in our region then endured, our bishop did not complain to the emperors. But a council having been held, it was decided that you should be peacefully approached, so that, if it could be done, you might have a conference among yourselves, and, with the error removed, brotherly love might rejoice in the bond of peace. And in that very approach, what Proculeianus first answered—that you would hold a council, and there see what you ought to answer; then what he afterward, when on account of his promise he was again approached, stated in the records, refusing a peaceful conference—let the records themselves instruct your Gravity. Then, since the monstrosity, most well known to all, of your clergy and Circumcellions did not cease, the case was pleaded; Crispinus was judged a heretic, and was not, through Catholic gentleness, permitted to be struck with the penalty of ten pounds of gold which had been established against heretics by the emperors, and yet he thought he should appeal to the emperors. That his appeal was answered as it was—did not the preceding wickedness of your people, and that very appeal of his, extort that it should be done? And yet even after that rescript, with our bishops interceding before the Emperor, he was not fined with the same condemnation of gold. But from a council our bishops sent legates to the court, who should obtain that not all the bishops and clergy of your party should be held to the same condemnation of ten pounds of gold, which was established against all heretics, but only those in whose places the Catholic Church suffered any violences from yours. But when the legates came to Rome, the horrible and most recent scars of the Catholic bishop of Bagai had already moved the Emperor, so that such laws were sent as indeed were sent. When these had come into Africa, since surely you had begun to be urged not toward evil, but toward good, what ought you to have done but also to send to our bishops, so that, in the way you yourselves had been approached, you might be approached by them, and rather, by a conference, the truth might appear?

**8.** But not only have you not done this; your people now do us worse evils. They not only batter us with clubs and cut us with iron; they even, with an incredible inventiveness of crime, put lime mixed with vinegar into our eyes to extinguish them. Plundering our houses besides, they have fabricated for themselves huge and terrible weapons, with which, armed, they run about through various places, threatening and panting after slaughters, robberies, fires, blindings. By these things we have been compelled to complain to you first of all, that your Gravity may consider how many of you—indeed, all of you, who say you suffer persecution—sit secure on your possessions and others' under those laws of the Catholic emperors, as if terrible, while we suffer such unheard-of evils from yours. You say you suffer persecution; and we are cut by your armed men with clubs and iron. You say you suffer persecution; and our houses are devastated by your armed men with plundering. You say you suffer persecution; and our eyes are extinguished by your armed men with lime and vinegar. Besides, if they bring any deaths upon themselves of their own accord, they wish these to be odious to us, glorious to themselves. What they do to us, they do not impute to themselves; and what they do to themselves, they impute to us. They live like robbers, they die like Circumcellions, they are honored like martyrs; and yet we have never heard even of robbers blinding those whom they plundered. They carry off the slain from the light; they do not take the light from the living.

**9.** We meanwhile, if ever we hold any of yours, keep them unharmed with great love, speak to them, and read to them all the things by which that very error is convicted which separates brothers from brothers; we do what the Lord commanded through the prophet Isaiah, saying: *Hear, you who fear the word of the Lord; say, You are our brothers, to those who hate you and who curse you, so that the name of the Lord may be honored, and may appear to them in joy; but let them themselves be ashamed*[^6]. And so, when some of them consider the evidence of the truth and the beauty of peace, we associate them—not by the Baptism which, as deserters, they had already received like a royal mark, but to the faith which they lacked, and to the love of the Holy Spirit, and to the body of Christ. For it is written: *cleansing their hearts by faith*[^7]; and likewise it is written: *Love covers a multitude of sins*[^8]. But if, either by excessive hardness, or by shame at not bearing the insults of those with whom they boasted so many false things against us and devised so many evils, or rather by fear, lest they suffer with us such things as they formerly did to us, they are unwilling to consent to the unity of Christ, then, just as they were kept unharmed, so they are released by us unharmed: this, as much as we can, we advise even our laymen, that they keep them unharmed, and bring them to us to be corrected and instructed. But some hear us, and do it if they can; others deal with them as with robbers, because they truly suffer them to be such. Some repel their blows, threatening their own bodies, by striking, lest they be struck by them first; some offer those they seize to the judges, and do not spare them even with us interceding, while they dread to suffer monstrous evils from them. In all of which, those men do not lay aside the deeds of robbers, and demand for themselves the honor of martyrs.

**10.** This, then, is our desire, which we lay before your Gravity by these letters, and through the brothers whom we have sent. First, if it can be done, that you confer peacefully with our bishops, so that in whomever it be found, not the men, but the error itself may be removed; that men be not punished, but corrected; that you now be approached, since you previously despised approaching them. For how much better do you do this among yourselves—that you send to the Emperor what you have done, written and signed—than that this be done before the earthly powers, which can only serve the laws already given against you? For your colleagues who had sailed said before the prefects that they had come to be heard. And they named our holy father, the Catholic bishop Valentinus, who was then at the court, saying that they wished to be heard with him: which the judge could not grant them, who was now judging according to the laws which were established against you; nor had that bishop come thus, or received any such mandate from his bishops. How much better, then, can the emperor himself—who is not subject to those same laws, and who has it in his power to pass other laws—when your conference has been recited to him, judge about that whole case, although it was long ago said to have been terminated? But we wish you to confer for this reason, not that the case be again finished, but that to those who do not know, it may be shown already finished. And if your bishops are willing to do this, what do you lose thereby, and do you not rather gain, since your will becomes known, lest your diffidence be deservedly reproached? Or do you perhaps think it is not permitted to be done, when it does not escape you that the Lord Christ spoke even with the devil about the Law[^9]; that with the apostle Paul not only the Jews, but even the philosophers of the Gentiles, of the heresy of the Stoics and Epicureans, conferred[^10]? Or do perhaps those laws of the Emperor not permit you to approach our bishops? Behold, meanwhile, approach your bishops who are in the region of Hippo, where we suffer such great evils from yours. For how much more freely and at liberty do your writings reach us, through your own people, than their weapons?

**11.** Finally, through these very brothers of ours whom we have sent to you, write back such things. But if you do not wish even this, at least hear us together with your people, from whom we suffer such things. Show us the truth, for which you say you suffer persecution, while we suffer so great a cruelty from yours. For if you convict us of being in error, perhaps you will grant us not to be rebaptized by you, thinking it just to grant us this who were baptized by those whom you have condemned by no judgment, as you granted to those whom Felicianus of Musti and Praetextatus of Assuras had baptized over so long a time, when you tried to expel them from the basilicas by the orders of judges, because they communed with Maximianus, with whom they were expressly and by name condemned by you in the council of Bagai. All which we demonstrate by the judicial and municipal records, where you also adduced that very council of yours, while you wished to show the judges that you were expelling your schismatics from the basilicas. And yet you who made a schism from that very offspring of Abraham, in which all the nations are blessed[^11], do not wish to be expelled from the basilicas, not by judges, as you did to your schismatics, but by those very kings of the earth, who, the prophecy being fulfilled, adore Christ, before whom you, accusing Caecilianus, withdrew defeated.

**12.** But if you wish neither to hear nor to teach us, come, or send with us into the region of Hippo, men who may see your armed army; although no soldier has added to the number of his weapons lime and vinegar for the eyes of barbarians. If you wish not even this, at least write to those men, that they no longer do these things, that they now restrain themselves from our slaughters, from robberies, from blinding. We do not wish to say: Condemn them. For you will see for yourselves how those whom we have now shown to be robbers in your communion do not defile you, while those whom you could never show to be traditors defile us. Of all these things, choose what you will. But if you despise our complaints, we shall in no way repent of having wished to act in a peaceful order. The Lord will be present to his Church, so that you rather may repent of having despised our humility.
