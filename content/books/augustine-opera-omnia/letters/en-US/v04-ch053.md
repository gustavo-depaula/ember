# Letter 260

TO AUGUSTINE, A LORD TRULY PRAISEWORTHY AND EXCEEDINGLY TO BE ESTEEMED, AND TO BE PURSUED WITH EVERY KIND OF PRAISE, A FATHER, AUDAX, GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** *I am grateful to Your Beatitude that you gladly received the attempts of my discourse: for then is boldness given to the sons of good faith, when it has been bedewed by the showers of the paternal fountain. I have therefore challenged you, sweet bishop, not that I might receive a tiny libation from your most generous breast, but that I might drink abundantly the huge river of the rich one. I desired the treasure of wisdom, but I received less than I wished—although it ought not to be called less, but a gift, that the oracle of the law conferred Augustine, the consecrator of justice, the restorer of spiritual glory, the dispenser of eternal salvation. The worldly globe is as known to you as it is noted by you; you are as known to it as you are approved. I wish, therefore, to be fed on the flowers of wisdom, and to be watered by draughts of the living fountain: grant to one desiring what will surely profit. For the genital covering of the half-bared oak can be made green, if it merit to be increased by your gradual streams. And so I pledge the presence of my humility not so much by pen as by vow, if I shall reread the writings of Your Venerability as in sight. May the divine clemency protect you for innumerable years, venerable lord.*  
Why has the fountain of the world flowed to me in scant speech?  
Or has it hoped that the hearts apt for its streams are too few?  
Since every mind lies open to the waters, and beholds the eloquent  
help of religion, give grateful showers to the senses,  
which a full faith awaits, hanging from the trunk of Christ.
