# Letter 184/A

To the most beloved lords, and holy sons Peter and Abraham, from Augustine, greetings in the Lord.

**1. 1.** Your holy zeal, by which you think many things must be asked of me, that you may be able to resist contentious impieties not unarmed, justice ought not to spurn, nor can charity. But a diligent answer to all things one letter, however lengthy it be, does not contain. But you should know that in very many of our little works I have already answered, as much as I could, either to all, or to almost all, the things you ask. Which if you read—since I hear that you have so ordered the life by which you serve God, that you have leisure for reading—either all instruction will lie open to you, or I judge not much will be lacking, especially because there is in you the inner Teacher, by whose grace you are such. For how does a man help a man to learn anything, if we are not *taught by the Lord*[^1]? And yet in this letter I will by no means defraud your expectation, as the Lord helps, of a brief answer at least.

**1. 2.** The Lord says: *He who believes and is baptized will be saved; but he who does not believe will be condemned*[^2]. If, then, when little ones are baptized, it is not said in vain but done truly, that they are held among believers, whence also a new offspring are named by the mouth of all Christians; surely, if they do not believe, they will be condemned: and through this, because they themselves added nothing by living badly to original sin, it can be said that in that damnation their punishment is by their merit the least, yet not none. But whoever thinks there will be no diversity of punishments, let him read what is written: *It will be more tolerable for Sodom in the day of judgment than for that city*[^3]. Let not, then, a middle place between the kingdom and punishment be sought for infants by deceivers; but let them pass from the devil to Christ, that is, from death to life, lest the wrath of God remain over them: for from this wrath of God none but the grace of God frees. But what is the wrath of God, but the punishment owed and the vengeance by a just God? for neither is God disturbed by any commotion, as a changeable mind is angered; but that which is called the wrath of God is nothing else than the just punishment of sin, which it is no wonder that it passes into posterity.

**1. 3.** For carnal concupiscence, by which they are sown and conceived, was not before sin, nor would it have been at all, unless a reciprocal punishment, namely the disobedience of his own flesh too, had followed the disobedience of man. By which evil, although the good of marriage uses it well, yet not even nuptial intercourse—that is, the licit and honorable intercourse for the sake of procuring children—can take place without it. But without it it could take place, if human nature had remained in the state in which it was created, not sinning. For the genital members too could, like the rest, be moved to perform their proper work by the nod of the will, not excited by the heat of lust. For who would deny that those words of God, *Increase and multiply*[^4], were not a curse of sins, but a blessing of marriage? Through this concupiscence, then, since Christ was neither sown nor conceived (since the bringing-forth of the Virgin was far otherwise); through this concupiscence, I say, whatever of men is sown, conceived, and born, that it may not be punished, must be reborn. Because, even if it is born of reborn parents, carnal generation cannot grant to this what spiritual regeneration alone granted to them. Just as not only from the seed of the wild olive, but even from the seed of the olive, nothing is born but the wild olive, although the olive is not the wild olive. Many things about these we have said also in our other writings, which I wish you rather to read than that you compel us to repeat the same things.

**2. 4.** But to the unbelievers, who are held by no authority of the Christians' Books, answer is made more laboriously. Nor can their perversity be corrected by the weight of the divine Scripture, against whom rather the Scripture itself, which is more openly accused by them, must surely be defended. And if the Lord helps you too, that you may be able, you nevertheless accomplish little with those whom you desire to be Christians, if you conquer their unbelief by truthful disputations, unless you also ask faith for them by suppliant prayers. For faith itself too, as you know, is a gift of God, who *divides to each a measure of faith*[^5]—and such a gift, that it is necessary that understanding precede it. For the prophet is not deceived who says: *Unless you believe, you will not understand*[^6]. And because, that they might believe, not for the already faithful, but still for the unbelieving Jews, the Apostle prayed, when he said: *Brothers, the good will indeed of my heart, and my prayer to God, is made for them unto salvation*[^7]; for them, namely, who had killed Christ, who surely would kill this man too, if power were given. For such the Lord too, when hanging he was mocked, and blessed Stephen, when he was stoned, prayed.

**3. 5.** Of these unbelievers, indeed, whom we are accustomed to call either Gentiles, or, by a word now in common use, Pagans, since there are two kinds; one of those who prefer the superstitions, as they think, to the Christian religion; the other of those who are bound by no name of religion: I, in certain books which I have entitled On the City of God, of which I think the notice has already reached you, and the rest of which, if the Lord wills, I am laboring to finish amid my occupations, against the first kind of these, which the Apostle marks where he says, *The things that the Gentiles sacrifice, they sacrifice to demons, and not to God*[^8]; or surely where he says, *They worshiped and served the creature rather than the Creator*[^9]; I have composed ten not small volumes, of which the first five refute those who contend that, for attaining or retaining that earthly and temporal felicity of human affairs, the worship not of the one supreme and true God, but of many gods, is necessary. But the other five following are against those who, lifting themselves up too swollenly and inflatedly against the saving doctrine, think to come to the blessedness which is hoped for after this life, even through the worship of demons and many gods. Where their noble philosophers too, in three of these five, but the last books, are refuted by us. The rest, from the eleventh, however many they may be able to be, of which I have already finished three and have the fourth in hand, will contain those things which we hold and believe about the City of God; lest we seem to have wished only to refute the views of others, and not also to assert our own in this work. But that fourth after the ten, which is the fourteenth book of the whole work, will, if the Lord wills, have unraveled all the questions which you set before me in your letter.

**3. 6.** Further, with the other kind of unbelievers, who either believe there is no divine power, or do not believe that human affairs pertain to it, I do not know whether there should be any discourse held about some question of piety; although scarcely anyone in our times is found so foolish, that he would dare to say even in his heart: *There is no God*[^10]. But those other fools are not lacking who have said: *The Lord will not see*[^11]; that is, he does not extend his providence to these earthly things. Nevertheless, in these books, which I wish your charity to read, while the City of God asserts not only that God exists—which is so implanted in nature that scarcely any impiety ever roots it out—but also that he bears the care of human affairs, from the very institution of men up to the blessing of the just with the holy angels, and the damnation of the impious with the malign angels, it will, if God wills, and to whom he wills, become credible.

**3. 7.** This letter, then, must not be burdened further, most beloved. For where you may hope to know through our ministry what you wish, we have said enough; and those same books, if you do not yet have them, that you may be able to have them, we have arranged—as far as our slender means allow—through the holy brother and my fellow presbyter Firmus, who loves you much, and who intimated to us rather diligently that he gives thanks to your mutual love.
