# Letter 132

To the illustrious lord, and deservedly most excellent son Volusianus, Augustine the bishop.

**1.** About your salvation, which I desire to be both in this world and in Christ, I am perhaps not myself unequal to the wishes of your holy mother. Whence, rendering to your merits the office of greeting, I exhort, as I am able, that it not irk you to spend care on the zeal of truly and certainly holy writings. For it is a sincere and solid thing, nor does it solicit the mind with painted eloquence, nor, with any plastering of the tongue, does it rattle something empty and hanging. It moves much one avid, not of words, but of things; and it terrifies much, about to make secure. I especially exhort you to read the tongues of the Apostles; for from these you will be roused to know the Prophets, whose testimonies the Apostles use. But if any question arises for you, whether when you read or when you think, in solving which I may seem necessary, write that I may write back. For this perhaps, with the Lord helping, I shall be more able to do, than, present, to speak such things with you; not only on account of the various occupations both mine and yours (since it does not happen that, when I have leisure, you too have leisure), but even on account of the intruding presence of those who for the most part are not fit for such business, and are delighted more by contests of the tongue than by the lights of knowledge: but what is held in writing is always at leisure for reading, when the reader has leisure; nor does it become burdensome, present, which is taken up when you will, laid down when you will.
