# Letter 236

TO DEUTERIUS, A MOST BLESSED LORD AND VENERABLY MOST DEAR BROTHER AND FELLOW BISHOP, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** I thought I could do nothing better than to write above all to Your Holiness, lest through negligence the enemy lay waste, in your province, the sheepfold of our Lord Jesus Christ—the enemy who does not cease to lie in wait, that he may destroy souls purchased at so great a price. It has been established before us that a certain subdeacon of Malliana, Victorinus, is a Manichee, and was lurking in so sacrilegious an error under the name of a cleric: for he is also now an old man in age. And he was so exposed that even he himself, questioned by me before he was convicted by witnesses, could not deny it. For he already knew that those were so many and such, to whom he had incautiously poured himself out, that, if he tried to deny it, he would appear nothing other than—I do not say most shameless, but—most insane. He confessed indeed that he was a Hearer of the Manichees, not one of the Elect.

**2.** Now those who are called Hearers among them both eat flesh, and till fields, and, if they wish, have wives; none of which things those do who are called the Elect. But the Hearers themselves bend their knees before the Elect, that hands may be laid on them as suppliants, not by their priests alone, or bishops, or deacons, but by any of the Elect. They also adore and pray to the sun and moon with them. On the Lord's day too they fast with them, and they believe with them all the blasphemies by which the heresy of the Manichees is to be detested; namely, denying that Christ was born of a virgin, and not confessing that his flesh was true, but false: and thereby they contend that his Passion too was false, and that there was no resurrection. They blaspheme the patriarchs and the prophets. The law given through Moses the servant of God they say was given not by the true God but by the prince of darkness. They suppose that the souls not only of men but also of cattle are of the substance of God, and are altogether parts of God. Finally, they say that the good and true God fought with the race of darkness, and mingled a part of himself with the princes of darkness, and they assert that this part, defiled and bound throughout the whole world, is purified through the foods of their Elect, and through the sun and the moon: and that what could not be purified of that part of God is, at the end of the age, bound by an eternal and penal chain; so that God is believed not only violable and corruptible and contaminable—God, a part of whom could be led to such great evils—but cannot even, in his entirety, be purified from such great defilement and uncleanness and misery, even at the end of the age.

**3.** These intolerable blasphemies, along with them, this subdeacon, as though a Catholic, not only believed but taught with what powers he could. For he was uncovered as he was teaching, when he trusted himself, as it were, to learners. He asked me indeed, after he confessed himself to be a Hearer of the Manichees, that I would recall him to the way of the truth of Catholic teaching: but, I confess, I vehemently shuddered at his pretense under the appearance of a cleric, and, having coerced him, I took care to have him driven from the city. Nor was this enough for me, unless I also made him known to Your Holiness by my letter, so that, fittingly cast down from the rank of the clergy by ecclesiastical severity, he might become known to all as one to be guarded against. But when he asks for a place of penance, let him then be believed, if he also makes known to you the others whom he knows to be there, not only in Malliana, but in the whole province altogether.
