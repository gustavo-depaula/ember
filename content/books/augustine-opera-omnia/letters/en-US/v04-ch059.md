# Letter 266

TO FLORENTINA, A DISTINGUISHED LADY AND, DESERVEDLY HONORABLE IN CHRIST, A DAUGHTER TO BE RECEIVED, AUGUSTINE, A BISHOP, SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Your holy purpose, and the *chaste fear of the Lord, abiding forever and ever*[^1], ingrafted in your heart, rouses our care for you, not only in prayers to God, but also in admonitions to you yourself, not a little. Which indeed I have done more than once in my letters, which I gave to the mother of your Reverence, to be named by me with due honor. But because she deigned to write back to me this, that you first wished to receive my letters; then at last, if perhaps anything were needful for you from my ministry, which I know I owe, by a free servitude, to your venerable zeal and that of all such people, as much as I can, you would not keep silent, but would suggest it by writing back: behold, I have done what I learned you wished, although not through you; lest I seem to you inhumanely to close the door of confidence: it remains that you yourself bring forth, if you think anything is to be asked of me. For either I know what you have inquired, and will not deny it: or I am so ignorant that I am ignorant with no detriment to faith and salvation, and about this too I will, if I can, make you secure by a reason rendered. Or certainly, if I both am ignorant, and yet it is to be known, either I will obtain from the Lord that I not fail you; for often the duty of imparting is the merit of receiving: or I will so answer you, that you may know, for this very matter which we alike do not know, on whom we ought to knock[^2].

**2.** These things I have prefaced for this reason, lest you hope for certain that you will hear whatever you ask of me, and, when this does not come about, think that I acted boldly rather than prudently, in that I gave you the faculty of asking, if you wish, anything. For I did this not as a perfect teacher, but as one to be perfected along with those to be taught, distinguished lady, and deservedly honorable in Christ, daughter to be received. Indeed, even in those things which I somehow know, I rather desire you to be knowing, than in need of our knowledge. For we ought not, in order to teach what we know, to wish for the ignorance of others: for much better are we all teachable by God[^3]; which surely, in that heavenly fatherland, when what is promised has been completed in us, will be perfected, so that a man may not say to his neighbor: *Know the Lord: for all will know him, as it is written, from the least to the greatest of them*[^4]. And the vice of pride is most anxiously to be guarded against in teaching, which in learning is not so. Hence holy Scripture too admonishes us, saying: *Let every man be swift to hear, but slow to speak*[^5]: and he in the Psalm says: *To my hearing you will give exultation and gladness;* and immediately subjoined: *And the humbled bones will exult*[^6]. For he saw that in hearing humility is most easily preserved, which is difficult in teaching; since it is necessary that the teacher have the higher place, where it is toilsome to obtain that elation not creep in.

**3.** Do you see how we are imperiled, we of whom this is expected, that we be not only teachers, but, since we are men, teach divine things? But the singular solace of our labors and perils is, when you so make progress that you arrive there where you need no man as teacher. But by this peril not only we; for, compared to him of whom I am about to speak, what are we? not only we, then, by this peril, but even that Teacher of the Gentiles testifies that he was imperiled[^7], when he says: *Lest I be exalted by the greatness of my revelations, there was given me a thorn of the flesh* etc.[^8] Hence the Lord himself too, the admirable physician of this swelling: *Do not,* he says, *be called Rabbi by men; for one is your master, Christ*[^9]; which, retaining, the same Teacher of the Gentiles says: *Neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters; but God who gives the increase*[^10]. This he too remembered *who, great as he was among those born of women*[^11], so humbled himself in all things[^12], affirming himself unworthy to carry the sandals of Christ[^13]. For what else does he show where he says: *He who has the bride is the bridegroom; but the friend of the bridegroom stands and hears him, and rejoices with joy on account of the bridegroom's voice*[^14]? This is that hearing, of which I mentioned a little before that it is set down in the Psalm: *To my hearing you will give exultation and gladness, and the humbled bones will exult*[^15].

**4.** Accordingly, know that I rejoice over your faith and hope and love the more certainly, the more solidly, the more soundly, the less you need not only to learn anything from me, but from any man at all. Nevertheless, because, when I was there—you indeed being bashful on account of your age—your good parents, most loving of your good pursuits, deigned to intimate to me with how great an ardor of devotion and true wisdom you burned, and most benevolently asked that I not deny my little work to your instruction, in what was needful; I believed you should be admonished by this letter, according to the above-said options, that you ask what you wish, lest I be superfluous, if I tried to teach what you know: provided only you most firmly hold that, even if you could wholesomely know anything through me, he will teach you who is the inner master of the inner man, who in your heart shows you that what is said is true; because *neither he who plants is anything, nor he who waters, but God who gives the increase*[^16].
