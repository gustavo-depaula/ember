# Letter 208

TO FELICIA, A LADY DESERVEDLY TO BE RECEIVED AND TO BE HONORED IN THE MEMBERS OF CHRIST, A DAUGHTER, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** I do not doubt that your mind is troubled, both for your own faith and for the weakness or iniquity of others; since the holy Apostle, full of the bowels of charity, confesses and says, *Who is weak, and I am not weak? Who is scandalized, and I do not burn?*[^1] Whence I myself too, pricked, and solicitous for your salvation which is in Christ, thought that these letters, whether of consolation or of exhortation, should be given to your Holiness: since in the body of our Lord Jesus Christ, which is his Church and the unity of his members, you have been made most truly a sister to us, who, as an honorable member, are loved in his framework, and live with us by his Holy Spirit.

**2.** Whence I admonish you not to be more gravely disturbed by these scandals, which were for this reason foretold as going to come, that when they came we might remember they had been foretold, and not be greatly moved by them. For the Lord himself in the Gospel thus foretold them: *Woe to the world because of scandals! It must be that scandals come: but woe to that man through whom the scandal comes!*[^2] Who are these men, except those of whom the Apostle says, *seeking their own, not the things of Jesus Christ*[^3]? There are, then, some who hold pastoral chairs in order to provide for the flocks of Christ; but others who sit in them in order to rejoice in their temporal honors and worldly advantages. These two kinds of pastors, some dying, others being born, must necessarily persevere in the Catholic Church itself until the end of the age and until the judgment of the Lord. For if in the times of the Apostles there were such men, among whom, as false brethren, the Apostle groaned and said, *In perils among false brethren*[^4], and did not separate them through pride, but bore with them through forbearance; how much more in our times is it necessary that there be such, when about the time of this age, which draws near to the end, the Lord says most plainly, *Because iniquity shall abound, the charity of many shall grow cold*[^5]? But what follows ought to console and exhort us: *He who shall persevere*, he says, *unto the end, he shall be saved*[^6].

**3.** But as there are pastors good and bad, so also in the flocks themselves there are both good and bad. The good are signified by the name of sheep; the bad are called goats: but they feed mingled together alike, until the Prince of pastors comes, who is called the one Pastor; and, as he himself promised, separates, *as a shepherd separates the sheep from the goats*[^7]. For to us he commanded the gathering, but to himself he reserved the separation: because he ought to separate who knows not how to err. For those who, proud servants, easily dared to separate before the time which the Lord reserved to himself, have rather themselves been separated from catholic unity: for, unclean by schism, whence could they have a clean flock?

**4.** Therefore, that we may remain in unity, and, offended by the scandals of the chaff, not forsake the Lord's threshing floor, but rather persevere as the wheat until the end of the winnowing, and bear the broken-up straw with the firm weight of charity, our Pastor himself in the Gospel admonishes us about the good pastors, that, on account of their good works, we should not place our hope in those very men, but should glorify him who made them such, the Father who is in heaven; and about the bad pastors, whom he wished to signify by the name of Scribes and Pharisees, teaching good things and doing evil.

**5.** For about the good pastors he speaks thus: *You are the light of the world. A city set upon a mountain cannot be hidden; nor do men light a lamp and put it under a bushel, but upon a lampstand, that it may give light to all who are in the house. So let your light shine before men, that they may see your good works, and glorify your Father who is in heaven*[^8]. But about the bad pastors he admonishes the sheep, saying: *They sit upon the chair of Moses. What they say, do; but what they do, do not wish to do: for they say, and do not*[^9]. Hearing these things, the sheep of Christ, even through bad teachers, hear his voice, and do not leave his unity; because the good which they hear them say is not theirs, but his: and therefore the sheep feed secure, because even under bad pastors they are nourished by the Lord's pastures. But they do not do the deeds of the bad pastors; because such works are not his, but theirs. But those whom they see to be good, they not only hear the good things they say, but also imitate the good things they do. Of such was the Apostle, who said, *Be imitators of me, as I also am of Christ*[^10]. He was a light kindled from the everlasting Light, the Lord Jesus Christ himself, and was set on a lampstand, because he gloried in his cross: whence he says, *But far be it from me to glory, except in the cross of our Lord Jesus Christ*[^11]. And because he sought not his own, but the things of Jesus Christ: although he exhorts those whom he had begotten through the Gospel to his imitation, he nevertheless gravely rebukes those who made schisms through the names of the Apostles; and chides those who said, *I am of Paul*: *Was Paul*, he says, *crucified for you? Or were you baptized in the name of Paul?*[^12]

**6.** Hence we understand both that the good pastors seek not their own, but the things of Jesus Christ; and that the good sheep, although they imitate the deeds of the good pastors, nevertheless do not place their hope in those by whose ministry they have been gathered, but rather in the Lord, by whose blood they have been redeemed: so that when perhaps they fall in with bad pastors, who preach his teaching and do their own evil works, they may do what they say, but not do what they do; nor, on account of the sons of iniquity, forsake the pastures of unity. For in the catholic Church—which is enlarged and spread not in Africa alone, like the party of Donatus, but, as it was promised, through all nations, bearing fruit and increasing, as the Apostle says, in the whole world—there are both the good and the bad. But those separated from it, so long as they think against it, cannot be good; because, even if some of them a praiseworthy way of life seems to show good, the very division makes them bad, as the Lord says, *He who is not with me is against me; and he who does not gather with me scatters*[^13].

**7.** Whence I also exhort you, lady deservedly to be received and to be honored in the members of Christ, daughter, that what the Lord has granted you, you faithfully hold; and that you love with your whole heart both him and his Church, who did not permit you to lose or to let perish the fruit of your virginity with the lost. For if you departed from this age separated from the unity of the body of Christ, the preserved integrity of your body would profit you nothing. But God, who is rich in mercy, did toward you what is written in the Gospel; when those invited to the supper of the householder excused themselves, he said among other things to his servants, *Go out into the highways and hedges, and whomever you find, compel them to come in*[^14]. You, then, although you owe a most sincere love even to his good servants, by whose ministry you have been compelled to enter, ought nevertheless to place your hope in him who prepared the banquet, for which you too are solicitous unto eternal and blessed life. For, commending to him your heart, and your purpose, and your holy virginity, and your faith, and hope, and charity, you will not be moved by the scandals which will abound until the end; but, by the stable strength of piety, you will be safe, and glorious in the Lord, by persevering in his unity until the end. But how you have received this solicitude of mine for you, which I have taken care to intimate to you by such letters as I could, make me know by your reply. May the mercy and grace of God ever protect you.
