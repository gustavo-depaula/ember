# Letter 254

TO BENENATUS, A MOST BLESSED AND VENERABLE AND DESIRABLE LORD AND BROTHER AND FELLOW PRIEST, AND TO THE BROTHERS WHO ARE WITH YOU, AUGUSTINE AND THE BROTHERS WHO ARE WITH ME SEND GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** The girl about whom Your Holiness wrote to me is of such a will that, if her age were already mature, she would consent to marriage with no one. But she is of such an age that, if she had a will to marry, she ought not yet to be given or promised to anyone. To this is added that God so protects her in the Church that he protects her against the wicked; not so that she can be handed over to whomever I wish, but so that she cannot be carried off by one by whom she ought not, most beloved lord Benenatus and venerable brother. And so the match which you deigned to suggest does not displease me, if she is to marry: but whether she is to marry, although what she has in her mouth we rather hope for, we are nevertheless now ignorant; because she is of such years that even what she says she wishes—to be a nun—is rather the jest of one prattling than the pledge of one professing. Then she has an aunt, whose husband is the honorable man our brother Felix, with whom, when I had conferred about this matter (for I could not, nor ought to, do otherwise), he received it not at all unwillingly—nay, even rejoiced; but, by the right of friendship, he grieved not inopportunely that nothing had been written to them about it. For perhaps her mother, who now does not appear, will also appear, whose will in handing over a daughter nature, as I think, sets before all; unless the same girl be already of such an age that, by a freer right, she chooses for herself what she wishes. Let your Sincerity also consider this, that if the highest and full power over her marriage were granted to me, and she herself too, now mature and wishing to marry, were to entrust herself to me, with God as judge, to be handed over to whomever I wished; I say thus, and say truly, that this match pleases me, in such a way that, on account of God the judge, I could not reject a better one: whether which is to come is surely uncertain. Wherefore your Charity sees how many things to be considered concur, so that now nothing can at all be promised by me to anyone.
