# Letter 175

To the most blessed and most honorable lord, holy brother Innocent, from Aurelius, Numidius, Rusticianus, Fidentianus, Evagrius, Antonius, Palatinus, Adeodatus, Vincentius, Publianus, Theasius, Tutus, Pannonius, Victor, Restitutus, Rusticus, Fortunatianus, another Restitutus, Ampelius, Ambivius, Felix, Donatianus, Adeodatus, Octavius, Serotinus, Maiorinus, Posthumianus, Crispulus, Victor, another Victor, Leucius, Marianus, Fructuosus, Faustinianus, Quodvultdeus, Candorius, Maximus, Megasius, Rusticus, Rufinianus, Proculus, Severus, Thomas, Januarius, Octavianus, Praetextatus, Sixtus, Quodvultdeus, Penthadius, Quodvultdeus, Cyprianus, Servilius, Pelagianus, Marcellus, Venantius, Didymus, Saturninus, Bizacenus, Germanus, Germanianus, Inventius, Maiorinus, Inventius, Candidus, Cyprianus, Aemilianus, Romanus, Africanus, Marcellinus, we who were present at the council of the Church of Carthage.

**1.** When, according to custom, we had come solemnly to the Church of Carthage, and a synod gathered from us for various causes was being held, our fellow presbyter Orosius gave us a letter of our holy brothers and fellow priests Heros and Lazarus, a copy of which we have determined should be appended to these. When these had been read, then, we recognized Pelagius and Celestius as the authors of a wicked error, and one to be anathematized by us all. Whence it came about that we asked to have reviewed what, almost five years before, had been transacted here at Carthage concerning the name of Celestius. When this had been recited, as your holiness will be able to gather from what is appended, although a manifest judgment too was established, by which at that time, by episcopal judgment, so great a wound seemed to be cut from the Church: nevertheless we decreed this by common deliberation, that the authors of a persuasion of this kind—although the same Celestius is also said afterward to have attained to the presbyterate—unless they most openly anathematize these things, ought themselves to be anathematized; so that, if not their own, at least the health of those who have been or can be deceived by them may be procured, the sentence which has been passed against them being known.

**2.** This action, then, lord brother, we have thought should be intimated to your holy charity, so that to the statutes of our mediocrity may be applied also the authority of the apostolic See, for guarding the salvation of many, and even correcting the perversity of certain ones. For these people, by their damnable disputations, act so that, by not defending but rather extolling free choice into a sacrilegious pride, they leave no place for the grace of God, by which we are Christians, by which also the very choice of our will is truly made free, while it is freed from the domination of carnal concupiscences, the Lord saying: *If the Son frees you, then you will be truly free*[^1]; which help faith obtains, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord. But these people assert, as we have learned from brothers who have even read their books, that the grace of God is to be reckoned in this: that he instituted and created such a nature of man as can, through its own will, fulfill the law of God, whether written naturally in the heart or given in letters; and that this same law too pertains to the grace of God, because God gave it as a help to men.

**3.** But that grace by which, as has been said, we are Christians, whose preacher the Apostle is, saying: *For I delight in the law of God according to the inner man: but I see another law in my members fighting against the law of my mind, and taking me captive under the law of sin which is in my members. Wretched man that I am! Who will free me from the body of this death? The grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord*[^2]—this they are wholly unwilling to acknowledge; yet they do not dare openly to attack it: but what else do they do, when they do not cease to persuade animal men, who do not perceive the things that are of the Spirit of God, that human nature alone can suffice them for working and perfecting righteousness and fulfilling the commandments of God? not attending to what is written: *The Spirit helps our weakness*[^3]; and: *It is not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who has mercy*[^4]; and that *we are one body in Christ, but individually members one of another, having diverse gifts according to the grace which has been given us*[^5]; and: *By the grace of God I am what I am, and his grace in me was not empty, but I labored more abundantly than all of them; yet not I, but the grace of God with me*[^6]; and: *Thanks be to God, who has given us the victory through our Lord Jesus Christ*[^7]; and: *Not that we are sufficient to think anything as of ourselves; but our sufficiency is from God*[^8]; and, *We have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the eminence of power may be God's, and not from us*[^9]; and innumerable such things, which, if we wished to gather them from all the Scriptures, a volume would not suffice. And we fear lest, by recalling to you these very things which you preach with greater grace from the apostolic See, we may seem to act unfittingly: but we do it for this reason, because, since we are weaker, wherever each of us is thought more attentive in preaching the word of God, the more frequently and the more boldly we suffer them rising up against us.

**4.** If, then, Pelagius shall justly have seemed to your veneration too to be absolved by the episcopal proceedings which are said to have been drawn up in the East; nevertheless the error itself and the impiety, which now has many assertors scattered through diverse places, must be anathematized even by the authority of the apostolic See. For let your holiness consider, and have compassion on us with pastoral bowels, how pestiferous and deadly to the sheep of Christ is what necessarily follows from their sacrilegious disputations: that we ought not even to pray that we enter not into temptation, which the Lord both admonished his disciples and placed in the prayer he taught; or that our faith fail not, which he testified he had asked for the apostle Peter. For if these things are placed in the power of the possibility of nature and the choice of the will, who does not see that they are asked of the Lord in vain, and prayed for falsely, when by praying are sought things which are obtained by the sufficient powers of our nature already so constituted? and that the Lord Jesus ought not to have said: *Watch and pray*, but only: Watch, that you enter not into temptation? nor to most blessed Peter, first of the Apostles: *I have prayed for you*, but, I admonish you, or I command and enjoin you, that your faith fail not?

**5.** By the contention of these people there is also contradiction to our blessings, so that we seem to say in vain over the people whatever we pray from the Lord for them, that by living rightly and piously they may please him; or those things which the Apostle prays for the faithful, saying: *I bow my knees to the Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, from whom all fatherhood in heaven and on earth is named, that he may grant you, according to the riches of his glory, to be strengthened with power through his Spirit*[^10]. If, then, we should wish, in blessing, to say over the people, Grant them, O Lord, to be strengthened with power through your Spirit; the disputation of these people contradicts us, affirming that free choice is denied, if this is asked of God which is in our power: "For if we wish to be strengthened with power," they say, "we can, by that possibility of nature which we do not now receive, but received when we were created."

**6.** They also deny that little ones must be baptized for the salvation which is given through the Savior Christ, and thus by this death-bearing doctrine they slay them forever, promising that even if they are not baptized, they will have eternal life, and do not pertain to those of whom the Lord says: *For the Son of man came to seek and to save what had perished*[^11]; because, they say, these have not perished, nor is there anything in them to be saved or redeemed at so great a price: since there is nothing vitiated in them, nothing held captive under the power of the devil, nor was the blood poured out for them which is read to have been poured out for the remission of sins. Although that through the baptism of Christ there is redemption even of little ones, Celestius has already confessed in his little book in the Church of Carthage: but many who are reported to be or to have been their disciples do not cease to affirm, wherever they can, these evils by which they try to overturn the foundations of the Christian faith. Whence, even if Pelagius and Celestius are corrected, or say that they never held these things, and deny that whatever writings have been brought forward against them are their own, nor is there a way by which they may be convicted of falsehood: yet in general, whoever dogmatizes and affirms that human nature can suffice itself for conquering sins and doing the commandments of God, and is in this way found an adversary to the grace of God, which is more evidently declared by the prayers of the saints; and whoever denies that little ones are freed from perdition through the baptism of Christ, and receive everlasting salvation, let him be anathema. But whatever other things are charged against them, we do not doubt that your veneration, when you have examined the episcopal proceedings which are said to have been drawn up in the East in the same case, will judge in such a way that we may all rejoice in the mercy of God. Pray for us, most blessed lord pope.
