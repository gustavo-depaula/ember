# Letter 18

Augustine to Caelestinus

**1.** Oh, that I could constantly say something to you! And that something is this: that we might strip off empty cares and put on useful ones. For as to security, I do not know whether anything is to be hoped for in this world. I wrote, and received no reply. I sent the books against the Manichees, which I was able to send ready and corrected, and nothing of your judgment and reaction to them has been made known to me. Now it is fitting that I ask for them back, and that you return them. I therefore ask that you not put off sending them back with a reply, by which I desire to know what you are doing about them, or what armor you think you still need for overthrowing that error.

**2.** Since indeed I know you, take this something great and brief. There is a nature changeable through places and times, such as body. And there is a nature in no way changeable through places, but changeable only through times, such as the soul. And there is a nature that can be changed neither through places nor through times; this is God. What I have here suggested as changeable in any way is called creation; what is unchangeable, the Creator. But since everything that we say exists, we say to exist insofar as it abides, and insofar as it is one, and since further the form of all beauty is unity: you surely see, in this distribution of natures, what is supreme, what is lowest and yet exists, and what is in the middle, greater than the lowest and less than the supreme. That supreme thing is blessedness itself; the lowest is that which can be neither blessed nor wretched; but that which is in the middle lives wretchedly by inclining toward the lowest, and blessedly by turning toward the supreme. He who believes in Christ does not love the lowest, does not grow proud in the middle, and thus becomes fit to cling to the supreme: and this is the whole of what we are commanded, admonished, and set afire to do.
