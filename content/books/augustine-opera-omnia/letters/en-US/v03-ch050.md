# Letter 201

THE EMPERORS HONORIUS AND THEODOSIUS, AUGUSTI, TO BISHOP AURELIUS, GREETING.

**1.** Some time ago indeed it had been decreed that Pelagius and Celestius, the inventors of an unspeakable doctrine, should be driven from the city of Rome, as certain contagions of catholic truth, lest they pervert the minds of the ignorant by a perverse persuasion. In this our clemency followed the judgment of your Holiness, by which it is established that they were condemned by all in a just examination of sentence. But because the persistent evil of an obstinate crime demanded that the decree be doubled, by a recent sanction too we have decreed that, if anyone, not unaware that they are lurking in any part of the provinces, should delay either to drive them out or to betray them, he should be subject, as an accomplice, to the prescribed penalty.

**2.** But especially for correcting the obstinacy of certain bishops, who either by tacit consent build up their depraved disputations, or do not destroy them by public assault, most dear and most loving father, it will be fitting that the authority of your Holiness press upon them; so that the Christian devotion of all may consent to the abolition of this preposterous heresy. Your Religiousness, then, by suitable writings, will cause all to be admonished, who shall know, by the determination of your Sanctity, that this determination is prescribed for them: that whoever by impious obstinacy shall have neglected to subscribe to the condemnation of the aforesaid men—whereby a pure mind may be made plain—shall, punished by the loss of the episcopate, be barred, expelled forever from the cities, deprived of communion. For since we ourselves, according to the Synod of Nicaea, venerate with sincere confession God the Founder of all things and the Author of our empire, your Holiness will not suffer the men of this detestable sect, devising new and unusual things to the injury of religion, to hide in secret tracts a sacrilege once condemned by public authority. For one and the same is the fault of those who shall have furnished harmful favor, whether by dissembling their connivance or by not condemning, most dear and most loving father. (And in another hand): May the Divinity keep you unharmed for many years. Given on the fifth day before the Ides of June, at Ravenna, in the consulship of Monaxius and Plinta. Given in the same tenor also to the holy bishop Augustine.
