# Letter 66

Augustine to Crispinus

**1.** You ought indeed to have feared God; but because, in rebaptizing the people of Mappala, you wished, like a man, to be feared, why should the royal command not avail in a province, if the command of a provincial availed so much on an estate? If you compare persons, you are a landholder, he an emperor. If you compare places, you are on an estate, he in a kingdom. If you compare causes, he that division may be repaired, you that unity may be divided. But we do not terrify you concerning man. For we could act so that you should pay ten pounds of gold according to the imperial commands. Or do you perhaps not have whence to pay what the rebaptizers are bidden to give, while you spend so much to buy those whom you rebaptize? But we, as I said, do not terrify you concerning man; let Christ rather terrify you. To whom I want to know what you answer, if he should say to you: Crispinus, was your price dear, for buying the fear of the people of Mappala, and my death cheap, for buying the love of all nations? Did that which was counted out from your worldly goods avail more for rebaptizing your tenants, than that which flowed from my side availed for baptizing my peoples? I know that you can hear more, if you lend your ear to Christ, and be admonished from your very possession how impiously you speak against Christ. For if by human right you presume that you firmly possess what you bought with your silver, how much more firmly by divine right does Christ possess what he bought with his blood! And he indeed will possess unshaken the whole, of whom it was said: *He will have dominion from sea to sea, and from the river to the ends of the world of lands*[^1]. But surely how do you trust that you will not lose what you seem to have bought in Africa, you who say that Christ, the whole world lost, has remained in Africa alone?

**2.** Why say much? If the people of Mappala crossed over to your communion of their own will, let them hear us both; so that what we say may be written down, and, subscribed by us, be interpreted to them in Punic, and, the fear of domination removed, let them choose what they will. For from the things we say it will appear whether, compelled, they remain in falsehood, or, willing, hold the truth. For if they do not understand these things, by what rashness did you lead over those who do not understand? But if they do understand, let them hear us both, as I said, and do what they will. If any peoples too have crossed over from you to us, whom you think compelled by their masters, let this be done there too; let them hear us both, and choose what shall please. But if you do not wish this to be done, to whom does it not appear that you do not presume upon the truth? But the wrath of God is to be feared, both here and in the future age. I adjure you by Christ, that you answer these things.

[^1]: Ps 72:8 (71:8)
