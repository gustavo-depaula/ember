# Letter 220

TO BONIFACE, MY LORD AND SON, TO BE PROTECTED AND RULED BY THE MERCY OF GOD UNTO PRESENT AND ETERNAL SALVATION, AUGUSTINE.

**1.** A more faithful man, and one who would have easier access to your ears, to carry my letter, I could never have found than the Lord has now offered—a servant and minister of Christ, the deacon Paul, most dear to us both—that I might say something to you, not about your power and the honor you bear in this evil world; nor about the safety of your corruptible and mortal flesh[^1], since it too is transitory, and how long it may last is always uncertain; but about that salvation which Christ has promised us: who for this reason was here dishonored and crucified, that he might teach us to despise rather than to love the goods of this world, and to love and hope from him for that which he showed in his resurrection. *For he rose from the dead, and dies no more, and death will no longer have dominion over him*[^2].

**2.** I know there is no lack of men who love you according to the life of this world, and according to it give you counsel, sometimes useful, sometimes useless; because they are men, and are wise for the present as best they can, not knowing what may happen the next day. But that your soul may not perish, no one easily counsels you according to God; not because there are none who would do this, but because it is difficult to find when they can speak of these things with you. For I too have always desired, and never found, place or time to deal with you as I ought to deal with a man whom I greatly love in Christ. And you know what sort of man you saw me at Hippo, when you deigned to come to me, that I could scarcely speak, worn out by weakness of body. Now, then, son, hear me, at least speaking to you through a letter, which in your perils I could never send to you, considering the danger to the bearer, and taking care lest my letter reach those to whom I would not want it to come. Hence I ask you to pardon me, if you think I feared more than I ought; yet I have said what I feared.

**3.** Hear me, then—nay, the Lord our God through the ministry of my weakness. Recall what sort of man you were while your former wife, of religious memory, was still in the body, and how, at her recent death, the vanity of this world made you shudder, and how you longed for the service of God. We know, we are witnesses, what you discussed with us at Tubunae concerning your mind and will. We alone were with you, I and brother Alypius. For I do not suppose that the earthly cares with which you have been filled have had such power as to be able utterly to delete this from your memory. Indeed, you desired to leave all the public actions in which you were occupied, and to betake yourself to holy leisure, and to live in that life in which the servants of God, the monks, live. But what called you back from doing so, except that you considered, as we pointed out, how much it profited the churches of Christ that you did what you were doing, if you did it with this intention alone: that, defended from the assaults of the barbarians, they might lead *a quiet and tranquil life,* as the Apostle says, *in all devotion and chastity*[^3]; while you yourself sought nothing from this world except what was necessary for sustaining this life of yours and your people, girded with the belt of a most chaste continence, and among bodily arms more safely and strongly fortified with spiritual arms?

**4.** When, therefore, we were rejoicing that you were in this purpose, you took ship and married a wife: but the voyage was a matter of obedience, which according to the Apostle you owed to the higher powers[^4]; the wife, however, you would not have married, had you not, deserting the continence you had taken up, been overcome by concupiscence. When I learned this, I confess, I was struck with amazement; yet my grief was in some part consoled by my hearing that you had been unwilling to marry her unless she were first made a Catholic; and yet the heresy of those who deny the true Son of God prevailed so far in your house that your daughter was baptized by them. And now, if no false things have been reported to us—would that they were false!—that even handmaids dedicated to God were rebaptized by those same heretics, with what great fountains of tears is so great an evil to be lamented? Men also say—and perhaps they lie—that your wife did not suffice for you, but that you were polluted by intercourse with concubines, I know not whose.

**5.** Of these so many and so great evils that are open to all, which followed from you after you were married, what shall I say? You are a Christian, you have a heart, you fear God: consider yourself the things I do not wish to say, and you will find how great are the evils for which you ought to do penance—on account of which I believe the Lord spares you and frees you from all dangers, that you may do it as it ought to be done; but only if you heed what is written: *Do not delay to be converted to the Lord, nor put it off from day to day*[^5]. You say indeed that you have a just cause, of which I am not the judge, since I cannot hear both sides: but whatever your cause may be—about which there is now no need to inquire or dispute—can you, before God, deny that you would not have come into this necessity had you not loved the goods of this world, which, as a servant of God, such as we knew you to be before, you ought to have utterly despised and counted as nothing; and indeed to take them when offered, that you might use them for devotion, but not so to seek them when denied or assigned elsewhere that on their account you should be led into this necessity—where, when vain things are loved, evils are perpetrated, a few indeed by you, but many on your account; and when those things are feared which harm for a brief time, if they harm at all, those things are committed which truly harm forever?

**6.** To say one thing of these: who does not see that many men cling to you for the protection of your power or safety, who, even if they are all faithful to you, and no plots are feared from any of them, nevertheless desire to attain through you those goods which they too love not according to God but according to the world; and thereby you, who ought to have curbed and restrained your own desires, are compelled to fulfill the desires of others? And for this to come about, it is necessary that many things be done which displease God: nor even so are such desires fulfilled; for they are more easily pruned away in those who love God than satisfied, as they sometimes are, in those who love the world. On account of which divine Scripture says: *Do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world. If anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him: because everything that is in the world is the concupiscence of the flesh, and the concupiscence of the eyes, and the ambition of the age; which is not from the Father, but from the world. And the world passes away, and its concupiscence; but he who does the will of God abides forever, as God also abides forever*[^6]. When, then, will you be able—of so many armed men, whose desire must be fostered, whose ferocity is feared—when, I say, will you be able not, indeed, to satisfy the concupiscence of those who love the world, which can in no way be done, but in some part to feed it, lest the whole perish the more—unless you do the things God forbids and threatens those who do them? On account of which you see so many things laid waste that scarcely anything cheap is now found to be plundered.

**7.** But what shall I say of the devastation of Africa, which the African barbarians are causing, with no one resisting, while you are occupied with such necessities of yours and arrange nothing whereby this calamity may be averted? Who would have believed, who would have feared, that, with Boniface count of the household and of Africa established in Africa with so great an army and power—he who as a tribune with a few allied troops had subdued and terrified and pacified all those very nations—the barbarians would now dare so much, advance so far, lay waste so much, plunder so much, make deserts of so many places that had been full of peoples? Who did not say that whenever you took up the power of the countship, the African barbarians would be not only subdued but even tributaries to the Roman commonwealth? And now you see how the hope of men has been turned to its opposite; nor need I speak with you longer about this, because you can think more of these things than we can say.

**8.** But perhaps you answer to these things that this is rather to be imputed to those who injured you, who repaid your dutiful services not in kind but with the contrary. These causes I can neither hear nor judge: look rather at your own cause, and examine it—the cause you know you have not with any men whatever, but with God; for since you live faithfully in Christ, you ought to fear to offend him. For I attend rather to higher causes, because, for Africa to suffer such great evils, men ought to impute it to their own sins. Nevertheless I would not have you belong to the number of those evil and wicked men through whom God scourges with temporal punishments whom he wills. For to those very wicked men, if they are not corrected, he reserves eternal punishments, who justly uses their malice to bring temporal evils upon others. Attend to God, consider Christ, who bestowed such great goods and endured such great evils. Whoever desire to belong to his kingdom, and to live ever blessedly with him and under him, love even their enemies, do good to those who hate them, and pray for those by whom they suffer persecution[^7]: and if sometimes they apply troublesome severity for discipline, yet they do not lose the most sincere love. If, then, goods have been bestowed on you, though earthly, transitory, by the Roman empire—since it too is earthly, not heavenly, and cannot bestow except what it has in its power—if, then, goods have been conferred on you, do not repay evil for good; but if evils have been inflicted on you, do not repay evil for evil[^8]. Which of these two it is, I neither wish to discuss nor am able to judge; I speak to a Christian: do not repay either evil for good, or evil for evil.

**9.** You will perhaps say to me: "In such great necessity, what do you want me to do?" If you seek counsel from me according to this world—how your transitory safety may be secure, and how the power and wealth you now have may be preserved, or even greater be added—I do not know what to answer you: for these uncertain things can have no certain counsel. But if you consult me according to God, lest your soul perish, and you fear the words of truth, which says, *What does it profit a man if he gains the whole world, but suffers the loss of his own soul*[^9]? then I plainly have something to say; there is with me a counsel for you to hear from me. But what need is there for me to say anything other than what I said above? *Do not love the world, nor the things that are in the world. For if anyone loves the world, the love of the Father is not in him: because all the things that are in the world are the concupiscence of the flesh, and the concupiscence of the eyes, and the ambition of the age; which is not from the Father, but from the world. And the world passes away, and its concupiscence; but he who does the will of God abides forever, as God also abides forever*[^10]. Behold the counsel; seize it, and act. Here let it appear whether you are a brave man; conquer the desires by which this world is loved, do penance for the past evils, when, overcome by those desires, you were dragged along by no good longings. If you take this counsel, if you hold and keep it, you will both attain to those sure goods and pass amid these uncertain things with the salvation of your soul.

**10.** But perhaps you ask me again, how you are to do these things, entangled as you are in such great necessities of this world. Pray strongly, and say to God what you have in the Psalm: *Rescue me from my necessities*[^11]: for these necessities are ended when those desires are conquered. He who heard you, and us for you, that you might be freed from so many and such great perils of visible and bodily wars, where only this life, one day to be ended, is endangered, while the soul does not perish unless it is held captive by malignant desires—he himself will hear you, that you may invisibly and spiritually conquer the inner and invisible enemies, that is, the desires themselves, and so use this world as not using it[^12]; that out of its goods you may do good, not become evil: for these things too are good, and are not given to men except by him who has power over all things heavenly and earthly. But lest they be thought evils, they are given to the good too; lest they be thought great or supreme goods, they are given to the evil too. Likewise they are taken away both from the good, that they may be tested, and from the evil, that they may be tormented.

**11.** For who does not know, who is so foolish as not to see, that the health of this mortal body, and the strength of corruptible members, and victory over enemy men, and temporal honor and power, and the other earthly goods, are given both to the good and to the evil, and taken away both from the good and from the evil? But the salvation of the soul with the immortality of the body, and the strength of righteousness, and victory over enemy desires, and glory and honor and peace forever, are given only to the good. These, then, love; these covet; these seek in every way. To acquire and obtain these, give alms, pour out prayers, practice fasts, as far as you can without harm to your body. But those earthly goods, do not love, however much they abound for you: so use them that out of them you may do many good things, but no evil thing on their account. For all such things will perish; but good works do not perish, even those that are done out of perishing goods.

**12.** For if you had no wife, I would say to you what we also said at Tubunae, that you should live in the holiness of continence: I would add—what we then forbade to be done—that you should now, as far as you could with the peace of human affairs preserved, withdraw yourself from these warlike matters, and be free for that life, in the company of the saints, for which you then desired to be free; where the soldiers of Christ fight in silence, not to kill men, but to overcome *the principalities and powers and spirits of wickedness*[^13], that is, the devil and his angels[^14]. For these are the enemies the saints conquer, whom they cannot see; and yet they conquer those they do not see, by conquering the things they do perceive. But that I should not exhort you to this life, your wife is an impediment, without whose consent it is not lawful for you to live in continence; for although you ought not to have married her after those words of yours at Tubunae, she nonetheless, knowing none of these things, married you innocently and simply. And would that you could persuade her to continence, so that without impediment you might render to God what you know you owe. But if you cannot deal with her in this, at least keep conjugal chastity, and ask God, who will rescue you from your necessities, that what you cannot do now you may be able to do someday. Nevertheless, that you may love God, not love the world; that in these very wars, if you must still be engaged in them, you may keep faith, seek peace; that out of the goods of the world you may do good works, and on account of the goods of the world may not do evil works—either your wife is no impediment, or she ought not to be one. Love commanded me to write these things to you, most beloved son, by which I love you according to God, not according to this world: for, considering too what is written—*Rebuke a wise man, and he will love you; rebuke a fool, and he will go on to hate you*[^15]—I ought surely to have thought you not a fool, but wise.
