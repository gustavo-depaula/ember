# Letter 12

Augustine to Nebridius

**1.** You write that you sent more letters than we have received; but I can neither disbelieve you, nor you me. For although I am unable to be your equal in writing back, yet your letters are kept by me with no less care than they are sent frequently by you. As for my longer letters, however, we agree between us that you have received no more than two; for we did not send a third. Indeed, on examining the copies, I noticed that nearly five of your requests have been answered; except that one question there, touched on as if in passing—though not rashly entrusted to your talent—perhaps did not satisfy your greed; which you need to rein in a little, and to bear certain abridgments gladly. So, plainly, if I in any way cheat your understanding while I am sparing in words, do not spare me at all; but by the right by which something might perhaps have more force with me, if anything could be more pleasant, demand the whole of what is owed. This letter, then, you will count among my smaller letters, which I did not allow to diminish my heap at all. For neither do you send smaller ones that do not increase that same heap. Therefore that question of yours about the Son of God—why he himself rather than the Father is said to have assumed the man, since both are present at once—you will discern most easily if you recall those discussions of ours by which, as we could (for it is something ineffable), we said what the Son of God is, by whom we are joined. To touch on this here briefly: the very discipline and form of God, through which all things were made that were made, is called the Son. But whatever was accomplished through that assumed man was accomplished for our instruction and shaping.
