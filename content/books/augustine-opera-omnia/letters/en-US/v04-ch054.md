# Letter 261

TO AUDAX, A MOST BELOVED AND IN CHRIST PRAISEWORTHY AND EXCEEDINGLY DESIRABLE LORD AND BROTHER, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Your brief letter, but plainly a vehement demander of a long letter from me, I received not unwillingly—nay, even gladly. Not that I could easily suffice for your avidity, but that I rejoiced for your Charity; because, although you seek from one not fit, yet it is good what you seek. And indeed for writing a long letter, leisure rather than ability is lacking to me; most occupied, namely, with ecclesiastical cares, from which a few little drops of time scarcely refresh me, whether thinking something, or dictating those things which more press and seem to me to be about to profit more people, or restoring the strength of the body necessary to our servitude. For words are not lacking by which much paper could be filled; but what you desire from us in that same length of discourse, to this I answer that I am not fit. For you said you had desired the treasure of wisdom, but had received less than you wished, when I, from that treasure, ask with a beggarly prayer a daily dole, and scarcely obtain it.

**2.** But how am I "the oracle of the Law," of whose broad and hidden inner chambers I know far more than I know, and whose manifold recesses and shadowy retreats I am not able to approach and penetrate as I wish, and I know myself to be nothing other than less worthy? Further, "consecrator of justice"—who am I, to whom it is a very great thing to have been consecrated? And now, as for your calling me "restorer of spiritual glory"; grant pardon, you do not know to whom, in great part, you speak: for I myself am as yet so restored in this glory that, from day to day, I confess that not only how much I advance, but even whether I advance at all, escapes me. "Dispenser plainly of eternal salvation" I am, with my other innumerable fellow servants. *But if I do it willingly, I have a reward: but if unwillingly, a dispensation has been entrusted to me*[^1]; for to be a dispenser of that salvation through word and Sacrament is not already to be also a partaker of it. For if it were not dispensed through the good, the Apostle would not rightly say: *Be imitators of me, as I too of Christ*[^2]. Again, if it were not dispensed through the evil, the Lord would not say of certain ones: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do: for they say, and do not do*[^3]. There are, then, many dispensers, through whose ministry one arrives at eternal salvation: *but it is sought among dispensers that one be found faithful*[^4]; and among those faithful themselves, in whose number may he who is not deceived count me, *one is so, another so*[^5], *as God has apportioned to each the measure of faith*[^6].

**3.** And so, most dear and most sweet brother, may the Lord rather feed you on the flowers of wisdom, and water you with draughts of the living fountain. But if you think anything can be conferred, through my little work, on your most religious zeal, since I understand you to be both capacious and perceive you to be avid; your mind is rather to be directed to other little works of ours, which are comprised in many volumes, than that anything be hoped through letters which could fulfill your desire. Or certainly take in the present what I can give, since I think you do not exhibit yourself to us for this reason, that you do not wish to: for what great thing is it, with the Lord helping, that you, a man free from the duty of any place, should come to us, whether to be with us for a long while, or, a little time having been spent, at least to return?

**4.** Behold, what you set down in the third of your five verses has nearly come to pass, that you would have my letter more loquacious than eloquent. But as for the fact that in the fifth and last verse there are seven feet, I do not know whether the number deceived your ear, or whether you wished to test whether I still remember to judge these things, which perhaps those have already forgotten who, once students of such matters, afterward made very great progress in ecclesiastical letters.

**5.** The Psalter translated by holy Jerome from the Hebrew I do not have. But we have not translated, but corrected some of the faults of the Latin codices from the Greek copies. Whence perhaps we have made something more serviceable than it was, yet not such as it ought to be. For even now, the things which perhaps then escaped us, if, as we read, they move us, we correct by collating the codices. So that which is perfect, we too, with you, seek.

[^1]: See 1 Cor 9:17
[^2]: 1 Cor 11:1
[^3]: See Matt 23:3
[^4]: See 1 Cor 4:2
[^5]: See 1 Cor 7:7
[^6]: See Rom 12:3
