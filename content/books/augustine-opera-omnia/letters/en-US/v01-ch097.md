# Letter 96

TO HIS MOST BELOVED LORD, AND IN THE MEMBERS OF CHRIST HONORABLY TO BE EMBRACED SON OLYMPIUS, AUGUSTINE.

**1.** Whatever you may be according to the course of this age, we nevertheless write most faithfully to our dearest and most sincere fellow-servant Olympius, a Christian. For we know that to be more glorious to you than all glory, and more sublime than all sublimity. For fame has brought to us that you have attained a higher honor; whether this was true had not yet been confirmed among us, when this occasion of writing arose. But since we know that you have learned from the Lord not to be high-minded, but to consent to the humble[^1], to whatever summit you have been advanced, we presume that you will receive our letter no otherwise than you are wont, most beloved lord, and in the members of Christ honorably to be embraced son. But we do not doubt at all that you will prudently use temporal felicity for eternal gains, so that, the more you can in this earthly republic, the more you may spend on that heavenly city which bore you in Christ, that it may be repaid you more abundantly in the region of the living[^2], and in the true peace of those secure and of joys remaining without end.

**2.** The petition of my holy brother and fellow-bishop Boniface I again commend to your Charity, lest perhaps it can now be done which before could not. For although he could, perhaps without any question, consequently retain himself what his predecessor had acquired—though under another's name—and had already begun to possess under the name of the Church, we are nevertheless unwilling, since he was a debtor to the treasury, that he have this scruple in his conscience. For that fraud, because it was done against the treasury, was not therefore not done. And that Paul indeed, after he was made a bishop, about to renounce all his goods on account of the immense heap of fiscal debts, having exacted a certain bond in which a fixed weight of silver was owed him, acquired these scanty little fields, from which to sustain his living, as though for the Church, then under the name of a most powerful house, so that even from them, following his custom, when he did not pay the treasury, he might suffer no troubles from the exactors. But this man, who, that one having died, was ordained for the same Church, feared to take up these fields: and although he could, for the fiscal debts alone which that man had contracted from the said little possessions, request an imperial benefit, he preferred to confess the whole—that Paul had bought them with his own silver, when he was liable to the treasury, from the auction—so that the Church might possess them, if it can be done, not by the hidden iniquity of a bishop, but by the manifest liberality of a Christian emperor. But if this cannot be done, the servants of God better tolerate the labor of want than that they obtain the means of necessities with a conscience of fraud.

**3.** To this end we ask that you deign to impart your support: because what was first obtained, he was unwilling to adduce, lest he again cut off for himself the faculty of supplicating; for there was no answer to the things desired. But now, since you are of the same benignity as you are wont, but ampler in power, we do not despair that, with the Lord helping, this can easily be granted to your merits: since even if in your own name you sought those same places, and yourself gave them to the said Church, who would reprehend, or who would not most highly proclaim your petition, serving not earthly cupidity, but Christian piety? May the mercy of the Lord our God keep you happier in Christ, lord son.

[^1]: See Rom 12:16
[^2]: See Ps 116:9 (114:9)
