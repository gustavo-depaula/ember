# Letter 219

TO THE MOST BELOVED AND HONORABLE BROTHERS AND FELLOW PRIESTS PROCULUS AND CYLINNIUS, AURELIUS, AUGUSTINE, FLORENTIUS, AND SECUNDINUS SEND GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Our son Leporius, deservedly and fittingly rebuked before Your Holiness for the presumption of his error, when he had come to us after being driven out from there, we received—wholesomely disturbed—to be corrected and healed. For just as you obeyed the Apostle in rebuking the restless, so we too in consoling the fainthearted and receiving the weak[^1]. For since he had been *overtaken,* as *a man in some fault*[^2], and that no small one, in that concerning the only-begotten Son of God—who *in the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God*[^3], *but when the fullness of time came*[^4] *the Word was made flesh and dwelt among us*[^5]—he was not wise in certain matters, nor did he think true things; denying that God was made man, lest, namely, an unworthy change or corruption should follow in the divine substance, by which he is equal to the Father[^6]; nor seeing that he was bringing in a fourth person into the Trinity, which is utterly foreign to the soundness of the Creed and of Catholic truth; we instructed him, as we could, with the Lord's help, *in a spirit of gentleness*[^7]: especially since, when he admonished this—*the chosen vessel*[^8]—he added: *Watching yourself, lest you too be tempted*[^9], lest certain ones should so rejoice to have come to spiritual advancement that they should think themselves no longer able to be tempted, as though they were not men; and he added the wholesome and peaceful sentence, that we should bear one another's burdens, since thus we shall fulfill the law of Christ: *For if anyone thinks himself to be something, when he is nothing, he deceives himself*[^10], most beloved and honorable brothers.

**2.** Yet this amendment of his we could perhaps by no means have accomplished, had you not first condemned the things that were faulty in him. The same one, therefore, both our Lord and our physician, using his vessels and ministers, who said, *I will strike, and I will heal*[^11], through you struck the swollen one, through us healed the grieving one. The same administrator and provider of his house, through you tore down what was ill-constructed; through us restored what was well-composed. The same diligent husbandman of his estate, through you uprooted the barren and harmful things; through us planted the useful and fruitful. Let us therefore give glory not to ourselves, but to his mercy, in whose hand are both we and our words[^12]. And just as in our aforesaid son our lowliness praised your ministry, so let Your Holiness too rejoice in our ministry. With a fatherly and brotherly heart, then, receive from us one corrected with merciful gentleness, as we received from you one rebuked with merciful severity. For although one thing was done through us and another through you, yet one love made both necessary to a brother's salvation. One God, therefore, did it, since *God is love*[^13].

**3.** Accordingly, just as through his penitence he was received by us, so through his letter let him be received by you: to which letter we thought we should also subscribe with our own hand, attesting that it is his. [For, once admonished, he easily saw that God was made man, because *the Word was made flesh, and the Word was God*[^14], and the Apostle taught him that this was done not by losing what he was but by assuming what he was not; for he emptied himself not by losing the form of God, but *by taking the form of a servant*[^15]. For this is what that man feared, when he was unwilling to confess that God was born of a woman, that God was crucified and suffered other human things, lest the divinity be believed to be changed in the man or corrupted by the admixture of the man; a devout fear, but an incautious error; devoutly he saw that the divinity cannot change, but incautiously he presumed that the son of man could be separated from the Son of God, so that the one would be one and the other another, and so that one of them would not be Christ, or there would be two Christs. But after he came to know that the Word of God, that is, the only-begotten Son of God, was so made the son of man that neither was turned into the other, but, with each remaining in its own substance, God so suffered human things in the man that he kept the divine things whole in himself, he confessed Christ to be God and man without any fear, and feared more the addition of a fourth person to the Trinity than that detriment of substance in the divinity.] This correction of his we in no way doubt that your love will both gratefully accept and make known to those to whom his error was a scandal. For those too who came with him to us were corrected and healed along with him, as is expressed and held by their subscriptions, which were made in our presence. It remains that, having rejoiced over a brother's salvation, you deign in turn to gladden us by a rescript of Your Beatitude. We wish you, most beloved and honorable brothers, to fare well in the Lord, mindful of us.
