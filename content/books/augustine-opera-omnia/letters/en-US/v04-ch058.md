# Letter 265

TO SELEUCIANA, A MOST RELIGIOUS HANDMAID OF GOD, TO BE HONORED IN THE LOVE OF CHRIST, AUGUSTINE, A BISHOP, SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Having read your letter, rejoicing over your welfare, I did not delay to answer the things you wrote. And first I wondered how that Novatian says Peter was not baptized, when a little above you wrote that he said the Apostles were baptized. Whence it seems to him that, among the baptized Apostles, Peter was not baptized, I do not know: and therefore I have sent you a copy of your letter, lest perhaps you do not have it, in which you may more diligently consider that I am answering the things I found in your letter; for if the notary did not take it down or write it faultily, I do not know what sort of heart he has who, while he says the Apostles were baptized, denies that Peter was baptized.

**2.** But as for Peter's being said to have done penance, care must be taken lest he be thought to have done it in the way that those do in the Church who are properly called penitents. And who could bear that we should think the first of the Apostles is to be numbered among such penitents? For it repented him to have denied Christ, which his tears indicate: for so it is written, that *he wept bitterly*[^1]. For they had not yet been confirmed by the resurrection of the Lord, and by that coming of the Holy Spirit who appeared on the day of Pentecost, or by that inbreathing which the Lord showed after he rose from the dead, when he breathed on their face, saying: *Receive the Holy Spirit*[^2].

**3.** Hence it can rightly be said that, when Peter denied the Lord, the Apostles had not yet been baptized; not, however, with water, but with the Holy Spirit. For this he said to them after he rose and conversed with them: *John indeed baptized with water, but you will be baptized with the Holy Spirit, whom you are also going to receive not many days hence*, up to Pentecost[^3]. But some codices have: *But you will begin to be baptized with the Holy Spirit:* but whether it be said, *you will be baptized,* or it be said, *you will begin to be baptized,* it makes no difference to the matter. For in whatever codices is found, *you will baptize,* or, *you will begin to baptize,* they are faulty; which are most easily refuted from the Greek. But if we say they were not baptized with water, it is to be feared lest we err gravely about them, lest we give men authority for despising baptism; which the apostolic discipline itself commends as so far not to be despised, that Cornelius the centurion and those who were with him, even having already received the Holy Spirit, were baptized[^4].

**4.** Just as the ancient just men, if they were not circumcised, had no sin; but after God commanded that Abraham and his posterity be circumcised[^5], then, if it were not done, it was a grave sin: so also, before the Lord Christ in his Church gave the Sacrament of the New Testament... holy baptism in place of the circumcision of the flesh, and said most openly: *If anyone is not reborn of water and the Holy Spirit, he will not enter into the kingdom of heaven*[^6], we ought not now to ask when each one was baptized; but whomever we read to pertain to the kingdom of heaven, in the body of Christ, *which is the Church*[^7], we ought to understand to be only the baptized: unless perhaps those whom the straits of the Passion found, and who, unwilling to deny Christ, were killed before they were baptized, to whom the Passion itself was reckoned for baptism. But can we say this of the Apostles, who had so ample a time in which they might be baptized, that they even baptized others? But not all the things that were done are also found written; nevertheless they are proved to have been done from other documents. It is written when the apostle Paul was baptized[^8], and it is not written when the other Apostles were baptized; nevertheless we ought to understand them too to have been baptized: just as it is written when the peoples of the Churches in Jerusalem and Samaria were baptized[^9]; but when the other peoples of the nations were baptized, to whom the Apostles sent Letters, it is surely not written, and yet we by no means doubt that they too were surely baptized, on account of that sentence of the Lord: *Unless one is reborn of water and the Holy Spirit, he will not enter into the kingdom of heaven*[^10].

**5.** But both are written of the Lord: both that *he baptized more than John,* and that *he himself did not baptize, but his disciples*[^11]; that we might understand both that he himself indeed baptized by the presence of his majesty, yet that he himself did not baptize with his own hands. For the Sacrament of baptism was his; but the ministry of baptizing pertained to the Disciples. Then, therefore, when John the evangelist says in his Gospel: *After these things Jesus and his disciples went out into the land of Judea, and there he stayed with them, and baptized*[^12]: then, a little after, speaking of him, he says: *When, therefore, Jesus knew that the Pharisees had heard that Jesus made more disciples and baptized more than John (although Jesus himself did not baptize, but his disciples), he left Judea, and went again into Galilee*[^13]. Then, therefore, when he went out from Jerusalem with his disciples into the land of Judea, and there stayed with them, he baptized not through himself, but through his disciples; whom we understand to have been already baptized, whether with the baptism of John, as some judge, or, what is more credible, with the baptism of Christ. For he would not shun the ministry of baptizing, so as to have baptized servants through whom he might baptize the rest, who did not shun the ministry of that memorable humility, when he washed their feet; and to Peter asking that he wash for him not only the feet, but also the hands and head, he answered: *He who is washed needs only to wash his feet, but is wholly clean*[^14]: where it is understood that Peter had already been baptized.

**6.** But how he says—what you set down in your letter—that the Apostles gave penance in place of baptism, is not evidently expressed. For if he says "in place of baptism" for this reason, that through penance sins are remitted, what he says has some reason. But such penance can be useful after baptism, if anyone has sinned. But he, since he denies that a place of penance is given after baptism, when he says, as you wrote, that penance is only before baptism; one is given to understand that he so said that the Apostles gave penance in place of baptism, that they gave it before baptism, and that those to whom it was given were not afterward baptized, because that was for them in place of baptism: which I have never heard the Novatians say. Hence inquire diligently, lest perhaps it be of some other error, and he feign, or think, himself to be a Novatian. Or whether the Novatians too say this, I do not know: this, however, I do know, that whoever says this is utterly alien from the rule of the Catholic faith, and from the teaching of Christ and the Apostles.

**7.** For men do penance before baptism for their former sins, yet in such a way that they are also baptized, as it is written in the *Acts of the Apostles,* Peter speaking to the Jews and saying: *Do penance, and let each of you be baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ; and your sins will be forgiven you*[^15]. Men also do penance, if after baptism they have so sinned as to merit to be excommunicated and afterward reconciled; as do those who in all the Churches are properly called penitents. For of such penance the apostle Paul spoke, where he says: *Lest again, when I come, God humble me before you; and I mourn many of those who sinned before, and did not do penance over the uncleanness, and luxury, and fornication which they committed*[^16]: for he would not write these things, except to those who had already been baptized. We have also in the *Acts of the Apostles,* Simon, already baptized, when he wished to buy with money that, by the laying on of his hand, the Holy Spirit might be given, admonished by Peter to do penance for this grave sin[^17].

**8.** There is also a penance of the good and humble faithful, almost daily, in which we beat our breasts, saying: *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive our debtors*[^18]. For we do not wish those things to be forgiven us which we do not doubt were forgiven in baptism; but surely those things which, however small, yet frequent, creep upon human frailty: which, if they are collected against us, will so weigh us down and oppress us, as some one great sin. For what difference does it make to a shipwreck, whether the ship is covered and overwhelmed by one great wave, or whether water creeping in little by little into the bilge, and by negligence left and despised, fills the ship and sinks it? On account of these things, fasts and alms and prayers keep watch: in which, when we say, *Forgive us our debts, as we too forgive,* we manifest that we have what is to be forgiven us; and in these words, humbling our souls, we do not cease in a way to do daily penance. To the things you wrote, I think I have answered briefly but sufficiently: it remains that he not be contentious, for the gaining of whom you thought such a letter should be directed to me.
