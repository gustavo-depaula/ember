# Letter 112

To the most excellent lord, and by most sincere love honorable brother Donatus, Augustine sends greetings in the Lord.

**1.** That, much desiring you while you held office, I could not see you even when you had come to Tibilis, I believe was done for this reason, that I might enjoy your mind freed from public cares rather than that that greeting—with me at leisure before you, and you busy—should temper the desire of neither of us as much as would suffice: for, recalling the honorableness of your character from your earliest age, I judge your breast abundantly fit for Christ to pour himself into most lavishly, so that you may bear him fruits worthier of eternal and heavenly glory than of temporal and earthly acclaim.

**2.** For from many—nay, indeed from all whom I could either question, or even hear of their own accord proclaiming it—praising and preferring the chastity and virtue of your administration altogether constantly, and without any scruple of dissonant variety, I received it, and the more surely in that they did not know our connection, and your proclaimers were wholly ignorant whether I knew you even slightly, lest I should believe that they had given themselves to my ears rather than scattered true things about you. For there praise is removed from vanity, where even blame is secure from offense. Yet, O excellent brother, and by most sincere love honorable, you are not now to be taught, but perhaps to be admonished, that all that popular glory and fame is to be rejoiced in not in the mouth of the crowd, but in the things themselves: which, even if they displease the crowd, are nonetheless precious by their own splendor and weight, not by the commendation of the unskilled; and he who disapproves such things is rather to be pitied than the one who is disapproved concerning such things to be judged wretched. But when they please, and are also popularly proclaimed with their due praise, not even so do those things themselves become greater and better by another's judgment; since they are made whole by inmost truth, and are made solid by the strength of conscience alone. Whence something of happiness accrues from it rather to men judging rightly than to him of whom the crowd thinks well.

**3.** Since you know these things very well, good man, gaze, as you have begun, with the strongest keenness of heart upon our Lord Jesus Christ, and, altogether subsiding from all empty pride, rise up to him who does not windily lift up those converted to him, but, as they lean and ascend by the sure steps of faith, places them on the everlasting summit of heavenly and angelic dignity. Through whom I beseech you to write back to me, and to exhort all your people whom you have in Sinitis or Hippo, kindly and benignly, to the communion of the Catholic Church. In whose bosom I have learned that your father too, a praiseworthy and distinguished man, begot you; whom I ask that you greet on my behalf with the office due to his merits, and that you not find it burdensome to visit us. Which I also, not impudently, demand on account of your own affairs, which you have here, to be made better before God. May the mercy of God enfold you, and preserve you from all iniquity.
