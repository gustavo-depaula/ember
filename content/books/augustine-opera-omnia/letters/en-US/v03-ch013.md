# Letter 168

To the truly most blessed lord, and deservedly venerable father, bishop Augustine, from Timasius and James, greetings in the Lord.

The grace of God, ministered through your word, has so refreshed and recreated us that we say in plain brotherhood: *He sent his word, and healed them*[^1], most blessed lord, and deservedly venerable father. We have indeed found that your holiness has sifted the text of that little book with such diligence, that we are astonished at the answers rendered to its single points—whether in those things which it befits a Christian to refute, detest, or flee; or in those in which he is found to have erred not slightly, although by I know not what cunning, in believing that even in those very things the grace of God should be suppressed. But there is one thing that affects us in so great a benefit: that this so splendid gift of God's grace shone forth late; since it happened that some became absent, to whose blindness this illumination of so clear a truth was owed; to whom, even if more slowly, we do not doubt that, God being propitious, the same grace will come, who *wills all men to be saved, and to come to the knowledge of the truth*[^2]. But we, even though long ago, taught by the spirit of clarity which is in you, had cast off the subjection of that error, give thanks even now for this also: that these things which we believed before, we have now learned to open to others, your holiness's more abundant discourse spreading open the way of ease. And in another hand: May the mercy of our God glorify your blessedness, safe and mindful of us, forever.

[^1]: Ps 107:20 (106:20)
[^2]: 1 Tim 2:4
