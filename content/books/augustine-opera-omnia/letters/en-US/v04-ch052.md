# Letter 259

TO CORNELIUS, A MOST BELOVED AND HONORABLE LORD AND BROTHER, AUGUSTINE.

**1.** You wrote to me that I should give you some long consolatory letter, because you were gravely moved by the death of your excellent wife, as you recalled that holy Paulinus did to Macarius. And that soul indeed, received into the fellowship of the faithful and the chaste, neither cares for nor seeks human praises; but because these things are done for the sake of those who live, it is first necessary that you, who desire to be consoled by her praise, so live that you may merit to be where she herself is. For I do not doubt that you do not believe her to be there where those women are who either polluted the conjugal bed with adulteries, or, bound by no marriage, flowed away in fornication. Wherefore, to wish, as it were, to drive away sadness by her praise from a husband far unlike her, is flattery, not consolation. For if you loved her as she herself loved you, you would have kept for her what she herself kept for you. And since she, if you had died first, is by no means to be believed to have been about to marry anyone; would you not, if you truly grieved her death, to be consoled by her praises, seek not even one and lawful wife after her?

**2.** Here you will say: "Why do you deal harshly with me? why do you rebuke me sternly?" Have we not, amid these words, behold, grown old, while life is led, to be ended before it is corrected? You wish me to pardon your deadly security: how much better is it that you pardon, if not my lovable, certainly my pitiable anxiety for you? Tullius indeed inveighed with a hostile mind, and far other was the intention of one governing an earthly commonwealth, and yet he says: *I desire, Conscript Fathers, to be merciful; I desire, in such great perils of the commonwealth, not to seem dissolute*[^1]. How much more justly do I say, since you yourself know what a friendly mind I bear toward you, established in the service of the eternal city, a minister of the divine word and Sacrament: "I desire, brother Cornelius, to be merciful; I desire, in such great perils of yours and mine, not to seem dissolute"!

**3.** A crowd of women keeps watch at your sides, the number of concubines grows by the day; but the lord—nay rather, the slave—of that same number, flowing away through so many harlots with insatiable lust, and demanding from us, by the right of friendship, as if to mitigate his sadness, the praises of his dead chaste wife, do we, a bishop, patiently hear? You who, when you were—I will not say a catechumen, but a young man established with us in a most pernicious error—had, by a most temperate will, corrected yourself from this vice in your younger days, with us younger: into which, after no long time, you rolled back more foully, then, baptized in the extreme peril of life, you have not yet—I will not say as one of the faithful, but even with us now surely old men and besides bishops—amended yourself. You wish to be consoled by us about the death of your good wife: who consoles us about this truer death of yours? Or, because we cannot forget your so great merits toward us, are we therefore still to be tormented by your morals, therefore to be despised and held for nothing, when we groan to you about you? But we confess that we are nothing toward correcting and healing you; let God be attended to, let Christ be thought of, let the Apostle be heard saying: *Taking, then, the members of Christ, shall I make them the members of a harlot?*[^2] If you spurn in your heart the words of a bishop, whatever sort, your friend, think of the body of your Lord in your body: finally, how you sin by deferring from day to day, when you do not know your last day?

**4.** Now I will prove how much you desire from our mouth the praises of Cypriana. Certainly, if I still sold words to pupils in the school of rhetoric, I would first take the fee from them. I wish to sell you the praise of your most chaste wife; first give me the fee, your chastity: give it, I say, and receive it. *I speak after the manner of men, on account of your weakness*[^3]; I think that Cypriana is not worthy of you, to whose praises you prefer the love of your concubines: which you will surely do, if you prefer to remain in that love rather than to come to these praises. What do you wish to extort from me by demanding, when you see that what I demand is for your sake? why do you, subject, beg for what you, corrected, can command? Let us send gifts to the spirit of your wife; you, imitation, I, praise. Although, as I said above, she now does not seek praise from men; but your imitation she seeks, even dead, as much as she loved you, even unlike her, while alive. I will do about her what you wish, when you have done what both I and she wish.

**5.** For if that rich man, proud and impious, as the Lord says in the Gospel, who was clothed in purple and fine linen, and feasted splendidly every day, when he was paying the penalties of his evil merits among the dead, and could not obtain a drop of water from the finger of the poor man despised before his door; he remembered his five brothers, and asked that the same poor man, whose rest in Abraham's bosom he saw far off, be sent to them, lest they too come into that place of torments[^4]: how much more does your wife remember you? how much more does she, chaste, not want you to come to the punishments of adulterers, if the proud man did not want even his brothers to come to the punishments of the proud? and since a brother did not want his brothers to be joined with him in evils, how much less does a wife, established in good things, want her husband to be separated in evils? Read the very place in the Gospel: *It is the devout voice of Christ; believe God*. You grieve, no doubt, your dead wife, and think that, if I praise her, you will be consoled by my addresses; learn what you should grieve, if you will not be with her. Or should you grieve more that she is not yet praised by me, than I that she is not loved by you? For surely, if you loved her, you would desire to be with her after death, where you certainly will not be, if, as you are, such you remain. Love, then, her whose praise you demand, lest what you demand falsely, I justly deny. (*And in another hand:*) May the Lord grant us to rejoice over your salvation, most beloved lord and honorable brother.

[^1]: See Cicero, In Catilinam 1.2.4
[^2]: 1 Cor 6:15
[^3]: See Rom 6:19
[^4]: See Luke 16:19-28
