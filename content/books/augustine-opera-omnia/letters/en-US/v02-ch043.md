# Letter 142

To the most beloved lords, the brothers and priests Saturninus and Eufrates, and the clerics who are with you, converted to the unity and peace of Christ, Augustine the bishop sends greetings in the Lord.

**1.** Your coming gladdened us: but let not our absence sadden you. For we are in that Church which, God being propitious, although it is everywhere extended and spread through the whole world, is yet one great body of one great head, which is its head, the Savior himself, as the Apostle says. But of the glorification of this head, which was to be after his resurrection, the prophet foretold so long before: *Be exalted above the heavens, O God*; and because, he being exalted above the heavens, his Church was to fill all the earth with copious fruitfulness, the same psalm consequently added: *and over all the earth be your glory*[^1]. Accordingly, most beloved, with a stable mind and a most firm heart, let us persist under so lofty a head, in so glorious a body, in which we are members of one another. Whence, even if my distant absence were in most remote lands, in him we would be together, from the unity of whose body we ought not to withdraw. For if one house held us, we would surely be said to be together: how much more are we together, when in one body we are together! Although even that we are in one house, the Truth itself attests, since holy Scripture, which says that the Church is the body of Christ, likewise says that the same Church is the house of God.

**2.** But this house is built not in one corner of the world, but through all the earth. Whence that psalm, in whose title is read, *When the house was built after the captivity*, so begins: *Sing to the Lord a new song, sing to the Lord, all the earth*[^2]. For in the oldness of the old man, the devil had taken the world captive; after this captivity, when the house is built, the renewal of the faithful is signified in the new man. Whence the Apostle says: *Put off the old man, and put on him who is created according to God*[^3]: and because this is done through all the earth in Catholic unity—as in another psalm it was said, "and over all the earth be your glory"—so in this one, when it had been said, "Sing to the Lord a new song," to show when the house is built in this new song, it was at once subjoined, "Sing to the Lord, all the earth." But the workers, through whom this so great house is built, the same psalm exhorts, when it consequently says: *Announce well, day from day, his salvation; announce among the nations his glory, among all peoples his marvels*[^4]: and a little after it says, *Bring to the Lord, families of the nations, bring to the Lord glory and honor*[^5]. What this house is—that is, the Catholic Church—I have said in another place.

**3.** To these and such testimonies of this so great house, which in the whole Scripture are found so many, its enemies so yielded that they confessed they had no cause against the Church overseas, which they yet confessed to be Catholic. With this Church we communicate, that we may deserve to be joined to the members of Christ, and may embrace the framework of his body with an affection of most faithful love. Since whoever in the unity of this Church has lived badly, eats and drinks judgment to himself, as the Apostle says. But whoever lives well, another's cause and another's person does not prejudice him. So even those men, when they were pressed about the cause of Maximian, were compelled by their own mouth to confess that "neither does cause prejudice cause, nor person person." Yet we are solicitous for one another, as members of one body; so that whoever of us, with the Lord helping, pertain to the future granary, may meanwhile in the threshing floor together tolerate the chaff, lest, on account of that destined for the future fire, we desert the Lord's threshing floor.

**4.** Carry on faithfully and cheerfully the ecclesiastical offices which pertain to you, according to your grades, and fulfill your ministry sincerely on account of that God under whom we are fellow servants, and to whom we consider that we are to render an account of our acts. Whence the bowels of mercy ought to abound in you, because judgment will be without mercy to him who has not done mercy. And through this, pray with us also for those who are still saddened, that the weakness of the carnal mind, gathered and contracted from a long-lasting habit, may be healed. For who would not understand how good and pleasant it is for brothers to dwell as one, if this pleasantness touch sound throats, whence my mind, which loves the sweetness of love, spits out the bitterness of division? But God, whom we pray for them, is powerful and merciful, so that by whatever occasions he may draw even them to salvation. May the Lord keep you in peace.

[^2]: Ps 96 title and 1 (95:1)
[^3]: See Eph 4:22-24
[^4]: Ps 96:2-3 (95:2-3)
