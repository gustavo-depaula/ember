# Letter 15\*

A MEMORANDUM FOR THE HOLY BROTHERS WHOSE NAMES MY LETTER CONTAINS, FROM AUGUSTINE

**1.** When the letter to be directed by us to your benevolence was now ready, there came from brother Alypius a memorandum which he gave to Thagaste, since the same ship put in at our shore. What I learned from that memorandum, this I have taken care that Your Holiness should know, and to insert its words into this letter of mine. A MEMORANDUM FOR THE PRIESTS, FROM ALYPIUS.

**2.** "[So] our son Severianus has set out from us. We still await the reply of the exalted man, as well as his coming, because he was announced as about to be present before the Ides of October by those who most certainly affirmed that he had left Gaul. May the prayers of your Charity, then, help us, that, by the Lord's help, we may accomplish something before winter, so that to you, according both to your desire and ours, by the Lord's aid, we may return before winter. Indeed, much has now been granted to our letters; for, with the indulgence sent to the people of Carthage, a court usher set out on the day we dictated these things. It remained, then, for us to deal about those who, in the same case, fled to the Church of Carthage."

**3.** These are the things which I thought necessary from the memorandum sent to Thagaste, that I should direct them to you written out. Besides, I ask that you deign to deliver the other letters which I have directed to those to whom they were directed, and to support them before them by your intercessions, each as he can; for if you should all wish to do everything together, you will fulfill what you wish with difficulty, because it is very rare that all of you have leisure at one hour. But to the honorable and most religious man, our son Dorotheus, [say] that he should not so be angered against a man of his—who violated by violent oppression a nun coming from another's estate to his house for the sake of wool-working—as to punish him more severely than is fitting through our complaint; for it suffices that he remove him from the management of that same place where he set him, lest, if he be left utterly unpunished, he invite others to imitate him. He is indeed already doing penance, but for this reason, because, after he was convicted and brought by conviction to confession, he was at once forbidden communion. For if he had confessed of his own accord and, by fear of the divine judgment, had disclosed what was unknown and which no one had objected, who would be so perversely severe as to seek a fuller amendment?

**4.** But now, just as, if he were a cleric, he would lose the honor of his rank, so this man too ought to lose the honor of management, in which his impunity is puffed up, and therefore precaution must be taken lest he have imitators. This man is Cresconius, manager of a Spanish estate, having—what is worse—a wife; but do not judge either the deed or the man, unless you first hold his promise, under attestation of God, that he will not avenge it further, nor state to him beforehand the very manner of the vengeance, who has promised that he will do nothing more severe than what he finds in this memorandum. For [it is] not for the sake of his faith and the religion he has in Christ, by which he could be gravely moved [...]; certainly, if he wishes to avenge it more mildly than I have asked, besides a bodily punishment, I do not forbid it, yet not more. Let him, then, first promise this, and, when he has promised—lest you labor in explaining—cause him to read the part of this memorandum pertaining to the case.
