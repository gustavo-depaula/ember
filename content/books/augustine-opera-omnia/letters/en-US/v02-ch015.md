# Letter 114

To the most beloved lord and son Florentinus, Augustine sends greetings in the Lord.

**1.** By the command of what authority you have carried off Faventius, you yourself shall see; but this I know, that every authority set under an empire serves the laws of its own emperor. Although, then, I have already sent, through my brother and fellow priest Caelestinus, the law—which indeed, even before I sent it, you ought surely not to have been ignorant of—by which it is granted to those who are ordered to be produced before the courts by some authority, that they be brought to the municipal records, and there be questioned whether they wish to spend thirty days, in the city where they are held, under moderate custody, to prepare for themselves their crops, or to set their affairs in order, as may be necessary; which law, as the aforesaid priest reported to me, was recited to your religion: yet even now I have again sent it with this letter; not terrifying, but asking, and interceding for a man humanely, and with episcopal mercy, as far as humanity and piety themselves permit, lord son, that you deign to grant both to your own esteem and to my petition, and that what the law of the Emperor commands, in whose republic you serve, you not find it burdensome to do, with my intervention and entreaty also added.
