# Letter 92

TO THE DISTINGUISHED LADY, AND DESERVEDLY MOST EXCELLENT, AND IN THE LOVE OF CHRIST HONORABLE DAUGHTER ITALICA, BISHOP AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** Not only by your letter, but also by the report of the one who brought it, I have learned that you greatly demand letters of mine, believing that from them you can have very much consolation. You yourself, then, will see what you take from them; I, nevertheless, ought not to deny or defer them. But may your faith and hope console you, and that very love which is poured out in the hearts of the pious through the Holy Spirit[^1], of which we have now received something as a pledge[^2], so that we may know to desire that very fullness. For you ought not to think yourself desolate, when in the inner man you have Christ present through faith in your heart[^3]; nor ought you so to be saddened as the nations which have no hope[^4], since by a most truthful promise we hope that we shall come, from this life—whence, about to migrate, we have not lost certain of our own who migrate, but have sent them ahead—to that life, where they will be to us, the more known, the dearer surely, and lovable without fear of any departure.

**2.** But here, although your husband, by whose departure you are called a widow, was most well known to you, yet he was more known to himself than to you. And whence is this, when you saw his bodily face, which he surely did not see, except because the knowledge of ourselves is more certain within, where no one knows the things of a man, except the spirit of the man which is in him[^5]? But when the Lord comes, and illuminates the hidden things of darkness, and makes manifest the thoughts of the heart[^6], then nothing of a neighbor will lie hidden in a neighbor, nor will there be what anyone may open to his own and conceal from strangers, where there will be no stranger. But that light itself, by which all these things which are now hidden away in hearts will be illuminated—of what sort or how great it is, who may bring it forth with his tongue, who may even touch it with his weak mind? Surely that light is God himself, since *God is light, and in him there is no darkness at all*[^7]; but the light of purified minds, not of these eyes of the body. There will be then, therefore, a mind fit to see that light, which now it is not yet.

**3.** But this the eye of the body can neither now see, nor will then be able to see. For everything which can be beheld by the eyes of the body must be in some place, nor is it whole everywhere, but with its lesser part occupies a lesser place, and with its greater a greater. Not so is God, invisible and incorruptible, *who alone has immortality, and inhabits light inaccessible; whom no man has seen, nor can see*[^8]. For he cannot be seen by man through that by which man sees bodies by the body. For if he were inaccessible to the minds of the pious, it would not be said: *Draw near to him, and be illuminated*[^9]; and if he were invisible to the minds of the pious, it would not be said: *We shall see him as he is*[^10]. For consider that whole sentence in the Epistle of John: *Beloved,* he says, *we are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be. We know that when he appears we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^11]. We shall see, then, in so far as we shall be like him; for even now we do not see in so far as we are unlike. Thence, therefore, we shall see, whence we shall be like. But who would say, in the greatest madness, that we are, or shall be, like God by the body? In the inner man, therefore, is this likeness; *who is renewed in the knowledge of God, according to the image of him who created him*[^12]. And we are made so much the more like him, the more we advance in the knowledge and love of him; because *although our outer man is corrupted, yet the inner is renewed from day to day*[^13]: so indeed that in this life, however far one has advanced, he is far from that perfection of likeness which will be fit for seeing God, as the Apostle says, *face to face*[^14]. In which words, certainly, if we wished to take it of a bodily face, it will follow that God too has such a face, and that there is some interval between ours and his, when we shall see him face to face. And if an interval, then surely an end, and a bounded disposition of members, and other absurdities, impious to say and to think, by which the animal man, not perceiving the things which are of the Spirit of God[^15], is sported with by most deceptive vanities.

**4.** For certain of those who prate such things say—as it could reach me—that we see God now by the mind, then by the body; so that they assert that even the impious will see him in like manner. See how much they have advanced for the worse, while, without limit of fear or shame, an unpunished loquacity is borne wandering hither and thither. Formerly they said that Christ granted this to his flesh alone, that with bodily eyes he should see God; then they added also that all the saints, with their bodies received again in the resurrection, would in the same way see God; now they have already given this possibility even to the impious. Let them give, indeed, as much as they wish, and to whom they wish; for who would dare to contradict men giving of their own? For *he who speaks a lie speaks of his own*[^16]. But you, with those who hold sound doctrine, dare to usurp nothing of these things of your own: but when you read: *Blessed are the pure in heart, for they shall see God*[^17], understand that the impious shall not see; for the impious are not blessed and pure in heart. Likewise, when you read: *We see now through a mirror in an enigma, but then face to face*[^18], understand that we shall then see face to face by that whence we now see through a mirror in an enigma. But both of these are a function of the inner man—whether when, in this pilgrimage, one still walks by faith, in which he uses the mirror and the enigma, or in that native land when one shall contemplate through sight: for which vision it is set down, *face to face.*

**5.** Let the flesh, drunk with carnal thoughts, hear: *God is Spirit, and therefore those who worship God must worship in spirit and in truth*[^19]. If they must worship, how much more see? For who would dare to affirm that the substance of God is seen bodily, when he was unwilling that it be worshiped bodily? But they think they say something acute, and, as if by questioning, to press us: Could Christ grant to his flesh that with bodily eyes it should see the Father, or could he not? So that, if we answer that he could not, they may proclaim that we have derogated from the omnipotence of God; but if we concede that he could, they may conclude their argument from our answer. How much more tolerably do those rave who assert that the flesh will be converted into the substance of God, and will be what God is, so that thus, at least by seeing, they may make it fit for God—now unlike by so great a diversity! Which vanity I believe these men drive from their faith, and perhaps from their ears too. And yet, if they are likewise pressed by a question about this, whether God can do this or cannot effect it, will they detract from his power, if they answer that he cannot, or will they confess that this will be, if they concede that he can? In what way, then, they would come out of this snare of another's, so let them come out of their own. Then, why do they contend that this gift was attributed to the bodily eyes of Christ alone, and not also to the other senses? Will God, then, be a sound, so that he can be perceived also by the ears? And will he be a breath, so that he can be perceived by smell? And will he be some liquid, so that he can also be drunk? And will he be a mass, so that he can also be touched? No, they say. What then? Can God do that, and not this? If they say he cannot, why do they derogate from the omnipotence of God? If they answer that he can but will not, why do they favor the eyes alone, but begrudge the other senses of Christ's body? Or how long do they wish to rave? How much better do we, who fix no limits to their folly, but are unwilling that they rave at all!

**6.** Many things can be brought forward to refute that madness. But if at some time they should burst upon your ears, read these things to them meanwhile, and let it not weary you to write back what they answer, as you can. For to this end are our hearts cleansed by faith[^20], because the vision of God is promised us as the reward of faith. Which, if it shall be through the eyes of the body, in vain is the mind of the saints exercised for perceiving it: indeed, a mind feeling so perversely is not exercised in itself, but is wholly in the flesh. For where will it dwell more tenaciously and more fixedly than whence it presumes it will see God? Which, certainly, how great an evil it is, I leave rather to your intelligence than labor to explain by a long discourse. May your heart always dwell in the protection of the Lord, distinguished lady, and deservedly most excellent, and in the love of Christ honorable daughter. Greet again, with the duty owed to your merits, your honorable sons, beloved by you and by us in the Lord.
