# Letter 135

To the truly holy lord, and deservedly venerable father, bishop Augustine, Volusianus.

**1.** You ask me, O proof of probity and justice, to inquire about some things to be skillfully learned from the ambiguities of reading. I embrace the favor of the task commanded, and gladly offer myself to your disciplines, following the authority of an old saying which believes that no age has an excess for thorough learning. And not without merit did the wise man enclose the studies of prudence by no limits or end, since virtue, far removed from its beginnings, is never so unlocked to those approaching that all of it forthwith lies open to recognition. Truly holy lord, and deservedly venerable father, it is worth the trouble to know a conversation held among us recently. We were present at certain gatherings of friends; frequent opinions were brought forth there according to talents and zeals. The discourse, however, was a rhetorical partition: I speak before one who recognizes it; for you taught these things too a little while ago. It was being established what the keenness of invention is, how great the labor of arrangement, what the grace of metaphor, what the beauty of imagery, and—according to the talent and nature of the matter—the apt faculty of speaking too. Others again, favoring, were exalting poetry. Not even this part of eloquence do you leave untouched or unhonored, so that the poet might fittingly have said: *Among the victorious laurels for you the ivy creeps.* It was being said, then, how great the adornment of economy is, what the comeliness of metaphors, how great the sublimity in comparison; now the smooth and unknotted verses, and, so to say, the modulated variation of caesuras. Then the discourse bent to your familiar philosophy, which you yourself were wont to foster, in the Aristotelian manner, as it were the Isocratic. We were asking too what the preceptor from the Lyceum did; what the manifold and continual hesitation of the Academy; what that disputer from the Porch; what the expertise of the Physicists; what the pleasure of the Epicureans; what, among all, the infinite lust of disputing, and then truth all the more unknown, after it was presumed that it could be recognized.

**2.** While our conversation lingered on these things, one of the many said: "And who, imbued with wisdom to the perfection of Christianity, can open the ambiguities in which I stick, and confirm my doubtful assents by a true or verisimilar credulity?" We were astonished, keeping silent. Then he of his own accord broke out into these words: "I wonder whether the Lord and ruler of the world filled the body of an unviolated woman, whether the mother bore those long disgusts of ten months, and yet a virgin gave birth with the solemnity of bearing, and after this her virginity remained intact." To these he subjoins other things: "He lies hidden within the little body of a wailing infancy, to whom the universe is scarcely thought equal; he suffers the years of boyhood, grows up, is made solid by youth; so long is that ruler absent from his seats, and the care of the whole world is transferred to one little body; then he is dissolved into sleeps, fed with food, feels all the affections of mortals; nor do any fitting signs make clear the indications of so great a majesty, since that spectral purification, the cures of the weak, life restored to the dead—these, if you consider others too, are small things for God." We intervened upon him inquiring further, and the gathering being broken up, we deferred it to the merits of better expertise, lest, while secrets are incautiously violated, an innocent error should bend into fault. You have received, O man capable of all glory, the confession of unskillfulness; you recognize what is desired from your party. It is in the interest of your fame that we should know what is sought. Somehow, without detriment to divine worship, ignorance is tolerated in other priests; but when one comes to bishop Augustine, whatever happens to be unknown is a defect in the law. May the supreme divinity guard your Veneration safe, truly holy lord, and deservedly venerable father.
