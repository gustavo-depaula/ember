# Letter 33

To the honorable and most beloved lord Proculeianus, Augustine

**1.** On account of the vanities of unskilled men, I ought not to argue longer with you about the heading of my letter. For when we try to call one another back from error, although, before the fullest discussion of the case, it can seem to some uncertain which of us errs; yet we serve one another, if we deal with one another in good will, that we may be freed from the perversity of discord. That I do this with a sincere heart, and with the trembling of Christian humility, even if it is not open to most men, yet he sees to whom no hearts are closed. But what I would not hesitate to honor in you, you easily understand. For I do not judge the error of schism—from which, as far as I am concerned, I desire all men to be healed—worthy of any honor: but you, before all else, because you are bound to us by the very bond of human society itself, and because there preeminent in you are certain indications of a more peaceable mind, by which it is in no way to be despaired that you can easily embrace the truth when it has been demonstrated, I think to be honored without any surge of doubt. But of love I owe you as much as he commands us who loved us even to the reproach of the cross.

**2.** But do not be surprised that I have long been silent toward your Benevolence; I did not think you were of this opinion, which brother Evodius, to whom I cannot but give credence, joyfully indicated to me. For when it had by chance happened that you came together into one house, and a conversation arose between you about our hope, that is, about the inheritance of Christ, he says that your Kindness said that you wished to confer with us, with good men sitting by. Which I greatly rejoice that you deigned to offer to our lowliness; nor can I in any way desert so great an occasion of your kindly disposition, but, as far as the Lord deigns to grant strength, I will seek out and discuss with you what cause, what origin, what reason there has been, in the Church of Christ, to whom he said: *My peace I give to you, my peace I leave to you*[^1], for so lamentable and deplorable a rending.

**3.** I heard indeed that you complained about the aforementioned brother, that he gave you some insulting answer: which, I beg you, do not reckon an insult; for it is certain to me that it did not proceed from a proud mind, for I know my brother; but if in arguing he said something perhaps too fervently for his faith and for the love of the Church, which your gravity would not wish to hear, that is to be called not contumacy, but confidence. For he desired to be a conferrer and debater, not a flatterer and fawner. For this is the oil of the sinner, with which the prophet does not wish his head to be made fat; for thus he says: *The just man shall correct me in mercy, and shall reprove me; but let not the oil of the sinner make fat my head*[^2]. For he prefers to be corrected by the severe mercy of the just than to be praised by the gentle anointing of flattery. Whence also that prophetic word: *Those who call you happy lead you into error*[^3]. And therefore of a man whom false flatteries make arrogant it is even commonly and rightly said: His head has grown; for it has been made fat with the oil of the sinner; that is, not with the harsh truth of one correcting, but with the gentle falsehood of one praising. Nor do I ask you to take this in such a way as if I wished it to be understood that I myself was corrected by brother Evodius as by a just man. For I fear lest you suppose that I too am saying something insulting against you, which I vehemently guard against, as much as I can. But he is just who said: *I am the Truth*[^4]. And so, when from the mouth of any man whatever the truth sounds to us with some harshness, we are corrected not by that man, who perhaps is a sinner, but by Truth itself, that is, by Christ, who is just; lest the anointing of flattering but ruinous adulation, that is, the oil of the sinner, make fat our head. Although even if brother Evodius, somewhat disturbed in defense of his communion, had said something more loftily with a too excited mind, you ought to pardon the man's age and the necessity of the case.

**4.** But what you deigned to promise, I beg you to remember: that, with men sitting by whom you yourself shall have chosen (provided our words are not vainly bandied about, but are taken down by the pen, so that we may discuss both more tranquilly and more orderly, and that, if perhaps something said by us should slip from memory, it may be recalled by the recitation), we may seek out with concord a matter so great and pertaining to the salvation of all. Or, if it pleases, with no intermediary interposed, let us first confer between ourselves, whether by letters, or by conversation and reading, wherever it shall please: lest perhaps some intemperate hearers prefer, as it were, to await our contest than to think about their own salvation in our conversation: so that what shall have been settled between us, the people may afterward learn through us; or if it pleases to act through letters, let those very letters be recited to the congregations, so that at length there may be said to be, not congregations, but one congregation. Just as you shall wish, just as you shall command, just as it shall please you, I gladly embrace it. And concerning the mind of the most blessed and venerable father Valerius, now absent, I promise with all assurance that he will learn this with great gladness: for I know how much he loves peace, and is puffed up by no emptiness of vain pride.

**5.** I ask you, what have we to do with old dissensions, that those wounds should have lasted until now which the animosity of proud men inflicted on our members; from the festering of which wounds we have even lost the pain by which the physician is wont to be summoned? You see with how great and how pitiable a foulness Christian homes and households have been disfigured. Husbands and wives agree together about their own bed, and disagree about the altar of Christ. By him they swear to one another, that they may have peace between themselves, and in him they cannot have it. Sons have one home of their own with their parents, and do not have one house of God. They desire to succeed to the inheritance of those with whom they quarrel about the inheritance of Christ. Slaves and masters divide their common Lord, who took the form of a servant[^5], that by serving he might free all. Your people honor us, our people honor you. By our crown your people adjure us, by your crown our people adjure you. We accept the words of all, we wish to offend no one. Why does Christ alone offend us, whose members we tear apart? And men indeed desiring to settle their worldly causes before us call us holy and servants of God in the manner we have been necessary to them, that they may transact the business of their earth; let us too at some time transact the business of our salvation and of their salvation, not about gold, not about silver, not about lands and herds, for which things we are daily greeted with bowed head, that we may settle the dissensions of men, but about our very head there is so base and ruinous a dissension between us. However much they bow their heads who greet us, that we may make them concordant on earth, from heaven even to the cross our head was bowed, in whom we are not concordant.

**6.** I ask you and beseech you, if there is in you any humanity, which many proclaim, let your goodness appear here, if it is not feigned for the sake of transitory honors, so that the bowels of mercy may quake within you, and you may wish that the matter be at some time discussed, by pressing on with prayers along with us, and conferring all things peaceably: lest the wretched congregations, which defer to our honors, oppress us by their deferences in the judgment of God; but rather, called back by our unfeigned love along with us from errors and dissensions, may be directed onto the paths of truth and peace. I wish you to be blessed before the eyes of God, honorable and most beloved lord.

[^2]: Ps 141:5 (140:5)
[^3]: See Isa 3:12
[^4]: John 14:6
[^5]: See Phil 2:7
