# Letter 92/A

TO THE LORD DESERVEDLY MOST SINCERE, AND HOLY BROTHER AND FELLOW-PRIEST CYPRIAN, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** I have sent letters to our blessed daughter Italica, which I ask that you yourself deign to deliver to her, in which I said something against the opinion of those who can hope nothing of God except what they feel about bodies—although they do not dare to say that God is a body. Yet in another way they altogether say this, when they assert that he can be seen by the eyes of the body, which he created only for beholding bodies. But these men seem to me to know neither what a body is, nor how far God, as Spirit, is from a body. On the occasion, then, on which I thought she was somehow to be consoled, I did not wish to pass over where the true consolation of the pilgrimage of us all is established. But what those who hold that opinion say against what we have tried to refute in few words, let your Holiness not find it burdensome to write to me, if perhaps her modesty should be reluctant to take up this conflict, as it were, even on another's presumption; or at least let your Charity bring it about that those who hold this opinion, and do not cease to scatter and trample these things everywhere, write back to me on these things which I have written, so that with them henceforth may be dealt what your holy prudence perceives with me ought to be dealt about this matter. For not for nothing is the rational soul, fed on such phantasms, shut off and utterly stopped up, lest it perceive that highest and unchangeable good, if its hope is fixed on this, that it presumes it will see God by the body. But I give thanks to your Charity, that you sent me to read the things which I asked for.
