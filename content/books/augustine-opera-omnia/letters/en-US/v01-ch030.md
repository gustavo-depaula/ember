# Letter 30

To the lord, brother of one mind and venerable, Augustine, Paulinus and Therasia, sinners

**1.** *Long ago, my brother of one mind in Christ the Lord, when I came to know you, unknowing, in your holy and pious labors, and saw you, absent, having embraced you with my whole mind, I hastened to approach you also with a familiar and brotherly address through letters. And I believe that, in the hand and the grace of the Lord, my discourse was brought to you; but, the boy still delaying whom we had sent before winter to greet you and others alike beloved of God, we could no longer suspend our duty and temper our most eager longing for your discourse. And so we have now written a second time, if our former letter deserved to reach you; or for the first time, if it did not have the good fortune of reaching your hands.*

**2.** *But you, a spiritual brother who judges all things*[^1], do not weigh our love for you by duty alone, or by the date of the letter. For the Lord is witness, who, one and the same, works[^2] his love in his own everywhere, that from the time when we came to know you, by the kindness of the venerable bishops Aurelius and Alypius, through your works against the Manichees, your love was so implanted in us that we seemed to ourselves not to be taking up some new friendship, but as it were to be resuming an old love. And so now, even if we write as those untrained in discourse, yet not as those untrained in affection, and in turn we, as it were, recognize you again in spirit through the inner man. Nor is it strange if, though absent, we are present to one another, and, though unknown, we know one another; since we are members of one body[^3], have one head, are flooded by one grace, live by one bread, walk on one road, dwell in the same house. Finally, in all that we are, with the whole hope and faith by which we stand in the present, we strive toward the future; as much in the spirit as in the body of the Lord we are one, lest we be nothing if we fall away from the One.

**3.** *How little, then, is that which bodily absence begrudges us of one another—unless indeed that fruit by which the eyes of those who watch for temporal things are fed? Although not even bodily favor ought to be called temporal among spiritual men, to whom the resurrection will lavish even the eternity of their bodies, as we dare to presume, even unworthy, in the power of Christ and the goodness of God the Father. Wherefore, would that the grace of God might grant us this gift too, through our Lord Jesus Christ, that we might see your face even in the flesh! Not only would great joy be conferred on our longings, but light too would grow upon our minds, and from your abundance our poverty would be enriched. Which indeed you can lavish even on the absent, especially on this occasion, on which our sons, of one mind and most dear to us in the Lord, Romanus and Agilis—whom, as we do others, we commend to you—will return in the name of the Lord, the work of love accomplished; in which we ask that they may especially make use of the affection of your love. For you know how lofty things the Most High promises to a brother helping a brother*[^4]. Through these men, if you should wish to repay me with any gift of the grace given to you, you will safely do so. For they are, I would have you believe, one heart and one soul[^5] in the Lord with us. The grace of God be with you, as it is, forever, brother in Christ the Lord of one mind, venerable, most beloved, and desirable. Greet from us all the saints in Christ, such as without doubt cleave to you. Commend us to all the saints, that they may deign to pray with you for us.

[^1]: See 1 Cor 2:15
[^2]: See 1 Cor 12:6, 11
[^3]: See Rom 12:5; 1 Cor 12:12
[^4]: See Prov 18:19
[^5]: See Acts 4:32
