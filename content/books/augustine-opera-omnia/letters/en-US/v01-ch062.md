# Letter 62

To the most blessed and venerably dearest and most sincere lord, brother and fellow priest Severus, and the brothers who are with you, Alypius, Augustine, and Samsucius, and the brothers who are with us, send greeting in the Lord

**1.** When we had come to Subsana, and were inquiring into the things which had there been done in our absence against our will; some things as we had heard, but some done otherwise, all however to be grieved over and to be tolerated, as the Lord helped us, partly by chiding, partly by admonishing, partly by praying, we corrected. That indeed which, after your Holiness's departure, saddened us most, that the brothers were sent from there without a guide for the journey, we ask you to pardon, and to know it was done more timidly than maliciously. For when they thought that they were sent by our son Timothy for this purpose, especially to provoke your Charity against us to anger, but themselves wished to keep all things intact for our arrival, which they hoped would be with you, they thought they would not set out if they did not receive a guide for the journey. But yet that it was a sin, who doubts? Hence too it came about that it was even said to Fossor that Timothy had now set out with those same brothers; which surely was false: yet not said by the presbyter; and that brother Carcedonius was wholly ignorant of all these things has been most manifestly declared to us, as far as these things are wont to be made manifest.

**2.** But why should we delay with more words? Our aforesaid son Timothy, most vehemently disturbed because, most unwilling, he had felt so unexpected a doubt, indicated to us that when you were dealing with him that he should serve God at Subsana, he burst out and swore that he would by no means withdraw from you. And when we inquired into his will, he answered that he was hindered by this oath from being where we wished him to be even before; especially since he is now secure about the manifestation of his freedom. And when we had disclosed to him that he would not be guilty of perjury, if not through him, but through you it came about that, on account of avoiding scandal, he could not be with you; since he could swear not about your will, but about his own, nor did he confess that you had in turn sworn anything to him; at last he said, what a servant of God, a son of the Church, ought to say, that whatever it should please us, with your Holiness, to be done about him, that he would without doubt follow. Accordingly we ask, and through the love of Christ beseech your prudence, that you remember all the things we have spoken, and gladden us with your reply. For we, the firmer ones (if indeed amid such great perils of temptations this must be dared to say), ought, as the Apostle says, to sustain the burdens of the weak[^1]. Brother Timothy did not write to your Holiness for this reason, because your holy brother indicated all the things that were done. Mindful of us, may you glory in the Lord, most blessed and venerably dearest and most sincere lord and brother.

[^1]: See Rom 15:1
