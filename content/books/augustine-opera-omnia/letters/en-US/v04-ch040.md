# Letter 248

TO SEBASTIAN, A HOLY AND DESIRABLE LORD, AND, IN THE HONOR OF CHRIST, A MOST SWEET BROTHER, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Although the sweet bond of love by no means permits you to be separated from our mind, and we unceasingly recall your holy morals and conversations; yet you did well, and we give thanks, that, by sending us notes that are messengers also of your bodily health, you have greatly gladdened us. But I perceived in your letter that a weariness held you back from the sinners who forsake the law of God[^1]: for you live by that spirit by which it was said: *I saw the senseless, and I wasted away*[^2]. That is a devout sadness, and, if it may be said, a blessed wretchedness—to be troubled by others' vices, not entangled in them; to mourn, not to cling; to be drawn together by grief, not drawn in by love. This is the persecution which *all who wish to live devoutly in Christ suffer, according to the biting and truthful apostolic sentence*[^3]. For what here more persecutes the life of the good than the life of the wicked; not when it compels to imitate what displeases, but when it compels to grieve at what it sees? Since one living impiously before the devout, even if it does not bind the consenting, tortures the perceiving. For often and long the bodies of the impious are spared by the secular powers and from the vexations of all sorts; but the hearts of the devout are never, even to the end of this world, spared the evil morals of men. So, then, is rather fulfilled what I mentioned the Apostle said: *That all who wish to live devoutly in Christ will suffer persecution*[^4]; and the more bitterly the more inwardly, until the flood passes, where the ark holds the raven and the dove[^5].

**2.** But cling, brother, to him from whom you have heard: *He who perseveres to the end, he will be saved*[^6]. *Join yourself to the Lord, that your life may increase in the last days*[^7]. For I know that there is no lack of refreshment of heart from the good brothers. To this add the faithful, great, sure, eternal promises of God, and the imperturbable and ineffable reward of that very endurance: and see how truly you sing to the Lord: *According to the multitude of my sorrows in my heart, your consolations have gladdened my soul*[^8]. Send our letter to brother Firmus. Your Holiness, and the household of God which is governed by your ministry, the brothers and sisters who are with us greet in return, along with us, in the Lord. (*And in another hand*): May you, safe and sound, pray for us, most beloved and holy brothers. *I, Alypius, greet most lavishly your Sincerity, and all who are joined to you in the Lord; and I ask that you reckon this letter as my own: for although I could have sent another of my own, yet I preferred to subscribe to this one, that one page too might testify our unanimity.*

[^2]: See Ps 119:158 (118:158)
[^3]: See 2 Tim 3:12
[^4]: 2 Tim 3:12
[^5]: See Gen 7-8

[^7]: See Sir 6:28; 2:3
