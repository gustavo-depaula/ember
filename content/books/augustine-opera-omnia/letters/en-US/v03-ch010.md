# Letter 165

To the truly holy lords and, in all charity of services, to be venerated, his sons Marcellinus and Anapsychia, from Jerome, greetings in Christ.

**1. 1.** At last I have received from Africa the letters of your concord; and I do not repent of the boldness by which, you being silent, I frequently pressed my letters upon you, that I might deserve a reply, and might learn that you are safe, not from others announcing it, but rather from your own discourse. Concerning the state of the soul I remember your little question, or rather a very great ecclesiastical question: whether it has fallen from heaven, as the philosopher Pythagoras and all the Platonists and Origen think; or is from the proper substance of God, as the Stoics, Mani, and the Spanish heresy of Priscillian suspect; or whether they are held in a treasury of God, made long ago, as certain churchmen trust by a foolish persuasion; or whether they are daily made by God and sent into bodies, according to what is written in the Gospel, *My Father works until now, and I work*[^1]; or whether they come by transmission, as Tertullian, Apollinaris, and the greatest part of the Westerners maintain, so that as body is born of body, so soul is born of soul, and subsists by a condition like that of the brute animals. On which, what seemed to me, I know I wrote in the little works against Rufinus—against that book which he gave to Anastasius of holy memory, bishop of the Roman Church; in which, by a slippery and crafty, or rather foolish, confession, while he strives to mock the simplicity of his hearers, he mocked his own faith, or rather his perfidy: which books I think your holy father Oceanus has; for they were published long ago, answering the calumnies in many books against Rufinus. Certainly you have there a holy and learned man, the bishop Augustine, who can teach you, as they say, by the living voice, and set forth his own—or rather, through himself, our—opinion.

**2. 2.** Some time ago I wished to take up the book of Ezekiel, and to fulfill the very frequent promise to my zealous readers; but at the very outset of dictating, my mind was so confounded by the devastation of the western provinces, and especially of the city of Rome, that, according to the common proverb, I was ignorant even of my own name: and for a long time I was silent, knowing that it was a time of tears. But this year, when I had unfolded three books, a sudden onslaught of barbarians—of whom your Virgil says, *and the widely wandering Barcaeans*, and holy Scripture says of Ishmael, *He will dwell against the face of all his brothers*[^2]—so overran the border of Egypt, of Palestine, Phoenicia, Syria, dragging all things with it like a torrent, that we could scarcely escape their hands by the mercy of Christ. For if, according to the renowned orator, the laws are silent amid arms, how much more the studies of the Scriptures, which need both a multitude of books, and silence, and the diligence of copyists, and—what is proper to them—the security and leisure of those dictating! I have therefore sent two books to my holy daughter Fabiola, copies of which, if you wish, you may borrow from her. For owing to the narrowness of the time I could not have others transcribed: which, when you have read them and seen the vestibules, it will be an easy conjecture what the house itself will be. But I trust in the mercy of God, who helped us in the most difficult beginning of the aforesaid work, that he himself will help also in the next-to-last parts of the prophet, in which the wars of Gog and Magog are narrated; and in the last parts, in which the building, variety, and measure of the most sacred and inexplicable temple is described.

**3. 3.** Our holy brother Oceanus, to whom you desire to be commended, is so great and such a man, and so learned in the law of the Lord, that without our asking he can instruct you, and set forth our opinion on all the questions of the Scriptures, according to the measure of our common ability. May Christ our God, the Almighty, watch over you, safe and flourishing in a long life, truly holy lords.

[^1]: Jn 5:17
[^2]: Gen 16:12
