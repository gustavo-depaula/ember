# Letter 196

TO ASELLICUS, MOST BLESSED LORD, BROTHER AND FELLOW BISHOP, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1. 1.** The letter of your Holiness, which you sent to the venerable old man Donatian on the dispute about avoiding Judaism, he himself deigned to send to me, and, by asking more vehemently, commanded that I should reply to it: and fearing to disregard him, I reply as I can with the Lord's help, judging it agreeable to your Charity too that, in writing to you, I have not refused to comply with him who commanded it—him whom, for his merits, we both venerate.

**1. 2.** That Christians, especially those coming from the Gentiles, ought not to judaize, the apostle Paul teaches when he says: *I said to Peter before them all, If you, being a Jew, live like the Gentiles and not like the Jews, how do you compel the Gentiles to judaize?*[^1] And following on, he added: *We who are Jews by nature, and not sinners from among the Gentiles, knowing that a man is not justified by the works of the Law, but through faith in Jesus Christ, we too have believed in Jesus Christ, that we might be justified by faith in Christ, and not by the works of the Law; because by the works of the Law no flesh shall be justified*[^2].

**1. 3.** Now not only do those works of the Law which are in the old sacraments, and which now, the New Testament being revealed, are not observed by Christians—such as the circumcision of the foreskin, the carnal rest of the Sabbath, abstinence from certain foods, the immolation of cattle in sacrifices, the new moon, the unleavened bread, and the rest of this kind—not only do these not justify a man, but even that which was said in the Law, *You shall not covet*[^3] (which surely no one doubts must be said to Christians too), does not justify a man except through faith in Jesus Christ and the grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord. For the same Apostle says: *What then shall we say? Is the Law sin? By no means. But I did not know sin except through the Law; for I did not know concupiscence, unless the Law said, You shall not covet. But sin, taking occasion, wrought in me through the commandment all concupiscence. For without the Law sin was dead. But I lived once without the Law. But when the commandment came, sin revived; and I died; and the commandment which was unto life was found by me to be unto death. For sin, taking occasion through the commandment, deceived me, and through it slew me. And so the Law indeed is holy, and the commandment holy and just and good. Did then that which is good become death to me? By no means. But sin, that it might appear sin, wrought death in me through what is good; that sin, through the commandment, might become sinful beyond measure. For we know that the Law is spiritual; but I am carnal, sold under sin. For what I do, I do not understand; for I do not do what I will, but what I hate, that I do. But if I do what I do not will, I consent to the Law, that it is good*[^4].

**1. 4.** We see, then, in these apostolic words, that the Law is not only not sin, but is even holy, and the commandment by which it was said *You shall not covet* holy and just and good. But sin deceives through what is good, and through it slays those who, since they are carnal, suppose that by their own powers they can fulfill the spiritual Law; and through this they become not only sinners, which they would be even if they had not received the Law, but also transgressors, which they would not be unless they had received the Law: for so he says in another place, *Where there is no Law, neither is there transgression*[^5]. *The Law, then, entered in*, as he likewise testifies elsewhere, *that the offense might abound. But where the offense abounded, grace abounded the more*[^6].

**2. 5.** This, then, is the usefulness of the Law: that it shows a man to himself, so that he may know his weakness and see how, through prohibition, carnal concupiscence is increased rather than healed. For things forbidden are desired more ardently, while the carnal person is compelled to observe what is spiritually commanded. But that one may become spiritual, so as to fulfill the spiritual Law, this comes about not by the Law itself, but by grace; that is, not by command, but by benefit; not by the letter commanding, but by the Spirit helping. And a man begins, according to grace, to be renewed in the inner man, so that he may do in mind what he loves, and not consent to the flesh doing what he hates; that is, not so that he does not covet at all, but so that he does not go after his concupiscences. And this indeed is so great that, even if it came about in every way—and although, while we are in the body of this death, the desires of sin are present, if we nevertheless gave assent to none of them—there would be no reason why we should say to our Father who is in heaven, *Forgive us our debts*[^7]. Nor would we therefore already be such as we shall be when this mortal puts on immortality: for then we shall not only obey no desire of sin, but there will be no such desires which we are commanded not to obey.

**2. 6.** Now, then, where it is said, *It is no longer I who do it, but the sin that dwells in me*[^8], it is said of the concupiscence of the flesh, which works its motions in us, even when we do not obey them, so long as *sin does not reign in our mortal body so that we obey its desires, nor do we yield our members as weapons of iniquity to sin*[^9]: persevering and progressing in which righteousness not yet completed, we shall at some time come to its completion, where the concupiscence of sin is not to be restrained and bridled, but is none at all. For the Law set this down by saying *You shall not covet*—not as what we can achieve here, but as that toward which by progressing we tend. But this comes about not by the Law which commands it, but by faith which obtains it; not by the letter by which it is ordered, but by the Spirit by which it is given: therefore not by the merits of the laboring man, but by the grace of the bestowing Savior. And so the usefulness of the Law is that it may convict a man of his weakness, and compel him to implore the medicine of grace which is in Christ. *For everyone who calls upon the name of the Lord shall be saved. How then shall they call upon him in whom they have not believed? How shall they believe him whom they have not heard?*[^10] Whence a little later he says: *Faith, therefore, is from hearing; and hearing through the word of Christ*[^11].

**2. 7.** Since these things are so, those who rejoice that they are Israelites carnally, and glory in the Law apart from the grace of Christ, are those of whom the Apostle likewise says that, *being ignorant of God's righteousness, and wishing to establish their own, they are not subject to the righteousness of God*[^12]. For he called it God's righteousness, which comes to a man from God; but their own, which they suppose suffices for them to do the commandments without the help and gift of him who gave the Law. And like these are those who, although they profess to be Christians, so oppose the very grace of Christ that they think they fulfill the divine commandments by human powers; and thus they too, being ignorant of God's righteousness and wishing to establish their own, are not subject to the righteousness of God, and judaize—not indeed in name, but yet in error. This kind of men had found for themselves, as heads, Pelagius and Celestius, the keenest advocates of this impiety, who by the recent judgment of God, through his diligent and faithful servants, have been deprived even of catholic communion, and, on account of their impenitent heart, still persist in their damnation.

**2. 8.** Whoever seeks to be a stranger to this carnal and animal—and therefore not undeservedly reprehensible and damnable—Judaism, ought to make estranged from himself not only those old observances which, the New Testament being now revealed, after those things came which were signified by them as future, doubtless ceased to be necessary, so that *he is not judged in food and drink, or in respect of a feast day, or a new moon, or sabbaths, which are a shadow of things to come*[^13]; but even those which are so commanded in the Law that they avail to form the morals of the faithful—that is, that, *renouncing impiety and worldly desires, we may live temperately and justly and piously in this world*[^14]; whence is also that which the Apostle especially chose to commend out of the Law, *You shall not covet*[^15], and whatever about loving God and neighbor is commanded in the Law without any figures of sacraments, on which two commandments the Lord Christ himself says that the whole Law and the Prophets depend—these he so accepts, so embraces, so does not doubt must be observed, that whatever he gains in them he attributes not to himself, but to the grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord.

**3. 9.** Nevertheless, when anyone has in this way become a true and genuine Christian, whether he should also be called a Jew or an Israelite is deservedly asked. And if this is understood to be so not in flesh but in spirit, he ought not to impose the name itself on himself in the usage of speech, but to retain it by spiritual understanding, lest, on account of the ambiguity of the word, which daily speech does not distinguish, he seem to profess what is hostile to the Christian name. And this question—that is, whether one who is a Christian can also be understood to be a Jew or an Israelite—the same blessed Apostle opens and resolves for us, where he says: *Circumcision indeed profits, if you keep the Law: but if you are a transgressor of the Law, your circumcision has become uncircumcision. If, then, the uncircumcision keeps the precepts of the Law, shall not its uncircumcision be reckoned for circumcision, and shall not that which by nature is uncircumcision, fulfilling the Law, judge you, who through the letter and circumcision are a transgressor of the Law? For he is not a Jew who is one outwardly, nor is that circumcision which is outward in the flesh; but he is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart, in the spirit, not in the letter; whose praise is not from men, but from God*[^16]. Since, then, we hear the Apostle of Christ commending to us the Jew in secret, not by circumcision of the flesh but of the heart, and in the spirit, not the letter—who is this, if not a Christian?

**3. 10.** And so we are Jews, not carnally but spiritually, just as we are the seed of Abraham not according to the flesh, like those who glory in that very name with carnal pride, but according to the spirit of faith, which they are not. For we know that we were promised, when God said to him, *I have made you a father of many nations*[^17]. And we know how much the Apostle says on this matter. *For we say*, he says, *that faith was reckoned to Abraham for righteousness. How then was it reckoned? When he was in circumcision, or in uncircumcision? Not in circumcision, but in uncircumcision. And he received the sign of circumcision, a seal of the righteousness of the faith which was in uncircumcision: that he might be the father of all who believe through uncircumcision, that it might be reckoned to them also for righteousness; and that he might be the father of circumcision, not only to those who are of the circumcision, but also to those who follow the footsteps of the faith which our father Abraham had in uncircumcision*[^18]. And a little later: *Therefore*, he says, *it is of faith, that according to grace the promise might be firm to all the seed; not to that only which is of the Law, but also to that which is of the faith of Abraham, who is the father of us all, as it is written, For I have made you a father of many nations*[^19]. Likewise to the Galatians: *As Abraham*, he says, *believed God, and it was reckoned to him for righteousness. Understand, then, that those who are of faith, these are the children of Abraham. And the Scripture, foreseeing that God justifies the Gentiles by faith, foretold to Abraham, In you shall all the nations be blessed: so that those who are of faith are blessed with faithful Abraham*[^20]. Now somewhat later in the same letter: *Brethren*, he says, *I speak after the manner of men; yet no one annuls or adds to a man's confirmed testament. To Abraham the promises were made, and to his seed. He does not say, And to seeds, as of many; but as of one, And to your seed, which is Christ*[^21]. Likewise a little later: *For you are all one in Christ Jesus*, he says; *and if you are Christ's, then you are the seed of Abraham, heirs according to the promise*[^22].

**3. 11.** According to this apostolic understanding, then, the Jews who are not Christians, although they trace their origin from the flesh of Abraham, are found not to be the children of Abraham. For when he says, *Understand, then, that those who are of faith, these are the children of Abraham*[^23], he surely signifies that those who are not of faith are not the children of Abraham. And thereby, unless Abraham is a father to the Jews in the way that he is to us, what does it profit them that they have sprung from his flesh and have held the name without the power? But when they pass over to Christ and begin to be by faith the children of Abraham, then they will be Jews, not outwardly but inwardly, by circumcision of the heart, in the spirit, not the letter; whose praise is not from men but from God. But estranged from this faith, they will be reckoned among the broken branches of that olive tree into whose root the same Apostle says the wild olive—that is, the Gentiles—is grafted; which surely happens not through the flesh but through faith, nor through the Law but through grace, nor through the letter but through the spirit, nor by circumcision of the flesh but of the heart, nor outwardly but inwardly, nor by praise from men but from God: so that each Christian is, as a son of Abraham not carnal but spiritual, so also a Jew not carnal but spiritual, and an Israelite not carnal but spiritual. For about this name too the Apostle speaks thus: *For not all who are of Israel, these are Israel; nor are they all children because they are the seed of Abraham; but in Isaac shall your seed be called: that is, not those who are the children of the flesh are the children of God, but the children of the promise are reckoned as seed*[^24]. Are these not great wonders, and a deep mystery, that many born of Israel are not Israel, and that many are not children, though they are the seed of Abraham? For how are they not, in the way that we are? Except that they are not children of the promise belonging to the grace of Christ, but children of the flesh bearing an empty name? And thereby neither are they Israel as we are, nor are we Israel as they are. For we are so according to spiritual regeneration, they according to carnal generation.

**3. 12.** For it must be observed and distinguished that one Israel is that which received the name on account of the flesh, but another that which obtained, on account of the spirit, the reality signified by that name. For were Israelites born from Hagar, Sarah's handmaid? Was not Ishmael born from her, and did he not propagate by his seed not the nation of the Israelites but of the Ishmaelites? But from Sarah came Israel through Isaac, who was born of the promise to Abraham. Yet, although in respect of carnal propagation the matter so stands, one comes to the spiritual understanding, and it is found that the carnal Israelites, who trace their carnal origin from her, do not belong to Sarah; and that there belong to her rather the Christians, who are not, like Ishmael, children of the flesh, but, like Isaac, children of the promise, belonging not to Isaac's carnal seed but to the spiritual mystery. For thus the Apostle speaks to the Galatians: *Tell me, you who wish to be under the Law, have you not heard the Law? For it is written that Abraham had two sons, one by a handmaid, and one by a free woman. But he who was of the handmaid was born according to the flesh; but he who was of the free woman, through the promise. These things are said by way of allegory. For these are the two Testaments. The one indeed from Mount Sinai, bringing forth unto bondage, which is Hagar: for Sinai is a mountain in Arabia, which is joined to the Jerusalem that now is, for she is in bondage with her children. But the Jerusalem which is above is free, which is our mother. For it is written, Rejoice, you barren one who do not bear; break forth and cry, you who do not travail; for many are the children of the desolate, more than of her who has a husband. But we, brethren, are, like Isaac, children of the promise. But as then he who was born according to the flesh persecuted him who was born according to the spirit, so also now. But what does the Scripture say? Cast out the handmaid and her son; for the son of the handmaid shall not be heir with the son of the free woman. But we, brethren, are not children of the handmaid, but of the free woman; by which freedom Christ has set us free*[^25].

**3. 13.** Behold, according to this spiritual and apostolic understanding, we rather belong to the free woman Sarah, who draw no carnal propagation from her; while the Jews, who draw carnal propagation from her, are shown to belong rather to the handmaid Hagar, from whom they draw no carnal propagation. In the grandsons too of Abraham and Sarah—that is, the sons of Isaac and Rebecca, those twins Esau and Jacob (who was afterward called Israel)—this same great and deep mystery is found. Speaking of which, the same Apostle, when he had recalled, through Isaac, the children of the promise belonging to the grace of Christ, says: *And not only that, but also Rebecca, having conceived from one act of intercourse with Isaac our father. For when they were not yet born, nor had done anything good or evil, that according to election the purpose of God might stand, not from works but from him who calls, it was said to her, The elder shall serve the younger; as it is written, Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated*[^26]. This apostolic and catholic teaching certainly indicates to us plainly enough that, according to the origin of the flesh, the Jews—that is, the Israelites—belong to Sarah, but the Ishmaelites to Hagar; while, according to the mystery of the spirit, the Christians belong to Sarah, the Jews to Hagar. Likewise, according to the origin of the flesh, the nation of the Idumeans belongs to Esau, who was also called Edom, but the nation of the Jews to Jacob, who was also called Israel; whereas, according to the mystery of the spirit, the Jews belong to Esau, the Christians to Israel. For thus is fulfilled what is written, *The elder shall serve the younger*—that is, the people of the Jews, born first, shall serve the people of the Christians, born later. Behold how we are Israel by divine adoption, glorying not in human kinship; and Jews not outwardly but in secret, not by the letter but by the spirit, not by circumcision of the flesh but of the heart.

**4. 14.** Since these things are so, we ought not, nevertheless, to confound the usage of human speech with inept loquacity, and, in distinguishing things, to mix common words with a disturbed signification—so that someone should affect to call by the unaccustomed word "Jews" those who are Christians and are called by the most usual appellation Christians; or, although he is and is called a Christian, should delight rather in the name of Israelite; and what he ought always to understand in mystery, but to bring forth more sparingly with the mouth, this he should rather choose to make frequent in the daily usage of speaking, by inept insolence and, if it can be said, unskilled knowledge. For did the Apostles, through whom we learned these things, not know them—how we are rather the seed of Abraham, heirs of the promise according to Isaac; and Jews in spirit, not the letter, by circumcision of the heart, not the flesh; and Israel not according to the flesh, but the Israel of God? They surely knew these things much more wisely and certainly than we; and yet in the usage of speaking they called Jews and Israelites those who, coming according to the flesh from the stock of Abraham, were commonly called by this name by all.

**4. 15.** *The Jews ask for signs*, says the apostle Paul, *and the Greeks seek wisdom: but we preach Christ crucified, to the Jews indeed a stumbling block, but to the Gentiles foolishness; but to those who are called, both Jews and Greeks, Christ the Power of God and the Wisdom of God*[^27]. Those whom he called Greeks he signified also by the name of Gentiles, because this language especially excels among the Gentiles; yet the Jews themselves he called those whom all call by this name. For if the Christians themselves are Jews, then to the Christians Christ crucified is a stumbling block, of whom it was said, *to the Jews indeed a stumbling block*. Who but a most insane person would hold this? Likewise he says: *Give no offense to Jews or Greeks or to the Church of God*[^28]. How would he distinguish these, if he ought to call the Church of God too "Jews" in the daily usage of speaking? Likewise he says: *Whom he has also called, namely us, not only from the Jews but also from the Gentiles*[^29]. How did he call from the Jews, if he rather called from the non-Jews, that they might be Jews? Likewise concerning the Israelites: *What then shall we say?* he says; *That the Gentiles, who did not pursue righteousness, attained righteousness; the righteousness, that is, which is of faith. But Israel, pursuing the law of righteousness, did not arrive at the law of righteousness. Why? Because it was not of faith, but as if of works: for they stumbled at the stone of stumbling*[^30]. Likewise: *But to Israel what does he say? All the day I have stretched out my hands to a people that does not believe and contradicts*[^31]. And following on, he adds: *I say then, Has God cast off his people? By no means. For I too am an Israelite, of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin. God has not cast off his people, whom he foreknew*[^32]. How here did the Apostle call Israel unbelieving and contradicting, if the Christians are Israel; or how did he call himself an Israelite? Was it from the fact that he had become a Christian? Surely not; but from the fact that according to the flesh he was of the seed of Abraham, of the tribe of Benjamin—which we are not according to the flesh, although according to faith we are the seed of Abraham, and thereby also Israel. But it is one thing that the understanding of the deeper sacrament recognizes, another that the daily usage of the word demands.

**4. 16.** Finally, this Aptus, whoever he is, of whom you wrote that he teaches Christians to judaize, calls himself, as your Holiness intimated, a Jew and an Israelite in such a way that he forbids the foods which the Law, given through God's holy servant Moses, forbade for the fittingness of that time, and urges the other observances of that time, now abolished and removed among Christians—which the Apostle calls *shadows of things to come*[^33], so that in them both the prophecy may be understood and their observance shown to be now made void: whence it appears why this Aptus wishes to be called an Israelite and a Jew, not spiritually but altogether carnally. But we are neither held bound by those observances which, the New Testament being revealed, are made void, and the precepts of the Law necessary even for this time—such as *You shall not commit adultery, You shall not kill, You shall not covet*, and if there is any other commandment, which is summed up in this saying, *You shall love your neighbor as yourself*[^34]—we have learned and we teach must be observed, not by human powers, as though establishing our own righteousness, but by the grace of God through Jesus Christ our Lord, in that righteousness which is to us from him. Nor do we for that reason deny that we are the seed of Abraham, to whom the Apostle says, *You, then, are the seed of Abraham*[^35]; or Jews in secret, of whom the same says, *For he is not a Jew who is one outwardly, nor is that circumcision which is outward in the flesh; but he is a Jew who is one inwardly, and circumcision is of the heart, in the spirit, not the letter, whose praise is not from men but from God*[^36]; or spiritual Israelites, belonging, namely, to him to whom it was foretold that the elder would serve the younger: but we impose these names on ourselves not unbecomingly, and we contain them by the understanding of mysteries, not bandy them about by the insolence of words.
