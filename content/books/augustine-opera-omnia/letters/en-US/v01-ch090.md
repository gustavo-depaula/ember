# Letter 90

TO THE DISTINGUISHED LORD, AND DESERVEDLY ESTEEMED BROTHER, BISHOP AUGUSTINE, NECTARIUS.

**1.** How great the love of one's native land is, since you know, I pass over. For it alone is the love that overcomes the affection owed to parents by right. If there were any measure or limit to consulting it for good men, we would already worthily have deserved to be excused from its duties. But since the love and favor of one's city grows with each single day, and the nearer one's age is to its end, the more one desires to leave one's native land safe and flourishing: *for this reason I rejoice, first, that this discourse of mine has been set before a man instructed in all the disciplines. In the colony of Calama there are many things which we deservedly love: whether that we were born in it, or that we seem to have conferred great services on it. This, then, most excellent lord, and deservedly to be esteemed, has fallen through no light error of its people. Which indeed, if we measure it by the rigor of public law, ought to be struck with a severer censure. But it is not lawful for a bishop except to impart salvation to men, and to be present for cases for a better state, and before the omnipotent God to deserve pardon for the faults of others. Wherefore, with all the supplication I can, I beseech that, if the matter is to be defended, the harmless be defended, that trouble be separated from the innocent. Grant this, which, according to your nature, you perceive is being asked. Of damages an easy assessment can be made; we deprecate only the punishments. May you live more acceptable to God, distinguished lord, and deservedly to be esteemed brother.*
