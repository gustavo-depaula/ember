# Letter 48

To the beloved and most longed-for lord, brother and fellow presbyter Eudoxius, and the brothers who are with you, Augustine, and the brothers who are with me, send greeting in the Lord

**1.** When we think of your rest, which you have in Christ, we too, although we are engaged in various and harsh labors, rest in your love. For we are one body under one head, so that you are busy in us, and we are at leisure in you, because *if one member suffers, all the members suffer with it; and if one member is glorified, all the members rejoice with it*[^1]. We therefore admonish and ask and beseech, by the most high humility and most merciful loftiness of Christ, that you be mindful of us in your holy prayers, which we believe you have more vigilant and more sober: for ours the fog and tumult of secular actions often wounds and weakens; which, although we do not have as ours, yet, of those who compel us to go a mile, and we are bidden to go with them another two[^2], are heaped upon us so greatly that we can scarcely breathe: yet trusting that he in whose sight enters the groaning of the fettered[^3], will, as we persevere in that ministry in which he has deigned to place us with a promised reward, with your prayers aiding, free us from all anguish.

**2.** But you, brothers, we exhort in the Lord that you guard your purpose, and persevere even to the end[^4]: and if mother Church should desire any work of yours, neither take it up with greedy elation, nor reject it with flattering sloth; but with meek heart obey God, with gentleness bearing him who rules you, who directs the meek in judgment, who teaches the gentle his ways[^5]. Nor prefer your leisure to the necessities of the Church, to which, in her travail, if no good men were willing to minister, you would not find how you should be born. But just as a way must be kept between fire and water, so that a man neither be burned up nor drowned; so between the peak of pride and the abyss of sloth we ought to temper our journey, as it is written: *Not turning aside, either to the right or to the left*[^6]. For there are those who, while they too greatly fear lest, snatched as it were to the right, they be exalted, slipping to the left, are drowned. And there are again those who, while they too greatly withdraw themselves from the left, lest they be swallowed by the torpid softness of leisure, corrupted and consumed on the other side by the pride of boasting, vanish into ash and smoke. So then, most beloved, love leisure in such a way that you restrain yourselves from all earthly delight, and remember that there is no place where he cannot stretch his snares who fears lest we fly back to God; and let us judge the enemy of all good things, whose captives we were; and let us think that there is no perfect rest for us, *until iniquity pass away, and judgment be turned into justice*[^7].

**3.** Likewise, when you do anything vigorously and cheerfully and work tirelessly, whether in prayers, or in fasts, or in almsgiving; whether giving something to the needy, or forgiving injuries, *as God too in Christ forgave us*[^8]; whether taming ruinous habits, and chastising the body, and bringing it into servitude[^9]; whether enduring tribulation, and before all things one another in love—for what does he endure who does not endure a brother?—whether looking out for the cunning and ambushes of the tempter, and with the shield of faith repelling and extinguishing his fiery darts[^10]; whether *singing and making melody in your hearts to the Lord*[^11], or with voices not out of tune with the heart, *do all things to the glory of God, who works all things in all*[^12]; and so be fervent in spirit that your soul may be praised in the Lord[^13]. For this is the action of the right journey, which always has its eyes toward the Lord, since he himself will pluck the feet out of the snare[^14]. Such action is neither chilled by business, nor cold in leisure, neither turbulent nor languid; neither bold nor fugitive; neither headlong nor prostrate. Do these things, and the God of peace will be with you[^15].

**4.** Nor let your Charity think me importunate, because I wished to speak to you even by letter. For I have not admonished you of this which I do not think you do; but I believed that I am no little commended to God by you, if you do the things which you do by his gift, with the memory of our address. For both before by fame, and now the brothers who have come from you, Eustasius and Andreas, have brought to us the good odor of Christ from your holy manner of life. Of whom Eustasius has gone before into that rest which, like an island, is beaten by no waves, nor desires Capraria, because it does not now seek to be clothed in haircloth.
