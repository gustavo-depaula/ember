# Letter 240

PASCENTIUS, TO AUGUSTINE

**1.** *I had wished, most beloved brother, that you would lay aside the counsel of your former error; now I wonder that you still remain in it, as the letter you sent me cries out. For Your Dignity is like one thirsting, kindled by too great heat, who, if he finds muddy water and gorges himself on it; afterward, even if, finding clear and cold water, he drinks, cannot profit enough, since his heart and soul are once overwhelmed with mud. Finally, by your leave I shall say it, the counsel of Your Excellence is like a tree crooked and knotty, which has nothing straight in it, and perverts the keenness of the eyes. Your Holiness writes back to me that the Father is God, the Son is God, the Holy Spirit is God, but one God. Which of the three is the one God? or is there perhaps one person, threefold, which is named by this name? You, if you had wished and trusted in your profession, would sit with me, with your fellow bishops, with a pure and peaceful mind and spirit, and confer about the things that are of God, and that pertain to glory and spiritual grace. Hence, what need is there to write and write back what does not edify us?*
