# Letter 53

To the most beloved and honorable brother Generosus, Fortunatus, Alypius, and Augustine send greeting in the Lord

**1. 1.** Since you wished the letter which a presbyter of the Donatists gave to you to be known to us—although you too, with a catholic mind, laughed at it: yet, that you may rather take thought for him, if he does not rave despairingly, we ask that you bring these things written back to him. For he wrote that an angel had bidden him intimate to you the order of the Christianity of your city, while you hold the Christianity not of your city only, nor only of Africa or of the Africans, but of the whole world of lands, which has been announced and is announced to all nations. Whence it is too little for them that they are not ashamed to be cut off, nor help themselves, that, when they can, they return to the root, unless they also try to cut off others with themselves, and, like dry wood, to destine them for the fire. Wherefore, if an angel himself had stood by you—whom that man, as we judge, with cunning vanity feigns to have stood by him on your account—and had said to you these very things which this man says he intimates to you by his command, you ought to be mindful of the apostolic sentence, who says: *Even if we, or an angel from heaven, should preach to you a gospel other than that which we preached to you, let him be anathema*[^1]. For it has been preached to you, through the voice of the Lord Jesus Christ himself, that his Gospel will be announced to all nations, and then the end will be[^2]. It has been preached to you, through the prophetic and apostolic writings, that the promises were spoken to Abraham and to his seed, which is Christ[^3], when God said to him: *In your seed all the nations will be blessed*[^4]. To you, then, holding these promises, if an angel from heaven should say: Abandon the Christianity of the world of lands, and hold that of the party of Donatus, whose order is set forth to you in the letter of the bishop of your city, he ought to be anathema; because he would try to cut you off from the whole, and to thrust you into a part, and to alienate you from the promises of God.

**1. 2.** For if the order of bishops succeeding one another is to be considered, how much more certainly and truly wholesomely do we number from Peter himself, to whom, bearing the figure of the whole Church, the Lord says: *Upon this rock I will build my Church, and the gates of hell will not conquer it*[^5]. For to Peter succeeded Linus; to Linus, Clement; to Clement, Anacletus; to Anacletus, Evaristus; to Evaristus, Sixtus; to Sixtus, Telesphorus; to Telesphorus, Hyginus; to Hyginus, Anicetus; to Anicetus, Pius; to Pius, Soter; to Soter, Alexander; to Alexander, Victor; to Victor, Zephyrinus; to Zephyrinus, Callistus; to Callistus, Urban; to Urban, Pontian; to Pontian, Anterus; to Anterus, Fabian; to Fabian, Cornelius; to Cornelius, Lucius; to Lucius, Stephen; to Stephen, Sixtus; to Sixtus, Dionysius; to Dionysius, Felix; to Felix, Eutychian; to Eutychian, Gaius; to Gaius, Marcellus; to Marcellus, Eusebius; to Eusebius, Miltiades; to Miltiades, Sylvester; to Sylvester, Mark; to Mark, Julius; to Julius, Liberius; to Liberius, Damasus; to Damasus, Siricius; to Siricius, Anastasius. In this order of succession no Donatist bishop is found. But, from across the way, they sent one ordained from Africa, who, presiding over a few Africans in the city of Rome, propagated the name of the Mountain-men or the Cutzupitae.

**1. 3.** But in that order of bishops which is led from Peter himself even to Anastasius, who now sits in that same chair, even if any traitor had crept in through those times, it would prejudge nothing against the Church and the innocent Christians, for whom the Lord, providing, says of evil rulers: *What they say, do; but what they do, do not do: for they say, and do not*[^6]. So that the hope of the faithful may be sure, which, placed not in man, but in the Lord, may never be dissipated by the storm of a sacrilegious schism; as these men are dissipated, who read in the holy codices the Churches to which the Apostles wrote, and have no bishop among them. But what is more perverse and more insane than, while the readers read those same Epistles, to say: Peace be with you, and to be separated from the peace of those Churches to which those very Epistles were written?

**2. 4.** Yet, lest he flatter himself even concerning the Constantinian, that is, the order of bishops of your city, recite to him the Records held before Munatius Felix, perpetual flamen and curator of your same city at that time, in the eighth consulship of Diocletian and the seventh of Maximian, on the eleventh day before the Kalends of June, by which it was clearly established that Paul the bishop so handed over that Silvanus was then his subdeacon, and handed over with him, bringing forth the instruments of the Lord, even those which had been carefully hidden, a silver casket and a silver lamp, so that a certain Victor said to him: You would have been dead, if you had not found those. This Silvanus, a most manifest traitor, this man recalls as a great matter in the letter which he writes to you, ordained bishop at that time by Secundus of Tigisi, bishop of the first see. Let their proud tongue, then, be quiet, and let it recognize its own crimes, lest, raving, it speak of another's. Recite to him also, if he wishes, the ecclesiastical Records of the same Secundus of Tigisi, held in the house of Urbanus Donatus, where he left the confessed traitors to God the judge—Donatus of Masculi, Marinus of Aquae Tibilitanae, Donatus of Calama—with which confessed traitors he ordained for them bishop the said traitor Silvanus. Recite to him the Records held before Zenophilus the consular, where a certain Nundinarius, a deacon angry with Silvanus because he had been excommunicated by him, disclosed all these things to the courts, which, by certain documents and the responses of witnesses, and the recitation of the Records and of many letters, were established clearer than light.

**2. 5.** There are many other things which you may recite to him, if he is willing not to act contentiously, but to listen prudently: the petitions of the Donatists to Constantine, that, for settling that cause among the African bishops, he should send bishops from Gaul as judges. The letters too of the same emperor, where he sent bishops to the city of Rome. The Records too held in the city of Rome, where by the bishops whom he had sent the cause was known and discussed. And likewise other letters, where the said emperor declares that they had complained to him about the judgment of their colleagues, that is, of the bishops whom he had sent to the city of Rome; where he also willed that other bishops should judge at Arles; where these men appealed even from their judgment to the same emperor; where finally he himself knew the cause between the parties; where he most vehemently detests that they were overcome by the innocence of Caecilian. Which, if he wishes, he will hear, and be silent, and cease to lie in wait for the truth.

**3. 6.** Although we presume not so much upon these documents as upon the holy Scriptures, where the inheritance of Christ is promised to the ends of the earth among all nations[^7]: whence these men, separated by a wicked schism, hurl crimes against the chaff of the Lord's harvest; which must be tolerated, mingled, even to the end, until by the last judgment the whole threshing floor is winnowed[^8]. Whence it is manifest that these crimes, whether true or false, do not pertain to the Lord's grain, which through the whole field, that is, this world, must grow even to the end of the age, as not a false angel in this man's error, but the Lord in the Gospel speaks[^9]. And therefore God deservedly repaid these wretched Donatists, who hurl many false and empty crimes against innocent Christians, who throughout the whole world are mingled with evil Christians as with their own chaff or tares: so that they condemned the Maximianists at Carthage, their own schismatics, the condemners of Primian, the baptizers apart from Primian, the rebaptizers after Primian, by their universal council: so that, after no little time, some of their number—Felicianus of Musti and Praetextatus of Assuras—they received into the honors of their episcopate, Optatus the Gildonian compelling, with all those whom the condemned had baptized outside. But if they are not stained by those whom they condemned with their own mouth as wicked and sacrilegious, and whom they compared to those first schismatics whom the earth swallowed alive[^10], when they communicate with them, received again into their honor; let them at some time wake up, let them think with how great blindness and how great madness they say that the world of lands is stained by the unknown crimes of the Africans, and that the inheritance of Christ, which, promised, has been displayed among all nations, was deleted by the sins of the Africans through the contagion of communication, when they are unwilling to seem themselves deleted and stained, while they communicate with those whose known crimes they have judged.

**3. 7.** Wherefore, since the apostle Paul says again that Satan himself transforms himself into an angel of light, whence it is no wonder if his ministers transform themselves as ministers of righteousness[^11]; if this man truly saw some angel, a messenger of error, and desiring to separate Christians from catholic unity, he himself suffered an angel of Satan transforming himself as it were into an angel of light. But if he lies, and saw nothing of the sort, he himself is a minister of Satan, transforming himself as it were into a minister of righteousness. And yet, considering all these things, if he is not willing to be too perverse and obstinate, he will be able to be freed from all seduction, whether another's or his own. For we, on your occasion, have approached without any hatred, observing toward him that which the Apostle says: *But the servant of the Lord ought not to quarrel, but to be gentle toward all, teachable, patient, correcting in moderation those who think differently; lest perhaps God give them repentance to know the truth, and they come to their senses out of the snares of the devil, captured by him into his own will*[^12]. If, then, we have said anything harshly, let him recognize that it avails not for the bitterness of dissension, but for the correction of love. May you live unharmed in Christ, most beloved and honorable brother. Amen.
