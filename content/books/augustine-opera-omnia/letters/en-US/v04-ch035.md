# Letter 243

TO LAETUS, A MOST BELOVED AND MOST LONGED-FOR LORD AND BROTHER, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** I read the letter you sent to the brothers, desiring to console you because your first trials are shaken by many temptations; in which you also indicated that you desire my letter. I suffered with you, brother, and could not cease from writing, lest I deny not only your desire but also mine what I saw I owed to the duty of love. If, then, you profess yourself a recruit of Christ, do not desert the camp; in which there is to be built by you also that tower of which the Lord speaks in the Gospel[^1]. For one standing in it, and serving under the arms of the word of God, no temptations can penetrate from any side: thence the darts hurled at the adversary come with grave weight, and, foreseen by a firm fortification, are avoided. Consider also our Lord Jesus Christ, since he is our king, yet, by that fellowship by which he deigned to be also a brother, called his soldiers kings, and forewarned that each one, to make war with a king who has twenty thousand, ought to be fit by the equipping of ten thousand[^2].

**2.** But a little before he set forth the exhortatory similitudes about the tower and the king, attend to what he said: *If anyone comes to me, and does not hate his father, and mother, and wife, and children, and brothers, and sisters, and his own soul too, he cannot be my disciple. And if he does not carry his cross and come after me, he cannot be my disciple.* Then he subjoins: *Which of you, wishing to build a tower, does not first sit down and reckon whether he has the means for its completion, lest, when he has laid the foundation, he be unable to build, and all who pass by and see begin to say, "This man began to build, and could not finish"? Or what king, going to wage war with another king, does not first sit down and consider whether he is able with ten thousand to meet him who comes against him with twenty thousand? Otherwise, while he is still far off, he sends an embassy, asking for peace*[^3]. But to what these similitudes pertained, he sufficiently opened by the very conclusion: for he says: *So, therefore, everyone of you who does not renounce all that he has cannot be my disciple*[^4].

**3.** And so both the means to build the tower, and the strength of ten thousand warriors against a king who has twenty thousand, are nothing other than that each one should renounce all that he has. But the earlier preface accords with the final conclusion. For in the fact that each one *renounces all that he has,* there is also contained this, that he should hate his father, and mother, and wife, and children, and brothers, and sisters, and his own soul too. For all these are his own, which for the most part entangle and impede toward obtaining—not these things, his own, about to pass away in time, but those things, common, about to abide forever. For that by which a certain woman is now your mother, by that very thing she is surely not also mine. Wherefore this is temporal and transitory: just as you now see to have passed away the fact that she conceived you, that she carried you in the womb, that she bore you, that she nourished you with milk. But that she is a sister in Christ, is so both to you and to me, and to all to whom one heavenly inheritance, and God as father, and Christ as brother, are promised in the same fellowship of love[^5]. These are eternal; these are worn away by no decay of time; these are hoped to be held the more firmly the more they are proclaimed to be obtained by no private but rather a common right.

**4.** You can recognize this most easily in your own mother. For whence does she now entangle and involve you, and, retarded from your appointed course, bend you back and curve you, except from the fact that she is your own mother? For from the fact that she is a sister of all to whom God is father and the Church mother, she now does not impede you, any more than me, or all our brothers, who love her not with a private love, as you in your house, but with a public love in the house of God. As to the fact, then, that you are bound to her by a carnal relationship too, it ought to avail toward the lot of conversing more familiarly, and of counseling by a more open door, that this very thing by which she loves you privately may be slain in her, lest she weigh more the fact that she begot you from her womb than the fact that she was begotten with you from the womb of the Church. But what I have said of a mother, this is to be understood of any such other kinship. Let each one also think this of his own soul, that even in it he should hate the private affection, which is without doubt temporal: but let him love in it that communion and fellowship of which it was said: *They had one soul and one heart toward God*[^6]. For so your soul is not your own, but of all the brothers; whose souls too are yours, or rather whose souls with yours are not souls, but one soul—the one soul of Christ, which, that it may be rescued from the hand of the dog, is sung in the Psalms[^7]. Thence one arrives most easily at the contempt of death.

**5.** Nor let parents be angry that the Lord commands this, that we should hate them, when this is commanded us about our own soul[^8]. For just as it is now commanded about the soul, that we should hate it, along with our parents, for Christ's sake; so what the same Lord says in another place about the soul can most fittingly apply to parents too: *He who loves,* he says, *his soul will lose it*[^9]. I will also say confidently: He who loves his parents will lose them. For about the soul he there said *hate,* what here he said *lose.* But just as this precept, by which we are commanded to lose our soul, does not avail to this, that each one should kill himself, which is an inexpiable crime; and yet avails to this, that he should kill in himself the carnal affection of the soul, by which the present life delights with the impediment of the future life—for this is what was said, *let him hate his soul,* and, *he will lose it*—which nevertheless is done by loving; since most openly he mentions, in the same precept, the fruit of acquiring that same soul, saying: *He who loses it in this world will find it unto eternal life:* so of parents it is most rightly said that he who loves them loses them; not killing them in the manner of parricides, but, with the spiritual sword of the word of God, devoutly and confidently striking and slaying their carnal affection, by which they try to bind both themselves and those they have begotten with the entanglements of this world, making that to live in them by which they are brothers, by which, with their temporal children, they come to know their eternal parents, God and the Church.

**6.** Behold, the zeal for truth seizes you, and for knowing and perceiving the will of God in the holy Scriptures; the office of evangelical preaching seizes you. The Lord gives the signal that we keep watch in the camp, that we build the tower from which we may be able both to look out for and to drive off the enemy of eternal life. The heavenly trumpet seizes the soldier of Christ to battle, and a mother holds him back!—not at all such as the mother of the Maccabees was, nor even like the Lacedaemonian mothers, of whom it has been handed down to memory that they roused their sons, to shed their blood for the earthly fatherland, into the battles of war much more and more ardently than the sound of the trumpets[^10]. For a mother who does not permit you to be removed from worldly cares to learn life, sufficiently shows how she would permit you, if it were needful, utterly to repudiate the world to meet death.

**7.** But what does she say, or what does she allege? Perhaps those ten months in which you burdened her womb, and the pains of childbearing, and the labors of upbringing. This, this slay with the saving word; this of your mother lose, that you may find her unto eternal life[^11]. Remember to hate this in her, if you love her, if you are a recruit of Christ, if you have laid the foundations of the tower; lest those passing by say, *This man began to build, and could not finish*[^12]. For this is a carnal affection, and still *sounds of the old man*[^13]. This carnal affection, both in ourselves and in our own, Christian warfare exhorts us to destroy; not, however, so that anyone be ungrateful to his parents, and, having counted up those very benefits by which he was brought forth into this life, received, and nourished, mock them. Let him rather everywhere preserve devotion: let these things have their place where greater things do not call.

**8.** Mother Church is the mother also of your mother. She conceived you both of Christ, she bore you in travail by the blood of the martyrs, she brought you forth into everlasting light, she nourished and nourishes you with the milk of faith; and, preparing greater foods, she shudders that you wish still, as little ones and without teeth, to wail. This mother, diffused throughout the whole world, is shaken by so various and manifold infestations of errors that her now abortive sons do not hesitate to make war against her with unbridled arms. By the cowardice too and torpor of certain ones whom she holds in her bosom, she grieves that her members grow cold in very many places and become less fit for warming the little ones. Whence, except through other sons, whence except through other members, of whose number you are, does she demand just and owed help? Will you, deserting her necessities, turn to carnal words? Does she not sound in your ears with graver complaints? does she not show more precious entrails, and heavenly breasts? Add the assumption of flesh by her husband, lest you cling to carnal things, and, by the eternal Word, all the things your mother reproaches you with, lest you be entangled in them, having been assumed; add the contumelies, the scourges, *the death, even the death of the cross*[^14].

**9.** Conceived from such seeds, and procreated into a new life by such a marriage, you grow languid and waste away into *the old man*[^15]! Did your Emperor, then, not have an earthly mother? Who nevertheless, when she was announced to him as he was doing heavenly things, answered: *Who is my mother, or who are my brothers?* And *stretching out his hand toward his disciples,* he said that none pertained to his kinship except those who did the will of his Father[^16]. In which number indeed he kindly included Mary herself too; for she too did the will of the Father. So the best and divine Master cast aside, in comparison with the heavenly kinship, even the maternal name, which they had announced to him as though private and proper, because it was earthly: and, mentioning that same heavenly kinship in his disciples, he showed by what fellowship of kind that virgin again cohered to him along with the other saints. And lest, by this most wholesome teaching by which he taught that the carnal affection in parents is to be despised, error should receive support, by which the having of a mother is denied by certain ones, in another place he admonished his disciples not to call themselves to have a father on earth[^17], so that, as it is manifest that they had fathers, so he might make it manifest that he had a mother—whose earthly kinship nevertheless being despised, he might afford his disciples an example of despising such relationships.

**10.** These things, then, are interrupted by the voices of your mother, and amid these the mention of the pregnant and nursing woman finds a place, so that from Adam and Eve another Adam might be born and nourished? Look rather, look upon the second Adam from heaven, and now bear the image of the heavenly, as you have borne that of the earthly[^18]. Nay, here too let those maternal benefits themselves have a place, which are counted up to you for the enervating of your heart: let them have a place indeed; do not be ungrateful, repay grace to your mother, repay spiritual things for carnal, eternal for temporal. But does she not wish to follow? let her not impede. Does she not wish to be converted for the better? beware lest she pervert and overturn you for the worse. What does it matter whether in a wife or in a mother, provided that Eve be guarded against in any woman whatever[^19]? For this shadow of devotion comes from the leaves of that tree, with which our first parents first covered themselves in that damnable nakedness. And whatever, in those words and that suggestion, she holds out to you as though a duty of love, in order to twist you from the most genuine and most sincere love of the Gospel, pertains to the cunning of the serpent, and to the duplicity of that king who has twenty thousand, which we are taught to overcome by the ten thousand, that is, by the simplicity of the heart, in which we seek God[^20].

**11.** Attend rather to these things, most dear one, and take up your cross, and follow the Lord[^21]. For when, present, I noticed that you were retarded from divine study by domestic cares, I perceived that you were rather carried and led by your cross, not that you carried and led it. For our cross, which the Lord commands to be carried by us, that we may follow him most unencumbered, what else does it signify but the mortality of this flesh? For it itself torments us until death is swallowed up in victory[^22]. This cross itself, therefore, is to be crucified, and to be transfixed with the nails of the fear of God[^23], lest, with the members loosed and free, we be unable to carry it as it resists. For to follow the Lord, except carrying it, you are altogether unable: for how will you follow him, if you are not his? But *those who are of Jesus Christ,* says the Apostle, *have crucified their flesh with its passions and desires*[^24].

**12.** If indeed your household property has any money, with the business of which you ought not and it does not befit you to be entangled, it must in truth be given to your mother and your household. For their indigence, if you have determined to distribute such things of yours to the poor, that you may be perfect, ought to hold the first place with you: *For if anyone does not provide for his own, and especially for his household,* says the Apostle, *he has denied the faith, and is worse than an unbeliever*[^25]. For ordering which affairs, if you set out from us to free your neck for the chains of wisdom to be put on, how do they harm you, or in what way do they pluck at you, the tears of your mother flowing with the flesh, or the flight of a slave, or the death of handmaids, or the sickly health of brothers? If there is in you an ordered love, knowing how to set the greater before the lesser, and to be moved by mercy, that the poor may have the Gospel preached to them[^26], lest the abundant harvest of the Lord, by the lack of workers, lie as prey for the birds, and to have a heart ready to follow the will of the Lord, in what he shall determine to do, whether by scourging or by sparing, with his servants: *meditate on these things, be in these things, that your progress may be manifest to all*[^27]. I beseech you to beware lest you give greater sadness to the good brothers by your torpor than the gladness you had already given by your eagerness. But to commend you by the letters you wished, I thought as superfluous as if anyone had wished to commend you to me in the same way.
