# Letter 263

TO SAPIDA, A MOST RELIGIOUS AND HOLY LADY AND DAUGHTER, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** I have received what, from the just and devout labors of your hands, you wished me to receive, lest I should sadden you more gravely than I should rather see you to be consoled: especially because you reckoned this very thing to be your no small solace, if the tunic which you had made for your brother, the holy minister of God, I should put on; since he, now departing from the land of the dying, has need of no corruptible things. I did, therefore, what you desired, and, whatever you may esteem this, or however small a solace, I did not deny it to your heart toward your brother. I received the tunic sent by you, and when I wrote these things to you, I had already begun to clothe myself with it. Be of good spirit; but use much better and much greater consolations, that the cloud of your heart, contracted by human weakness, may be made serene by divine authority: and live perseveringly so that you may live with your brother; since your brother so died that he lives.

**2.** There is indeed matter for tears, that you do not see your beloved brother—who reverenced you greatly for your life and your profession of sacred virginity—the deacon of the Church of Carthage, as you used to, entering and going out, and busying himself in the strenuousness of his ecclesiastical office; and you do not hear from him those honorable words, which he expended on the holiness of your sisterly bond with a compliant, devout, and dutiful affection. When these things are thought of, and the violence of habit is felt, the heart is pierced, and, as it were, the blood of the heart, weeping, arises. But let the heart be on high, and the eyes will be dry. For not because these things, which you grieve to have been withdrawn from you, have passed away in their temporal course, has that love perished by which Timothy loved Sapida, and loves her; it abides preserved in its treasure, and *is hidden with Christ in God*[^1]. Those who love gold, do they lose it when they store it away? are they not then, as far as can be, more secure about it, when they keep it, removed from their eyes, in safer caskets? Does, then, earthly desire think it has its thing more securely guarded, if it does not see what it loves; and does heavenly love grieve, as though it had lost what it sent ahead into the heavenly granaries? Sapida, attend to what you are called, and *savor the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God*[^2]; who deigned to die for us, that we might live even dead; and lest death itself be feared by a man, as though about to consume the man; nor any of the dead, for whom life died, be grieved as though he had lost life. Let these and such be your divine consolations, before which human sadness should blush and yield.

**3.** Grief over dear dead ones is indeed not to be reproached in mortals; but a long-lasting grief ought not to be that of the faithful. If, then, you have been saddened, let it now be enough; nor be saddened *as the Gentiles, who have no hope*[^3]. For the apostle Paul did not forbid being saddened, when he said this, but being saddened *as the Gentiles, who have no hope.* For both Martha and Mary, devout and faithful sisters, wept for their brother Lazarus, who was to rise again[^4], although they did not then know he was about to return to this life: and the Lord himself wept for the same Lazarus, whom he was about to raise up[^5]; doubtless so that we too may weep for those dead whom we believe will rise to true life—even if he did not command it by precept, he conceded it by example. Nor in vain does Scripture say in the book of Ecclesiasticus: *For the dead bring forth tears, and, as one having suffered dire things, begin lamentation*: but a little after it says, *and console yourself on account of the sadness; for from sadness death proceeds, and the sadness of the heart bends strength*[^6].

**4.** Your brother, daughter, lives in mind, sleeps in flesh; *shall he who sleeps not add that he rise again?*[^7] God, who has already received his spirit, will restore to him his body, which he took away not to destroy but deferred to return. There is, therefore, no cause for long-lasting sadness, because there is a stronger cause for everlasting gladness. Since not even that mortal part itself of your brother, which is buried in the earth, will perish for you; in which he was presented to you, through which he addressed you and conversed with you; from which he brought forth a voice so known to your ears, as he offered his face to your eyes, so that wherever it sounded, even unseen he was wont to be recognized. For these things are withdrawn from the senses of the living, so that the absence of the dead may cause grief. But since not even those bodies will perish forever, just as not *a hair of the head will perish*[^8], and, laid aside for a time, will be so received that they will never henceforth be laid aside, but, changed for the better, be made firm; surely there is a greater cause for congratulation in the hope of inestimable eternity, than a cause for mourning in a matter of the briefest time. This hope the Gentiles do not have, *not knowing the Scriptures nor the power of God*[^9]; who is able to repair things lost, and give life to things dead, and reintegrate things corrupted, and join again things separated, and henceforth keep without end things corrupted and ended. These things he promised he would do, who made faith from these things which he has already accomplished, having promised them. May your faith converse these things with you, since your hope will not be defrauded, even if your love is now deferred: meditate on these things; by these be more abundantly and more truly consoled. For if, because I am clothed (since he could not be) with that garment which you had woven for your brother, this consoles you something; how much more ought you to be consoled, and more surely, because he for whom it had been prepared will then, needing no incorruptible garment, be clothed with incorruption and immortality!
