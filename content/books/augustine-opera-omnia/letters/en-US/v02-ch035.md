# Letter 134

To the distinguished lord, and deservedly exalted, and most excellent son Apringius, Augustine.

**1.** I do not doubt that in this power which God, a man, has given you over men, you think of the divine judgment, where even judges will stand to render an account of their judgment. For I know you are imbued with the Christian faith; whence greater confidence is granted me toward your Excellency, not only of asking, but even of admonishing, on account of that Lord in whose family you are reckoned with us by heavenly right, in whom we have alike the hope of eternal life, and whom we invoke for you in the sacrosanct mysteries. Accordingly, distinguished lord, and deservedly exalted, and most excellent son, first I ask that I not seem to you importunately to rush upon your acts, with that solicitude which it especially behooves me to bear for the Church entrusted to me, whose benefits I serve, over which I desire not so much to preside as to profit: then I beseech that what I admonish or ask, you not disdain to receive, not hesitate to assent to.

**2.** Certain Circumcellions and Donatist clerics, the care of those who serve public discipline having sent ahead an official notification, brought to the courts and laws. These, when they were heard by the most illustrious and respectable man, the tribune and notary, your brother, my son Marcellinus, coerced not by the torments of claws and flames, but of rods, confessed horrible crimes perpetrated by them against my brothers and fellow priests: namely that they slaughtered one of them, caught by ambush; the other, snatched from his house, they maimed, his eye gouged out and his finger amputated. When I had learned that they had confessed these things, and therefore least of all doubted that they would come under the jurisdiction of your axe, I hastened this letter to your Nobility, by which I entreat, and through the mercy of Christ beseech, that we may so rejoice over your greater and more certain happiness, that like things not be repaid them; although the laws cannot, in punishing, cut off a finger and tear out an eye with blows of a stone, which these men, in raging, could. Whence I am secure about those who confessed they did this, that they will not bring back this requital; but lest either they themselves, or those whose homicide was disclosed, be punished by the sentence of your power, this I fear, this lest it be done both a Christian asks of a judge, and a bishop admonishes a Christian.

**3.** Of you indeed we read the Apostle said that *you bear the sword not without cause, and are ministers of God, avengers against those who do evil*[^1]; but one cause is that of the province, another that of the Church: the administration of the former is to be borne terribly; the meekness of the latter is to be commended clemently. If my speech were with a non-Christian judge, I would act otherwise: yet not even so would I desert the cause of the Church, and, as much as he deigned to admit, I would insist that the sufferings of the Catholic servants of God—which ought to profit as examples of patience—not be befouled by the blood of their enemies; and if he were unwilling to assent, I would suspect that he resisted with a hostile mind. But now, when the matter is dealt with before you, my reasoning is one thing, my consultation another. We see you indeed a ruler of most lofty power, but we recognize you also a son of Christian piety. Let your sublimity be subjected, let your faith be subjected; I treat with you a common cause, but you can in it what I cannot: confer counsel with us, and stretch out help.

**4.** It was diligently brought about that the enemies of the Church—who are wont, by the empty talk of seduction, to solicit the minds of the unskilled, as it were glorying in the persecution which they boast they suffer—should confess the horrible crimes perpetrated by them against Catholic clerics, and be entangled in their own words. The records are to be read for healing the souls which they have poisoned by pestiferous persuasion: does it please you that we fear, in reading, to come to the end of the records, if it shall contain a bloody punishment of these men, where we set our very conscience too, lest those who suffered should seem to have repaid evil for evil? If, then, nothing else could be established for bridling the malice of the lost, the extreme necessity perhaps would urge that such men be killed; although, as far as pertains to us, if nothing milder could be done to them, we would prefer them to be released free than that the sufferings of our brothers be avenged by the shedding of their blood: but now, when something can be done by which both the Church may be commended as gentle, and the audacity of the savage be restrained; why do you not bend to a more provident and more lenient sentence what it is lawful for judges to do even in causes not of the Church? Fear, then, with us the judgment of God the Father, and commend the meekness of the mother: for when you do it, the Church does it, for whose sake you do it, and whose son you do it. Contend in goodness with the bad: they, by a monstrous crime, tore off members from a living body; you, by a merciful work, bring it about that those members, which they exercised in wicked works, may, whole, serve some useful work. They did not spare the servants of God preaching correction to them; you, spare those seized, spare those led off, spare those convicted. They, with impious iron, shed Christian blood: you, restrain from their blood even the legal sword, for the sake of Christ. They, a minister of the Church being killed, wrenched from him the span of living: you, to the living enemies of the Church relax a span of repenting. Such ought you to be in the cause of the Church, a Christian judge, we asking, admonishing, interceding. Men are wont, when it is dealt more leniently with their convicted enemies, to appeal from the milder sentence: but we so love our enemies that, unless we presumed on your Christian obedience, we would appeal from your severe sentence. May the omnipotent God preserve your Excellency more enlarged and more happy, distinguished lord, and deservedly exalted, and most excellent son.

[^1]: See Rom 13:4
