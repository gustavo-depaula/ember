# Letter 200

TO VALERIUS, ILLUSTRIOUS LORD AND DESERVEDLY MOST EXCELLENT, AND MOST DEAR SON IN THE LOVE OF CHRIST, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** When for a long time I was taking it ill that I had several times written and merited no reply from your Sublimity, suddenly I received three letters of your Benignity: one given not to me alone, through my fellow bishop Vindemialis, and not long after, two through the fellow presbyter Firmus; which holy man—joined to us, as you could learn from him, in a most intimate love—by conversing with us much about your excellence, and by truly intimating what sort of man he had known you to be in the bowels of Christ, surpassed not only those letters which the said bishop, or which he himself, brought, but even those which we were complaining we had not received. And therefore his narration about you was sweeter to us, because he was saying things which you yourself could not write back, not even at my inquiry, lest you become a herald of your own praises, which holy Scripture forbids. Although I too fear to write these things to you, lest I incur the suspicion of one flattering—illustrious lord and deservedly most excellent, and most dear son in the love of Christ.

**2.** See, then, what delight and gladness it was to me to hear your praises in Christ—or rather, in you, the praises of Christ—from him who could neither deceive me, on account of his faith, nor be ignorant of them, on account of his friendship with you. But other things too, and from others, even if not so many or so certain, we have nevertheless heard: how sound and catholic your faith is, how pious your expectation of things to come, what your love of God and of the brethren, how you are not proudly wise amid your exalted honors, nor hope in the uncertainty of riches, but in the living God, and are rich in good works; how your house is the rest and solace of the saints, and the terror of the impious; how great your care is that no one lay snares for the members of Christ, covered with the veil of the name of Christ, whether among his old or his more recent enemies; and how provident you are for the salvation of those same enemies, hostile to their error. These things and the like, as I said, we are accustomed to hear from others too; but now, through the aforesaid brother, we have learned them more numerous and better attested.

**3.** But further, concerning conjugal chastity, that we might be able to praise and love it too in you, what would we hear, except from some intimate of yours, who knew your life not on the surface but inwardly? About this good of yours, then, the gift of God, it delights me too to speak with you more intimately and somewhat longer. I know that I am not a burden to you if I send something lengthy, by reading which you may be with us longer. For I have learned this too, that amid your many and great cares you read easily and gladly, and are much delighted by our little works—even those which we have written to others, if any could come into your hands; how much more by what is written to you, where I shall speak as though to one present, and you will deign to attend more carefully, and receive more gratefully? From this letter, then, proceed to the book which I have sent together with it, which will more conveniently intimate to your Reverence, by its own beginning, both why it was written and why it was sent especially to you.
