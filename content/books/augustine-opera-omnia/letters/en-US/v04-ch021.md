# Letter 229

TO DARIUS, A LORD DESERVEDLY ILLUSTRIOUS, AND MOST MAGNIFICENT, AND MOST DEAR IN CHRIST, A SON, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** From the holy brothers and my fellow bishops Urbanus and Novatus I have learned what sort of man and how great you are: one of whom came to know you at Carthage, in the town of Hilara, and lately at Sicca; the other at Sitifis. Through these, then, it has come about that I too could not have you unknown. For not because weakness of body, and a twofold cold—that is, of winter and of age—does not allow me to converse with you in person, have I therefore not seen you: for the one, present with me, when he deigned to come to me, and the other by letters, showed me not the face of your flesh, but of your heart, that I might see you the more sweetly the more inwardly. This face of yours both we and you yourself, God being favorable, behold most gladly, as in a mirror, in the holy Gospel too, where it is written by the Truth speaking: *Blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called the sons of God*[^1].

**2.** Great indeed they are, and have their own glory—not only the bravest, but also, what is a kind of truer praise, the most faithful warriors, by whose labors and perils, with the help of God protecting and aiding, the untamed enemy is conquered, and quiet for the commonwealth and pacified provinces are procured: but it is of greater glory to slay wars themselves with a word, than men with the sword, and to acquire or obtain peace by peace, not by war. For even those who fight, if they are good, without doubt seek peace, but yet through blood; you, however, were sent that no one's blood should be sought: and so for others there is that necessity, for you this happiness. Accordingly, lord deservedly illustrious and most magnificent, and most dear in Christ, son, rejoice in this so great and truly good thing of yours, and enjoy in God whence you received it, that you should be such a man and should undertake such things to be done. May God confirm what he has wrought in us through you. Receive this greeting of ours, and deign to repay your own. As brother Novatus wrote to me, he brought it about that your excellence and erudition should come to know me even in my little works. If, then, you have read what he gave, I too have become known to your inner senses. They do not, as I think, much displease, if you have read with a more inclined love than with severity. It is not much; but it is much appreciated, if for our letters, both these and those, you render us one letter. I greet also the pledge of peace, which, with the help of the Lord our God, you have happily received, with that love which I owe.

[^1]: Matt 5:9
