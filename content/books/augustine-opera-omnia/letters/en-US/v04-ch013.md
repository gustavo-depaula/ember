# Letter 221

TO AUGUSTINE, A BISHOP, A LORD DESERVEDLY VENERABLE AND TRULY MOST BLESSED FATHER, QUODVULTDEUS, A DEACON.

**1.** *For a long time I was anxious, and several times I put off this venture of mine: but I was chiefly emboldened, as is commonly said, by the goodwill of Your Beatitude, attested by all; and while I consider it, I feared the more that I might be judged by the Lord proud for not asking, slothful for not seeking, lazy for not knocking*[^1]. *I would have believed such a will alone to suffice for me, if I could not obtain the fruit: but since I know for certain that the door of the divine word*[^2]*, which heavenly grace has opened to Your Reverence, has made your holy mind, which Christ possesses, ready not only to open it to all who are willing, but even to persuade the unwilling that they should not be reluctant to enter, I will not, by superfluous wordiness, delay the occupation of Your Reverence, but will briefly indicate the desire of my entreaty.*

**2.** *That there are some in the clergy even of this most great city who are unskilled, I conjecture from myself; and how much it will profit that whole order, what I seek, I offer to be weighed by the consideration of Your Holiness. For I presume that even I, unworthy, shall obtain the privilege of all who deserve this from your spiritual labors, lord deservedly venerable and truly most blessed father. I beg, therefore, Your Piety, that you would deign to set forth what heresies there have been, and are, from the time the Christian religion received the name of the promised inheritance; what errors they introduced, and introduce; what they held, and hold, against the Catholic Church, concerning faith, the Trinity, baptism, penance, Christ as man, Christ as God, the resurrection, the New and Old Testament; and absolutely everything in which they dissent from the truth: which of them have baptism, and which do not; and after which the Church baptizes, and yet does not rebaptize; how it receives those who come, and what it answers to each by law, authority, and reason.*

**3.** *Let not Your Beatitude believe me of such great folly that I do not see how many and how huge volumes would be needed for these things to be unraveled. But this I do not seek to be done; for I do not doubt that it has been done in many ways: but I ask that the opinions of each heresy be set forth briefly, concisely, and summarily, and that what the Catholic Church holds against them be added, as far as is enough for instruction: so that, as by a kind of reminder conceived from all of them, if anyone should wish to know some objection or refutation more fully, completely, and plainly, he may be referred to the rich and magnificent volumes on which it is established that work has been done by various authors, and especially by Your Reverence, on this very subject. Such an admonition will suffice, I think, for the learned and the unlearned, for the leisured and the busy, and for those promoted from anywhere to the ministry of any rank of the Church; since both he who has read much briefly recalls the same things, and the ignorant is instructed by a summary, so that he may know what to hold, what to avoid, what to shun lest he do it, what to pursue that he may do it. But perhaps, if what I judge is true, even against the malicious minds and deceitful lips*[^3] *of slanderers, this little work, among the other magnificent ones, will not be lacking to the testimony of your crown: so that those for whom a more abundant field of slandering lies open, hemmed in by the strong and on every side enclosed boundaries of the faith, and surrounded by every kind of the darts of truth, and suddenly laid low even by a single multiform javelin, may not dare to breathe out their carrion spirits.*

**4.** *I see how burdensome I prove to be to one thinking of better things and arranging greater things, a holy old man who endures the complaints of the body. But through Christ the Lord, who granted you to be a partaker of his wisdom without envy, I ask that you give this grace to the unlearned of the Church, you who acknowledge yourself a debtor to the wise and the foolish*[^4]*, who can deservedly and rightly say:* See that I have not labored for myself alone, but for all who seek the truth[^5]. *I could still offer suppliant and manifold prayers, and enlist with me the throngs of the unskilled; but I preferred now to hear you dictating rather than to occupy you still in reading.*

[^1]: See Matt 7:7-8
[^2]: See Col 4:3
[^3]: See Ps 12:2 (11:3)
[^4]: See Rom 1:14
[^5]: See Sir 24:34 (24:47); 33:18
