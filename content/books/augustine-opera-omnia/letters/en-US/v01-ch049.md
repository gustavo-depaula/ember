# Letter 49

To Honoratus, a bishop of the party of Donatus, Augustine, bishop of the catholic Church

**1.** Your counsel pleased us much, which you deigned to convey through brother Herotes, a man most dear to us and praiseworthy in Christ, that we should deal between ourselves by letters, where no tumult of crowds could disturb our arrangement, which is to be undertaken and conducted with all gentleness and peace of mind, as the Apostle says: *But the servant of the Lord ought not to quarrel, but to be gentle toward all, teachable, patient, correcting in moderation those who think differently*[^1]. And so we briefly intimate what we desire to be answered by you.

**2.** Since we see the Church of God, which is called catholic, as was prophesied of it, spread throughout the world of lands, we judge that we ought not to doubt about so most evident a fulfillment of the holy prophecy, which the Lord also confirmed in the Gospel, and the Apostles, through whom that same Church was spread, as had been foretold of it. For in the head of the most sacred Psalter it is written of the Son of God: *The Lord said to me: You are my Son, today I have begotten you. Ask of me, and I will give you the nations as your inheritance, and the ends of the earth as your possession*[^2]. And the Lord Jesus Christ himself says that his Gospel will be in all nations[^3]. And the apostle Paul, before the word of God had reached Africa, in the very head of the Epistle which he wrote to the Romans: *Through whom we received*, he says, *grace and apostleship, for the obedience of faith, among all nations for his name*[^4]. Then he himself, from Jerusalem round about through all Asia even to Illyricum, preached the Gospel[^5], established and founded Churches; not he, but the grace of God with him[^6], as he himself testifies. But what can appear more evident than that in his Epistles we find even the names of regions or cities? He writes to the Romans, to the Corinthians, to the Galatians, to the Ephesians, to the Philippians, to the Thessalonians, to the Colossians. John too writes to seven Churches[^7], which he recalls as established in those parts, in which we understand also that the universal Church is commended by the sevenfold number—Ephesus, Smyrna, Sardis, Philadelphia, Laodicea, Pergamum, Thyatira. With all which Churches it is manifest that we today communicate, just as it is manifest that you do not communicate with these Churches.

**3.** We ask, then, that you not be reluctant to answer us, what cause you perhaps know, by which it came about that Christ lost his inheritance, spread throughout the world of lands, and suddenly remained among the Africans alone, and not even all of them. For indeed the catholic Church is also in Africa, because through all lands God willed it to be and foretold it. But your party, which is called that of Donatus, is not in all those places in which both the writings and the discourse and the deeds of the apostles ran. But lest you say that our Church is not called catholic but Macarian, as you call it; you ought to know—which can most easily be done—that in all those parts whence the Gospel of Christ flooded these lands, neither is the name of Donatus known, nor the name of Macarius. But that yours is called the party of Donatus, neither can you deny, and it is known to all, wherever your communion is. Deign, then, to write back to us, that we may know how it can come about that Christ lost his Church from the whole world, and began to have it among you alone: for it is for you to show this; for it suffices for our cause that we see the prophecy and the holy Scriptures fulfilled throughout the world of lands. But this I, Augustine, have dictated, because I have long wished to speak with you about it. For it seems to me that, even on account of our very nearness, we can converse by letters about this matter without any tumult, God aiding, as much as the very necessity requires.

[^1]: See 2 Tim 2:24–25
[^2]: Ps 2:7–8
[^3]: See Matt 24:14
[^4]: See Rom 1:5
[^5]: See Rom 15:19
[^6]: See 1 Cor 15:10
[^7]: See Rev 1:4, 11
