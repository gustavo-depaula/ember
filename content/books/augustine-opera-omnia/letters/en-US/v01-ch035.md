# Letter 35

To the excellent lord, deservedly to be received and honorable brother Eusebius, Augustine

**1.** I, a troublesome exhorter or petitioner, did not impose upon your unwilling will a judgment, as you say, to be undertaken among bishops. And indeed, even if I had wished to persuade you of this, I could perhaps easily show how able you are to judge between us in a case so manifest and open, and what sort of thing it is that you do—that, the parties not heard, you do not hesitate already to pass sentence for one party, you who dread to judge; but this, as I said, I omit for now. But I had asked nothing else of your honorable kindness—which I beg you at last in this letter at least to deign to attend to—except that you ask Proculeianus whether he himself said this to his presbyter Victor, which the public office reported as said to it by him; or whether perhaps those who were sent recorded in the Records, not what they heard from Victor, but something false; and then what seemed to him about the whole question itself to be discussed between us. But I think that he does not become a judge who is asked to question someone, and to deign to write back what shall have been answered to him. This, then, even now I ask you not to be reluctant to do, because he, as I have also experienced, is unwilling to accept my letter: which if he had been willing to do, I would surely not be doing this through your Excellence. But since he is unwilling, what can I do more gently than that, through you, such a man, and one who loves him, something be asked, about which my burden forbids me to be silent? But that a mother beaten by her son displeased your gravity—yet, you said, if he knew, he would forbid so wicked a young man from his communion: I answer briefly: now he has learned, now let him forbid.

**2.** I add also another matter: A former subdeacon of the church of Spania, by name Primus, when he was forbidden the undisciplined approach to the nuns, and despised the orderly and sound precepts, was removed from the clergy, and he himself, provoked against the discipline of God, transferred himself to those people, and was rebaptized. He also transferred—or they followed him—two nuns, his fellow tenants, from the estate of catholic Christians, and they too were rebaptized; and now, with the bands of Circumcellions, among the wandering bands of women who refused to have husbands lest they should have discipline, in the riotous frenzies of detestable wine-drinking he exults, proud, rejoicing that the widest license of evil conduct has been opened to him, from which he was forbidden in the Catholic Church. And this perhaps Proculeianus does not know. Therefore let it be brought to his notice through your gravity and modesty; let him order that the man, who chose that communion for no other reason than that he had lost his clergy-rank in the Catholic Church on account of disobedience and lost morals, be removed from his communion.

**3.** For I, if it pleases the Lord, keep this rule: that whoever, degraded among them on account of discipline, shall wish to cross over to the Catholic Church, be received in the humiliation of penance—to which they perhaps would even compel him, if he had wished to remain among them. But consider, I beg you, how execrably it is done by them, that those whom, living evilly, we rebuke by ecclesiastical discipline, are persuaded to come to another washing, and, in order that they may deserve to receive it, answer that they are Pagans; that which word, lest it should proceed from a Christian mouth, so much blood of the martyrs was shed: then, as though renewed and as though sanctified, made worse for the discipline which they could not bear, under the appearance of a new grace, they insult by the sacrilege of a new fury. But if I do wrong to take care, through your goodwill, that these things be corrected, let no one complain of me if I cause these things to be brought to his notice through the public records, which, as I judge, cannot be denied me in the Roman city. For since God commands that we speak and preach the word, and refute those teaching what they ought not, and be insistent *in season and out of season*, as I prove from the Lord's and the Apostle's writings[^1], let no man suppose that silence about these matters must be persuaded to me. But if they think anything must be dared violently or by brigandage, the Lord will not be lacking to protect his Church, who has subjected all earthly kingdoms to his yoke in her bosom spread throughout the whole world.

**4.** For when a certain tenant of the church wished, with paternal severity, to recall his daughter—who had been a catechumen among us and was seduced over to them, against her parents' will, so that, baptized there, she might also take up the form of a nun—to catholic communion, and I had been unwilling that a woman of corrupted mind be received unless willing and, by free choice, loving the better things; that rustic began to press her even with blows, that his daughter should consent to him; which I forbade to be done at once in every way: yet, as we were passing through Spania, his presbyter, standing in the midst of the estate of a catholic and praiseworthy woman, cried out after us with a most shameless voice that we were traitors and persecutors; which reproach he also hurled at that woman, who is of our communion, in the midst of whose estate he was standing; which words, when I heard them, I not only restrained myself from a quarrel, but even checked the multitude that accompanied me. And yet if I should say: Let it be inquired who are or were the traitors or persecutors; it is answered to me: We do not wish to dispute, and we do wish to rebaptize. Let us plunder your sheep, in the manner of wolves, with treacherous bites; you, if you are good shepherds, be silent. For what else did Proculeianus order, if indeed he himself ordered? If you are a Christian, keep to this judgment of God: unless we do it, you be silent. The same presbyter even dared to threaten a rustic man, the tenant of the church's estate.

**5.** All these things too, I beg, let Proculeianus learn through you; let him restrain the madness of his clergy, about which, honorable Eusebius, I have not been silent toward you. You will therefore deign to write back to me, not what you yourself think about all these things—lest you suppose that the burden of a judge is being imposed on you by me—but what they answer me. May the mercy of God keep you unharmed, excellent lord and deservedly to be received and most beloved brother.

[^1]: See 2 Tim 4:2; Titus 1:9
