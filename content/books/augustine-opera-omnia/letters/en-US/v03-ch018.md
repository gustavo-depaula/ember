# Letter 172

To the truly holy lord, and venerable to me with all affection, pope Augustine, from Jerome, greetings in Christ.

**1.** The honorable man, my brother, son of your worthiness, the presbyter Orosius, I received both by his own merit and at your bidding. But there fell a most difficult time, when it was better for me to be silent than to speak, so that our studies ceased and, as Appius says, a doglike eloquence was exercised. And so to your two little books, which you dedicated to my name, most learned and shining with all the splendor of eloquence, I could not for the time answer: not that I think there is anything in them to be blamed, but because, according to the blessed Apostle, *Let each one abound in his own sense; one indeed in this way, another in that*[^1]. Certainly, whatever could be said, and drawn by a sublime mind from the fountains of the holy Scriptures, has been set down and discussed by you. But I beg your reverence, suffer me a little to praise your genius. For we discuss between ourselves for the sake of erudition. But rivals, and especially heretics, if they see diverse opinions between us, will calumniate that they descend from rancor of mind. But it is my decree to love you, to look up to you, to revere, to admire you, and to defend your sayings as my own. Certainly even in the dialogue which I recently published, I made mention of your blessedness, as was fitting: and rather let us give effort, that the most pernicious heresy be taken from the churches, which always feigns penitence, that it may have the faculty of teaching in the churches; lest, if it betrays itself in the open light, expelled outdoors it die.

**2.** Your holy and venerable daughters Eustochium and Paula walk worthily both of their lineage and of your exhortation, and especially greet your blessedness; as does also the whole brotherhood which strives to serve the Lord Savior with us. The holy presbyter Firmus we sent last year, on their business, to Ravenna, and thence to Africa and Sicily, whom we think to be now staying in the parts of Africa. The holy ones clinging to your side, I pray that you greet with my service. I have also directed my letters to the holy presbyter Firmus; which, if they come to you, be not reluctant to direct to him. May Christ the Lord keep you safe and mindful of me, truly holy lord and most blessed pope. Postscript: We suffer a great scarcity of notaries of the Latin tongue in this province; and therefore we cannot obey your commands, especially in the edition of the Septuagint, which is marked with asterisks and obeli: for very much of our prior labor we lost through the fraud of someone.

[^1]: Rom 14:5
