# Letter 46

To the beloved and venerable father, bishop Augustine, Publicola

**1.** *In the Arzuges, as I have heard, the barbarians are wont to swear to the decurion who is in charge of the frontier, or to the tribune, swearing by their demons, those who have made a pact to lead the pack-animals, or some to guard the crops themselves; and the individual landholders or tenants are wont to receive them, as now trustworthy, to guard the crops, the decurion sending a letter, or the individual travelers to whom it is necessary to pass through their territory. But a doubt has arisen in my heart, whether that landholder who has received a barbarian, whose good faith has seemed firm through the swearing by demons, is not himself defiled, or those things which he guards, or that man who is led by the barbarian guide. But this you ought to know, that the barbarian who swears receives from the landholder gold for guarding the crops, or the guide from the traveler; but yet, along with this, as it were, payment which is wont to be given by the landholder or by the traveler, that mortal oath too, given to the decurion or tribune, stands in the midst: which disturbs me, lest it pollute him who receives the barbarian's oath, or those things which the barbarian guards. For on whatever condition—even gold given and hostages given, as I have heard—yet an iniquitous oath has intervened in the midst. Deign to write back to me definitively, and not in suspense. For if you yourself should write doubtfully, I can fall into greater doubts than before I asked.*

**2.** *This too I have heard, that those very tenant men who are in charge of my property receive an oath, for guarding the crops, from the barbarians swearing by their demons. If, then, when they swear by their demons that they will guard the crops, they do not pollute the crops themselves—so that if a Christian should eat of them knowingly, or should have profited from the price of those very things, he be defiled—deign to indicate.*

**3.** *Again, from another I have heard that no oath is sworn to the tenant by the barbarian, and another said that an oath is sworn to the tenant. If even he who said that an oath is sworn to the tenant told me falsely, ought I now, on account of this alone which I have heard, not to use those crops, or the price of them, merely because of hearsay, because it is said:* But if anyone says: This is offered in sacrifice to idols, do not eat, on account of him who pointed it out[^1]*? If, however, this case too is like the case about what is offered in sacrifice. But if it is so, what ought I to do about those crops, or about the price of them?*

**4.** *Ought I to inquire of both—he who told me that no oath is sworn to the tenant, and he who said that an oath is sworn to the tenant—and to prove the statement of each by witnesses, which of those two spoke the truth, and so long not to touch those crops, or the price, until it has been proved to me whether he spoke the truth who said that no oath is sworn to the tenant?*

**5.** *If the barbarian who, by his oath, swears an evil oath, has made that Christian tenant, or the tribune who is in charge of the frontier, swear to him, for keeping faith with him in guarding the crops, by that same mortal oath by which he himself swears—is that Christian alone defiled? Are not also those things for the sake of which he swears? Or if a pagan who is in charge of the frontier has sworn to a barbarian, for keeping faith with him, by a mortal oath—does he not defile those for whom he swears? If I send someone to the Arzuges, is it permitted him to receive that mortal oath from a barbarian; and is he not defiled, if a Christian accepts such an oath?*

**6.** *If, from the threshing floor of wheat or of any pulse, or from the winepress, something has been offered to a demon, is it permitted for a Christian knowingly to eat thereof?* Concerning wood taken from a grove.

**7.** *Concerning a grove, is it permitted for a Christian knowingly to take wood for any use of his own?*

**8.** *If someone going to the meat market buys meat which is not offered in sacrifice, and has had two thoughts in his heart—that it was offered, and that it was not offered—and held that thought by which he will think it not offered, does he sin if he eats it?*

**9.** *If someone does a good thing, about which he doubts whether it is good or evil, thinking it good, although he had also thought it evil, is the sin imputed to him?*

**10.** *If someone says that it is offered in sacrifice, lying, and afterward says again that he lied, and truly lied as to the fact, is it permitted for a Christian thereupon to eat, or to sell, and to use the price, from what he has heard?*

**11.** *If some Christian walking, suffering necessity, overcome by the hunger of one day, or of two, or of many days, so that he can now no longer hold out, should so come upon it that, in that very necessity of hunger, in which he sees death now drawing near to him, he finds food set in an idol-shrine, where there is none of men, and cannot find other food, ought he to die or to be fed thereon?* How far it is permitted to repel force with force.

**12.** *If a Christian sees that a barbarian or a Roman wishes to kill him, ought that Christian himself to kill them, lest he be killed by them: or is it permitted, without killing, to repel them or to fight them off, because it is said:* Do not resist *evil*[^2]*?* Concerning harms foreseen but not desired.

**13.** *Ought a Christian to build a wall for his property on account of an enemy, and is that Christian who built the wall not the cause of homicide, when from it some have begun to fight and to kill enemies?*

**14.** *Is it permitted to drink from a spring, or from a well, into which something of a sacrifice has been cast? Ought a Christian to drink from a well that is in a temple, and has been made deserted? If in a temple where an idol is worshipped there is a well or a spring, and nothing has been done there in the same well or spring, ought a Christian to draw water thence, and drink?*

**15.** *Ought a Christian to wash in the baths, or in the hot baths, in which sacrifice is made to images? Ought a Christian to wash in the baths in which the Pagans have washed on their feast day, whether with them or without them?*

**16.** *If into a basin into which the Pagans have gone down, coming from the idols on their feast day, they have done something there in the basin of their sacrilege, and a Christian knows it, ought he to go down into the same basin?*

**17.** *If a Christian, invited by someone, has had meat set before him for food, of which it has been said to him that it is offered in sacrifice, and he has not eaten it; but afterward, the same meat having been moved elsewhere by someone, he finds it by some chance for sale, and buys it, or, invited by some other, has it set before him, and does not recognize it, and eats it, does he sin?*

**18.** *Ought a Christian knowingly to buy from the garden or the property of the idols or of their priests a vegetable, or any fruit, and to eat thereof? Indeed, concerning the oath, or concerning the idols, that you may not suffer labor in inquiring, I wished to set before your eyes what, by the Lord's granting, we have found: but if you find anything else more open or better in the Scriptures, deign to indicate it to me. Whence the things we have found are these: where Laban said to Jacob:* The God of Abraham and the God of Nahor[^3]*; but what God, the Scripture did not indicate. And again:* Abimelech, when he came to Isaac, where he swore, or those who were with him[^4]*; but what kind of oath, the Scripture did not indicate. Again, concerning idols, where it was said to Gideon by the Lord in the book of Judges, that of the calf which he had killed he should make a holocaust*[^5]. And in Joshua, concerning Jericho, that all the gold, silver, and bronze should be brought into the treasuries of the Lord, and called holy, from the city which is accursed[^6]. And what is that which is set in Deuteronomy: You shall not bring an abomination into your house, and become accursed, as this very thing is[^7]? *May the Lord keep you: I greet you; pray for me.*

[^1]: See 1 Cor 10:28
[^2]: See Matt 5:39
[^3]: See Gen 31:53
[^4]: See Gen 26:26–31
[^5]: See Judg 6:25–26
[^6]: See Josh 6:19, 24
[^7]: See Deut 7:25–26
