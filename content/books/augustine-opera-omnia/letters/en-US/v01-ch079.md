# Letter 79

**1.** Without cause you shuffle about, since it is plain from afar what sort of man you are. What the brothers said to you, they have reported to me. It is good that you do not fear death; but you ought to fear that death which you make for yourself by blaspheming such things about God. And as for your understanding that this visible death, which all men know, is the separation of the mind from the body—it is no great thing to understand. But what you add from your own teaching, that it is the separation of good from evil: if the mind is good and the body evil, then he who joined them together is not good; but you say that God, who is good, joined these things together; therefore he is either evil, or he feared evil. And do you boast that you do not fear a man, while you fashion for yourself such a God, who feared the darkness, so as to mingle good and evil? But do not be lifted up in spirit, as you have written, because we make you great by wishing to hinder your poisons, lest the plague creep on to men; for the Apostle does not make great those whom he calls dogs, when he says: *Beware of the dogs*[^1]; nor did he make great those whose speech he said creeps like a cancer[^2]. And so I declare to you, in the name of Christ, that if you are prepared, solve the question in which your predecessor Fortunatus failed. And he had gone from here in such a way as not to return, unless, after conferring in disputation with his own, he should find what he could answer in reply, disputing with the brothers. But if you are not prepared for this, depart from here, and do not pervert the ways of the Lord, and ensnare so as to infect with poisons the weak souls, lest, with the right hand of our Lord helping, you blush as you did not think you would.

[^1]: Phil 3:2
[^2]: See 2 Tim 2:17
