# Letter 77

TO HIS MOST BELOVED LORDS AND DESERVEDLY HONORABLE BROTHERS FELIX AND HILARINUS, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** I do not wonder that Satan disturbs the minds of the faithful; resist him, abiding in the hope of the promises of God, who cannot deceive, who has deigned not only to promise eternal rewards to us who believe in him, and hope, and persevere in his love to the end, but has also foretold that temporal scandals would not be lacking, by which our faith should be exercised and proved. For he says: *Because iniquity will abound, the love of many will grow cold;* but immediately he added: *But he who perseveres to the end will be saved*[^1]. What wonder is it, then, if men disparage the servants of God, and—because they cannot pervert their life—try to discolor their reputation, when they do not cease daily to blaspheme God himself and their Lord, since whatever he does against their will by his just and hidden judgment displeases them? Hence I exhort your prudence, my most beloved lords and deservedly honorable brothers, to consider the Scripture of God—who foretold to us all these things to come, and forewarned us that we ought to stand firm against them—against the slanderous idle talk of men and rash suspicions, with a most Christian heart.

**2.** Briefly, then, I say to your Charity that the priest Boniface has been detected by me in no crime, and that I have by no means believed or believe any such thing of him. How, then, would I order his name to be removed from the number of the priests, when the Gospel terrifies me vehemently, where the Lord says: *With what judgment you have judged, you will be judged*[^2]? For since the case that has arisen between him and Spes hangs under the divine examination according to their agreement—which can be recited to you, if you wish—who am I to dare to anticipate the sentence of God by deleting or suppressing the name of one of whom I, as bishop, ought neither rashly to suspect anything evil, nor, as a man, could clearly judge concerning the hidden things of men? For even in secular cases, when the power of judging is referred to a higher authority, while all things remain as they were, that sentence is awaited from which it is no longer permitted to appeal, lest an injury be done to the higher judge if, while his judgment is pending, something were changed. And surely there is a great difference between the divine power and any human power, however exalted. May the mercy of the Lord our God never desert you, my most beloved and honorable lord brothers.

[^1]: Matt 24:12-13
[^2]: Matt 7:2
