# Letter 3\*

**TO FELIX, A SINCERELY MOST BELOVED LORD AND SON AND FELLOW DEACON, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.**

**1.** Considering your consultation more and more, I am compelled to cry out: *Sons of men, how long will you be heavy of heart? why do you love vanity and seek a lie?*[^1] For what is so vain as, out of the delight and desire of earthly posterity, to begrudge a daughter the heavenly rewards which are prepared for sacred virgins, to be beaten with so many bereavements of sons, and, about the one daughter who remains—whose temporal welfare, like that of all men, is surely uncertain—by a sure refusal and fraud done not to a man but to God, to wish to receive from God himself mortal grandchildren, subject to the many chances of mortals? But what is more lying than to vow virginity to God for an infant girl in peril, that, almost dead, she may be raised up, and then to slay that very vow, because she has revived, and to substitute one's own widowhood to God—as though he did not know what is better and were unable to discern these two things—for the daughter's virginity withdrawn? Much of earth does this thought savor; let the heart be on high, and it will despise all lowly things for heavenly happiness. *If you have risen with Christ,* says the Apostle, *seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God; savor the things that are above, not the things that are on earth*[^2].

**2.** Accordingly, this daughter of ours, *if she does not contain herself, let her marry*[^3], and be among those of whom the same Teacher says: *I wish the younger to marry, to bear children, to be mothers of families*[^4], since that which follows—*to give no occasion to the adversary for the sake of reviling*[^5]—is to savor the things that are above. But if she can contain herself, so that thus [the daughter] becomes her inferior, how much more can it be that she herself be better, although not equal to her daughter, a holy widow; if that one be a holy widow, let this one, then, be added to her, not substituted for her. Let her not so offer her own good as to take away another's better; but she has the power over herself as to what she may choose[^6]. For she is not to be said to have vowed her widowhood, since, a condition having been proposed, she still inquires whether she should compensate her preserved widowhood with her daughter's unpreserved virginity. But by no reasoning does she compensate, not only because virginity is preferable to widowhood, but because the one is her own, the other another's, and surely she ought not, for her own gain, to seek another's loss. Finally, if what cannot be done could be done, that she should recall virginity to herself through continence, not even so should such a compensation be sought. For she ought to add herself, not take another away from so great a good, nor for that reason ascend to that higher place, so as thence to cast down her daughter.

**3.** She has herself, then, as has been said, in her own power[^7], because she has not yet vowed, since she still inquires whether she ought to vow for such a thing. But what she has already vowed is to be rendered, and it is not to be inquired whether it ought to be rendered, although to one inquiring whether she ought to vow her widowhood I would more truly and rightly answer: you ought, but for the taking away of your daughter's virginity you ought not. But why I have said: You ought, yet not for the cause you think you ought, you should attend a little; when the Lord Jesus had shown the hard condition of the married about not dismissing a wife[^8], his disciples answered: *If such is the case with a wife, it is not expedient to marry*[^9], and to these the Lord: *Not all,* he says, *grasp this word, but those to whom it is given*[^10], and a little after: *He who can grasp it, let him grasp it*[^11]. You, then, grasp it, since, when, for your daughter—nay rather, against your daughter, that she be not a virgin—you are prepared to remain [a widow], you are convicted of being able, nor is there any way you can excuse yourself from him who said: *He who can grasp it, let him grasp it.* Grasp it, then, because you can, but attend that those eunuchs were praised for this reason, that they castrated themselves for the sake of the kingdom of heaven[^12], not for the sake of earthly fruit and solace; grasp it that you may be increased, not that your daughter be diminished; grasp it that you too may have what you have vowed, not that you may take from your daughter what you have vowed; grasp it for the sake of having more and richer virtues, not for the sake of grandchildren, in whom you fear such bereavements as you have already experienced! But about your daughter, as far as is in you, render your vows! *For it is better not to vow,* as Scripture says, *than to vow and not render*[^13]; and the Apostle says: *Having,* he says, *damnation, because they made void their first faith*[^14]. But I have said: "As far as is in you, render your vows," because it is not in your power what the girl may choose if she grows up, when she comes to the age at which she ought to choose, but it pertains to you with what hope you nourish her whom you have vowed. But not only without your, but even without her own, sin, before the profession of virginity, if she has preferred this, she will choose marriage, losing only this, which she would have attained, if, consenting to your vow, she had persisted a virgin. For just as one who is baptized as a little child, if, coming to the years of an age in which he can use reason, he disapproves and rejects what was conferred on him by the will of his parents, loses this, which he would have attained if he had remained in the same grace, that is, to be in eternal life with Christ, whence it will remain for him to be in eternal punishment with the devil; so an adult virgin, who was raised by her parents in the hope of future sacred virginity, if perhaps, when she has become adult, before she professes the same holiness, she chooses to be married, will suffer a detriment not, like that one, of the kingdom of God, but of an ampler honor in the kingdom of God. For just as it was said: *If anyone is not reborn of water and the Spirit, he will not enter into the kingdom of God*[^15], it was not also said: "She who has not chosen sacred virginity will not enter into the kingdom of God." But indeed, if after her profession she falls from that purpose, she will be condemned, because she *made void her first faith*[^16]. What, therefore, she ought to beware, when she has professed, this you ought to beware in that which you have vowed about her virginity; and rather, on account of the uncertainty of her will, your widowhood too ought to be joined to your vow about her, so that, if she agrees with your vow, you may remain with her in holy widowhood, since in holy virginity you no longer can, but if she perhaps chooses otherwise, you may remain for her in what you can. But God is powerful, who may make you both abide in his gifts. These things I would say to a Christian woman, about whom you thought I should be consulted.

**4.** But to you I say that you should deal with her in such a way as you see that you love her, which is good for the sake of the kingdom of heaven. For care must be taken lest perhaps, desiring widowhood not for the reason for which it is to be desired, she be unable to bear the impulse of her age, especially on account of the always uncertain chances of mortals. For it is not in her power that her daughter marry or bear children, and surely it is to be feared lest, when something else perhaps—which God avert—happens, she think herself defrauded and, as it were, to have lost the reward of her purpose, and fail through this carnal thought. But may our God, who said: *Not all grasp this word, but those to whom it is given*[^17], hold her heart and lift it on high, that she may love the heavenly promises and despise the earthly things she has experienced. But about her daughter she ought to intend and ask nothing else, except that what she has vowed be fulfilled; which may he grant her *in whose hand are both we and our words*[^18].

**5.** You do not know—nay rather, you know—how much I desire to rejoice over the offspring of the religious Innocentia, whom I learned to have departed from the body only from this more recent letter of yours. But how each one is in the faith of Christ whom she here left from her own womb, I ask that you not be reluctant to write back at the first occasion that occurs.
