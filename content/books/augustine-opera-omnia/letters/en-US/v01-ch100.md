# Letter 99

TO THE MOST RELIGIOUS AND, IN THE MEMBERS OF CHRIST, DESERVEDLY AND HOLILY PRAISEWORTHY HANDMAID OF GOD ITALICA, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** I had received three letters of your Benignity, when I wrote this back. One which still demanded my letters, another which indicated that mine had now reached you, the third which contained your most benevolent care for us, even concerning the house of the most distinguished and excellent young man Julian, which adjoins our walls. Having received it, I did not defer to answer at once, because the procurator of your Distinction wrote that he could quickly send to Rome: by whose letters we were gravely saddened, because what is being done there in the City, or around the City, he did not take care to insinuate to us, so that it might become certain among us, which we were unwilling to believe to uncertain fame. For by the letters of the brothers sent before, although troublesome and hard, yet much lighter things were announced. I wondered, indeed, more than can be said, that, even on so great an occasion of your people, the holy brother bishops did not write, nor did your letter insinuate to us anything about your so great tribulations; which surely, through the bowels of love, are ours too; unless perhaps you thought you should not do what you judged would profit nothing, or you were unwilling that we be saddened by your letters. It profits something, as I judge, even to know these things. First, because it is unjust to wish to rejoice with those who rejoice, and to be unwilling to weep with those who weep[^1]: then, because tribulation works patience, patience proving, proving hope; but hope does not confound, because the love of God is poured out in our hearts through the Holy Spirit, who has been given to us[^2].

**2.** Far be it, therefore, that we refuse to hear even what is bitter and sad concerning our dearest ones. For, I know not in what way, less becomes what one member suffers, if the other members suffer with it[^3]. Nor is the relief of the evil itself made through the communion of the calamity, but through the consolation of love, so that, although some suffer by bearing, others suffer with them by knowing, yet the tribulation may be common to those to whom the proving, the hope, the love, and the Spirit are common. But the Lord consoles us all, who both foretold these temporal evils, and after these promised eternal good things: nor ought he, when he does battle, to be broken who wishes to be crowned after the battle, with him supplying strength to those contending, who prepares unspeakable gifts for the victors.

**3.** Let that reply of ours not take away from you the confidence of writing to us, especially because you have softened our fear by a not improbable defense. We greet again your little ones, and we wish them to grow up for you in Christ—who already at this age discern how perilous and harmful is the love of this age; and would that, when they are shaken by great and hard things, the small and flexible things may be corrected. About that house, what shall I say, except give thanks to your most benign care? For the one which we can give, they do not wish; but the one which they wish, we cannot give. For it was not, as they falsely heard, left to the Church by my predecessor, but is possessed among its ancient estates, and so adjoins another ancient church as that one in question adjoins another.

[^1]: See Rom 12:15
[^2]: See Rom 5:3-5
[^3]: See 1 Cor 12:26
