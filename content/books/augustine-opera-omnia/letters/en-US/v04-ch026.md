# Letter 234

TO AUGUSTINE, A LORD TO BE VENERATED, AND TRULY AND DESERVEDLY TO BE HONORED, A HOLY FATHER, LONGINIANUS.

**1.** *I am blessed, and much enlightened by the pure light of your shining virtue, you who have judged me worthy to be heaped with the honor of your divine address. But you lay upon me, lord to be venerated, a heavy burden and a most difficult task of answering, especially in your inquiries, and at this time, in explaining such things, through the opinion of my own view—that is, by a pagan man. Since indeed it abounds in questions, which in part either has long since been agreed between us, or now again and again agrees by letters more and more with precepts—I will not say only Socratic, nor your prophetic ones, O truly best of Romans, or the few of Jerusalem; but also the Orphic and the Agetic and the Trismegistic, far more ancient than those, born when the world was still almost untrained, with gods as authors, and the whole world divided into three parts shown by divine appointment with fixed boundaries, before either Europe took, or Asia received, or Libya possessed a good man, such as you, so help me God, both will be and have been. For indeed, since the memory of men—unless you grant the schema of fables composed in the fictions of Xenophon—I have as yet heard, read, seen no one, or certainly after one, none, which, God being witness, I would say at a good and sure risk, except you, who both ever strive to acknowledge God, and can, with purity of mind and the heaviness of the body cast off, follow him most easily, and hold him in the hope of a perfect conscience, with no doubtful credulity.*

**2.** *But by what way it can be brought about is rather for you, who are not ignorant of it, to discourse on—and that without anything being suggested to me from without—than for you to learn from me, lord to be honored. Because then, I confess, although about to set out for the seat of this good, sufficient, as my priesthoods require, I am by no means there yet, and, if I can at all, I gather provisions for the journey. But what I hold and keep as handed down holily and from antiquity, I will declare in a few words as I can. There is a better way to God, by which a good man—proved by devout, pure, just, chaste, true words and deeds, without any catching at the boasting of changed times, and walled about by the company of the gods, having at any rate earned merit by the powers of God; that is, the powers being fulfilled of him who is one, and universal, and incomprehensible, and ineffable, and untiring Creator, which, as is your custom, you call angels, or whatever else there is after God, or with God, or from God—hastens to go to God by the intention of mind and soul. There is a way, I say, by which those purified hasten, refined by the devout precepts and most pure expiations of the ancient rites, and by abstemious observances, steadfast in soul and body.*

**3.** *But about Christ—now of your faith, both carnal and in spirit, God, through whom you go secure to that highest, blessed, true Father of all—lord father to be honored, I neither dare nor am able to express what I think; because what I do not know, I believe most difficult to define. But since you have deigned to suggest to me, already knowing, that you love me as a cultivator of your virtues, I have enough for the testimony of a good life, which I keep, lest I displease you, who daily commend God and your soul to yourself: you understand without doubt that I too love you delightedly, since I take and hold the rule and line of your judgment formed about me. But before all things, I ask you to grant pardon to my most slight opinion, and easily to indulge my discourse to you, perhaps loose and incongruous, because you compelled it; and to deign, if I deserve it, to inform me both what you think about these matters, and what you yourself feel, by your holy writings—which, as that one says, are now sweeter not than honey but than nectar*[^1]. *May you fully enjoy the goodness of God, lord father, and may you please God by perpetual holiness—which is necessary.*

[^1]: See Homer, Iliad 1.249
