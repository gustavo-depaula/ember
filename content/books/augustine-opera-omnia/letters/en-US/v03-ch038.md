# Letter 189

To the excellent lord, and deservedly distinguished and honorable son Boniface, from Augustine, greetings in the Lord.

**1.** I had already written back to your charity, but while an occasion for directing the letter was being sought, there came up my most beloved son Faustus, proceeding to your excellency. Who, when he had received the very letter which I had already made, to be carried to your benevolence, suggested to me that you much desire that I write you something which may build you up to everlasting salvation, the hope of which you have in our Lord Jesus Christ. And although he so insisted with me, occupied as I am, that I should not delay to do it, you know how sincerely he loves you. To meet him, then, as he hastened, I preferred hastily to write something, rather than to delay your religious desire, excellent lord, and deservedly distinguished and honorable son.

**2.** What, then, I can briefly say: *Love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole strength; and, love your neighbor as yourself*: for this is the word which the Lord made short upon the earth, saying in the Gospel: *On these two precepts the whole Law and the Prophets hang*[^1]. In this love, then, advance daily, both by praying and by acting well, that, with him helping who commanded and granted it to you, it may be nourished and grow, until, perfect, it perfect you. For it is itself the love which, as the Apostle says, *is poured out in our hearts by the Holy Spirit who has been given to us*[^2]; it is that of which he likewise says: *The fullness of the law is love*[^3]: it is that through which faith works; whence he says again: *Neither circumcision avails anything, nor uncircumcision; but faith which works through love*[^4].

**3.** In this love, then, all our holy fathers and Patriarchs and Prophets and Apostles pleased God. In this all the true martyrs strove against the devil even unto blood; and because in them it neither cooled nor failed, therefore they conquered. In this all the good faithful daily advance, desiring to come not to the kingdom of mortals, but to the kingdom of heaven; not to a temporal, but to an everlasting inheritance; not to gold and silver, but to the incorruptible riches of the angels; not to any goods of this world, in which one lives with fear, nor can anyone take them with himself when he dies, but to seeing God: whose sweetness and delight exceeds the beauty of all bodies, not only earthly, but even heavenly; exceeds all the comeliness of souls however just and holy; exceeds all the form of the supernal angels and powers; exceeds whatever is not only said, but even thought, about him. And let us not, because this so great promise is exceedingly great, on that account despair of it; but rather, because the exceedingly great one promised it, let us believe that we shall receive it: for, as the blessed apostle John says: *We are sons of God, and it has not yet appeared what we shall be: we know that when he appears, we shall be like him, for we shall see him as he is*[^5].

**4.** Do not suppose that no one can please God who serves in the arms of war. Among these was holy David, to whom the Lord bore so great a testimony: among these too were very many just men of that time. Among these was also that centurion who said to the Lord: *I am not worthy that you should enter under my roof; but only say the word, and my servant will be healed. For I too am a man set under authority, having soldiers under me, and I say to this one, Go, and he goes; and to another, Come, and he comes; and to my servant, Do this, and he does it*: of whom the Lord also said: *Amen I say to you, I have not found such faith in Israel*[^6]. Among these was also that Cornelius to whom the angel sent said: *Cornelius, your alms are accepted, and your prayers are heard*: where he admonished him to send to the blessed apostle Peter, and to hear from him what he ought to do; to which Apostle, that he might come to him, he sent even a religious soldier. Among these were also those who, when they had come to John, the holy precursor of the Lord and friend of the Bridegroom, of whom the Lord himself says, *Among those born of women there has not arisen a greater than John the Baptist*, to be baptized, and had asked of him what they should do; he answered them: *Do violence to no one, accuse no one falsely; let your wages suffice you*[^7]. He surely did not forbid them to serve under arms, to whom he commanded that their wages ought to suffice.

**5.** They are indeed of a greater place with God who, having abandoned all these secular actions, serve him even by the supreme continence of chastity; but each one, as the Apostle says, *has his own gift from God; one in this way, another in that*[^8]. Some, then, by praying for you, fight against invisible enemies; you, by fighting for them, labor against visible barbarians. Would that there were one faith in all, because both there would be less labor, and the devil with his angels would more easily be conquered! But because in this world it is necessary that the citizens of the kingdom of heaven be agitated by temptations among the erring and the impious, that they may be exercised, and proved like gold as in a furnace, we ought not to wish to live before the time with the saints and just alone, that in its own time we may deserve to receive this.

**6.** This, then, first consider, when you are armed for battle, that your strength, even the bodily itself, is a gift of God. For so you will think, of the gift of God, not to act against God. For faith, when it is promised, must be kept even to the enemy against whom war is waged; how much more to the friend for whom one fights! The will ought to have peace, war necessity, that God may free from necessity and preserve in peace. For peace is not sought that war may be stirred up, but war is waged that peace may be acquired. Be, then, even in waging war, peaceable, that you may, by conquering, bring those whom you fight to the usefulness of peace: *For blessed are the peacemakers*, says the Lord, *for they shall be called sons of God*[^9]. But if human peace is so sweet for the temporal safety of mortals, how much sweeter is the divine peace for the eternal safety of the angels! And so let necessity, not will, slay the enemy fighting. As violence is rendered to one rebelling and resisting, so mercy is now owed to one conquered or captured, especially in one in whom no disturbance of peace is feared.

**7.** Let conjugal chastity adorn your morals, let sobriety and frugality adorn them: for it is very base that one whom a man does not conquer should be conquered by lust, and that he be overwhelmed by wine who is not conquered by the sword. Worldly riches, if they are lacking, let them not be sought in the world by evil works; but if they are present, let them be kept in heaven by good works. A manly and Christian mind ought neither, if they come, to be lifted up, nor, if they depart, to be broken. Let us rather think that which the Lord says: *Where your treasure is, there will your heart be also*[^10]; and surely when we hear that we should have the heart on high, we ought not to answer falsely what you know we answer.

**8.** And in these things indeed I have known you to be well zealous, and I am much delighted by your reputation, and much congratulate you in the Lord, so that this letter may be for you rather a mirror where you may see what you are like, than where you may learn what you ought to be like: nevertheless, whatever, whether in this or in the holy Scriptures, you find that is still lacking to you for a good life, urge yourself to acquire it both by acting and by praying; and for those things which you have, give thanks to God, as to the fountain of goodness whence you have them; and in all your good acts give him the glory, yourself the humility. For as it is written: *Every best gift, and every perfect gift, is from above, descending from the Father of lights*[^11]. But however much you advance in the love of God and neighbor, and in true piety, as long as you dwell in this life, do not believe yourself to be without sin: for of this very thing it is read in the holy Letters: *Is not human life upon the earth a temptation?*[^12] Accordingly, since always, as long as you are in this body, it is necessary for you to say in prayer what the Lord taught, *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*; remember to forgive quickly, if anyone sins against you and asks pardon of you, that you may truly be able to pray, and may avail to obtain pardon for your sins. These things I have written to your charity hastily, since the haste of the bearer pressed me. But I give thanks to God, since I have not, in whatever way, been lacking to your good desire. May the mercy of God always protect you, excellent lord, and deservedly distinguished and honorable son.
