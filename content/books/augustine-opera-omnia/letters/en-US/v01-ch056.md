# Letter 56

To the excellent lord and deservedly honorable and most beloved son Celer, Augustine

**1.** I am not unmindful of my promise and of your wish. But since by necessity I set out to visit the churches belonging to my care, and could not at once render the debt by myself: yet I did not wish to owe it to you longer, since it could be repaid even with me away. Accordingly I have delegated to my most beloved son, the presbyter Optatus, that at those hours which you see to be more opportune for you, he read with you the things I promised: when he has perceived that the whole can be done, your Excellence too, however gratefully you receive this, he will urge to do as tirelessly and keenly. But how much I love you, and how I would wish you to be delighted and exercised in wholesome studies in the knowledge of divine and human things, I believe you very well understand.

**2.** If you do not spurn the love of my duty, I hope, in the Christian faith itself and in the morals befitting your person, now so constituted, that you will have such advances that the last day of this smoke or temporal vapor, which is called human life, which no mortal is granted to escape, you may await—whether eager, or secure, or at least not despairingly anxious—not in the vanity of error, but in the solidity of truth. For as certain as it is to you that you live, so certain let it be, by wholesome teaching, that this life which is spent in temporal delights, in comparison with the eternal life which is promised to us through Christ and in Christ, is to be reckoned not life but death. But in no way would I doubt of your character, that you will most easily extricate yourself from that custom of the Donatists, if you most religiously do not lightly esteem Christian purity itself. For by what unshaken foundations of documents that error is convicted, it is no great matter even for those slow of talent to perceive thoroughly, if only they listen patiently and attentively. But to follow an unaccustomed rightness, to break off the bond of an accustomed and as it were familiar perversity, is for greater strength. And by no means is one to despair, with our Lord God himself aiding and exhorting, concerning your noble freedom and plainly manly breast. May the mercy of our Lord God keep you unharmed, excellent lord and deservedly honorable and most beloved son.
