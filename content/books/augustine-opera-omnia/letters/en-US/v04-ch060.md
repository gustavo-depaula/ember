# Letter 267

TO FABIOLA, A MOST RELIGIOUS AND MOST EXCELLENT LADY AND, IN THE LOVE OF CHRIST, PRAISEWORTHY DAUGHTER, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Although you had sent a reply, yet I so read the letter of Your Holiness that I thought it a debt to answer it. For you grieved over the pilgrimage by which it falls to us to rejoice perpetually with the saints; and you deservedly preferred the desire of the heavenly fatherland, where we shall no longer be divided by the space of lands, but shall ever be glad in the contemplation of the One. You are happy in faithfully thinking such things; happier in loving them; and therefore you will be also most happy in attaining them. But even now look more diligently whence we are rather said to be absent; whether because we do not see each other's bodies, or because we do not give and receive signs of minds, which is to converse. For I think that, although separated in body by distant regions, if we could know each other's thoughts, we would be more with one another, than if, in one place, beholding each other, we sat silent, bringing forth nothing of our inmost selves in words, indicating our minds by no movements of the bodies. Wherefore you understand that each one is more present to himself than one to another, for this reason, that each one is more known to himself than to another; not by beholding his own face, which, unless a mirror be present, is carried about and hidden, but by beholding his conscience, which he sees even with closed eyes. How great, therefore, is even our life, which is held for a great thing?
