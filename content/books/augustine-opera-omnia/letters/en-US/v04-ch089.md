# Letter 24\*

TO THE EXCELLENT LORD, DESERVEDLY HONORABLE AND MOST LONGED-FOR SON EUSTOCHIUS, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD

**1.** Since you owe true responses faithfully to all who consult you, how much more to us, the ministers of Christ, by whose faith you are faithful, for the obtaining of that inheritance whose testament is the Gospel, O lord [...]. Since, then, the Apostle has commanded that secular judgments, if Christians have them among themselves, take place not in the forum but in the church, whence we are obliged to endure such questions of those in dispute, in which we must look into even earthly laws—especially concerning the temporal condition of men (for we can, according to apostolic discipline, command slaves that they be subject to their masters[^1], but not lay the yoke of servitude upon free men)—I ask your most sincere Charity that you deign to instruct me what is to be observed concerning those who are born of a free woman and a slave. For that slaves are born of a slave woman and a free man I already know; what too of those whose fathers sell their labor for a fixed number of years? For I ask whether, when the selling fathers have died, they are compelled to complete the same number of years, or are freed by the death of those to whom they had been sold, or rather in a manner hired out, since, as it is held, they begin to be already their own masters; I ask also whether free fathers can sell their sons into perpetual slavery, and whether mothers can sell even the labor of their sons; likewise I ask whether, if a tenant farmer has sold his son—in the way it is permitted that he be sold by his father—the buyer has more right in the one who is sold than the owner of the estate, from whom the status of tenancy takes its origin, and whether it is permitted to an owner to make slaves of tenant farmers or of the sons of his tenant farmers.

**2.** I ask also what has been clearly established by law or by statutes concerning those stewards; for it seems to me very harsh that prejudice be done to free birth and to one's own good deed. For freeborn men are often requested to be stewards, and they think they are conferring a favor if they do what they are asked, and they really do confer it, to such a degree that the one who requested it even gives thanks if he has been deemed worthy to obtain it; and if by this favor of his a free man is made a slave, he would by no means do it if he knew this, nor would anyone dare to ask this of one who knew. Yet I am moved by certain constitutions that have been laid before me, when such a question was being turned over among us about the sons of a certain man who will perhaps be proved to have been a steward; but the one who contends I do not wish to compel to prove it, unless I first know what I am to follow, if perchance he should prove it. Accordingly I have sent these same constitutions to your Excellency to consider, two of which I sense speak to the very matter, but the rest I either do not understand, or they do not pertain at all to what is asked. I beseech you to help me even though absent in body, just as you are accustomed to help me when present.

[^1]: See Titus 2:9
