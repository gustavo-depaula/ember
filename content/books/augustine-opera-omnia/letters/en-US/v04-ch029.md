# Letter 237

TO CERETIUS, A MOST BLESSED LORD AND DESERVEDLY VENERABLE BROTHER AND FELLOW BISHOP, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Having read what Your Holiness sent, it seems to me that Argirius either fell among the Priscillianists unknowingly, so that he was altogether ignorant whether they were Priscillianists, or was already entangled in the nets of that same heresy. For I do not doubt that those writings are of the Priscillianists. But scarcely could I find leisure, somehow, with one necessity after another without intermission overcoming me, for even one whole one of those two books to be read to me. For somehow the other went astray, and, most diligently sought among our people, could in no way be found, most blessed lord and deservedly venerable father.

**2.** The hymn indeed which they say is of our Lord Jesus Christ, which especially moved Your Reverence, is wont to be found in the apocryphal writings. These are not proper to the Priscillianists, but other heretics too use them with the impiety of the vanity of various sects; thinking indeed diverse things among themselves, whence each followed his own various heresies, but they have these writings in common in their diversity, and those especially are wont to frequent them who do not accept the old Law and the canonical prophets. For they deny that these pertain to the good God and to Christ his Son; as do the Manichees, as do the Marcionites, and the rest to whom this damnable blasphemy was pleasing. These too, even in the canonical Scriptures of the New Testament, that is, in the true evangelical and apostolic writings, do not accept everything, but what they wish, and they choose the books they accept, the others being rejected. But even in each single book they distinguish the places that they think suit their errors: the rest in them they hold for false. For certain Manichees repudiate the canonical book whose title is *The Acts of the Apostles*. For they fear the most evident truth, where the Holy Spirit appears sent, who is promised by the Lord Jesus Christ in the evangelical truth[^1]. For under the name of that Spirit, from whom they are utterly alien, they deceive the unlearned hearts of men, asserting with wondrous blindness that the same promise of the Lord was fulfilled in their heresiarch Mani. This those heretics too do who are called Cataphrygians, saying that through certain madmen, namely Montanus and Priscilla, whom they have as their own prophets, came the Holy Spirit whom the Lord promised he would send[^2].

**3.** The Priscillianists, however, accept everything, both canonical and apocryphal together. But whatever things are against them, they pervert into the senses of their own perversity by an exposition sometimes crafty and cunning, sometimes ridiculous and dull. Nor even so do they believe that those very things which they expound to people outside their sect are true; otherwise they would either be Catholics, or not much alien from the truth, who would find Catholic senses even in those apocryphal writings, or would seem to wish to find them: but since they themselves think other things with their own, and among their own teach or learn things they do not dare to bring forth—because they are in truth wicked and detestable—nevertheless they preach the Catholic faith to those they fear, not the faith they hold, but the faith under which they hide. For some heretics can perhaps be found more unclean, but none is comparable to these in falsehood. For others, as are the vices of men, lie from the custom or weakness of this life: but these are reported to have, in the very wicked teaching of their heresy, a precept that, for the sake of concealing their dogmas, they should lie even with false swearing. Those who have had experience of them, and had been of them, and were freed from them by the mercy of God, recall even the very words of this precept: *Swear, forswear, but do not betray the secret.*

**4.** Accordingly, that it may be seen without any difficulty how they do not think about the apocryphal writings what they pretend to expound, the reason must be considered which they seem to render, that divine authority, as it were, is attributed to those same writings—so that, what is more wicked, they even prefer them to the canonical ones. You have their words in that book set down thus: "A hymn of the Lord, which he said secretly to the holy Apostles his disciples, because it is written in the Gospel, *A hymn having been said, he went up into the mountain*[^3]; and which is not placed in the canon, on account of those who think according to themselves, and not according to the spirit and truth of God, for the reason that it is written: *It is good to hide the secret of the king; but to reveal the works of God is honorable*[^4]." This is their great reason why this hymn is not in the canon: that, like the secret of the king, it had to be hidden from those who think according to the flesh, and not according to the spirit and truth of God. Therefore the canonical Scriptures do not pertain to the secret of the king, which to these seems to be hidden; and they were written for those who think according to the flesh, and not according to the spirit and truth of God. What is this but to say that the holy canonical Scriptures neither savor according to the Spirit of God, nor pertain to the truth of God? Who could hear this? who could bear the horror of so great an impiety? Or if the canonical Scriptures are understood spiritually by the spiritual, carnally by the carnal, why is this hymn too not in the canon, if the spiritual too understand it spiritually, the carnal carnally?

**5.** Then what cause is there that these people try to expound this same hymn according to the canonical Scriptures? For if it is not in the canonical Scriptures for this reason, that those Scriptures were written for the carnal, but this hymn for the spiritual; how is a hymn that does not pertain to the carnal expounded from Scriptures pertaining to carnal men? For if, for example, in this hymn it is sung and said, *I wish to loose, and I wish to be loosed*—because, as these people expound these words, the Lord Christ looses us from the conversation of the world, that we may not again be bound in it—this we have surely learned in the canonical Scriptures, that the Lord looses us from the conversation of the world, and that in it we ought not again to be bound. For what else is, *You have broken my bonds*[^5]? what else is, *The Lord looses those in chains*[^6]? And those already loosed the Apostle admonishes, saying: *Stand therefore, and be not again held under the yoke of servitude*[^7]: and the apostle Peter says: *For if, having fled the defilements of the world in the knowledge of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ, they are again entangled in them and overcome, their last state has become worse for them than the former*[^8]; thus showing that, when we have been loosed, we ought not again to be bound to the world. Since, then, these things are manifest in the canon—whether from these testimonies which I have mentioned, or from very many others—and do not cease to be read and preached; what is it that these people say that this hymn, where, to speak according to them, the words are most obscurely set down, is therefore not in the canon, lest they be veiled from the carnal? when we rather see those things revealed in the canon, but in this hymn altogether veiled, as they themselves assert; for, as is rather to be believed, they are not those things at all, but I-know-not-what others, which by such an exposition they much more veil, and dread to reveal.

**6.** For surely if by those words this is signified, that the Lord looses us from the conversation of the world, and that we may not again be bound in it, it would not be said, "I wish to loose, and I wish to be loosed," but, "I wish to loose, and those whom I have loosed I wish not to be bound." Or if he transfigures his members, that is, his faithful, into himself—just as he says, *I was hungry, and you gave me to eat*[^9]—he would rather say, I wish to be loosed, and I wish not to be bound. Or if he himself looses, and he himself is loosed, for this reason, that, the head being loosed, the members are loosed, the members which he was persecuting, to whom he cried from heaven, *Saul, Saul, why do you persecute me?*[^10]—this indeed the expositor of these words did not say; but even if he had said it, we would answer him what we answered a little before: since we read these things in the canonical Scriptures, there we understand them, thence we confirm them, thence we daily preach them. What is it, then, that this hymn is said to have been withdrawn from the carnal, so as not to be placed in the canon, when what is covered in it is open in the canon? Or are they so foolish—nay, so insane—that they dare to say that the secret of the king is hidden in this hymn from the spiritual, but manifest in the canon to the carnal?

**7.** This can be said of the earlier words of the same hymn, where it says, "I wish to save, and I wish to be saved." For if, as they themselves expound, these words signify this, that we are saved by the Lord through baptism, and that we save, that is, guard in ourselves, the Spirit given to us through baptism; does not the canonical Scripture cry out this sense, where we read: *He saved us through the washing of regeneration*[^11]? and where it is said to us: *Do not extinguish the Spirit*[^12]? How, then, is this hymn not in the canon, lest it become known to the carnal, when that which is obscure in it shines in the canon; unless because, under this exposition, whatever it is, which they hold out to others, they try to conceal that which they themselves here understand? Who nevertheless are so blind that they even apply certain words from the canon itself to expounding a hymn which for that reason they say is not in the canon, lest the secret of the king be betrayed to the carnal. What, then, do the things more clearly set down in the canon accomplish, through which the obscure things in this hymn are opened up?

**8.** For if this, as they say, is to be understood in this hymn, where it says, "I wish to be begotten," what is written in the canonical Letter of the apostle Paul: *For whom I am again in travail until Christ be formed in you*[^13]; if this is to be understood in this hymn, where it says, "I wish to sing," what is written in the canonical Psalm: *Sing to the Lord a new song*[^14]; if this is to be understood in this hymn, where it says, "Dance, all of you," what is written in the evangelical song: *We sang to you, and you did not dance*[^15]; if this is to be understood in this hymn, "I wish to mourn, beat yourselves all of you," what is written in the evangelical song: *We mourned to you, and you did not lament*[^16]; if "I wish to adorn, and I wish to be adorned" signifies in this hymn what is written in the canon: *That Christ may dwell through faith in your hearts*[^17]; and: *You are the temple of God, and the Spirit of God dwells in you*[^18]; if what it says in this hymn, "I am a lamp to you, you who see me," signifies what is written in the canonical Psalm: *In your light we shall see light*[^19]; if what it says in this hymn, "I am a door to you, whoever knocks at me," signifies what is read in the canonical Psalm: *Open to me the gates of righteousness; entering into them, I will confess to the Lord*[^20]; and in another psalm: *Lift up your gates, you princes, and be lifted up, eternal gates, and the king of glory will enter in*[^21]; if what it says in this hymn, "You who see what I do, keep silent about my works," signifies what is written in the book of Tobit: *It is good to hide the secret of the king*[^22]: why is this hymn said to be not in the canon for this reason, that the secret of the king may be hidden from the carnal; when the things expounded in this hymn are read even in the canon, and are there found so manifest that through them the obscure things of the hymn are expounded: unless because they have these expositions in which they hide; but in the words of that hymn, which they pretend to expound, they understand what they fear to expound to outsiders?

**9.** It would be long to show everything by disputing. But from the things we have said, it is most easy to consider the rest, and to see that the things which they say in the exposition of this hymn that are good and honorable are found in the canon too. Hence that is not their reason but their evasion: that it was separated from the canon for this reason, that the secret of the king had to be hidden from carnal men. Hence not undeservedly are they believed, by these expositions, not to wish to open what they read, but rather to cover what they think. Nor is it any wonder, since they have believed that the Lord Jesus himself, speaking not through the mouths of the prophets, or of the apostles, or of angels, but through his own mouth, was a mocker rather than a teacher of truth. Attributing, at any rate, divine authority to this hymn, where some composer of that same hymn feigned him to have said, "By a word I mocked all things, and I was not mocked at all": let them answer, if they can, those excellent spiritual men, where we are to go, to whom we are to open our ears, whom, speaking, we are at all to believe, in whose promise we are to place hope, if by a word Christ mocked all things, if by a word the omnipotent master mocked all things, if by a word he who is the Only-begotten Word of God the Father mocked all things. What more shall I say of these lost vaniloquent men and seducers of the mind—first of their own mind, then of the rest, whom they have been able to associate with themselves, predestined to eternal destruction? I have written back to Your Reverence, both much later than I had wished, and more than I had arranged. You do most rightly to watchfully guard against the wolves; but also for the healing of the sheep, if perhaps they have violated, or already wounded, any, labor with pastoral diligence, with the Lord of shepherds himself helping.
