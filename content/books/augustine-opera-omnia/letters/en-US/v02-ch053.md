# Letter 152

TO MY LORD, DESERVEDLY VENERABLE AND UNIQUELY TO BE REVERED FATHER AUGUSTINE, FROM MACEDONIUS.

**1.** I received the much-desired letter of your Holiness through Boniface, a bishop of the venerable law; whom I loved the more for being received, because he had brought what I desired, the discourse of your Holiness and your well-being, my deservedly venerable and uniquely to be reverered father. And so without delay he obtained what he asked. But since the occasion presents itself, I do not wish this very thing, however small it is, which, being admonished, I granted, to remain without reward. For I desire to receive that reward which profits me without disadvantage to the one paying, indeed which profits me with glory to the one paying.

**2.** You say that it is the office of your priesthood to intervene for the accused, and, unless you obtain it, to be offended, as though you brought back nothing of what was your office. Here I vehemently doubt whether this descends from religion. For if sins are so forbidden by the Lord that not even the opportunity of repenting is granted after the first; how can we contend from religion that whatever that crime may have been should be remitted to us? Which surely, when we wish it unpunished, we approve. And if it is agreed that the author no less than the approver is held guilty in all things that are sinned, it is certain that we are bound by fellowship in the fault, as often as we desire him to be unpunished who is held liable to the fault. Then, besides, there is added this, which is more grievous. For all sins seem more pardonable when he who is accused promises correction: but now, as our manners are, men both desire the punishment of their crime to be relaxed for them, and to possess that on account of which the crime was committed. For these too your priesthood thinks it should intervene, concerning whom there is so little hope of the future that even in the present the same reasoning of the crime persists. For he who so obstinately holds onto the things for which he committed the crime shows himself, where there is license, about to sin in like manner.

**3.** For these reasons, then, I consult your prudence, and I desire to be freed from this doubt under which I labor; nor think that you are consulted for any other cause. For the rest, this is my purpose: that I should even give thanks to intercessors, especially of such merit as you are. For very many things which I do not wish to seem to do of my own accord—lest a relaxation of severity should arm others for crimes—I wish to relax to good intercessors, so that what I willingly grant, the severity of judgment being saved, may seem to be granted by the merit of another. You had promised me some of the writings of your Holiness, and I have not received them; I ask that you send them even now, and reply to this letter of mine, so that, since it is not given me meanwhile to see your Holiness, I may at least be fed by your discourses. May the eternal Divinity keep your Holiness safe for a most ample age, my deservedly venerable and truly to be reverered father.
