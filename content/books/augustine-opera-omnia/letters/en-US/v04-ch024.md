# Letter 232

TO THE PRAISEWORTHY LORDS AND MOST BELOVED BROTHERS OF MADAUROS, WHOSE LETTER I RECEIVED THROUGH BROTHER FLORENTINUS, AUGUSTINE.

**1.** If perhaps those among you who are Catholic Christians sent me such writings, I wonder only at this, that they did so not rather in their own name than in the name of the council. But if in truth all, or nearly all, the men of the council deigned to send me a letter, I wonder that you wrote "to the father" and "greetings in the Lord"; whose superstitious worship of idols—against which idols your temples are more easily closed than your hearts, or rather which idols are shut up not so much in your temples as in your hearts—is most well known to me with great grief: unless perhaps you are now at last thinking, with prudent consideration, about that very salvation which is in the Lord, through whom you wished to greet me. For if it is not so, I ask you, how have I injured, how have I offended your goodwill, that you should think me to be mocked rather than honored by the title of your letter, praiseworthy lords and most beloved brothers?

**2.** For when I read what you wrote—"To father Augustine, eternal salvation in the Lord"—I was suddenly raised up by such great hope that I believed you either to be already converted to the Lord himself and to that eternal salvation, or to desire to be converted through our ministry. But when I read the rest, my mind grew cold: yet I asked the bearer of the letter whether you were already either Christians or desirous of being. After, by his answer, I learned that you were by no means changed, I grieved the more grievously that the name of Christ, to which you see the whole world now subjected, you believed was to be not only repelled by you, but even mocked in us. For I could not think of any other Lord, according to whom a bishop could be called "father" by you, except the Lord Christ: and if there were any doubt hence about the interpretation of your sentence, it would be removed by the subscription of the letter, where you openly set down: "We wish you, lord, in God and his Christ, ever to rejoice for many years in your clergy." All these things having been read through and discussed, what else could occur to me, or can occur to any man, except that these things were written with either a truthful or a deceitful mind of the writers? But if you write these things with a truthful mind, who has barred you the way to this truth? who has strewn rough thickets beneath? what enemy has set the crags of cliffs against you? finally, who has closed the door of the basilica to you desiring to enter, that in the same Lord through whom you greet us, you should be unwilling to have the same salvation with us? But if you write these things deceitfully and mockingly, do you then so lay upon me the care of your affairs that you dare to lift up the name of him through whom I can do anything, not with the veneration that is owed, but to fan it about with flattering insult?

**3.** Know, most dear ones, that I say these things with an ineffable trembling of heart for you: for I know how much more grievous and more pernicious a cause you will have before God, if I have said these things to you in vain. All the things that in past times our ancestors remembered to have been done concerning the human race, and handed down to us; all the things too that we see and hand down to posterity—which nevertheless pertain to the seeking and holding of the true religion—divine Scripture has not been silent about; but all things so pass away that they were foretold as about to pass away. You see surely the Jewish people torn from their seats, scattered and diffused through almost all lands: and the origin of that same people, and its increases, and the loss of its kingdom, and its dispersion through all places, as they were foretold, so were they done. You see surely the word of God and the law going forth from that very people through Christ, who was born from them wondrously, having occupied and held the faith of all nations: so we read all these things foretold as we see them. You see surely many cut off from the root of the Christian society—which through the Sees of the Apostles and the successions of bishops is diffused with a sure propagation throughout the world—glorying, from the mere figure of their origin, under the Christian name, like withering branches: which we call heresies and schisms; all things foreseen, foretold, written. You see surely the temples of idols partly collapsed without repair, partly demolished, partly closed, partly converted to other uses; and the idols themselves either broken, or burned, or shut up, or destroyed: and the very powers of this world, which once persecuted the Christian people for the idols, conquered and tamed, not by those resisting but by those dying, and turning their assaults and laws against the very idols for which they killed Christians, and the most eminent summit of the most noble empire, with its diadem laid aside, supplicating at the tomb of the fisherman Peter.

**4.** All these things the divine Scriptures, which have now come into the hands of all, testified would be, long ages before. All these things we rejoice come to pass with a faith the more robust the greater the authority with which we find them foretold in the holy Scriptures. Is it then, I beseech you, is it then only the judgment of God—which we read in those same Scriptures will be between the faithful and the unfaithful[^1]—that, when all those things have come as they were foretold, we shall suppose only the judgment of God is not to come? On the contrary, it will come, just as all those things have come. Nor will there be any man of our times who could in that judgment defend himself concerning his unbelief; since Christ is sung both by the just man toward equity, and by the perjurer toward fraud, and by the king toward rule, and by the soldier toward battle, and by the husband for governance, and by the wife for compliance, and by the father for command, and by the son for obedience, and by the master for dominion, and by the slave for service, and by the humble toward devotion, and by the proud toward emulation, and by the rich that he may give, and by the poor that he may receive, and by the drunkard toward the cup, and by the beggar toward the door, and by the good that he may bestow, and by the evil that he may deceive; both the Christian as a worshiper, and the pagan as a flatterer—all sing Christ, and with what will and mouth they sing, they will without doubt render an account to that very one whom they sing.

**5.** There is something invisible, from which, as from the Creator and beginning, are all the things we see—highest, eternal, unchangeable, and utterable to none but only to itself. There is something by which that very summit of majesty narrates and proclaims itself, the Word not unequal to the one begetting and narrating, by which he who begets the Word is shown. There is a certain holiness, the sanctifier of all things that are made holy, the inseparable and undivided communion of that unchangeable Word through which that beginning is narrated, and of that beginning itself which narrates itself by an equal Word. But who could behold, with a most serene and most sincere mind, this whole which I have tried to say by not saying, and by saying not to say; who could behold it, and by that beholding lead a blessedness, and, in a manner failing into that which it beholds, forget itself, and proceed into that whose vision is invisible to us—which is to be clothed with immortality, and to obtain the eternal salvation through which you deign to greet me? Who could do this, except one who, leveling all the empty couches of his pride, confessing his sins, has laid himself low, meek and humble, to receive God as teacher?

**6.** Since, therefore, we are first to be brought down from the vanity of pride to humility, so that, rising from there, we may hold a solid loftiness; this could not be breathed into us the more magnificently the more gently—so that our fierceness might be calmed not by force but by persuasion—unless that Word, through which God the Father makes himself known to the angels, which is his Power and Wisdom[^2], which could not be seen by the human heart blinded by desire of visible things, deigned to enact and show his person in a man, so that a man might fear more to be lifted up by the pride of man, than to be humbled by the example of God. And so it is not Christ adorned with an earthly kingdom, nor Christ rich with earthly resources, nor Christ resplendent with that earthly happiness, but Christ crucified, that is preached throughout the whole world[^3]. Which the peoples of the proud first laughed at, and the remnants still laugh: but first few believed, now peoples; because then, for the faith of the few and against the mockery of the peoples, when Christ crucified was preached[^4], the lame walked, the mute spoke, the deaf heard, the blind saw, the dead rose again. So at last earthly pride took notice that there is nothing in earthly things themselves more powerful than divine humility[^5], so that even the most wholesome human humility might be protected, against the pride that insults it, by the patronage of the divine imitation.

**7.** Awake at last, my brothers and my kinsmen of Madauros; God has offered me this occasion of writing to you. As far as I could indeed, in the business of brother Florentinus, through whom you sent the letter, as God willed, I was present and helped; but it was such a business as could easily be carried through even without my work: for nearly all the men of his own household who are at Hippo know Florentinus, and much grieve his bereavement. But the letter was sent to me by you, so that my letter might not be shameless, when, an occasion having been received from you, it should speak something about Christ to the worshipers of idols. But I beseech you, if you did not name him in vain in that letter, that I have not written these things to you in vain. But if you wished to mock me, fear him whom, first judged, the proud world mocked, and now, subjected, awaits as judge: for the affection of my heart toward you, expressed through this page as far as I could, will be a witness; it will be a witness to you in the judgment of him who will confirm those who believe in him, and confound the unbelieving. May the one and true God, freeing you from all the vanity of this world, convert you to himself, praiseworthy lords and most beloved brothers.

[^2]: See 1 Cor 1:24
[^3]: See 1 Cor 1:23
[^4]: See 1 Cor 1:23
