# Letter 94

TO THE HOLY ONE OF THE LORD, MOST BLESSED AND UNIQUELY LIKE-MINDED WITH US, AND VENERABLE FATHER, BROTHER, MASTER, BISHOP AUGUSTINE, PAULINUS AND THERASIA, SINNERS.

**1.** *Your word is always a lamp to my feet, and a light to my paths*[^1]. *So, as often as I receive the letters of your most blessed Holiness, I feel the darkness of my foolishness scattered, and, as if with the salve of clarification poured into the eyes of my mind, I see more purely, the night of ignorance driven off and the mist of doubt wiped away. Which, although I have often felt granted to me at other times through the gift of your letters, especially in this little book of recent letters, whose bearer was to me as welcome as he was worthy, the blessed man of the Lord, our brother Quintus the deacon: who indeed, a long while after he had come to the City, when I had come there with him, according to my solemn custom after the Lord's Pasch, for the veneration of the Apostles and martyrs, rendered to us the blessing of your mouth; yet, with the time blotted out which, without my knowing, he had spent at Rome, he seemed to me most recently come from your sight, so that I could scarcely then believe that he had come to me from you, when I first saw him, and when he offered me the full odor of your sweetness in your words, fragrant with the chastity of a heavenly ointment. Yet I confess to your venerable Like-mindedness that I could not read that volume at Rome, as soon as I had received it. For there were such crowds there that I could not diligently inspect your gift, and enjoy it as I desired; namely, so as to read it through continuously, if I had begun to read. And so, as is wont to happen in the secure expectation of a prepared banquet, I reined in the hunger of my eager mind, and with the sure hope of taking my fill, while I held in my hand the loaves of my desire in the volume to be devoured—which afterward, to me devouring it, was both in mouth and in belly most sweet—I easily suspended my gluttony, gaping after the honeycombs of your letters, until I should set out from the City, and devote wholly to this work that day which we were to interpose, at the stations of the journey, in the town of Formiae, so that in the spiritual delights of your letter I might feast, free from all dregs and from the suffocation of crowds.*

**2.** *What, then, shall I, humble and earthly, answer to this wisdom which has been given you from above, which this world does not grasp, and which no one knows, except the wisdom of God, wise, and eloquent by the Word of God? And so, because I have experience of Christ speaking in you, in God I will praise your words*[^2], and I will not fear from the terror by night[^3]. For you have taught me, in the spirit of truth, the wholesome tempering of moderating the mind amid the falling things of mortal life, by which you saw that blessed mother and grandmother Melania weep the carnal death of her only son, with a silent grief indeed, yet not dry of a mother's tears in her sorrow. Whose modest and grave tears, as you understood the more deeply, being nearer or more equal to her spirit, and, of a woman perfect in Christ, with the bravery of a manly mind preserved, you contemplated her maternal heart the better, from an equal state, out of the likeness of your own heart, so as to perceive that she wept, first moved by natural affection, then pricked by a weightier cause—not so much that human thing, that she had lost her only son, deceased by a mortal condition, in the present age, as that, anticipated, as it were, in worldly vanity (because the ambition of senatorial dignity had not yet deserted him), she did not think him taken up according to the holy avarice of her vows—so that he might have passed from the glory of his manner of life to the glory of the resurrection, about to take a rest common with his mother, and a crown, if in the life of this age he had, by his mother's example, preferred the sackcloth to the toga, and the monastery to the Senate.

**3.** *Nevertheless, that same man, as I think I have also previously related to your Holiness, departed enriched by these works, so that, the nobility of his mother's humility, if he did not wear it in garb, yet he displayed it in mind. For he was, according to the word of the Lord, so gentle in morals and humble in heart*[^4], that he is not undeservedly believed to have entered into the rest of the Lord. For there are remnants for the peaceful man[^5], and the meek shall possess the earth[^6], pleasing God in the region of the living[^7]. For surely he also fulfilled that word of the Apostle, not only by the silent affection of the mind, but also by conspicuous religious offices, so that, although he was a colleague of the high ones of this age in rank and honor, yet, not as one glorious of the earth, he was high-minded, but, as a perfect imitator of Christ, he consented to the humble[^8], and persisted all the day long in showing mercy and lending[^9]. Whence his offspring too became powerful in the earth, among those who, the strong gods of the earth, are exceedingly exalted[^10]; so that even from the blessed visitation of his family and house the man's holy merit may be revealed. The generation, *he says,* of the upright shall be blessed. Glory, *not perishable,* and riches, *not slipping away,* in his house[^11]: *a house which is built in the heavens, not by the labor of hands, but by the holiness of works. But I cease to relate more about the memory of a man as beloved by me as he was devoted to Christ, since I both recall having narrated not a few things about him in former letters, and I can say nothing better or holier about the blessed mother of this son, and the holy root equal to the branches, Melania, than your Holiness has deigned to speak and discuss about her; so that, because I, a sinner, having unclean lips, could say nothing worthy, being far from the merits of her faith and the virtues of her soul, you, that man of Christ, teacher of Israel in the Church of truth, by the grace of God procuring better things, might be made a worthier herald of so manly a soul in Christ, who would both behold her mind strengthened by divine virtue, as I said, with a nearer spirit, and praise her piety mixed with virtue in worthier eloquence.*

**4.** *But what the action of the blessed will be after the resurrection of the flesh in that age, you have deigned to ask me. But I consult you, as master and spiritual physician, about the present state of my life, that you may teach me to do the wills of God, and to walk in your footsteps after Christ, and first to die by that evangelical death, by which we anticipate the carnal dissolution by a voluntary departure; withdrawing from the life of this age not by death, but by resolve, which is wholly of temptations—or, as you once said to me, is wholly a temptation. Would, then, that my ways be so directed after your footsteps, that, by your example, loosing the old sandal from my feet, I may break my bonds, and, free, exult to run the way*[^12], by which I may be able to attain that death by which you died to this age, that you may live to God in Christ living in you, whose death and life are known in your body and heart and mouth! For your heart does not savor earthly things, nor does your mouth speak the works of men; but the word of Christ abounds in your breast[^13], and the spirit of truth is poured out on your tongue, with the rush of a heavenly river gladdening the city of God[^14].

**5.** *But what virtue effects this death in us, except love, which is strong as death*[^15]? For it so blots out and slays this age for us, that it fulfills the effect of death through the affection of Christ, to whom turned, we are turned away from this world, and to whom living, we die from the elements of this world[^16]. Nor do we, as living in their sight and use, make decisions, because our portion is the death of Christ: whose resurrection from the dead we do not lay hold of in glory, unless we imitate his death on the cross, with the members and senses of the flesh mortified; so that now we may live not by our own will, but by his, whose will is our sanctification[^17], and who therefore died for us and rose again, that now we may live not for ourselves, but for him[^18], who died for us and rose again, and gave us the pledge of his promise by his Spirit, just as he placed the pledge of our life in the heavens in his body, which is the head of our body. Whence now our expectation is the Lord and our substance[^19], which was made by him with him, and in him, and through him, who was conformed to the body of our humility, that he might conform us to the body of his glory[^20], and set us with him in the heavenly places. Therefore those too who shall have been worthy of eternal life will be in the glory of his kingdom, so that they may be with him[^21], as the Apostle says, and remain with him, as the Lord himself said to the Father: I will that where I am, they also may be with me[^22].

**6.** *Without doubt this is that which you have in the Psalms:* Blessed are those who dwell in your house; forever and ever they will praise you[^23]. *But I think that this praise is to be brought forth by the voices of those singing together: even though the bodies of the saints rising again will be changed, so that they may be as the Lord's body appeared after the resurrection; in which, surely, the living image of the human resurrection shone forth, so that the Lord himself, who in that very body in which he had suffered and risen, was, as it were, a mirror of contemplation to all. For surely, when he had risen in the same flesh in which he had died and been buried, he displayed all the functions of all his members, expressed to the eyes and ears of men, having often been together with them. But if even angels, whose creation is simply spiritual, are said to have tongues, with which surely they sing praises to the Lord the Creator and do not cease to give thanks, how much more will men—even if their bodies are now spiritual after the resurrection, yet with all the members of the glorified flesh remaining, and, throughout all the members, their forms and numbers—both have tongues in their mouths, and, with tongues uttering, give voices*[^24], by which they may bring forth through words the divine praises, or the affections of their senses and joys. Perhaps too the Lord will add this to the grace and glory of his saints, in the ages of his kingdom, that they may sing with tongues and voices so much the more excellent, the more they have advanced, by a blessed change, to a happier nature of bodies, so that, now established in bodies already spiritual, they may now speak perhaps not in human, but in those angelic and heavenly words, such as the Apostle heard in paradise[^25]. And therefore perhaps he testified that those words were unspeakable to man, because for the saints, among the other kinds of rewards, new tongues are already prepared. Which therefore the men of this age are not yet permitted to use, so that, now with these befitting their glory, the immortals may speak, of whom it was said, For they will cry out and will say a hymn[^26]; *without doubt in the heavenly places, where they will be with the Lord, and will be delighted in the abundance of peace*[^27], rejoicing in the sight of the throne, sending before the feet of the Lamb their bowls and crowns, and singing to him a new song[^28], joined to the choirs of Angels, Virtues, Dominations, Thrones, so that they too, with the Cherubim and Seraphim, and those four living creatures, singing together with a perpetual voice, may say, Holy, holy, holy, Lord God of hosts[^29], *and the rest which you know.*

**7.** *This, then, is what I, the needy and poor one, that foolish and little one of yours—whom, as a true wise man, you have been accustomed to bear—ask: that you teach me your knowledge or opinion about this (because I know that you are illuminated by the spirit of revelation from the very leader and fount of the wise, so that, as you have known things past, and see things present, so also you may estimate things to come); what you judge about these eternal voices of the heavenly creatures, or even of those acting above the heavens in the sight of the Most High—by what organs, after all, they are expressed. For although the Apostle, by saying,* If I speak with the tongues of Angels[^30], *showed that they have a certain speech proper to their nature, or, so to say, to their race, so much higher than human senses and eloquence, as the very creation and station of the Angels surpasses mortal dwellers and earthly seats: yet perhaps he said "the tongues of Angels" for kinds of voices and speech, just as, discoursing about the variety of charisms, he numbers among the gifts of graces "kinds of tongues"*[^31], surely signifying this to be in a sign, that it was granted to individuals to speak in the speech of many nations. But the voice of God too, often sent forth to the saints from a cloud, shows that speech can be without a tongue. For although the tongue is a member of the body, small, and great. But perhaps from this very thing, that God placed in this member the function of voice, he called also the speech and voices of the incorporeal creation of Angels a "tongue," just as the Scripture is wont to assign to God too, according to the kinds of operations, the names of members. Pray for us, and teach us.

**8.** *Our dearest and sweetest brother Quintus, as slowly as he returns to us from you, so quickly does he hasten to return from us to you: but his insistence in demanding letters this letter too speaks, fuller of erasures than of lines—the excessive haste of the said exactor has made it a hasty draft. For on the day before the ides of May he came to us to ask for a reply, and on the ides, before the sixth hour, he obtained leave to be dismissed. See, then, whether by a testimony of this kind I have commended him, or accused him. For perhaps—indeed, without doubt—he will be judged praiseworthy rather than blameworthy, who most justly hastened back from the darkness, which we are in comparison with your light, to his own light.*
