# Letter 19

Augustine to Gaius

**1.** Since we parted from you, it cannot be told with what great sweetness the memory of you has flooded over us, and often floods over us still. For we recall that, by the ardor of your inquiry, though it was marvelous, the modesty of your discussing was not disturbed. For I could not easily find anyone questioning more ardently or listening more tranquilly. And so I would like to talk much with you: for it would not be much, however much it were, if I were talking with you. But since this is difficult, what need is there to seek the reasons? It is downright difficult: it will perhaps at some time be very easy; God willing it so: now at least it is otherwise. I have therefore given a charge to the brother through whom I send this letter, to offer all our writings to be read by your most prudent Charity. For he will not force anything of mine upon you unwilling; for I know what kindliness you bear toward us in your mind. Yet if you should read and approve them, and see through to their truth, do not think that they are ours, except in that they were given; and you may turn for this to that source from which it was given to you too to approve them. For no one perceives that what he reads is true in the book itself, or in the one who wrote it, but rather in himself, if upon his mind there has been impressed a light of truth not commonly bright and most far removed from the dregs of the body. But if you should find some things false and to be disapproved, know that they have been bedewed from the human cloud, and reckon those to be truly ours. I would urge you to inquire, did I not seem to myself to see certain open mouths of your heart; I would urge you also to hold manfully to the truth you have come to know, did you not display before you the most evident strength of your mind and counsel. For in a short time, with the coverings of the body almost cast off, all that lives in you opened itself to me. And in no way may the most merciful providence of our Lord allow that you, so good a man and outstandingly wise of heart, be a stranger to the catholic flock of Christ.
