# Letter 42

To the praiseworthy lords, most holy in Christ, brothers Paulinus and Therasia, Augustine sends greeting in the Lord

**1.** Could this too be hoped for or expected, that through brother Severus we should demand a reply, not given back to us for so long, ardent as we are, by your Charity? What is it that we are compelled to thirst for two summers, and those in Africa? What more shall I say? O you who daily give away your own goods, render the debt. Or have you perhaps, because I had heard that you were writing against the demon-worshippers, and had shown that I vehemently desired that work, wishing to finish and send it, put off your letters to us for so long a time? Would that at least with so rich a table you would now receive my year-long fast from your pen: which, if it is not yet prepared, we will not cease to complain, if you do not, while you are finishing that, meanwhile refresh us. Greet the brothers, especially Romanus and Agilis. Those who are with us here greet you, and are a little angry with us, if they love a little.
