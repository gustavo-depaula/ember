# Letter 131

To the distinguished lady, and deservedly illustrious, and most excellent daughter Proba, Augustine sends greetings in the Lord.

**1.** It is indeed so, as you say, that the soul, set in a corruptible body, is constrained by a certain earthly contagion, and in a manner bowed, pressed down by such a load, that it more easily covets and thinks of many things in the depths than the one thing in the heights. For holy Scripture too says this thus: *For the body that is corrupted weighs down the soul, and the earthly habitation presses down the sense thinking of many things*[^1]. But for this reason our Savior came, who, by the word of salvation, raised up that woman in the Gospel, bent for eighteen years—who perhaps signified this—so that the Christian soul might not in vain hear, "Hearts on high," nor in vain answer that it has them to the Lord. Considering which, you do rightly to count the bearable evils of this world tolerable by the hope of the future. For thus, by a certain good use, they are turned to good, while they do not increase our concupiscence, but exercise our patience; about which the Apostle says, *We know that for those who love God all things work together unto good*[^2]. "All things," he says: not only, then, those which are sought as sweet, but even those which are avoided as troublesome; when we so take the one that we be not captured, so bear the other that we be not broken, and, according to the divine precepts, in all things give thanks to him, of whom we say, *I will bless the Lord at all times; always his praise is in my mouth*[^3]; and, *It is good for me that you have humbled me, that I may learn your justifications*[^4]. For in truth, if the tranquility of a deceptive prosperity were always to smile here, the human soul would not seek that harbor of true and certain security, distinguished lady, and deservedly illustrious, and most excellent daughter. Rendering, then, to your Eminence the due office of greeting, and giving thanks that you bear a most religious care for our salvation, I ask for you from the Lord the rewards of the future life, and the solaces of the present; and to the love and prayer of all of you, in whose hearts Christ dwells through faith, I commend myself. And in another hand: May the true and truthful God truly console your heart, and protect your safety, distinguished lady, and deservedly illustrious, and most excellent daughter.

[^1]: Wis 9:15
[^2]: Rom 8:28
[^3]: Ps 34:1 (33:2)
[^4]: Ps 119:71 (118:71)
