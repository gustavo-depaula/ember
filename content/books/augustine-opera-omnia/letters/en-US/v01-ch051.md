# Letter 51

[Augustine to Crispinus] missing in the manuscripts

**1.** Because your people reproach our lowliness, therefore I have so prefaced the letter; for I would seem to have done it to your insult, if I did not so await from you that you write back. About the Carthaginian promise—whether your or our insistence—why should I recall much? However we conducted those things, let them pass, lest they hinder what remains. Now there is no excuse, unless I am mistaken, the Lord aiding; we are both in Numidia, and we are near to one another in respect of place. Rumor has brought to me that you still wish to try, by disputing with me, the question which divides our communion. See how briefly all circuitous evasions are taken away: answer this letter, if it please, and perhaps it will suffice, not only for us, but also for those who desire to hear us; or if writings and replies do not suffice, until they suffice, they shall be repeated. For what can the so great nearness of the cities we inhabit afford us more conveniently? For I have resolved to deal with you about this matter in no way except by letters, either lest what is said slip from the memory of any of us, or lest the zealous for such things, who perhaps cannot be present, be defrauded. You are wont to boast false things about past deeds, what you will—perhaps not from zeal for lying, but from error. Accordingly, if it please, let us measure those by present things. Without doubt it does not escape you that in the times of the former people both the sacrilege of idolatry was committed[^1], and a prophetic book was burned by a contemptuous king[^2]; by neither of which crimes would the evil of schism be punished more atrociously, unless it were weighed more gravely. For surely you recall how the gaping earth swallowed the authors of schism alive, and the fire rushing from heaven consumed those who had consented[^3]. So neither did the idol fashioned and adored, nor the sacred Book burned, deserve to be avenged.

**2.** Why, then, you who are wont to object to us crimes not only not proved in our people, but rather proved in your own, of those who, driven by the dread of persecution, handed over the books of the Lord to be burned with fire—why did you receive into the same episcopate in which you condemned them those whom, for the crime of schism, you condemned, as is there written, with the *truth-telling* mouth of your *plenary council*? I mean Felicianus of Musti and Praetextatus of Assuras. For these were not, as you say to those who do not know, of that number to whom your council had prolonged and fixed a day, within which, unless they returned to your communion, they would be held by the same sentence; but they were of that number whom you condemned without delay on the very day on which you gave a delay to the others. I will prove it, if you deny it: your council speaks; we have the proconsular Records in hand, by which you alleged it not once. Prepare, then, another defense, if you can, lest, while you deny what I will prove, we make delays. If, then, Felicianus and Praetextatus were innocent, why were they so condemned? If wicked, why were they so received? If you prove them innocent, why should we not believe that by far fewer of your elders, by the false crime of betrayal, innocent men could have been condemned, if by three hundred and ten of their successors—where it is even written, as a great thing, "with the truth-telling mouth of a plenary council"—innocent men could be condemned with the false crime of schism? But if you prove that they were rightly condemned, what defense remains why they were received into the same episcopate, except that, exaggerating the usefulness and wholesomeness of peace, you show that these things too are to be tolerated for the bond of unity? Which would that you did with the strength not of the mouth but of the heart! you would surely perceive how, by no calumnies, was the peace of Christ to be violated throughout the world of lands, if it is permitted in Africa for those condemned even in sacrilegious schism to be received into the same episcopate for the peace of Donatus.

**3.** Likewise you are wont to object to us that we persecute you through earthly powers. In which matter I do not dispute either what you deserve for the monstrousness of so great a sacrilege, or how much Christian gentleness tempers us; this I say: if this is a crime, why did you grievously assail those same Maximianists through judges sent by those emperors whom our communion begot through the Gospel, and disturb them out of the basilicas which they held, in which the very split found them, both by the din of controversies and by the power of orders and by the onset of auxiliaries? In which conflict what they suffered in the several places, the recent traces of the matter attest; what was ordered, the papers indicate; what was done, the lands cry out, in which also the holy memory of that Optatus, your tribune, is proclaimed.

**4.** Likewise you are wont to say that we do not have the baptism of Christ, and that, apart from your communion, it is nowhere. I could discourse on this somewhat more copiously. But against you there is now no need, who, with Felicianus and Praetextatus, have received even the baptism of the Maximianists. For as many as they baptized when they were in communion with Maximian—when you were even trying to expel those very men by name, that is, Felicianus and Praetextatus, from their basilicas, as the records testify, by a long conflict of judgments—as many, then, as they baptized at that time, they now have with themselves and with you, baptized, not only through the perils of sicknesses, but also through the paschal solemnities in so many churches belonging to their cities, and in those very great cities, outside, in the crime of schism, none of whom had baptism repeated. And would that you could prove that those whom Felicianus and Praetextatus had baptized outside, in the crime of schism, as it were emptily, were, when those men were received within, again, as it were usefully, baptized. For if these were to be baptized again, those were to be ordained again. For they had lost the episcopate when they withdrew from you, if they could not baptize outside your communion. For if, withdrawing, they had not lost the episcopate, they surely could baptize. But if they had lost it, then, that what they had lost might be restored to them, they ought, returning, to be ordained. But do not fear: just as it is certain that they returned with the same episcopate with which they had gone out, so it is certain that all whom they baptized in the schism of Maximian they reconciled to your communion with themselves without any repetition of baptism.

**5.** With what tears, then, are we sufficient to bewail that the baptism of the Maximianists is received, and the baptism of the world of lands is blown upon with contempt? Whether heard or unheard, whether justly or unjustly, you condemned Felicianus, you condemned Praetextatus; tell me: which bishop of the Corinthians did any of you hear, or condemn? which of the Galatians, which of the Ephesians, which of the Colossians, the Philippians, the Thessalonians, and of all the other cities, of which it was said: *All the families of the nations will adore in his sight*[^4]? Therefore the baptism of these is accepted, and that of those is blown upon; which is neither of these nor of those, but of him of whom it was said: *This is he who baptizes*[^5]. But I do not deal from this; attend to those things which are at hand, behold those things which strike even blind eyes: the condemned have baptism; and the unheard do not have it! Those expressly named and cast out in the crime of schism have it; and the unknown, far foreign, never accused, never judged, do not have it! Those who, from a cut-off part of Africa, were cut off again, have it; and those whence the Gospel itself came to Africa do not have it! Why do I burden you with more? Answer to these things. Attend to the sacrilege of schism exaggerated by your council against the Maximianists; attend to the persecutions through judiciary powers which you inflicted on them; attend to their baptism, which, with those whom you condemned, you received; and answer, if you can, whether you have anything by which you may cast fogs upon the unskilled, why you should be separated from the world of lands by a far greater crime of schism than that which you boast you condemned in the Maximianists. May the peace of Christ conquer in your heart.

[^1]: See Exod 32
[^2]: See Jer 36:23
[^3]: See Num 16:31–35
[^4]: Ps 22:27 (21:28)
[^5]: John 1:33
