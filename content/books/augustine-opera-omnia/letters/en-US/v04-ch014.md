# Letter 222

TO QUODVULTDEUS, MY MOST BELOVED SON AND FELLOW DEACON, AUGUSTINE, A BISHOP.

**1.** Having received the letter of your Charity, in which you asked me with most ardent zeal to write something briefly about all the heresies which, since the coming of the Lord Savior, have sprung up against his teaching, I wrote back at once, an opportunity having been found, how difficult this is, through my son Philocalus, a leading man of Hippo; but soon this occasion too was offered, by which I now write back and briefly show the difficulty of its accomplishment.

**2.** A certain Philastrius, bishop of Brescia, whom I myself also saw at Milan with holy Ambrose, wrote a book on this subject; not omitting even those heresies which were among the Jewish people before the coming of the Lord, of which he recounted twenty-eight, and after the coming of the Lord, one hundred and twenty-eight. On this subject Epiphanius too, the bishop of Cyprus, laudably renowned in the doctrine of the Catholic faith, wrote in Greek: but he, gathering the heresies of both periods, included eighty. Since, then, both wished to do what you ask of me, you see how much they differ between themselves about the number of sects: which surely would not have happened, unless one thing seemed a heresy to one of them and another thing to the other. For it is not to be thought that Epiphanius was ignorant of any that Philastrius knew, since we have found Epiphanius far more learned than Philastrius; so that we ought rather to say that very many escaped Philastrius, if the former included so many more and the latter fewer. But without doubt, in that question where it is disputed what a heresy is, the same thing did not seem so to both; and indeed it is altogether difficult to define this: and therefore care must be taken, when we try to reduce them all to a number, lest we omit some, although they are heresies, or count some, when they are not heresies. See, then, whether perhaps I ought to send you the book of holy Epiphanius: for I think he spoke more learnedly on this subject than Philastrius; which book could be more easily and conveniently translated into the Latin language at Carthage, so that you may rather supply us with what you ask of us.

**3.** I greatly commend the bearer. He is a subdeacon of our diocese; from the estate, moreover, of the honorable man, most dear to us, Orontius. For him, and for his father, by whom he was adopted, I have also written to that man himself: which letter, when your Christian Kindness has read it, I ask that you deign to support it before the aforesaid man by your intercession. I have also sent with him a man of the Church, lest access to Your Holiness be difficult for him: for I am not a little anxious about him; from which anxiety the Lord, I hope, will free me through the earnestness of your love. I ask too that you not find it burdensome to write back to me how that Theodosius is in the Catholic faith, through whom some Manichees were betrayed; and how those very ones whom we suppose to have been betrayed by him are corrected. If you have perhaps heard anything about the departure of the holy bishops, let me know. May you live for God.
