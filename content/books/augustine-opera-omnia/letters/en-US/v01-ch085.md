# Letter 85

TO THE LORD SINCERELY MOST BELOVED, AND TO BE BLESSED WITH ALL VOWS, BROTHER AND FELLOW-PRIEST PAUL, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** You would not call me so inexorable, unless you also thought me a liar. For what else do you believe about my mind, when you write such things to me, except that I hold against you a blemish of discord and a detestable hatred—as though, in a manifest matter, I did not take care, lest, preaching to others, I myself be found reprobate[^1]; or as though I wished so to cast the straw out of your eye that I nourish a beam in my own[^2]? It is not what you think. Behold, again I say and call God to witness, that if you wished for yourself what I wish for you, you would long since live secure in Christ, and in the glory of his name would gladden his whole Church. Behold, I have now written that you are not only my brother, but also my colleague. For it cannot be that any bishop whatever of the Catholic Church—of whatever sort he may be, condemned by no ecclesiastical judgment—should not be my colleague. But that I should not commune with you, there is no cause, except that I cannot flatter you. For I owe most to you, because in Christ Jesus through the Gospel I begot you[^3], by truthfully rebuking with the wholesome bite of love. Nor do I so rejoice that many, with the Lord helping, have been gathered into the Catholic Church through you, that I ought not to lament that more are scattered thence. For you have so wounded the Church of Hippo that, unless the Lord recall you—freed of all secular cares and burdens—to a true episcopal life and sustenance, such a wound cannot be healed.

**2.** But since you cease not to entangle yourself more and more, so that you have inserted yourself, even after your renunciation, into the very things you renounced—which cannot in any way be defended even before human laws themselves—and you are said to live in that profession for which the frugality of your Church cannot suffice: why do you seek my communion, when you have never wished to hear my admonition? Is it so that men, whose complaints I cannot endure, may impute to me whatever you do? But in vain do you suspect that those are your detractors who have always been opposed to you, even in your former life. It is not so; nor is it a wonder that many things are hidden from you. But even if this were true, they ought to have found nothing in your morals which they could rightly reprove, and from which they might blaspheme the Church. Perhaps you still think I say these things because I have not accepted your satisfaction. On the contrary, I say them for this reason, lest I myself be unable to make sufficient satisfaction to God for my sins, if I keep silent about these things to you. I know that you have a heart; but even a slow heart is secure when it is in heaven; and a sharp heart is nothing when it is on the earth. The episcopate is not a craft for transacting a deceitful life. The Lord God will teach you what I say—he who has shut off for you all the ways which you wished to use for that end, so that he may direct you, if you understand, into that way for the walking of which so holy a burden was imposed on you.

[^1]: See 1 Cor 9:27
[^2]: See Matt 7:3-5
[^3]: See 1 Cor 4:15
