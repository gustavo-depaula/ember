# Letter 18\*

AUGUSTINE, BISHOP, TO THE CHURCH THAT IS AT MEMBLIBANUM, GREETINGS IN THE LORD

**1.** It quickly became clear how right I was in what I was telling you who were with me at Unapompeia—that you ought not to ask for the priest of Gitta, about whose life I was very anxious; but God was bringing this about through you, though you did not know it, so that, by your occasion, he might also be removed from the very clergy of the Church that is at Unapompeia, where he was a deacon, since, as he has been exposed, he ought not even to have been a deacon. For if there is any truth in what the woman said about him, then it is settled; but if she also perjured herself and the only truth about him is what he himself confessed, even so he cannot be a cleric, since all Christians—how much more clerics—ought to be pure not only from unlawful intercourse but also from an unlawful kiss and an unlawful embrace and from all uncleanness.

**2.** In these matters, then, since he has not been found clean even by his own confession, we have had to remove him from the rank of the priesthood. May the Lord console you, for we are very sad on account of your sadness; may his mercy console you all—the mercy of him who gathered you together and snatched you from the dominion of the devil, since he does not abandon you, if you also do not abandon him. Look, then, for someone to be ordained your priest, or, if you do not find one, we will provide in the name of the Lord and with his help.

**3.** For are people therefore slow to sow, because not all the seeds come up, but ants drag off some, birds gather others, and others perish by various mishaps? Or are they therefore slow to graft fruit-bearing trees, because not all of them take or come to fruit, but the shoots of some dry up, cattle gnaw at others, and still others die from various causes? So too in the Church, as in the field of so great a father of the household, we do not cease to labor; it is God's to give the increase, since, even if not all make progress and persevere to the end, nevertheless, as the Apostle says, *The Lord knows those who are his*[^1]—he who foretold all these scandals that grieve us as things to come, and warned us not to lose heart, and promised a reward to us who persevere with his help, that we may live there forever with him, where such temptations and such scandals can have no place, since there will be no sadness there, but only joy and the sure security of everlasting joys and blessed immortality and happiness without end. In him, then, place your hope, my sons and brothers, and let not your love grow cold, that you may be found approved on the last day, on account of him for whom you became Christians by the grace of the one who redeemed you with his blood. I have sent the priest Restitutus to you with this letter, whom I have scarcely consoled, troubled as he is by a greater sadness; through him may the Lord console you also, just as he has consoled him through me.

[^1]: 2 Tim 2:19
