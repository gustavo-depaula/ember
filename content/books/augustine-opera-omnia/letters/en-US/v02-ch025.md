# Letter 124

To the lords distinguished in the Lord, and in holiness most dear and most longed-for brothers, Albina, Pinian, and Melania, Augustine sends greetings in the Lord.

**1.** Although by the condition of my health, or by nature, I cannot bear cold, yet I have never been able to endure greater heats than in this so horrible winter, because to you—to whom seas were to be crossed by flight—set so near, coming from so far away for the sake of visiting us, I could not, I will not say go, but fly. And perhaps your Holiness will think the same wintry harshness to be the only cause of my penalty; far be it, dearest ones. For what grave and troublesome thing, or even dangerous, do these rains have, that I should not have undergone and borne it, to come to you—so great consolations amid our so great evils, in this tortuous and perverse generation, lights so ardently kindled from the highest Light, sublime by accepted humility, more illustrious by despised brightness? At the same time too I would enjoy the so spiritual happiness of my carnal fatherland, which has deserved to have even you present: about whom, absent, when it heard what you were born and what you were made by the grace of Christ, although it believed by love, yet, lest it not be believed, perhaps feared to narrate it.

**2.** I shall say, then, why I have not come, and by what evils I have been hindered from so great a good; so that not only from you I may obtain pardon, but even by your prayers may deserve mercy from him who works in you that for which you live to him. The people of Hippo, to whom the Lord gave me as a servant, since in great and almost in every part they are so weak that by the pressure even of a lighter tribulation they can grievously sicken, are now beaten by so great a tribulation that, even if they were not so weak, they could scarcely bear it with any soundness of mind. And just now, when I returned, I found them most dangerously scandalized about my absence: but to your people, of whose spiritual strength we rejoice in the Lord, with sound throats surely it tastes how it was said, *Who is weak, and I am not weak? who is scandalized, and I am not on fire?*[^1] Especially since there are many here who, by detracting from us, try to disturb against us the minds of others by whom we seem to be loved, that they may make in them a place for the devil. But when they grow angry at us, about whose salvation we are busy, their great counsel of avenging is a lust of dying—not in body, but in heart, where the corpse is secretly felt by its own stench before it is foreseen by our thought. To this solicitude of mine you without doubt gladly grant pardon; especially since, if you were angry and wished to avenge yourselves, you would perhaps find nothing graver than that which I suffer, when I do not see you at Thagaste. But I hope, aided by your prayers, that it will be granted me to come to you, wherever in Africa you are, as soon as possible, when this by which I am now detained has passed; if this city in which we labor is not worthy—because I do not dare to think it worthy—to rejoice together with us over your presence.

[^1]: 2 Cor 11:29
