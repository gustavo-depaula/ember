# Letter 59

To the most blessed and venerable lord, father and fellow priest Victorinus, Augustine sends greeting in the Lord

**1.** The circular letter came to me on the fifth day before the Ides of November, the day being already ended, and found me very much indisposed, so that I could not come at all. Nevertheless, whether it has moved my inexperience, or whether I am justly moved, it is for your Holiness and Gravity to judge. I read in the same circular letter that it was written also to the Mauretanias, which provinces we know have their own primates. But if from them too a council was to be convoked to Numidia, it surely behooved that the names of some of the Moorish bishops, who there are the senior ones, be set in the circular letter; which, not finding in this circular letter, I greatly wondered. Then, to the Numidians themselves it was written with the order so disturbed and neglected, that I found my name in the third place, who know how long after many bishops I was made. Which thing is both injurious enough to others, and invidious to me. Besides, our venerable brother and colleague, Xantippus of Tagosa, says that since the primacy itself befalls him, he is both so regarded by very many, and sends such letters. Which error too, even if it can easily be known and corrected among your Holiness, yet his name ought not to have been passed over in the circular letter which your Venerability sent. But if it were written in the middle places, and not set in the first, I would greatly wonder; how much more is it to be wondered at that no mention was made to you of him, who most of all ought to come to the council, that concerning the very order of the primacy, before all the bishops of the Churches of the Numidians, the primacy might be dealt with?

**2.** For these causes I would even hesitate to come, lest perhaps the circular letter be false, by which so great a perversity appears; although both the strait of time and other grave necessities hinder me in manifold ways. Whence I ask your Blessedness to pardon me, and first to deign to insist that between your Holiness and old Xantippus it be settled concordantly which of you ought to convoke the council: or surely—which I judge more wholesome—without anyone's prejudice, do you both convoke our colleagues, especially those who are near to you in age of episcopate, who may easily recognize which of you speaks the truth, so that among you few that same question may be settled before the rest, and, the error removed, the lesser ones may be convoked by the rest, who can neither nor ought, except to believe you in this matter as the senior ones, and now do not know which of you they should especially believe. This letter I have sent sealed with the ring which depicts the face of a man looking to the side.
