# Letter 19\*

TO THE LORD, DESERVEDLY TO BE HONORED IN THE HEART OF CHRIST, THE HOLY BROTHER AND FELLOW PRIEST JEROME, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD

**1.** I have received, through our son and fellow citizen, the deacon Palatinus, a letter of your Holiness together with another letter that you deigned to send through the holy bishop Lazarus. But I had already received earlier, through our son the priest Orosius—from whom I learned much—and a few days ago another letter of yours sent through the priest Innocentius; through him I had already written back not only to your Love but also to others whose letters he delivered to me, and to certain people who had written nothing to me through him, asking him to carry to your Holiness the necessary copies of my letters that I gave him at the same time. In these I did not pass over in silence what I thought should be said about the wicked error of men, by which the Church is no small disturbed.

**2.** I have indeed heard that your books against the same plague, recently published, have already reached as far as the imperial court, and that, as they become known, the crowd of the perverse who were brazenly building up such doctrines has now begun to be much diminished, since even Pelagius himself does not dare openly to defend such things, but rather himself—claiming that he does not hold them. For he has seen to it that a recent, brief defense of his should reach even me, in which, as he writes against the objections of the Gauls, he denies that much of it, and that grave, is his, while with hidden cunning he twists the meaning of certain points. It is therefore no small thing, with the help of God's mercy, that the things we feared the weak might believe he himself no longer dares to defend with open face. Hence I would not despair even of him—since he is a man—that he may someday, with truthful repentance, confess that he had been involved in impious error, O lord, deservedly to be honored in the heart of Christ, holy brother.

**3.** Now, therefore, having found the opportunity of Lucas, a servant of God, the bearer—whom the deacon Palatinus made known to me as very well acquainted with him, and promised that he would return to us as quickly as possible, and gave me his pledge for him that I should not hesitate to entrust to him whatever letters were to be carried—I have sent through him the book of this same Pelagius, which the servants of God Timasius and Jacobus, whom the Lord freed from that error through my little effort, gave me. They were his hearers and very dear to him. I have also sent the book in which I replied to him (for they had earnestly asked this of me, and I had foreseen that it would be useful and wholesome for them); I addressed it, to be sure, to them, not to Pelagius, while nonetheless replying to his work and words, with his name still left unspoken, since I desired him to be corrected as a friend—which I confess I still desire, and which I have no doubt your Holiness also wishes.

**4.** Finally, I have now also written to the man himself something that, unless I am mistaken, he will take bitterly, but which may perhaps afterward profit him unto salvation. I have also written a lengthy letter about him to the bishops Eulogius and John, and briefly to the holy priest Passerio; and I gave these in charge in such a way that everything should be brought to your Sincerity. And whatever opportunity next presents itself, I will see to it, with the Lord's help, that copies of all those same letters, signed in my own hand—which I trust is very well known to you—are sent to your Brotherhood, so that you may know and write back to me whether all of them, and indeed whole and genuine, have reached you.
