# Letter 128

To the honorable and most beloved son, the most illustrious and respectable man, the tribune and notary Marcellinus, Aurelius, Silvanus, and all the Catholic bishops.

**1.** By this letter we intimate that we consent in all things—as you deigned to admonish—to the edict of your Respectability, by which provision was made for preserving the tranquility and quiet of our conference, and for manifesting and fortifying the truth: that is, about the place and time of the conference itself, and about the number of those who must be present. We consent too that those to whom we delegate the office of conferring subscribe to their proceedings; and in that writing by which we impose this duty on them, and promise that we will hold ratified what they do, you may have the subscriptions of all of us not only made, but even, when they are made, perceived by yourself; we being also about to admonish, with the Lord helping, the Christian people, that, for the sake of quiet and tranquility, they altogether abstain their own assembly from the place of the conference, and not hasten to hear the things that are done while they are done, but wait to learn them written down, as you have promised to bring them forth to all.

**2.** By this bond too, trusting in the truth, we bind ourselves under this condition: that if those with whom we deal can demonstrate to us that, when, according to God's promises, the Christian peoples, growing everywhere, were already filling a great part of the world, and were being spread into the rest to be filled, the Church of Christ suddenly perished by the contagion of the sins of I know not whom, whom these men accuse, and remained in the party of Donatus alone—if this, as has been said, they can demonstrate, we will seek among them no honors of the episcopal office, but will follow, for our eternal salvation alone, the counsel of those to whom we shall owe the grace of so great a benefit for the truth known. But if we rather are able to show that the Church of Christ—now holding the most fertile spaces of all the provinces, not only African but also overseas, and of many nations, with an abundance of peoples, and, as it is written, bearing fruit and growing in the whole world—could not perish by the sins of any men mixed in with it; if, finally, we shall have shown that the question of those very men whom they then wished rather to accuse than were able to convict has been ended (although the cause of the Church does not rest in them); and that Caecilian was judged innocent, but they violent and calumnious, by that emperor to whose examination they sent their accusations, accusing of their own accord: finally, if whatever they say about the sins of any men, we shall have proved, either by human documents or by divine ones, either that their innocence was assailed by false crimes, or that the Church of Christ, to whose communion we cleave, was destroyed by none of their offenses; then let them hold its unity with us in such a way that they not only find the way of salvation, but do not even lose the honor of the episcopate. For in them we do not detest the Sacraments of divine truth, but the inventions of human error: which being taken away, we embrace a brotherly breast, joined to us by Christian love, which we now grieve to be separated by a diabolical dissension.

**3.** For each of us will be able, an associate in honor being coupled to him, to sit in turn more eminently, as a colleague sits beside a visiting bishop. When this is granted on both sides in alternate basilicas, each is forestalled by the other in mutual honor; because where the precept of love has widened the hearts, the possession of peace does not become narrow, so that, one of them being dead, thereafter individuals may succeed individuals in the former manner: nor will anything new be done; for this, from the very beginning of the separation, Catholic love has kept in those who, condemning the error of wicked schism, tasted, even late, the sweetness of unity. Or if perhaps the Christian peoples are delighted with individual bishops, and cannot bear the partnership of two, by an unusual face of things, let both of us withdraw from the midst, and, the cause of schism being condemned in the individual churches, and they being established in peaceful unity, let individual bishops be established through the necessary places by those who shall be found, individuals in individual churches, unity being made. For why should we hesitate to offer our Redeemer the sacrifice of this humility? Or did he descend from heaven into human members, that we might be his members; and do we dread to descend from our chairs, lest his very members be torn by cruel division? For ourselves nothing is more sufficient than that we are faithful and obedient Christians: this, then, let us always be. But we are ordained bishops for the sake of the Christian peoples: what, then, profits the Christian peoples for Christian peace, let us do this with our episcopate. If we are useful servants, why do we envy the eternal gains of the Lord for our own temporal sublimities? The episcopal dignity will be more fruitful to us if, laid down, it gathers the flock of Christ rather than, retained, scatters it. For with what countenance shall we hope in the age to come for the honor promised by Christ, if our honor in this age hinders Christian unity?

**4.** These things we have taken care to write to your Eminence for this reason, that through you they may become known to all. We request that, in the help of the Lord our God, by whose admonition we promise these things, and by whose help we trust that we can fulfill them, even before the conference, if it can be, devout love may either heal or tame the hearts of men, whether weak or hard; and so, with now peaceable minds, may we not resist the most manifest truth, and either precede our disputation with concord, or follow it. For we ought not to despair, if they recall that *blessed are the peacemakers, for they will be called sons of God*[^1], that they would, much more worthily and easily, wish that the party of Donatus be reconciled to the whole Christian world, than that the whole Christian world be rebaptized by the party of Donatus: especially since, with men coming from the sacrilegious and condemned schism of Maximian—whom they took care to correct even by persecuting them with the commands of earthly powers—they sought with so great love that they neither dared to rescind the baptism given by them, and received certain of them, condemned, without any diminution of their honor, but judged certain ones to have been unpolluted in the fellowship of that division. Of whose concord among themselves we are not envious; but they surely ought to notice how devoutly, with so great zeal, the Catholic root seeks the branch broken from itself, if the branch itself similarly labored so to gather the small fragment cut from itself. (And in another hand): We wish you, son, well in the Lord. I, Aurelius, bishop of the Catholic church of Carthage, have subscribed to this letter. (Likewise in another hand): I, old Silvanus of the church of Summa, have subscribed.

[^1]: Matt 5:9
