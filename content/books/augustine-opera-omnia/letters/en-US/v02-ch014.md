# Letter 113

To the most beloved lord, and deservedly honorable and worthy to be received brother Cresconius, Augustine sends greetings in the Lord.

**1.** If I should dissemble about this cause, concerning which, behold, I again write to your religion, not only Your Eminence, but also the man himself, whoever he is, in whose cause Faventius has been thus carried off, will deservedly blame me and rightly reprehend me: judging surely that, if he himself too had fled for refuge to the help of the Church, if something similar had befallen him, I would have so dissembled about his necessity and tribulation. Then, if the esteem of men is to be despised, what shall I say to the Lord our God himself, and what account shall I render, if I do not act, as far as I can, for the safety of him who committed himself to be protected and helped by the Church which I serve, most beloved lord and venerable son? I ask, therefore, your Benignity—since it is difficult and incredible that you do not now either know or are able to know in what cause he is detained—meanwhile to deign to aid my petition with the apparitor who holds him, that he do what is prescribed by the Emperor's law: that he have him questioned before the municipal records, whether he wishes thirty days to be granted him, in which he may act under moderate custody in that city in which he is detained, so as to set his affairs in order and provide for his expenses. In the space of which days, with your benevolence granting it to us, if we are able to end his cause by friendly negotiation, we shall be glad: but if we are not able, the outcome of the trials which has pleased God will find him, according to the merit of the cause itself or the will of the most omnipotent Lord.
