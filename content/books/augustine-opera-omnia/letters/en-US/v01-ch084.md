# Letter 84

TO THE MOST BLESSED LORD, AND VENERABLE AND DESIRABLE BROTHER AND FELLOW-PRIEST NOVATUS, AND TO THE BROTHERS WHO ARE WITH YOU, AUGUSTINE AND THE BROTHERS WHO ARE WITH ME SEND GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1.** I too feel how hard I may seem, and I can scarcely bear myself, in that I do not send and grant to your Holiness my son the deacon Lucillus, your brother. But when you yourself also begin to grant from yourself some, very dear and very sweet from your own nurturing, to the needs of Churches set far away, then you will feel by what goads of longing I am pierced, that certain men joined to me in the greatest and sweetest familiarity are not also bodily with me. For—to send your thought far—however much the brotherhood of your blood may avail, it does not overcome the bond of friendship by which I and the brother Severus cleave to each other in turn; and yet you know how rarely it happens to me to see him. And this was done surely not by my will or his; but while we set the needs of mother Church—on account of the age to come, in which we shall live together with one another inseparably—before the needs of our own time. How much more fairly, then, ought you to tolerate, for the usefulness of mother Church herself, the absence of that brother of yours, with whom you do not so long ruminate the Lord's food as I do with my sweetest fellow-citizen Severus, who nevertheless now scarcely, and only occasionally, speaks with me through scant little papers—and those indeed more filled with other cares and businesses than carrying anything of our meadows in the sweetness of Christ?

**2.** Here perhaps you will say: What then? Will my brother not be useful to the Church among us also, or do I desire to have him with me for some other reason? Plainly, if his presence seemed to you as useful for gaining or ruling the Lord's sheep as it does to me here, no one would not rightly blame, I do not say my hardness, but my iniquity. But since the Latin tongue, of whose scarcity in our regions the dispensing of the Gospel labors much, while there the use of the same tongue is altogether common—do you so judge that we ought to provide for the salvation of the Lord's peoples by sending this faculty there, and taking it away from here, where we seek it with a great heat of heart? Grant pardon, therefore, that I do not only against your desire, but even against my own sense, what the care of our burden constrains me to do. The Lord, in whom you have placed your heart, will grant that your labors be such that you may be rewarded for this benefit; for thus, to the most burning thirst of our regions, you rather have granted the deacon Lucillus. For you will furnish no small thing, when you no longer burden me with any petition about this matter, lest I appear nothing other than harder to your venerable and holy Benevolence.
