# Letter 241

AUGUSTINE, TO PASCENTIUS

**1.** Your letter could neither provoke me to return abuse, nor call me back from returning a letter. For the things you wrote would move me, if they were said from the truth of God, not from the power of a man. You said "that my counsel is a crooked and knotty tree, which has nothing straight in it, and perverts the keenness of the eyes." What would you say against me, if I had withdrawn from the agreement we had established between us in the morning, and in a most easy matter that had rightly pleased had set up a crooked opposition and the knots of difficulty? For you would judge me not gorged with muddy water, but—what is much worse—submerged in the drunkenness of perfidy, if I had not returned after the meal such as I had withdrawn before the meal. But behold, did you not write back what you wished, and dread no calumny? So, then, you can do the rest too, so that there may be something which either we ourselves or others can consider and judge. For as to your saying that I believe in a threefold person of God, if you had deigned to read the other, somewhat longer thing I sent, and had wished to answer the things written there, you would perhaps not say this. But yet behold, this very thing—that I say God's person is threefold—you both dictated, and sent written, and feared no calumny: behold, you have shown that what I say is true, that it was not for this reason that you were unwilling, as had pleased when we were together, to dictate your words, because you feared calumny, but because you did not trust in the truth. Now, because it has now pleased you to dictate whether I believe in a threefold person of God; I answer that I do not so believe: for there is one form, because there is one, so to speak, deity, and therefore one God, Father and Son and Holy Spirit.

**2.** But you, I ask, deign to answer briefly how you take what the Apostle says: *He who clings to a harlot is one body; but he who clings to the Lord is one spirit*[^1]. For bodies of diverse sex clinging to each other he called one body. And although the human spirit can in no way say, "I and the Lord are one"; yet when it clings to the Lord, it is one spirit: how much more is he who most truly said, *I and the Father are one*[^2], because he inseparably coheres to the Father, himself and the Father one God? If indeed even this word is admitted in that divinity, that we say "coheres," which was never at all, nor could ever be, separated by any distance. To this answer, whether it pleases you to say that the spirit is twofold, when he who clings to the Lord is one spirit. But if this does not please you, neither did I call God—Father and Son and Holy Spirit—threefold, but one God. But if you wish that we converse in person, I am indeed grateful for your dignity and goodwill. But just as you deigned to write back to me the other thing you wished; so deign to write back that we are going to dictate the things we are going to say, and I will not fail your will, as far as the Lord helps me. For "if you write and I write" does not edify us; how does "you say and I say" edify us, where, after the din of words, we may not find what to review by reading? *I, Augustine, dictated these things, and, the things being reread, subscribed.* Let us refrain from abuse, lest we expend time in vain; and let us rather attend to that which is being treated between us.

[^1]: 1 Cor 6:16-17
[^2]: John 10:30
