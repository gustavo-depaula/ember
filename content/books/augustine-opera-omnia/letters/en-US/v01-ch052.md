# Letter 52

To the much-to-be-longed-for lord and very dear brother Severinus, Augustine

**1.** The letter of your Fraternity, although very late, although otherwise than I had hoped, I yet received with joy, and was flooded with an especially ampler gladness, when I learned that your man had come to Hippo for this one cause alone, that he might bring to me a letter of your Fraternity. For I thought that not without cause this had arisen in your mind, that you should recall our kinship; except because perhaps you perceive—as I know the not-light weight of your prudence—how it is to be grieved, that we who are brothers according to the flesh do not live in the body of Christ in one fellowship, especially because it is easy for you to attend to and see the city set upon a mountain, of which the Lord says in the Gospel that it cannot be hidden[^1]. For that is the catholic Church; whence it is called in Greek *katholikē*, because it is spread throughout the whole world of lands. This no one is permitted to be ignorant of; therefore, according to the word of our Lord Jesus Christ, it cannot be hidden.

**2.** But the party of Donatus, among the Africans alone, calumniates the world of lands, and does not consider that, by that sterility by which it was unwilling to bear the fruit of peace and love, it has been cut off from that root of the Eastern Churches, whence the Gospel came to Africa: whence, if earth from there be brought to them, the faithful man adores; but if a faithful man come thence, they even blow upon him and rebaptize him. For this too the Son of God, who is the truth[^2], foretold: that he is the vine, but his sons are the branches, and his Father the husbandman: *The branch*, he says, *which in me does not bear fruit, my Father will take away; but the branch which in me bears fruit, he purges it, that it may bear more fruit*[^3]. It is no wonder, then, if from that vine which grew and filled all lands[^4], those were cut off who were unwilling to bear the fruit of love.

**3.** If they had objected true crimes to their colleagues, their elders, when they made the schism, would themselves have won their cause before the Church across the sea, whence the authority of the Christian faith flowed to these parts, so that those would be outside to whom they objected those same crimes. But now, since those are found within, communicating with the apostolic Churches, whose names they have in the holy Books and recite, but these are placed outside and separated from that communion, who does not understand that those had a good cause, who could win it before mediating judges? Or if they had a good cause, and could not prove it to the Churches across the sea, what did the world of lands harm them, where bishops could not rashly condemn their colleagues, who had not been convicted before them by objected crimes? And so the innocent are rebaptized, and Christ in the innocent is blown upon with contempt. But if these same Donatists knew true crimes of their African colleagues, and neglected to demonstrate and prove them to the Churches across the sea, they themselves cut themselves off from the unity of Christ by a most wicked schism; they have nothing to excuse, and you know it; especially because so many wicked men arose among them, and they tolerated them through so many years, lest they should rend the party of Donatus, and did not hesitate at that time, objecting their false suspicions, to disrupt the peace and unity of Christ, and you see it.

**4.** But some carnal habit, brother Severinus, holds you there; and I have long grieved, long groaned, especially thinking of your prudence, and I have long desired to see you, that I might speak with you about this matter. For what does either temporal safety or kinship profit, if we despise the eternal inheritance of Christ and perpetual salvation in our kinship? Let it suffice that I have written these things meanwhile, which to hard hearts are very few and almost none; but to your mind, which I know well, are very many and very great. For they are not mine, who am nothing, except that I await the mercy of God; but of God himself, the almighty, whom whoever despises in this age as a father, will find in the future as a judge.

[^1]: See Matt 5:14
[^2]: See John 14:6
[^3]: See John 15:1–2
[^4]: See Ps 80:8–11 (79:9–12)
