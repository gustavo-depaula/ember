# Letter 178

To the most blessed lord, and to be revered in the truth of Christ, brother and fellow priest Hilary, from Augustine, greetings in the Lord.

**1.** Our honorable son Palladius, sailing from our shore, by asking a favor rather gave one, that I should commend not only him to your kindness, but even myself to your prayers, most blessed lord, and to be revered in the love of Christ, brother. When I do this, surely your holiness too will do what we both presume about the matter. But what things are about us, since I know that your love is anxious for us, as ours for you, your holiness will hear from the said bearer. Nevertheless, what is most necessary I will briefly say. A certain new heresy, hostile to the grace of Christ, tries to rise up against the Church of Christ; but it is not yet evidently separated from the Church: of men, namely, who dare to attribute to human weakness so much power that they contend that this alone pertains to the grace of God—that we were created with free choice and the possibility of not sinning, and received the commandments of God to be fulfilled by us; but that for keeping and fulfilling those same commandments we need no divine help. But that the remission of sins is necessary for us, because the things which were done badly by us in the past we are not able to make undone. But for avoiding future sins and conquering them, and for overcoming all temptations by virtue, the human will, by the natural possibility, suffices without any further help of the grace of God. Nor do little ones need the grace of the Savior, by which through his baptism they may be freed from perdition, on the ground that they have drawn no contagion of damnation from Adam.

**2.** How hostile these things are to the grace of God, which has been granted to the human race through Jesus Christ our Lord, and how they strive to overturn the foundations of the whole Christian faith, your venerability sees with us. Nor ought we to have kept silent before you, so that with pastoral care you may beware of men of this kind, whom indeed we wish and choose rather to be healed in the Church than to be cut off from it. For already, while I was writing these things, we had learned that in the Church of Carthage a decree of an episcopal council had been established against them, to be directed by letter to the holy and venerable pope Innocent; and we, from the council of Numidia, had likewise already written to that same apostolic See.

**3.** For all of us who have hope in Christ ought to resist this pestiferous impiety, and concordantly condemn and anathematize it: which contradicts even our prayers, granting indeed that we say, *Forgive us our debts, as we also forgive our debtors*; and so granting it that it asserts that a man, in this corruptible body which weighs down the soul, can by his own strength attain to so great a righteousness that it is no longer necessary for him to say, *Forgive us our debts*. But that which follows, *Lead us not into temptation*, they do not so take as though God must be prayed to help us to overcome the temptations of sins, but lest some human mishap, rushing upon us bodily, afflict us; since to conquer the temptations of sins is so now placed in our power, by the possibility of nature, that we judge it must be obtained by prayers in vain. We cannot in one brief letter gather all or most of the arguments of so great an impiety, especially because, when I was writing these things, the bearers about to sail did not allow me to delay longer. But I think I have not been burdensome to your holy senses, that about so great an evil, to be avoided with all vigilance, the Lord helping, I could not keep silent.
