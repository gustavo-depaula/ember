# Letter 60

To the most blessed lord and, with due observance, venerable, and most sincerely dear brother and fellow priest, pope Aurelius, Augustine sends greeting in the Lord

**1.** I have received no letter of your Venerability, from the time when we parted bodily from one another. But now I have read the letter of your Kindness about Donatus and his brother, and what I should answer I have long wavered over. But yet, thinking again and again what is useful to the salvation of those whom, to be nourished in Christ, we serve, nothing else could occur to me except that this way ought not to be given to the servants of God, that they should think themselves more easily chosen for something better, if they have become worse. For both an easy fall comes to them, and a most unworthy injury is done to the order of the clergy, if deserters of the monasteries are chosen for the service of the clergy, since from those who remain in the monastery we are nevertheless wont to take into the clergy none but the more approved and better: unless perhaps, as the common folk say: A bad piper is a good chorus-singer; so those same common folk will joke about us, saying: A bad monk is a good clergyman. It is too much to be grieved, if we raise up monks to so ruinous a pride, and think the clergy worthy of so grave an insult, in whose number we are; when sometimes even a good monk scarcely makes a good clergyman, if there be in him sufficient continence, and yet there be lacking the necessary instruction, or the integrity of a regular person.

**2.** But about these I believe your Blessedness has judged that by our will, that they might rather be useful to their own fellow countrymen, they had withdrawn from the monastery: but it is false; they went of their own accord, deserted of their own accord, we resisting, as much as we could, for their salvation. And about Donatus indeed, because it has now been done, that before we settled anything about this matter in council, he was ordained, if perhaps he has been corrected from the perversity of pride, let your prudence do what it wishes. But about his brother, on whose account most of all Donatus himself too withdrew from the monastery, although you understand what I feel, I do not know what to answer. Yet I do not dare to contradict your prudence, honor, and love; and indeed I hope that you will do that which you perceive to be wholesome for the members of the Church.
