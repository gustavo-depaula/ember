# Letter 199

*ON THE END OF THE AGE*

TO HESYCHIUS, MOST BLESSED LORD, AND BROTHER AND FELLOW BISHOP TO BE LOOKED UP TO WITH VENERATION, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETING IN THE LORD.

**1. 1.** I have received the letter of your Veneration, in which you very wholesomely exhort that the coming of our Savior be loved and desired. You do this as the good servant of that same Householder, eager for the gains of your Lord, and wishing to have very many companions in the love by which you yourself are kindled especially and perseveringly. Considering, then, what you recalled from the Apostle—that he said the Lord would render the crown of righteousness not only to himself, but also to all who love his manifestation—we live rightly, and act in this world as pilgrims, when our heart progressively extends itself in this love; whether he come sooner or later than is thought, whose manifestation is loved with faithful love and desired with pious affection. For that servant who says, *My lord delays*, and beats his fellow servants, eats and drinks with the drunken[^1], surely does not love his manifestation. For his mind appears from his morals; which morals, though briefly, the good Master therefore took care to express, that is, pride and luxury, lest what he said, *My lord delays*, be believed to be said with desire for his lord, with which that one burned who said, *My soul has thirsted for the living God: when shall I come and appear before the face of God?*[^2] For by saying *When shall I come?* he bore it grievously that he was suffering delays; because even what is hastened in time seems slow to desire. But how is his coming slow, or how is it to be far off, when the Apostles themselves, at the time when they were still in the flesh, said, *It is the last hour*[^3], although they had heard from the Lord, *It is not for you to know the times*[^4]? This, then, they did not know, which we too do not know (I speak as far as concerns me, and those who, with me, do not know this); and yet those to whom it was said, *It is not for you to know the times which the Father has placed in his own power*, loved his manifestation, and gave their fellow servants their food in due time, and did not beat them by lording it over them, nor revel with the lovers of the world, saying, *My lord delays*.

**1. 2.** The ignorance of the times, then, is one thing; the corruption of morals and love of vices another. For when the apostle Paul said, *Be not quickly moved in mind, nor be terrified, neither by word nor by letter, as though sent through us, as if the day of the Lord were at hand*[^5], he surely did not wish them to believe those who thought that the coming of the Lord was now drawing near: nor yet did he wish that, like that servant, they should say, *My lord delays his coming*, and give themselves over to pride and luxury to be ruined; but he so did not wish them to give ear to false rumors about the last day drawing near, that he nevertheless wished them to await the coming of their Lord prepared, *with loins girded and lamps burning*[^6]: to whom he says, *But you, brethren, are not in darkness, that that day should overtake you like a thief. For you are all sons of light and sons of the day; we are not of the night nor of darkness*[^7]. But that one who says, *My lord delays his coming*, so as to beat his fellow servants and feast with the drunken, is not a son of light, but of darkness; and therefore that day will overtake him like a thief: which each one ought to dread even of the last day of this life of his. For in whatever state his own last day finds anyone, in that state the world's last day will overtake him; since such as anyone dies on that day, such will he be judged on that day.

**1. 3.** To this pertains what is written thus in the Gospel according to Mark: *Watch, therefore, for you do not know when the lord of the house comes—at evening, or at midnight, or at cockcrow, or in the morning—lest, coming suddenly, he find you sleeping. But what I say to you, I say to all, Watch*[^8]. For to whom does he say "to all," if not to his elect and beloved belonging to his body, which is the Church? He said it, then, not only to those who were then hearing him as he spoke, but also to those who were after them before us, and to us ourselves, and to those who will be after us until his last coming. But will that day find all in this life, or will anyone say that what he says, *Watch, lest, coming suddenly, he find you sleeping*, pertains even to the dead? Why, then, does he say "to all" what pertains only to those who will then be alive, except that it pertains to all in the way I have said? For that day will then come to each one, when the day comes for him to depart hence in such a state as he is to be judged on that day. And thereby every Christian ought to watch, lest the coming of the Lord find him unprepared. But that day will find unprepared the one whom the last day of this his life shall find unprepared. For certainly this at least was manifest to the Apostles, that the Lord was not going to come in their times, while they lived here in the flesh: and yet who would doubt that they especially watched and kept what he said to all, lest, coming suddenly, he find them unprepared?

**2. 4.** I do not yet indeed understand in what way what your Holiness wrote ought to be taken—that the Lord said to the Apostles, *It is not for you to know the times or the moments which the Father has placed in his own power*[^9], for this reason: because, following on, he added, *But you shall be witnesses to me in Jerusalem, and in Judea, and in Samaria, and to the uttermost part of the earth*[^10]. This sense of Scripture you so expound as to say: "He willed, then, that the Apostles be understood as witnesses not of the consummation of the world, but of his name and resurrection." He did not, indeed, say "It is not for you to preach the times"; but, *It is not for you to know*. But if you wish what he said, *It is not for you to know*, to be so understood as if he said, "It is not for you to make known," that is, "it is not for you to teach this"—who of us would dare to teach, or presume that he knows, what that divine Master did not teach even those disciples by whom, present, he was questioned, present, nor could those so holy and great teachers teach the Church?

**2. 5.** Or will it be answered that the Apostles did not teach these things, but the Prophets did? For you said this, and it is true, that those things *which are to be are made known by the voices of the holy Prophets, who*, you say, *before they came about, spoke to men, by the divine will, the things that were to be*. But if your Veneration says it is full enough of wonder if God established that the things he willed to be foretold could in no way reach men's perception, how much more full of wonder is it if the things which the Prophets spoke to men, these the Apostles were either prohibited from knowing or from teaching! But how would the Prophets, teaching these times of which we treat, not be understood by the Apostles, if they are understood by us? Or if the Apostles understood them, prophesying this measure of time, how would they not teach what they understood, when through their proclamation the very Prophets too became known, who taught them these things in their Books? And therefore, through the same writings by which they themselves had learned these things, others too could learn them, among those nations among whom the Apostles commended the prophetic authority. Why, then, was it said to them, *It is not for you to know*—or, if it is to be so understood, "It is not for you to teach"—the times which the Father has placed in his own power; since they so taught them, when those in whose writings they were learned became known through them? Whence it is more credible that God did not wish to be known what he willed to be foretold, but did not wish to be foretold what he saw was not usefully known.

**2. 6.** "Why, then," you say, "does the Lord himself admonish concerning the knowing of the times, where he says, *Who, then, is the faithful and prudent servant, whom the Lord has set over his household, to give them food in due time?*[^11]" and so forth. Nay rather, he admonishes, not that the good servant should know the end of the times, but that at every time he should watch in good work, because he does not know the end of the times. He does not admonish that we should attain the times, which the Father has placed in his own power, beyond the Apostles; but he admonishes that, since we do not know when the Lord comes, we should imitate the Apostles in preparation of heart: of which I have already said enough above. But he reproaches the Jews because they do not know the time, saying: *Hypocrites, you can discern the face of the sky*[^12], and so forth; because they did not recognize that time which he now wished to be recognized, that is, that of his first coming, by which they should believe in him who would wish to await his other coming, watching, whenever it should be. For he who has not recognized the first coming of the Lord will not be able to prepare himself for the second, by believing in him and faithfully watching, lest that day overtake him like a thief in the darkness, whether it come later or sooner than is hoped.

**3. 7.** The apostle Paul also says, as you remind, *In the last times perilous times shall be at hand*[^13], and the rest. But does he therefore teach the times which the Father has placed in his own power? Or does anyone therefore know how long or short these very times are, which it must be confessed are the last? For we ought indeed to consider how long ago it was said, *Little children, it is the last hour*[^14].

**3. 8.** Again you recall that the same Apostle said: *But of the times and the moments we have no need to write to you: for you yourselves know diligently that the day of the Lord will come as a thief in the night; and when they shall say, Peace and security, then sudden destruction shall come upon them, as the pangs upon a woman in travail, and they shall not escape*[^15]. And here he did not say after how much time this would be, but how it would be; that is, not how great would be the brevity or length of the period, but that, however great the interval and span of time, this last evil would nevertheless not come upon them except when they shall have said, *Peace and security*. By which words the Apostle seems to have removed the hope or fear of this last day from this our time: for we do not see the very lovers of this age, upon whom sudden destruction will come, already saying, *Peace and security*.

**3. 9.** What, then, it suffices to know, the Apostle himself sufficiently shows, by saying: *Of the times and the moments we have no need to write to you*; or, as other manuscripts have it, *You have no need that it be written to you*[^16]. Nor, following on, did he add, "For you yourselves know diligently how much time remains"; but he says, *For you yourselves know diligently that the hour of the Lord will come as a thief in the night*[^17]. This, then, it is needful to know, so that those who are unwilling to be overtaken by that hour as by a thief in the night may take care to be sons of light and to watch with a prepared heart. For if, to avoid this evil—that is, lest the hour of the Lord find him unprepared like a thief—it were needful to know the spans of times, the Apostle would not say it was not needful to write this, but would rather judge, as a most provident teacher, that this must be written to them. But now he showed that not even this was needful for them, for whom it sufficed to know that the hour of the Lord would come like a thief upon the unprepared and the sleeping, and that, by knowing this, they themselves might be watchfully prepared, after however long a time it should come; and he kept his own measure, so that, although an apostle, he nevertheless did not presume to teach others what he knew the Lord had said to the Apostles: *It is not for you to know*.

**3. 10.** You set down also what the same Apostle said: *Do you not remember that, when I was with you, I told you these things? And now you know what restrains, that he may be revealed in his own time. For the mystery of iniquity already works; only that he who holds, let him now hold, until he be taken out of the way: and then shall that wicked one be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus shall slay with the spirit of his mouth*[^18]. Would that you deigned not only to set down these apostolic words, but also to expound them: so obscure indeed are they and mystically said, that nevertheless it appears that he said nothing of fixed times, and opened up no interval or span of them. For he says, *that he may be revealed in his own time*: nor did he say after how much time this would be. Then he subjoined, *For the mystery of iniquity already works*. This mystery of iniquity, however it be understood as to what it is—by one in this way, by another in that—nevertheless, so long as it works, is hidden. Nor did the Apostle express it as a man from the number of those to whom it was said, *It is not for you to know the times*: for although he was not yet among them when this was said to them, we do not doubt that he too belonged to their college and fellowship.

**3. 11.** Likewise, what follows—*only that he who now holds, let him hold, until he be taken out of the way: and then shall that wicked one be revealed, whom the Lord Jesus shall slay with the spirit of his mouth*[^19]—teaches us that the Antichrist will be manifest: since indeed he seems to have touched, with a somewhat clearer signification, that he is to be slain by the spirit of the mouth of the Lord Jesus Christ; but after how much time this will be, he did not speak even obscurely. For who he is who now holds, or what he holds, or what he means by saying *be taken out of the way*, anyone can constrain himself to understand, or in some measure surmise, since he reads it written in some fashion; but how long he holds, and after how great spans of time he is taken out of the way, is here altogether passed over in silence.

**4. 12.** "Likewise," you say, "the Lord in the Gospel rebukes the Jews, saying: *And you, if you had known the time of your visitation, perhaps you would have remained; but now they are hidden from your eyes*[^20]." But this pertains to the time of the Lord's first coming, not the second, about which the question now is. For about his second coming he said, *It is not for you to know the times*, not about the first: for about that one the disciples asked him, which they hoped for, not about this one, which they already saw. For if the Jews had known his first coming, they would never have crucified the Lord of glory; and therefore they could have not been overturned, but remained. Now, as for what he says to them, *Repent; the times are fulfilled; believe the Gospel*[^21], you yourself too have affirmed that it was said of those times of the Jews which were soon, after a few years, to pass away, and which we now know are already past—the city, that is, being overturned in which their kingdom had been established.

**4. 13.** Accordingly, as for what your Veneration set down from Daniel about the beast slain, and about the kingdom of the other beasts, and amid these about the Son of man coming with the clouds of heaven, you said it was manifest to those who understand the Scripture. But if you will deign to expound how these things pertain to knowing the span of time after which the coming of the Savior will be, in such a way that they are found manifest without any ambiguity, I myself too will confess, with great giving of thanks, that what the Lord said, *It is not for you to know the times*, was said only to the Apostles, not to their successors, who were going to know them.

**5. 14.** The coming of the Lord, then, must be loved and awaited, as you holily exhort; and you say there is great blessedness for those who love his coming, and you bring forward the testimony of the Apostle, whose words you set down thus: *For the rest, there is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the just judge, will render to me in that day: and not to me only, but to those also who love the coming of the Lord*[^22]. For then, as you recall from the Gospel, *The righteous shall shine like the sun in the kingdom of their Father*[^23]; and what the prophet says, *For behold, gloom and darkness shall cover the earth over the nations; but in you the Lord shall appear, and his majesty shall be seen in you*[^24]; likewise what is written, *But those who wait for the Lord shall exult with strength; they shall put forth wings like eagles; they shall run and not be weary, they shall walk and not hunger*[^25].

**5. 15.** This you say plainly most piously and most truly, commending the blessedness of those who love the coming of the Lord. But those too to whom the Apostle said, *Be not quickly moved in mind, as though the day of the Lord were at hand*[^26], surely loved the coming of the Lord: nor in saying this did the Teacher of the Gentiles break them off from that love by which he wished them to be inflamed; and therefore he did not wish them to believe those from whom they heard that the day of the Lord was at hand, lest perhaps, when the time had passed in which they had believed he would come, and they did not perceive that he had come, they should think that the rest too was deceitfully promised them, and despair even of the very reward of faith. He, then, does not love the coming of the Lord who asserts that it draws near, nor he who asserts that it does not draw near; but rather he who, whether it be near or far, awaits it with sincerity of faith, firmness of hope, and ardor of love. For if the Lord is loved the more, the more he is believed and preached to be coming sooner, then those who said his coming was already at hand loved him more than those whom the Apostle forbade to believe them, or than the Apostle himself, who surely did not believe this.

**6. 16.** But if my weakness is not burdensome to your Holiness, I ask that you not be reluctant to expound more openly how you said that "no one can gather the measures of the times"; lest perhaps this too seem to me as it does to your Charity, and any of us await in vain to be instructed by the other. For when you had said this, you then subjoined and said: "The Gospel indeed says, *Of the day and hour no one knows*[^27]; but I," you say, "on account of the capacity of my understanding, say that neither the month nor the year of his coming can be known." For this seems to sound as though it cannot be known in what year he will come, but it can be known in what week of years, or in what decade; as though it could be said and defined within those or those seven years, or within those or those ten years. But if not even this can be comprehended, I ask whether the time of his coming can at least be defined in such a way that, when we say he will come within, for example, fifty or a hundred years, or however many of a greater or lesser number of years, we are ignorant in which of them. If you have already comprehended this, it is much that you have been able to comprehend. And this very thing is what I ask that you kindly impart to us, applying suitable proofs by which you have been able to track it down: but if you do not presume to have comprehended even this, you think what I think.

**6. 17.** For that these are the last times, with many signs of things appearing which we read the Lord foretold, all of us who believe these things perceive. But even a span of a thousand years, if their end were the end of the age, could the whole of it be called the last time, or even the last day, because it is written, *A thousand years before your eyes are as one day*[^28]; so that whatever was carried on through those thousand years would be said to be carried on in the last time or the last day. For again I say what in this case must often be said: let us consider how many years ago the blessed evangelist John said, *It is the last hour*[^29]. For if we were then, on hearing this, when would we believe that so many years would pass afterward, and not rather, with John himself still set in the body, hope that the Lord would come? For he did not say, "It is the last time," or "the last year," or "month," or "day"; but, *It is the last hour*: and behold, how long is this hour! And yet he did not lie: but surely he must be understood to have put "hour" for "time." Which some so take as to set up six thousand years as one day, and to divide it into twelve parts, as it were hours, so that thus the last five hundred years seem to have the last hour: in which years, they say, John was already speaking, when he said it was the last hour.

**6. 18.** But to know something is one thing, to surmise it another. For why not rather, if six thousand years are to be reckoned one day, is one hour not the twelfth but the twenty-fourth part of it—that is, not five hundred but two hundred fifty years? For the whole day, the whole circuit of the sun, is more truly named not from the East to the West, but from the East to the East, whence, the whole day being completed—that is, twenty-four hours—it rises again: and thereby this last hour, from the time when John spoke this, nearly seventy years before, is found already to have passed at the least, and the end of the age has not yet come. To this is added that, the ecclesiastical history being diligently inspected, the apostle John is found to have died long before five thousand five hundred years from the beginning of the human race were completed. It was not yet, then, the last hour, if the twelfth part of six thousand years—that is, five hundred years—is reckoned as the span of one hour. Moreover, if, according to the Scriptures, we set one day to be a thousand years, much more long ago did the last hour of so long a day pass; I do not say if its twenty-fourth part, which is a little more than forty years, but if its twelfth part be reckoned, which has double those years. Whence it is more fittingly believed that that apostle put "hour" for "time"; how long which hour is, we are ignorant, because *it is not ours to know the times which the Father has placed in his own power*[^30]: although we know it to be the last hour—much more surely we, then, than those who were before us, from the time it began to be, or to be called, the last hour.

**7. 19.** But as for why it seems to your Veneration that the measures of times cannot be comprehended, so as to define in what year the end will be, because, according to the Lord's promise, *those days shall be shortened*[^31], I do not understand it at all. For if they will be so shortened that out of more they become fewer, I ask, according to what truth were they going to be more, unless they were shortened? For the Weeks of holy Daniel you suppose pertain not to the first coming of the Lord, as has seemed to many, but rather to the second. Will they, then, be so shortened that there will be even one week less in them, and the prophecy will be false, which with so great diligence took care to define the number of the weeks, so that it said something to be fulfilled even in the half of a week? I wonder if by the prophecy of Christ the prophecy of Daniel is destroyed. Then, what sort of thing is it that we should suppose that Daniel—or rather the angel from whom he was learning these things—was ignorant that the Lord would shorten those days, and erred in saying this; or knew indeed, but lied to the one whom he was teaching? But if this is absurd, why do we not rather believe that Daniel prophesied so many weeks according to that by which the Lord was going to shorten those days?—if indeed that number of years refers to the second coming of the Lord (which I do not know how it can be shown).

**7. 20.** Finally, much more certainly and securely is the Lord's coming said to be within seventy, or at most within a hundred, years, if these Weeks foretell it. For there are four hundred ninety years in seventy weeks: and from the nativity of the Lord today are computed nearly four hundred twenty years; and from his resurrection or ascension, more or less three hundred ninety years. And thereby, if it be computed from the time he was born, seventy remain; if from the time he suffered, about a hundred remain: within which all the Weeks of Daniel, if they were prophesied of his last coming, will be fulfilled. He, then, who says, "Within so many years it will be," then says something false if it will be beyond: but because they will be shortened, they will be able to be fewer, not more. Rightly, therefore, is it said, "Within these it will be," since, however much they are shortened, it will be true: for that very shortening, if it is to be understood in such a way that they become fewer, does not allow the day of the Lord to come beyond them, but more and more within them, the fewer they are. This shortening, then, does not disturb the reckoner who has defined in such a way as to say that within so many years the day of the Lord will come; rather it helps him, since the more the days are reduced to fewness, the more it will be within, and it will not be able to be beyond: and therefore that will be true which is so defined as to say, "Within so many years it will be," although it is not known in what year it will be.

**7. 21.** Accordingly, the whole question is whether the Weeks of Daniel were fulfilled at the first coming of the Lord, or prophesied the end of the age, or pertain to both. For there were not lacking those who held even this, saying that they were completed at the time of Christ's first coming, and that from there until the end of the age just as many are again to be completed. For my part I see that, if the first coming did not complete them, the second must necessarily complete them; since that prophecy cannot be false: which, if it was fulfilled at the time of the first coming, does not compel us to understand that it will be fulfilled also concerning the end of the age. And thereby it is uncertain, even if it is true; and indeed it must not be denied, but neither must it be presumed, that this will be. It remains, therefore, that whoever wishes to compel this prophecy to be believed to be fulfilled at the end of the age should contend as much as he can, and show, if he can, that it was not completed at the first coming of the Lord—against so many expounders of the divine words, who demonstrate that it was completed thereby, not only by the computation of times, but also by the very facts, especially because it is written there, *And the Holy of holies shall be anointed*[^32]: or because in the same prophecy the Hebrew manuscripts have more expressly, *Christ shall be slain, and it shall not be his*[^33]—that is, it shall not be his city; since he was so alienated from the Jews, who for that reason believed that he was not their Savior and redeemer, because they were able to kill him. For Christ is not to be anointed or slain at the end of the age, so that this prophecy of Daniel should then be awaited to be fulfilled, which is not yet believed fulfilled.

**8. 22.** But that, from the evangelical and prophetic signs which we perceive coming to pass, we ought to hope for the near coming of the Lord, who denies? For daily it becomes more and more near. But at how great an interval it draws near—this, it has been said, *it is not for you to know*. See when the Apostle said, *For now our salvation is nearer than when we believed. The night has gone before, and the day has drawn near*[^34]: and behold, how many years have passed! And yet what he said is not false. How much more now must it be said that the coming of the Lord draws near, when so great an approach to the end has been made! The Apostle certainly says: *The Spirit manifestly says that in the last times some will depart from the faith*[^35]. Surely those times were not yet—that is, of the heretics and of such men as he describes in the same discourse—but they have now come: and thereby in the last times we seem to be admonished even through them concerning the end of the age. And likewise, saying elsewhere, *But know this, that in the last days fierce times shall be at hand*, or, as other manuscripts have it, *perilous*[^36]; then expressing what they will be like, *For men shall be lovers of themselves*, he says, *lovers of money, haughty, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents, ungrateful, wicked, irreligious, without affection, slanderers, incontinent, unmerciful, without kindness, traitors, insolent, blinded, lovers of pleasures more than of God, having an appearance of piety but denying its power*[^37]. It is a wonder if such men were ever lacking. Finally, because they were then too, he adds and says, *And avoid these. For of these are they who penetrate homes*[^38]. He does not say "They will penetrate," as though here too foretelling something future, as he had said above, *perilous times shall be at hand*; but he says, *They penetrate homes, and lead captive silly women*[^39]: he does not say "They will lead," or "they are going to lead," but, they already lead.

**8. 23.** Nor must it be thought that in this place, instead of words of future time, he put words of present time; since he was admonishing that they be avoided by the one to whom he writes. Nor yet did he say in vain, *In the last days perilous times shall be at hand*; and, showing from this that those times would be perilous, he foretold that such men would be, except because they will be so much the more numerous, and will abound the more, the more it draws near to the end. We see, then, that such men now abound. But what, if they will be more abundant after us, and altogether most abundant when the very end is at hand and pressing, which, how long it will be away, is not known? For "the last days" were said even in those very first days of the Apostles, when the Lord's ascension into heaven was recent, when on the day of Pentecost he sent the promised Holy Spirit, and some were amazed, marveling at those who spoke in tongues they had not learned, while others, mocking, said they were full of new wine. On which day Peter, speaking to those who were variously moved about this matter, says: *For these are not drunk, as you suppose, since it is the third hour of the day. But heed that this is what was said through the prophet, It shall be in the last days, says the Lord, I will pour out of my Spirit upon all flesh*[^40], and the rest.

**8. 24.** Already then, therefore, those were the last days; how much more now, even if only so many days remain until the end as have passed from the Lord's ascension to this day, or whether something less or more remains?—which we surely do not know, because *it is not for us to know the times or the moments which the Father has placed in his own power*[^41]: since nevertheless we know that we act in the last times, in the last days, in the last hour, like the Apostles; but much more those who were after them before us, and much more we, and more than we those who will be after us, until one comes to those who will be, if it can be said, the last of the last, and to the very last of all, whom the Lord wishes to be understood where he says, *And I will raise him up on the last day*[^42]: how far off which is, cannot be comprehended.

**9. 25.** The signs which in the Gospel are foretold as future, as your Holiness recalls, according to Luke, are the same according to Matthew and according to Mark. For these three narrate what the Lord said, when he was asked by his disciples when those things would be which he had foretold concerning the overthrow of the temple, and what would be the sign of his coming and of the consummation of the age. For they do not disagree in the facts, if one says something that another passes over in silence, or says it in another way: rather, compared with one another, they help, so that the reader's understanding may be guided. But to discourse about them all is now too long. For to those asking, the Lord answered the things that were going to follow from that time onward—whether concerning the destruction of Jerusalem, whence arose the occasion of the very question; or concerning his coming through the Church, in which he does not cease to come until the end: for in his own he is recognized as coming, while his members are daily born; of which coming he says, *Henceforth you shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds*[^43]—of which clouds it was said through the prophet, *I will command my clouds not to rain upon it*[^44]; or concerning the very end, in which he will appear to judge the living and the dead.

**9. 26.** Since, then, he speaks of signs which pertain to these three things—that is, to the destruction of that city; to his coming in his body, which is the Church; and to his coming in the head of the Church, which is he himself—which of those signs is to be referred to which of these three must be discerned by diligent consideration, lest perhaps what pertains to the overthrow of Jerusalem we think must be referred to the end of the age; or, on the contrary, what pertains to the end of the age we affirm to pertain to the destruction of that city; or what pertains to his coming in his body, which is the Church, we say pertains to his last coming in his body, which is the head of the Church; or, again, what pertains to his last coming through himself, we assert to pertain to that coming which is through the Church. Among all of which there are some things manifest, but some so obscure that it is either laborious to discern them, or rash, so long as they are not understood, to define anything about them.

**9. 27.** For who does not see that to that city pertains what was said, *But when you see Jerusalem surrounded by an army, then know that its desolation has drawn near*[^45]? Likewise, who does not see that to the last coming of the Lord pertains what was said, *When you see these things come to pass, know that the kingdom of God is near*[^46]? But that which was said, *Woe to those who are with child and to those who nurse in those days. But pray that your flight be not in winter or on the sabbath. For then shall be great tribulation, such as has not been from the beginning of the world, nor shall be*[^47], is so set down according to Matthew and according to Mark that it is uncertain whether it ought to be understood of the destruction of that city or of the end of the age. For thus this very thing is read in Mark: *But woe to those who are with child and to those who nurse in those days. Pray that they may not be in winter. For those days shall be such tribulation as has not been from the beginning of the creation which God founded, until now, nor shall be. And unless the Lord had shortened those days, no flesh would have been saved; but for the sake of the elect, whom he chose, he has shortened the days*[^48]. And not differently does Matthew. But Luke so set this down that it appears to pertain to the destruction of that city; for thus it is read in him: *But woe to those who are with child and to those who nurse in those days. For there shall be great distress upon the earth, and wrath upon this people; and they shall fall by the edge of the sword, and shall be led captive into all nations; and Jerusalem shall be trodden down by the Gentiles, until the times of the nations be fulfilled*[^49].

**9. 28.** But how one came to this, Matthew writes thus: *When, therefore, you shall see the abomination of desolation, which was spoken of by Daniel the prophet, standing in the holy place—let him who reads understand—then let those who are in Judea flee to the mountains; and let him who is on the housetop not come down to take anything out of his house; and let him who is in the field not return to take his tunic. But woe to those who are with child and to those who nurse in those days*[^50], and so forth. But Mark thus: *But when you shall see the abomination of desolation standing where it ought not—let him who reads understand—then let those who are in Judea flee to the mountains; and let him who is upon the housetop not come down into the house, nor go in to take anything out of his house; and let him who is in the field not return back to take his garment. But woe to those who are with child and to those who nurse in those days!*[^51] and the rest. But Luke, to show that the abomination of desolation which was foretold by Daniel was then brought about, when Jerusalem was stormed, recalls in the same place these words of the Lord: *But when you shall see Jerusalem surrounded by an army, then know that its desolation has drawn near*[^52]. It appears, then, that there was then set the abomination of desolation, of which those two evangelists spoke. Finally, this one too likewise follows: *Then let those who are in Judea flee to the mountains*[^53]. And in place of what those said, *and let him who is upon the housetop not come down into the house, nor go in to take anything out of the house*, this one said, *and let those who are in the midst of it depart*[^54]: so that it might be shown, by those words set down among the other Evangelists, that haste of flight was commanded. And in place of what those set down, *and let him who is in the field not return back to take his garment*, this one says more openly, *and let those who are in the countryside not enter into it; because these are the days of vengeance, that all things which are written may be fulfilled*[^55]. Then this one too likewise follows, so that it is altogether manifest that this passage of the Gospel of all three is about this very matter: *But woe to those who are with child and to those who nurse in those days!*[^56] and the rest pertaining to this, which I have already recalled above.

**9. 29.** Luke, then, laid open—what could be uncertain—that what was said about the abomination of desolation, and what was said about the shortening of the days for the sake of the elect, pertains not to the end of the age but to the storming of Jerusalem; because, even if he himself did not say those very things, he nevertheless said more openly the rest about this very matter, by which he showed that those things too pertain to it. For we ought not to doubt that, when Jerusalem was overthrown, there were in that people elect of God, who from the circumcision had believed, or were going to believe—elect before the foundation of the world—on account of whom those days would be shortened, that the evils might become tolerable. For some seem to me to have understood more fittingly that the evils themselves are signified by the name of "days," just as evil days are spoken of in other places of divine Scripture: for the days themselves are not evil, but the things that come to pass in them. These, then, are said to be shortened, so that, God granting endurance, they might be felt less, and thus the things that were great might become brief.

**9. 30.** But whether that shortening of days is to be understood in this way, or as their being reduced to fewness, or as their being shortened by a swifter course of the sun (for there are not lacking those who think this too—that the days are said to be going to be shorter, just as the day was longer when Joshua the son of Nun prayed): nevertheless the evangelist Luke taught that both this shortening of days and the abomination of desolation—the two things which he himself did not say, but Matthew and Mark said—pertain to the overthrow of Jerusalem, saying with them other things more openly about this same matter, which they set down more obscurely. For Josephus, who wrote the history of the Jews, says that such evils then befell that people that they seem scarcely credible: whence it was not undeservedly said that such tribulation neither had been from the beginning of creation, nor would be. But even if in the time of Antichrist such, or perhaps greater, will be, it must be understood as said of that people, that for it no greater such will be: for if they are going to receive Antichrist first and especially, that same people will then be making the tribulation rather than suffering it.

**9. 31.** There is no reason, then, why we should think that the Weeks of the prophet Daniel were either disturbed on account of the shortening of days, or were not completed at that time, but are to be completed at the end of the age. For they were not fulfilled before the passion of the Lord. Those who think this are most rightly refuted by your own sentence, in which you said: "If, then, this abomination had already been fulfilled, how does the Lord admonish and say, *When you see the abomination of desolation, which was spoken of through Daniel the prophet, standing in the holy place—let him who reads understand?*" By these words of your Blessedness those are deservedly to be corrected who say that it, when the Lord spoke these things, had been fulfilled before his passion and resurrection. But those who say, as the evangelist Luke too most plainly testifies, that it was brought about when Jerusalem was overthrown—what is to be answered to them, those ought to see who suppose these things to be future at the end of the age, or near the end; although the very abomination of desolation, on account of the obscurity of the saying, could not be understood by all in one way.

**9. 32.** And what was said, *Let him who is on the housetop not come down to take anything out of his house; and let him who is in the field not return to take his tunic*[^57], can be more fittingly understood spiritually—that in all tribulations care must be taken lest anyone, overcome, come down to the carnal life from spiritual loftiness, or, having progressed, *reaching out to the things before*[^58], by failing look back to the things behind. But if in every tribulation, how much more in that one ought it to have been commanded that care be taken, which was foretold as going to be such for that city as had not been from the beginning, nor would be; and if in this one, how much more in that which will be the last for the world, that is, for the Church spread throughout the whole world! For Luke himself also—not indeed when the Lord was asked by his disciples about his coming, where Matthew and Mark recalled this, but in another place where the Pharisees asked him when the kingdom of God would come—narrated that he said something of this kind: *In that hour*, he says, *let him who is on the housetop, and his goods in the house, not come down to take them; and let him who is in the field likewise not return back*[^59].

**9. 33.** But now, on account of the computation of times, it is the Weeks of Daniel that are in question, which, if they were not completed about the times of the Lord's first coming, but are to be completed at the end—who would believe that the Apostles were ignorant of this, or knew it indeed but were forbidden to teach it? Although, if this is so, the nations usefully do not know what the Lord forbade to teach those whom he wished to be teachers of the nations. But if they have already been completed—because the Holy of holies has now been anointed, Christ has now been slain, so that it should not be his city, the sacrifice has now been taken away from that temple, the anointing has been taken away—deservedly was it answered to the Apostles asking about the end, *It is not for you to know the times which the Father has placed in his own power*[^60]; since the times which through Daniel's prophecy they could know did not pertain to the end of the age, about which they had asked.

**10. 34.** But as for signs from heaven and earth—have we seen greater ones than those who were before us? Is it not the case that, if the history of the nations is read, so great wonders are found to have occurred from heaven and earth that some are not even believed? But—to omit many things which it would be long to pursue—when have we seen two suns? Which were seen, those who were then committed to writing, before the Lord had come in the flesh. When have we seen the sun so darkened as it was darkened when the Light of the world hung on the wood? Unless perhaps we shall number among the heavenly portents the eclipses of sun and moon, which the computers of the stars are accustomed to note and foretell, because we have seen the moon more often in its fullness, but the sun more rarely, yet nonetheless fail according to their computation at the end of the moon. That eclipse of the sun was not such, when Christ was crucified, and therefore it was truly marvelous and portentous. For it had been the Passover of the Jews, which is celebrated only at the fullness of the moon: but according to the numbers of the astrologers it is certain that the sun cannot fail when the moon is full, but when it is the end of the moon; not indeed always, but otherwise never, when it fails according to those numbers. What such thing, then, has anyone ever remembered to have appeared from heaven, since the Lord foretold those things, such as appeared when he suffered? Wherefore, if such things too will appear—if they are not rather to be understood spiritually—they will then appear, when the end will so draw near that they ought to appear.

**10. 35.** But by wars, through diverse intervals of times and places, when has the earth not been ground down? For—to pass over things too ancient—under the emperor Gallienus, when barbarism was overrunning the Roman provinces on every side, how many of our brethren who were then in the flesh do we suppose could have believed the end to be near, since it happened long after the Lord's ascension! And thereby what these things too will be like, when the end will be altogether near, we are ignorant; if indeed these were not so foretold that they ought rather to be understood in the Church. For there are two nations, and two kingdoms; one, namely, of Christ, the other of the devil, of which it could be said, *Nation shall rise up against nation, and kingdom against kingdom*[^61]: which does not cease to happen, from the time it was said, *Repent; for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near*[^62]. See when this was said, and from then how many years have passed; and yet it was said most truly. For in the last days the Lord came through the Virgin: nor would this hour be called the last, unless with the kingdom of heaven drawing near—through which whole hour come to pass the things which the Lord, his kingdom drawing near, foretold would be: but at how great a span this hour is extended, if it was said to the Apostles, *It is not for you to know*, how much more ought any man, such as I am, to recognize his own measure, *lest he be wise beyond what he ought to be wise*[^63]!

**11. 36.** "But our punishment," you say, "compels us to confess that the end is already at hand, while what was foretold is fulfilled: *Men withering away for fear and expectation of the things which shall come upon the whole world*[^64]. It is certain," you say, "that no country, no place, in our times is not afflicted or troubled; as it was said, *For fear, and expectation of the things which shall come upon the whole world*." If, then, these evils which the human race now suffers are sure tokens that the Lord is now going to come, what is it that the Apostle says, *When they shall say, Peace and security*[^65]? For when the Gospel had said, *Men withering away for fear and expectation*, it immediately subjoined: *For the powers of the heavens shall be moved: and then they shall see the Son of man coming in a cloud with great power and majesty*[^66].

**11. 37.** Let us see, then, whether perhaps it is better understood that not those things foretold by those words are now being fulfilled, but rather that they are then going to come, when there will be such tribulation upon the whole world; so that what will be troubled throughout the whole world pertains to the Church, not to those who will trouble it. For they themselves are going to say, *Peace and security*, so that sudden destruction may come upon them, and the coming of the Lord may overtake them like a thief in the night; while, on the contrary, those who love the manifestation of the Lord will rejoice and exult. But now these evils, which are believed to be, as it were, the highest and the last, we see to be common to both nations and both kingdoms—that is, of Christ and of the devil: for by these the good are afflicted equally with the evil; nor is there anyone who says *Peace and security*, wherever they befall, or where it is feared lest they befall. Among which evils, nevertheless, luxurious banquets are still everywhere frequented, drunkenness is hot, avarice rages, wanton songs resound, organs, pipes, lyres, citharas, dice, many and various kinds of sounds and games: is this to wither away for fear, or rather to grow sodden with lust? But these things the sons of darkness will have and practice more abundantly, when they shall say, *Peace and security*.

**11. 38.** What of the very sons of light and sons of the day, who are not in darkness, that that day should overtake them like a thief? Do they not still *use this world as not using it*[^67]? For although it was said many years ago, in the times of the Apostles, they nevertheless consider with pious solicitude what was said, *The time is short*[^68]. Is it not the case that by the greatest part of them planting, building, buying, possessing are done, honors are still borne, wives are taken? I speak of those who, awaiting their Lord, when he comes from the wedding, nevertheless do not abstain from carnal weddings, but with most obedient love hear the Apostle commanding how wives ought to live with husbands, husbands with wives, children with parents, parents with children, slaves with masters, masters with slaves: do not all these, in all these things, use this world? They plow, sail, buy, beget, serve as soldiers, administer. I think they will not be so, when there will be the things which were foretold in the Gospel—signs in the sun and moon and stars, and on the earth distress of nations, by reason of the confusion of the roaring of the sea and the waves; men withering away for fear and expectation of the things which shall come upon the whole world: for the powers of the heavens shall be moved.

**11. 39.** These things indeed I judge are better understood in the Church, lest the Lord Jesus, with his second coming drawing near, seem to have foretold as something great the things that were accustomed to befall this world even before his first coming, and lest we be mocked by those who have read of these things—which we dread as, so to speak, the last and the greatest of all—many in the history of the nations, and much greater. For the Church is the sun and the moon and the stars, to which it was said, *Fair as the moon, chosen as the sun*[^69]. By this our Joseph is adored in this world, as in Egypt, exalted from the most humble estate: for that Joseph's mother certainly could not adore him, who died before Jacob came to his son, so that the truth of that prophetic dream might be reserved to be fulfilled in Christ the Lord. For when the sun shall be darkened, and the moon shall not give its light, and the stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens shall be moved, as this passage was recalled by the other two Evangelists, the Church will not appear; the impious persecutors then raging beyond measure, and, fear being removed, as though the felicity of the world were smiling, saying, *Peace and security*: then the stars shall fall from heaven, and the powers of the heavens shall be moved; since many who seemed to shine with grace will yield to the persecutors and fall, and indeed the most courageous of the faithful will be troubled. But for this reason, according to Matthew and Mark, it is said that this will be after the tribulation of those days, not because these things will happen when that whole persecution is past; but because the tribulation will precede, so that the falling away of some may follow: and because through all those days it will so come to pass, therefore it is "after the tribulation of those days," but nevertheless it will come to pass in those same days.

**11. 40.** What, then, was said according to Luke, *and on the earth distress of nations*[^70]—by "nations" he wished to be understood not those belonging to the seed of Abraham, in which all nations shall be blessed, but the nations which will stand on the left, when all nations shall be gathered before the Judge of the living and the dead. For both parties will be in all nations; one which oppresses, the other which is oppressed: one which says *Peace and security*; the other in which the sun is darkened, and the moon does not give its light, and from which the stars fall, and in which the powers of the heavens are moved.

**11. 41.** *And then they shall see the Son of man coming in a cloud with great power and majesty*[^71]. This I see can be taken in two ways: either as coming in the Church, as in a cloud, just as even now he does not cease to come, according to what he says, *Henceforth you shall see the Son of man sitting at the right hand of power, and coming in the clouds of heaven*[^72]—but then "with great power and majesty" for this reason, because his greater power and majesty will appear to the saints, to whom he will give great strength, lest they be overcome by so great a persecution: or as coming in his body, in which he sits at the right hand of the Father, in which also he died, and rose again, and ascended into heaven, according to what is written in the Acts of the Apostles, *When he had said these things, a cloud received him, and he was taken up from them*[^73]. And because there too it was said by the angels, *So shall he come, as you have seen him going into heaven*[^74], deservedly is he to be believed to be going to come not only in the same body, but also in a cloud; since he will come as he departed, and a cloud received him as he departed.

**11. 42.** But which of these two is rather to be chosen here, it is difficult to judge. The more ready sense indeed is that anyone, when he has heard or read, *And then they shall see the Son of man coming in a cloud with great power and majesty*, should take it straightway as his coming, not through the Church but through himself, when he is going to come to judge the living and the dead: but since the Scriptures must be searched, and we ought not to be content with their surface—which has been so modulated for our exercise that it wishes itself to be penetrated more deeply—the things that follow must be diligently inspected. For when he had said, *And then they shall see the Son of man coming in a cloud with great power and majesty*, he added and said, *But when these things begin to come to pass, look up and lift up your heads, for your redemption draws near. And he told them a parable: See the fig tree and all the trees; when they now put forth fruit from themselves, you know that summer is near: so also you, when you see these things come to pass, know that the kingdom of God is near*[^75]. When, then, he says, *When you see these things come to pass*, what shall we be able to understand but those things which were recalled above? But among these is also what he says, *And then they shall see the Son of man coming in a cloud with great power and majesty*. Accordingly, even when this has been seen, it will not yet be the kingdom of God, but it will be near.

**11. 43.** This order we see the other two evangelists too keep. For in Mark, when it had been said, *And the powers that are in the heavens shall be moved*, *And then*, he says, *they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds with much power and glory*[^76]. Then he subjoins what Luke did not say: *And then he shall send his angels, and gather his elect from the four winds, from the uttermost part of the earth to the uttermost part of heaven*[^77]. Then, what the former set down about the fig tree and the other trees, this one, setting down about the fig tree alone: *But from the fig tree learn the parable. When its branch is now tender, and the leaves are put forth, you know that summer is near: so also you, when you see these things come to pass, know that it is near, at the doors*[^78]. What is *When you see these things come to pass*, but those things which he said above? Among which is also that which he says, *And then they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds with much power and glory: and then he shall send his angels, and gather his elect*. Not, then, will the end be then, but then it will be near.

**11. 44.** Or must it be said that not all the things recalled above are to be understood where he says, *When you see these things come to pass*, but some of them—this, namely, being excepted, which was said, *And then they shall see the Son of man coming*, etc.? For this will be the very end, not then will it be near. But Matthew made clear that what was set down, *When you see these things come to pass*, is to be taken with none excepted: for in him too, when it had been said, *And the powers of the heavens shall be moved; and then*, he says, *shall appear the sign of the Son of man in heaven, and then shall all the tribes of the earth mourn. And they shall see the Son of man coming in the clouds of heaven with much power and majesty: and he shall send his angels with a trumpet and a great voice, and they shall gather his elect from the four winds, from the heights of the heavens to their ends. But from the fig tree learn the parable. When its branch is now tender, and the leaves put forth, you know that summer is near: so also you, when you see all these things, know that it is near, at the doors*[^79].

**11. 45.** Then, therefore, we know it is near, when we have seen not some of these things, but all these things, among which is also this, that the Son of man will be seen coming, and he will send his angels, and from the four parts of the world—that is, from the whole globe of the earth—gather his elect; which the whole last hour does, he coming in his members as in clouds, or in the whole Church itself, which is his body, as in a great cloud bearing fruit and increasing in the whole world, from the time he began to preach and to say, *Repent; for the kingdom of heaven has drawn near*[^80]: so that perhaps all the things said by these three evangelists about his coming, more diligently compared with one another and discussed, may be found to pertain to this, that he comes daily in his body, which is the Church (of which coming of his he said, *Henceforth you shall see the Son of man sitting at the right hand of power, and coming in the clouds of heaven*[^81]); except those places where that last coming, in himself, when he is going to judge the living and the dead, is so promised by him that it is said to draw near, and where, at the end of the discourse according to Matthew, that very coming is evidently expressed, whose nearness is shown above to be understood by certain signs. For to this the discourse itself is concluded in Matthew: *But when the Son of man comes*, he says, *in his majesty, and all the angels with him; then shall he sit upon the seat of his majesty, then shall all nations be gathered before him*[^82]; and the rest, up to that point where he says, *And these shall go into eternal punishment; but the righteous into eternal life*[^83]. For no one doubts that this was foretold concerning Christ's last coming and concerning the end of the age. For about those five and five virgins too there were not lacking those who, by no contemptible disputation, wished to teach that they pertain to this coming of his which now comes to pass through the Church. But these things must not be rashly promised, lest something occur which strongly contradicts—especially because, in such obscurities of the divine words, by which it has pleased God to exercise our understandings, not only is one moved more acutely than another among those who treat the holy Scriptures not improbably; but even any single one of them sometimes understands less, sometimes better.

**12. 46.** I do not know, however, whether we could behold anything more certain in this question, by any reasoning or means—if we could by any—than what I have already set down in the earlier letter, namely, when the whole world will be filled with the Gospel. For that your Veneration supposes this was already done through the very Apostles, I have proved by sure proofs is not so. For there are among us—that is, in Africa—innumerable barbarian nations, among whom the Gospel has not yet been preached, as we can readily learn daily from those who are led captive from there and now mingle with the servitude of the Romans. Yet it is only a few years since some of them—a very few and rare ones, who, made peaceful, cleave to the Roman borders in such a way that they do not have their own kings, but prefects are set over them by the Roman empire—both they and their very prefects began to be Christians. But the interior ones, who are under no Roman power, are altogether not held by the Christian religion in any of their own, nor can it in any way rightly be said that these do not pertain to the promise of God.

**12. 47.** For not the Romans, but all the nations, did the Lord promise to the seed of Abraham, with an oath also intervening: from which promise it has already come to pass that some nations which are not held under Roman dominion have received the Gospel, and been joined to the Church, which bears fruit and increases in the whole world. For it still has wherein to increase, until that comes to pass which was prophesied of Christ in the figure of Solomon: *He shall have dominion from sea to sea, and from the river to the ends of the earth*[^84]. From the river, namely, where he was baptized, because from there he began to preach the Gospel: but "from sea to sea" is the whole globe with all nations, since the whole is girded by the ocean sea. But how otherwise will that prophecy be fulfilled, *All nations, as many as you have made, shall come and adore before you, O Lord*[^85]? For they will not come by migrating from their own places, but by believing in their own places. For about believers the Lord said, *No one can come to me, unless it be given him by my Father*[^86]. But the prophet says, *And they shall adore him, each one from his own place, all the islands of the nations*[^87]. He said "all the islands," as though he said, "even all the islands"; hence showing how no land is left where the Church is not, when no island is left—of which some are situated even in the Ocean, and some of them we have learned have already received the Gospel. And thus in each single island that is fulfilled which was said, *He shall have dominion from sea to sea*, by which each island is girded; just as in the whole globe of lands, which is, as it were, in a manner the greatest of all islands, because the Ocean girds it too, to whose shores in the western parts we now know the Church has reached, and to whatever of the shores it has not yet reached, it surely will reach, by bearing fruit and increasing.

**12. 48.** If, then, because the prophecy of truth cannot lie, it is necessary that all nations, as many as God has made, adore him, how will they adore him unless they call upon him? But how shall they call upon him in whom they have not believed? Or how shall they believe him whom they have not heard? But how shall they hear without one who preaches? Or how shall they preach unless they be sent? For he sends his angels, and gathers his elect from the four winds, that is, from the whole globe of lands. Among those nations, then, in which the Church is not yet, it behooves that it be—not that all who are there should believe; for all nations are promised, not all the people of all nations: for faith is not of all. Every nation, therefore, believes in all who were elect before the foundation of the world, in the rest it does not believe, and it hates the believers. For how will that too be fulfilled, *You shall be hated by all nations for my name's sake*[^88], unless in all nations there are both those who hate and those whom they hate?

**12. 49.** In what way, then, was this preaching completed by the Apostles, when there are still up to now nations (which is most certain to us) in which it has just begun, and in which it has not yet begun, to be fulfilled? It was not, therefore, said to the Apostles, *You shall be witnesses to me in Jerusalem, and in all Judea, and Samaria, and to the uttermost part of the earth*[^89], as though they alone, to whom he was then speaking, were going to fulfill so great an office: but just as he seems to have said to them alone what he said, *Behold, I am with you until the consummation of the age*[^90]—which yet who does not understand he promised to the whole Church, which, some dying, others being born, will be here until the consummation of the age? Just as he said to them that too which does not pertain to them at all, and yet was so said as though it pertained to them alone: *When you see all these things, know that it is near, at the doors*[^91]: for to whom does this pertain, if not to those who will then be in the flesh, when all things will be fulfilled? How much more that which was for the great part to be done through them, although the same action was reserved for their successors too?

**12. 50.** But what the Apostle said, *Have they not heard? Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their words to the ends of the world*[^92]—although he spoke with words of past time, yet he said what was going to be, not what was already done and completed; just as the very prophet whom he used as witness did not say, "Their sound shall go out into all the earth," but, *Their sound has gone out*[^93], which surely had not yet been done: such as is also that, *They have dug my hands and feet*[^94], which we know was done so long afterward. But lest we believe these only to have been prophetic ways of speaking, and not also apostolic ones, does not the same Apostle himself say: *Which is the Church of the living God, the pillar and ground of the truth. And without doubt great is the sacrament of piety, which was manifested in the flesh, justified in the spirit, appeared to angels, was preached among the nations, was believed in the world, was taken up in glory*[^95]? Surely it is manifest that this, which he set last, even now is not yet completed; how much more then, when he was saying these things? For the Church will then be taken up in glory, when it will be said, *Come, you blessed of my Father, receive the kingdom*[^96]: and yet it was said as though it had been done, what was certainly known to be going to be.

**12. 51.** Much less, then, must it be wondered at that he used also words of present time in that which you have recalled he likewise said: *Because of the hope which is laid up for you, which you heard before in the word of the truth of the Gospel, which has come to you, as it is also in all the world, bearing fruit and increasing*[^97], although the Gospel did not yet hold the whole world: but he said it was bearing fruit and increasing in the whole world, that he might thus signify how far it was going to come by bearing fruit and increasing. If, then, it is hidden when the whole world, altogether from sea to sea, will be filled, the Church bearing fruit and increasing, doubtless it is hidden when the end will be; for it will not be before.

**13. 52.** But, to open to you, as to a holy man of God and a most sincere brother, what I think about this question: error indeed must be avoided in both directions, as far as it can be avoided by a man—whether the Lord be believed going to come sooner or later than he is going to come; but to me no one seems to err when he knows that he does not know something, but when he thinks he knows what he does not know. Let us, therefore, remove from our midst that evil servant who, saying in his heart, *My lord delays his coming*, lords it over his fellow servants, and mingles in feasting with the drunken: for he doubtless hates the coming of his Lord. That evil servant being removed, let us set before our eyes three good servants, treating the Lord's household diligently and soberly, thirstily desiring the coming of their Lord, watchfully awaiting it, faithfully loving it: if one of them thinks the Lord will come sooner, another later, but the third confesses his ignorance about this matter; although all are in harmony with the Gospel, since all love the manifestation of the Lord, and desiringly and watchfully await it, let us nevertheless see which is more in harmony.

**13. 53.** One says: "Let us watch and pray, because the Lord will come sooner." Another says: "Let us watch and pray, because this life is short and uncertain, although the Lord will come later." The third says: "Let us watch and pray, because this life is both short and uncertain, and we do not know the time when the Lord will come." The Gospel says: *See, watch, and pray; for you do not know when the time is*[^98]. I beg you, what else do we hear this third one say than what we hear the Gospel say? All indeed, out of desire for the kingdom of God, wish that to be true which the first one says; but the second denies this; the third, however, denies none of these, but confesses that he does not know which of those speaks the truth. Accordingly, if that comes to pass which the first foretold, the second and the third will rejoice with him; for all love the manifestation of the Lord. They will exult, then, that what they love has come sooner. But if it does not come to pass, and it begins to appear that what the second was saying is rather true, it is to be feared lest, amid the very delays, those who had believed what the first had said be disturbed, and begin to think the Lord's coming not late, but none at all: which—you see how great is the destruction of souls. But if they are of such great faith that they turn to the predictions of the second, and faithfully and patiently await the Lord even as he delays; there will nevertheless abound reproaches and insults and mockeries of the enemies turning many of the weak away from the Christian faith, by saying that the kingdom was as deceitfully promised to them as it was deceitfully said to be coming soon. But those who believe what the second says, that the Lord will come later, if it is found false, the Lord coming sooner; in no way will those who had believed him be troubled in faith, but will enjoy an unexpected joy.

**13. 54.** Wherefore he who says the Lord will come sooner speaks more desirably, but is dangerously deceived. Would, then, that it be true; because it will be grievous, if it be not true! But he who says the Lord will come later, and yet believes, hopes, loves his coming, surely, even if he is deceived about his delay, is happily deceived: for he will have greater patience, if it will be so; greater gladness, if it will not be. And thereby, by those who love the manifestation of the Lord, the former is heard more sweetly, the latter believed more safely: but he who confesses that he is ignorant which of these is true, desires the one, tolerates the other, errs in none of them, because he neither affirms nor denies any of them. I beg you not to despise me, being such a one; because I too love you, who affirm that which I desire to be true, and so much the more do I not wish you to be deceived, the more I love what you promise, and the more I see it dangerous if you are deceived. Grant pardon, if I have been burdensome to your holy feelings: for the more rarely it happens, the more it has delighted me to speak with you longer, at least by letter.
