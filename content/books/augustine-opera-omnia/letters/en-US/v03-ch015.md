# Letter 170

To the excellent lord, and deservedly honorable and religious brother Maximus, from Alypius and Augustine, greetings in the Lord.

**1.** When we inquired of our holy brother and fellow bishop Peregrinus especially about the salvation, not corporeal but spiritual, of yourself and of your people, his answers made us glad about you, but sad about your people, because they are not joined to the catholic Church by a salutary correction. And because we hoped this would soon come to pass, we greatly grieve that it has not yet been done, excellent lord, and deservedly honorable and religious brother.

**2.** Accordingly, greeting your charity in the peace of the Lord, we both enjoin and ask that you not delay to teach those people what you have learned; namely, that there is one God alone, to whom is owed that service which by the Greek word is called *latreia*. For the very word is in the law, where it is written: *You shall adore the Lord your God, and him alone shall you serve*[^1]: whom if we should say to be the Father only, it will be answered, Then *latreia* is not owed to the Son; which it is wicked to say. But if it is owed, how then is it owed to one God only, if it is owed both to the Father and the Son, except because the one God, whom alone we are bidden to serve by *latreia*, is so called the one God that both the Father is understood and the Son, indeed even also the Holy Spirit? For of him the Apostle says: *Do you not know that your bodies are the temple of the Holy Spirit who is in you, whom you have from God, and you are not your own? For you were bought with a great price. Glorify God in your body*[^2]: what God, but the Holy Spirit, whose temple he had said our bodies were? *Latreia*, therefore, is owed to the Holy Spirit. For if we were bidden to make a temple for him, as Solomon made one of wood and stones, surely in making the temple we would be convicted of offering *latreia*: how much more do we owe *latreia* to him for whom we do not make a temple, but are one!

**3.** And through this, if *latreia* is owed to the Father and the Son and the Holy Spirit, and is offered by us—of which it was said, *You shall adore the Lord your God, and him alone shall you serve*—without doubt the Lord our God, whom alone we ought to serve by *latreia*, is not the Father alone, nor the Son alone, nor the Holy Spirit alone, but the Trinity itself, one God alone, Father, and Son, and Holy Spirit: not that the Father is himself who is the Son, or the Holy Spirit is himself either Father or Son, since in that Trinity the Father is Father of the Son alone, and the Son is Son of the Father alone, but the Holy Spirit is the Spirit both of the Father and of the Son; but on account of the one and the same nature and inseparable life, the Trinity itself, as far as a man can, faith going before, is understood to be the one Lord our God, of whom it was said, *You shall adore the Lord your God, and him alone shall you serve*, and whom the Apostle proclaims; for he says: *For from him, and in him, and through him are all things: to him be glory forever and ever. Amen*[^3].

**4.** For the only-begotten Son is not from God the Father in the way that the whole creation is from him, which he created from nothing. For him he begot of his own substance, he did not make from nothing: nor did he beget him in time, through whom he founded all times. For just as a flame does not precede in time the brightness which it begets, so the Father never was without the Son. For he is the Wisdom of God the Father, of which it is written: *For he is the brightness of eternal light*[^4]. To the light, then, whose brightness he is, that is, to God the Father, he is without doubt coeternal: and therefore not as *in the beginning God made heaven and earth*[^5], so in the beginning he made the Word; but *in the beginning was the Word*[^6]. The Holy Spirit too was not made from nothing as a creature: but so proceeds from the Father and the Son that he was made neither by the Son nor by the Father.

**5.** This Trinity is of one and the same nature and substance: not less in the individual persons than in all, nor greater in all than in the individual ones; but as great in the Father alone, or in the Son alone, as in the Father and Son together; and as great in the Holy Spirit alone as together in the Father and Son and Holy Spirit. For the Father, that he might have the Son from himself, did not diminish himself; but so begot from himself another self, that he remained whole in himself, and was in the Son as great as he was even alone. Likewise the Holy Spirit too, whole from the whole, does not precede that from which he proceeds, but is as great with him as he is from him, neither diminishing him by proceeding, nor increasing him by cleaving to him. And all these are neither confusedly one, nor disjointedly three: but since they are one, they are three, and since they are three, they are one. Accordingly, he who granted to so many hearts of his faithful to be one heart, how much more does he preserve in himself that these three, both individually God, and all together not three gods, but one God! This is the one Lord our God, who is served with all piety, to whom alone that *latreia* is owed.

**6.** Since he, in things which are born in time, has by his goodness brought it about that any thing should beget offspring of its own substance—as man begets man, not of another nature, but of that of which he himself is—see how impiously it is said that he himself did not beget that which he himself is. For these are names of relationship, not of nature, and therefore are said toward something, or relatively, which are sometimes the same, sometimes diverse. The same, namely, when brother is referred to brother, friend to friend, neighbor to neighbor, kinsman to kinsman, and any like terms, which it is endless to wish to run through all. For in these, what this one is to that one, that the other is to this one. But they are diverse, as father to son, son to father, father-in-law to son-in-law, son-in-law to father-in-law, master to servant, servant to master. This one is not indeed to that one what that one is to this one; but both are nevertheless men: the relation is diverse, not the nature. For if you attend to what each is to the other, this one is not to that one what that one is to this one: because this one is father, that one son; or this one father-in-law, that one son-in-law; or this one master, that one servant. But if you attend to what each is to himself, or in himself, the one is what the other is; because the one is man as the other is. Whence your prudence understands that it is not reasonably said by those from whose error the Lord freed you, that the nature of God the Father and of God the Son is therefore diverse, because the one is Father, the other Son; and that God the Father did not beget that which he himself is, because he did not beget the Father of his Son, which he himself is to him: for who does not see that these words do not show natures in themselves, but signify the persons of one to another?

**7.** Such too is that which they say with a like error, that the Son is therefore of another nature, and of a diverse substance, because the Father is not God from another God, but the Son is indeed God, but from God the Father: for here too not substance is indicated, but origin, that is, not what each is, but whence each is or is not. For Abel and Adam were not therefore not of one nature and substance, because the one was a man from that man, the other from no one. If, then, the nature of each is asked, Abel is a man, Adam is a man: but if the origin, the first man from whom Abel is, no man from whom Adam is. So in God the Father and God the Son, if the nature of each be asked, each is God, nor is the one more God than the other: but if the origin, the Father is the God from whom is God the Son; but from whom the Father is, there is no God.

**8.** In vain, then, trying to answer this, they say: But man begets with passion, while God begot the Son without passion. For this not only helps them in nothing, but helps us very much. For if to temporal and passible things God granted that they should beget what they are, how much more did he himself, eternal and impassible, beget not other than what he himself is, one the only one; to our unspeakable admiration on this account, since without passion, and with so great equality with himself, that he preceded him neither in power nor in age! But for this reason the whole that he has, that he can, he attributes not to himself but to the Father, because he is not from himself, but from the Father. For he is equal to the Father, but this too he received from the Father; nor did he so receive that whence he is equal, as though he were first unequal; but born equal, as always born, so always equal. He did not, then, beget an unequal one, and add equality to one already born; but in begetting he gave it, because he begot an equal, not an unequal. Therefore *to be in the form of God equal to God was not robbery*[^7] to him, but nature; since he took this by being born, not presumed it by being proud.

**9.** But he says that the Father is greater for this reason: because *he emptied himself, taking the form of a servant*[^8], not losing the form of God: on account of which form of a servant, he was made less not only than the Father, but even than himself and the Holy Spirit; nor only than this most excellent Trinity, but he was even made *a little less than the angels*[^9]; and he was less than men too, when he was subject to his parents. On account of this form of a servant, surely, which, the fullness of time coming, having emptied himself he received, he said: *The Father is greater than I*[^10]. But on account of that form which, even emptied, he did not lose, he said: *I and the Father are one*[^11]; both made man, that is, and remaining God: for the man was assumed by God, God was not consumed in the man. Therefore very reasonably the man Christ is both less than the Father, and the same Christ, God, is equal to the Father.

**10.** Since, then, we rejoice that you, in our presence and with great exultation of the people of God, have been joined to this right and catholic faith, why are we still sad about the sluggishness of your people? We beseech you, through the mercy of God, that with his help you take away this grief from our hearts. For it is not to be believed that your authority could avail much for the perversity of your people, and avail nothing for their correction. Or do they perhaps despise you, because you came into the participation of the catholic Church at this age, when they ought rather to admire and venerate you, because you conquered a most ancient error by a kind of senile youth? Far be it that they should resist you speaking true things, who had consented to you straying from the truth; far be it that they should be unwilling to think rightly with you, with whom it delighted them to err. Only pray for them, and press upon them. Nay rather, bring them with you to the house of God, who are with you in your house; or let it not irk you to come into the house of God with those who had been wont to gather in your house; especially since the catholic mother both asks some of you, and asks back others: she asks those whom she found with you; she asks back those whom she lost through you. Let her not be tortured by losses, but rather rejoice in gains: let her acquire children she did not have, not bewail those she had. We pray to God that you may do what we exhort; and we hope of his mercy that, by the letters of our holy brother and fellow bishop Peregrinus, and by the replies of your charity, our heart will soon be filled with joy about this matter, and our tongue with exultation.
