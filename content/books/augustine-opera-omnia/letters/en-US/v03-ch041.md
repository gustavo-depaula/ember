# Letter 192

To the venerable and exceedingly longed-for lord, holy brother and fellow deacon Celestine, from Augustine, greetings in the Lord.

**1.** Although I was far absent when, through Proiectus the cleric, the writings of your holiness directed to me came to Hippo; yet after I came, and, having read them, recognized that I had been made a debtor of a reply, I awaited a time for rendering it: and behold, suddenly there occurred the most pleasing occasion of our most dear brother Albinus the acolyte, about to set out from us. Rejoicing, then, at your well-being, which is most longed-for to me, I render to your holiness the greeting due. But I always owe love, which alone, even when rendered, always holds the debtor. For it is rendered when it is bestowed, but is owed even if it has been rendered; because there is no time when it is not now to be bestowed. Nor, when it is rendered, is it lost, but rather by rendering it is multiplied: for it is rendered by having, not by lacking. And since it cannot be rendered unless it be had, nor can be had unless it be rendered: nay rather, even when it is rendered by a man, it grows in the man; and so much the greater is acquired, the more it is rendered to. But how is that denied to friends, which is owed even to enemies? But to enemies it is bestowed cautiously, to friends it is repaid securely. It acts, however, as much as it can, that even from those to whom it repays good for evil, it may receive what it bestows. For we desire that one to become a friend whom we truly love as an enemy: because we do not love him unless we wish him good; which surely he will not have, unless he lose the evil of his enmities.

**2.** Love, then, is not bestowed as money. For besides that by bestowing the latter is diminished, the former is increased; they differ between themselves also in this, that, the money to whomever we give, we will then be more benevolent to him, if we do not seek to receive it back: but one cannot be a true bestower of love, unless he be a kindly exactor. Since money, when it is received, accrues to him to whom it is given, but departs from him by whom it is given: but love not only grows in him who exacts it from one whom he loves, even if he does not receive it; but also that one from whom he receives it then begins to have it when he renders it. Accordingly, lord brother, I gladly render to you mutual love, and joyfully receive it: which I receive, I still demand again; which I render, I still owe. For one Master, with whom we are fellow disciples, we ought, through his Apostle, to hear as docile, commanding and saying: *Owe no one anything, except that you love one another*[^1].

[^1]: Rom 13:8
