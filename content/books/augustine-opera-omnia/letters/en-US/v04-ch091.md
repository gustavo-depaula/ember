# Letter 26\*

TO THE MOST BLESSED LORD, DESERVEDLY VENERABLE, THE HOLY BROTHER AND FELLOW BISHOP HONORIUS, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD

**1.** A certain Donantius of Suppa—his father being established at Hippo and passing his life on the ecclesiastical alms—had begun to be in our monastery; and when he could in no way endure there, the old man Antiphus of blessed memory, not knowing his case, by stealth had him ordained a deacon, contrary to the episcopal statutes of the councils. But when the said venerable old man had learned this through my letters, he soon restored him to me, removed from his place; and I, lest by his burden he should offer the rest an example of perdition, had somehow provided that he be a doorkeeper at the church of holy Theogenes, having nothing from which to live; nor was he able even there to keep his office worthily, so that, in my absence, he was thrown out from there by the priests.

**2.** Now his fellow citizens ask me to make him a deacon—a man who for so many years has been in communion among the laity in the congregation, O most blessed lord, deservedly venerable, holy brother. Hence, since they do not know what they are requesting, I admonish your Holiness—of whose modesty and vigilance, by the Lord's gift, I am secure, since you do not permit him, to the ruin of the discipline by which the servants of God must be governed, to insult episcopal councils—that, if he should wish to live in his own city (which I do not forbid), you order nothing more to be granted to him, except perhaps the office of reader, which we recall is wont to be granted even to laymen where it is necessary. For if you should judge that he ought to be something more, I would certainly not begrudge him my own nurturing or that of the Church of God. About his character, however, I do not wish to say anything, since for the case it suffices what I thought should be said; and would that, by the Lord's mercy, he so live that there be no complaint about him—it is enough for him that, serving God in a humble place, he reach his promises—lest, when he has striven as it were for greater things, he gain for himself and for those through whom he strives the offense of God, by struggling to break what has been wholesomely established for the preservation of discipline.
