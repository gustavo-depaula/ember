# Letter 1\*

TO CLASSICIANUS, A DISTINGUISHED AND MOST EXCELLENT AND MOST BELOVED LORD AND SON, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** The letter of your Love gravely saddened me, and, long fluctuating as to what I should write back, counsel, I confess, was lacking to me, and there occurred to my mind what is written: *If you have counsel, answer your neighbor; if not, let your hand be over your mouth*[^1]. And would that, from the occasion of your case, something might be decreed by the bishops, which we ought thereafter to follow in such cases. But now indeed there are no decrees of councils, or, if perhaps there are, they escape me; nevertheless examples are not lacking that men have been anathematized—not only those alone who seemed worthy of such correction, but along with their whole household, although they themselves committed nothing of the kind—nor has any of the bishops who did this been accused or compelled to render reasons for it, doing in the flock of Christ what he judged expedient for the sheep entrusted to his dispensation. But as far as concerns the holy Scriptures, sinners are found to have paid the punishment of the same sin along with all their own who did not sin, but in the time of the Old Testament, when that punishment was bodily, not spiritual. For although each wicked man was killed in the flesh along with his own who had not been partakers of the crime, bodies someday to die died in all, to strike great fear into the rest, lest they so die that none of their own be left after them; yet the punishment of the soul always pertained to him alone, whoever sinned, according to the sentence of the Lord speaking through the Prophet: *The soul of the father is mine, and the soul of the son is mine, the soul that has sinned, it will die*[^2]. In the time, therefore, of the New Testament, when a spiritual punishment was evidently established in the Church, Christ saying: *What you loose on earth will be loosed in heaven; what you bind on earth will be bound in heaven*[^3], I do not know how it is rightly done that father and son be bound at the same time, when the father has sinned and the son has not sinned; how much more a wife, a slave, a maidservant, a little boy, a little girl, and absolutely the whole household; in which, even if anyone is born who, when the household was anathematized, had not yet been born, it would be too little for him to draw original sin, according to which in the one Adam all have sinned[^4], but he will find a new guilt through another's iniquity which was committed before he beheld the light, and—what is more cruel—if he come into the peril of death, he be not helped by the Sacrament of baptism. **2.** Since these things vehemently move me, I have never at all done anything of the kind; and I know very many of my brothers and colleagues, whether preceding, or contemporary in the episcopate, or following, to be in this opinion which is also mine. But we know that certain men, placed even on the summit of a secular power higher than yours, were anathematized by bishops along with their whole household, and that these neither raised any question about this deed, nor came to other bishops with complaints, but rather came, with satisfaction, to those by whom they had been bound, that, pardon received, they might be loosed. These things I have said as to why counsel is lacking to me to answer your Excellence anything definite to the things you wrote, that, even if [you yourself] sinned, you were anathematized by the bishops not [yourself] alone, but along with your whole household, lord deservedly distinguished and most excellent and most beloved son. **3.** But now, when I consider the case which you intimated to me by letter, on account of which it is inquired whether even you yourself alone were worthy of such coercion, I do not find your fault, if you narrated true things. For those who had deceived you by forswearing, and had deceived their own surety by despising the Sacraments of Christ, and, that they might break faith with impunity, had fled to the house of faith itself—if this wickedness, this crime, this impiety they corrected not by being violently dragged from the church, but by going out themselves of their own accord, although you yourself came to the church accompanied by soldiers, without whom you could not be on account of the administration of your power, [and] said to the bishop what grief compelled you to say, that the faithless not be favored against the surety, and that against him who believes those who swear by the Gospel, those rather who forswore against the Gospel not be defended in that place where, with the greatest authority and veneration, that faith may be held and kept, the Gospel itself is read—you sinned nothing at all. **4.** But pardon me, I beg, if I keep most fair ears for the other side, and do not easily believe your Sublimity against the holiness of a bishop. You lose nothing indeed—nay rather, you acquire very much by the merit of devout humility—by asking pardon of the bishop, if perhaps, in the altercation which you signified you had with him, you said something which it neither befitted you to say nor him to hear. **5.** But I, on account of those who, for the sin of one soul, bind the whole household of it, that is, very many souls, with anathema—especially lest anyone there depart from the body without baptism, and whether those too are not to be expelled from the church who flee there, that they may break faith with their sureties—desire, with the Lord helping, both to deal with it in our council, and, if it be needful, to write to the Apostolic See, so that in these cases what we ought to follow may be established and confirmed by the concordant authority of all. This indeed I would not rashly say, that, if any of the faithful has been anathematized unjustly, it will rather harm him who does it than him who suffers this injury. For the Holy Spirit dwelling in the saints, through whom each one is bound or loosed, inflicts an undeserved punishment on no one; for through him love is poured out in our hearts, which *does not act wrongly*[^5]. **6.** But I have written to the same brother and my fellow bishop, your bishop, as I thought I should write, long constrained by the great straits of deliberation, whether I ought to do this. But if this does not suffice for your Love, grant pardon; for I judged that I ought not to do more.

[^1]: See Sir 5:12
[^2]: Ezek 18:4
[^3]: See Matt 18:18
[^4]: See Rom 5:12
[^5]: See Rom 5:5; 1 Cor 13:4-5
