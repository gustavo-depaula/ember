# Letter 10

Augustine to Nebridius

**1.** Never has any of your inquiries held me so feverish in thought as that which I read in your most recent letter, where you charge us with neglecting to take counsel that we might be allowed to live together. A great accusation, and, were it not false, a most dangerous one. But since a probable argument seems to show that we can live here according to our wish rather than at Carthage or even in the country, I am altogether uncertain, my Nebridius, what to do with you. Should a most suitable carriage be sent to you? For our friend Lucinianus assures us that you could be conveyed safely in a litter. But I think of my mother, who could not bear my absence when she was well, and would bear it far less when she is weak. Should I myself come to you? But here there are those who could not come with me, and whom I think it wrong to abandon. For you are able to dwell pleasantly even in your own mind; but that these men may do the same is being worked at. Should I go to and fro often, and be now with you, now with them? But this is to live neither together nor according to our wish. For the road is not short, but altogether so long that often to undertake the business of completing it is not to have arrived at the longed-for rest. Add to this the weakness of the body, by which I too, as you know, cannot do what I wish, unless I cease altogether to wish anything more than I am able to do.

**2.** Therefore to think about journeys, which you cannot have quiet and easy, through one's whole life is not the mark of a man who thinks about that one last journey which is called death—about which alone you understand that we must truly think. God has indeed granted to a certain few, whom he willed to be governors of the churches, both to await that journey not only bravely but even to desire it eagerly, and to undertake the labors of carrying it out without any anguish. But neither to those who are dragged toward administrations of this kind by love of temporal honor, nor again to those who, though they are private men, seek a busy life, do I think this so great a good is granted: that amid the din and the restless gatherings and comings and goings they should attain that familiarity with death which we seek; for to both groups it was possible to be made godlike in leisure. Or if this is false, I am of all men—not to say the most foolish—at least the most idle, since unless a certain secure rest comes to me, I cannot taste and love that pure good. There is need, believe me, of a great withdrawal from the tumult of fleeting things, so that it may come about in a man, not by hardness, not by audacity, not by desire for empty glory, not by superstitious credulity, that he fear nothing. For from this comes also that solid joy which is to be compared with no other joys, not in any single part.

**3.** But if such a life does not fall within human nature, why does this security sometimes come? Why does it come the more often, the more each person adores God in the innermost recesses of his mind? Why does this tranquillity for the most part remain even in human action, if each one proceeds to act from that inner sanctuary? Why do we sometimes, even when we are speaking, not dread death, but when we are not speaking, even desire it? I say to you—for I would not say this to just anyone—to you, I say, whose journeys into the heavenly realms I know well: will you, when you have often experienced how sweetly one lives when the mind dies to corporeal love, deny in the end that the whole life of a man can become fearless, so that he may rightly be named wise? Or will you dare to assert that this disposition, on which reason rests, has ever befallen you, except when you are anguished in your inmost depths? Since this is so, you see that one thing remains: that you too take counsel for the common good, that we may live together. For what is to be done about your mother, whom surely brother Victor does not abandon, you understand much better than I. I did not want to write other things, lest I turn you away from this reflection.
