# Letter 244

TO CHRISIMUS, A TRULY AND DESERVEDLY MOST DEAR LORD, AND A PRAISEWORTHY BROTHER, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Rumor has reported to me (may God grant it not be so) that you are so disturbed in mind that I much wonder that your prudence and Christian spirit think too little of the condition of earthly things, which can in no way be equaled to the heavenly, where our heart and our hope are to be placed. Man of heart, was either your whole good in these things which you now seem to lose; or did you reckon there so great a good that, with it withdrawn, your mind is so darkened by excessive sadness, as though its light were not God, but earth? For I have heard (I have already said: may God grant that I heard what is false) that you even wished to lay hands on yourself; which I rather do not believe either ascended into your heart, or went forth from your mouth. But yet, because you are so disturbed that this could be said of you, gravely saddened about you, I thought your Charity should be consoled by this address of a letter; although I do not doubt that the Lord our God speaks better things in your heart: for I know with how devout a zeal you have always heard his word.

**2.** Lift up your mind, therefore, most dear brother in Christ; our God neither perishes from his own, nor will destroy his own: but he wishes to admonish us how fragile and uncertain are these things which men love too much, that we may loose from them the bond of desire, by which, entangled, they drag us, and accustom all our love to run toward him in whom we may fear no losses. He himself exhorts you, through our ministry, manfully to consider that you are a faithful Christian, and redeemed by the blood of him who taught us, not only by eternal wisdom but also by human presence, temperately to despise the prosperity of this world, and bravely to bear adversity; promising the reward of that happiness which no one can take away from us. I have indeed written also to the praiseworthy man the count; which letter it will be in your discretion whether you wish to be given. For one through whom it may be given, I do not doubt, cannot be lacking, with the Lord helping—whether a bishop, or a priest, or anyone.
