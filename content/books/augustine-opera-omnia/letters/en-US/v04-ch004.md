# Letter 213

ECCLESIASTICAL PROCEEDINGS

**1.** *In the consulship of the most glorious Theodosius for the twelfth time and of Valentinian Augustus for the second time, on the sixth day before the kalends of October, when Bishop Augustine had taken his seat, together with his fellow bishops Religianus and Martinianus, in the Church of Peace of Hippo Regius, in the presence of the priests Saturninus, Leporius, Barnabas, Fortunatianus, Rusticus, Lazarus, and Eraclius*, *with the clergy standing by and a crowded congregation, Bishop Augustine said:*  
What I promised your Charity yesterday—on account of which I wished you to gather more frequently, and I see that you have gathered more frequently—must be done now, all delay set aside. For if I should wish to speak of something else, you would, being in suspense about this, listen to it less. We are all mortal in this life, and the last day of this life is always uncertain for every person; yet in infancy childhood is hoped for, and in childhood adolescence is hoped for, and in adolescence youth is hoped for, and in youth maturity is hoped for, and in maturity old age is hoped for: whether it will come is uncertain, yet there is something to be hoped for. But old age has no other age to hope for: it is uncertain even how long old age itself may last for a person; this much, however, is certain, that no age remains that could succeed old age. Because God willed it, I came to this city in the vigor of my age; yet I was young, and I have grown old. I know that after the deaths of bishops the churches are wont to be disturbed by the ambitious or the contentious; and what I have often experienced and grieved over, I ought, as far as it concerns me, to provide against, lest it befall this city. As your Charity knows, I was lately in the church of Milevis: for the brothers, and especially the servants of God who are there, asked me to come, because after the death of my brother and fellow bishop Severus, of blessed memory, some disturbance was feared there. I came; and, as the Lord willed, he helped us by his mercy, so that they received in peace the bishop whom their bishop had designated while still alive: for when this had become known to them, they gladly embraced the will of their preceding and departed bishop. Yet something had been done too little, on account of which some were saddened, because brother Severus believed it could suffice to designate his successor before the clergy, and did not speak of it to the people; and from this there was some sadness among certain ones. What more? It pleased God; the sadness was put to flight, joy succeeded: the bishop was ordained whom the preceding bishop had designated. Therefore, lest anyone complain about me, I bring my will, which I believe to be God's, to the knowledge of you all: I want the priest Eraclius as my successor.  
*The people acclaimed: "Thanks be to God, praise to Christ"; this was said twenty-three times. "Hear, O Christ; long life to Augustine"; this was said sixteen times. "You our father, you our bishop"; this was said eight times.*

**2.** *And when there was silence, Bishop Augustine said:* There is no need for me to say anything about his praises; I defer to his wisdom, and I spare his modesty: it is enough that you know him; and I say that I want this which I know you want; and if I did not know it before, I would prove it today. This, therefore, I want: this I ask of the Lord our God, now even in the chill of age, with fervent prayers; this I exhort, admonish, and beg you to pray for with me, that, with the minds of all gathered and fused together in the peace of Christ, God may confirm what he has wrought in us. May he who sent him to me keep him; may he keep him unharmed, keep him without crime, so that he who gives joy to the living may fill the place of the dying. By the notaries of the church, as you see, what we say is taken down, and what you say is taken down; and my discourse and your acclamations do not fall to the ground. To say it more openly, we are now drawing up ecclesiastical proceedings; for I want this to be confirmed, as far as it concerns men, that it is so. *The people acclaimed thirty-six times: "Thanks be to God, praise to Christ." "Hear, O Christ; long life to Augustine"; this was said thirteen times. "You our father, you our bishop"; this was said eight times. "He is worthy and just"; this was said twenty times. "Well deserving, well worthy"; this was said five times. "He is worthy and just"; this was said six times.*

**3.** *And when there was silence, Bishop Augustine said:*  
Therefore, as I was saying, I want my will and your will confirmed by ecclesiastical proceedings, as far as it concerns men; but as far as it concerns the hidden will of the Almighty, let us all, as I have said, pray that God may confirm what he has wrought in us.  
*The people acclaimed: "We give thanks for your judgment"; this was said sixteen times. "So be it, so be it"; this was said twelve times. "You our father, Eraclius our bishop"; this was said six times.*

**4.** *And when there was silence, Bishop Augustine said:* I know what you too know, but I do not want done concerning him what was done concerning me. What was done, many of you know; only those do not know it who at that time either were not yet born or were not yet of an age to know. While my father and bishop, the aged Valerius of blessed memory, was still in the body, I was ordained bishop, and I sat with him: I did not know that this had been forbidden by the Council of Nicaea, nor did he himself know it. What therefore was found blameworthy in me, I do not want to be blameworthy in my son. *The people acclaimed: "Thanks be to God; praise to Christ"; this was said thirteen times.*

**5.** *And when there was silence, Bishop Augustine said:*  
He will be a priest as he is; when God wills, a bishop to come. But plainly I am now going to do, with the help of Christ's mercy, what until now I have not done. You know what I wished to do some years ago, and you did not permit it. It was agreed by me and by you, on account of the care of the Scriptures, which my brothers and fathers, my fellow bishops, deigned to lay upon me in two councils, of Numidia and of Carthage, that for five days no one should trouble me. The proceedings were drawn up, it was agreed, you acclaimed: your decision is read out, and your acclamations. For a short time it was observed in my regard; and afterward it was violently broken in upon, and I am not permitted to have leisure for what I wish: before noon and after noon I am entangled in the affairs of men. I beseech you and bind you by Christ to allow me to pour back the burdens of my occupations upon this young man, that is, upon the priest Eraclius, whom today, in the name of Christ, I designate bishop as my successor.  
*The people acclaimed: "We give thanks for your judgment"; this was said twenty-six times.*

**6.** *And when there was silence, Bishop Augustine said:*  
I give thanks for your charity and goodwill before the Lord our God—nay rather, I give thanks to God for it. Therefore, brothers, whatever it is that used to be brought to me, let it be brought to him: where he has need of counsel, I will not deny my help; far be it that I should withhold it. Yet whatever it is that used to be brought to me, let it be brought to him. Let him either consult me, if perhaps he does not find what he ought to do, or call for the helper whom he knows as a father: so that both nothing may be lacking to you, and I, at last someday, if God grants me some little span of this life, may give my own little life, such as it is, not to sloth, nor surrender it to idleness, but exercise it in the holy Scriptures, as far as he himself permits and grants; this will profit him too, and through him also you. Let no one, then, begrudge my leisure, for my leisure has great business. I see that concerning this matter, for which I invited you, I have done with you all that I ought. This last thing I ask: that those of you who can deign to subscribe to these proceedings. Here I have need of your response: let me hold your response; about this assent acclaim something.  
*The people acclaimed: "So be it, so be it"; this was said twenty-five times. "It is worthy, it is just"; this was said twenty-eight times. "So be it, so be it"; this was said fourteen times. "Long worthy, long deserving"; this was said twenty-five times. "We give thanks for your judgment"; this was said thirteen times. "Hear, O Christ; preserve Eraclius"; this was said eighteen times.*

**7.** *And when there was silence, Bishop Augustine said:*  
It is well that we may be able to do the things that are God's around his sacrifice; in which hour of our supplication I especially commend to your Charity that you set aside all your affairs and business, and pour out a prayer to the Lord for this church, and for me, and for the priest Eraclius.
