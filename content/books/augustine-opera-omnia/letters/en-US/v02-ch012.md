# Letter 111

To the most beloved and most longed-for lord, brother and fellow priest Victorianus, Augustine sends greetings in the Lord.

**1.** Your letter has filled our heart with great grief, by which you asked that I answer something in a lengthy work; whereas for such evils lengthy groans and weeping are owed rather than lengthy books. For the whole world is afflicted with such great disasters that there is scarcely any part of the lands where such things as you wrote of are not committed and lamented. For a little while ago, even in those solitudes of Egypt, where monasteries separated from all din had chosen, as it were, security, the brethren were killed by barbarians. And now what wicked things have lately been perpetrated in the regions of Italy, what in Gaul, I do not think are hidden even from you; from the so many provinces of Spain too, which long seemed untouched by these evils, such things have already begun to be reported. But why do we go far? Behold, in our own region of Hippo, since the barbarians have not reached it, the brigandages of the Donatist clerics and Circumcellions so lay waste the churches that the deeds of the barbarians are perhaps milder. For what barbarian could have devised what these men did, to put lime and vinegar into the eyes of our clerics, whose other limbs too they have wounded with horrible blows and injuries? They also plunder and burn some houses, seize the dry crops, pour out the moist, and, threatening such things to the rest, even compel many to be rebaptized. The day before I dictated this to you, from one place, through terrors of this kind, forty-eight souls were reported to me as rebaptized.

**2.** These things are to be lamented, not wondered at, and we must cry out to God that he free us from such great evils, not according to our merits but according to his mercy. For what indeed was to be hoped for the human race, when these things were foretold so long before, both in the Prophets and in the Gospel? We ought not, then, to be so contrary to ourselves that we believe when they are read, and complain when they are fulfilled: but rather even those who were unbelieving, when they read or heard these things written in the holy Books, ought now at least to believe when they see them already fulfilled; so that from these now great pressures, as in the winepress of the Lord our God, just as the dregs of the unbelievers murmuring and blaspheming flow off, so the oil too of the faithful confessing and praying may not cease to be pressed out and made to flow. For to those who do not cease to fling impious complaints against the Christian faith, saying that before this doctrine was preached through the world the human race did not suffer such great evils, it is easy to answer from the Gospel; for the Lord says, *The servant who does not know his master's will, and does what deserves blows, will be beaten with few; but the servant who knows his master's will, and does what deserves blows, will be beaten with many*[^1]. What wonder, then, if in Christian times this world, like a servant now knowing his Lord's will and doing what deserves blows, is beaten with many? They attend to how great a swiftness the Gospel is preached, and do not attend to how great a perversity it is despised. But the humble and holy servants of God, who suffer temporal evils in a twofold way, because they suffer both from the impious and with the impious, have their consolations, and the hope of the age to come; whence the Apostle says, *The sufferings of this time are not worthy to be compared with the future glory which will be revealed in us*[^2].

**3.** Accordingly, dearest one, even to those whose words you say you cannot bear, since they say, "If we sinners have deserved these things, why have the servants of God too been slain by the sword of the barbarians, and the handmaids of God been led captive?"—answer humbly and truthfully and devoutly: For however great a justice we keep, however great an obedience we show to the Lord, can we be better than those three men who were cast into the furnace of burning fire for keeping the law of God? And yet read what Azariah, one of the three, says there, who, opening his mouth in the midst of the fire, said: *Blessed are you, O Lord, God of our fathers, and praiseworthy; and glorious is your name forever: for you are just in all that you have done to us, and all your works are true, and your ways right, and all your judgments truth. And you have executed true judgments in all that you have brought upon us, and upon the holy city of our fathers, Jerusalem; for in truth and judgment you have brought all these things upon us, because of our sins. For we have sinned, and have not obeyed your Law, and have not heeded your commandments, that it might be well with us; and all that you have brought upon us, you have brought in true judgment. And you have delivered us into the hands of the wicked, of our most hateful enemies, of transgressors, and to a king unjust and most wicked above all the earth. And now there is no way for us to open our mouth: we have become a confusion and a reproach to your servants, and to those who worship you. Do not deliver us up forever for your name's sake, O Lord, and do not despise your covenant, and do not take away your mercy from us, for the sake of Abraham who was beloved by you, and for the sake of Isaac your servant, and Israel your holy one, to whom you spoke that you would multiply their seed like the stars of heaven and the sand of the sea: for, O Lord, we have become the least of all nations, and are humbled this day in the earth, because of our sins*[^3]. You certainly see, brother, what sort of men, how holy, how strong in the midst of tribulation—where nevertheless they were spared, and the flame itself feared to burn them—confessed their sins, for which they knew they were worthily and justly humbled, and did not keep silent.

**4.** Can we be even better than Daniel himself, of whom God says through the prophet Ezekiel to the prince of Tyre, *Are you wiser than Daniel?*[^4] And who is set as one among three just men whom God says he alone will free; showing surely in those three certain forms of the just, whom he says he will so free that they do not free even their own sons with them, but they alone are freed—*Noah, Daniel, and Job*[^5]? Yet read also the prayer of Daniel, and see how, placed in captivity, he confesses the sins not only of his people but also his own, and says that for these, through the justice of God, he came to that punishment and reproach of captivity. For thus it is written: *And I set my face toward the Lord God, to seek prayers and supplications in fastings and sackcloth; and I prayed to the Lord my God, and confessed, and said: O Lord, great and wonderful God, who keep your covenant and mercy to those who love you and keep your precepts, we have sinned, we have acted against the Law, we have done impiously, and have departed and turned aside from your precepts and from your judgments, and have not heeded your servants the Prophets, who spoke in your name to our kings and to all the people of the land. To you, O Lord, justice, but to us confusion of face, as on this day, to the man of Judah, and to the inhabitants of Jerusalem, and to all Israel, those who are near and those who are far off in all the land in which you have scattered them there, on account of their contumacy, because they reproved you, O Lord. But to us confusion of face, to our kings, and to our princes, and to our fathers, who have sinned. To you, O Lord our God, mercies and propitiations, because we have departed, and have not heard the voice of the Lord our God, to walk in the precepts of this law which he gave in our sight by the hand of his servants the Prophets. And all Israel have sinned against your law, and have turned aside not to hear your voice: and the curse and the oath came upon us, which is written in the law of Moses the servant of God, because we have sinned; and he has established his words which he spoke to us and to our judges who judge us, to bring upon us great evils, such as have never been done under all heaven, according to those that were done in Jerusalem. As it is written in the law of Moses, all these evils have come upon us, and we have not entreated the Lord our God, that he turn from us our offenses, and that we might understand all your truth. And the Lord God was watchful over all his holy work, and brought upon us the things he did, because the Lord our God is just in all his work which he has made, and we have not heard his voice. And now, O Lord our God, who brought your people out of the land of Egypt with a strong hand, and made yourself a name as on this day, we have sinned against your law. O Lord, in all your mercy let your impulse and your wrath be turned away from your city Jerusalem, and your holy mountain. For on account of our sins and the iniquities of our fathers, Jerusalem and your people have come into confusion to all who are around us. And now hear, O our God, the prayers of your servant and his supplication, and show us your face toward your sanctuary which is deserted. For your own sake incline your ear, O Lord my God, and hear; open your eyes, and see our destruction and that of your city Jerusalem, over which your name has been invoked, because not in our own justice have we cast our prayer in your sight, but to your mercy which is great. Hear, O Lord; be merciful, O Lord; attend, O Lord, and do not delay, for your own sake, my God; for your name has been invoked over your city and your people. And while I was still speaking and praying and enumerating my sins and the sins of my people*[^6]. See how he first told his own sins, and afterward those of his people. And he commends this justice of God, and speaks this praise of God: that not unjustly, but for their sins, he scourges even his own holy ones. If, then, those say these things who by their most excellent holiness had the fires and the lions harmless about them, what ought we to say in our humility, who are so far unequal to them, however great a justice we may seem to keep?

**5.** But lest anyone suppose that those servants of God whom you say were slain by the barbarians ought to have escaped that death in the way that those three men were freed from the fires, and Daniel from the lions, let him know that those miracles were done for this reason, that the kings, by whom they were handed over to those punishments, might believe that they worshiped the true God. For this was in the hidden judgment and mercy of God, that he might in that way take thought for the salvation of those kings. But for king Antiochus, who slew the Maccabees with cruel punishments, he was unwilling so to take thought; rather, by their most glorious sufferings he punished the heart of the hard king with a sharper severity. Yet read what one of them too, who was suffering in the sixth place, says. For thus it is written: *And after him they brought forward the sixth. And when, tormented, he was dying, he said: Do not deceive yourself on our account; we suffer these things for sinning against our God, and these things have become worthy of us. But do not think that you will go unpunished, you who have willed to fight by your laws against God and his Law*[^7]. You see that even these understand how humbly and truthfully, who confess that they are scourged by the Lord for their sins: of which it is written, *For whom the Lord loves he reproves; and he scourges every son whom he receives*[^8]; whence also the Apostle: *For if we judged ourselves, we would surely not be judged by the Lord. But when we are judged, we are reproved by the Lord, lest we be condemned with this world*[^9].

**6.** Read these things faithfully, preach them faithfully, and, as far as you can, beware, and teach men to beware, lest there be murmuring against God in these temptations and tribulations. You say that good and faithful and holy servants of God were slain by the sword of the barbarians. But what difference does it make whether a fever or the sword released them from the body? The Lord does not attend, in his servants, by what occasion they go out, but of what sort they go out to him; except that a long-lasting languor has greater penalty than a most swift departure: and yet we read even of long-lasting and horrible languor itself, such as that Job suffered, of whose justice surely God himself, who cannot be deceived, bears such a testimony.

**7.** Most grave indeed, and much to be lamented, is that captivity of chaste and holy women; but their God is not captive, nor does he desert his captive women, if he knows them as his own. For even those holy ones whose sufferings and confessions I have recalled from the holy Scriptures, led by enemies and placed in captivity, said those things which we read written down, that we might learn that the captive servants of God are not deserted by their Lord. But whence do we know what the omnipotent and merciful God may will to be done of his marvels even through these women, in that very barbarian land? Only do not cease to groan to God for them, and to inquire, as far as you can, and as far as he himself permits, when he has given time and opportunity, what has been done about them, or what consolations you may be able to have. For from Sitifis, a few years ago, a consecrated virgin, the niece of bishop Severus, was led away by the barbarians, and through the marvelous mercy of God was restored to her parents with great honor. For that house of the barbarians which she entered as a captive began to be tossed by a sudden infirmity of its masters, so that all the barbarians themselves—three brothers, if I am not mistaken, or more—labored under a most dangerous infirmity. Their mother noticed the girl devoted to God, and believed that by her prayers her sons could be freed from the danger of death now imminent; she asked that she pray for them, promising that, if they were made well, they would restore her to her parents. She fasted and prayed, and was at once heard. For to this it had been done, as the outcome taught. So they, having received health by so sudden a benefit of God, marveling at her and honoring her, fulfilled what their mother had promised.

**8.** Pray to God, then, for them, and ask that he teach even these women to say such things as the above-mentioned holy Azariah, among other things, poured out to God in his prayer and confession. For they are in the land of their captivity in the same way as those men were in that land, where they could neither sacrifice to the Lord after their manner, as neither can these women, nor bring an offering to the altar of God, nor find there a priest through whom to offer to God. May the Lord, then, grant them to say to him what Azariah said in the sequel of his prayers: *There is at this time no prince, or prophet, or leader, no holocausts, nor offering, nor supplications, nor place to sacrifice in your sight, and to find mercy; but in a contrite soul and a spirit of humility may we be received. As in holocausts of rams and bulls, and in a multitude of fat lambs, so let our sacrifice be in your sight this day, that it may be accomplished, following after you; for there will be no confusion for those who trust in you. And now we follow you with our whole heart, and we fear you, and we seek your face, O Lord; do not put us to confusion, but deal with us according to your gentleness, and according to the multitude of your mercy, and deliver us according to your marvels, and give glory to your name, O Lord; and let all be put to shame who show evils to your servants, and be confounded by your omnipotence, and let their strength be ground down, and let them know that you are the Lord God alone, and glorious over the whole earth*[^10].

**9.** When these women say these things and groan to God, he who is wont to be present to his own will altogether be present to his own, and will either permit nothing to be perpetrated against their most chaste members by hostile lust; or, if he permits it, will not impute it. For when the mind is stained by no foulness of consent, it defends even its own flesh from crime: and whatever in it the lust of the one who suffers neither committed nor permitted will be the fault of the doer alone; and all that violence will be reckoned, not as the foulness of corruption, but as the wound of suffering. For so much does the integrity of chastity avail in the mind that, it remaining inviolate, chastity cannot be violated even in the body whose members could be overcome. Let this letter, brief for your desire, yet for my occupations much too lengthy, and on account of the haste of the bearer too greatly hurried, suffice for your charity. The Lord will console you much more abundantly, if you read his Scriptures most intently.
