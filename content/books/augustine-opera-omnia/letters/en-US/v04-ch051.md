# Letter 258

TO MARTIANUS, A LORD DESERVEDLY TO BE RECEIVED, AND IN CHRIST A MOST BELOVED AND MOST LONGED-FOR BROTHER, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** I have snatched, or rather stolen, and in a way pilfered, myself from my many occupations, to write to you, a most ancient friend, whom nevertheless I did not have, so long as I did not hold you in Christ. For you know how friendship was defined by *Tullius, the greatest author of Roman eloquence,* as someone says[^1]. For he said, and most truly said: *Friendship is agreement, with goodwill and love, on things human and divine*[^2]. But you, my most dear one, once agreed with me in things human, when I desired to enjoy them in the manner of the crowd; and, by favoring me, you set sail for my pursuit of the things I now repent of—nay rather, the sails of my desires, along with my other lovers then, you, among the foremost, filled with the breeze of praise. But in things divine, of which at that time no truth had shone for me, surely in the greater part of that definition our friendship was limping: for it was agreement, with goodwill and love, on things only human, not also divine.

**2.** And after I ceased to desire those things, you indeed, with persevering goodwill, desired that I be safe with mortal safety, and happy with that prosperity of things which the world is wont to wish for. And so even then there was somewhat between us a benevolent and dear agreement on things human. But now, with what words shall I explain how much I rejoice over you, when him whom I in some way long had as a friend, I now have as a true friend? For there has been added even agreement on things divine; since you, who once led the temporal life with me with most pleasant kindness, have now begun to be with me in the hope of eternal life. And now indeed even about things human there is no disagreement between us, who weigh them by the knowledge of things divine, so that we may not attribute to them more than their measure most justly demands; nor, by casting them away with unjust contempt, do injury to their creator, the Lord of things heavenly and earthly. So it comes about that, among those friends among whom there is no agreement on things divine, neither can the agreement on things human be full and true. For it is necessary that he estimate things human otherwise than he ought who despises things divine, nor would anyone know how to love a man rightly who does not love him who made man. Accordingly I do not say, you are now more fully my friend, who were so in part; but, as much as reason indicates, you were not even in part, when you did not even hold true friendship with me in things human. For you were not yet my partner in things divine, by which things human are rightly weighed; whether when I myself was not in them, or after I had somehow begun to savor those things from which you far recoiled.

**3.** But I do not want you to be angry, nor let it seem absurd to you, that at that time, when I was seething amid the vain things of this world, although you seemed to love me much, you were not yet my friend; when I myself was not my own friend, but rather my enemy: for I loved iniquity; and true—because divine—is the sentence by which it is written in the holy Books: *But he who loves iniquity hates his own soul*[^3]. Since, then, I hated my own soul, how could I have a true friend, wishing me those things in which I myself suffered myself as an enemy? But when the kindness and grace of our Savior shone on me, not according to my merits, but according to his own mercy[^4]; you, alien to this, how could you be my friend, who utterly did not know whence I could be blessed, and did not love me in that wherein I had somehow already been made a friend to myself?

**4.** Thanks, therefore, to God, that he deigns at last sometime to make you my friend. *For now there is between us agreement, with goodwill and love, on things human and divine* in Christ Jesus our Lord, our most true peace. Who concluded all the divine proclamations in two precepts, saying: *You shall love the Lord your God with your whole heart, and with your whole soul, and with your whole mind;* and: *You shall love your neighbor as yourself. On these two precepts the whole Law and the Prophets depend*[^5]. In that first is agreement, with goodwill and love, on things divine; in this second, on things human. If you hold these two most firmly with me, our friendship will be true and everlasting; and will associate us not only with one another, but also with the Lord himself.

**5.** That this may come about, I exhort your gravity and prudence now to receive even the Sacraments of the faithful: for it befits your age, and accords, as I believe, with your morals. Remember what you said to me as I was setting out, recalling a verse, comic indeed, from Terence, but yet most apt and most useful:  
*Now this day brings another life, demands other morals*[^6].  
Which if you said truly, as I ought not to doubt about you; you already surely live in such a way that you are worthy to receive, by the saving baptism, the remission of past sins. For there is altogether none other than the Lord Christ to whom the human race may say:  
*Under your leadership, if any traces of our sin remain,*  
*made void, they will free the lands from perpetual dread*[^7].  
Which Virgil confessed he translated from the Cumaean, that is, from the Sibylline song; since perhaps that prophetess too had heard in spirit something about the one Savior, which she had need to confess. These things to you, lord deservedly to be received, and in Christ most beloved and most longed-for brother, whether they be few, or perhaps many, I have written, most occupied as I am: I desire to receive your reply, and to learn now and now that you have given your name among the candidates for baptism, or are going to give it. May the Lord our God, in whom you have believed, preserve you both here and in the future world, lord deservedly to be received, and in Christ most beloved and most longed-for brother.

[^1]: See Lucan, Pharsalia 7.62-63
[^2]: Cicero, On Friendship 6.20
[^3]: Ps 11:5 (10:6)
[^4]: See Titus 3:4-5
[^5]: Matt 22:37-40
[^6]: Terence, Andria 189
[^7]: Virgil, Eclogues 4.13-14
