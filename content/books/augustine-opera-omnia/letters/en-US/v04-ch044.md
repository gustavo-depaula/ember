# Letter 251

TO PANCARIUS, A MOST BELOVED LORD AND DESERVEDLY HONORABLE SON, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Since, before your Reverence came, the priest Secundinus did not displease the people of Germaniciana, whence it has now come about that, as you wrote, they are ready to accuse him even of I-know-not-what crimes, I do not know, most beloved and deservedly honorable son. Nevertheless, we can in no way despise what they seem to object against the priest, provided only those who object are Catholics: for to admit the accusations of heretics against a Catholic priest we neither can nor ought. Accordingly, let your prudence first see to this, that there be no heretics where, before your coming, there were none; and we will hear the priest's case, as it ought to be heard. This indeed I admonish, since you deign it, because both your safety and your reputation are most dear to us, and the people of Germaniciana themselves pertain to the care of our lowliness, that the things you obtained from the most glorious Emperors, and the things you did before the competent judges, you would deign confidently to allege; so that it may appear to all that you do nothing inordinately, lest again in your case, who contend about possession, the wretches themselves be worn out, and, more grievously afflicted, perish. At the same time I also commend that the same priest's house not be plundered or laid waste: for it has been reported to us about his church, that certain people, I know not who, wish to pull it down; but I do not think that this could in any way be permitted by your religion.
