# Letter 13\*

A MEMORANDUM FOR THE PRIEST RESTITUTUS, FROM AUGUSTINE

**1.** Although your letter gravely moved me about the contrivances of the devil, by which he either draws the servants of God into sin, or stains their reputation for the scandal of the Church, yet, as far as I could as a man, I explored the man's mind, dealing with him not once but often, and terrifying him about the judgment of God, that he should confess to me, if perhaps he had communicated with that woman, by whom he is accused, in intercourse or any uncleanness. But if what he says is true, to what great and holy a man could that temptation befall, that to him—where he himself was sleeping alone—a woman should come and lie down beside him, whom he was unwilling to betray, especially as she said nothing to him of the shamefulness of concupiscence, but only of her own tribulations and necessities? Where he would judge that nothing in such a temptation pertained to him, except to forbid her in every way to approach him; and that, if she had been unwilling to do this, he should abstain from her unchaste embrace and from disgraceful conversation.

**2.** This he says he did, when he was sleeping on an upper floor, removed from all who dwelt in the house in which he had been received as a guest, since, established there on account of the necessity of his office, he was so overtaken by the evening time that he could not return thence to his own house. When, therefore, awakened, he had shuddered at her coming to him, as he asserts—he saw that he, whom he did not yet believe to have perished, could perish, and took precaution lest he perish by himself, or, by an ill-advised outcry, injure the reputation of a nun perhaps not yet lost—he allowed her to linger beside him and murmur certain complaints, I know not what, about her parents, containing himself from her and answering nothing else than that she should rise thence and depart. And when she had been unwilling for somewhat too long, [he says] that rain burst in, and so he was freed from her conversation, since, descending from the upper floor, he went out of that house and placed himself before the door, under the still-wet eaves, where she would not dare to approach, lest she be found by any of his people going out, or be perceived opening the door, and, not having returned, come into suspicion among those who had perceived her go out.

**3.** Thus far the man confessed to me, whence I do not judge that he is to be condemned, unless perhaps he be convicted of lying, and the words of that woman not be heard and accepted against him—because without doubt a lost woman seeks one to whom to cling—but some other documents arise by which his disgraceful wickedness be uncovered. But these things would not easily have befallen clerics, if they did not walk alone on account of any necessities, whether their own or the Church's. But since we scarcely obtain it of priests, especially in the country districts, that they not walk alone, how much less can we obtain this of clerics of a lower rank? If, then, the people to whom he was ordained are not moved against him about this rumor, nor have believed anything base about him, and he is not convicted of the disgrace by other documents, as has been said, let him remain in his status; but if other things are demonstrated, about which we can judge something, let me know them; but if the Church to which the priest has been appointed is moved by it—since this is said to have happened when he was still a deacon—read them this letter of mine and explain it to them, as the Lord shall grant you, that they may love, as they began, their priest, to whom such a temptation befell as could befall any holy man, and that their love toward him not grow cold[^1], so that, by their peace, the devil rather be conquered, whose arts and snares these are wont to be. But if you see it to be necessary that I send them a letter, you will take care to intimate it.

[^1]: See Matt 24:12
