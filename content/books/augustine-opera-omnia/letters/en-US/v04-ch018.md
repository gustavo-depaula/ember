# Letter 226

To Augustine, a most blessed lord, to be longed for with all affection, and much to be received in Christ, a father, Hilary.

**1.** *If, when the questions of opponents cease, the inquiries of the studious are for the most part welcome, so that they may learn even those things which could be unknown without danger, I think the diligence of our report will be more welcome, which, while it indicates, according to the pursuits of certain ones, some things adverse to truth, strives, through the counsel of Your Holiness, to provide not so much for itself as for those who are disturbed and who disturb, most blessed lord, to be longed for with all affection, and much to be received in Christ, a father.*

**2.** *These, then, are the things that are ventilated at Marseilles, or in other places too in Gaul. That it is novel and useless to preaching that certain ones are said to be chosen according to a purpose, so that they can neither lay hold of it nor hold it, unless the will of believing is given. They think that all force of preaching is excluded, if it is said that nothing has remained in men which could be roused by it. They agree that every man perished in Adam, and that no one can thence be freed by his own choice: but they assert this to be in agreement with truth, or suited to preaching, that, when to those laid low and never about to rise by their own strength the occasion of obtaining salvation is announced, by that merit by which they have willed and believed that they can be healed of their disease, they may obtain both the increase of faith itself and the effect of their whole healing. But as for any work, whether to be begun, much less perfected, they agree that no one can suffice for himself: for they do not think that any work of their cure is to be reckoned to a sick man's being willing, with a terrified and suppliant will, to be healed. For as to what is said,* Believe, and you will be saved[^1], *they assert that one of these is exacted, the other offered; so that on account of that which is exacted, if it has been rendered, that which is offered may thereafter be granted. Hence they think it follows that faith is to be exhibited by him to whose nature this was granted by the will of the Creator; and they think none so depraved or extinguished that it ought not, or cannot, will itself to be healed; on account of which either one is healed of his own disease, or, if he is unwilling, is punished with his disease. Nor is grace denied, if such a will is said to precede, which only seeks the physician, but is not itself yet able to do anything. For those testimonies, such as that,* As to each one he has apportioned the measure of faith[^2], *and the like, they want to avail to this, that he is helped who has begun to will, not also that it is given that he will; with others equally guilty rejected from this gift, who could likewise be freed, if that will of believing which is equally bestowed on the unworthy were likewise bestowed on them too. But if, they say, such a will be said to have remained in all, by which one is able either to despise or to obey, they think the account of the elect and the rejected is rendered by a shortcut, in that to each is joined the merit of his own will.*

**3.** *But when it is said to them why it is preached to others, or in some place, or not preached, or why that is now preached which once was preached to almost none, just as now it is not preached to certain nations; they say this is a matter of the divine foreknowledge, so that at that time, and there, and to those, the truth was, or is, announced, when and where it was foreknown to be about to be believed. And this they testify to prove not only by the testimonies of other Catholics, but even by an older disputation of Your Holiness; where nevertheless you taught the same grace with no less perspicuity of truth: as is that which Your Holiness said in the question against Porphyry, about the time of the Christian religion: "That Christ then willed to appear to men, and his teaching to be preached among them, when he knew and where he knew there would be those who were going to believe in him*"[^3]: *or that from the book on the Letter to the Romans:* You will say to me then, why does he still find fault? For who resists his will?[^4] "*To which inquiry, you say, he so answers that we may understand that to spiritual men, living not according to the earthly man, the first merits of faith and impiety can lie open: how God by foreknowledge chooses those who will believe, and condemns unbelievers: neither choosing the former from works, nor condemning the latter from works; but both granting to the faith of the former that they may work well, and, by abandoning the impiety of the latter, hardening them, that they may work evil"; and again in the same book, above, "All are equal before merit, nor can there be a choice named in things in every way equal. But because the Holy Spirit is not given except to believers, God does not indeed choose the works which he himself bestows, when he gives the Holy Spirit, that through love we may work good things: but nevertheless he chooses faith, because unless one believes, and remains in the will of receiving, he does not receive the gift of God, that is, the Holy Spirit, through whom, love being poured in, he can work good. He does not therefore choose anyone's works in foreknowledge, which he himself is about to give: but he chooses faith in foreknowledge; so that whom he foreknew would believe, him he chose to whom he would give the Holy Spirit, that by working good he might also attain eternal life. For the Apostle says:* The same God who works all things in all[^5]. *But nowhere has it been said, God believes all things in all: for what we believe is ours; but what we work is his"*[^6]; *and the rest in the same work; which they testify they accept and approve, as agreeing with evangelical truth.*

**4.** *But foreknowledge, and predestination, or purpose, they contend avail to this, that he foreknew, or predestined, or purposed to choose those who were going to believe: nor can it be said of this faith,* What have you that you have not received?[^7] *since it remained in the same nature, though vitiated, which earlier was given sound and perfect. But what Your Holiness says, that no one perseveres unless he has received the power of persevering, they accept thus far: that it is granted to those to whom it is given by their own preceding choice, inert though it be: which they assert to be free only to this, that one may will or be unwilling to admit the medicine. But they too testify that they abominate and condemn it, if anyone supposes that any strength has remained in someone by which he can advance to health. But they do not want this perseverance to be so preached that it cannot be either suppliantly merited or contumaciously lost. Nor do they want themselves to be led to the uncertainty of God's will, where it is, as they think, evident to them—as to obtaining or admitting—what sort of beginning of will there is. That testimony too which you set down,* He was snatched away lest malice change his understanding[^8]; *they define as not canonical, to be omitted. Hence they so take that foreknowledge that men are to be understood to have been foreknown on account of their future faith: and that to no one is such a perseverance given that he is not permitted to transgress; but one from which he can by his own will fall away and be weakened.*

**5.** *They assert that the practice of exhorting is useless, if it is said that nothing has remained in man which rebuke could rouse: which indeed they confess they say is in nature in such a way that, by the very fact that the truth is preached to one ignorant of it, this is to be referred to the benefit of present grace. For if they are so predestined, they say, to either side, that no one can cross over from one to the other, to what does so great an insistence of rebuke from without pertain? If from man, even if not whole faith, at least some grief of a compunctious infirmity arises, or the peril of demonstrated death is dreaded. For if one cannot fear that by which he is terrified, except by that will which is received; he is not to be blamed for the fact that he now is unwilling: but in him and with him who once was so unwilling that he merited to incur, with his posterity, that damnation, that he should never will to desire right things, but always perverse. But if there is some grief, of whatever kind, which arises at the exhortation of the one rebuking: this very thing they say is the cause on account of which one is rejected, or another taken up: and so there is no need for portions to be established, to which nothing is either to be added or to be taken away.*

**6.** *Then they take it ill that grace is so divided—the grace that was either then given to the first man, or now given to all: "That he received a perseverance not by which it should come about that he persevere, but without which he could not persevere through free choice: but now to the saints predestined to the kingdom through grace there is given not such a help of perseverance, but such that perseverance itself is given to them; not only that without this gift they could not be persevering, but even that through this gift they are nothing but persevering"*[^9]. *By these words of Your Holiness they are so moved that they say a kind of despair is shown to men. For if, they say, Adam was so helped that he could both stand in righteousness and turn aside from righteousness, and now the saints are so helped that they cannot turn aside, since indeed they have received such a perseverance of willing that they cannot will otherwise; or if certain ones are so abandoned that they either do not come near, or, if they have come near, even withdraw: they say that the usefulness of exhortation or of threatening pertained to that will which held the free power both of persisting and of desisting; not to this will, to which unwillingness toward righteousness is joined by an inevitable necessity, except for those who were so created together with those who were damned with the whole mass, that they were excepted, to be freed by grace. Hence in this alone they want the nature of all to differ from the first man, that grace was to help him willing, with the powers of his will whole, without which he could not persevere: but these, with their powers lost and ruined, only believing, it not only raises up prostrate, but even supports as they walk. But whatever has been given to the predestined, they contend that it can be both lost and retained by one's own will: which would then be false, if they thought it true that certain ones have received such a perseverance that they cannot but be persevering.*

**7.** *Hence it is that they likewise do not accept this, namely that they are unwilling there be a fixed number of those to be chosen and those to be rejected: and that they take up an exposition of that sentence other than the one drawn out by you*[^10]*, that is, that he wills not only all men to be saved; and not only those who will belong to the number of the saints, but absolutely all, so that no one is held excepted*[^11]. *Nor is this to be feared, that someone is said to perish against his will: but, they say, just as he does not will sin to be committed or righteousness deserted by anyone, and yet righteousness is continually deserted against his will, and sins are committed; so he wills all men to be saved, and yet not all men are saved. The testimonies too of Scripture which you set down about Saul or about David*[^12]*, they think do not pertain to the question that turns on exhortation: but others they refer to this, that from them they take it that that grace is commended by which each one is helped after his will; or to the calling itself, which is bestowed on the unworthy. For this they testify they demonstrate both from those places of your little works and of others, which it would be long to follow.*

**8.** *But they do not allow the case of little children to be brought forward as an example for adults: which they say Your Holiness too touched on only so far as to be willing that it be uncertain, and rather to prefer that there be doubt about their punishments. Which you will remember was so placed in the third book on Free Will*[^13] *that it could afford them this occasion. This too they do with the books of others whose authority is in the Church—which Your Holiness perceives can not a little help the opponents, unless greater things, or at least equal ones, are brought forth by us: for your most prudent piety is not ignorant how many more there are in the Church who are held in an opinion by the authority of names, or are transferred from an opinion. In short, with all of us wearied, their pursuit, or rather complaint, turns to this—with even those agreeing who do not dare to disapprove this definition—that they say: What need was there that by the uncertainty of such a disputation the hearts of so many less intelligent people should be disturbed? For no less usefully, they say, without this definition, for so many years, by so many treatise-writers, by so many preceding books both of yours and of others, both against others and especially against the Pelagians, was the Catholic faith defended.*

**9.** *These things, my father, and innumerably many others—to confess my highest wishes—I would rather have conveyed in person; or, since I did not merit this, at least, over a more ample time, have directed all the things by which they are moved, collected together: so that I might hear whatever is contradicted about this matter, and how far it ought to be refuted, or, if it cannot now be refuted, to be tolerated. But because neither came about as I wished, I preferred to direct these things, comprehended as I could, rather than to keep utterly silent about so great a contradiction of certain ones. There are in part such persons that to them, by ecclesiastical custom, laymen must show the highest reverence. This indeed we have taken care, with God's help, so to preserve, that, when there was need, we did not keep silent about the things which the slightness of our powers suggested for the assertion of this question. But now summarily, as far as the haste of the bearer allowed, I have suggested these things as if by way of reminding. It is for your holy prudence to discern what needs to be done, so that the intention of such great men may be overcome or tempered. To which I now think it of little profit that you render an account, unless there be added also the authority which contentious hearts cannot untiringly transgress. But plainly I ought not to keep silent about this, that they say they admire Your Holiness, this excepted, in all your deeds and words. It will be yours to decide how their contradiction in this matter is to be tolerated. And do not wonder that I have added otherwise, or some things in this letter which, as I think, I had not said in the former; for such is now their definition, besides those things which through haste or forgetfulness I have perhaps passed over.*

**10.** *The books which you are working on concerning your whole work, when they have been published*[^14]*, I beg that we may be worthy to have; especially that through their authority, if anything in your writings displeases you, we may, no longer trembling, set it apart from the dignity of your name. The book too On Grace and Free Will we do not have; it remains that, because we trust it is useful to the question, we may be worthy to receive it. But I do not want Your Holiness to think I write these things as if I doubted those things which you have now published. Let my own punishment suffice for me, that, exiled from the delights of your presence, where I was nourished by your wholesome breasts, I am tortured not only by your absence, but also by the obstinacy of certain ones, who not only reject manifest things, but even reprehend things not understood. But from this suspicion I am so far that I rather think my own weakness notable, by which I bear such people with too little patience. But how you judge these things are to be advised on, as I said, I leave to your wisdom. For I believed this pertained to me, for that love which I owe to Christ or to you, that I should not keep silent about the things that come into question. Whatever, for that grace which we, small with the great, admire in you, you shall will or be able to do, we shall most gratefully receive, as decreed by an authority most dear and most reverend to us. Indeed, because, with the bearer pressing, I feared that, conscious of my powers, I might be able to direct either not all things, or these very things less worthily, I dealt with a man renowned both in character and in eloquence and zeal, that he might convey in his own letter as many things as he could, collected together: which, joined to these, I have taken care to send. For he is such a one that, even apart from this necessity, he is judged worthy of the notice of Your Holiness. The holy deacon Leontius, your devotee, with my parents, greets you much. May Christ the Lord deign to grant your Paternity, mindful of me, to his Church for many years, lord father.* And below: *Let Your Holiness know that my brother, on whose account chiefly we departed from here, has, by consent with his wife, vowed perfect continence to God. Hence we ask Your Holiness that you would deign to pray that the Lord may deign to confirm and guard this very thing in them.*
