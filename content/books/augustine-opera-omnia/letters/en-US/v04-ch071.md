# Letter 7\*

A MEMORANDUM FOR THE DEACON FAUSTINUS

**1.** Although you have been sent to the Gauls, on the business of the priest Heraclius, to commandeer a ship, yet take occasion to deal with my brother and fellow bishop [Novatus], that he would deign to attend to the business of the Church. About that money which the widow of Bassus, a delay having been requested, deposited in escrow until the Kalends of July—since the tribune Felicianus afterward wrote to the very escrow-holder that he should not give to the Church that same money which Count Sebastianus had written ought to be given—the case is such, about which you will have to instruct the bishop: some years ago, while the domestic Florentinus was still alive, Count Boniface so gave a certain amount of money to the Church that an order of payment was made to two men, with whom the tribune Bassus had deposited it from his accounts, and from whom he had received notes of deposit issued in his own name. One of them, then, who was ready to repay to the Church the part of that same money which he had with him, demanded back his note which he had issued to Bassus. But by now Bassus had died, and those notes had remained with his wife. Yet she paid out something from there to the Church—if I am not mistaken, about eighty solidi—which the Church, as received from its own, paid out for its own necessities. Then the collector himself too died, leaving heirs. But afterward the widow of Bassus came to Hippo and received the remaining money, of which the greater sum is, from the heirs of that same collector, in solidi, and gave them the note of deposit. But to the Church she said she was prepared to pay the same solidi and the note which had remained with it of that man who had the other part of the money. For we said that the order of payment had been made to two. She said, then, that both the solidi which she had received from the heirs of the one and the note of the other she wished to repay to the Church; and that her husband's bond, which he had issued to the count about this matter, should either be returned to her or, if it could not be returned, be voided. And when this had been done, she began to be unwilling to repay to the Church what she before said she wished, but deposited it with the escrow-holder and requested a delay until the Kalends of July. Then she set out from Hippo to Sitifis, either to do something there, as she said, or even to proceed to Tipasa. But after some days the tribune Felicianus wrote to the escrow-holder that he should not repay to the Church, according to the woman's agreement, the solidi which Count Sebastianus had ordered to be repaid by her, her husband's bond being voided.

**2.** You will therefore ask brother Novatus that he deign to inquire into this case diligently, especially lest we seem to press a widow, so that, if he learns that the same money pertains to her, he may suggest this to Count Sebastianus, especially on account of the solidi which the Church has already paid out from there as its own. For it is not a grave thing that he cause them to be repaid to the woman, lest they be demanded back from the Church. But if the bishop learns that the woman's case is unjust, let him bring it about that the escrow-holder be ordered, or permitted, to repay to the Church what is deposited with him, the interdiction of the tribune Felicianus being removed. But if the woman is not at Sitifis, let brother Novatus see what the tribune Felicianus answers about it, and let this case be brought to the notice of Count Sebastianus, who can establish about it what he sees to be just.
