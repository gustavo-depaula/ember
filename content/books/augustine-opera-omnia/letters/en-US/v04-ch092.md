# Letter 27\*

TO THE TRULY HOLY AND MOST BLESSED LORD, BISHOP AURELIUS, JEROME

**1.** You who conquer in merit, conquer also in courtesy. To me, lying hidden and bewailing my own faults, a single discourse of your Dignity announced you to be both safe and most friendly to me and bishop of the Church of Carthage. Where the beginnings of intimacy are such and the seed of love so abundant, we believe, in Christ our God, that a most plentiful harvest is destined to arise, and that you are summoning the famine of so long a time to an incredible ripeness; and I recall that you, with the holy bishop Cyrus of blessed memory, a priest of the Church of Carthage, had been sent to Rome as a legate; and when one day I inquired of the holy and venerable bishop Damasus who you were (for the keenness of your mind your countenance promised even while silent), he answered that you were the archdeacon of the Church of Carthage, such a man as both your life and his testimony about you deserved. But that I might not enter into fuller familiarity with you, nor by gradual conversation enter into friendship, shame prevented us, lest I seem imprudently to seek out one unknown and thrust myself into intimacy with a man who had given me no occasion of speaking with him; but let us pass over the losses of the past in silence, and from the courtesies that follow let us show that it was an old mistake, not contempt.

**2.** You write that you have certain little works of my smallness, that is, a few *Homilies on Jeremiah* and two on the *Song of Songs*; while I was a very young man, at the request of a certain brother, I played in an exercise of this kind, except for the two homilies on the *Song of Songs* that I translated at Rome at the prompting of the blessed Damasus. So if I have now written anything, you should reckon it more mature and suited to my age; moreover, as to what you add, that you have my little commentaries on Matthew, I am wholly unaware of having published this work, unless perhaps, by the love with which you esteem me, you think whatever you see distinguished to be mine. And so now, since brother Felicissimus has gone on to Africa on account of certain domestic necessities, which he can report to you in person (he went by the post-horse, and there was no means of carrying a larger load), for that reason I have sent you small little works, that is, my little commentaries *on the Tenth Psalm* and of *Hebrew Questions on Genesis*, which I wish you to read as a friend, not as a judge.

**3.** Otherwise, when in your province so great rivers have flowed into the holy Scriptures—Tertullian, Cyprian, Lactantius—it is ridiculous now to wish for me as a dry little stream; but since we read in the Old Testament that not only gold and silver and precious gems, but also skins and goats' hair were brought, according to the abilities of the offerers, into the tabernacle of God, we too dare to offer as it were certain goats' hair, as we have said, the sweat of our penitence. These few things out of many; but since, at your prayer, we have composed no small things from the holy Scriptures, if it pleases you and seems convenient, do what your other brothers, the holy bishops from Gaul and from Italy, have done: that is, send someone trusted by you to spend a year here—with me supplying copies—and to carry to you all that we have written. Of Latin copyists there is no scarcity at Jerusalem; for I have two holy brothers whom I have as notaries, and they can scarcely keep up with what we dictate. My holy brother Paulianus and my holy brother Eusebius and all who are with our smallness humbly greet you; I pray that you greet, with my service, the holy ones who are with you. I commend brother Felicissimus to you and beseech that through him I may receive your writings. May Christ our God enlighten you, mindful of me; may the Almighty protect you, truly holy and most blessed lord and father.
