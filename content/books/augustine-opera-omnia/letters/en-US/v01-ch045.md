# Letter 45

To the most genuine, most beloved, and in Christ praiseworthy lords and brothers Paulinus and Therasia, Alypius and Augustine send greeting in the Lord

**1.** By no means has some delay of yours—through which, for two whole years, from the time when our most sweet brothers Romanus and Agilis returned to you from us, we have received no letter from you—made us slothful to write. For while in other matters the more anyone is loved, the worthier of imitation he seems, in this matter it is the contrary. For the more ardently we love you, the less do we bear that you do not write to us; nor do we wish to imitate you in that. Behold, then, we greet you, if not answering your letters—which none come to us—at least demanding them, and not grieving moderately, although perhaps your Charity too similarly complains, since you know that those sent by you have not reached us, and in turn that those which had been sent by us were not given to you. But if it is so, let us turn our complaints into prayers to the Lord, that he not deny us such consolations.

**2.** We had heard that you were writing against the Pagans; which, if it is finished, we ask that you not delay to send through the bearer of this letter. He is dear to us, to whose esteem in our regions we can, not rashly, bear good testimony. He asks, through us, your Holiness, that you deign to commend him to those with whom he has business, and before whom he fears lest his good cause be oppressed. What is being done in the matter he himself will narrate more conveniently: he can also be asked about each thing which may perhaps move the mind, and we hold it most welcome, and give thanks before our Lord God to your most sincere kindness, if through your work we may rejoice over the safety of a Christian brother.
