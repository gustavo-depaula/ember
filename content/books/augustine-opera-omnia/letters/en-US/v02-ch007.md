# Letter 106

To his beloved lord and brother Macrobius, Augustine

**1.** I have heard that you are arranging to rebaptize a certain subdeacon of ours. Do not: so may you live for God, so may you please God, so may you have the Sacraments of Christ not in vain, so may you not be separated from the body of Christ forever. Do not, I beg you, brother; rather I ask it of you for your own sake; at least attend a little to what I say. Felician of Mustis condemned Primian of Carthage, and was in turn condemned by him. For a long time Felician was in the sacrilegious schism of Maximian, and there baptized many through his churches; now he is your bishop along with Primian, but he baptizes no one after him. By what license, then, do you think there should still be rebaptizing after us? Solve this question for me, and baptize me; but if you cannot solve it, spare another's soul, spare your own. Or if you charge that I have spoken falsely about Felician, demand that I prove it; surely if I do not prove it, then do what you think. I add even this: if I do not prove it, may I not be a bishop of my own communion; but if I do prove it, do not be an enemy of your own salvation. I wish, lord brother, that you be at peace with us.
