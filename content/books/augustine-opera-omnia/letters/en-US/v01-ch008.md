# Letter 8

Nebridius to Augustine

**1.** *For me, hastening to get to the point, no preface, no introduction pleases. How is it, my Augustine, or what is that method which the higher powers use—those I wish to be understood as heavenly—when it pleases them to show us, while we sleep, certain dreams? What, I say, is the method; that is, how do they do it, by what art, by what machinery, by what instruments or medicines? Do they impel our mind through their own thoughts, so that we too, thinking those things, imagine them? Or do they themselves, in their own body or in their own imagination, present and show us things they have made? But if they do these things in their own body, it follows that we too have other, bodily eyes within us when we sleep, by which we see those things which they have formed in their own body. But if they are not aided in these matters by their own body, but arrange these things in their imaginative faculty, and thus touch our imaginations, and what is a dream is seen—why, I ask you, do I not compel your imaginative faculty, by my own, to generate those dreams which I first formed in my own? Surely I too have imagination, and what I wish it is able to fashion, yet I make no dream for you at all; rather, I see that our very body generates dreams within us. For when once it has, through the affection by which it is coupled to the soul, it forces us by wondrous ways to simulate that very thing through imagination. Often, when we are thirsty in sleep, we dream of drinking, and when we are hungry, we seem as it were to be eating; and many such things, which are transferred, as if by a kind of exchange, from the body into the soul in imaginative fashion. Because of their obscurity, and because of my own inexperience, do not be surprised if these things are explained less elegantly and less subtly: you will labor to do this as well as you can.*
