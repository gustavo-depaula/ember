# Letter 250

TO AUXILIUS, A MOST BELOVED LORD AND VENERABLE BROTHER AND FELLOW PRIEST, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** The distinguished man, our son Classicianus, has gravely complained to me by letter that he has suffered the injury of anathema from Your Holiness: narrating that he came to the church accompanied by an escort of a few befitting his power, and dealt with you, that you would not, against his salvation, favor those who, by forswearing through the Gospels, sought aid for violating their pledge in the very house of faith; whom nevertheless, considering what evil they had done, he says were not violently dragged out thence, but went out of their own accord, and that hence Your Reverence was so angered at him that, by the drawing up of ecclesiastical proceedings, he was struck with the sentence of anathema along with his whole household. Having read this letter of his, no little troubled, with thoughts fluctuating in a great storm of the heart, I could not keep silent with your Charity; so that, if you have on this matter an opinion explored by sure reasons or testimonies of the Scriptures, you would deign to teach us too how rightly a son is anathematized for the sin of his father, or a wife for her husband, or a slave for his master, or anyone in the house even not yet born, if at the same time at which the whole house is bound by anathema, he be born, and cannot be helped, through the washing of regeneration, in the peril of death. For this is not a bodily punishment, by which we read that certain despisers of God were slain together with all their own who were not partakers of the same impiety. Then indeed, for the terror of the living, mortal bodies were destroyed, surely about to die someday: but a spiritual punishment, by which comes about what is written, *Whatever you bind on earth will be bound also in heaven*[^1], binds souls, of which it was said: *The soul of the father is mine, and the soul of the son is mine: the soul that has sinned, it will die*[^2].

**2.** You have perhaps heard that certain priests of great name have anathematized some sinner along with his household: but perhaps, if they were questioned, they would be found able to render a reason for it. But I, since, if anyone asks of me whether it is rightly done, I do not find what to answer him; have never dared to do this, although I have been most gravely moved by the deeds of certain ones perpetrated monstrously against the Church. But if perhaps the Lord has revealed to you how justly it is done, I by no means despise your youthful age and the first beginnings of your ecclesiastical honor: behold, I am here, an old man ready to learn from a young one, and a bishop of so many years from a colleague not yet a year old, how we can render either to God or to men a just reason, if we punish innocent souls with a spiritual punishment for another's crime, from which they do not draw, as from Adam, *in whom all have sinned*[^3], original sin. For Classicianus's son, although he drew from his father the fault of the first man, to be expiated by the sacred font of baptism; yet whatever sin his father committed after he begot him, where he himself was not a partaker, who doubts that it does not pertain to him? What shall I say of the wife? what of so many souls in the whole family? whence, if one soul, through that severity by which this whole house is anathematized, perish by going out of the body without baptism; the death of innumerable bodies, if innocent men be violently dragged from the church and killed, cannot be compared to this loss. If, then, you can render a reason about this matter, would that you also grant it to us by writing back, that we too may be able to: but if you cannot, what is it for you to do, by an ill-advised commotion of mind, that whence, if you were questioned, you cannot find a right answer?

**3.** But I have said these things, even if our son Classicianus committed something which seemed to you most justly to be punished by anathema. But for the rest, if he sent me a true letter, neither ought he alone in his household to have been coerced by that rebuke. But hence I deal with Your Holiness in nothing, except only I ask that you pardon one asking pardon, if he acknowledges his fault: but if you prudently acknowledge that he sinned in nothing, since he himself, in the house of faith, more justly demanded that faith ought to be kept, lest it be broken where it is taught; do what it befits a holy man to do, so that, if it has befallen you as a man, what surely a man of God says in the Psalm, *My eye has been troubled because of anger,* you may cry out to the Lord: *Have mercy on me, Lord, for I am weak*[^4]; that he may stretch out to you his right hand, and repress your wrath, and make tranquil your mind for seeing and doing justice. For as it is written: *The wrath of a man does not work the justice of God*[^5]. And do not think that an unjust commotion cannot creep upon us for the reason that we are bishops: but let us rather consider that we live most perilously among the snares of temptations, because we are men. Take away, therefore, the ecclesiastical proceedings which, more disturbed perhaps, you made, and let there return between you the love which you had with him even as a catechumen: take away the quarrel and recall peace, lest a friend, a man, perish for you, and the devil, the enemy, rejoice over you. But the mercy of our God is powerful, which may both hear me praying, that my sadness about you may not be increased, but rather that which has arisen may be healed; and may raise up by his grace, and gladden, your youth not despising my old age. Farewell.

[^1]: Matt 16:19
[^2]: Ezek 18:4
[^3]: See Rom 5:12
[^4]: See Ps 6:7 (6:8); 6:2 (6:3)
[^5]: Jas 1:20
