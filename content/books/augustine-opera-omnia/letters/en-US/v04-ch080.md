# Letter 16\*

TO POPE AURELIUS, A MOST BLESSED LORD AND, WITH DUE LOVE, VENERABLE HOLY BROTHER AND FELLOW PRIEST, AUGUSTINE SENDS GREETINGS IN THE LORD.

**1.** Since no trustworthy opportunity had presented itself, I have sent by our [\*\*\*] two sermons, one on the Birth of the Lord, the other on the Epiphany, which I was able to steal away from all my occupations, asking that you graciously accept this dutiful greeting of ours and continue to pray for us—which indeed you do even if we do not ask or remind you, most blessed lord and holy bishop, to be venerated with the love that is owed you. If your Holiness has perhaps heard anything through this bearer about the brothers whose return from their company we await, I beg that we may be informed of it.

**2.** But through the memorandum of brother Alypius that he sent to Thagaste—since the same ship put in at our shore—we have learned this much: that an usher has already been dispatched with the pardon of the Carthaginians, and he said that he had obtained this by letter. For the most illustrious man had not yet returned from Gaul, and Alypius indicated that the only business remaining for his presence was that those too who had taken refuge in the Church should have their case removed; but I believe that by now your Reverence has been able to learn something certain about them as well. For there was no lack among us of less than decorous people to report to us that, through a certain bishop of Numidia named Renatus, brother Alypius had sent a copy of the favor that they were already saying had been granted to our son Largus. But whether it is rather the case that, as he announced in his memorandum to Thagaste, it was sent through the usher, or whether something was also commanded by him himself for those who are in the Church, I do not know—except that I think the said bishop has by now been able to come to Carthage.

**3.** And so we rather expect, through this young man whom we have sent with these others, to learn something certain by a reply from your Beatitude. I do not doubt, moreover—for he himself reported this to us—that your Holiness has heard from that old man Priscian, bishop of Mauretania Caesariensis, what the venerable bishop Boniface did at Rome concerning the Pelagians, and what he dared with wholesome sincerity. But why the encyclical letter of your Beatitude has not reached the regions of our Numidia, that is, Consular Numidia, I do not know; I know that it was sent while I was still at Carthage, unless perhaps it was thought not to be sent to old Valentinus—which I suggest your Venerableness inquire into, and if it has been neglected, let it be corrected. For it certainly ought also to reach the primate of Numidia, by whom it may be forwarded to the brothers belonging to the same council.
