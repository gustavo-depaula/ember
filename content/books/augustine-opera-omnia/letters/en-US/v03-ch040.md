# Letter 191

To the venerable lord, and to be received in the love of Christ, holy brother and fellow presbyter Sixtus, from Augustine, greetings in the Lord.

**1.** From the time that the letters of your kindness, directed through our holy brother the presbyter Firmus, came to Hippo while I was absent, after I was able to read them when I had returned—although their bearer had already set out from there—this first and most pleasing occasion of writing back occurred, through our most beloved son Albinus the acolyte. But because we were not then together, to whom together you wrote, it came about that you received separate letters of individuals, not one letter of both. For the bearer of this departed from me by way of my venerable brother and fellow bishop Alypius, who would write back another to your holiness, passing through; to whom he himself also carried those letters of yours which I had already read. With how great joy these suffused us, what would a man strive to speak which he cannot speak out? Nor do I think you yourself know it sufficiently, but believe us, how great a good you did by sending us such writings. For just as you are a witness of your own mind, so we of ours, how it is affected by the most luminous sincerity of those letters. For if we copied with exulting alacrity that most brief letter of yours, which about this very matter you directed to the most blessed elder Aurelius through Leo the acolyte, and read it with great zeal to those we could, where you set forth to us what you think about that most pernicious dogma, or what, on the contrary, about the grace of God, which he bestows on little and great, to which that dogma is most hostile: with how great exultation do you think we read these more lengthy writings of yours, or with what care offered them to others, that they might be read, to whom we were able, and still offer to whom we are able! For what can be read or heard more pleasing, than so pure a defense of the grace of God against its enemies, from the mouth of him who was before boasted of as a patron of great weight of those same enemies? or whence ought we to give more abundant thanks to God, than that his grace is so defended by those to whom it is given, against those to whom either it is not given, or what is given is thankless; because, that it may be thankful to them, by the hidden and just judgment of God it is not given?

**2.** Wherefore, venerable lord, and to be received in the love of Christ, holy brother, although you do most excellently when you write about this matter to the brothers among whom those people are wont to boast of your friendship; yet this greater care remains for you, that not only those be punished with a healthful severity who dare to chatter more freely that error most hostile to the Christian name, but also that these be most diligently guarded against by pastoral vigilance, on account of the weaker and simpler sheep of the Lord, into whose ears, more pressingly indeed and more timidly, but yet, they do not cease to whisper it, *penetrating houses*, as the Apostle says, and doing the rest that follow with practiced impiety. Nor are those to be neglected who, by fear, suppress to the depth of silence what they think, but yet do not cease to think that same perversity. For some of them, before that pestilence was condemned even by the most manifest judgment of the apostolic See, could become known to you, whom you now perceive to have suddenly fallen silent; nor can it be known whether they are healed, except when they shall not only have kept silent those false dogmas, but even shall have defended the true things contrary to them with that zeal with which they are wont to defend those: who, however, are surely to be treated more gently. For what need is there that those be terrified, whom their very taciturnity sufficiently shows to be terrified? Nor for that reason are those to be passed over, as if sound, by the diligence of medicine, whose wound is in the hidden. For although they are not to be terrified, yet they are to be taught; and, as I judge, they can more easily, while in them the fear of severity helps the teacher of truth, that, with the Lord aiding, his grace understood and loved, they may even by speaking assail what they now do not dare to speak.
