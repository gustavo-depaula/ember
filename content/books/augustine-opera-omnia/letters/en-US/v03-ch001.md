# Letter 156

To the holy lord Augustine, bishop, rightly and deservedly venerable and to be revered in all things, from Hilary.

**1.** The grace of your holiness, which is known to all, has persuaded my littleness to send you this letter in praise of your venerability by our people returning from Syracuse to Hippo, begging the supreme Trinity that you may receive this writing of mine for review, safe and sound by the favor of our God, holy lord, rightly and deservedly venerable, and to be revered in all things. And so I ask that you would deign to be mindful of me in your holy prayers, and to instruct our ignorance concerning what certain Christians at Syracuse maintain, saying: that a man can be without sin and can easily keep the commandments of God if he wills; that an infant not baptized, taken away by death, cannot deservedly perish, since it is born without sin; that a rich man remaining in his riches cannot enter the kingdom of God unless he sells all that he has, and that it cannot profit him even if he should perhaps fulfill the commandments out of those very riches; that one ought not to swear at all; and concerning the Church, which is the one of which it is written that it has neither *wrinkle nor spot*[^1], whether this is the one in which we are now gathered or the one for which we hope—for some think that the Church is this one which is now thronged by the people, and that it can be without sin. Concerning all these things I ask your holiness, with as great entreaties as I can, that you would order us to be more openly instructed, so that we may know how far we ought to think. May the mercy of our God keep your holiness safe, for countless years I pray, holy lord, rightly and deservedly venerable, and to be revered in all things.

[^1]: Eph 5:27
