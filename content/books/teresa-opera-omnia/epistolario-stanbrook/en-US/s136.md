# Letter 132. To Father Gracian, Seville, Toledo, November, 1576

*The Saint warns him to be reticent with the nuns and not to read her letters to them.*

I often explain how important he is to the Order, that we are forced to ad: as we do, and that, but for that, I should behave differently.

How prosy I am! But do not let my Father be wearied by what I say. You and I are charged with a heavy burden of which we must render an account to God and man. Knowing the love that prompts my words you will forgive me and grant me the favour I have asked before — that of not reading my letters in public. People's minds differ, and there are some things that superiors should never say outright. What I write either about others or about myself should be told to no one. There is a great difference between our discussing a matter privately or in the presence of some one else, even my sister. I should not like any one to overhear what I say to God nor to disturb my intercourse with Him, and it is the same as regards Paul. . .
