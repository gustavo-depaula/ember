# Chapter XI. Of petition

Then having made so great an Offering we may confidently make our petition for gifts and graces in response. And, first, let us pray with all the fulness of love, and zeal for the honour of our Blessed Lord, that all people and nations of the world may know and praise and adore Him as the One Only true God and Lord, saying from the bottom of our hearts those words of the Prophet-King, “Let the people praise Thee, O God: let all the people i praise Thee.” Let us pray also for the prelates of the i Church, the Pope, Cardinals, Bishops, and all other clergy and ministering servants, that the Lord may govern and enlighten them, that they may lift up all men to the — knowledge of, and obedience to, their Creator. In like manner we must pray (as S. Paul exhorts us) for all kings, and — for all placed in authority, that through their wisdom and care we may live quiet and peaceful lives, for this is acceptable to God our Saviour, “ Who will have all men to _be saved and to come unto the knowledge of the truth.”?

Let us pray, also, for all the members of His Mystical Body ; for the righteous, that the Lord may keep them in their righteousness ; for sinners, that He may convert them; for the departed, that He may mercifully deliver them from travail, and bring them to eternal rest.

Let us pray, also, for all poor and sick, — prisoners and captives, that, for the merits of His dear Son, God may vouchsafe to help and set them free from evil; and, after having prayed for our neighbours, Tim. ii, 4.

But for the better understanding of this teaching, we may pray for these gifts following. First, through the Merits and Passion of our Lord, let us ask for remission of all our sins, and their deserts, and, especially, let us ask for His gracious help against all those inclinations and vices to which we are most prone, and most tempted, laying open before our Heavenly Physician all the wounds of our souls, that with the holy unction of His grace He may heal them.

Then let us ask for those most excellent and noble virtues in which cohsists all Christian perfection, namely: Faith, Hope, Love, Holy Fear, Humility, Patience, Obedience, Fortitude in all trouble, Poverty of Spirit, Contempt for worldly things, true Discretion, Purity of intention, and other like virtues which are the summit of the spiritual building which we aim at. For Faith is the very root of all Christianity. Hope is the weapon, and the remedy, against the temptations of this life. Charity is the end of all Christian perfection. The Fear of God is the beginning of true Wisdom. Humility is the foundation of all virtues. Patience is the strong armour against the blows and — attacks of the enemy. Obedience is the most acceptable oblation wherein man offers himself in sacrifice to God. Discretion is the very eye with which the soul sees, and travels on its way. Fortitude is the hand and arm for all its works, and Purity of intention that which guides and directs all its works to Godward.

Lastly, let us, then, pray for other virtues, which, without being of themselves the very chiefest, will yet help us to keep the greater ones, such as temperance in eating and danlane’ control of the tongue, guardianship of the senses, the gravity and recollectedness of the outer man, sweetness of manner and good example to our neighbours, strictness and severity towards oneself, and other like virtues.
