CHAPTER 1. 


Or THE FRUIT TO BE DERIVED FROM 
Prayer anD MEDITATION. 


INCE this short treatise speaks of 
Prayer and Meditation, it will be 
well to state in a few words what is 

the fruit which may be derived from this 
holy exercise, that men may give them- 
selves to it with more willing heart. It 
is a well-known fact that one of the 
greatest hindrances we have to attaining 
our final happiness and blessedness, is the 
evil inclination of our hearts, the diffculty 
and dulness of spirit we have in respect 
of good rules ; for, if there were not this 
in the way, it would be the easiest thing 
possible to run in the path of virtues,

and attain to the end for which we were 
created. On which saith the Apostle, 
“I delight in the Law of God, after 
the inward man, but I see another 
law in my members warring against 
the law of my mind, and bringing me 
into captivity to the law of sin.”* This, 
then, is the universal cause of all our evil. 
One of the most efficacious means for 
overcoming this dulness and difficulty, and 
for facilitating this matter, is Devotion ; 
for as saith S. Thomas, “ Devotion is 
nothing else than a certain readiness and 
aptitude for well-doing.” For this takes 
away from our mind all that diffculty and 
dulness, and makes us quick and ready 
for all good. It is a spiritual refection, 
a refreshment, as the dew of heaven, a 
breath and inspiration of the Holy Spirit, 
a supernatural affection. It so orders, 
strengthens, and transforms man’s heart, 
that it imparts a new taste and inspiration 
for spiritual things, a new distaste and 
abhorrence for things sensual. The ex- 
perience of every day shews us this. For 
when a spiritually-minded person rises 
from deep devout prayer, then straightway 
all his good resolutions are renewed, to-

From this it is manifest, that the life of 
a true and perfect Christian, to speak

and consequently, that two feet are neces- |

through too great confidence in God does 
he yield to sleep, nor through such con-

rate the help of Divine grace. 

Hence, everyone will perceive that the — 
Christian life is nothing else than a con- | 
tinual cross, and a continual prayer. 
And when I speak of the cross, I 
mean, it is needful for the whole of 
man, and all- his parts, since all are, 
through sin, diseased, and all need the 
operating knife and remedy. So that one 
cross is needed for the body, and another 
for the eyes, one for the ears, and another 
for the tongue, one for the affections and 
lusts, and another for the imagination. 
All these crosses are needed, and this is 
the conquest and death which our soul has 
to choose and embrace, that, being dead
