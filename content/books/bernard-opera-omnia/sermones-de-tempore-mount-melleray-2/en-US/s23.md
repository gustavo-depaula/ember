# First Sermon for Easter Sunday: On the Apocalyptic Book and its Sevenfold Seal, and on the Proper Mode of Celebrating Easter

“And one of the ancients said to me: Weep not; behold the Lion of the tribe of Juda, the root of David, hath prevailed to open the book and to loose the seven seals thereof.’ — Apoc. v. 5.

“The Lion of the tribe of Juda hath prevailed ” (Apoc. v. 5). Wisdom clearly hath prevailed over malice (Wisdom vii. 30), Wisdom that “ reacheth from end to end mightily and ordereth all things sweetly” (ibid. viii. 1), acting mightily for me, sweetly with me. He, the Wisdom of God, prevailed on the cross over the blasphemies of the Jews, He bound the strong man armed (Mark iii. 27), and triumphed over the very empire of death. Where now, O Jew, are thy reproaches? Where, O Zabulon, are now the captives of thy hand? “O death, where is thy victory ” now (r Cor. xv. 55)? The slanderer has been put to confusion, the robber has been stripped of his spoil. We have here presented to us a novel kind of power.* Death hitherto victorious has been confounded. What will you do now, O ye Jews, who on the day of the crucifixion were wagging your sacrilegious heads before the cross, and heaping insults on Christ, the true Head of man, calling out to Him in mockery, “ Let Christ the King of Israel come down from the cross ’’ (Mark xv. 32)? Wherefore, instructing the tongues of the blasphemers with malice exceeding great, he inspired them to say, “Let Him now come down from the cross and we will believe Him.” For he supposed that after this promise there was nothing more to prevent Christ from descending since what He had most at heart was the conversion of the Jews.

But what is the guileful serpent scheming for and against whom is he plotting? He is plotting against Him over Whom “ the enemy shall have no advantage, nor the son of iniquity have power to hurt Him” (Ps. Ixxxviii. 23). He cannot be deceived by an empty promise Who knows the hearts of all His creatures, any more than He Who is the meekest of men can be moved to anger by blasphemous insults. As for Satan’s design in urging Him to descend, it was not in order that the Jews might believe in Him, but rather to ruin our faith in Him (supposing we should have any) by every means possible. For having read that “‘ the works of God are perfect” (Deut. xxxii. 4) how could we acknowledge

But let us hear what answer Christ Himself makes through His Prophet to this invitation : “ Seekest thou a sign, O Jew? ‘Expect Me in the day of My resurrection that is to come ’” (Soph. iii. 8). ‘“ If thou hast the good will to believe in Me,” He seems to say, “I have already offered thee signs enough, signs more wonderful than that which thou art now soliciting. I multiplied prodigies, and I wrought miraculous cures yesterday and the day before. But to-day it is necessary that I be consummated (Luke xiii. 32). But if this “evil and adulterous generation still seeketh a sign, a sign shall not be given it, but the sign of Jonas the Prophet ” (Matt. xii. 39): that is to say, not the sign of a descent from the cross, but the sign of a resurrection from the tomb. And if the Jew does not seek this sign, let the Christian at least embrace it and rejoice in it. But if for the purpose of repelling the slanders of the Jews this one fact appears to suffice, that the Lord passed out from the closed sepulchre, He to Whom they said in mockery, “If He be the King of Israel, let Him come down from the cross’’—for they took more pains to close and seal the tomb (Matt. xxvii. 64-66) than they did to drive home the nails—if, I say, “the Lion of the tribe of Juda hath prevailed ” by this miraculous passage through the rock and offered His enemies a greater wonder than they demanded: to what can we compare the miracle of the resurrection itself? We read, it is true, of some other resurrections, or rather of certain resuscitations which preceded the resurrection of Christ ; but they were only figures of the latter, which, as you know, ex-celled them all in two important respects. In the first place, the other individuals who were restored to life were destined to die again; whereas “ Christ, rising again from the dead, dieth now no more: death shall no more have dominion over Him. For in that He died to sin, He dieth once ; but in that He liveth, He liveth unto God ” (Rom. vi. 9, 10), He liveth unto eternity. Justly, therefore, is Christ called the “ first-fruits” of them that rise from the dead (r Cor. xv. 20), because He arose in such a manner that He can fall no more, Who alone hath attained to immortality (1 Tim. vi. 16).

My brethren, let us listen to no one who counsels us to descend from the cross, whether it be flesh and blood or an intangible spirit. Let us remain upon the cross and let us expire upon the cross. Let us be taken down from the cross by the hands of others, rather than by our own inconsistency. Our Divine Head was taken down by holy men. But the holy angels will condescend to take us down. But how am I to interpret this sevenfold seal ? Shall I take the seven seals to signify the three faculties of the soul, viz., memory, will, and understanding, together with the four elements of which the body is constituted, so that we may know that nothing of the reality of human nature is wanting to the Saviour? Or had I better suppose the book itself to be the sacred Humanity, and endeavour to find the seven seals thereof? In fact, it appears to me that I can discover seven circumstances which in a special manner concealed the presence of the Divine Majesty in the flesh, preventing the book from being opened by any one and the Wisdom hidden within it from becoming known. The circumstances or seals which occur to me at the moment are these: first, the Mother’s espousals, which veiled Christ’s virginal birth and the purity of His conception, so that the Creator of men was considered the Son of a carpenter ; second, the weakness of His Body, by reason whereof He used to weep and wail like an ordinary child of Adam, to feed and sleep, and to experience all the other necessities of the flesh, which served as a mask for the divine power dwelling in Him; third, His submitting Himself to the rite of circumcision, the remedy for sin and for the diseases of the soul—He Who came to take away all sin and all our moral distempers ; fourth, His flight into Egypt from the face of King Herod, which made it impossible to recognise in Him the Son of God or the King of heaven ; fifth, the threefold temptation to which He was subjected by the enemy, in the desert, on the pinnacle of the temple, and on

Weshould likewise ponder in diligent thought what is that which is especially commended to us by this glorious solemnity of Easter. As for myself, I take it to be three things: a resurrection, a passage, and a transmigration. For Christ, dearest brethren, has not fallen back to-day, but has arisen ; He has not returned, but passed on ; He has transmigrated, not retraced His steps. The very word Pasch, the name of the feast we are celebrating, signifies, not a going back, but a passing over ; and the name of Galilee, where it is promised us that we shall see Him Who has arisen, means a transmigration, not a return. I suppose the minds of some of you are now running ahead of me and have divined the drift of my remarks. But even so, I will speak my thoughts: yet in few words, lest, amongst other reasons, the prolixity of the sermon should weary your devotion on so great a solemnity as to-day’s. If, then, after the consummation of the cross, our Lord Jesus Christ had arisen again to this our mortal existence and to the miseries of this present life, I should have said He had returned, not that He had passed over : that He had gone back to His former condition, not transmigrated to a state more sublime. Now, however, having Himself passed on to a newness of life, He invites us also to make the same passage, He summons us to Galilee. For therefore “in that He died to sin, He dieth once,” because “in that He liveth now, He liveth—not unto flesh but—unto God ” (Rom. vi. 10).

What shall we say of ourselves, my brethren, who empty the holy resurrection of the Lord of the title

The days of Lent we passed in mourning, devoting ourselves to compunction, prayer, recollection, and abstinence, anxious to make amends and satisfaction during that sacred quadragesimal season for the sins and negligences of other times. We have communicated in the sufferings of Christ, having been again “planted together with Him in the likeness of His death ”?” (Rom. vi. 5), by a second baptism, as it were, of tears, penance, and confession. If therefore we have truly “ died to sin,” how can it happen that we shall ever again live in sin? If we have sincerely lamented our shortcomings, how is it that we so soon fall back into them once more? By and by we shall again be found as much given to curiosity as we were before, as talkative as we were before, as slothful and negligent as we were before ; we shall be found as much infected as ever with the vices of vanity, suspicion, detraction, irascibility, and all the other spiritual maladies, which we lamented so bitterly during the days just elapsed. “T have put off my garment, how shall I put it on? I have washed my feet, how shall I defile them? ” (Cant. v. 3). This, my brethren, is not a transmigration. Not so shall Christ beseen. This is not the way in which God has promised to show us His Salvation (Ps. xl. 23). For “no man putting his hand to the plough and looking back is fit for the kingdom of God (Luke ix. 62).

It is thus the lovers of this world, “ enemies of the cross of Christ ” (Phil. iii. 18), Whose name they take in vain by calling themselves Christians : it is thus they long during the whole time of Lent for the coming of the present paschal season, in order, alas! that they may indulge more freely their appetite for pleasure. So sorrowful a reflection, dearest brethren, overcasts with a cloud of gloom the joy of to-day’s solemnity. But it is the dishonour done to the solemnity that makes me weep, a dishonour which I cannot shut my eyes to, no, not even on the day of the solemnity itself, nay rather much less on this day. Oh, the shame of it! That the day of Christ’s resurrection should be made a time for sinning, an occasion for relapsing! From this day the “rioting and drunkenness ” shall return, the “‘ chamberings and impurities’’ shall reappear (Rom. xiii. 13), and a free rein shall be given to passion: as if, forsooth, it is for such things Christ arose from the dead and not rather “ for our justification’ (Rom. iv. 25). O miserable men, is it thus you honour Christ now that you have received Him? You made ready a lodging for Him in your hearts before He had yet arrived, confessing your sins with tears, chastising your bodies, distributing alms to the poor: and behold, no sooner has He become your Guest than you deliver Him up to His enemies, yea, oblige Him to withdraw from your souls by readmitting your former vices ! For light can have no “fellowship with darkness ” (2 Cor. vi. 14), neither can Christ dwell in the same heart with arrogance, with cupidity, with ambition, with uncharitable feelings, with luxury and the vices of the flesh. How can it be that less honour is due to Him when arrived than when approaching? Why should not the time of His resurrection demand from us as much reverence as the |timeZof#His passion ? What, then, are you to do? O ye prevaricators, you have been “taken in the midst of straits” (Jer. Lament. x. 3), not because you have transgressed, but because you have continued obstinate in sin and added prevarication thereto, either wholly impenitent or coldly contrite, refusing, even after lamentable experience, to avoid the occasions of sin. The enemy has entangled you with a net of “ sinews wrapped together ” as the Scripture says (Job. xl. 12). Now if you refuse to participate in the Sacrament of Christ because of this evil state of your conscience, you shall have nothing in common with Him, and “ you shall not have life in you ” (John vi. 54). For this is what He has said Himself, “ Except you eat of the Flesh of the Son of Man and drink His Blood, you shall not have life in you.” On the other hand, if you approach the altar unworthily, “you eat judgment to yourselves, not discerning the body of the Lord” (x Cor. xi. 29). Therefore, ‘return, ye transgressors to the heart ”’ (Is. xlvi. 8). Seek ye the Lord with your whole heart (Deut. iv. 29) and “hate evil” (Ps. xcvi. 10), proving yourselves to be penitent “not in word and in tongue only but in deed and in truth” (t John iii, 18). But inasmuch as he does not seem sincerely sorry for having fallen who is resolved to remain still upon the ice, nor he for having strayed who refuses to seek for a guide: let the abandonment of opportunities and the avoidance of the occasions of sin prove the reality of your repentance. Otherwise it is greatly to be feared that this day, which “is set for the fall and for the resurrection of many ” (Luke ii. 34) shall be a day of reprobation for you, either as openly alien from Christ Whom you

ON THE APOCALYPTIC BOOK 185 refuse to receive in the Holy Communion, or as the associates of Judas Iscariot, into whom “ Satan entered after the morsel’’ (John xiii. 27). . But, my brethren, “ what have I to do to judge them that are without ? ” (x Cor. v. 12). The only reason for referring to them at all is in order that we may grieve for having been once ourselves caught in the same snare, and that we may rejoice at having been delivered by the pure mercy of God from the same snare in which, with sorrow springing from brotherly love, we behold them still entangled. Yet would to God that even we ourselves were now found to be altogether purified and estranged from the wretched and sacrilegious custom I have been condemning, and that the advent of Easter did not cause us to diminish or relax anything of our spiritual fervour, but rather encouraged us to advance and increase! For whosoever does not return to the consolations of the flesh after the lamentations of penance, but passes out of himself into an assured hope in the divine mercy, and enters upon a new life of devotion and “ joy in the Holy Spirit’ (Rom. xiv. 17), and is more ravished with the thought and more inflamed with the desire of his heavenly crown than disquieted by the memory of his past transgressions: such a soul has indeed arisen with Christ, and is celebrating the pasch, and is hastening forward to Galilee. ‘‘ Therefore,’’ dearest brethren, “if you be risen with Christ, seek the things that are above, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God. Mind the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth” (Col. iii. 1, 2); “ that as Christ is risen from the dead by the glory of the Father, so you also may walk in newness of life’’ (Rom. vi. 4), and may have the joy of passing over from earthly delights and consolations through compunction and “the sorrow that is according to God”’ (2 Cor. vii. 10) into holy fervour and spiritual exultation, by the grace of Him Who hath “ passed out of this world to the Father ” (John xiii. 1), and Who now deigns to draw us after Him and to invite us to Galilee, where He will show Himself to us (Mark xvi. 7), He Who is over all things, God blessed for ever. Amen.
