Where now, O Peter, where now are those joyous exclamations which burst from thee upon the Mount, “ Lord, it is good for us to be here: If Thou wilt, let us make here three tabernacles `` (Matt. xvii. 4)? For behold thy Lord has gone inte “a greater and more perfect tabernacle not made with hand, that is, not of this creation’’ (Heb. ix. 11).

How, therefore, can it be good for us to be here any longer? Is it not rather painful, and grievous, and perilous? For here we find an abundance of malice and but very little wisdom, if any at all (Wisdom vii. 30) ; here everything is glutinous, and slippery, and shrouded in darkness (Ps. xxxiv. 6), and covered with the snares of sinners ; here our souls are ever in danger ; here our spirits are afflicted, for there is nothing under the sun but “vanity and vexation of spirit” (Eccles. i. 14). Therefore, my dearest brethren, “let us lift up our hearts with our hands to the Lord in the heavens ” (Lament. iii. 41); let us endeavour to follow our ascending Lord with the feet of our faith and devotion.

The time indeed will come when, without the least difficulty or delay, ‘‘ we shall be taken up in the clouds, = to meet Christ, into the air” (1 Thess. iv. 16), and our then spiritual bodies shall have a power of ascending which is now justly denied to our animal souls. For, at present, what labour it costs us to lift up our hearts! And that because—as each of us may read in the book of his own sad experience—the corruption of the body is a load upon them, and the earthly habitation presseth them down (Wisdom ix. I).

But perhaps it is necessary to explain to you what I mean by lifting up the heart, and in what manner it ought to be lifted up? Well, then, receive the explanation, not from myself, but from the Apostle : “ If you be risen with Christ,” he calls out to us, “seek the things that are above, where Christ is sitting at the right hand of God. Relish the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth ” (Col. iii. 1, 2). As if he should say in clearer terms, “ If you have arisen with Christ from the dead, ascend into heaven with Him also: if you live with Him, reign also with Him.” Let us follow Him, brethren, let us “‘ follow the Lamb whithersoever He goeth ” (Apoc. xiv. 4). Let us follow Him in His suffering, let us follow Him in His rising, let us follow Him more joyously in His ascension into heaven. Let us see to it “that our old man is crucified with Him, that the body of sin may be destroyed, to the end that we may serve sin no longer” (Rom. vi. 6), having “ mortified our members which are upon the earth ” (Col. iii. 5). And “as Christ is risen from the dead by the glory of the Father, so let us also walk in newness of life” (Rom. vi. 4). “For to this end

But inasmuch, as this newness of life demands a safer abode than can be found upon earth, whilst the dignity of our moral resurrection requires a position more exalted, let us also follow Him in His ascension, that is to say, let us “ seek the things that are above,” where He reigns in glory, let us “ relish the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth.” Do you wish to know, my brethren, what is that place above whereof I am speaking? Attend to St. Paul: “That Jerusalem,” he says, “‘ which is above, is free, which is our mother ” (Gal. iv. 26). And would you like me to tell you what is there to beseen? It isa vision of peace.* “Praise the Lord, O Jerusalem: praise thy God, O Sion. Because He hath strengthened the bolts of thy gates, He hath blessed thy children within thee, Who hath placed peace in thy borders ” (Ps. cxlvii. 1-3). O peace, “which surpasseth all understanding ” (Phil. iv. 7)! O measure above measure “ pressed down, and shaken together, and running over” (Luke vi. 38)! Suffer, then, with Christ, O Christian soul, rise with Christ, ascend with Christ : all which thou shalt do by obeying the Prophet’s injunction, “ Turn away from evil and do good, seek after peace and pursue it” (Ps. xxxiii. 15). The Apostle also, as you may read in the Acts, gave his hearers the same recommendations, discoursing to them of justice and continence and the hope of eternal life (Acts xxiv. 25). And speaking to the same purpose, Truth Itself admonishes us in the Gospel to have our loins girt and our lamps lighted, and to be henceforth

“like to men who wait for their Lord” (Luke xii. 35, 36).

But if you consider the matter carefully, you will notice that the Apostle is commending to us a twofold ascension, in that he exhorts us not alone to seek but likewise to relish, not the things below, but the things which are above. And perhaps it will appear to you that the Prophet also wished to mark this distinction when he said, “ Seek after peace and pursue it’’: so that to seek after the peace which we are bound to pursue, and to pursue it when the seeking is ended, signify the same respectively as to seek after what we should relish, and to relish what our seeking has found, that is to say, “ the things which are above, not the things which are upon the earth.” For as long as our hearts are divided, as long as they are found to have as yet many folds and do not appear to be perfectly one in themselves, so long do they require to be raised up little by little, and (if I may so speak) member by member, in order that they may be bound into unity in that heavenly Jerusalem “ wherein all are united together” (Ps. cxxi. 3), where not alone each heart singly, but all collectively begin to “ dwell together in unity ” (Ps. cxxxii. 1), that is to say, neither divided in themselves nor from each other. Now the two principal of what I have called the members of our hearts or minds are the intellect and the will. These are frequently in opposition to each other, because whilst the former endeavours to mount to the things above, the latter desires to embrace the things below. Would to God that what has given light to the intellect gave also motion to the will! Then we should not experience in ourselves that most bitter contradiction and most painful divorce, one of our faculties drawing us up to the things above, the other dragging us down to the things that are upon the earth.

There are to be found in almost every religious community certain members who are always replenished with divine consolation and superabounding with heavenly joy; who are always pleasant and cheerful, always full of spiritual fervour ; who meditate day and night on the law of the Lord (Ps. i. 2), often raising their eyes to heaven, and “lifting up pure hands in prayer ” (x Tim. ii. 8) ; who keep a careful watch over their conscience and show themselves diligent “‘ pursuers of good works” (Titus. ii. 14) ; to whom monastic discipline appears pleasant, fasting sweet, manual labour attractive, watching in prayer a delight which never lasts long enough, in short, all the austere practices of our way of life easy and delectable. But you will find others of the contrary disposition: who are pusillanimous and remiss; who sink under the burden, and require to be stimulated with whip and spur; who grow lax in joy and cowardly in sadness ; whose compunction is as infrequent as it is short-lived ; whose thoughts are all carnal, and whose lives are all tepid ; who obey without devotion and speak without prudence ; who pray without recollection and read without profit ; who lastly—as we may see for ourselves—can scarcely beheld back by the fear of hell-fire, or hindered by shame, or governed by reason, or restrained by the discipline of the rule.* Does it not seem to you, my express the effect of training to wit, good order, and by a further use of the same figure of metonymy is sometimes made to

For the Spirit would be duplicated in him by the sight of his master ascending, who, borne up to heaven before his eyes, would carry with him thither all his (Eliseus’s) affections, so that he also might begin to “relish the things that are above, not the things that are upon the earth ” (Col. iii. 2): the Spirit, I say, he would receive in double measure from the vision of the departing Prophet, in the sense that to an enlightened understanding should be added a spiritualised affection, his heart being lifted up to heaven together with that flesh to which it was most tenderly attached.

In the case of the apostles also, and indeed more evidently, you may see the same thing accomplished. For when, “‘ while they looked on,” their beloved Master “was raised up” (Acts i. 9) and borne to heaven so manifestly that they had no longer any need to inquire, “Lord, whither goest Thou?”’ (John xiii. 36), they were taught by their faith, now endowed with the faculty of vision, if I may express it so, to “lift up pure hands in prayer” (x Tim. ii. 8), with suppliant eyes upraised to God, eagerly imploring the promised gifts of grace: until “suddenly there came a sound from heaven, as of a mighty Spirit coming ” (Acts ii. 2), of the coming of that Fire doubtless Which the Lord Jesus was casting on the earth, and Which He desired should be mightily enkindled (Luke xii. 49). It is certain, indeed, that they had previously also received the Holy Ghost, that is, when “He, the Saviour, breathed on them and said: Receive ye the Holy Ghost” (John xx. 21). But He came upon them on that occasion as the Spirit of faith and understanding to enlighten their intellect, rather than as the Spirit of fervour to inflame their will; and of this “ double Spirit ” they had need.
