# Letter CCCXXV. To the Same, Respecting the Novice Idiek

*(Circa A.I). 1139.)*

*Bernard advises, in answer to his inquiry, what course should be taken with a novice of difficult character, named Idier.*[^1]

To his very dear brother and co-Abbot, ROBERT, of Dunes, Brother BERNARD, called Abbot of Clairvaux, wishes health.

On the subject of the religious whom you have mentioned to me, and whom you believe will be not only useless, but also a burden to the community, without speaking of secret defects, such as you suspect, I will give you such counsel as I should act on myself were I in your place. After what you have told me, it seems to me that during his probation he has shown himself neither worthy nor capable of being rendered so, and that, therefore, you are able with a good conscience to put away an evil person from among you. If, however, it pleases your charity to exalt mercy above judgment, you are able to allow him to remain in your house as long as you shall see tit, but without permitting him to take the vows. But I dissuade you most strongly from receiving him to profession whilst he is in this condition; and let him be subjected to a new probation if perchance he should show any sign of becoming that which he ought to be. If not, use resolutely your power of expulsion, so that one diseased sheep may not infect tin[^2] entire flock.

[^1]: Formerly No. 337; the former No. 325 is now No. 395.

[^2]: Formerly No. 391; the former No. 326 is now No. 400.
