Benedict Zimmerman, 1922) consulted only as a secondary reference for sense; its chapter division of Book III is offset by one (see `book.json` sources).
Target: pt-BR

No other work of St. John of the Cross has a pt-BR directory in this corpus. Conventions taken from the sibling Church Fathers works (`ambrose/repentance`, `athanasius/*`):
- Scripture references with Portuguese book names and arabic numbers, chapter:verse ("Mateus 24:19", "Salmo 87:16"). The numbers are the source's (Vulgate); they are mirrored, not converted.
- Curly quotes “ ”; the source's «» and straight quotes become “ ”.
- `vós` for plural address.

## Translation Decisions

- **Paragraph numbers.** The source writes them as `1. ` list markers; the pt-BR writes `**1.**` (`.claude/rules/books.md`). Stanza numbers in the Argument (`1. — En una noche…`) likewise become `**1.** —`. Gaps in the source's numbering are mirrored (l3-c15 goes 1, 3; l3-c19 goes 10, 12).
- **Monte verses (l1-c13).** "Para vir a gostar de tudo, / não queiras ter gosto em nada…", kept as verse lines.
- **Citation errors** carried by the edition are mirrored, not corrected (e.g. l3-c38 note 6 "Lucas 9:41"; l1-c5 note 26 "1616").
- **Page cross-references** in Silverio's notes ("véase la pág. 297") point to pages of the 1929 volume. Where the target note is identifiable they become chapter/note references ("nota 15 do capítulo XXII deste livro"); where it is not, the page is kept with "[da edição de 1929]" (l2-c1 note 2). Page headers caught in the OCR of notes are dropped.
- **Garbled source text** (OCR) is translated from the reading the context makes certain; the heaviest cases are l3-c16 ¶1, l3-c19 ¶7 and ¶10, l3-c22 ¶2, l3-c25 ¶6, and many variant readings inside notes.
- **l2-c31 title.** The source's summary is truncated ("…en."); rendered "nelas".
- **The poem.** The stanzas are translated line by line into Portuguese verse that keeps the Saint's words and order, without forcing rhyme ("Numa noite escura, / Com ânsias, em amores inflamada, / oh ditosa ventura!"). The same rendering is reused wherever a stanza or line is repeated in the commentary.
- **Latin.** The Vulgate quotations stay in Latin; the Saint's Spanish rendering that follows is translated from his words, not from a Portuguese Bible.
- **Silverio's notes.** Every note is translated. The manuscript readings they report (the Spanish words of a codex or of the *editio princeps*) stay in Spanish, set in italics so they read as quoted readings; Silverio's own words, and quotations of the Saint given for their sense, are translated. OCR-garbled sigla in the notes ("Ale." for Alc., "«• P" for e. p.) are rendered as the intended siglum.
