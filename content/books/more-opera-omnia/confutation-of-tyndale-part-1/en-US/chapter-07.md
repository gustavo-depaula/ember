# Tyndall

☞And thys same is it that Paule saythe in the seconde chapter of the fyrste epystle to the Corynthians / how that the naturall man that ys not borne agayne and created a new wyth the spyryte of god, be he neuer so grete a phylosopher, neuer so well seene in the lawe, neuer so sore studyed in the scrypture, as we haue ensamples in the pharysies: yet he can not vnderstond the thynges of the spyryte of god / but (sayth he) the spyrytuall iudgeth all thynges, & his spyryte sercheth the depe secretis of god / so that what so euer god commaundeth hym to doo, [^1] he neuer leueth serchyng tyll he come at the botom, the pyth, the quycke, the lyfe, the spyryte, the marye, and very cause why, and iudgeth all thynge.

[^1]: Saynte Poule in that place spekyth n[…]t of eny serche that spirytuall mē shuld make of the cause of godd is cō maūdementis / but Tyndall fayneth y• thing to finde by y• se[…]che of y• cause some dyscharge of the cōmaundmēt.
