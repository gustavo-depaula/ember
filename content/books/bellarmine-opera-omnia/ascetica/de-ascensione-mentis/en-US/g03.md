# Step III: Consideration of the World

## Chapter 1: That as our body finds its rest in the world, so our mind can rest in God alone

E, have considered the world as a whole; now let us pass on to its chief divisions, that from these we may raise a ladder such as it is possible to make, which will bring us to the contemplation of its Maker. }

The first is earth, which although holding the lowest place and appearing less than the other elements, yet is in reality not inferior to water, and indeed in dignity and value it surpasses all the elements. Hence we read in the holy Scriptures that God made heaven and earth, as if to say they were the chief parts of the world, to which all the rest minister: for he made heaven, as it were, the palace of God and of the angels, and the earth the palace of man.. ‘ The heaven of heavens is the Lord’s and the earth hath he given to the children of men,’ saith the prophet.t And this is the reason why the heaven is full of shining stars and the earth abounds in stores of metals, precious stones, herbs, trees, and many kinds of animals: while the water abounds in fish alone, and the air and fire are elements barren and wellnigh empty. But these facts apart, there are three things in the earth particularly worthy of consideration, and if we approach to consider them

with watchful mind we shall without difficulty be able to rise by means of them, to God.

First, the earth is the very first foundation of the whole world, and if we had not this foundation man could neither walk, nor be at rest, nor work, nor spend his life in any kind of way.

“He hath made the round world so sure,’ saith David, ‘that it cannot be moved.’! ‘ He laid the foundations of the earth that it never should move.’ 2

Secondly, the earth, as a good nurse to man and living creatures, with great care brings forth herbs, fruits, seeds, berries, and countless other things of the kind. For thus God spake, ‘ Behold, I have given you every herb bearing seed which is upon the face of the earth and every tree in which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed, to you it shall be for meat, and to every beast of the earth.’ 3

Thirdly, the earth produces stones and wood for building houses, and metals of brass and iron for various purposes, and gold and silver, of which is coined money, a convenient means for gathering together-all that is necessary for the life of man.

And that primary property of earth, namely that it is the place in which our bodies rest, which they cannot do either in water or air or fire, is a symbol of the Creator, in whom alone the human soul can find a place of rest: ‘ Thou hast made us for thyself, O Lord,’ says Saint Augustine, ‘and our heart is unquiet until it rest in thee.’4 King Solomon, if any one did, sought rest in his government, in riches, in pleasures : he maintained his extensive kingdom in peace, so that, as Scripture witnesseth, ‘ Solomon reigned over all the kingdoms from the river unto the land of the Philistines, and unto the border of Egypt: they brought presents, and served Solomon all the days of his life.’ 5 He had

For nothing was wanting to him—kingdom, riches, or pleasures, not even that which is reckoned of the very highest value, namely human wisdom, and there was not even lacking peace and quietness in the possession and retaining such good things for a length of time. Let us then ask whether he found rest in these things and if they were able to satisfy the capacity of his soul: he says, ‘ Then t Eccles. ii. 4-10.

I looked on all the works that my hands had wrought, and on the labour that I had laboured to do: and behold, all was vanity and vexation of spirit, and there was no profit under the sun.’! And so Solomon found not rest in these great riches, pleasure, wisdom, and honours, nor could he have found it had they been far more in number and much greater, for the human soul is immortal, and these things are mortal, and cannot abide long under the sun: nor indeed could it ever come to pass that the mind of man, capable of infinite good, should be satisfied with finite good things. For just as the human body cannot rest in the air however open, nor in water however deep, because its centre is not air or water but earth: so the human soul cannot rest in aerial dignities, worthless riches, or in watery, that is soft and sordid, pleasures, nor in the false glory of human knowledge; but it can rest in God alone, who is the centre of souls, and the true and only place of their rest.

O how true and how wise was the father of Solomon when he said, “ Whom have I in heaven but thee, and what is there upon earth that I desire in comparison of thee? For thou art the God of my heart and my portion for ever’;2 as if he would say: I find nothing in heaven, in earth, nor in any created thing, whether under heaven or upon the earth, able to bring me true rest: thou alone art the God of my heart, that is, thou alone art the solid rock for my heart—for the word ‘God’ in the Hebrew text in this place signifies “rock’—thou only art the most firm rock for my heart, in thee alone will I rest, thou alone art my portion, mine inheritance, all my good. All other things are nothing, and nothing can fill and satisfy me, and this not for one or two days or a year, but for ever. Thou alone sufficest me for ever, all other things fail to satisfy even for one day.

Dost thou not learn then, my soul, that God alone is thy rock, in whom thou canst rest, that all other things are vanity and vexation of spirit, which indeed have no existence, but only appear to have, and do not afford solace, but only affliction, since they are gained by labour and with grief held in possession ?

Therefore, if thou art wise, despise all things that pass, lest they carry thee away with them: and abide in one alone and cleave in a bond of love, to him who abideth for ever. Lift thine heart to God in heaven, lest it become corrupt upon earth; learn true wisdom from the folly of many, in whose name Wisdom speaketh and saith: ‘ Therefore have we erred from the way of truth, and the light of righteousness hath not shined unto us, and the sun of righteousness rose not upon us: we have gone through deserts where there lay no way: but as for the way of the Lord, we have not known it: we wearied ourselves in the way of wickedness and destruction. What hath pride profited us, and what good hath riches with our vaunting brought us? All those things are passed away like a shadow and we were consumed in our own wickedness.’ !

## Chapter 3: Earth nourishes the body by the power of God, who alone 1s the support of the whole man

Now a second property of earth is this, that, as a good nurse, she produces liberally herbs and fruits to be the ‘food of men and other animals. And this very property leads us to the Founder and the true nourisher: for it is not the earth in itself, but God who produces all good things in the earth. For thus saith the Holy Spirit by the mouth of David, ‘ These wait all upon thee, that thou mayst give them meat in due season. When thou givest it them they gather it and when thou openest thy hand they are filled with good’; * and the Lord in the Gospels saith, ‘ Behold the fowls of the air: for they sow not, neither do they reap, nor gather into barns, yet your heavenly Father feedeth them’; 2 and the Apostle, “ Nevertheless God left not himself without witness, in that he did good, and gave us rain from heaven, and fruitful seasons, filling our hearts with food and gladness.’3' And that is true which is said in the beginning of the book Genesis, ‘ Let the earth bring forth grass, the herb yielding seed, and the fruit tree yielding fruit after his kind.’ 4 For indeed the earth doth bring forth grass and trees which bear fruit, but by the power bestowed upon it by God, by which very power God himself produces and preserves and gives the increase.

And so when David invites all created things to praise the Creator, he joins to the company ‘ fruitful trees and all cedars’;5 and the three children with Daniel are exhorted with all the rest of creation, all the things that grow on the earth, to bless the Lord and ‘ praise him and exalt him above all for ever.’ 6

And since all things praise God after this manner, with how great affection oughtest thou, O soul, to bless and praise God for all the benefits which thou enjoyest so freely, beholding in them the hidden hand of God bestowing all things, and the love of thy God not hidden, but plainly to be seen showing itself most pure and paternal, never ceasing to bless thee from heaven and to provide for thee in all things. But this is little in the eye of thy Lord God: for it is he who in thee, as in his own spiritual field, brings forth the most splendid fruit of love. For love is not of the world, but it is of God, as the beloved disciple says in his Epistle: From charity, as from a divine and heavenly tree, are produced the white and sweet-scented flowers of holy thoughts, verdant leaves of words profitable to man’s salvation, fruits of good works, by which God is glorified and the neighbour assisted, merits are acquired and preserved unto life eternal.

But woe to those who, after the manner of senseless beasts, desire satisfaction from the fruits of the earth and eagerly collect them and hide them away, thinking not of their Maker nor giving him thanks. The souls of such are like the earth which God cursed and which brought forth thorns and thistles. For when God is not sowing the seed of holy thoughts, what can fill their minds but murders, thefts, treachery, and other things of that kind, and what is their speech but of blasphemy, perjury, cursing, heresies, banquets, contumely, false witnessing, lying, and such things learnt from their father the devil ? ## Chapter 4: The treasures of the earth are as it were a shadow of the eternal good things of God

THERE remains the last glory of the earth, namely that it contains within itself, gold, silver, and precious stones. But in truth the earth does not produce these precious things by its own power, but he produces them, who speaks by Hageai, ‘ The silver is mine and the gold is mine.’! O thou lover of men, is this not pleasing to thy loving-kindness, that not only were stones, wood, iron, brass, lead, and other kinds of things which are necessary for building houses, ships, and various instruments produced from the earth, but also gold and silver and precious stones for adorning and beautifying them ? And if thou bestowest these things on those who wander about the earth and often even upon thine enemies who blaspheme thy Name, what wilt thou give to thy beloved ones, blessing thee and reigning with thee in heaven? But we are not to think that the heavenly Jerusalem, the city that is above, is adorned or builded with gold and gems and pearls belonging to this earth: we know that these words are used by the Holy Spirit in speaking to us because we are unable to perceive the better and greater things; but most certainly that land which is the home of the elect of God will be far more beautiful than all the cities of this pilgrimage, just as a city all gold and precious stones surpasses the village of huts of rustics, which are built of mud and straw.

Raise therefore, my soul, the eyes of thy mind to heaven, and think how highly the good things therein should be valued, since the gold, silver, and precious stones which here are so highly regarded,. when compared with them, are scarcely worthy of the name of mud or straw. O unbelief of the sons of men! A liar promises ten for a hundred, and that he will repay to a lender the whole sum of money, and he is believed: God, who cannot lie, promises to him who bestows alms treasure in heaven, and to give even more than a hundredfold, and life eternal: the avaricious man is fearful and _ cannot be persuaded to believe it, and would rather hide his treasure where rust consumes it and where thieves break in and steal, than lay it up in heaven where neither thief steals nor rust consumes.

But, O unhappy man, even if thieves do not rob, nor moth or rust corrupt, to whom will belong those things which thou hast gathered together with such great labour? ‘Thine, for certain, they will not be, and yet they might have been thine if thou hadst laid them up in the heavenly treasury by the hands of the poor.
