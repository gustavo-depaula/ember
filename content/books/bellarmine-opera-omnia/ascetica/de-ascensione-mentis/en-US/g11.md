## Chapter 1: Concerning the breadth of the power of God. Gees is the Lord and of his greatness there is

no measure or end: not alone is he great because his height is omnipotence, his depth wisdom unsearchable, his breadth mercy shown forth in every place, his length righteousness like unto a rod of iron: but also because each of these separate attributes 1s great with the magnitude of infinite breadth, length, depth, and height.

And as by a power, or rather by omnipotence, we are kept in place: the power of God has its own breadth which exists in him, so that he reaches forth to things which have no limit.

For first, he reaches forth to all things that are made: for there is nothing in this whole universe of things from the first angel to the last little worm, and from the highest heaven to the lowest hell, which was not made by the power of God: ‘ All things,’ saith Saint John, ‘ were made by him, and without him nothing was made that is made’; and further on, ‘ The world was made by him.’ !

## Chapter 2: Of the length of the power of God

Now the length of the divine power is perceived in this, that God himself works continuously with all that he has made, and he will never be weary: indeed that power of God cannot be lessened or weakened or broken in any way, since it is united with true eternity, or rather it is itself the eternity of true divinity.

Many persons marvel how the sun and moon and stars can be moved in such regularity and such swiftness from rising to setting, and without intermission turn in their own orbits; and indeed the matter would be most worthy of all admiration, did we not know that they are borne along by the Almighty God, who ‘upholds all things by the word of his power.’ *

And others wonder because God bears and sustains all things and is not wearied by bearing and sustaining such an immense mass of wellnigh infinite weight : for a strong man, or a horse, or an ox, or an elephant can bear a great weight for a short time, or carry a heavy burden for a short time: but to bear an immense weight always without fatigue is beyond the strength of all created beings. These persons would rightly marvel if God had strength in such weight and measure as all created things possess it: but since the strength of God completely surpasses all measures, and since he himself is unlimited in every respect, it is not wonderful :f his infinite strength upholds a mass, however weighty it be, for an unlimited time and without weariness.

Therefore again let us say with the holy prophet Moses, ‘ Who is like unto thee, O Lord, among the mighty ones ?’ 2

## Chapter 3: Of the height of the power of God. Tuer: follows the height of the power of God, which

consists chiefly in two things.

First, omnipotence may be called very high, because it alone makes exceeding high things. In the first creation of things God alone made those things which are beneath the moon, but they have the power, through the action of other created things, to be generated, transformed, corrupted: for the elements mutually work changes one on another, according to their functions: thus from the earth herbs and trees are brought forth : animals are produced from animals: fishes are born in water, clouds and rain in the air, comets in fire. But the heaven and the stars, which are the highest bodies, God alone created, alone he preserves them, and no created being can’ possess the power of forming or transforming, of dissolving or even preserving, any. “I will consider,’ saith the prophet, ‘ thy heavens, even the works of thy fingers: the moon and the stars which thou hast ordained’:! for the Most High reserved to himself the highest works, he himself began to build for the foundation, and he himself brought them to full completion.

So also spiritual things, angels and the souls of men, which are the most noble and sublime of all works, the Most High alone, by his infinite power, formed and preserves and will preserve eternally from destruction: and in these actions created beings have no share: and although they were all to join in one act, they could never produce or destroy one single angel or one single soul.

I omit the enlightening of the blind, the raising of the dead, and many other miracles of that kind which God hath done and still does by his prophets and apostles and other of his faithful servants: these all cry aloud, ‘ Who is like unto thee, O Lord, among the mighty ones ?’

But I cannot omit that highest and greatest miracle which God will show in the Last Day when all the dead will rise at once, when many bodies having been reduced to ashes and dispersed, having been either consumed by beasts and converted into other things and other bodies, or having been buried in gardens and fields, have been transformed and passed into herbs of various kinds, shall then rise.

This is therefore the height of the power of God, and by reason of it again we may say, ‘ Who is like unto thee, O Lord, among the mighty ones ?’

## Chapter 4: Of the depth of the power of God

THERE remains the depth, which seems to me to consist in the method which God uses in working: for who can penetrate the manner in which something is made out of nothing? To this depth it was not possible for those to turn their eyes, who declared that the beginning was explored and certain, that nothing was made of nothing. And we also in this matter

But how a thing could be made when nothing went before it, is a profound abyss which we cannot scrutinize or investigate.

Next, not only did God make everything out of nothing, but he made them in nothing, that is without space or place existing before, in which what was made could be placed, a thing which can scarcely be understood, especially in regard to corporeal things. Hence this abyss is also impenetrable. Take away the spaces of places from bodies, says Saint Augustine in his letter to Dardanus, and they will be nowhere: and since they will be nowhere, they will not exist. 3

Now then, if before God created heaven and earth there was nothing, where did God place heaven and earth? They certainly could not be placed within nothing, and yet they were created, and those very things are to themselves places, because he who can do all things willed it, even if we are unable to understand in what way those things happened. God himself had regard to this, when desiring to show his Almighty power to Job he said: ‘ Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth? Declare if thou hast understanding. Who hath laid the measures thereof if thou knowest ? Or who hath stretched the line upon it ? Whereupon are the foundations thereof fastened ? Or who hath laid the corner stone thereof ?’4
