Wye have ascended as far as we are able by means of created substances: but we have not yet attained to that degree of the knowledge of God which it is possible to reach by consideration, even in this vale of tears.

And so it remains for us to see whether, by the dimensions of bodily quantity which we understand, we can rise to the length and breadth and height and depth of the Essence of God: for in things created those are considered to be great whose four dimensions are great.

Now in the Psalms and elsewhere God is said to be great,! and of his greatness there is no end.

Indeed, Saint Bernard, a remarkable contemplative, made for himself from these dimensions steps to the knowledge of God, which he wrote in his books concerning contemplation to Pope Eugenius. If any one will consider carefully, he will surely find that there is nothing complete or solid outside of God, and that everything is narrow, short, lowly, and empty, or superficial: but that in God there is true breadth, immensity; true length, eternity; true height, sublimity of nature ; true depth without limit, incomprehensibility : and again true height, omnipotence ; true depth, infinite wisdom ; true breadth, fullest bowels of mercy ; true length, discerning judgement, that is perfect righteousness.

Now, if we wish to ascend and to find what we are seeking, it is not enough for us to touch lightly on these considerations, but it behoves us to understand fully, as the Apostle says, ‘That ye may be able to comprehend with all saints what is the breadth and length and depth and height.’ That person comprehends who, considering very attentively, is altogether persuaded that he grasps the matter, and is so persuaded that he sells all his possessions and hastens to buy the treasure he has found; and so the Apostle adds ‘ with all saints.” Indeed the saints alone comprehend fully, and no one comprehends those things as he ought without becoming holy.

## Chapter 4: The length of the Divine Essence is his Eternity

Now the length of the divine Essence is his eternity, which hath no beginning of duration nor will ever have an end, and will always abide the same without any change: ‘ But thou art the same and thy years shall not fail,’ saith David,t and so Tobit,2 and after him the Apostles They call God the Everlasting King, because he alone is not subject to time, but is before all ages and rules all ages, since he alone ‘precedes all ages. Other things either have a beginning and end and never abide in one state, or they have a beginning and no ending and that without change of substance, but they can cease to exist, if it so please their Creator. And thus eternity so belongs to God alone that it is unsuitable to a created thing, and there was never a prince, however arrogant, who presumed to arrogate to himself amongst the titles, however many, which he might take, that of ‘ everlasting,’ unless perchance it might be with another meaning, in the way in which Constantius was called “ Eternal Emperor,’ because he was not emperor for a certain time, but for his life. But, my soul, thou canst be reckoned amongst both kinds of creatures: for thou hast a body which had a beginning when it was conceived and born, and little by little grew to that size ordained for it by God: but later it begins to diminish, and a little after, dying, it ceases to exist, and thus it never remains in the same state in every part at every hour: it is subject to change. Tue sublimity of God is next to be considered, according to which it is said to God, ‘ Thou only art the Most Highest.’ Now God alone is most high by the nobility of his nature: for things are nobler and higher in proportion as they are purer and more withdrawn from material things. ## Chapter 6: The Throne of the Most High signifies the blessed and beatifying rest of God

Nort only hath God a very high throne, because he judges all men, but also because he rests beyond all and makes those to rest in whom he has his dwelling.
