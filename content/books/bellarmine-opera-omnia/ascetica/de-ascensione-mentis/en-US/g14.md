# Step XIV: Consideration of the Mercy of God

## Chapter 1: The width of the Mercy of God which bears our

miseries.

Cee Holy Spirit sets forth the mercy of God in the Scripture, praising it so wonderfully that he does not hesitate to magnify it above all the works of God: for thus the prophet David sings, “The Lord is loving unto every man and his mercy is over all his works.’* We shall easily perceive the greatness of this divine attribute if we consider it in its width, length, height, and depth.

The breadth of the divine mercy consists in this, that God, and he alone, can take away all miseries from all creatures ; and some he takes away for the love which he bears towards his creatures, not on account of their profit to himself. Certain created things take away certain miseries, as bread takes away hunger, drink, thirst, clothing nakedness, knowledge ignorance and the like ; but no created thing can take away every kind of trouble. Besides, there are certain troubles so much the more serious the more secret and inward they are, for which God alone can supply a remedy: such as the assault of devils, who are very many, very crafty, very powerful, and are most wickedly disposed towards us: such also are errors and blindness of mind and

Who can snatch us from troubles of this kind but only our Almighty Physician ? And since God often delivers us from this kind of trouble without our being aware of it, all men may deservedly be called ungrateful towards God, as God himself witnesses when he says that the heavenly Father is ‘ kind unto the unthankful and to the evil.’ ! For we know hardly the least part of God’s kindness, yet for that part we do not give thanks with the devotion and humility that we ought.

Next, creatures cannot take away all but only a few miseries, and not even from all persons, but from a few: but God can take away all miseries from all persons, and although he does not take away all from all, yet there is no one who is not in some way partaker of his mercy. Truly, therefore, does the prophet sing, ‘ The earth is full of the goodness of the Lord.’ The Church says in her collect, ‘O God, whose nature is ever to have mercy and to forgive’;3 since it appertains to him who is free from all manner of miseries to remove them, and it pertains to him alone to take away all miseries from all men, who alone is entirely without miseries. But who is entirely free from all misery but God alone, who is pure act, and the highest good, and whose Essence is beatitude ? O my soul, if by consideration thou couldst conceive what is the life of thy Lord and thy Father, a life raised above all misery, and pure and unmixed felicity, how wouldst thou sigh for thy heart to be in his bosom, that of thee it might also be said, * There shall no evil happen unto thee: neither shall any plague come nigh thy dwelling.’4 But thou wilt say, if God can take away all trouble from all things,

## Chapter 2: The length of the Mercy of God ts his long-suffering

or patience.

Now the length of mercy is long-suffering or patience, which Scripture usually couples with mercy, as a part thereof: for thus David speaks, ‘ The Lord is full of compassion and mercy: long-suffering and of great goodness’ ;3 and again, ‘The Lord is gracious and merciful : long - suffering and of great goodness.’ 4 Wonderful indeed is this long-suffering and patience of God our most merciful Father towards the human race, such as we do not find in a master towards his servants, nor in parents towards their children, though all are men.

And first, God is long-suffering towards sinners, waiting for them with incredible patience, sometimes from their early youth to extreme old age, permitting them to blaspheme his Name and to neglect his law, and in the meanwhile doing good to them and giving them rain from heaven, and fruitful seasons, filling their hearts with food and gladness,5 as saith the Apostle. And who is there among men, lord or parent, so mild and easy, who if he sees himself despised and harmfully used by slaves or sons, and knowing that they have continued a long time in this perversity, would not at length drive them out of his house ? For some men have pity on other men because they are in need of their help, and this is indeed the lowest degree of pity, since it does not rise above our convenience; and after this measure we have pity on horses and dogs and cattle. Others have mercy on others on account of relationship or friendship, namely because they are children, brothers, acquaintances, or friends, and this degree is a little higher, and begins to have the form of virtue. Certain, again, have mercy on others because they are neighbours, that is they are men as they themselves, created by the same God and of the same mould: therefore they do not discriminate between friends and enemies, good and evil, their own countrymen or foreigners, but they have pity upon all men, because they know them to be created in the image of God: and this is the highest degree to which mortal men can rise. But God hath pity upon all things because they are his creatures, and specially he hath pity upon men, because they are in his image, and more especially he hath pity on the righteous, because they are his children, heirs of his kingdom and co-heirs with the only-begotten Son. But if thou inquirest why God created the world, why he made man in his image, why he adopted him as his child and made him inheritor of his kingdom, there is no answer but this, because he so willed. And why did he will it, except that he is good? for goodness pours itself forth and communicates itself freely. Therefore his mercy is established in the heavens and from that highest dwelling, namely from the Heart of the highest Father, it came down to earth and filled it, as the prophet sings, ‘The earth is full of the goodness of the Lord.’ 3

## Chapter 4: The depth of the Mercy of God is seen in its effects

Ir remains to consider the depth of the mercy of God. As the height of the mercy is most manifest in the cause, so the depth would seem to be in its effects. That mercy therefore is not said to be deep, but rather shallow, and superficial, which descends to words alone : that is deeper which raises and refreshes the unhappy, not only with consolations but with benefits: that very deep which comforts the needy not only by speech and deeds, but also by suffering and undergoing labours and sorrows for their sakes. God, whose mercy is infinite, hath pity upon us in all these ways. And first, he hath sent to us letters of consolation which are the divine Scriptures, of which the Maccabees say, ‘ We have the holy books of Scripture in our hands to comfort us.’t And not in letters only, but also by sermons of preachers, ambassadors for Christ to our reconciliation ;2, and God speaks to us by interior inspirations, promising us his help and protection: ‘ I will hearken,’ saith David, ‘ what the Lord God will say concerning me: for he shall speak peace unto his people and to his saints, that they turn not again.’ 3 _ Secondly, the blessings of the mercy of God against our manifold miseries, both spiritual and temporal, are so many that they cannot be counted, for “He crowneth us with mercy and loving-kindness ’;4 that is, he surrounds us on all sides with the benefits of his mercy.
