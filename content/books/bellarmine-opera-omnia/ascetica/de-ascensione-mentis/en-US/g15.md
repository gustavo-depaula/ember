## Chapter 1: The breadth of the righteousness of God is universal justice

Rae righteousness of God is taken in Holy Scripture in four ways: first as universal righteousness, which comprises all virtues and is the same as holiness or uprightness. Thus, ‘ The Lord is righteous in all his ways and holy in all his works.’! Next it is taken as truth or fidelity, as in another Psalm, ‘ That thou mayst be justified in thy saying ’;? thirdly it stands for justice distributing rewards, as in the second Epistle to Timothy, ‘There is laid up for me a crown of righteousness, which the Lord, the righteous judge, shall give me at that day ’;3 and lastly for justice punishing sins, as in another Psalm, ‘ Upon the ungodly he shall rain snares, fire and brimstone, storm and tempest: this shall be their portion to drink, for the righteous Lord loveth righteousness.’ 4

Therefore the greatness of the divine righteousness may be noted thus, by considering the breadth of his universal righteousness: the length thereof, that is of his truth and fidelity: the height of his righteousness distributing rewards in heaven: and its depth scourging the wicked in hell with everlasting punishments.

And to begin with the breadth: that righteousness is called by men universal, which so places a man that in every action he may conduct himself according to all the laws: and in this it comprehends all virtues, both theological and moral. But there is also a certain virtue called charity, which embraces all virtues in her bosom, commands the action of all virtues, and directs them to their final end. And this virtue, although it be a particular theological virtue, may yet be rightly called universal righteousness : for it so places man that he may conduct himself rightly towards God and towards his neighbour and thus fulfil the whole law. Thus the Apostle says, ‘ Love worketh no ill,’ and, “He that loveth, hath fulfilled the law,’ and, ‘ Love is the fulfilling of the law,’! and Saint Augustine in his book on Nature and Grace says, ‘ Love begun is righteousness begun, love increased, righteousness increased, great love, great righteousness, perfect love is perfect righteousness.’ 2

Moreover, in God are all virtues without any imperfection, and in the place of those which may imply imperfection there is something far better and more excellent ; and thus no goodness is wanting to him, since in him goodness and holiness are so great and so boundless, that in the best sense he is worthy to be called the only good and only holy.3 Faith is therefore in God not a theological virtue, because faith is ‘of those things which are not seen ’;4 but God sees all things. Nor is there hope in God, because hope is the expectation of things to come; but God expects nothing, since from eternity he possesses all things. Neither is there in God repentance for sin, because God cannot sin: nor humility, since humility hinders a man from rising vainly above himself, and causes him to remain in his proper place; but God has nothing above himself

## Chapter 3: The height of the righteousness of God is percetved in the bestowal of heavenly rewards

Tue height of the righteousness of God is perceived in the bestowal of heavenly reward which God himself, as the supreme and most righteous judge, hath prepared for those that have lived godly and righteously. And first we shall discern how great is the magnitude of his righteousness if we compare God as a Judge, with men that are judges: and next if we compare reward with reward, that 1s, the reward which God will give, with that which men usually give.

Men as judges, even when princes or prelates, who have subordinates or servants, for the most part do not give just rewards to those that deserve them, for either they are unable for want of ability to give all according to their deserts, or because they do not know accurately what they deserve, since this depends on sincerity of heart and devotion: or again, because of their own

covetousness or malice or some other perverse inclination, they will not bestow a just reward on the righteous labours of their underlings: or lastly, because they themselves are overtaken by death before they can pay the rewards they owe: or they to whom the rewards are due depart this life before they begin to taste the fruits of their labours. But God bestows upon all righteous men rewards for all their good deeds, not only just rewards, but even beyond their deserts; for what good deed can be more lowly and more hidden than to give a cup of cold water to the thirsty ? And yet for this the Lord hath promised a reward,! and of the magnificent reward promised by the Lord, Saint Luke writes, ‘Good measure, pressed down and shaken together and running over, shall men give into your bosom.’2 Nor is there any danger that means will be wanting to God for giving rewards to all the righteous, since he himself is Lord of all things and can with his word alone multiply and increase all things to an infinite degree. And there is no fear that perchance he will be deceived in the number or value of the good deeds, since he is most wise, and all things lie open before his eyes and he himself trieth the reins and the heart of his well-deserving servants, and therefore he understands the mind, intention, fervour, and diligence with which they did all these things. And no one could suspect that there should be in God any ill will to defraud his servants and his children of their just recompense, since he is faithful in all his words. And lastly, he cannot die, since before all he is immortal, and all things live unto him, so that there is no danger of any, on account of death, being deprived of the due rewards.
