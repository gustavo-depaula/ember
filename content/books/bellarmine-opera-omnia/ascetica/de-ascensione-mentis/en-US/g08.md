## Chapter 1: The Soul is a created spirit: God uncreated Spirit and the Creator of all things

Now we find human souls higher in dignity than all bodies, and we know that they belong to the lowest order of spiritual beings, between whom and God we find no intermediate being, save the hierarchies and ranks of angels.

The human soul possesses such a likeness to God its Creator, that I hardly know whether by any other way a man could ascend to God more easily than by consideration of his own soul.

Thus it is that God wills man to be without excuse if he has no knowledge of God, since by the help of God he may easily draw it from the knowledge he possesses of his own soul.t

Wherefore greatly may the soul rejoice in being spiritual in the nature of its being, and therefore by the nobility of its nature superior to the heavens and the stars, and also how deeply ought it to humble itself and submit to God its Creator, since from nothing it was made, and is of itself nothing !

## Chapter 6: The free judgement of man is compared with the freedom of God

In the sixth place, the soul of man is endowed with a free judgement of the will, which is common to him with God himself and with the angels, and which separates him from other created beings. Great 1s this nobility and a wonderful excellence.

But in God, the Creator of all things, liberty exceeds it so greatly that the freedom of the soul, when compared with it, seems scarcely to be its shadow.

First, the liberty of the human will is weak, and easily prone to choose for itself evil and harmful things: the freedom of the divine will is very strong and can in no way fail or be inclined to evil. For as to be subject to death is a proof of the weakness of the mortal body, to be unable to die is a proof of the strength of the glorified body: so also to be able to sin is a weakness of the free judgement, not to be able to sin will be the health of the judgement when in the heavenly country God will bestow upon us by grace that which he himself for ever possesses by nature.

Hence our free judgement is indeed free, for it 1s able to will and not to will, or even to will and to refuse: but it is not able to accomplish what it wills, or to cause that what it does not will should not take place, even as regards itself, and how much more in regard to others. Hear the Apostle lamenting in his letter to the Romans, * For the good that I would I do not, but the evil which I would not, that I do.’ And who of us has not experienced this? The presence of the soul in the body is a picture of the existence of God in created things.

EicHTH: the human soul is in the human body, but in a way very different from that in which the souls of beasts are within their bodies.

The souls of brutes are material, and extend to the . extent of the body, so that part is in a part, and the whole in the whole. But the human soul which is indivisible spirit, is in a wonderful way wholly in the whole and also whole in each part: and when it fills the whole body it nevertheless occupies no place in the body: and when the body grows, the soul does not grow, but that which before was not, now begins to be: and if a limb is cut off or burnt, the soul is not on that account lessened or dried up, but in that limb, where it was before, it ceases to exist, and without any bruise or mutilation of itself.

This is a true picture of the existence of God in created things. For God is indivisible Spirit: and although he fills the whole world and all its parts, he does not occupy any space, and he is wholly in the whole world and wholly in any part of it: and when a ~ new creature is produced, God begins to exist in it, and yet he is not moved: and when any creature ts perchance destroyed or dies, God is not destroyed, nor does he die, but he ceases to be in that creature, and does not change his place.

God surpasses the soul by a long way. If the soul is in the body and controls and moves it, it has of necessity to take the form of the body, and to be united with it so that of soul and body one man should be made. God does not need that a form should be produced, nor that the world should have a soul, nor that of himself and the world one substance should be produced : but from his own immensity, he has this, that

Next, the soul, although it may be said to exist in the whole body, yet it does not belong to it, except in the living and animate parts; hence it is not in the humours, in the hair, nails, or in limbs which are withered or dead: but God is absolutely in all things, not only in corporeal, but also in spiritual, and it is not possible that anything should exist in which God is not. Besides, the soul does not exist except in its own body, and in that narrow and confined space all the parts are gathered together: for if any part is separated from the others, in that part the soul cannot exist.

But God is wholly in this whole, although the whole is exceeding great, and its parts, though very close, not united together, and if another world were formed and yet another, God would be in them all. For thus it is written, “ Heaven and the heaven of heavens cannot contain thee.’ And if other heavens and other earths should be formed, in them also he would be: and if new heavens and new earths were multiplied without end he would fill all things, and where he was not there would be altogether nothing.

Next, the soul of man produces a word which does not last long, and the will produces love which has no long continuance ; but God the Father begets the eternal Word and the Father and the Son breathe forth the eternal Holy Spirit, for God without the Word and without his Spirit cannot exist.

Lastly, the soul of man by one word represents one thing, and so he multiplies words, not only of the mind, but of the lips, and the human will is forced to produce many acts of love if it desire to love many objects: but God speaks all truth by one single Word, and loves all good things by one act of love.
