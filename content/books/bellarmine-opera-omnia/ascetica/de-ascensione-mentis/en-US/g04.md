# Step IV: Consideration of Waters and Chiefly of Fountains

## Chapter 1: W ater cleanseth bodily stains ; God cleanseth spiritual

Me tes holdeth the second place among the elements: and if rightly looked upon, a step may be made of it to assist the heart’s ascent towards God. And first let us consider water in general, and afterwards the particular ascent towards God which we learn from wells. Water is moist and cold, and on this account it has, as it were, five properties: it washes and wipes away stains; it quenches fire; refreshes and tempers the heat of thirst; joins many and different things into one whole; and lastly, so low as it sinks, so high will it rise again. All these properties are plain symbols and footsteps of that God who is the Creator of all things. -

Water washes off bodily stains ; God washes off those that are spiritual. ‘Thou shalt wash me,’ says David, ‘and I shall be whiter than snow.’ For though we say contrition cleanses the heart from sin, that is from stain, and sacraments, priests, almsgiving, and other works of piety, cleanse : yet all these are either the instruments or dispositions, and the author of the cleansing is God alone. ‘ I, even I, am he that blotteth out thy transgressions for mine own sake.’! And so the Pharisees, who murmured against Christ, said,

‘Who can forgive sins but God alone ?’’! They were not mistaken in attributing to God alone the supreme power of forgiving sins, but they were wrong in not believing Christ to be God, and therefore they were blaspheming and speaking truth at the same time. And not only does God cleanse the stains of the heart after the manner of water, but he wills to be called by the Name: for thus writes Saint John: ‘ He that believeth on me, as the Scripture hath said, out of his belly shall flow rivers of living water. But this spake he of the Spirit, which they that believe on him should receive: for the Holy Ghost was not yet given; because that Jesus was not yet glorified.’ Therefore the Holy Spirit, who is indeed God, is the living water, and of this water Ezekiel says, ‘I will sprinkle clean water upon you, and ye shall be clean from all your filthiness.’ 3

And since that heavenly uncreated water far surpasses in power created and earthly water, we will note three differences between the washing of created and uncreated water. First, created water washes the stains of the body, but not all: for there are many which it is powerless to cleanse without the help of soap or other instruments; but uncreated water entirely cleanses all stains ; thus we read in a passage shortly before that quoted above, ‘ And ye shall be cleansed from all your iniquities.’ Secondly, created water seldom so Wipes away all stains as to leave no trace or mark of the stain; but uncreated water so cleanses that the thing cleansed is made more shining and beautiful than it was before it was defiled. ‘ Thou shalt wash me,’ saith David, ‘ and I shall be whiter than snow ’; 4 and the Lord saith by Isaiah, ‘ Though your sins be as scarlet, they shall be as white as snow; though they be red like crimson, they shall be as wool.’5 And

Who can understand, O Lord, how wonderfully thou dost breathe faith into the hearts of the unbelieying, and pourest humility into the hearts of the proud, and instillest charity into the hearts of thine enemies, so that a man who a short time before was breathing threats and slaughter and persecuting thee in the persons of thy disciples, is suddenly changed, and for thy sake and for thy Church willingly bears the threats and blows of persecutors ?! It is too much for me to penetrate into these thy secrets and I had rather know the efficacy of thy grace by experience than by search. And as I know that that water of thine is a gracious rain, refreshing thine inheritance, as thy prophet sang,? But let us pass on to the rest.

## Chapter 2: Water quenches fire: God quenches the flame of evil desires

Water quenches fire, and that heavenly water, namely the grace of the Holy Spirit, in a wondrous manner quenches the fire of carnal desires. Fasting and afflict-

ing of the body indeed avail much in this quenching, but only when they are used as means of the grace of the Holy Ghost: otherwise of themselves alone they are of no great value. For love it is which controls the affections and movements of the mind; it rules all men, and to it all men submit. Love will not be forced, and if it be hindered in one path it will find a passage by another. Love fears nothing, it ventures everything, conquers all things, thinks nothing hard or impossible for itself: in short, love yields to nothing but a greater or more powerful love. Thus a love altogether carnal, whether it pursues riches or pleasures, yields only to the love of God, and as soon as the water of the Holy Spirit begins to be instilled into the heart of a man, carnal love forthwith begins to cool. Saint Augustine may be our witness, who when he was accustomed to indulge his lust, considered it impossib.e to do without alliance with a woman: yet when he began to taste the grace of the Holy Spirit he exclaimed: ‘ How sweet did it at once become to me, to waive the sweetness of those toys! and what I feared to be parted from was now a joy to part with. For thou didst cast them forth from me, thou true and highest sweetness. Thou castedst them forth, and for them enteredst in thyself, sweeter than all pleasure, though not to flesh and blood; brighter than all light, but more hidden than all depths, higher than all honour, but not to the high in their own conceits.’ !

## Chapter 4: Water unites material things : God accomplishes union of spirits

But with far greater easé and permanence doth the water of the Holy Spirit work, making many men to be ‘ of one heart and of one soul,’ which in the book of the Acts of the Apostles 3 is said of the first Christians, upon whom the Holy Ghost had immediately before descended. ## Chapter 6: The spring of water an image of God as the Fount of being

I come now to the likeness to God which we see in springs of water; for thence the mind may rise to contemplate the exceeding and wondrous excellence of the Creator. Not without cause does Holy Scripture speak of God as the ‘ well of life,’! ‘the fountain of wisdom,’ 2 ‘ the fountain of living waters.’3 And that he is the fount of being may be gathered from the words of God himself spoken to Moses,‘ I am that I am: I am hath sent me unto you.’4 And all these names the Apostle seems to have gathered into one, when he says, ‘ In him we live and move and have our being.’5 For in him we exist as in the fountain of being, in him we live as in the fountain of life, and in him we move as in the fountain of wisdom, for “Wisdom is more moving than any motion: she passeth and goeth through all things by reason of her pureness.’ 6

Here among ourselves, water has this quality, namely, that from it rivers spring, and if they cease to draw from their source they gradually become dry: but the spring itself is not dependent on rivers because it does not receive its waters from them, but holds them in itself, and shares them with others. This is a true symbol and picture of the Divinity, for God is the very true fountain of being: he receives being from no other, and all things receive their being from him: God receives his being from no other because the Essence of God is his existence and his existence is his Essence, so that it can neither happen nor be conceived that God should not always have existed, or that he will not always exist. But the pre-eminence of him that is the fountain of being does not consist only in receiving his being from no other fountain and in communicating being to all other things. Waters of springs and waters of rivers are of the same nature, and although the waters of springs do not receive their waters from other springs, they yet have some other causes of their being, namely mists, and they again have other causes, until we reach the final Cause, which is God. Now, my soul, God is thy Creator, and his Being is not of the same kind as the being of created things, but is separated from them by an infinite distance of dignity and nobility and excellence, and he is truly and really the fountain of being, but he is wholly without any cause.

A well of created water, as was said, is not derived from other water, yet it is from another source: the uncreated fountain of being has nothing before himself, depends on nothing, lacks nothing: nothing can hurt him, but all things depend upon him and he is able to extinguish all created things and ‘at a beck can cast down all the world’ as the most valiant Maccabeus says.t Admire, O my soul, this pre-eminence, this beginning without beginning, cause without cause, infinite, boundless, immense, and absolutely necessary Being, since all other things in comparison with him are mere contingencies.

## Chapter 7: God the fountain of life has life in himself

AND in truth we most rightly say that God 1s the fountain of life, for he lives and has life in himself, yea is himself eternal life. ‘This is the true God and ‘eternal life,’ saith Saint John,‘ and all things that live receive life from that fountain, and if he ceased to bestow life they would fail and turn again into their dust as the holy prophet David says.?

To beget a being like unto oneself is a property of living beings. God begets his Son, wholly like unto himself, God God, and the Living One the Living One: for ‘as the Father hath life in himself, so hath he given to the Son to have life in himself,’ as Saint John witnesseth in his Gospel.3 Now the Father hath life in himself because he is the fountain of life and receiveth life from no other: and he hath given to the Son to have life in himself, because he hath given him the same life which he himself hath, and for this reason the Son also is a fountain of life, but a fountain of life from the fountain of life, as he is God from God, and Light from Light. Who can explain, nay, who can conceive, what that life of God is, and what is that fountain of life in which living things in earth and in heaven have their source ? Life as it is known to us in this state of exile is nothing else than an inward principle of movement: for those things are said by us to be alive which in some way move themselves; and for this reason we commonly speak of the waters of rivers as living waters, and those in pools as dead, since the former appear to be moved of themselves and the latter cannot move unless they be driven by winds, or by some other external force. Thy God, O my soul, doth most truly live, and he is the author and

the fountain of life. For he himself impresses this truth on us in many passages of Holy Scripture: ‘ I live, saith the Lord ’’;1 and the prophets often repeat, ‘ As the Lord liveth ’;2 and God, lamenting over his people, saith, ‘ They have forsaken me, the fountain of living waters.’3 And nevertheless he neither moves, nor 1s he moved by any other : ‘I am God, I change not,’ 4 and elsewhere, ‘God is not the son of man that he should repent.’5 And in the Church’s hymn which we say daily :—

Who from the morn till evening’s ray, Through all its changes guid’st the day.6

And though God begets the Son, he begets him without changing; and although he sees, hears, speaks, loves, pities, judges, it is without moving that he does all these things; and although he creates and preserves things or, on the contrary, destroys and scatters them, and again renews and changes them, yet he himself works resting and changes things while himself unmoved.

How then doth he live if he moveth not, and how can he not live if he is the fountain and author of life ? This difficulty is easily removed. For to be alive it is quite sufficient for the living thing to act of itself and not to be moved by another: for the most part, life in created things is a principle of internal movement, because created things are imperfect and need many things in order that they may fulfil the functions of life, but God is infinite perfection and needs nothing outside himself: he acts of himself and is not moved by another, and he has no need of movement or change. Created things need change that they may generate and be generated; because they beget outside

Created things need movement for increase, because they are born imperfect: but God the Son is born most perfect, and God the Holy Ghost is breathed forth also and produced most perfect.

Created things need the movement of alteration, that they may obtain the various qualities of which they are in need: but God is in need of nothing, seeing his Essence is absolute perfection.

Created things require to move from place to place, because they are not everywhere: but God is wholly everywhere.

Besides this, created things need many things in order to see, hear, speak, or work, because though indeed they have a certain life, yet it is imperfect and very poor: but God needs nothing outside himself in order to see and hear all things, to speak or to work all things in all; because he has not only life, but life most rich and happy, and he himself is life and the fountain of life.

Let us take an example in the act of seeing: in order that a man may see he needs power of vision which is. distinct from the soul which properly lives: he needs an object, some coloured body placed outside himself: he needs sunlight or else light from some other luminous body: he needs a medium, that is a clearly-marked object: he needs a sensible species which can be drawn from the object to his sight: he needs the bodily organ, that is the eye, made of various humours and fleshy membranes: he needs sensitive spirits, and optic nerves through which those spirits

may pass: he needs distance duly proportioned: he needs the application of power.

See how many minute things men and other creatures need that they may accomplish one vital action: but God, who in truth holds all life within himself, is in need of nothing at all: his infinite Essence is for him power, object, species, light, and all things else of himself, by himself, and in himself. God sees all things that are, that have been, that will be; and what they can be he clearly knows: and before the world was, God saw all things and nothing new has increased his knowledge or vision by the creation of all things. What therefore shalt thou be, O my soul, when thou art a partaker of his life? What great demand did God make upon thee when he bid thee lay down that bodily, animal life, imperfect as it is, and needy, lay it down for thy brethren and for God himself, that thou mayst become partaker of an eternal life most rich and blissful ? And if this command to despise life is not a great demand, how slight and small it should seem to thee to be bidden to bestow freely thy dead riches on the poor, and to abstain from carnal pleasures, truly to renounce the devil and his pomps, and to yearn with the whole affection of thy heart after that life which alone is the true life.

Aspire to that bliss, and that thou mayst safely attain to it, love the Lord Jesus Christ with all thy heart; in him are all the treasures of the wisdom and knowledge of God: 3 and he himself said in the Gospel, ‘He that loveth me shall be loved of my Father, and I will love him and will manifest myself to him.’ 4 And what is that ‘I will manifest myself to him’ but, I will manifest all the treasures of the knowledge and wisdom of God which are hidden in myself? Most certainly all men naturally desire knowledge, and though at the present time many persons find a certain satisfaction in worldly pleasures, yet when we lay down the body which is corruptible and now weighs down the soul, we shall find the fire of this desire for knowledge blazes above all other desires. How great then will be thy happiness, O soul, when Christ thy Lover and thy Beloved shall show thee all the treasures of the knowledge and the wisdom of God !_ But in order that thou be not defrauded of so great a hope, be diligent

in keeping the commandments of Christ: for he hath said, ‘ If a man love me, he will keep my words, . . . and he that loveth me not, keepeth not my sayings.’ ! And in the meanwhile let thy wisdom be that which holy Job describes, ‘The fear of the Lord, that is wisdom; and to depart from evil is understanding.’ 2 And what good soever thou seest in creatures, know that it flows down from God, the fountain of all goodness, and so with blessed Francis learn to taste of the fount of all good in each created thing, even though it be but in small streams.
