David, being about to exhort the people, in rather a long discourse, endeavours, at the outset, to arrest their attention by saying he is going to speak on matters of utility and importance. “ Attend to my law ;” to my precepts, which, like good and most wi-e laws, will direct you to happiness... And he repeats the same at greater length when he says, ‘ zneline your ears;” and what he expressed at first by the words, “‘ my law,” he now expresses by the words, “to the words of my mouth;” thereby insinuating that when he mentioned the law, he did not mean the law of Moses, though often called simply the law, but his own words, with which he meant to instruct and to exhort his people; in which sense Christ himself uses the term when he said, John xv., “ But that the word may be fulfilled which is written in their law, they have hated me without cause.” To incline the ear, when applied to the people, means to hear with humility and obedience, but, when applied to God, means to hear with clemency and mercy.
