## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-27 — **address form**: the letter is from the Church of Rome to the Church of Corinth, a collective addressee. "You" → plural **vós** throughout. God in the prayers (Ch. 59–61) and in the quoted Psalms → singular **tu**, the usual Portuguese liturgical form.
- 2026-09-27 — **Scripture**: the quotations (LXX-based and often loose) follow Keith's English clause by clause, not a Portuguese Bible. Examples: Ch. 3 "and was enlarged and became fat, and kicked"; Ch. 4 the LXX Cain speech ("If you offer rightly, but do not divide rightly…"); Ch. 16 the long Isaiah 53, including "He is, as it were, a child"; Ch. 18 Psalm 51 in the source's wording ("establish me by Your governing Spirit" → "firma-me com o teu Espírito soberano"); Ch. 42 "I will appoint their bishops in righteousness, and their deacons in faith". The one Latin/biblical formula adopted as is: "Crescei e multiplicai-vos" (Ch. 33), because it translates "Increase and multiply" word for word.
- 2026-09-27 — **Scripture references**: the inline New Advent references are kept in the same place, mid-sentence, as in `polycarp/epistle-to-the-philippians/pt-BR`, with Portuguese Catholic book names (Atos, 1 Pedro, Tito, Provérbios, Deuteronômio, Sabedoria, Gênesis, Êxodo, Números, Ezequiel, Isaías, Hebreus, Jó, Tiago, Romanos, Mateus, Lucas, Malaquias, Daniel, 1 Coríntios, Efésios, Judite, 1 Samuel). References with Roman-numeral chapters ("Genesis xxxvii", "Jonah iii", "Genesis xix", "Exodus xiv") keep their Roman numerals: "Gênesis xxxvii", "Jonas iii", etc.
- 2026-09-27 — **footnotes**: this New Advent edition has no footnotes. The ANF editorial apparatus was already removed on import, so there was nothing to drop, and no translator notes were added.
- 2026-09-27 — **brackets**: every editorial bracket in the English (`[of the church]`, `[the Scripture]`, …) is kept, with the same count per paragraph. When the English bracket holds a word Portuguese needs anyway, it still goes inside the bracket, as far as the grammar allows. Ch. 15 keeps the source's unbalanced `[and “let the Lord destroy all the lying lips, ]` exactly as it is.
- 2026-09-27 — **source oddities left as they are**:
  - Ch. 15 "They loved Him with their **month**" is an obvious typo for "mouth", translated as "boca". The en-US file was not changed (out of scope for this pass); worth fixing there as OCR-class corruption.
  - Ch. 25 "the juices of the **deed** bird" is a typo for "dead", translated as "ave morta". Same note.
  - Ch. 45 "Ananias, and Azarias, and **Michael**": the Greek and the biblical name is Misael, but Keith's English prints "Michael". Rendered "Miguel" so the language files agree; this is the edition's reading, not an OCR error.
  - Ch. 23 the quotation ends in `;”` followed by a capitalized "You foolish ones!", and Ch. 13 has `” )`. Both punctuation quirks are kept.
- 2026-09-27 — **chapter headings**: the ANF chapter summaries ("Chapter N. …") are part of the imported source and are translated as headings ("Capítulo N. …"), in sentence case like the sibling letters. "Hortatory" (Ch. 63) → "Exortação"; "Summary and Conclusory" (Ch. 62) → "Resumo e conclusão".
- 2026-09-27 — **"instant gentleness"** (Ch. 58, 62), meaning constant or earnest (ἐκτενής), → "constante mansidão". "Instant in prayer" (Ch. 59) → "perseverantes na oração".
- 2026-09-27 — **review round 1 fixes**: Ch. 14 heading had an ungrammatical `antes que` + bare noun phrase; corrected to the comparative already used in the chapter's own body ("obedecer antes a Deus do que aos autores da sedição"). Ch. 26 opening verb was future ("Consideraremos") where the English is a present-tense rhetorical question; corrected to "Consideramos". Ch. 65 heading added "final" to "The Benediction" ("A bênção final"); trimmed to "A bênção" to match the English exactly.
- 2026-09-27 — **review round 1, checked and left as is** (not defects): Ch. 11 "pillar of salt" → "estátua de sal" reads like a familiar-Bible pull, but a corpus-wide grep shows this exact phrasing used independently in three unrelated books (Morrow, Montfort, a catechism-in-pictures) — it's the standard Brazilian idiom, not a mistranslation. Ch. 30 "self-control" → "domínio de nós mesmos" instead of the Key Terms table's "domínio de si" (used elsewhere, e.g. Ch. 35, Ch. 64 — not Ch. 62, which has no "self-control" in the English at all): same meaning, a lexical variant rather than an error; not normalized. Ch. 61 "rulers and governors" → "governantes e magistrados" ("magistrados" = magistrates, not literally "governors"): defensible in the Roman-administrative context, treated as a word choice. Ch. 59 "killest and makest to live" → "fazes morrer e fazes viver" (literally "makest to die"): a deliberate parallel with "fazes viver", not a sense error. Ch. 37 heading capitalizes "Chefe" for Christ, by analogy with the corpus's capitalized-title convention (Sumo Sacerdote, Criador, Soberano) — not yet in the Key Terms table; worth adding if the pairing recurs in another book.
- 2026-09-27 — **review round 3**: a per-chapter term-frequency diff was run on every Key Terms row in both directions; a commitment-point pass checked every relative-clause referent, pronoun antecedent, and adversative/causal connector; every falsifiable claim in this journal (quoted forms, chapter cites, counts) was grepped against both chapter files. No defects surfaced. The apparent frequency mismatches were all explained by grep artifacts, not translation errors, and are recorded so a later round doesn't re-investigate them: "beloved" (20 in English) includes two non-vocative "beloved Son"/"my beloved" uses (Ch. 15, 59) correctly rendered as singular "amado", outside the vocative "amados" count; "Amen"/"Amém" and "Creator+Maker"/"Criador" tie exactly (11=11, 9=9) once counted precisely; "self-control"/"domínio de si" and "High Priest"/"Sumo Sacerdote" (capitalized vs. lowercase) tie exactly per the round 1 notes above; "godly"/"godliness" vs. "piedoso"/"piedade" tie exactly once feminine and plural inflections ("piedosa", "sedições", "eleitos") are counted, and the two extra "piedade" hits (Ch. 18, 59) translate the unrelated word "mercy"/"pity"; "Ruler"/"Soberano" ties exactly once the generic lowercase "ruler(s)/governor(s)" (→ "governantes") is excluded, per the Ch. 61 note above. The prayer of Ch. 59–61 was checked clause by clause for the tu/vós split: every relative clause addressing God stays in "tu" form throughout, with no lapse into "vós".
