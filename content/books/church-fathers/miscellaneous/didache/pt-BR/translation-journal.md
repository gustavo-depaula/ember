# Translation Journal — The Didache (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (trans. M.B. Riddle, Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 7; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Key Terms

| English | Portuguese | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| The Didache | Didaquê | standard Brazilian Portuguese transliteration of the Greek Διδαχή ("teaching"), the name under which this work is universally known in Portuguese Patristics editions (Vozes/Paulus "Padres Apostólicos" collection uses "Didaquê") |
| The Lord's Teaching Through the Twelve Apostles to the Nations | O Ensinamento do Senhor às Nações, Através dos Doze Apóstolos | the work's traditional subtitle, rendered literally |
| the two ways (life / death) | os dois caminhos (da vida / da morte) | central structural metaphor of Ch. 1–6; kept literal throughout, matching how the metaphor is cited in every published Portuguese edition |
| Ways of Life / Way of Death headings | Caminho da Vida / Caminho da Morte | capitalized as proper structural terms within the headings, consistent with how the corpus's other doctrinal/liturgical proper terms are capitalized |
| gifts (free gifts) | dons (dons gratuitos) | Ch. 1 — "of our own blessings (free gifts)"; kept the parenthetical gloss as the English does |
| the Thanksgiving (Eucharist) | a Ação de Graças (Eucaristia) | Ch. 9–10 headings and body — "Eucharist" transliterated as the now-standard Portuguese liturgical term, paired with the literal translation "Ação de Graças" exactly as the English source pairs "Thanksgiving (Eucharist)" |
| Marana tha | Marana tá | kept as the Aramaic liturgical acclamation, per the corpus's convention of preserving untranslated liturgical formulas (cf. "Amém" left as-is); not expanded to "Vem, Senhor" since the source itself keeps the transliterated form rather than translating it |
| Christ-monger (v. 12, χριστέμπορος) | mercador de Cristo | literal compound, matching the English's own literal compound rather than a looser paraphrase like "explorador" |
| first-fruits | primícias | standard theological/biblical term |
| bishops and deacons | bispos e diáconos | standard ecclesiastical terms, matching every other church-fathers pt-BR translation |
| Christian Reproof (Ch. 15 heading) | A Correção Fraterna | the editorial heading names the general practice described in the chapter (mutual correction "not in anger, but in peace"); rendered as the settled corpus term for fraternal correction rather than a literal "Repreensão Cristã", which would read as harsher than the chapter's own peaceable tone |
| Watchfulness (Ch. 16 heading) | Vigilância | literal |
| Master (divine title, Ch. 10, distinct from "Lord" one sentence later in the same prayer) | Soberano | "Mestre" was unavailable — already the corpus's established term for "teacher" (Ch. 11/13/15's mestres); "Soberano" preserves a second, distinct divine vocative alongside "Senhor" rather than collapsing both English titles into one Portuguese word |

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-03: First pt-BR translation of a *miscellaneous/*-authored work in `content/books/church-fathers/` (the `Miscellaneous` author field is a catalog bucket for anonymous/collective works, not a literal author name — confirmed by checking all 17 sibling `book.json` files under `church-fathers/miscellaneous/`, all of which use the same `"en-US": "Miscellaneous"` placeholder). Rendered the pt-BR author field as **"Diversos"**, the natural Portuguese equivalent of the same bucket label.
- Single chapter file (Greeting-less; 16 numbered chapters + italic subtitle), ~3,080 words. One of the earliest and best-known post-apostolic Christian texts (c. 100), a natural next pick after the Ignatian epistles and the short Church Fathers fragments already translated in this pass.
- **Address form**: the whole work addresses the reader/community directly in the imperative second person ("thou"/"you" mixing singular precepts in Ch. 1–4 with plural community instructions from Ch. 7 onward). Followed the source's own switch: singular imperative ("amarás", "não matarás", "meu filho") for the individual moral precepts of Ch. 1–5, plural imperative ("batizai", "reuni-vos", "elegei") once the text turns to communal/liturgical practice from Ch. 6 onward — this mirrors the English's own shift from "thou" to "you [pl.]" and is not a translation choice so much as a direct carry-over of the source's structure.
- No footnotes, editorial or authorial, in the source beyond the inline Scripture cross-references (added by the 19th-century edition, not the ancient author) — these were kept inline exactly as the English source places them, following the established corpus convention (cf. Ignatian epistles) of preserving inline Scripture citations rather than treating them as droppable editorial apparatus.
- Scripture references preserved with the same book/chapter/verse as the English source, book names localized to standard Portuguese usage: Matthew → Mateus, Exodus → Êxodo, Luke → Lucas, Ephesians → Efésios, Colossians → Colossenses, Deuteronomy → Deuteronômio, 2 Thessalonians → 2 Tessalonicenses, 1 Timothy → 1 Timóteo. The one "cf." cross-reference (Ch. 13, Luke 10:7) kept its "cf." abbreviation, which is standard in Portuguese scholarly usage too.
- The Lord's Prayer (Ch. 8) rendered with the traditional Brazilian Catholic liturgical wording ("Pai nosso, que estais nos céus..." is the usual Mass form, but this source text uses a slightly different, older English wording than the Matthean text used liturgically today — e.g. "our daily (needful) bread" carries its own parenthetical gloss, and the doxology "for Yours is the power and the glory for ever" omits "kingdom"). Followed the English source's own wording and parentheticals literally rather than substituting the familiar liturgical Portuguese text, since this is a translation of *this* Ante-Nicene Fathers edition's English, not a citation of the Missal.
- No translator notes were added — the text is plain narrative/exhortatory prose throughout with no obscure allusions needing a gloss beyond what the inline Scripture references already supply.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 1** (3 parallel passes: structural/mechanical, monolingual pt-BR proofread, bilingual clause-by-clause fidelity). Structural pass came back fully clean (headings, paragraph counts, all 21 Scripture citations, subtitle placement, parentheticals). The other two passes together found six genuine defects, all fixed:
  - Ch. 1 — "shall pay the penalty, why he received and for what" had been softened to "prestará contas do porquê e do para quê recebeu" (will render an account), losing the clause's punitive force, and "do para quê" was itself an awkward, non-standard nominalization. Fixed to "pagará a pena — por que recebeu e para quê —", restoring both the penalty and the accounting.
  - Ch. 4 — "onde se profere o domínio do Senhor" paired "proferir" (a verb that collocates with speech) with the abstract noun "domínio", reading as non-idiomatic Portuguese. Fixed to "onde se proclama o senhorio do Senhor".
  - Ch. 4 — "pois se sois participantes" broke the sentence's own tu-singular register mid-clause (desviarás/dirás/tuas earlier in the same sentence) with no addressee shift signaled. Fixed to "pois se és participante".
  - Ch. 4 — the archaic English "lest ever they shall fear not God" (= lest they cease to fear God) had collapsed into a single negation, "para que não venham a temer... ao Deus", which literally says the goal of gentle treatment is that servants *stop* fearing God — the reverse of the source's sense. Fixed to "para que não venham a deixar de temer, eles também, ao Deus que está sobre ambos", restoring the "cease to" sense.
  - Ch. 16 — "the Son of God" (the specific christological title the deceiver counterfeits) had gone in lowercase, "como filho de Deus", inconsistent with this same file's own capitalization of the title elsewhere (Ch. 7 "do Filho", Ch. 10 "Deus (Filho) de David"). Fixed to "como o Filho de Deus".
  - Ch. 16 — minor parallelism break: the three-item list of end-times signs read "o sinal de X; o sinal de Y; e o terceiro, Z", dropping "sinal" from the third item alone. Fixed to "e o terceiro sinal, a ressurreição dos mortos" for consistency with the first two items.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 2** (2 parallel passes: one re-verified all six round-1 fixes + a fresh bilingual clause-by-clause pass over all 16 chapters, the other a monolingual pt-BR proofread + book.json consistency check). All six round-1 fixes confirmed sound, no regressions. Four new defects found and fixed:
  - "David" → "Davi" (2 occurrences, Ch. 9 and Ch. 10) — the corpus's own house style for the biblical king's name (213-to-23 "Davi" vs. "David" across all pt-BR content, including every other translated `church-fathers/` work), not a general BP-spelling call.
  - Ch. 4 — "não te aproximarás **para** a tua oração" used the wrong preposition; `aproximar-se` takes "de" in Portuguese, confirmed by ~20 other corpus uses of "aproximar-se de" and none of "aproximar-se para". Fixed to "não te aproximarás **da** tua oração".
  - Ch. 8 — the Lord's Prayer's "as in heaven, so on earth" had been rendered "assim **na terra como no céu**", reversing the source's heaven-then-earth order to match the familiar Brazilian liturgical Pai Nosso instead — the one place the standard liturgical wording crept back in, against this file's own documented decision to follow the English source's wording literally rather than the Missal text. Fixed to "assim **no céu como também na terra**".
  - Ch. 4 — "a stretcher forth of the hands to receive and a drawer of them back **to give**" had gone in as "...**para dar**", which in Portuguese reads as a purpose clause (withdraws the hand *in order to* give — self-contradictory) rather than the intended "quick to withdraw when it's time to give". Fixed to "...**na hora de** dar", removing the misleading purpose-clause reading while keeping the archaic cadence.
  - Ch. 11 — minor unmarked addition, "the prophet" had gained an unstated "verdadeiro" ("o profeta verdadeiro") not present in the English's simple contrast of "the false prophet and the prophet". Reverted to "o profeta" for exact parity.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 3** (2 parallel passes: a fresh full bilingual re-read of all 16 chapters plus verification of the five round-2 fixes, and an independent monolingual pt-BR proofread). The bilingual pass came back fully **clean** — all five round-2 fixes confirmed sound, zero new fidelity defects after three full passes over the text. The monolingual pass, reading the Portuguese with no English in view, still found three defects, all fixed:
  - Ch. 4 — "Na igreja confessarás" was lowercase while every other reference to the Church in this file ("tua Igreja" x2 in Ch. 9–10, "da Igreja" in Ch. 11) is capitalized as the proper theological term. Fixed to "Na Igreja".
  - Ch. 1 (both occurrences) — "não o reclames de volta" ("ask it not back") read as an English "claim it back" calque rather than natural Portuguese. Fixed to "não o exijas de volta".
  - Ch. 4 — "Não estarás indeciso quanto a se há de ser ou não" was a convoluted construction with an unclear referent for "ser", mirroring the awkwardness of the archaic English ("whether it shall be or no") too literally. Fixed to "quanto a se algo há de ser feito ou não", clarifying the referent while keeping the sentence's own register.
  - **Not a defect, worth recording so it isn't re-flagged**: the bilingual pass noted this file's tu/vós person switches occur between sentences, never within one — and, checked against Hoole's 1885 Didache translation (which marks the Greek's thee/ye distinction explicitly), this pt-BR translation's tu/vós pattern independently lines up with the actual underlying Greek's number shifts, which the en-US source (Riddle, ANF) flattens to number-neutral "you". Not something to "fix" toward the English's flattened form.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 4** (2 parallel passes: fresh full bilingual re-read + round-3 fix verification, and an independent monolingual proofread). All three round-3 fixes confirmed sound. Three new defects found and fixed, all narrow/isolated (the text is converging):
  - Ch. 10 — "Master almighty" and "Lord" are two distinct divine vocatives in the same Eucharistic prayer ("You, **Master** almighty, created..." / "**Remember, Lord**, Your Church..."), both of which had collapsed into a single "Senhor". Fixed by rendering "Master" as "Soberano" (see Key Terms) — restoring the two-title distinction without colliding with the corpus's existing "Mestre" = teacher convention.
  - Ch. 4 — "participante **naquilo**" was a regência slip; "participante" governs "de" in Portuguese (cf. "participantes da natureza divina," 2 Pedro 1:4). Fixed to "participante **daquilo**".
  - Ch. 14 — "dai **ação de graças**" was lowercase while the identical Eucharistic-rite term is capitalized as a formal/technical term everywhere else in the file ("a Ação de Graças (Eucaristia)" x3 in Ch. 9–10). Fixed to "dai **Ação de Graças**".
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 5** (2 parallel passes: fresh full bilingual re-read + round-4 fix verification, and an independent monolingual proofread). All three round-4 fixes confirmed sound. Two new defects found and fixed:
  - Ch. 8 (Lord's Prayer) — "bring us not into temptation" had been rendered "não nos **deixes cair em** tentação", the standard Brazilian liturgical Pai Nosso wording (a passive "let us not fall" image) rather than the source's own active "bring...into" (matching the Latin *ne nos inducas in tentationem*). This is the same "familiar liturgical text creeping back in" failure the round-2 heaven/earth fix caught earlier in this same prayer — round 2 fixed one instance and missed this second one. Fixed to "não nos **induzas à** tentação", the literal cognate of "inducas", consistent with this file's documented policy of following this specific English edition's wording rather than the Missal text.
  - Ch. 13 — "dai-**o** aos pobres" (referring back to "as primícias", fem. pl.) used a masculine singular clitic, disagreeing with its antecedent — an internal inconsistency, since the same paragraph correctly uses "dai-**as** aos profetas" a few sentences earlier for the identical noun. Fixed to "dai-**as** aos pobres".
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 6** (2 parallel passes: fresh full bilingual re-read + round-5 fix verification, and an independent monolingual proofread covering diacritics, all ~30 clitic pronouns, regência, and capitalization). Both round-5 fixes confirmed sound. **Both passes came back CLEAN** — zero new defects. The bilingual pass specifically re-scanned Ch. 9–10's prayers for the liturgical-creep failure mode found twice before (round 2, round 5) and found none — including confirming the Portuguese correctly preserves the English source's own doxology-order variation between Ch. 8/10 ("o poder e a glória") and Ch. 9 ("a glória e o poder") rather than normalizing all three to one default order. First of two consecutive clean rounds needed to close.
