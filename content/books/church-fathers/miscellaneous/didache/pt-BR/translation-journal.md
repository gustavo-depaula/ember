# Translation Journal — The Didache (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (trans. M.B. Riddle, Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 7; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Key Terms

| English | Portuguese | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| The Didache | Didaquê | standard Brazilian Portuguese transliteration of the Greek Διδαχή ("teaching"), the name under which this work is universally known in Portuguese Patristics editions (Vozes/Paulus "Padres Apostólicos" collection uses "Didaquê") |
| The Lord's Teaching Through the Twelve Apostles to the Nations | O Ensinamento do Senhor às Nações, Através dos Doze Apóstolos | the work's traditional subtitle, rendered literally |
| the two ways (life / death) | os dois caminhos (da vida / da morte) | central structural metaphor of Ch. 1–6; kept literal throughout, matching how the metaphor is cited in every published Portuguese edition |
| Ways of Life / Way of Death headings | Caminho da Vida / Caminho da Morte | capitalized as proper structural terms within the headings, consistent with how the corpus's other doctrinal/liturgical proper terms are capitalized |
| gifts (free gifts) | dons (dons gratuitos) | Ch. 1 — "of our own blessings (free gifts)"; kept the parenthetical gloss as the English does |
| the Thanksgiving (Eucharist) | a Ação de Graças (Eucaristia) | Ch. 9–10 headings and body — "Eucharist" transliterated as the now-standard Portuguese liturgical term, paired with the literal translation "Ação de Graças" exactly as the English source pairs "Thanksgiving (Eucharist)" |
| Marana tha | Marana tá | kept as the Aramaic liturgical acclamation, per the corpus's convention of preserving untranslated liturgical formulas (cf. "Amém" left as-is); not expanded to "Vem, Senhor" since the source itself keeps the transliterated form rather than translating it |
| Christ-monger (v. 12, χριστέμπορος) | mercador de Cristo | literal compound, matching the English's own literal compound rather than a looser paraphrase like "explorador" |
| first-fruits | primícias | standard theological/biblical term |
| bishops and deacons | bispos e diáconos | standard ecclesiastical terms, matching every other church-fathers pt-BR translation |
| Christian Reproof (Ch. 15 heading) | A Correção Fraterna | the editorial heading names the general practice described in the chapter (mutual correction "not in anger, but in peace"); rendered as the settled corpus term for fraternal correction rather than a literal "Repreensão Cristã", which would read as harsher than the chapter's own peaceable tone |
| Watchfulness (Ch. 16 heading) | Vigilância | literal |

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-03: First pt-BR translation of a *miscellaneous/*-authored work in `content/books/church-fathers/` (the `Miscellaneous` author field is a catalog bucket for anonymous/collective works, not a literal author name — confirmed by checking all 17 sibling `book.json` files under `church-fathers/miscellaneous/`, all of which use the same `"en-US": "Miscellaneous"` placeholder). Rendered the pt-BR author field as **"Diversos"**, the natural Portuguese equivalent of the same bucket label.
- Single chapter file (Greeting-less; 16 numbered chapters + italic subtitle), ~3,080 words. One of the earliest and best-known post-apostolic Christian texts (c. 100), a natural next pick after the Ignatian epistles and the short Church Fathers fragments already translated in this pass.
- **Address form**: the whole work addresses the reader/community directly in the imperative second person ("thou"/"you" mixing singular precepts in Ch. 1–4 with plural community instructions from Ch. 7 onward). Followed the source's own switch: singular imperative ("amarás", "não matarás", "meu filho") for the individual moral precepts of Ch. 1–5, plural imperative ("batizai", "reuni-vos", "elegei") once the text turns to communal/liturgical practice from Ch. 6 onward — this mirrors the English's own shift from "thou" to "you [pl.]" and is not a translation choice so much as a direct carry-over of the source's structure.
- No footnotes, editorial or authorial, in the source beyond the inline Scripture cross-references (added by the 19th-century edition, not the ancient author) — these were kept inline exactly as the English source places them, following the established corpus convention (cf. Ignatian epistles) of preserving inline Scripture citations rather than treating them as droppable editorial apparatus.
- Scripture references preserved with the same book/chapter/verse as the English source, book names localized to standard Portuguese usage: Matthew → Mateus, Exodus → Êxodo, Luke → Lucas, Ephesians → Efésios, Colossians → Colossenses, Deuteronomy → Deuteronômio, 2 Thessalonians → 2 Tessalonicenses, 1 Timothy → 1 Timóteo. The one "cf." cross-reference (Ch. 13, Luke 10:7) kept its "cf." abbreviation, which is standard in Portuguese scholarly usage too.
- The Lord's Prayer (Ch. 8) rendered with the traditional Brazilian Catholic liturgical wording ("Pai nosso, que estais nos céus..." is the usual Mass form, but this source text uses a slightly different, older English wording than the Matthean text used liturgically today — e.g. "our daily (needful) bread" carries its own parenthetical gloss, and the doxology "for Yours is the power and the glory for ever" omits "kingdom"). Followed the English source's own wording and parentheticals literally rather than substituting the familiar liturgical Portuguese text, since this is a translation of *this* Ante-Nicene Fathers edition's English, not a citation of the Missal.
- No translator notes were added — the text is plain narrative/exhortatory prose throughout with no obscure allusions needing a gloss beyond what the inline Scripture references already supply.
