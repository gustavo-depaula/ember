# Translation Journal — The Passion of the Scillitan Martyrs (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (J. Armitage Robinson translation, Ante-Nicene Fathers Vol. 9)
Target: pt-BR

## Key Terms

|---------|-----------|-------|
| Scillitan Martyrs | Mártires Escilitanos | "Scillitani" (from Scillium/Scillita, a town in Roman Africa) takes the same word-initial prosthetic vowel Portuguese always adds before an *sC-* cluster (cf. *schola* → *escola*), giving "Escilitanos" rather than a bare "Scilitanos". |
| Præsens (consul) | Praesens | Civil dating reference only (never speaks, not a martyr) — the æ ligature is dropped as a typographic adaptation (Portuguese has no æ). Unlike Claudianus below, the Latin -us → Portuguese -o rule does not apply here: "Præsens" is a 3rd-declension participial cognomen (nominative in -ens, genitive -entis), not a 2nd-declension -us noun, so there is no regular sound-change to invoke. No Portuguese-adapted form is invented for this otherwise-unknown consul with no cult or martyrology entry. |

| pay heed to (our Emperor) | acatamos o nosso Imperador | Originally rendered "respeitamos o nosso Imperador". Round 2 logged this as a "soft nuance, left as-is" without ever running the corpus-pairing measurement, and rounds 3-5 let that verdict stand unexamined — the same diagnostic round 4 applied, one line later, to "welfare"→"saúde". Round 6 ran it. **Why "respeitamos" is wrong:** this corpus renders "heed" as atentar (para) / dar atenção (a) / dar ouvidos (a) and never as "respeitar" (`barnabas/epistle` ch2; `ignatius/epistle-to-polycarp` ch6; `ignatius/epistle-to-the-philadelphians`; `ignatius/epistle-to-the-smyrnaeans` "give heed to the prophets" → "dai atenção aos profetas"; `ignatius/epistle-to-the-trallians` ch4), while "respeitar/respeito" maps back to "respect" without exception, including in this exact genre (`polycarp/martyrdom` ch9: "Have respect to your old age" → "Tem respeito à tua idade"). The sense matters too: Speratus is making a claim about *conduct* (they never did ill, they gave thanks, and at line 15 he pays the tax), which Saturninus immediately cashes out in cultic conduct ("juramos... e rogamos... como também vós deveis fazer"). An inner attitude of esteem carries that less well than compliance does, and blunts the proconsul's rejoinder. **Why not the literal precedent rendering:** "damos atenção" was tried first as the closest formal match (Ignatius's "give heed to the bishop" → "dai atenção ao bispo") and rejected on register. Those precedents are hortatory epistolary prose urging a congregation to listen to ongoing guidance, where "heed" is verb-like; here it is a defendant's sworn answer in a capital trial, where "heed" names a settled civic stance. In modern pt-BR "dar atenção a" is interpersonal/cognitive ("dar atenção às reclamações dos clientes") and lands as "we take notice of him" — bathetic from a man on trial for his life — besides echoing the "ouvidos" imagery this file uses literally at lines 11/13 for a different English phrase. **Why "acatamos":** `acatar`/`acatamento` is this corpus's word for the deference owed to authority, in precisely this slot — `trent-catechism/commandment-04`: "duty to superiors" → "o **acatamento** devido aos superiores", in a paragraph that simultaneously keeps "obedecer" (obey), "respeitar" (respect) and "honra" (honour) distinct from it; also `trent-catechism/commandment-02`: majesty that even the impious "**acatam** com temor". It is judicial/institutional register, apt for a tribunal, and collides with nothing else in this file. Also considered and rejected: "obedecemos" (overclaims — the English says "heed," not "obey," and Speratus is at that moment refusing the one act that would constitute real obedience), "atentamos para" (Barnabas's precedent, but reads as self-directed watching rather than deference to a sovereign), "dar ouvidos" (valid heed-precedent corpus-wide, but collides with the "ouvidos" idiom at lines 11/13), "observamos" (collocates with laws, not persons — "observamos o Imperador" reads as watching him), "prestamos acatamento" (correct but repeats "prestar" from "não nos prestamos à injustiça" earlier in the same sentence). |
| indulgence (of the Emperor) | clemência | Originally rendered "indulgência" (direct cognate). Round-1 review flagged this: the corpus itself uses "indulgência" almost exclusively in its Catholic doctrinal sense (temporal-punishment remission — cf. `morrow-my-catholic-faith/pt-BR/lesson-153.md`), so a reader hitting it in this pagan-Roman-trial scene risked a confusing anachronistic echo that the English "indulgence" (which keeps a live secular sense) doesn't carry as strongly. Changed to "clemência", which is unambiguous and equally faithful to the sense (imperial leniency/pardon). |
| pray for his welfare | rogamos por seu bem-estar | Originally rendered "rogamos por sua **saúde**". Post-merge round 4 measured the corpus's actual English→Portuguese pairings and found "saúde" is what the corpus uses for **health**, not welfare (`origen/africanus-to-origen`: "pray for your and your circle's health" → "rogo pela vossa saúde"; `john-chrysostom/letter-to-some-priests-of-antioch`: "the state of your health" → "o estado da vossa saúde"), while "welfare" has a direct precedent rendering as **bem-estar** in this same church-fathers pt-BR set (`barnabas/epistle`: "what tends to your general welfare" → "o que contribui para o vosso bem-estar geral"; also `trent-catechism/petition-02`). The narrowing matters here beyond word choice: the line is half of the Roman civic-religious loyalty formula (oath by the emperor's *genius* + prayer *pro salute eius*), where *salus* is the emperor's safety/preservation/wellbeing as head of state — a plain-sense modern pt-BR reading of "rogamos por sua saúde" turns a loyalty cult act into concern for the emperor's physical health, which also weakens the parallel Saturninus is drawing when he tells the Christians they ought to do the same. |
| genius of our lord the Emperor | gênio tutelar de nosso senhor o Imperador | Originally rendered bare "gênio" as a direct scholarly cognate. Round-1 review checked actual corpus usage and found every other occurrence of "gênio" in this corpus (`augustine-confessions`, `pictorial-lives-of-saints`, `morrow-my-catholic-faith`) uses it in the talent/intellect sense, not the Roman tutelary-spirit sense — so the claimed "consistent with how the corpus renders other classical-religious terms" was unsupported, and a reader would likely misread "juramos pelo gênio do Imperador" as an oath by the Emperor's talent, which is nonsensical. Added "tutelar" ("gênio tutelar" — a standard Portuguese scholarly collocation for the Roman guardian-spirit sense) to disambiguate without departing from the cognate. |

## Translation Decisions

- No footnotes (author or editor) in the source — a continuous, unbroken dialogue transcript. Nothing to drop.
- No YAML frontmatter; single-file, single-chapter book (like several other short church-fathers works in this corpus).
- The source uses **no quotation marks** around direct speech ("Saturninus the proconsul said: You can win..." — no `"..."`), unlike the sibling `justin-martyr` account, which does quote. Preserved exactly as in the source rather than imposing the sibling file's convention — the two source texts simply use different typographic conventions for direct speech, and the translation must match its own source, not a different one.
- **Grammatical person (tu/vós) traced turn-by-turn against who Saturninus is actually addressing at each point**, per the defect class documented in this book's own genre-sibling (`justin-martyr/martyrdom-of-justin-chariton-and-other-roman-martyrs/pt-BR/translation-journal.md`):
  - Plural (**vós**) where Saturninus addresses the group of defendants as a whole: the opening "You can win the indulgence..." (no one has spoken yet — addressed to all six standing in the hall), "as you also ought to do", "to the rest: Cease to be of this persuasion", "Be not partakers of this folly" (continuing the address to "the rest"), and "Have a delay of thirty days and bethink yourselves" (English "yourselves" is explicitly plural).
  - Singular (**tu/te**) where Saturninus and Speratus address each other one-to-one: "If you will peaceably lend me your ears" (Speratus → Saturninus), "I will not lend mine ears to you... when you begin to speak" (Saturninus → Speratus), "Do you persist in being a Christian?" (explicitly "said to Speratus" in the source), and the two follow-on lines continuing that same exchange — "Will you have a space to consider?" and "What are the things in your chest?" ("your chest", singular, Speratus's own chest of books) — both read as continuing the direct Saturninus–Speratus exchange rather than reverting to the group, since Speratus alone answers each in turn.
- Scripture references: book names translated to their standard Portuguese form (1 Timothy → 1 Timóteo, Romans → Romanos), chapter:verse numerals kept as-is — matches the convention already used throughout this corpus (cf. `ignatius/epistle-to-polycarp/pt-BR`, `barnabas/epistle/pt-BR`).
- The italicized scriptural allusion in the source (*whom no man has seen, nor with these eyes can see*) is kept in italics in the translation, matching the source's own emphasis.
- The source names only six defendants as initially brought to trial ("Speratus, Nartzalus, Cittinus, Donata, Secunda and Vestia") but the closing sentence and execution order list twelve names in total (adding Veturius, Felix, Aquilinus, Lætantius, Januaria, Generosa). This is a known feature of the historical account (the Scillitan Martyrs were twelve in number; the opening line names only those who are individually examined, "and the rest" covers the other six) and is preserved exactly as in the source, not "corrected" — same rule the corpus already follows for source-level naming quirks (cf. the Chariton/Charito/Hierax naming mismatch noted in the `justin-martyr` sibling journal).
- `book.json` updated: added `pt-BR` to `languages`, added `pt-BR` name/title entries (book title and the single `ch001` TOC entry) and a `pt-BR` author entry ("Miscellaneous" → "Diversos", matching the established form at `miscellaneous/didache/book.json`).
