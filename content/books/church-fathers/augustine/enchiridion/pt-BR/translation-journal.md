## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-27: Address. Augustine writes to Laurentius in the singular: rendered with "tu" and 2nd-person-singular verbs, as `ignatius/epistle-to-polycarp/pt-BR` does for a personal letter. Where the source addresses God (psalm verses, the Lord's Prayer, Augustine's exclamation in ch. 80), the reverent "Vós" is used, per `augustine-confessions/pt-BR` and `augustine/creed/pt-BR`. God's words to others (ch. 49 "Hoje eu te gerei", ch. 98–99 addressing Moses and Pharaoh) and the pot addressing the potter (ch. 99) keep "tu".
- 2026-09-27: Pronouns for God lowercase ("ele", "nele"), as in `augustine/creed/pt-BR`; divine titles keep the source's capitals (Mediador, Verdade, Criador, Artífice, Unigênito).
- 2026-09-27: Lord's Prayer. "Forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors" → "Perdoai-nos as nossas dívidas, assim como nós perdoamos aos nossos devedores", as in `augustine/creed`, since the argument of ch. 71–73 turns on debts. "Lead us not into temptation" → "não nos induzais em tentação" (not the familiar "não nos deixeis cair"): ch. 116 glosses the petition as "not being led into temptation" ("não ser induzido em tentação"), and the gloss needs the verb. "*but* deliver … not *and* deliver" → "*mas* livrai … não *e* livrai". Matthew 6:14–15 "trespasses" → "ofensas", kept distinct from "dívidas".
- 2026-09-27: Scripture quotations follow the source's wording clause by clause, not a standard Portuguese Bible (e.g. ch. 1 "A multidão dos sábios é a salvação do mundo"; ch. 36 "Ave, tu que és cheia de graça"; ch. 53 "Afeiçoai-vos às coisas do alto"). The source gives only two chapter-and-verse references (intro "cf. 1 Corinthians 13:13"; ch. 68 heading "1 Corinthians 3:11-15") plus "I Tim. II. 4" in the ch. 103 heading; all given with full pt-BR book names ("1 Coríntios", "1 Timóteo 2:4"). No references were added.
- 2026-09-27: Chapter headings translated in sentence case (Portuguese practice), numbering kept as "Capítulo N." to match the source's "Chapter N."; part headings Introdução / Fé / Esperança / Amor.
- 2026-09-27: The New Advent editorial introduction (four paragraphs before "Introduction") is in the source body and was translated. It contains no footnotes. The source carries no NPNF editor footnotes (already stripped at import); nothing dropped. Bracketed Latin glosses ([*pietas*], [*anima*], *sperare* [esperar], *stipendium*) are kept as in the source; ch. 91 "*animate* … soul [*anima*]" → "*animal* … alma [*anima*]", and ch. 91 "natural body" → "corpo animal", so the gloss still connects.
- 2026-09-27: Obvious transcription slips in the en-US file were translated as the intended word, and the en-US file was not edited: ch. 1 "the apostles wishes" (→ "o apóstolo deseja"), ch. 14 "thelogicians", ch. 76 "give aims" (alms), ch. 80 "sameas", ch. 97 heading "GodWill" and "mostly truly", ch. 99 "Pharoah" (Faraó), ch. 118 "The, law", and stray spaces before closing parentheses/quotes. In ch. 112 the quote marks around Ps 77:9 are misplaced in the source (`For “Hath God” they say, forgotten…`); the pt-BR quotes the whole verse ("Terá Deus", dizem eles, "esquecido de ter piedade? …").
- 2026-09-27: Straight quotes were converted to curly quotes to match the en-US file and `augustine/creed/pt-BR`.
- 2026-09-27: Ch. 44 poet's line "They fill its belly with the armed soldier" kept singular ("o soldado armado"), since the point is the singular-for-plural figure. Ch. 44 "serpent/serpents" kept singular/plural likewise.
- 2026-09-27: Ch. 91 "it shall not then be flesh, but body" and ch. 34 "by 'flesh' … 'man'" keep "carne"/"homem"/"corpo" fixed, since the arguments hang on those words.

## Round 1 Review (2026-09-27)

- Book title fixed: "O Enchirídion" → "O Enquirídio" (book.json name + toc title, and the two body occurrences in the intro of ch001.md). The earlier draft cited `augustine-confessions/pt-BR`'s Key Terms table as precedent for "Enchirídion" — that citation is true (that book's book.json and running text do use "Enchirídion", and its pt-BR is this corpus's own translation, not an aligned independent historical one, so the precedent isn't voided by the "check `sources`" rule). But "Enchirídion" itself is not an attested Portuguese word: Infopédia's Língua Portuguesa dictionary has an entry for "enquirídio" and none for "enchirídion", and the standard published Portuguese title of this work is "Enquirídio sobre a Fé, a Esperança e o Amor/Caridade" (pt.wikipedia.org; liriocatolico.com.br's full Portuguese translation). `augustine-confessions/pt-BR` was left untouched — out of scope for this book.
- Ch. 103: pt-BR had added a negation not in the source — "…e portanto **não** os salve?" where the English says "…and therefore does save them" (no negation), reversing the sense of the rhetorical question about God's universal salvific will. Fixed to "…e portanto os salve?".
- Ch. 76: "e assim não começavam a sua esmola em casa, estendendo em primeiro lugar a sua compaixão a si mesmos" — the gerund "estendendo" left the negation's scope over the second clause ambiguous (English has "did not commence... and [did not] extend..."). Changed to "nem estendiam" to make the negation explicit and parallel to the English; sense was already recoverable from context, so this is a precision fix, not a correction of a wrong reading.
- Full clause-by-clause bilingual read of all 122 chapters (6 parallel batches of ~20 chapters) plus the mechanical audit (completeness, headings, scripture refs/pt-BR book names, diacritics, quote style, markdown) found nothing else to fix. All Key Terms rows spot-checked against the text (love/charity split at ch. 76 and ch. 121; free-will vs. freedom-of-the-will at ch. 30–32; mass/heap of perdition at ch. 92/107; Todo-Poderoso/Onipotente at ch. 96; Ps 77:9 quoting at ch. 112; Lord's Prayer wording at ch. 115–116) matched the actual files.

## Round 2 Review (2026-09-27)

- **Ch. 103 re-verified, round-1 fix CONFIRMED correct (not reverted).** Quoted the full English sentence and checked the logic of Augustine's 1 Tim 2:4 argument: the rhetorical question "For which of all these classes is there out of which God does not will that men should be saved in all nations through His only-begotten Son, our Lord, and **therefore does save them**; for the Omnipotent cannot will in vain..." has a *single* negation ("does not will"), with no "not" before "does save them" — the two clauses are not both under the negation's scope; the question's implied answer ("none") is what makes "therefore does save them" true. Cross-checked against an independent modern translation (Outler, via CCEL): "For from which of these groups doth not God will that some men from every nation should be saved...? **Therefore, he doth save them**, since the Omnipotent cannot will in vain..." — same single-negation structure, confirming the en-US wording is correct as given and the pt-BR's current "…e portanto os salve?" (no "não") is the right rendering. The round-1 fix stands.
- **Ch. 76 re-verified, round-1 fix CONFIRMED correct.** English "did not commence their almsgiving at home, and extend their pity to themselves" is "did not [commence... and extend...]" (one negation governing both coordinated infinitives). pt-BR "não começavam a sua esmola em casa, nem estendiam em primeiro lugar a sua compaixão a si mesmos" correctly negates both. Fix stands.
- **Ch. 1: a round-2 sub-agent changed "A multidão dos sábios é a **salvação** do mundo" → "...é a **saúde** do mundo", reasoning that English "welfare" ≠ "salvação". Reverted — checked against the actual published Portuguese Catholic Bible (Bíblia Ave Maria and others, via biblia.pt) for the underlying verse (Wisdom 6:26): it reads verbatim "A multidão dos sábios é a salvação do mundo, e um rei sábio a prosperidade do seu povo." "Salvação" is the attested, standard Portuguese Catholic rendering of this exact verse (the Greek/LXX tradition has σωτηρία = salvation, vs. the Vulgate/English "sanitas"/"welfare" tradition the NPNF translator followed) — not a mistranslation of the English clause. Recorded here so a future round doesn't re-flag it.
- Negation-scope sweep across all 122 chapters (4 parallel batches by chapter range) plus a source-blind grammar/spelling/crase skim. Fixes applied:
  - Ch. 8: "com mais propriedade se diz que teme **do que que** espera" → "do que espera" (duplicated word, typo).
  - Ch. 95: added a comma before "é para nós um problema insolúvel" (English "...rather than the other, **is** to us an insoluble problem") — without it the clause garden-paths as a subordinate "antes que a outra é...".
  - Ch. 100: "tirando assim o sumamente Bom proveito até do que é mau" → "tirando assim, o sumamente Bom, proveito até do que é mau" — English "the supremely Good thus turning to good account..." has "the supremely Good" as the clause's subject (an epithet for God); without commas it misreads as an adjective on "proveito".
- Candidates checked and rejected (source clause + reasoning, so later rounds don't re-raise them):
  - Ch. 14 "Nenhum tempo é ao mesmo tempo escuro e claro" (weather/time ambiguity in isolation) — reads as "weather" in context (parallel list of physical opposites: food/drink, bodies). Not an error.
  - Ch. 20 "se se suprime o assentimento" / "não sabem aquilo que é impossível ignorarem" — required reflexive/passive construction and correctly-preserved double-negative scope respectively. Not errors.
  - Ch. 28 "nem todos procediam de um só anjo" / Ch. 29 "não tinha caído em rebelião todo o corpo dos anjos" — both correctly render the English's partitive "not all" (confirmed by the surrounding sentence about "a parte deles que caíra"), not a universal "none". Not errors.
  - Ch. 30 "De modo nenhum" for two instances of "God forbid" (ch. 20 uses literal "Deus não permita" for the same English phrase) — a translation-choice inconsistency, not a factual/negation error; left alone (style, not fidelity).
  - Ch. 34, 35, 49, 50 (free-will/grace chapters): several clunky-on-source-blind-read constructions ("E, se a sua virgindade tivesse sido lesada ainda que fosse ao dá-lo à luz...", "Mas aniquilou-se a si mesmo e tomou a forma de servo", "como ele tinha dado a morte a Cristo com a maior injustiça", "E não sucede com o dom como com aquele um que pecou") all checked against the English and found to accurately mirror either the source's own convoluted 19th-century syntax or (ch. 35, Phil 2:7) the standard Almeida Bible wording this corpus's Scripture quotes generally follow. Not errors.
  - Ch. 64 "nem todo pecado é crime" for "every sin is not a crime" — correctly resolves the archaic English as "not every sin is a crime" (not "no sin is a crime"). Not an error.
  - Ch. 97 "pois ele não quer umas coisas e as faz, e quer outras e não as faz" for "for He does not will some things and do them, and will others and do them not" — the split-negation structure (one marker per clause, not one covering both) is in the English itself; pt-BR mirrors it marker-for-marker. Not an error.
  - Ch. 111 "Os primeiros não terão vontade, os segundos não terão poder de pecar" for "the former shall have no will, the latter no power, to sin" — English shares "to sin" across both clauses by ellipsis; pt-BR only writes "de pecar" once (attached to the second clause) but it's recoverable from context and not a scope error, just a minor economy-of-ellipsis difference. Left alone per "don't naturalize/improve style".
  - Full re-check of the dense correlative/triple-negative rhetorical questions in ch. 32, 43, 51, 56–58, 67 (1 Cor 6:9–10 vice list), 96, 98, 99, 104–107: all preserve English negation scope correctly.

## Round 3 Review (2026-09-27)

- **Ch. 1 Wisdom 6, round-2 call REVERSED.** Round 2 kept "A multidão dos sábios é a **salvação** do mundo" reasoning that "salvação" is the attested standard Portuguese Catholic Bible rendering of this verse. That reasoning is the exact anti-pattern the skill's Scripture rule forbids: the source's own English says "the **welfare** of the world" (NPNF/Shaw, following the "sanitas" branch of the tradition, not the LXX σωτηρία branch the Portuguese Bíblia Ave Maria follows) — matching a familiar published Bible instead of the source edition's actual word is not a "deliberate per-verse adoption of the standard verse" with independent justification, it's drift. Changed to "A multidão dos sábios é o **bem-estar** do mundo". No other verse-level decision in the book was reopened by this reversal.
- **Ch. 35, Phil. 2:7, round-2 call REVERSED for the same reason.** Round 2 kept "Mas aniquilou-se a si mesmo" ("emptied/annihilated himself") reasoning it "mirrors the standard Almeida wording this corpus's Scripture quotes generally follow." The English source says "But He made Himself of no reputation" (KJV-style wording, about losing reputation, not kenotic self-emptying). Changed to "Mas se fez sem reputação".
- **Ch. 107, Rom. 6:23 preposition fixed.** "a vida eterna **em** Jesus Cristo nosso Senhor" → "a vida eterna **por** Jesus Cristo nosso Senhor" — English says "eternal life **through** Jesus Christ our Lord"; this wasn't a familiar-Bible pull (Almeida also uses "por" here) but a plain preposition slip away from both the source and the standard wording.
- **Full re-scan of every Scripture quotation in all 122 chapters** (4 parallel batches by chapter range), checking each against the English clause by clause for drift toward familiar/standard Portuguese Bible (Almeida) phrasing where it diverges from the source. Confirmed correct, left alone (source's own wording already followed, not Almeida's): ch. 22 "vem do mal" (not Almeida's "procedência maligna"), ch. 32 Rom 9:16 "him that wills" singular + Prov. 16:1 "preparation of the heart" (not Almeida's differing phrasing), ch. 33 Ps 90:9 / Job 14:1, ch. 46 Ps 51:5 plural "iniquidades/pecados" (the chapter's argument turns on the plural), ch. 55 John 5:29 / Ps 43:1, ch. 59 "the angel that spoke *in* me", ch. 66 "castigados" (not Almeida's "ensinados"), ch. 67 vice list + "está morta, estando só" (not Almeida's "devassos"/"sodomitas"/"é morta em si mesma"), ch. 69 "herdai o reino" (not Almeida's "possuí por herança o reino"), ch. 91 "corpo animal" (recorded gloss-preservation decision, round 1), ch. 99 "mas odiei" (not Almeida's "e odiei"), ch. 103 (re-verified twice already, stands), ch. 112 "castigo eterno" (not Almeida's "tormento eterno"), ch. 119 "culpa original" vs. "pecado original" (tracks the English's own "original guilt"/"original sin" distinction, not an inconsistency).
