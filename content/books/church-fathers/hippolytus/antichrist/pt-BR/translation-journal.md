## Translation Decisions

- Paragraph numbers are written as bold `**N.**` per `.claude/rules/books.md`. The en-US still uses `N. ` list markers; it was left as is because this run edits the source only for OCR slips. Convert it when the en-US is next touched.
- Address: tu for Theophilus and for each prophet addressed singly (Isaiah, Daniel, John); vós in ¶31 where the prophets are addressed together. Scripture quotations keep the person and number of the source (Isa 47 and Ezek 28 in tu; Isa 1:7 "your country" rendered in tu, following the singular address).
- Reverential capitals follow `against-plato`: Ele, Seu, Lhe, O, Si, n'Ele, d'Ele for God and Christ where the source capitalizes, including inside Scripture quotations (Dan 7, Ps 110). Where the source leaves a pronoun lowercase inside a quotation (e.g. Gen 49 "he couched"; Isa 47 "his name"), the pt-BR keeps it lowercase.
- ANF's parenthetical glosses and supplied words — "(couched)", "(visit)", "(lit. = deficient)", "(power of the)" — are kept in parentheses in the same places. ANF italics (supplied or lacuna-marking words) are kept as italics over the corresponding Portuguese words.
- Scripture is rendered from the source's wording (largely the LXX via ANF), not from a Portuguese Bible: e.g. Isa 1:21 "nela se alojava (repousava) a justiça", where ¶8 argues from the gloss "couched"; Gen 49:9 "do rebento", which ¶8 links to Isa 11:1.
- ¶35 "I was angry with my people" → "Indignei-me contra o meu povo" (avoids "irei-me", which reads as the future of *ir*).
- ¶43 "1, 260 days" → "1.260 dias" (Portuguese thousands separator; the source's space is a typesetting artifact).
- Inline Scripture references are kept where the source places them, without parentheses, as in en-US; their spacing is normalized (the source sometimes runs them into the preceding word).
- No editor footnotes in the source; none added.

## Review log

### Round 1

Focus: completeness paragraph by paragraph (67 numbered paragraphs + title aligned 1:1), Bible book names and chapter:verse numbers, plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 8 defects, all fixed. All 39 Scripture references match the source; no paragraph dropped, merged or split.

Fixed:
- ¶4 "the holy flesh wrought (woven) by the Spirit": "a santa carne tecida pelo Espírito" → "a santa carne trabalhada (tecida) pelo Espírito". The ANF gloss was dropped.
- ¶17 (Isa 14:13) "And you said in your mind": "em teu coração" → "em tua mente". The verse had been pulled toward the familiar Bible wording; ¶53 says "in your heart" and keeps "coração".
- ¶44 (Isa 53:3) "He was despised, and esteemed not": "e não O estimamos" → "e não foi estimado". The source is passive, with no first person.
- ¶36 (Rev 17:5) "the Mother of Harlots": "a Mãe das Prostitutas" → "a Mãe das Meretrizes", per Key Terms (harlot → meretriz; whore → prostituta).
- ¶63 (2 Thess 2:1) "our gathering together at it": "e da nossa reunião com Ele" → "e da nossa reunião nela". "it" refers to the coming; "com Ele" was the familiar Bible wording.
- ¶58 "on the ground of their refusal to reverence": "sob o pretexto de se recusarem" → "por se recusarem". "Pretexto" implies a false reason, and the source states the actual reason.
- ¶38 (Rev 17:14) "for he is Lord of lords" (lowercase in the source): "porque Ele é" → "porque ele é", per the Translation Decisions rule on lowercase pronouns inside quotations.
- ¶17 (Isa 14:6) "He that strikes the people" (capitalized in the source): "aquele que fere" → "Aquele que fere", per the same rule.

Rejected:
- ¶41 (Rev 18:13) "and beasts" → "animais de carga". "De carga" separates the merchandise from "o animal" (the beast of the vision) and matches the sense of the cargo list. Keep.
- ¶54 (Jer 17:11) "making himself riches without judgment" → "sem justiça". "Judgment" here is the archaic KJV sense of right or justice; "sem juízo" would read as "senselessly". Keep.
- ¶17 (Isa 14:20) "You shall not abide … a wicked seed" → "ó semente perversa". The vocative "ó" makes explicit the apposition to "you". Keep.
- ¶8 "his sleep and rising again" → "do Seu sono e da Sua ressurreição". The lowercase rule covers pronouns inside quotations; this is Hippolytus's own sentence about Christ. Keep.
- ¶61 "endued with the Father's word" → "revestida do Verbo do Pai". The phrase refers to the Logos (Greek τῷ λόγῳ τῷ πατρῴῳ); the same paragraph has "the Word that is persecuted". Keep.
- ¶40 (Rev 18:2) the italic span "*e gaiola de toda ave imunda* e odiosa" takes in "ave", which falls outside the source's italics. This follows from Portuguese word order. Keep.

Evidence: `ember-translation-evidence/church-fathers__hippolytus__antichrist/review-1.md` (outside the repo).

### Round 2

Focus: clause-by-clause bilingual read of ¶1–67 against the en-US, plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed. No mistranslation found.

Fixed:
- ¶46 "by the illustrious Arbiter, to wit, who Himself first accomplished the course": "pelo ilustre Árbitro, que Ele mesmo primeiro cumpriu a carreira" → "pelo ilustre Árbitro, que primeiro cumpriu Ele mesmo a carreira". Putting the explicit subject "Ele mesmo" right after the subject relative "que" broke the grammar: "que" read as an object.

Rejected:
- ¶17 (Isa 14:9) "the lords from their thrones" → "os senhores de seus tronos". Portuguese "de" can mean "from" here, and the verse's sense (risen from their thrones) is still available. A style choice. Keep.
- ¶40 (Rev 18:5) "her sins did cleave even unto heaven" → "chegaram até o céu". The image of reaching heaven is kept, and nothing in the argument depends on "cleave". Keep.
- ¶61 (Ps 110:1) "until I make Your enemies Your footstool" → "por escabelo dos Teus pés". In Portuguese, "escabelo" is the footstool itself, so "dos Teus pés" adds no content. Keep.
- ¶3 "who was again the Son of God" → "que era também o Filho de Deus"; ¶25 "to raise the kingdom of the Jews" → "restaurar"; ¶47 "they will not give glory" → "não quererão dar glória". Each is a defensible reading of the source's sense. Keep.

Evidence: `ember-translation-evidence/church-fathers__hippolytus__antichrist/review-2.md` (outside the repo).

### Round 3

Focus: source-blind cold read of the pt-BR, each mark then checked against the source; then a mechanical sweep of spelling, diacritics, crase, hyphenation, punctuation and quote pairing, plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: clean. No changes.

Rejected:
- ¶2 "Pois, assim como os bem-aventurados profetas foram feitos … olhos para nós, eles previram" reads as an incomplete comparison. It is grammatical as written, and the source has the same construction ("For as the blessed prophets were made … eyes for us, they foresaw"). Keep.
- ¶18 (Ezek 28:10) "You shall die the deaths of the uncircumcised" → "Morrerás a morte dos incircuncisos". The plural is a Hebraism, and the singular is the Portuguese idiom; no sense is lost. Keep.
- ¶41 (Rev 18:11) "no man shall buy their merchandise" → "as suas mercadorias". "Suas" defaults to the clause's subject, "os mercadores", so it matches "their". Keep.
- ¶49 "in its being broken up or even dishonoured" → "por ter sido despedaçado". The masculine refers to "o quarto animal" (the empire), consistent with "o curará … o restaurará" for "heal it … restore it". Keep.
- ¶32 "whence can you know this, for you are already gone to your rest?" → "se já partiste". In this rhetorical question "se" works as "given that", which is idiomatic. Keep.
- ¶67 "having raised the saints among us" → "dentre nós" ("of our number"). Defensible. Keep.

Evidence: `ember-translation-evidence/church-fathers__hippolytus__antichrist/review-3.md` (outside the repo).

### Round 4

Focus: function words, headings and forced commitments: prepositions, articles, demonstratives, connectors, possessive seu/sua, consigo; gender and antecedent commitments; a turn-by-turn tu/vós trace through every apostrophe (Theophilus, Isaiah, Daniel, John, the prophets together in ¶31, Babylon in Isa 47 and Rev 18). Plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 2 defects, fixed. Both are "seu/sua" attaching to the Antichrist (the clause subject) where the source names another owner.

Fixed:
- ¶54 "promising to restore their country, and establish again their kingdom and nation": "prometendo restaurar a sua terra e estabelecer de novo o seu reino e a sua nação" → "prometendo restaurar-lhes a terra e estabelecer de novo o reino e a nação deles". Coming straight after "seus próprios filhos" (= his), "sua terra / seu reino" read as the Antichrist's.
- ¶56 "to persecute the saints, their enemies and antagonists": "os santos, seus inimigos e adversários" → "os santos, inimigos e adversários deles". In the source the saints are the enemies of the unbelievers, not of the subject "Ele".

Rejected:
- ¶35 "These things does Isaiah prophesy for you" → "a teu respeito". In the quotation just before, Isaiah addresses Babylon, so the nearest "you" is Babylon, and "concerning you" is one valid reading. Keep.
- ¶64 "the one week being divided into two parts" → "a única semana". It refers back to the single week of Dan 9:27 (¶43), and "única" keeps the singleness. Keep.
- ¶59 "we who hope for the Son of God" → "esperamos no Filho de Deus". "Esperar em" (hope in) carries the same expectant hope. Keep.
- ¶60 (Rev 12:17) "the saints of her seed" → "os santos da sua descendência". The clause subject is the dragon, but "contra a mulher" comes just before, and Portuguese Bibles carry the same construction. Keep.
- ¶52 "has got their spoils" → "os seus despojos". Obtaining one's own spoils makes no sense, so "seus" can only mean the defeated kings'. Keep.
- Divine first-person "Eu" is capitalized mid-sentence (¶18, 46, 58, 61) while divine possessives stay lowercase, as in the source. This is consistent within the file, and books.md forbids normalizing reverential capitals. Keep.

Evidence: `ember-translation-evidence/church-fathers__hippolytus__antichrist/review-4.md` (outside the repo).

### Round 5

Focus: terms, journal and names. A per-paragraph term-frequency diff against Key Terms in both directions; every falsifiable journal claim checked by grep; proper names checked against standard Portuguese forms and sibling works. Plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: clean. No changes.

Rejected:
- ¶25 (Dan 7:8) "another (horn), an offshoot" → "um rebento", which is also the Key Terms rendering of "shoot" (Gen 49:9). "Offshoot" and "shoot" are the same image, and Hippolytus's argument is that the Antichrist mimics Christ, so a shared word misleads no one. Keep.
- ¶2 "the mysteries of the word" (lowercase) → "os mistérios do Verbo". The next sentence has "honoured by the Word Himself", so the word is the Logos. The Key Terms row only rules on "the word of salvation" (¶1). Keep.
- ¶49 "Dionysus" → "Dioniso", while other church-fathers pt-BR works (Clement, Tatian, Tertullian, Theophilus, Minucius Felix) write "Dionísio" for the god. Both are standard Portuguese, and no sibling Hippolytus work names him. Keep.
- ¶52 "Sidon" → "Sidônia" rather than "Sidom". This is the Catholic Bible form, also used in `augustine/enchiridion`; "Berito" matches `hippolytus/apostles-and-the-disciples`. Keep.

Evidence: `ember-translation-evidence/church-fathers__hippolytus__antichrist/review-5.md` (outside the repo).

### Round 6

Focus: completeness paragraph by paragraph (title + ¶1–67 aligned 1:1; parentheses, quote marks and italic spans counted per paragraph), Bible book names and chapter:verse numbers, plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: clean. No changes. All 39 inline references match the source and use Portuguese book names.

Rejected:
- ¶51 "Edom, and Moab, and … Ammon" → "Moab" rather than "Moabe". "Moab" is the Catholic Bible form (Ave-Maria); "Moabe" is the Almeida form. Both are standard, and the file uses "Moab" consistently. Keep.
- ¶65 (Rev 22:15) "whoremongers" → "fornicadores". This matches the Key Terms row "fornication → fornicação", and the word means men who practise fornication. Keep.
- ¶49 "after the manner of the law of Augustus, by whom the empire of Rome was established" → "pelo qual". The masculine relative can only refer to Augusto, not to the feminine "lei". Keep.
