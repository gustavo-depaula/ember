## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-07: Rendered the closing scriptural allusion (1 Cor. 2:9, "eye has not seen...") using the wording already established in this corpus (`morrow-my-catholic-faith/pt-BR/lesson-083.md`): "olho não viu, nem ouvido ouviu, nem entrou no coração do homem, o que Deus preparou para aqueles que O amam" — for consistency across books rather than a fresh literal rendering.
- 2026-09-07: "distributing according to each one's deeds the temporary punishments for (different) characters" restructured slightly for natural Portuguese word order ("distribuindo os castigos temporários segundo as obras e a índole de cada um") — same meaning, avoids an awkward literal calque of "characters."
- 2026-09-07: "hymned by the angels" rendered as "louvados com hinos pelos anjos" rather than a coined verb, since Portuguese has no natural verb form of "hinar."
- No editor footnotes present in the source (single continuous patristic fragment); nothing to drop.
- No Latin phrases in the source to preserve inline.
- 2026-09-07 (round-1 review): Capitalized "teu" → "Teu" in "Justo é o Teu julgamento" for reverential-pronoun consistency with "O" in "aqueles que O amam" later in the same paragraph, matching the en-US source's capitalization of both "Your" and "Him."
- 2026-09-07 (round-2 review): Fixed a meaning-reversing agent/patient swap in paragraph 3 — "Pois já não são vistos pelos justos" (wrongly said the unrighteous are no longer seen by the righteous) corrected to "Pois os justos já não são vistos por eles" (the righteous are no longer seen by the unrighteous), matching "For neither are the righteous seen by them any longer."
- 2026-09-07 (round-2 review): Capitalized "deus" → "Deus" in paragraph 1 ("que honraram como Deus as obras vãs...") for consistency with all 10 other references to God in the file.
- 2026-09-07: Rounds 3 and 4 of review found no further issues (clean). Review closed after 4 rounds, 2 of them consecutive clean.
- 2026-09-07 (post-merge review): Fixed a null-subject antecedent inversion in paragraph 3. "Pois os justos já não são vistos por eles, nem são dignos de lembrança" left "os justos" as the implicit subject of the second clause, asserting that *the righteous* are unworthy of remembrance. The en-US "nor are they worthy of remembrance" refers to the unrighteous — the next sentence's contrastive "But the righteous will remember only the righteous deeds" only works if what precedes says the wicked are not remembered. Made the subject explicit: "nem os injustos são dignos de lembrança." Portuguese cannot carry the English pronoun's ambiguity here, so the antecedent has to be named.
- 2026-09-07 (post-merge review): The antecedent above is a deliberate, contested call — a later review round argued for the opposite reading (that "they" continues the grammatical subject "the righteous"). Recorded here so it is not re-litigated blindly. Three reasons the unrighteous is the right antecedent:
  1. **Theology.** "Worthy of remembrance" is a judgment of desert. Saying the righteous are *not* worthy of remembrance contradicts Ps 111:6 ("the just shall be in everlasting remembrance"); saying it of the wicked is the standard patristic trope from Prov 10:7 ("the name of the wicked shall rot") and Ps 9:6.
  2. **The contrastive "But".** "But the righteous will remember only the righteous deeds by which they reached the heavenly kingdom" only forms a contrast if the preceding clause says the wicked go unremembered.
  3. **Whiston's independent rendering** of the same Greek (the text also circulated as Ps.-Josephus, *Discourse to the Greeks concerning Hades*) reads "nor are they **thought** worthy of remembrance" — an explicit judgment passive, which is coherent of the wicked and incoherent of the just.
  Grammatical parallelism does favour the other reading in English, which is why the ambiguity survives in ANF; it cannot survive in Portuguese, so the antecedent is named.
- 2026-09-07 (post-merge review): Capitalized "lhe" → "Lhe" in "Pois o Pai Lhe confiou todo o julgamento" (en-US "the Father has committed all judgment to Him"). The dative clitic was the one divine pronoun in the file still lowercase; every other one — Ele, Aquele, Teu, "que O amam", "A Ele seja a glória" — was already capitalized, including by the round-1 "teu" → "Teu" fix. Oblique clitics are easy to miss when sweeping for reverential capitalization; relative pronouns ("a quem", "do qual") stay lowercase by the same convention.
- 2026-09-07 (second post-merge review): Restored a dropped divine possessive in paragraph 3. "estando presentes a essa sentença judicial" rendered en-US "And being present at **His** judicial decision" with a demonstrative, so the attribution of the decision to the Judge was only inferable from the preceding sentence rather than asserted. Corrected to "estando presentes à Sua sentença judicial." Note *why* three earlier reverential-capitalization sweeps missed it: the file's divine-pronoun inventory realizes nominative (Ele, Aquele), dative clitic (Lhe), accusative clitic ("que O amam"), prepositional (A Ele) and possessive-of-address (Teu) — but the third-person possessive was **absent, not miscased**, and a form that was never rendered cannot be caught by checking casing. en-US has exactly two divine possessives: "Your judgment" (rendered, Teu) and "His judicial decision" (unrendered until now).
- 2026-09-07 (second post-merge review): Considered and rejected — "estes últimos", "por isso" in "ainda que se corrompa, não é por isso destruído" (the concessive still asserts the body is not destroyed; propositions match "it is not at least destroyed"), and the bracket scope of "(como se já) sentissem" vs "(as if they were) already feeling" (parenthesis placement only, no proposition differs).
- 2026-09-07 (second post-merge review): Further considered-and-rejected findings, recorded so later rounds do not re-tread them. None is a defect; in each the proposition is unchanged. (1) "ídolos por eles mesmos fabricados" for "idols fashioned (by themselves)" — content rendered, only ANF's supplement-marking parentheses dropped, same class as "(como se já)". (2) "E ali habitam os justos desde o princípio" for "And there the righteous from the beginning dwell" — pt reads "desde o princípio" adverbially with the verb rather than attributively with the subject; the en word order is itself ambiguous and the two readings are truth-conditionally equivalent here (unlike the "worthy of remembrance" case, where they were theologically opposite). Whiston is likewise adverbial: "in which the just have dwelt from the beginning of the world." (3) "é algo que não se pode dizer de Deus" for "is a word which cannot be said of God" — "word" flattened to "algo". (4) "para ser novamente plasmado" for "to be moulded again anew" — collapses ANF's redundant "again anew" to one adverb. (5) "os conduzem" for "send them along". (6) "por Deus no tempo, segundo a opinião de Platão" vs "by God, according to the opinion of Plato, in time" — the Plato attribution is parenthetical over the whole clause in both, so its scope over "in time" is unchanged.
- 2026-09-07 (second post-merge review): Review closed after 4 further rounds, the last 2 consecutive clean. Reverential capitalization is now complete across all pronoun forms the file realizes (Ele, Aquele, Lhe, O, A Ele, Teu, Sua), with relatives (a quem, do qual) correctly lowercase. book.json metadata verified: name/toc title/H1 agree in both languages, "Hipólito" matches the corpus pattern for father-name localization, and the source URL does not collide with the other Hippolytus books.
- 2026-09-07 (second post-merge review): The en-US source file was cross-checked against the published New Advent ANF text and matches it, **including its oddities** — do not "correct" these locally. "drag them on to the confines **or** hell" is a typo in ANF/New Advent itself for "confines **of** hell"; the pt-BR already renders the intended sense ("os confins do inferno"), which is correct. Likewise "although it lacks no water" (litotes, cf. Whiston "though it will not be void of moisture"), the redundant "no burning sun", and the hyphenations "un-fading"/"un-quenchable" are all genuine ANF readings. book.json cites New Advent's *modernized* ANF ("Righteous is Your judgment"), so a future diff against CCEL's 1886 printing will show systematic Thy/Your-class differences that are editorial, not errors.
- 2026-09-07 (third post-merge review): Realized the numeral in "no qual **uma só** sentença de justo julgamento será justamente aplicada a todos" (en-US "in which **one** sentence of righteous judgment shall be justly applied to all"). **This reverses an earlier round's considered-and-rejected verdict**, which held that pt "uma" is article/numeral ambiguous and that "a todos" already carried the universality. The new evidence is internal to this translation: en-US uses "one" as a numeral before a noun in exactly four places, and this file explicitly marks the numeral in three of them — "there is one descent" → "há **uma só** descida", "shall utter one voice" → "hão de proferir **uma só** voz", "one and the same way" → "**um único** e mesmo caminho" — leaving only this one bare. The odd one out is also the theologically loaded one: the single common verdict of the general judgment is deliberately contrasted, two sentences earlier, with the *per-person* temporary punishments the angels distribute "segundo as obras e a índole de cada um". Bare "uma" leaves the distributive reading available where the source excludes it. Whiston agrees: "one righteous sentence shall deservedly be passed upon all men."
- 2026-09-07 (third post-merge review): **When a translation has established its own device for a construction, inconsistent use of that device is evidence, not style.** The earlier rejection was argued from what pt "uma" *can* mean in the abstract. The question that settled it was narrower and checkable: what does *this translator* do with en "one" elsewhere in *this file*? Three of four marked, one bare. Prefer that test over general claims about the target language's ambiguity.
- 2026-09-07 (third post-merge review): Considered and rejected. (1) "eles não seguem **todos** por um único e mesmo caminho" for "they do not proceed on one and the same way" — "todos" is added, but with a plural subject the en sentence already means "not all by the same route" (the next clauses send the righteous right and the unrighteous left), so truth conditions are unchanged. (2) The second "now" in "finds it in nothing **now** a traitor" is unrendered, but the whole clause sits under the scope of the preceding "como **agora** convive com ele", which supplies the temporal frame. (3) "Deus, o Verbo" for "God the Word" — two other corpus files render the identical source phrase "Deus Verbo" (`ambrose/mysteries` ch001:117, `athanasius/statement-of-faith` ch001:9), but "Deus, o Verbo" is also attested in this corpus — **two genuine instances**, both verified by opening the file: `augustine-confessions/pt-BR/book-seven-chapter-09.md:5` ("li ali que Deus, o Verbo, nasceu") and `gabriel-stmm-intimita-divina/pt-BR/giorno-028.md:11` ("Deus, o Verbo eterno que assume no tempo uma natureza humana"). Both forms are house-attested for this title; correct as written. **Corrected within this round:** this entry first cited four attestations. Two were grep artifacts and are struck — `gabriel-stmm-intimita-divina/pt-BR/giorno-027.md:44` reads "Sendo Deus, o Verbo é eterno" (there "o Verbo" is the subject of "é", not an appositive to "Deus"), and `pictorial-lives-of-saints/pt-BR/the-five-wounds.md:7` reads "o Filho de Deus, o Verbo feito Carne" (the appositive head is "o Filho de Deus"). The conclusion survives on the two real hits, but "well attested" overstated it: `Deus Verbo` has two files rendering this exact source phrase, `Deus, o Verbo` has two rendering the title in other contexts. Same failure mode as the lesson-083 claim caught this session — **a substring match is not an attestation until you read the sentence around it.** **Corrected, same round:** this entry first justified the comma-appositive by syntactic position, claiming the bare compound "reads badly" after a preposition. That reasoning is false and was refuted by this very file, which has "por ordem de **Deus Artífice**" and "a quem **Deus Pai** glorificou" — bare compounds after prepositions. The distribution tracks *which title* is being rendered, not which syntactic slot it sits in. Do not apply a prepositional-position rule to "Deus Artífice" or "Deus Pai". (4) "o rosto ... vê-se sempre sorridente, enquanto **aguardam**" — silepsis (agreement by sense with "dos patriarcas e dos justos"), mirroring the en-US's own shift from singular "the face ... is seen" to plural "as they wait".
- 2026-09-07 (third post-merge review, round 2): Restored the two definite articles in the closing 1 Cor 2:9 quotation — "as quais “**o** olho não viu, nem **o** ouvido ouviu, nem entrou no coração do homem…”". The 2026-09-07 entry above records a deliberate decision to adopt the wording already established in this corpus at `morrow-my-catholic-faith/pt-BR/lesson-083.md:31`, but that file reads "**O** olho não viu, nem **o** ouvido ouviu, …" — this file was lesson-083 verbatim *minus* the two articles, which is the signature of an accidental drop while mirroring the article-less English/KJV line, not of a deliberate switch. Portuguese does not drop the article on singular count nouns in subject position the way archaic English does, and nothing in the "as quais…" frame forces it. (The bare form *is* attested for this verse at `gabriel-stmm-intimita-divina/pt-BR/giorno-318.md:19`, so it was not ungrammatical — but the journal recorded a specific, checkable intent, and the file missed it. `montfort-true-devotion/pt-BR/introduction.md:23` also carries the article.)
- 2026-09-07 (third post-merge review, round 2): "à ordem de Deus Artífice" → "**por** ordem de Deus Artífice", for en-US "at the command of God the Artificer it buds". In Portuguese `à ordem de` is a fixed juridical/commercial locution meaning *at the disposal of / to the order of* (`preso à ordem do juízo`, `cheque à ordem de X`) — a calque of the English preposition. The sense required is agentive: the seed buds *because God commands it*. Decided on corpus usage, by the same test that settled `uma só`: `à ordem de` occurs exactly once in all of `content/` — this line — while `por ordem de` is the established house form (14 occurrences across montfort, bonne-presse, butler, trent-catechism and others).
- 2026-09-07 (third post-merge review, round 2): Considered and rejected. (1) "a substância prolífica" for "the bursting substance" — the two English "bursting"s are different senses (the worm bursts *out of* the body, rendered dynamically as "continua a irromper"; the substance bursts *forth into being*, i.e. propagates), and in the coordinate pair with "geração de feras" the adjective names exactly the generative capacity being denied. Do not "fix" it into a participle and break the coordination. (2) "bênçãos futuras" for "these future blessings" while ¶1 has "os bens" for "the blessings" — the corpus house form is `bens futuros/eternos/vindouros` (~10 files), but ¶3's context is a promise rather than a contemplated good, where "bênçãos" is the normal Portuguese choice; the two English uses are not the same sense. (3) "nem nenhum … nem nenhum" — negative concord, attested in montfort, liguori and polycarp pt-BR files; corpus-normal, not a Gallicism. (4) "designados sobre eles" — "sobre" for delegated authority is biblical Portuguese.
- 2026-09-07 (third post-merge review, round 3): Considered and rejected — "também ali **são atormentados**" for "they are also **punished** there" (¶1). The case for changing it is real and has the same evidence shape as the three fixes that landed: this is the only place the en-US uses *punish* as a verb, the noun *punishment* is rendered `castigo` 6/6 elsewhere in this file, the only two other bilingual church-fathers files render en "punished" as `punidos` (4/4, `mathetes/epistle-to-diognetus` ch001:27, `tatian/fragments` ch001:29), and `atormentar` is otherwise reserved corpus-wide for en *torment*. **Left as written anyway**, and the reason is worth recording: unlike `uma só` (which left a wrong distributive reading available) and `à ordem de` (which meant something else entirely), nothing here changes truth-conditionally — and the sense is punishment *by the sight* of the blessed, which "atormentados" renders more precisely than the juridical `punidos` would. The evidence shape is necessary but not sufficient: **a corpus singleton is a reason to look, not a reason to change. Ask what the reader loses, and if the answer is nothing, leave it.**
- 2026-09-07 (third post-merge review, round 3): The Whiston appeals in the entries above (lines on "worthy of remembrance", "uma só sentença", and "desde o princípio") rest on WebSearch result summaries, not on a page anyone in this environment has read — direct fetches are blocked, and the Ps.-Josephus *Discourse to the Greeks concerning Hades* is not in this repo (`justin-martyr/discourse-to-the-greeks` is a different work with no Hades material). In each case Whiston is corroborating, never load-bearing: the "worthy of remembrance" call stands on the contrastive "But" plus Ps 111:6 / Prov 10:7, and "uma só" stands on the 3-of-4 numeral-marking count inside this file. Treat the Whiston quotations as unpinned until someone can diff a real text.
- 2026-09-07 (third post-merge review): **Open question for the maintainer, deliberately not "fixed": this file's reverential capitalization is an outlier against the rest of the corpus.** Measured, not assumed: no other pt-BR file under `content/books/church-fathers/` capitalizes divine possessives or oblique clitics. The sibling `hippolytus/apostles-and-the-disciples` has no reverential form at all (its one "Seu" is title-case in a heading). Roughly half the pt-BR patristic files capitalize nominative "Ele" mid-sentence (barnabas, tatian, gregory-thaumaturgus, origen); the other half capitalize nothing (ambrose, athanasius, polycarp, mathetes, dionysius-rome). This file capitalizes Ele, Aquele, Lhe, O, A Ele, Teu and Sua — beyond both patterns. Four review rounds arrived there by pursuing *internal* consistency with the en-US source's own Him/His/Your, which is a defensible rationale, and nothing here is a grammatical error: reverential capitalization is standard in Brazilian Catholic publishing. But no convention is documented in `docs/CONVENTIONS.md`, `docs/content/`, or the translate-book skill, so the corpus has no house rule to be right or wrong against. **Left as written**; normalizing in either direction is a corpus-wide editorial decision for the maintainer, not a per-book review fix. Later rounds should not re-open this as a defect.
- 2026-09-07 (post-merge review): Considered and rejected a reported defect in "não sem que primeiro tenha morrido, se dissolvido e se misturado com a terra" — a shared auxiliary elided across a coordinated participle chain carrying a clitic is ordinary Portuguese (cf. "tinha caído e se machucado"). The reflexive also renders the en-US passives ("been dissolved", "mingled with earth") idiomatically. Left as written.
