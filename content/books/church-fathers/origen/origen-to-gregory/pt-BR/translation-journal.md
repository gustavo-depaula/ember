## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-04: Preserved the source's plain ordered-list numbering (`1.`, `2.`, `3.`) for the letter's three paragraphs, rather than bolding it — the source file itself uses Markdown `1. `/`2. `/`3. ` list syntax, not `**1.**`.
- 2026-09-04: No footnotes (author or editor) present in the source file — none to drop or translate.
- 2026-09-04: The two Scripture quotations ("These be your gods, O Israel...", "Knock, and it shall be opened...", "Ask, and it shall be given unto you") were translated directly from the English (matching the letter's own KJV-flavored English wording) rather than substituted with a canonical Portuguese Bible translation's wording, since Origen is paraphrasing/summarizing rather than quoting a fixed liturgical text verbatim, and the letter's argument doesn't turn on the exact wording of any one Bible edition.
- 2026-09-04: "children of Israel" rendered consistently as "filhos de Israel" throughout (not "israelitas"), matching the source's consistent phrasing.

## Review Rounds

- 2026-09-04, round 1 (adversarial bilingual clause-by-clause): clean. Verified all 9 negations, all 3 Scripture quotations, all proper names/references, and no omitted/added content against the en-US source. No defects found.
- 2026-09-04, round 2 (cold monolingual pt-BR-only proofread): **three genuine defects found and fixed**, all verified against the en-US source before fixing:
  - **"se havia... seriam feitos" → "se houvesse... seriam feitos".** Mood mismatch: the passage is a hypothetical ("if there was a third and fourth quality of gold, from it would be made the holy vessels" — en-US's own loose historical-conditional), which in Portuguese pairs an imperfect-subjunctive protasis (*se houvesse*) with the conditional apodosis, not an indicative "se havia".
  - **"ganharam com essa morada que não lhes faltasse..." → "ganharam disso o seguinte: que não lhes faltava...".** Broken verb valency — *ganhar* doesn't take a bare "que" clause; the en-US has "they gained **this** from their dwelling there, **that** they had no lack..." (object + appositive clause), which the first draft had dropped the object of. Also fixed the mood: "that they had no lack" is a factual appositive, not a hypothetical, so indicative *faltava* replaces the wrongly-subjunctive *faltasse*.
  - **"a irmã de sua mulher" → "a irmã da mulher deste".** Pronoun ambiguity: en-US "was made a kinsman of Pharaoh by marrying **his** wife's sister" is itself ambiguous on "his," but the correct referent (per 1 Kings 11:19-20 Douay-Rheims: Pharaoh gave Adad "the own sister of his wife, Taphnes the queen") is Pharaoh, not Ader. Portuguese "sua" defaults to the grammatically nearest antecedent (the subject, Ader), producing an incoherent reading (Ader marrying his own already-established wife's sister) that the English's real-world-resolved ambiguity doesn't have. "Deste" unambiguously points back to the immediately preceding "Faraó."
  - One finding rejected as a faithful mirror of the source: "trabalho costurado e bordado, ... costurado com a sabedoria de Deus" repeats *costurado*, matching the en-US's own "needed **sewed** and embroidered work, **sewed** with the wisdom of God" — not a translation defect.
- 2026-09-04, round 3 (adversarial bilingual, independently re-deriving the round-2 fixes without reading this journal + fresh full clause-by-clause pass): all three round-2 fixes independently re-derived and confirmed CORRECT. **One more genuine omission found and fixed**: "Saudação em Deus, excelentíssimo senhor..." → "Saudação em Deus, **meu** excelentíssimo senhor...". The en-US salutation is "Greeting in God, **my** most excellent sir, and venerable son Gregory" — the possessive (part of the formal epistolary address, applying to both "sir" and "son") had been dropped with no compensation. No other new defects found in the rest of the chapter; all proper names, negations, and Scripture quotations re-verified clean.
- 2026-09-04, round 4 (cold monolingual pt-BR-only proofread): four candidate defects raised, checked against en-US before acting:
  - **"dela seriam feitos os vasos sagrados" — REJECTED.** Reader flagged "dela" (singular) as disagreeing with the two-part antecedent "uma terceira e uma quarta qualidade de ouro." But the en-US source itself treats this as one collective quality with a singular pronoun — "if there was a third and fourth quality of gold, **from it** would be made the holy vessels" — so "dela" faithfully mirrors "from it," not a translator-introduced error.
  - **Mood shift "foram feitas" → "seria usada" across two adjacent sentences — REJECTED.** The en-US has the identical shift in the same two sentences: "These things were probably made... An inferior kind **would be used**..." (indicative-with-hedge, then bare conditional, no "if"). A faithful mirror, not a defect.
  - **Antecedent of "que fez isso" at the end of the Ader sentence — REJECTED.** The en-US has the exact same long-distance correlative: "**It was** when he fled... and begetting a child, who was brought up with the children of Pharaoh, **that** he did this." The pt-BR "Foi quando fugiu... e gerou um filho que foi criado com os filhos de Faraó, que fez isso" uses the equivalent Portuguese correlative ("Foi quando... que..."), which spans the whole sentence back to the opening "Foi quando" the same way the English "It was... that" does — a fluent Portuguese reader parses it the same way. Faithful mirror.
  - **"não havia posto **a** um uso adequado" → "não havia posto **em** uso adequado" — FIXED.** Not a fidelity issue (the source just says "had not put to a proper use") but a genuine pt-BR idiom fix: Portuguese pairs *pôr* with *em uso*, not *a um uso*, which read as a preposition calqued from the English "put **to** a ... use."
  - One low-confidence tense-mixing note ("esteve"/"não havia provado" in the same "enquanto" clause) also checked and rejected: en-US has the identical pairing, "so long as he **was** in the land of Israel, and **had not tasted** the bread of the Egyptians."

- 2026-09-04, round 7 (post-merge `/review-book-translation` audit of PR #401): inventory and structural parity clean — 1 chapter present in both languages, heading hierarchy matches, the source's plain `1.`/`2.`/`3.` numbering preserved, 12 quote characters in each file (all 6 quotations present), all 10 negations correct, all 22 proper names accounted for. Straight quotes in pt-BR vs curly in en-US was checked against the corpus and **rejected**: 14 of 17 quoted `church-fathers/*/pt-BR` chapters use straight quotes, so this file follows the dominant pt-BR convention (the sibling `africanus-to-origen`'s curly quotes are the minority). **Three defects found and fixed:**
  - **"o candelabro sólido de ouro" → "o candelabro de ouro maciço" — FIXED.** Modifier mis-parse: en-US "the solid golden candlestick" means a candlestick of *solid gold*, but "candelabro sólido de ouro" attaches *sólido* to *candelabro* and *de ouro* separately, yielding "the solid candlestick of gold." Portuguese also collocates solid metal as *ouro maciço*, not *ouro sólido* (*sólido* reads as firm/robust or as a state of matter).
  - **"Ader, o idumeu, ao menos enquanto esteve..." → "Ader ao menos, o idumeu, enquanto esteve..." — FIXED.** Adverb scope shift, translator-introduced rather than mirrored. In en-US, "at least" sits adjacent to the subject and is fenced off from the temporal clause by "the Idumæan;" — it hedges the *exemplar* ("Ader, for one"), picking Ader out as an instance of the "certain persons" just described. Placed immediately before "enquanto" with no comma, "ao menos" instead scopes over the *timeframe* ("at least while he was in the land of Israel"), a different claim shape. The fix restores the source's own word order.
  - **Key Terms row "Ader, the Idumæan" — FIXED.** The row's English column named a comma-form string that occurs nowhere in `en-US/ch001.md` (the source has "Ader at least, the Idumæan" and bare "Ader the Idumæan"). Per the standing corpus rule in `docs/journal.md`, every Key Terms row was re-grepped against *both* files this round; the other 11 rows verify in both columns. The row now names the forms that actually occur and notes that the name surfaces twice in each file, once bare and once with the hedge spliced in.
  - One low-confidence item checked and **rejected as style, not a defect**: "com fiéis pressupostos tais como são agradáveis a Deus" — classical Portuguese prefers the correlative *tais quais* over *tais como* before a finite clause, but *tais como* is well attested in Brazilian usage and the source's own "such as are well pleasing to God" is equally archaic. Out of scope for an objective-defect audit.
