## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-08: Single-paragraph pseudepigraphal letter, ~260 words, one chapter. Register kept formal/epistolary throughout, matching the source's own slightly stilted 19th-century English translation — this is itself a translation of a late Latin apocryphon, and the awkwardness of a few clauses (e.g. the final long sentence about "that innocent blood ... should be sold and suffer") is present in the source translation itself, not introduced here. Rendered as faithfully as Portuguese syntax allows rather than smoothed into clearer prose, per the "faithful but accessible" rule — accessible does not mean rewriting a genuinely tangled period sentence into something the original doesn't say.
- The editorial insertion *brought against it* (Walker's supplied words, italicized in the source to mark them as not literally in the underlying text) is kept italicized in the Portuguese equivalent (*que lhe fora movida*), following the convention already used in the sibling apocryphal work `hippolytus/apostles-and-the-disciples` pt-BR.
- "By Hercules" kept as a literal pagan oath (Pilate swearing by a Roman god, not a Christian one) rather than softened — consistent with the letter being presented as a pagan Roman official's voice.
- No footnotes in the source; none added.
- Title kept parenthetical "(unknown date; late)" translated as "(data desconhecida; tardia)", matching how the book.json TOC titles this single chapter.
- 2026-09-08, review pass 1 (clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read): the closing sentence's final connective "yet, as the Scriptures signify, to their own destruction" had been rendered with a second *ainda que* ("although"), duplicating the sentence's opening concessive connector and turning a contrastive consequence into a second concession. Fixed to *mas, como as Escrituras significam, para a própria destruição deles* — "yet/but" as a contrastive conjunction, not "although."
- 2026-09-08, review pass 2 (mechanical structural count + monolingual pt-BR-only naturalness read): structural counts matched exactly (9/9 periods, 3/3 semicolons, 1/1 colon, 6/6 asterisks, 1/1 heading, 2/2 bold markers); the two em dashes the translation uses to set off *livre de toda a acusação que lhe fora movida* (where the source uses commas) are a legitimate punctuation choice for an already dense sentence, not a content change, so left as-is. One naturalness defect: "ameaçavam de destruição o mundo inteiro" put the direct object after the prepositional phrase, an unidiomatic word order reading as a calque of the English "threatened destruction to the whole world." Fixed to "ameaçavam o mundo inteiro de destruição."
- 2026-09-08, review pass 3 (deliberate sense-level bilingual re-read, checking each clause for false friends/idiom traps rather than just surface fluency — per the lesson from the `On the Trinity` review that clean mechanical/naturalness verdicts can still miss wrong-but-fluent sentences): re-checked every clause against the source (íntegro/strict, sangue inocente/innocent blood, malícia/malignity, benfeitores/beneficent, levante/rising, eclodir/breaking out, à nossa maneira/after our manner, and the negation "não resisti" preserving "did not resist"). No defects found — clean.
- 2026-09-08, review pass 4 (mechanical formatting/encoding integrity + book.json/TOC cross-check): asterisk pairs balanced, no mojibake or double-spacing, file ends with a single trailing newline; `book.json` name/author/languages/TOC all carry matching pt-BR entries. Clean. Two consecutive clean passes (3–4) close out the review.
