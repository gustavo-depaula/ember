## Translation Decisions

- Conventions from `gregory-nyssa/baptism-of-christ`, `infants-early-deaths` and `pilgrimages`: author without "São", straight double quotes, divine pronouns lowercase. Scripture references keep the source's numbers with Portuguese book names (Gênesis, Êxodo, Provérbios, Eclesiastes, Sabedoria, Isaías, Daniel, Mateus, Lucas, João, Romanos, Coríntios, Gálatas, Efésios, Filipenses, Colossenses, Timóteo, Hebreus, Pedro).
- Inline references sit where the source puts them, moved after the closing quote when the source has them inside it (`flesh 2 Corinthians 5:16”` → `carne" 2 Coríntios 5:16`), with the source's stray space before punctuation dropped. References standing between sentences ("…instruments of sin. Romans 6:13 If…", "Genesis 3:16", "Exodus 15:10", "Exodus 15:20", "Exodus 19:15") stay in the same slot. "1 Timothy 4:2" stays before the quotation, as in the source.
- Edition citation slips mirrored, not corrected: "Matthew 12:11" (ch. 6, for Matthew 11:11) and "2 Timothy 2:16" (ch. 8, for 2 Timothy 2:26).
- Scripture quotations translated from the source's wording, not from a Portuguese Bible (e.g. "the righteous should shine forth as the sun", "From fear of You, O Lord, have I conceived", Temperance "is a tree of life to them that lay hold upon her").
- Address: the treatise's generic "you" rendered with `tu` (Verias, Compreenderás, Toma, Supõe); the closing exhortation of ch. 24 is also `tu`. "Rejoice with me" (the parable) → `Alegrai-vos`; Matthew 10:16 → `Sede`.
- Structure: title, 24 chapter headings and 28 paragraphs, one to one (ch. 3 has three paragraphs, ch. 21 three, the rest one). No footnotes in the source; no translator notes added.
- ch. 2 "when human nature has been based by passionate inclinations": read "based" as "abased" → "rebaixada"; the source is left as it stands.
- ch. 8 "the prudent husband man": rendered "o lavrador prudente", following the irrigation image of the same passage.
- ch. 8 "this way of serving the state" → "este modo de servir ao bem comum".
- ch. 22 "fling this latter nature to savages" → "lançará este último aos selvagens" (literal; the source's image is kept).

## Review log

### Round 1

Focus: completeness, paragraph by paragraph; Bible book names and chapter:verse numbers. Verdict: 3 defects, all fixed. The paragraph structure (title, 24 headings, 28 paragraphs), every quotation, parenthetical, emphasis and Scripture reference matches the source.

Fixed:
- ch. 6, "than whom 'of those that are born of women there was none greater'": `que nenhum "dentre os nascidos de mulher houve maior"` → `do qual nenhum "dentre os nascidos de mulher houve maior"`. A relative "que" with no preposition cannot carry the comparative "than whom".
- ch. 15, "not having spot or blemish or any such thing": `nem ruga` → `nem defeito`. "Ruga" (wrinkle) comes from the familiar Ephesians 5:27 wording, not from the source. "Defeito" matches ch. 24's "without spot or blemish" → "sem mancha nem defeito".
- ch. 18, the fish parable, "getting into the 'vessels' with them": `"cestos"` → `"vasos"`. The source has vessels, and "cestos" (baskets) comes from the familiar Bible wording of Matthew 13:48.

Considered and rejected:
- ch. 16 "wise as serpents, and harmless as doves" → "prudentes … simples". The sense is the same, and "simples" keeps the link to the gloss on "simplicity" in the same passage.
- ch. 24 "a tree of life to them that lay hold upon her" → "para os que a abraçam". "Abraçar" means taking hold of something. It is not a word the source lacks.
- ch. 20 "the agents of bodily habitation" → "coabitação corporal". The passage contrasts the spiritual union with the bodily one, so "cohabitation" is the sense here.
- ch. 12 "till she … was sentenced" → "até que ela … foi condenada". An indicative after "até que" for a past fact is acceptable Portuguese.
- ch. 13: death is "he" in the source and "ela" in pt-BR ("aquela que reinara desde Adão"). This follows the grammatical gender of "a morte" and is used consistently through the passage.
- ch. 17 "who would call that feature faultless" → "um rosto". Here "feature" means the face, made up of features.

### Round 2

Focus: clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of all 28 paragraphs, plus the mechanical audit (blocks, headings, references, italics, quotes, `book.json`). Verdict: 3 defects, all fixed. The mechanical audit found nothing.

Fixed:
- ch. 10, "in a certain choiceness of colour": `numa certa seleção de cores` → `num certo primor de cores`. "Choiceness" means fineness. "Seleção" means choosing.
- ch. 12, "when they all look towards the Beautiful and the Good": `o Belo e o Bom` → `o Belo e o Bem`. Key Terms: the Good → o Bem.
- ch. 22, "nor sickly and unstrung and nerveless for the required work by immoderate mortification": `nem doentia, frouxa e sem nervo para o trabalho requerido por uma mortificação imoderada` → `nem, por uma mortificação imoderada, doentia, frouxa e sem nervo para o trabalho requerido`. In the old order the work was "required by" the mortification. In the source the mortification is what weakens the flesh.

Considered and rejected:
- ch. 4 "through armour on the right hand and on the left" → "pelas armas". Older English "armour" covers arms in general, so "armas" is a correct reading.
- ch. 8 "the *dimness* of the Patriarch's eyes" → "*fraqueza* dos olhos". Both mean failing sight, which is the point Gregory draws from it.
- ch. 12 "this world which lies in the Evil" → "jaz no Maligno". "The Evil" stands for the ambiguous phrase of 1 John 5:19. "O Maligno" is one of its two readings, not an addition.
- ch. 13 "He makes the barren woman to keep house, and to be a joyful mother of children" → "habitar em casa, como alegre mãe de filhos". Both clauses are kept, and Gregory's gloss ("a joyful mother is the virgin mother") still finds its words.
- ch. 13 "of bloods" → "do sangue". The plural is a literalism of the English. Gregory argues nothing from it, and "dos sangues" is unidiomatic.
- ch. 5 "prevent the necessity of ever descending" → "evitar a necessidade de jamais descer". "Jamais" in the sense of "ever" after "evitar" is standard.

### Round 3

Focus: a source-blind cold read of the pt-BR text, with each mark then checked against the source; a spelling-only sweep (orthography, diacritics, crase, hyphenation, punctuation, quote pairing); and the mechanical audit. Verdict: 5 defects, all fixed. The spelling sweep and the mechanical audit found nothing else. The Key Terms rows were checked against the text and are accurate.

Fixed:
- ch. 4, "none of that evil in life … can have any hold over a man": `nenhum daquele mal da vida` → `nada daquele mal da vida`. Portuguese does not use "nenhum de" with a singular mass noun.
- ch. 4, "Except that the foolish man thinks his own that which never belongs to him": `A não ser que o insensato julga seu` → `Apenas isto: o insensato julga seu`. "A não ser que" needs the subjunctive, and the subjunctive "julgue" would turn "except that" into "unless".
- ch. 11, "if any one wants to know … he would find it in nothing else": `não a encontraria` → `não a encontrará`. The future subjunctive "quiser" cannot pair with a conditional.
- ch. 13, "and it becomes to him the worker of mourning and of loss": `e ele se torna para ele o artífice` → `e o casamento se torna para ele o artífice`. Two "ele"s with different referents left the subject unresolvable. In the source, "it" is marriage.
- ch. 24, "why do you not hear Moses, … that they may take in the vision of God.": the final period → `?`. This is a direct question. The source's period is not carried over.

Considered and rejected:
- ch. 3 "change their rejoicing into lamentation" → "trocar o regozijo em lamentação". "Trocar X em Y" in the sense "turn X into Y" is attested, and the meaning is clear.
- ch. 2 "The estimate of it which he will create" → "que dela ele criar". The "ele" takes up "quem quer que seja" from the sentence before, just as the source's "he" takes up "any one's".
- ch. 11 "this feat of mental analysis and of discriminating the material vehicle from the immanent beauty" → "proeza de análise mental e de discernir … da beleza imanente". "De discernir" depends on "proeza", as in the source.
- ch. 18 "and how such a hoplite would be likely to fare in battle, then …" and ch. 4 "as 'a bird from the snare,' on virtue's wings": both elliptical constructions are the source's own.
- ch. 24 "one makes oneself worthy … one is made worthy" → "se torna digno … se é tornado digno". "Tornar-se digno" covers "make oneself worthy", and the active/passive contrast survives.

### Round 4

Focus: function words (prepositions, articles, demonstratives, connectors, possessive "seu/sua"), headings, and every place where the Portuguese has to commit and the source is open (gender, pronoun antecedent, tu/vós), plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed. Headings, `book.json`, the Key Terms rows and the tu/vós trace (generic tu throughout, vós only for plural addressees in Luke 15:9, Luke 17:21 and Matthew 10:16) are correct.

Fixed:
- ch. 23, "to search out a fitting guide and master of this way": `procurar uma guia e um mestre adequados` → `procurar um guia e mestre adequado`. The source means one person, who is both guide and master (ch. 24 continues "look to that man"). "Uma guia" made the guide a woman, and the two articles made two people out of one.

Considered and rejected:
- ch. 3 "She hates her friends when they try to soften her grief" → "as amigas". The scene is the women's quarters of a house in mourning, with waiting-women singing the dirges, so female friends fits the context.
- ch. 3 "How they congratulate those who have chosen from the first the virgin life" → "as que escolheram". The subject is "those women who actually know it". They compare their own lot with that of women who chose virginity.
- ch. 13 "by their own deed have checked his advance" → "por seu próprio ato, detiveram o seu avanço". The second "seu" is death's. "A linha divisória da morte" in the same clause settles it, and "their own advance" makes no sense there.
- ch. 8 "They 'are taken captive by him at his will'" → "São tomados cativos" after "essas pessoas … instaladas". The masculine is inside the Scripture quotation, and the surrounding text keeps "pessoas" feminine.
- ch. 23 "there is a plainer guide … practice" → "uma guia mais clara"; "youth is generally a giddy guide" → "uma guia estouvada". Here the guide is a thing ("a prática", "a juventude"), not a person, and feminine "guia" in the sense of guidance agrees with it.
- ch. 21 "nor yet make this latter our leading aim" → "deste último". The masculine points to "o gozo", not "a satisfação", which is what the sense requires.

### Round 5

Focus: Key Terms, journal claims and proper names. This round ran a per-paragraph term-frequency diff against every Key Terms row in both directions, grepped every falsifiable journal claim, and checked names against sibling pt-BR works. The mechanical audit ran too. Verdict: 2 defects, both in the Key Terms table, both fixed. No chapter text changed. The mechanical audit, the 48 Scripture references, the Names line, the decision log and the earlier review-log quotes all check out.

Fixed:
- Key Terms, "marriage / wedlock": the note said `"matrimônio" where the source has "wedlock" or the institution`. That was false. Ch. 8's "marriage as an institution" reads "casamento como instituição", and the only "matrimônio" not tied to "wedlock" is ch. 15's spiritual marriage ("this truly honourable marriage"). The note now lists where each word is used.
- Key Terms, "Bridegroom (Christ) | Esposo": "Esposo" also translates "Husband" (Christ) in ch. 15 ("the Good Husband" → "o Bom Esposo", "the Husband who has married her" → "o Esposo"), and the row didn't say so. The row now covers both words and notes that a human bridegroom is lowercase "esposo".

Considered and rejected:
- ch. 6 "their sojourn in the desert" → "sua permanência no deserto". The "sojourn → peregrinação" row covers the Psalm 120 passage, where the sojourn is the soul's exile. Here the word just means staying in the desert.
- ch. 10 "Even a perfect type of such beauty" → "um tipo perfeito". Here "type" means an example of beauty, not a foreshadowing, so the "type → figura" row does not apply.
- ch. 21 "over-indulgence is the parent of most of the painful diseases" → "a intemperança". This is excess in eating, not the moral "indulgence" (concession) that the "condescendência" row covers.
- ch. 19 "from Jerusalem to Illyricum" → "até a Ilíria". "Ilíria" is a standard Portuguese form, and sibling works (Sulpitius Severus's *Life of St. Martin* and *Dialogues*, Hippolytus) use it. "Ilírico" is the other standard form.
- ch. 3 "when her bridegroom enters" and ch. 15 "but her bridegroom" → lowercase "esposo". These are human husbands. Only Christ gets the capital.

### Round 6

Focus: completeness, paragraph by paragraph. The source and pt-BR paragraphs were aligned one to one and read sentence by sentence. Bible book names and chapter:verse numbers were checked, and the mechanical audit ran too. Verdict: clean, nothing changed. Title, 24 headings and 28 paragraphs align. All 48 Scripture references match in order and number, with Portuguese book names. The 16 italic spans, 15 parentheticals and every quotation are present, and `book.json` is valid.

Considered and rejected:
- ch. 5 "This evil, ignorant, and prejudiced bias towards material things will prevent it" → "o impedirá". The "it" is the soul's turning its eye upward, not the soul, so the neuter "o" is correct. "A impedirá" would not be.
- ch. 8 "But what have I to do to judge them that are without?" → "Pois que tenho eu de julgar…". The question is the Apostle's rhetorical one. "Pois" opening the question keeps its force, and nothing is added or lost.
- ch. 22 "the one for the sustenance of man, the other for burning" → "um para o sustento do homem, a outra para queimar". The genders follow "o trigo" and "a palha", which is the source's pairing.

### Round 7

Focus: a clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of all 28 paragraphs against the source, sentence by sentence, looking for reversed negation, wrong subject or object, wrong tense or mood, false friends, dropped qualifiers and Scripture pulled toward the familiar Bible wording. The mechanical audit ran too. Verdict: clean, nothing changed. Title, 24 headings and 53 blocks align. The 16 italic spans, 15 parentheticals, 139 quotation pairs and 48 Scripture references (same numbers, Portuguese book names) match, and `book.json` is valid.

Considered and rejected:
- ch. 1 "In the business of this lower world words certainly act as a basement" → "servem de alicerce". In older English, "basement" means base or foundation, which is what "alicerce" says.
- ch. 10 "Every man a liar!" → "Todo homem é mentiroso!". Portuguese grammar needs the copula, and it adds nothing to the sense.
- ch. 10 "lest … we should slip away from it altogether" → "nos escapemos dele de todo". Pronominal "escapar-se de" means "slip away from", and the subject and object match the source.
- ch. 24 "How can you in your full life obey the Crucified?" → "na plenitude da tua vida". "Full life" means life at its height, set against the Crucified, and "plenitude" keeps that contrast, as the next question does with "hale in sin" → "robusto no pecado".
