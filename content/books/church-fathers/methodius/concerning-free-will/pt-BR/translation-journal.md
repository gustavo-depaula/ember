# Translation Journal — Concerning Free Will (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (William R. Clark, Ante-Nicene Fathers Vol. 6, via New Advent). No Greek or Latin text is in the corpus; en-US is the only source-language directory.
Target: pt-BR

Conventions follow the siblings `methodius/banquet-of-the-ten-virgins` and `methodius/oration-concerning-simeon-and-anna`:
- Author "Metódio"; book name, toc title and heading "Sobre o Livre-Arbítrio" (title case, as in `ambrose/repentance`, `athanasius/incarnation-of-the-word`).
- Scripture references keep the source's numbers and inline placement, with Portuguese book names ("João 1:18", "Gênesis 4:5", "Jó 38:11", "Jeremias 13:23").
- Curly quotes “ ”. Reverential pronouns lowercase.
- Scripture and the Homer verses (*Ilíada* 9) are rendered from the source's wording, not from a Portuguese Bible or translation of Homer.
- No footnotes in the source; none added.
- The source's italic supplied words (*of God*, *So the wounded man*, *is a subject that*, *question of*) stay italic on their Portuguese counterparts.

## Translation Decisions

- Address: `tu` between the two speakers; `vós` for the assembly addressed in the second paragraph.
- Two answers sit inline in the source after a question ("**Valentinian.** Yes." and an unbolded "Valentinian. I do."). Both stay on the same paragraph as the question so the paragraph count matches; the pt-BR preserves the source’s bolding, including the unbolded second label.
- "I do" answering "Do you think…?" → "Penso." ([24], [38], [48], [52], [55]); "I think so." ([44]) → "Penso que sim."
- The closing paragraphs (from "Because there is nothing evil by nature…") are fragments joined by the edition, with an orphan "says he" and an ellipsis; both are kept ("diz ele", "...").
- Punctuation slips normalized in pt-BR only: "mysteries *of God*?" → "!"; "Oh. that" → "Oh, que"; "it.For" → space; "substances. we must" → comma.

## Source edits (en-US, OCR-class)

- "often beard" → "heard"; "bat I am" → "but"; "that be attacked" → "he"; "sup pliant" → "suppliant"; "necessarily he something" → "be"; "conic into being" → "come"; "its qualifies" → "qualities"; "mat-ter" → "matter"; "existing same evil" → "some evil".
- Dittography removed: "but wishes to deprives him again of free-will, but wishes to point out" → "but wishes to point out".
