## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-03: Single-chapter work, 11 numbered "Canons" (κανόνες), ~1,700 words — the earliest surviving canonical epistle in Church history, written by Gregory Thaumaturgus, Bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus, c. 254–258, addressing penitential discipline for offenses committed during and after the Gothic/Boradic invasions of Pontus. Chosen from the `church-fathers/` backlog as a well-scoped single-work pick, continuing the pattern from the other Gregory Thaumaturgus work already translated (`trinity/`, pt-BR name "Gregório Taumaturgo" — reused here for consistency in `book.json`).
- No footnotes in the source beyond inline Scripture cross-references (added by the 19th-century edition), which are kept inline exactly as the English places them, per the established corpus convention (cf. Ignatian epistles, Didache).
- Scripture references localized to standard Portuguese book names: 1 Corinthians → 1 Coríntios, Matthew → Mateus, Deuteronomy → Deuteronômio, Ephesians → Efésios, Joshua → Josué, Exodus → Êxodo.
- Quoted Scripture text throughout is translated directly from this specific 19th-century English rendering (Salmond's own wording, which sometimes differs slightly from any standard Bible translation, e.g. "Meats for the belly, and the belly for meats" for 1 Cor 6:13), not substituted with an existing pt-BR Bible translation's wording — consistent with the Didache and Ignatian-epistle precedent of translating the source edition's own words rather than citing a standard target-language Bible.
- Canon 3's "whine these now take what belongs to brethren" — "whine" is almost certainly a transcription artifact for "while" in the digitized source (the sentence structure and sense require a contrastive conjunction, not the verb "to whine," which would be grammatically and semantically incoherent here). Translated as intending "while" (*ao passo que*). Not treated as a deliberate archaism to preserve literally, since it produces no coherent English reading either.
- Formal register throughout — this is a bishop's canon-law letter regulating penitential discipline, addressed to "most holy father" (a fellow-bishop), so the register matches the corpus's other conciliar/canonical documents (Trent Catechism, penance sections) rather than the more devotional register of, say, the Ignatian epistles.
- Second person plural ("vós"/imperative plural) used throughout for direct address to the recipient bishop and his clergy (Canons 5–7 addressing "you" as those who must adjudicate cases), matching the letter's own plural address once it shifts from general moral exposition (Canons 1–4, which use no second person) to direct instruction.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 1** (3 parallel passes: structural/mechanical, monolingual pt-BR proofread, bilingual clause-by-clause fidelity). Structural and bilingual passes came back fully clean. The monolingual pass found five genuine defects, fixed, plus a stylistic tweak, and raised three more items that were checked against the English and found to correctly mirror the source rather than being translation defects:
  - Fixed — Canon 1: "e a força e se deita com ela" was a broken clitic construction (article + noun instead of verb + clitic), leaving the clause without a verb for "forces her". Fixed to "e força-a, e se deita com ela".
  - Fixed — Canon 1: "a convenceu **de** andar" was wrong regência; "convencer alguém **a** fazer algo" is the correct Portuguese government. Fixed to "a convenceu **a** andar".
  - Fixed — Canon 4: "e **não** lhes prestarás atenção; antes, sem falta, os trarás de volta" was a genuine archaic-double-negative sense reversal, the same failure mode logged for the Didache — the English "shall not see... and pay no heed to them" is itself a double negative (shall-not + no-heed) that cancels to an affirmative obligation to attend to them, but translating it as one flat Portuguese negation ("não prestarás atenção") drops one of the two negatives and asserts the reader will ignore them, directly contradicting the very next clause ("but you shall bring them back"). Fixed by recasting as a "não... sem" construction that correctly encodes the cancellation: "sem lhes prestar atenção; antes, sem falta, os trarás de volta" (= "you shall not see them stray without attending to them").
  - Fixed — Canon 4: "quanto mais **não** há de ser o caso quando..." used the European-Portuguese expletive-negation idiom ("quanto mais não seja"), which reads in pt-BR as a real negation and can invert the a fortiori sense on a fast read. Fixed to "tanto mais isso se impõe quando...", keeping the a fortiori force without an ambiguous negation.
  - Fixed — Canon 5: "enviamos **até** vós" used "até" (as far as/until) governing a person as the recipient of "enviar", which reads as a spatial/temporal-limit calque rather than natural Portuguese for "sent to you". Fixed to "enviamos **a** vós".
  - Stylistic — Canon 6: reordered "para que os raios do céu não caiam, com toda a certeza, sobre..." to "para que, com toda a certeza, os raios do céu não caiam sobre..." — the original order split the verb from its complement across the interposed adverbial; not a fidelity defect, just smoother.
  - **Not a defect (verified against English)** — Canon 4: "Certamente **os** trarás de volta" (plural clitic) was flagged as disagreeing with the singular antecedent "o que é de um inimigo". Checked against the source: the English itself shifts from singular "what is a friend's... what is an enemy's" to plural "bring **them** back... **their** master" for this Exodus 23:4 paraphrase — the Portuguese plural correctly mirrors the English's own singular-to-plural drift, not an error introduced in translation. Left unchanged.
  - **Not a defect (verified against English)** — Canon 4: the tense shift "é alcançado... foge... **teve** de abandonar" (present, present, preterite) was flagged as an inconsistency. Checked against the source: the English itself shifts from present ("is met", "is fleeing") to present perfect ("**has had** to abandon") for the same reason — marking the third action as already-completed against the other two as ongoing. The Portuguese preterite is the standard rendering of that English present-perfect resultative sense and deliberately preserves the same three-way distinction. Left unchanged.
  - **Not a defect (verified against English)** — Canon 1: "mulheres... pelos bárbaros, **que** ultrajaram os seus corpos" was flagged as a cold-read antecedent ambiguity (could attach to "bárbaros" or "mulheres"). Checked against the source: the English's own "the captive women defiled by the barbarians, who outraged their bodies" carries the identical structural ambiguity — not something the translation introduced. Left unchanged.
  - **A monolingual reviewer without the source can still name the correct failure mode even when its own diagnosis of "why" is incomplete.** The Canon 4 double-negative fix is the clearest instance yet in this corpus of the "archaic English negation is easy to get backwards" pattern (cf. Didache journal) being caught by a monolingual read alone — the reviewer correctly flagged the sentence as **internally contradictory Portuguese** without knowing the English contained a second, source-side negative, and that diagnosis was enough to locate a real defect even though the reviewer's own proposed reading of "what's wrong" undersold it as ordinary incoherence rather than a dropped double negative.
