## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-03: Single-chapter work, 11 numbered "Canons" (κανόνες), ~1,700 words — the earliest surviving canonical epistle in Church history, written by Gregory Thaumaturgus, Bishop of Neocaesarea in Pontus, c. 254–258, addressing penitential discipline for offenses committed during and after the Gothic/Boradic invasions of Pontus. Chosen from the `church-fathers/` backlog as a well-scoped single-work pick, continuing the pattern from the other Gregory Thaumaturgus work already translated (`trinity/`, pt-BR name "Gregório Taumaturgo" — reused here for consistency in `book.json`).
- No footnotes in the source beyond inline Scripture cross-references (added by the 19th-century edition), which are kept inline exactly as the English places them, per the established corpus convention (cf. Ignatian epistles, Didache).
- Scripture references localized to standard Portuguese book names: 1 Corinthians → 1 Coríntios, Matthew → Mateus, Deuteronomy → Deuteronômio, Ephesians → Efésios, Joshua → Josué, Exodus → Êxodo.
- Quoted Scripture text throughout is translated directly from this specific 19th-century English rendering (Salmond's own wording, which sometimes differs slightly from any standard Bible translation, e.g. "Meats for the belly, and the belly for meats" for 1 Cor 6:13), not substituted with an existing pt-BR Bible translation's wording — consistent with the Didache and Ignatian-epistle precedent of translating the source edition's own words rather than citing a standard target-language Bible.
- Canon 3's "whine these now take what belongs to brethren" — "whine" is almost certainly a transcription artifact for "while" in the digitized source (the sentence structure and sense require a contrastive conjunction, not the verb "to whine," which would be grammatically and semantically incoherent here). Translated as intending "while" (*ao passo que*). Not treated as a deliberate archaism to preserve literally, since it produces no coherent English reading either.
- Formal register throughout — this is a bishop's canon-law letter regulating penitential discipline, addressed to "most holy father" (a fellow-bishop), so the register matches the corpus's other conciliar/canonical documents (Trent Catechism, penance sections) rather than the more devotional register of, say, the Ignatian epistles.
- Second person plural ("vós"/imperative plural) used throughout for direct address to the recipient bishop and his clergy (Canons 5–7 addressing "you" as those who must adjudicate cases), matching the letter's own plural address once it shifts from general moral exposition (Canons 1–4, which use no second person) to direct instruction.
