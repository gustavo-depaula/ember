## Translation Decisions

- Source text is the only language directory (en-US). It carries no footnote markers, only stray spaces where NPNF notes were stripped ("Leontius , now"); these spaces are not reproduced. No editor footnotes to drop; no translator notes added.
- Section numbers: the en-US writes each section as a `N. *summary.*` line followed by the body. The pt-BR writes them as `**N.** *resumo.*` per `.claude/rules/books.md`, keeping the summary italic and as its own line. The en-US form is left as imported.
- §10 has two body paragraphs in the source; kept as two.
- Scripture references are bare, inline, at the source's position, with Portuguese book names (Mateus, Lucas, João, Isaías, 1 Samuel, 1 Reis, 2 Reis, Êxodo, Gênesis, Eclesiastes, Deuteronômio, Sabedoria, Hebreus, Romanos, 2 Coríntios, Gálatas, 2 Timóteo), as in `vita-s-antoni/pt-BR`. Where the source puts a reference inside a quotation, the pt-BR closes the quotation first and puts the reference after it. Citation oddities the edition carries are mirrored: §13 "Isaiah 9:5" for "fuel of fire", §14 "Exodus 23:26; Genesis 25:8" as a pair.
- Scripture quotations follow the source wording clause by clause, not a Portuguese Bible: §14 Job 5:26 "like ripe grain… like as a shock of grain" → "como o grão maduro… como o feixe de grão"; §21 Eccl 5:8 "He that is higher than the highest regards" → "Aquele que é mais alto que o altíssimo vigia"; §20 Ps 44 "My heart uttered a good word" → "O meu coração proferiu uma boa palavra".
- Psalms named by the source's (Septuagint) numbers in §27: "Salmo vinte e seis", "Salmo trinta".
- Quotation marks: the source's outer '…' become “…”, inner “…” become ‘…’ (§23).
- Pronouns and clitics referring to God and Christ are capitalized (Ele, Lhe, O, Se, nEle), as in `vita-s-antoni/pt-BR` (`incarnation-of-the-word` and `statement-of-faith` use lowercase; no house rule).
- Bracketed passages kept in brackets as in the source: §6 "[11 de maio]", §26 "[Embora o herético Constâncio…]".
- §1 "because forsooth" (ironic) → "porque, vejam só,".
- Title kept in Latin, "Apologia de Fuga", as in en-US.

### Round 3

Focus: source-blind cold read of the pt-BR, with each mark then checked against `en-US/ch001.md`; a mechanical sweep of spelling, diacritics, crase, hyphenation, punctuation and quote pairing; plus the mechanical audit and `book.json`.

Verdict: clean. No changes.

Considered and rejected:
- §15 "o caso é claro: que os santos sabem …, mas que ninguém conhece o fim desse tempo, indicam-no claramente as palavras de Davi": the source has the same broken construction ("the case is clear, that the saints know …, but that no one knows the end of that time is plainly intimated by the words of David"). The pt-BR mirrors it, and `-no` picks up the `que`-clause, so it parses.
- §21 "E assim também o Pregador, que conhecia as conspirações contra os justos, e disse: …" has no main verb: the source has none either ("And so also the Preacher, who knew the conspiracies against the righteous, and said, …").
- §3 "o êxito das suas tramas contra os seus bispos": the two possessives refer to different things (the Arians' plots, the Church's bishops; source "their plots against her Bishops"), and the sense makes clear which is which.
- §9 "certo como é, segundo a palavra do nosso Salvador": it follows the English "certain as it is", but it is grammatical and clear, so this is a matter of style.
- §20 "teve o Senhor a seu lado, que repreendeu Labão": the relative clause makes the Lord the subject. That matches the source's "rebuking Laban" (Gen 31:24).
- §6 "partiu a atacar" ("proceeded to attack"), and §2 "tendo Deus lhes dado" (proclisis with the gerund): both are grammatical Brazilian usage.
- "Eufrátion": this is the name form in Key Terms. It is not a spelling slip.

### Round 4

Focus: function words (prepositions, articles, demonstratives, connectors, possessives), all 27 section headings, and places where Portuguese must commit and the source is open (gender, antecedents, a turn-by-turn tu/vós trace of every quoted address); plus the mechanical audit and `book.json`.

Verdict: two defects, fixed.

Fixed:
- §14: "Ora, enquanto era Deus e Verbo do Pai, não tinha tempo" → "Ora, enquanto Deus e Verbo do Pai, não tinha tempo". The source says "in so far as He was God". With a finite verb, "enquanto era" reads as "while He was God", and the contrast with "Mas, feito homem" then suggests that He stopped being God.
- §16: "Nosso Senhor, portanto, como disse antes, ofereceu-Se" → "… como eu disse antes, …". The source has "as I said before". Next to the subject "Nosso Senhor", a null-subject "disse" reads as "as He said before". In §22 and §27 the nearby subjects are plural, so "como disse" there can only be first person and stays.

Considered and rejected:
- §18 "quando lhes foi dito: 'Deves dar testemunho em Roma'" (source "when they were told, 'You must bear witness at Rome'"): the singular is the wording of Acts 23:11, which is spoken to Paul. The source's "You" is open, so keeping the verse's singular is defensible.
- §13 "or rather even hear of them, for see they do not" → "ou antes, quando apenas ouvem falar delas": here "even" means "just": they hear of these things but do not see them. "apenas" renders that sense.
- §22 "or the Judge determine something concerning them" → "ou que o Juiz determine algo a seu respeito": "seu" refers to the men, who are the subject of the law's infinitives. A reflexive reading ("about Himself") is not a natural one.
- §23 "a qual devemos submeter-nos": "qual" is interrogative and takes no article. The "a" is the bare preposition, not "à".

### Round 5

Focus: Key Terms, the journal's claims, and proper names. Per-paragraph term-frequency diff of all 56 blocks in both directions; every falsifiable journal claim grepped against the chapter files and the sibling works it cites; every proper name checked against standard Portuguese and the pt-BR works in `content/books/church-fathers/`. Plus the mechanical audit and `book.json`.

Verdict: five defects, fixed (one in the text, four in the journal).

Fixed:
- §27, Psalm 26: "O Senhor é a fortaleza da minha vida" → "… a força da minha vida". The source has "strength". "fortaleza" is the familiar Portuguese Bible wording, and in this book it is the Key Terms rendering of "fortitude", the cardinal virtue argued in §§17-19 and §22.
- Translation Decisions, Scripture book names: the list omitted Lucas and João, which the text uses (3 and 9 references). Added both.
- Key Terms, "dispensation → disposição": the note said "as in the sibling patristic translations". None of them uses it: the siblings that render the word use "dispensação" or "economia", and no Athanasius sibling in pt-BR has the word in its source. The note now says it is this book's choice. The text stays, because "disposição" correctly gives God's ordering of events.
- Translation Decisions, capitalized divine pronouns "as in the sibling Athanasius translations": only `vita-s-antoni` capitalizes them. `incarnation-of-the-word` and `statement-of-faith` use lowercase. The claim is corrected and the text is left alone (`.claude/rules/books.md`: no per-book normalization of reverential capitalization).
- Key Terms, reverse direction: three target terms also render a second source word, and the table did not say so. "tramas" also renders "schemes" (§2), "fuga" also renders "escape" (§25 heading), and "retirar-se" also renders "went thence" (§12, John 11:54) and "departed thence" (§13, Matthew 14:13). In each case the second word means the same thing, so the rows were updated and the text was left alone.

Considered and rejected:
- §10 "dispositions more savage than wild beasts" → "disposições": it is the literal cognate in its ordinary sense (temper). It does not clash with "disposição" for God's dispensation.
- §12/§13 "retirou-Se dali" for "went/departed thence": "retirar-se" also means simply "to leave". Matthew 14:13 is ἀνεχώρησεν ("withdrew") in the Greek. No claim is added.
- "Eufrátion", "Nilamon", "Balaneia", "Eustólia": Portuguese has no established form for these, and no sibling pt-BR work uses them. The forms recorded in Key Terms are consistent across the text.
- Names checked against siblings and found consistent: Cucuso (`four-letters-to-olympias`), Dracôncio (`life-of-s-hilarion`, the same exiled bishop), Constâncio, Libério, Acab, Abdias, Ocozias, Labão, Abimelec, Roboão, Miqueias, Jezabel, Bereia, Ancira, Adrianópolis, Laodiceia, Ilírico, Sárdica, Fílon, Arquelau, Atanásio (all four `athanasius/*` book.json files).
