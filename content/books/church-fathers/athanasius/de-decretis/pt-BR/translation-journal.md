## Translation Decisions

- The en-US is the only language directory. It carries no footnotes, only stray spaces where NPNF notes were stripped ("Scripture ,"); these are not reproduced. No editor footnotes to drop; no translator notes added.
- Structure: title, seven `##` chapter headings (NPNF/New Advent analyses, kept and translated, italic parts mirrored), 32 numbered sections. The en-US writes section numbers as `N. ` list markers; the pt-BR writes `**N.**` per `.claude/rules/books.md`. Unnumbered paragraphs, including the quoted extracts of Theognostus, Dionysius of Alexandria, Dionysius of Rome and Origen, stay as plain paragraphs in quotation marks, as in the source. 56 blocks on each side.
- The source ends after §32. It has no text of the letter of Eusebius that §3 promises to subjoin; nothing was added.
- Scripture references: Portuguese book names, placed in parentheses after the closing quotation mark, as in `deposition-of-arius` and `tomus-ad-antiochenos`. A reference the source sets outside a quotation stays at its position ("como disse Tiago (Tiago 1:8)"); the bare "Provérbios 8:22" in the Chapter 3 heading stays bare. Quotations the source leaves unreferenced stay unreferenced. 55 references on each side, same order.
- Scripture quotations follow Newman's wording, not a Portuguese Bible: Isa 1:2 "I have begotten and exalted children" → "Gerei e exaltei filhos" (§10 argues from "begotten"); Prov 8:22 "created me a beginning of His ways unto His works" → "me criou princípio dos Seus caminhos para as Suas obras"; Ps 44 "My heart uttered a good Word" → "O Meu coração proferiu um bom Verbo"; 2 Cor 4:11 "we which live are always" → "nós, os que vivemos, somos sempre"; Joel 2:25 "power" and "great power" → "poder" e "grande poder" (§20 argues from the word); Heb 11:3 "framed by the Word of God" → "pelo Verbo de Deus", following the source's capital and §18's argument.
- Quotation marks: the source's outer '…' and “…” both become “…”; nested quotations become ‘…’.
- Pronouns and clitics referring to God and Christ are capitalized (Ele, Lhe, -O/-Lo/-No, Se, Si, Seu, Meu, Tu, Te, d'Ele, n'Ele), as in `apologia-de-fuga`, `deposition-of-arius` and `tomus-ad-antiochenos`.
- Address: the letter's recipient is "tu" throughout (the source mixes "thou" and "you" for the same person); the Arians, addressed directly in §7–9 and §25–27, are "vós".
- §23 "separated … in nature and virtues" (brass/gold) → "em natureza e propriedades": "virtues" here means natural powers, not moral virtue.
- §25 the source's garbled "was suspected of saying that the Son as a thing made" is rendered by its evident sense ("foi suspeito de dizer que o Filho era coisa feita"). The Dionysius of Alexandria extract ends without a full stop in the source; mirrored.
- Title and toc kept in Latin, "De Decretis", as `apologia-de-fuga` and `tomus-ad-antiochenos` keep their Latin titles.
