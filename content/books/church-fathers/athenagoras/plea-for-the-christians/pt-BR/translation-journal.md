# Translation Journal — A Plea for the Christians (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (B. P. Pratten translation, Ante-Nicene Fathers Vol. 2, via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Key Terms

No other Athenagoras work has a pt-BR translation yet. Conventions follow `athanasius/incarnation-of-the-word/pt-BR` and `ambrose/repentance/pt-BR`: author name without "Santo" (Athenagoras has no liturgical cult; Athanasius's precedent is "Atanásio"), "On X"-style titles with capitalized nouns, Scripture book names in full Portuguese with the source's chapter:verse, curly quotes “ ”, lowercase pronouns for God (ele, dele) with capitalized titles, `vós` for the emperors.

## Translation Decisions

- Editor footnotes: none exist in the New Advent import; none added.
- Headings: the 37 `## Chapter N.` headings are the ANF editor's chapter titles; translated as "Capítulo N." with the source's trailing period. Italic dedication and bold byline mirrored.
- Scripture is translated from Pratten's wording, not from a Portuguese Bible. References stay inline where the source places them (after the quotation, before the next sentence); "Luke 6:27-28; Matthew 5:44-45" → "Lucas 6:27-28; Mateus 5:44-45".
- Verse quotations (Homer, Euripides, Orpheus, etc.) are rendered from the English verse, line for line, without meter or rhyme. Lines kept in the same count; the source's ellipsis lines in ch. 21 mirrored.
- English `:—` before quotations rendered as `:`; stray spaces before closing punctuation (“this one )”, “twin brothers” )”) not reproduced.
- Greek glosses copied as in the source.
- Ch. 22 "on whose murder by Typhon his brother Isis with her son Orus sought": read as "Typhon, his brother, Isis…" (Isis is Osiris's sister) → "por Tífon, seu irmão, Ísis com seu filho Hórus". Only a comma is missing; en-US left as is.
- Ch. 21 "Hush! Homer" → "Cala-te, Homero"; the following address to Homer and to Apollo uses `tu`.
- Ch. 33 "a specious adultery" → "um adultério decoroso"; "cloaked adulterer" → "adúltero disfarçado".
- Ch. 34 "the once-married" → "os casados uma só vez".

## Source edits (en-US, OCR-class)

- Ch. 1 "you will team from this discourse" → "learn".
- Ch. 6 "(τὄ ζέον)" → "(τὸ ζέον)" — the article cannot carry a breathing.
- Ch. 17 "concering" → "concerning".
- Ch. 21 "A crisius' daughter" → "Acrisius' daughter".
- Ch. 21 "superior to the god. prophet and wise one" → "god. O prophet and wise one" (dropped vocative, lowercase after a period).
- Ch. 22 "Nê stis" → "Nêstis".
- Ch. 27 "comformably" → "conformably".
- Ch. 30 "a Syria goddess" → "a Syrian goddess".

## Review log

### Round 1

Focus: completeness, paragraph by paragraph (plus the mechanical audit and `book.json`). Verdict: 6 defects, all fixed.

Fixed:
- Ch. 8 "Socrates was compounded and divided into parts": "composto e divisível em partes" → "composto e dividido em partes". "Divisível" says able to be divided; the source says divided.
- Ch. 14 "shave themselves when they die": "raspam a cabeça" → "raspam-se". The source does not name the head (Herodotus has eyebrows for cats, the whole body for dogs).
- Ch. 16 "they do not neglect to pay their homage to you … and address themselves to the magnificence of your palace": "não deixa de prestar homenagem a vós … e de dirigir-se à magnificência" → "… para se dirigir à magnificência". The pt-BR said the subjects do address the palace; the source says they do not leave the rulers for it (the next clause, "but, if they chance to come upon the royal residence", depends on this).
- Ch. 23 "the second moving about the seconds": "o segundo em torno das segundas coisas" → "o segundo, que se move em torno das segundas coisas". "Moving" was dropped from the quotation.
- Ch. 35 "by whom we could not help being seen": "aos quais não poderíamos deixar de ser vistos" → "pelos quais …". The passive agent takes "por".
- Key Terms, "intercourse (sexual) → união": the row claimed every instance; ch. 33 "intercourse of the race" reads "comunhão da espécie". Text kept (avoids repeating "união" in the same clause, and matches the Greek κοινωνία); the row now records the exception.

Considered and rejected:
- Greek words in pt-BR use tonos (U+03AC–03CE) where en-US uses oxia (U+1F71–1F7D) (ch. 6 ζέον/ἀήρ, ch. 10 λόγος, ch. 17 κοροπλαθική, ch. 18 οὐρανός, ch. 20 θηλή, ch. 22, ch. 23 ψυχικαί). Unicode NFC folds oxia into tonos, so the letters are the same; not a defect.
- Ch. 1 "These we hold in contempt, though … they appear matters of great importance" → "Isso nós desprezamos, embora … pareçam coisas de grande importância". The plural verb takes "coisas" as its predicate, which Portuguese allows after "isso"; not a concord error.
- Ch. 16 "we do not neglect to adore God … and descend 'to the poor and weak elements'" → "não deixamos de adorar a Deus … para descer". Same construction as the ch. 16 fix above, and the pt-BR already gives the source's sense.
- Ch. 22 "explained of natural objects in different ways" → "explicadas … como objetos naturais". Means "taken as referring to natural objects", which is the source's sense.

### Round 2

Focus: clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read (plus the mechanical audit and `book.json`). Verdict: 2 defects, both fixed.

Fixed:
- Ch. 11 "(to abstain from which is of itself an evidence of no mean forbearance)": "(abster-se do que já é por si prova …)" → "(abster-se disso já é por si prova …)". The old wording reads as "to abstain from what is already proof". In the source, abstaining from evil speech is itself the proof.
- Key Terms, "Zeus / Jove / Jupiter" row: it said Diana and Minerva are Latin names kept "in verse". Both occur only in the ch. 28 Herodotus prose quotations. The text is correct; the row now says where each name occurs.

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 18 "Homer speaks of 'Old Oceanus, The sire of gods, and Tethys'" → "Homero fala do “Velho Oceano, / Pai dos deuses, e de Tétis”". "e de Tétis" is governed by "fala … de" outside the quote, so Tethys is a second subject of Homer's speech, not Oceanus's child. This matches the source.
- Ch. 24 "to exercise providence for God over the things created" → "exercer a providência, por Deus, sobre as coisas". "Por" here means "on God's behalf", which is the source's sense, and the following clause ("de modo que Deus tenha a providência universal") rules out reading it as an agent.
- Ch. 26 "eager for the blood of the sacrifices, and lick them" → "ávidos do sangue dos sacrifícios e o lambem". The pronoun takes "sangue"; what is licked on a sacrifice is its blood, so the meaning is the same.
- Ch. 26 "And what can brass do of itself, which may be made again into a different form" → "se pode ser refeito noutra forma". The "se" clause gives the ground ("given that it can be"), not a doubt. Same sense.
- Ch. 7 "to cease to believe in the Spirit … and to give heed to mere human opinions" → "deixarmos de crer no Espírito … para dar ouvidos a meras opiniões humanas". Both describe giving up belief in the Spirit and following human opinion instead; "para" does not add a purpose the source lacks.

Evidence (quotes, source beside pt-BR): `ember-translation-evidence/church-fathers__athenagoras__plea-for-the-christians/review-2.md`, kept outside the repo.

### Round 3

Focus: source-blind cold read, then a mechanical spelling sweep (quotes, spacing, post-1990 orthography, crase, hyphens, concord), plus the mechanical audit and `book.json`. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed.

Fixed:
- Ch. 10 "power ineffable, by whom the universe has been created through His Logos": "poder inefável, pelo qual o universo foi criado" → "por quem o universo foi criado". "Pelo qual" attaches to the nearest noun, so the power read as the agent. In the source the agent is God.

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 12 "Are, then, those who consider … to be accounted pious; while men … shall we not … be accounted pious?" → "Devem, pois, ser tidos por piedosos os que …? … não seremos nós … tidos por piedosos?" The question mark comes only at the end, but the inverted "Devem … ser tidos" reads as a question. Not a defect.
- Ch. 12 "we not only love our friends (…)—" → "não só amamos os nossos amigos (…) —", with no "mas também". The source breaks off the same way.
- Ch. 32 "the inventor of these tales, which made Zeus more unholy" → "o inventor destes contos, que tornaram Zeus". The plural agrees with "contos", which is what the source says.
- Ch. 28 "against the back wall, which it entirely covers" → "contra o muro posterior, que cobre inteiramente". "Que" is the object and the tomb is the implied subject, as in the source.

### Round 4

Focus: function words, headings, and places where Portuguese must commit and the source is open (gender, antecedent, possessive `seu/sua`, demonstratives, tu/vós traced turn by turn), plus the mechanical audit and `book.json`. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed.

Fixed:
- Ch. 17 "traced his shadow on a wall as he lay asleep": "a sombra dele enquanto dormia" → "a sombra dele enquanto ele dormia". The null subject of "dormia" takes the clause subject, so the pt-BR said the girl was asleep. In the source the young man sleeps.

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 4 "openly declared that there was no God at all" → "não havia deus algum". Diagoras denies every god, as in ch. 1 "no god at all" → "deus algum". Reverential capitals are not normalized (books.md).
- Headings ch. 2 "Claim to Be Treated …" → "Reivindicação de ser tratados …" and ch. 37 "Entreaty to Be Fairly Judged" → "Súplica para ser julgados …". A plural participle after an uninflected infinitive with an implied plural subject is accepted usage, and the two headings agree.
- Heading ch. 25 "Have Denied a Divine Providence" → "uma Providência divina". The source heading is title case, so it does not fix the case.
- Ch. 22 "air, born of Kronos, of which the male part is called Zeus" → "o ar, nascido de Cronos, cuja parte masculina". "Cuja" could reach back to Cronos, but the source is just as open, and "(donde é irmã e esposa)" ties it to the air.
- Ch. 27 "some they fashion and bring forth for themselves" → "modelam-nas e dão-nas à luz por si mesmas". The contrast is with images "derived from matter", so "on their own" carries the source's point. The difference from "for themselves" is a shade, not a different claim.
- Ch. 30 "you in fact proclaim him dead, even to those who are ignorant" → "até para os que o ignoram". "O" refers to the fact just stated, so nothing is added.

### Round 5

Focus: terms, journal and names. A per-paragraph term-frequency diff against the Key Terms table in both directions, a grep of every claim in the journal, and every proper name checked against the standard Portuguese form and sibling pt-BR works. Also the mechanical audit and `book.json`. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed. The chapter text is unchanged.

Fixed:
- Key Terms, "Zeus / Jove / Jupiter" row: the English column listed "Jupiter", which the source never uses (0 hits). The note also left out Latona (verse in ch. 21 and 29, and the ch. 28 Herodotus quotation) and Bacchus (ch. 28 Herodotus, → Baco). The row is now "Zeus / Jove", and the note lists every Latin name with where it occurs.

Considered and rejected:
- "Criador" renders "Creator" once, in ch. 8 ("the Creator of the world is above the things created"), as well as all 13 instances of "Maker". Both words name the same maker of the world, so this does not contradict the table.
- "divindade" (lowercase) renders "divinity" twice: ch. 19 "to which divinity is ascribed" and the ch. 30 heading. It also renders the two instances of "deity" that the table covers. The sense is the same in all four.
- "artífice" (lowercase) in ch. 6 "an artistic fire" → "um fogo artífice". This is an adjective, not the title that "Framer / Artificer → Artífice" covers.
- Name forms differ from some sibling works (Asclépio vs Esculápio, Dioniso vs Dionísio, Cronos vs Saturno). Each book follows its own source's form. This source uses the Greek names, and so does Tatian's *Address to the Greeks*, whose pt-BR also has Asclépio and Dioniso. Justin's *First Apology* has Esculápio because its source has Æsculapius.
- The Greek "(τὸ ζέον)" fails a byte-exact grep against the journal's source-edit line, because the two use different code points for the accent. After NFC normalization the strings are identical (see Round 1).

### Round 8

Focus: source-blind cold read, then a spelling-only sweep (quotes, spacing, post-1990 orthography, crase, hyphens, name accents), plus the mechanical audit and `book.json`. Verdict: 2 defects, both fixed.

Fixed:
- Ch. 18 "and Argos, whom also he bound and hurled down to Tartarus": "aos quais também amarrou e precipitou" → "os quais também amarrou e precipitou". Both verbs take a direct object, so the relative takes no preposition.
- Ch. 6 "Lysis and Opsimus": "Opsimo" → "Ópsimo". The name (Ὄψιμος) is stressed on the first syllable, so the Portuguese proparoxytone needs the accent. Without it, the name reads Op-SI-mo.

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 1 "for do not, like the multitude, be led astray by hearsay" → "pois não vos deixeis … desencaminhar". The source is imperative, and so is the pt-BR.
- Ch. 6 "ten … and the Nine" → "o dez … o Nove". The case follows the source.
- Ch. 28 "but the females, they are not allowed to sacrifice" → "mas as fêmeas não lhes é permitido sacrificar". The topicalized object follows the source's anacoluthon.
- Ch. 26 "dão-no fortíssima prova". "Dão" + "o" → "dão-no" is the correct enclitic form.

### Round 10

Focus: terms, journal and names. A per-paragraph term-frequency diff against the Key Terms table in both directions, a grep of every claim in the journal, and every proper name checked against the standard Portuguese form and sibling pt-BR works. Also the mechanical audit and `book.json`. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed. The Key Terms table and all journal claims hold.

Fixed:
- Ch. 18 "the hundred-handed Cottys, Gyges, Briareus": "Cotis" → "Cótis". Κόττυς is stressed on the first syllable, and the standard Portuguese form of the name (the Thracian Cotys) is "Cótis". Without the accent the name reads co-TIS. This is the same rule as the Round 8 fix to "Ópsimo".

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 22 and 28 "Typhon" → "Tífon", where Aristides' *Apology* pt-BR has "Tifão". Both forms are current Portuguese, Athanasius's *Vita S. Antoni* pt-BR also has "Tífon", and this book is consistent.
- Ch. 30 "Derceto" → "Derceto", unaccented. Latin quantity would give "Dérceto", but "Derceto" is the form current in Portuguese. Not an objective error.
- "União" also renders "union" (ch. 10 "their power in union", ch. 18 "by his union with Gê"). The source word there is "union". The Key Terms row governs "intercourse", and every instance of "intercourse" follows it.
