## Translation Decisions

- Editor material: the source has no footnotes. The ANF note in chapter 2 ("*(On the tenth of the kalends of April)*" and the six paragraphs that discuss the date) sits in the body text. It is translated in place, as `ambrose/repentance` translates its editor's introduction, so that the files match paragraph for paragraph. The Latin quotations in it stay in Latin, with the source's stray spaces before commas.
- Edicts (chapters 34 and 48) follow the source's punctuation: the unquoted paragraphs stay unquoted and the quoted last paragraphs stay quoted. Emperors address the governor as `tu`.
- Licinius's prayer (chapter 46): the source capitalizes "You"; the pt-BR uses lowercase `te/ti`, matching `ambrose/repentance`'s lowercase reverential pronouns. "Deus" and "Divindade" stay capitalized.
- Chapter 27 "a father against his son-in-law": kept "pai", not "sogro", because that is what the source says.
- Chapter 38 "His conflicts also imitated the example of the emperor": "conflicts" makes no sense here and is probably a scan slip for "courtiers" or "companions" (Latin *comites*). The intended word cannot be confirmed, so the source is left as is. The pt-BR has "os seus companheiros".
- Chapter 9 "How long am I to be Cæsar?" has no quotation marks in the source, and none in the pt-BR either.
- Verse quotations (Virgil and others, chapters 12, 16, 30, 33) are translated plainly, line for line, with no attempt at meter. The attribution "— Pitt" is kept.
- Heading "Chapter 34" has no period in the source; mirrored ("Capítulo 34").
- Quotes: curly “ ” throughout, as in the source.

## Review log

### Round 1

Focus: completeness, paragraph by paragraph (164 blocks, 168 non-empty lines, in each file, aligned one to one), plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed.

Fixed:

- Ch. 24, "for Galerius meant nothing less than to grant it": "pois Galério nada menos pretendia do que atendê-lo" → "pois nada estava mais longe das intenções de Galério do que atendê-lo". Portuguese *nada menos que X* means "no less than X", so the clause said Galerius fully meant to grant the request, the opposite of the source.

Rejected:

- Ch. 10, "that from their livers he might obtain a prognostic" → "das suas entranhas": a generalization that states nothing false. Style.
- Ch. 13, "These are the triumphs of Goths and Sarmatians" → "os triunfos sobre godos e sármatas": the jibe targets the emperors' victory titles (*Gothicus*, *Sarmaticus*). "sobre" is a correct reading of the English genitive.
- Ch. 7, "Here public halls" → "Aqui basílicas": the Roman sense of *basilica*, the word Lactantius uses. Defensible.
- Ch. 2 editor note, "Jarvis adheres to the reading septimam": pt-BR italicizes *septimam* and en-US does not. This follows the Latin-in-italics convention and changes no content.
- Ch. 49, "as if he had been racked into confession" → "forçado pela tortura à confissão": the meaning is kept. The Key Terms row "rack → cavalete" covers the noun, not this verb.

### Round 2

Focus: clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of the whole text against en-US, plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed.

Fixed:

- Ch. 48, "having received part of the soldiers of Daia into his service, and properly distributed them": "e distribuído-os convenientemente" → "e tendo-os distribuído convenientemente". Portuguese does not attach a clitic to a past participle.

Rejected:

- Ch. 21, "after a manner unknown in the chastisement of slaves" → "desconhecida até no castigo dos escravos": "até" spells out the contrast the source implies (a punishment not even slaves received). It adds no content.
- Ch. 24, "one evening gave Constantine a warrant" → "certa tarde": Portuguese "tarde" covers the early evening. Defensible.
- Ch. 44, "his troops stood to arms" → "pegaram em armas": same meaning.
- Ch. 46, prayer, "unto You we commend all right; unto You we commend our safety" → "toda a justiça … a nossa salvação": "right" here means what is right (Latin *iustitiam*), and "salvação" is a normal rendering of *salus* in a prayer. Defensible.

### Round 3

Focus: source-blind cold read of the pt-BR, with each mark then checked against en-US, followed by a mechanical sweep of spelling, diacritics, crase, hyphenation, punctuation and quotation marks, plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed.

Fixed:

- Ch. 27, "forces too powerful to be resisted": "forças poderosas demais para serem resistidas" → "forças poderosas demais para que se pudesse resistir a elas". *Resistir* takes *a* and has no standard passive.

Rejected:

- Ch. 10, "I have learned that the cause of his fury was as follows" → "Soube que a causa da sua fúria foi a seguinte": *soube* is also first person, and reading "he" (Galerius learning the cause of his own fury) makes no sense, so the reader lands on the narrator.
- Ch. 39, "the ladies who enjoyed most of her friendship and confidence" → "da sua amizade e confiança": the sentence lists Daia's acts against Valeria ("os seus bens", "os seus servidores", "desterrou-a"), and "para coroar tudo" marks one more blow against her. *Sua* means hers.
- Ch. 12, "the prefect, together with … came … they searched" → "o prefeito, junto com …, veio … buscaram": the plural follows the group, as the source's "they" does.
- Ch. 44/46, "directed in a dream to cause" / "admonishing him to arise" → "advertido … a fazer desenhar" / "advertindo-o a levantar-se": *advertir alguém a* + infinitive ("to admonish someone to") is attested.

### Round 4

Focus: function words (prepositions, articles, demonstratives, connectors, possessive *seu/sua*, *consigo*), every heading and label, and each place where the pt-BR must commit and the source is open (referent gender, pronoun antecedent, a turn-by-turn tu/vós trace through Donatus's apostrophes, the ch. 18 dialogue, the ch. 31 apostrophe, the ch. 46 prayer and the ch. 48 edict), plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed.

Fixed:

- Ch. 24, last paragraph, "made it his first care to restore the Christians": "fez o seu primeiro cuidado restituir" → "teve como primeiro cuidado restituir". This was a calque of English "made it his first care to". In Portuguese, *fazer* + noun phrase leaves the infinitive without a grammatical slot.

Rejected:

- Ch. 2 editor note, "reconciling Lactantius with himself" → "conciliar Lactâncio consigo mesmo": *consigo* refers to the object here, but *conciliar alguém consigo mesmo* is a fixed, accepted pattern.
- Ch. 40, "protested … that the women died innocent" → "morriam inocentes": the English "died" is a backshifted present in reported speech, and the imperfect reproduces it. Defensible.
- Ch. 44, "made his title of the Greatest a theme of abuse" → "fez do seu título de o Maior": "seu" is as open as "his", and the context resolves it (Daia mocks the title the senate gave Constantine). The uncontracted "de o Maior" keeps the title whole.
- Ch. 30, "solicited her to betray her husband" → "solicitou-a a trair o marido": *solicitar alguém a* + infinitive is literary but attested.

### Round 5

Verdict: no defect in the chapter text or `book.json`; 3 false journal claims, fixed.

Fixed (journal only):

- Key Terms, emperor row: "capitalized as in the source" → César/Augusto capitalized; *imperador*/*imperatriz* lowercase even before a name. The source has "the Emperor Tiberius", "the Emperor Nero", "the Empress Valeria", "The Emperor Severus", and the pt-BR has lowercase in all four (correct Portuguese), so the old note was false.
- Key Terms, tax-gatherer row: added the ch. 31 aphorism "No threshing-floor without a tax-gatherer" → "Nenhuma eira sem cobrador". The row claimed "cobrador de impostos" everywhere. The elliptic form is clear in context, so the table now records it and the text is unchanged.
- Round 1 entry: "168 blocks in each file" → "164 blocks, 168 non-empty lines". 168 counts the lines of the multi-line verse blockquotes; the files have 164 blank-line-separated blocks.

Rejected:

- Ch. 2, "the two prophets Enoch and Elias" → "Henoc": the corpus uses "Enoque" more often, but "Henoc" is the form in Catholic Bibles (Ave-Maria, CNBB) and in sibling `theophilus/theophilus-to-autolycus`. Both are standard.
- Ch. 8, "such as Africa and Spain" → "a Hispânia": it names the same region by its Roman name. Defensible.
- Ch. 2 editor note, "Baluzius" → "Balúzio" but "Walchius" kept: both are accepted ways to cite these scholars. Mixing them is a style choice, not a wrong name.
- Ch. 2, "Ruberius Geminus" → "Rubério Gêmino": follows the ANF spelling (historically Rubellius). The source's form is mirrored, as `.claude/rules/books.md` requires.
- Ch. 33, "his whole seat putrefied" → "todo o assento apodreceu": *assento* also means the buttocks (Houaiss). Accurate.

### Round 7

Focus: clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of all 164 blocks against en-US, checking negation, subject and object, tense and mood, false friends and dropped qualifiers, plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: clean, no defect found and nothing changed.

Rejected:

- Ch. 20, "he meant to have solemnized the vicennial festival; to have conferred … and … to have resigned" → "pretendia ter solenizado … ter conferido … ter renunciado"; ch. 43, "his true purpose was to have destroyed his son … and then to have reinstated" → "era ter destruído … e depois ter restabelecido". The perfect infinitive mirrors the source's (an aim to have done these things by a set point). In Portuguese, *pretender* means "intend", not "claim", so the clause cannot be read as a false claim.
- Ch. 43, "from the time that the preference was given to him by Galerius" → "desde o tempo em que Galério lhe dera a preferência": *lhe* is as open as "him", and the only candidate in the clause is Licínio ("contra Licínio" just before). Correct.
- Ch. 31, "all was raked together into the imperial treasury" → "tudo era rapinado para o tesouro imperial": the sentence describes spoliation ("os homens eram despojados dos seus bens"), and *rapinar* says that. Defensible.
