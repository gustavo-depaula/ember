# Translation Journal — Discourse to the Greeks (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (trans. Marcus Dods, Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 1; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-16: First pt-BR translation from Justin Martyr's `discourse-to-the-greeks` (single chapter, ~1,500 words, one continuous address with 5 numbered chapter-headings original to this edition). No prior Justin Martyr pt-BR glossary existed in the corpus (`martyrdom-of-justin-chariton-and-other-roman-martyrs/pt-BR` exists but is a martyrdom narrative with no shared mythological vocabulary); terms chosen fresh.
- 2026-09-16 — **address form**: the whole work is a direct rhetorical address to "you Greeks" as a people, never to a single interlocutor, so every second-person verb is rendered with the plural imperative/subjunctive (vós-register: "suponhais", "lede", "ensinai", "vinde"), not "tu".
- 2026-09-16 — **mythological names kept in their Roman forms** because the source itself uses the Roman pantheon names (Jupiter, not Zeus; Neptune, not Poseidon; Pluto, not Hades-as-god — though Hades is also used as the place name) throughout, rather than the Greek names one might expect given the addressees. Matched that choice in Portuguese rather than "correcting" it to Greek forms.
- 2026-09-16 — **"Hades" used both as place and left untranslated**: the source's "brought up from Hades the three-headed dog" and "Pluto... the portion of Hades" both use "Hades" as the place/underworld (not the god, who is called "Pluto" in this text) — rendered literally as "Hades" in both places, matching standard Portuguese usage which also uses "Hades" for the underworld.
- 2026-09-16 — **no editor footnotes** in the source; the bracketed "[Apollo]" in Chapter 2 is the translator's (Dods') own clarifying gloss on "son of Latona" and was kept in brackets, consistent with how other Ante-Nicene Fathers translations in this corpus preserve translator brackets (see `ignatius/epistle-to-polycarp`).
- 2026-09-16 — **em-dash sentence-ending construction** in Chapter 1 ("the beginning and end... is— a woman") kept as an em-dash pause in Portuguese ("é — uma mulher") to preserve the rhetorical anticlimax, rather than smoothing it into a plain declarative.
- 2026-09-16 — **review round 1 fixes** (3 parallel independent passes: structural/mechanical, Portuguese-only proofread, sentence-by-sentence fidelity): 7 genuine defects found and fixed —
  - Ch. 3: "as **velozes** aves" — number agreement (was singular "veloz" modifying plural "aves").
  - Ch. 3: "invídia" → "**inveja**" — not a standard modern Portuguese word (Latinism slip); "inveja" is the corpus's term for envy.
  - Ch. 3: "não se enfureça" ("become furious") → "não **seja invejoso**" — "Let Vulcan... not be jealous [of Mars]" names jealousy specifically (the whole point of the sentence is envy toward a rival), not rage.
  - Ch. 4 heading: "Práticas **vergonhosas**" ("shameful practices") → "Práticas **despudoradas**" — the source's "Shameless practices" (done *without* shame) is the near-opposite polarity of "shameful" (deserving shame); a load-bearing heading needs the right polarity.
  - Ch. 4: "**encheis**" (indicative "you fill") — was mistakenly "enchei" (imperative "Fill!"), flipping a descriptive accusation about existing Greek practice into a command, against every neighboring indicative verb in the same sentence (banis, sois, entregais).
  - Ch. 4: "sendo **gregos**" — number agreement with the surrounding vós address; the English "being a Greek" is grammatically singular but idiomatic (a generic "as a [any] Greek" that doesn't commit English to a number), while Portuguese "vós... sendo grego" must agree with the plural vós it modifies.
  - Ch. 4: "acusações **caluniosas**" — "calunhosas" is not a word; the adjective from *calúnia* is *caluniosa*.
- 2026-09-16 — **considered and rejected as defects** (verified against the en-US source directly, not just against the Portuguese):
  - Ch. 2 "o ciúme de **Sêmele**" ("the jealousy of Semele") — flagged by one reviewer as nonsensical (Semele can't be jealous of herself) without checking the source. The en-US source itself reads "the jealousy of Semele" verbatim — an ambiguous genitive in the *source* (could mean jealousy Semele felt, or jealousy provoked by/over her, e.g. Juno's) — not a translation defect. Portuguese "de Sêmele" preserves the exact same ambiguity as English "of Semele." Left as-is; egress to newadvent.org to check for a possible source transcription variant ("Juno" vs. "Semele") was blocked by the network proxy, so this rests on internal evidence only — worth a second look if the source ever becomes reachable.
  - Ch. 3 "ele mesmo ateando... assim morreu" (Nessus's death) — flagged as an ungrammatical gerund-with-no-finite-verb construction by the Portuguese-only pass (which had no access to the source). The en-US source uses the identical stacked-participle construction ("not being able to put off the tunic of Nessus, himself kindling his own funeral pile, so he died") — the Portuguese mirrors the source's own long periodic sentence rather than introducing a new defect. Left as-is.
  - Ch. 5 "Ó arma que **pões** em fuga..." / "Ó ensino que **apagas**..." — flagged by one round-2 pass as breaking the vós register used everywhere else. Rejected: the en-US source itself switches to archaic 2nd-person-singular here ("that **puttest** to flight" — note that the neighbouring "that quenches" is 3rd-person and does *not* support the claim; only *puttest* does) because these lines apostrophize a *singular* personification (the weapon, the instruction) one at a time, not the plural Greek audience — singular "tu" (pões/apagas) is the correct match, not a register slip.

## Review Round 2

- 2026-09-16 — 2 parallel independent passes (fresh cold fidelity re-read; adversarial re-verification of every round-1 fix + a full diacritics/verb-conjugation/naming-consistency sweep). Both independently surfaced the same defect, plus one more:
  - Ch. 2 "a sua **inconstância**" ("his unchastity") → "a sua **incastidade**" — "unchastity" (sexual impurity) was mistranslated as *inconstância* (fickleness), a different fault; the same English word is correctly rendered *incastidade* in Ch. 4 ("viver na incastidade"). Internal inconsistency on the same term across chapters — now aligned. Added to Key Terms.
  - Ch. 3 "tornando **Egito** sem filhos" ("making Ægyptus childless") → "tornando **Egito [seu irmão]** sem filhos" — *Ægyptus* is Danaus's brother in the myth (father of the fifty sons killed on their wedding night), not the country; the English spelling keeps person and place apart, but Portuguese *Egito* is the only word for both, so the bare name reads as "making Egypt [the country] childless." Added the bracketed gloss "[seu irmão]" (translator's clarification, matching the corpus's existing bracket convention for Dods' own "[Apollo]" gloss in Ch. 2) to disambiguate without asserting anything the myth doesn't already establish.
  - Both passes independently re-derived and confirmed the two round-1 "considered and rejected" calls (Semele's jealousy, the Nessus sentence) directly against the source, and one pass raised a third candidate (Ch. 5's singular tu-forms "pões"/"apagas") that the *other* pass had already resolved correctly in round 1's own fresh read — confirmed correct (see above) after checking the source's own switch to singular "thou" for those apostrophes.

## Review Round 3

- 2026-09-16 — 2 parallel independent passes (fresh cold fidelity+mechanical read; verification of round-2 fixes + full independent re-read). Both passes independently found the same defect, and one raised a legitimate style objection to a round-2 fix:
  - Ch. 3 "os vossos deuses são **convictos** de intemperança" ("your gods are convicted of intemperance") → "são **provados culpados** de intemperança" — *convicto de* is a false friend in modern Portuguese, reading as "convinced of" (firmly believing) rather than "found/proven guilty of." Notably the *other* occurrence of "convicted" — eight sentences earlier, in the previous chapter ("convicted himself of lying", of Apollo) had already been correctly avoided with a different construction ("provou-se mentiroso a si mesmo") — the false-friend trap was dodged once and not the second time.
  - **Round-2 fix corrected in kind, not reverted**: one pass objected that the round-2 bracketed gloss "Egito **[seu irmão]**" mimics the source's own genuine editorial bracket ("[Apollo]" in Ch. 2), making a translator's disambiguation look like part of the original 1885 apparatus. The underlying problem (bare "Egito" collides with the country name; the English spelling "Ægyptus" does not have this collision) is real and still needs fixing — but the skill's own convention for exactly this case is a translator footnote, not an inline bracket. Replaced with `Egito[^1]` plus a `*Nota do tradutor:*` footnote at the end of the chapter, matching the corpus's established footnote pattern.
  - No other defects found by either pass — mood, polarity, prepositional agency, and every proper name across all five chapters confirmed faithful on this round.

## Review Rounds 4–5 (two consecutive clean rounds — stopping point)

Both CLEAN — no new defects, and both independently re-confirmed the round-1–3 fixes hold (envy→inveja, convicted→provados culpados, the Egito footnote correctly formed).
- 2026-09-16 — Round 5: 2 independent passes (an adversarially-primed cold read explicitly trying to break the "clean" verdict by re-deriving every mythological pairing and re-checking every prior false-friend fix from scratch; a final structural/metadata check verifying book.json, the H1-vs-TOC-title match, journal-vs-file consistency, and encoding). Both CLEAN. Two consecutive clean rounds reached — stopping here per this corpus's review protocol (5 rounds total, 11 independent passes, 10 genuine defects found and fixed across rounds 1–3). **Superseded — see Review Round 6 below; this stopping point was premature, as it has been for every prior book in this series.**

## Review Round 6 — post-merge audit (PR #446 already merged)

- 2026-09-16 — 4 independent passes, deliberately re-partitioned away from the pre-merge cuts: (a) a **cold, source-blind Portuguese-only read** (run *first*, per the lesson carried forward from the Spurious Epistles audit); (b) bilingual fidelity scoped to Ch. 1–3; (c) bilingual fidelity scoped to Ch. 4–5 + H1 + footnote; (d) structural/metadata/diacritics + a journal-vs-file consistency check. **36 word-level edits** after two "clean" rounds had declared convergence (round 7 added a 37th — see below) — the stopping rule has now failed post-merge on four consecutive books.
- **Hard orthographic error missed by all 11 pre-merge passes**: Ch. 5 `retorna **a Aquele** que a fez` → `retorna **Àquele** que a fez`. The contraction of *a* + *aquele* is obligatory. Three of the four round-6 passes flagged it independently, including the structural sweep and the source-blind read — it was invisible to bilingual sentence-aligned reading because the English ("to Him who made it") is perfectly matched by the Portuguese *content*; only the orthography is wrong. **Sentence alignment cannot see a contraction error, because the defect lives below the clause level the eye is comparing.**
- Other grammar/collocation fixes: `ao ponto de` → `a ponto de` (BR idiom; *ao ponto de* is a Lusism); `ateando a própria pira` → `acendendo a própria pira` (*atear* requires *fogo* as its object); `através da qual` → `pela qual` and `através do Verbo` → `por meio do Verbo` (*através de* for means, not passage); `tornando Egito sem filhos` → `deixando Egito sem filhos`.
- **A defect can hide inside a previous round's own fix.** Round 1 correctly changed "Let Vulcan… not be jealous" from `não se enfureça` (rage) to `não seja invejoso` (envy) — but *invejoso* collapsed onto `inveja`, the rendering of "envy" in the immediately preceding clause, erasing the source's envy/jealousy pair; and the conditional "**if** he is hated" had been asserted as a causal `por ser odiado`. Now `não tenha ciúmes **se é** odiado`. **When a round fixes a word, the next round must re-check that word against its neighbours, not just against the source.**
- **Generic source nouns silently narrowed to the specific myth detail**: "the huge **Augean building**" had become `o enorme **estábulo** de Augias` — the translation supplied "stable," which Dods deliberately withholds. Now `a enorme construção de Áugias` (also fixing the missing proparoxytone accent on **Áugias**). Same class: "did not **gain possession of** her" (Daphne) had been weakened to `não a alcançou` (merely "did not catch up with"), losing the sexual-failure point that parallels the Hyacinthus line.
- **A false friend in the *other* direction**: `foi **cativado** por Criseida` — *cativar* in modern Portuguese means *to charm*, not *to take prisoner*, so the English pun ("they took captives… was himself taken captive") inverted into Agamemnon being charmed. Now `foi feito cativo`. The correct register was already on the page two clauses earlier (`fizeram cativos`) — the same "right instinct that didn't transfer" pattern the round-3 *convicto* entry names.
- Dropped content restored: "a host of Nereids not a few" (the doublet had been halved to `não poucas Nereidas`); "would cost **us** a multitude of words"; "so many **and such like** and so great deeds" (one member of a tricolon deleted).
- Term-distinction fixes: "instruction" was rendered `ensino` / `doutrina` / `ensino` across one paragraph — normalized to `ensino`; "hymeneal **robe**" and "the **tunic** of Nessus" both `túnica` → now `veste nupcial` / `túnica`; "departure" (heading) and "separation" (body) both `afastamento` → body now `separação`; "ravished" / "carried off" both `raptou` → Ganymede now `arrebatou`.
- Word-choice fidelity: "drivelling theogony" `desvairada` (deranged) → `disparatada` (the charge is nonsense, not frenzy); "a **herd** of bacchanalian women" `turba` → `rebanho` (the animalizing insult is the punchline); "not lawful to **utter**" `revelar` → `proferir`; "**guest-slaying** Busiris" `o hospedeiro assassino` (reads "murderous innkeeper") → `o matador de hóspedes`; "**successful** labours" `vitoriosos` → `bem-sucedidos`; "beauty of **feature**" `aparência` → `feições`; "Procne… **flitting about**… **shrills**" `voa`/`grita` → `esvoaça de um lado para outro`/`estridula`. Also restored the subordinating causal in "But **since**… who will believe" (`Mas, visto que…`), which had become a bare dash-joined coordinate clause.
- **`Progne` → `Procne`**: the Key Terms row claimed the source reads "Progne" and that *Procne* was non-standard. The en-US file reads **Procne**, and *Procne* is at least as well attested in modern pt-BR references. Row corrected and the rendering normalized.
- **Rejected on verification (the source-blind pass flagging the source's own oddities — exactly the failure mode this corpus's journal already warns about):** the Ch. 3 "sentence fragment" (`Uma vez que, portanto, ó gregos…` never reaching a main clause) is **the English's own fragment**, verbatim; the Semele/Semele circularity (re-raised for the third time) is the source's own ambiguous genitive; the Œdipus "goad", the "leprous shepherd", the dead Amazon, the *Works and Days*-vs-*Theogony* non-sequitur, `fogo eleusino`, and the snake-charmer's "lures… and causes it to flee" are all faithful. `sendo gregos` was re-raised as a number error and re-rejected: Portuguese *vós* requires the plural agreement that English "being a Greek" can dodge.
- **The source-blind pass proposed a direct regression** — `são provados culpados de intemperança` → "`ficam **convictos** de intemperança`" — which is precisely the false friend round 3 removed. **A Portuguese-only pass optimizes for Portuguese idiom and will cheerfully re-introduce a false friend, because the false friend *is* the idiomatic-sounding word. Never apply a source-blind suggestion without re-checking it against the English.**
- Further fixes not covered by the categories above: "kindled a feud" `incitou uma rixa` → `acendeu uma rixa` (*incitar* takes a person or a passion, not a quarrel); "donning" `vestindo` → `trajando`, paired with the `túnica` → `veste nupcial` change; "Jupiter receiving… **the air**" `os ares` → `o ar` (the three allotments — air, deep, Hades — are singular and parallel in the source); "strife, envy" `discórdias, invejas` → `discórdia, inveja`, restoring the source's own singular/plural pattern in the vice list.
- **Round 7 (3 passes, adversarially framed against round 6's own edits) caught a regression round 6 had introduced.** Changing `o meu afastamento` to `a minha separação` left the predicative adjective stranded: `a minha separação … seja irrazoável e **irrefletido**`. *Irrazoável* is common-gender and masked the break; *irrefletido* did not agree with the new feminine head noun. Now `irrefletida`. Two of the three round-7 passes flagged it independently. **Every fix that changes a noun's gender or number silently puts its whole agreement chain back in scope — re-read the clause, not the replaced word.**
- Round 7 also re-raised, and this journal again rejects: the Cronos/Saturno split in Ch. 2 (the source's own, already documented above), `Ceres buscou a sua filha` for "her child" (Portuguese has no comfortable generic here; *criança* would be wrong for a grown Proserpine), and `sendo gregos` (vós agreement, documented in round 1).
- Journal self-consistency: round 1 listed 7 fixes under a "6 genuine defects" header; the final tally said "9 independent passes / 9 defects" where the rounds declare 11 passes and 10 defects; a stale duplicate Key Terms row for *Ægyptus* survived round 3 still describing the superseded bare-`Egito` rendering, and asserted — falsely, and against this journal's own round-2 entry — that the name "is homographic with the country's name in the source language too" (the source spells it *Ægyptus* and never writes *Egypt*). All corrected. **A translation journal drifts out of sync with its own file and needs auditing as a deliverable in its own right, not as a record of one.**

## Review Round 8 — second post-merge round

- 2026-09-16 — 2 independent passes, again re-partitioned (a cold source-blind read; a combined bilingual-fidelity + journal-accuracy audit covering both this journal and the `docs/journal.md` entry). The cold read came back **CLEAN**. The combined pass found 3 further translation defects and 5 journal-accuracy defects.
- **A fix applied to one member of a parallel series and not the others is a defect the fix itself creates.** Round 6 changed `os ares` → `o ar` on the stated ground that the three allotments (air / deep / Hades) are singular and parallel in the source — and then left the middle member plural (`as profundezas`). Now `o abismo`, the standard Portuguese for "the deep". **When the justification for a fix is a pattern, apply the fix to the whole pattern in the same edit, or the journal entry becomes evidence against the file.**
- `velho e **coxo**` → `velho e **de pés tortos**`: "club-footed" names Vulcan's specific deformity; *coxo* generalizes it to "lame." Round 6 considered and rejected this on the ground that *coxo* is the traditional Portuguese epithet for Hephaestus — but that reasoning is the mirror image of the principle round 6 applied to `estábulo`/"Augean building", where the translation was faulted for supplying a myth detail the source withholds. Here the source *supplies* a detail and the translation generalized it. **The same principle has to run in both directions, or it is not a principle.**
- "the love of **woman**" (generic, womankind) `pelo amor de uma mulher` → `pelo amor de mulher`.
- **Journal-accuracy defects, the most serious of which this journal and the dev journal had propagated together:** the round-1 lesson about the *convicto* false friend attributed "convicted himself of lying" to **Chronos**; the source attributes it to **Apollo**, convicting *himself* ("your illustrious son of Latona [Apollo]… convicted himself of lying"). The same lesson claimed the two occurrences of "convicted" were "one sentence apart" / "two sentences later" / "two sentences earlier" in three different places; they are eight sentences apart and in **different chapters** (Ch. 2 and Ch. 3). The lesson is still true — the false friend did survive next to its own correct rendering — but its headline overstated the evidence, and **a lesson stated more strongly than its evidence will be trusted more than it deserves by the next reviewer who reads it.**
- Also corrected in `docs/journal.md`: the stale "9 independent passes / 9 genuine defects" tallies, which round 6 had fixed in *this* file only, leaving the dev journal asserting the very numbers its own newer entry described as corrected; and an off-by-one backlog count (386 works remain under `church-fathers/`, not 387 — 420 books, 34 with a populated `pt-BR/`). **A correction applied to one of two journals that mirror each other is half a correction.**
