## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-27: First pt-BR translation. Single file `ch001.md` (68 chapters plus an appendix of three imperial letters), same heading levels and 1:1 paragraph structure as the en-US file (291 lines both; verified by script, including per-line Scripture reference and bracket counts).
- **Editor footnotes**: the en-US file carries none (the New Advent text has no footnote markers). Nothing dropped. Translator's square brackets in the source ([a reputation for], [the Eucharist], [ἔννοια], etc.) are part of the Dods/Reith text and are kept, translated, in brackets.
- **Scripture references** are inline in the source, running into the sentence without parentheses (e.g. `Isaiah 44:9-20; Jeremiah 10:3.`). Kept inline in the same position, with full pt-BR book names and colon notation (`Isaías 44:9-20`), as sibling Church Fathers pt-BR books do. `Isaiah 1:16, etc.` = `Isaías 1:16 etc.`.
- **Scripture wording**: every quotation (Isaiah, Psalms, Genesis 49, the Gospel sayings) is rendered from the source's wording clause by clause, not pulled toward a Portuguese Bible. Justin's variants are kept: "The Lord has reigned from the tree" (Ch. 41), "a young man is given" (Ch. 35), "Unless you be born again, you shall not enter into the kingdom of heaven" (Ch. 61), the conflated Gospel sayings in Ch. 15–16.
- **Source attributions left as the source has them**: Ch. 35 names "Zephaniah" but cites Zechariah 9:9; Ch. 51 names "Jeremiah" but cites Daniel 7:13. Both are Justin's (or the edition's) attributions and are mirrored: "Sofonias … Zacarias 9:9", "Jeremias … Daniel 7:13".
- **"Esaias" (Ch. 61)**: one-off spelling in the source; Portuguese has one form, rendered "Isaías".
- **Probable transcription errors in the en-US text, rendered by sense (the en-US file is not edited here)**:
  - Ch. 25 "the unbegotten and impossible God": the ANF print reads "impassible"; rendered "impassível".
  - Ch. 46 "But who, through the power of the Word … He was born": ungrammatical; the ANF print reads "But why"; rendered "Mas por que".
  - Ch. 55 "His power and role": the ANF print reads "rule"; rendered "poder e domínio".
  - Ch. 55 "with which all your state possessions are made": kept literally ("com os quais se fazem todas as vossas possessões do Estado"); the Greek means processions, but no reading fixes it, so the source wording stands.
- **Ch. 67 "Sunday"**: rendered "dia do Sol", not "domingo". The chapter ends by explaining the name ("the day after that of Saturn, which is the day of the Sun"); "domingo" would sever that explanation from the name it explains. "Saturday" in parentheses = "(sábado)", as the source glosses.
- **Ch. 4 "*excellent* (Chrestian)"**: the pun is on Greek *chrestós*; rendered "*excelente* (*chrestós*)" so the Portuguese reader sees the word the pun rests on.
- **Ch. 61–67 (baptism, Eucharist, Sunday assembly)** rendered with the closest literalness the Portuguese allows; no smoothing of repetitions ("washed … washing … laver", "prayers and thanksgivings").
- **Quote style**: curly “” as in the source; inner quotes ‘ ’ (Ch. 33). Internally consistent.
- **Watch list from sibling reviews applied**: obligatory crase in `Àquele` (none needed here; `Aquele` without a preceding `a` throughout), `através de` avoided for means (`por meio de`), `cativar`/`convicto` false friends avoided, `a ponto de` (not `ao ponto de`).

## Round 1 Review (2026-09-27)

Mechanical audit (line/paragraph count, headings, book.json, Scripture book names, brackets, markdown) came back clean — no changes needed there. Bilingual clause-by-clause read across 5 chapter-range batches found 5 genuine fidelity defects, all fixed directly in `ch001.md`:

- **Ch. 6** (line 25): "conhecendo-os... declarando-**o**" — the second pronoun broke number agreement with the three-part antecedent (Deus, Filho, Espírito profético) that "conhecendo-os" correctly referred to. Fixed to "declarando-**os**".
- **Ch. 18** (line 75): "would be a godsend to all the wicked" was rendered "seria uma dádiva **dos deuses** para todos os perversos" — "godsend" is an idiom (a lucky break), not a reference to plural pagan gods; the addition contradicted Justin's own argument against real gods. Removed "dos deuses".
- **Ch. 29** (line 119): "lest some of them be not picked up, but die" was rendered "para que algumas delas **não deixem de ser recolhidas** e morram" — the Portuguese idiom "não deixar de" ("be sure to") asserts the opposite of the source (that the children ARE picked up). Fixed to "não sejam recolhidas".
- **Ch. 40** (line 163, Psalm 2 quotation): "lest ... you perish from the right way" was rendered "...não Se ire em algum momento **e pereçais** fora do reto caminho" — the negation carried only the first clause, leaving "e pereçais" as an affirmed outcome instead of the second half of what is to be avoided. Fixed to "e **não** pereçais".
- **Ch. 54** (line 219, Genesis 49:10 quotation): "binding His foal to the vine" was rendered "atando o Seu **jumentinho** à videira" — the chapter's whole argument two sentences later is that the word "foal" is ambiguous (ass or horse), which is why the demons could point to Pegasus as a counterfeit. Translating the quote itself as "jumentinho" ("little donkey") pre-committed to the ass reading and undercut that argument. Fixed to "potro" (matching the neutral term used later in the same paragraph).

**Reviewed and confirmed not a defect**: Ch. 25 "the unbegotten and impossible God" / "Deus ingênito e **impassível**" — flagged by one reviewer as a mismatch, but this is the pre-existing, already-documented decision above ("Probable transcription errors in the en-US text, rendered by sense"): the en-US source's "impossible" is itself a probable transcription error for "impassible" (no printed edition attests "impossible" as sense-bearing here), and per `.claude/rules/books.md` the source is not edited for this class of error while the translation renders it by sense. No change.

## Round 2 Review (2026-09-27)

Different sweeps from Round 1 (negation scope, pronoun/referent commitment points, idiom sense, source-blind Portuguese grammar/crase), run in 7 parallel chapter-range batches (1–10, 11–20, 21–30, 31–40, 41–50, 51–60, 61–68+appendix). Found and fixed 3 genuine defects, all confirmed against the English before and after:

- **Ch. 37** (line 151): "Que espécie de coisas são ensinadas por meio dos profetas **da parte de** [da pessoa de] Deus" — the bare preposition "de" run into the contraction "da" ("de da") is not grammatical Portuguese; not present in the English ("from [the person of] God"). Fixed to "**da parte** [da pessoa de] Deus".
- **Ch. 61** (line 249): "aquele que conduz ao lavacro a pessoa que há de ser lavada chamando-**O**" — capitalized divine pronoun wrongly made the referent of "calling him" God; the English ("calling **him** by this name alone") is the baptismal candidate being named, not God, who is invoked but not addressed here. Fixed to lowercase "chamando-**o**", consistent with this file's own convention (capital only for divine referents, e.g. line 257 "-Lhe" for Christ vs. "lhe" for Moses).
- **Appendix, Marcus Aurelius's letter** (line 291): "perdoemos aos que são cristãos, para que não peçam **e obtenham** tal arma" — same class of defect as the Round 1 Ch. 40 fix: English "lest they pray for **and obtain** such a weapon" puts both verbs under the negation, but only the first was negated, leaving "obtenham" as an affirmed (backwards) outcome. Fixed to "não peçam **nem** obtenham".
- **Appendix, Antoninus's letter** (line 287): "sendo pródigos de suas vidas, **antes que** prestar a obediência" — "antes que" requires a finite clause, not a bare infinitive; ungrammatical as written. Fixed to "**antes de** prestar" (sense: "rather than yield that obedience").

**Reviewed and confirmed not a defect** (recorded so later rounds don't re-raise them):

- Ch. 9 (line 37): "their own girls who work along with them" / "as próprias moças" and Ch. 4 (line 17) "his own children" / "os próprios filhos" — possessive left implicit via "próprio(a)" rather than an explicit "seus/suas"; unambiguous from context in both cases, not a referent error.
- Ch. 14 (line 57): "reasoning sophistically" / "raciocinamos sofisticamente" — correct formal cognate for "using sophistry" in this register, not the modern false-friend sense of "sofisticado" (elaborate/refined).
- Ch. 14 (line 57) and Ch. 18 (line 75): constructions that resemble the "não deixar de" idiom-reversal trap from Round 1 ("não descuidar de vos convencer", double negatives with "enganem"/"desviem" both kept in subjunctive) — checked clause by clause; both correctly preserve the source's negation, unlike the Round 1 case.
- Ch. 21 (line 87), Ch. 28 (line 115): a single entity referred to by two or three names (demons/Satan/the devil; Rhadamanthus and Minos as one judgment) followed by singular pronouns — correct; not a number-agreement defect against a compound antecedent.
- Ch. 51 (line 223): "you name them gods" / "os chamais deuses" — English "them" is structurally ambiguous between images and emperors; the Portuguese masculine "os" resolves toward "imperadores," a defensible reading of the surrounding clause, not a mismatch.
- Ch. 32 (line 131): "binding His foal to the vine" / "atando o Seu jumentinho à videira" — this is the Genesis 49:10 quotation itself (distinct from the Ch. 54 quotation of the same verse, already fixed to "potro" in Round 1 for its ambiguity argument); no such argument depends on this instance, and the narrative sentences shortly after already disambiguate with "potro de uma jumenta." Left as is.
- Ch. 65 (line 265): "todo o povo... exprime o seu assentimento" for "their assent" — singular collective noun "povo" correctly takes a singular possessive.
- Appendix (line 291): "para que **fôssemos** livrados" for "that **they** might be delivered" (the whole army) — deliberate 1st-person-plural collapse since the emperor narrates as part of "the whole army," consistent with the following sentence ("we had got no water"); not a referent slip.

## Round 3 Review (2026-09-27)

Sweeps not yet run in Rounds 1–2: a script-assisted term-frequency diff of every Key Terms row (both directions, per chapter), a capitalization audit of every divine pronoun/title (Ele/ele, O/o, Verbo, Filho, Pai, Espírito, Senhor) against its referent, a false-friend sweep (convict, sensible, actual, pretend, realize, assume, intend, argument, sympathy, notice, prejudice, apology), and a grep-verification of every falsifiable claim already recorded above. No translation defects found; two documentation corrections made:

- **Line count**: the Translation Decisions note claimed "292 lines both"; `wc -l` gives 291 for both `en-US/ch001.md` and `pt-BR/ch001.md`. Corrected above. (Chapter-heading count 68/68, appendix subheading count 3/3, bracket count 23/23, and Scripture-reference count 60/60 were all re-verified and are accurate.)
- **Key Terms row for "the Word/Logos"**: added a note that Ch. 21's "the Word, who is the **first-birth** of God" — a one-off phrasing distinct from the source's usual "first-born"/"first-begotten" — is rendered with the same "primogênito" as those, since Portuguese has no natural third form; not a missed distinction, just previously undocumented.

**Term-frequency diff findings**: demônio(s)/diabo(s) counts match the source's demon(s)/devil(s) counts in every chapter (word-boundary-checked, ruling out a "demonstrate" false-positive). ingênito/unbegotten (5/5), primogênito/first-born+first-begotten+first-birth (7/6, explained above), regenerad*/regenerat* (3/3), iluminaç*/illuminat* (4/4), and ateu*/atheis* (10/12, the gap being the adjective form "ateia(s)" for "atheistic"/"atheistical," which a narrower regex missed but which is present and correctly rendered) all check out.

**Capitalization audit**: every lowercase "ele" (31 instances), every capitalized enclitic "-O"/"-Lo" (2), every lowercase enclitic "-lo"/"-la" (16), and every instance of Pai/Filho/Espírito/Senhor was checked against its referent. All correctly cased — human, pagan, demonic, and animal/object referents lowercase throughout; God, Christ, and the Holy/prophetic Spirit capitalized throughout, including the deliberately source-mirrored mixed case in the Ch. 40 Psalm 19 quotation ("pôs **Ele** o Seu tabernáculo, e **ele**, como um esposo..." — "He"/"he" the sun, personified, matches the source's own capitalization).

**False-friend sweep**: no instances of convicto (0 occurrences, consistent with the existing rule), atual for "actual" (rendered "efetivamente"/"efetiva" correctly), pretender for "pretended" (Ch. 54 uses "fingiram," correctly), notícia for "notice" (rendered "atenção"/"despercebido" correctly), or prejuízo for "prejudice" (rendered "preconceito" correctly, both occurrences). "Realizar" (Ch. 26, 30) glosses English "did"/"performed," not a false-friend borrowing of "realize." No occurrences of "argument" or "sympathy" in the source to check.
