# Translation Journal — Theophilus to Autolycus (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (Marcus Dods translation, Ante-Nicene Fathers Vol. 2, via New Advent). No Greek directory exists; the en-US is the only source.
Target: pt-BR

## Conventions

No other Theophilus work has a pt-BR translation. Conventions follow `athenagoras/plea-for-the-christians/pt-BR`, `athanasius/incarnation-of-the-word/pt-BR` and `ambrose/repentance/pt-BR`:
- Author "Teófilo" without "Santo", after Athanasius ("Atanásio") and Athenagoras ("Atenágoras"). Title "Teófilo a Autólico", the traditional Portuguese title of *Ad Autolycum*. TOC "Livro I/II/III", as in Ambrose's *Sobre a Penitência*.
- Headings "Capítulo N." with the ANF editor's titles translated, sentence case.
- Scripture book names in full Portuguese; the source's chapter:verse kept, inline where the source places them.
- Curly quotes “ ”; English `:—` before quotations becomes `:`.
- Pronouns for God lowercase (ele, dele, o); titles capitalized as in the source (Verbo, Médico, Imortal, Providência).
- `tu` for Autolycus throughout, including "your gods / poets / writers / practices", which Theophilus aims at him; `vós` only where the source addresses a plural (the Sibyl's oracles, Scripture commands to the people).
- Verse (Sibyl, Hesiod, Aratus, tragedians, Ariston) rendered line for line from Dods's English, without rhyme or meter; line counts and stanza breaks mirrored, including the source's misplaced opening quote marks (Book II ch. 36 second oracle, ch. 37 Aeschylus).

Biblical names follow the Catholic (Vulgate-based) Portuguese forms: Henoc, Lamec, Melquisedec, Codorlaomor, Senaar, Nemrod, Abimelec, Nabucodonosor, Teglatfalasar, Salmanasar, Senaquerib. Exceptions, which use the common modern forms as sibling church-fathers works do: the Gn 2 rivers and land "Pisom", "Giom", "Havilá" (Bk II ch. 20, 24; "Giom" as in Cyril's *Catechetical Lectures*) and the judge "Eli" (Bk III ch. 24, as in Chrysostom's *On the Priesthood*), not Fison, Geon, Hevilat, Heli. Source variants that differ from the Bible are kept as variants: "Mathusala" (Bk II ch. 30) → Matusala vs "Methuselah" (Bk III ch. 24) → Matusalém; "Nebroth" → Nebrot; "Gothonoel", "Chusarathon", "Samera" transliterated.

## Translation Decisions

- Editor footnotes: none in the New Advent import; none added.
- Scripture is translated from Dods's wording (Septuagint-based), not from a Portuguese Bible: e.g. Gn 1-3 (Bk II ch. 11, 20, 21), the Decalogue (Bk III ch. 9), Is 58:6 ("rasgai todo título injusto").
- Bk I ch. 3 "if I call Him Word" → "se o chamo Verbo".
- Bk I ch. 7 "He will couch the eyes" (cataract surgery) → "operará os olhos".
- Bk II ch. 7 Satyrus's genealogy: names in the usual Portuguese forms (Hilo, Têmeno, Pérdicas, Ptolomeu Evérgeta…); family adjectives rendered as "a alteia, a téstia, a toantia, a estafília, a euênia, a marônia".
- Bk III ch. 19 "Come there, God calls you to repentance" (the source's Deucalion etymology, δεῦτε καλεῖ) → "Vinde, Deus vos chama à penitência"; no note added.
- Bk III ch. 27 "220 A.U.C." kept; "Superbus" kept in Latin where the source explains the word.

## Source slips mirrored (edition errors, not corrected)

- Bk II ch. 31 "another son of Shem … Mizraim" (Mizraim is a son of Ham, Gn 10:6).
- Bk III ch. 25 "Josiah, 40 years" after Athaliah (the king is Joash); Josiah appears again later.
- Bk III ch. 26 "15 times 10,375 years" vs ch. 16 "153,075 years".
- Bk III ch. 27 "Nero, 13 years 6 months 58 days".

## Source edits (en-US, OCR-class)

- Bk I ch. 13 "has passed though so great a heat" → "through"; "grew, Or whence" → "or".
- Bk II ch. 10 "[ἁρκή]" → "[ἀρχή]" (garbled Greek; the gloss is on "governing principle").
- Bk II ch. 32 "may the loves of learning" → "lovers".
- Bk III ch. 5 "who will riot eat" → "not".
- Bk III ch. 9 "afterwards was called judæa" → "Judæa".
- Bk III ch. 24 "when 132 And" → "when 132. And"; "Jesus the sun of Nun" → "son".
- Bk III ch. 27 "from the rounding of Rome" → "founding".

## Review log

### Round 1

Focus: completeness, paragraph by paragraph (blocks aligned one to one, with every quotation, parenthetical, bracket, Greek word, Scripture reference and number checked), plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: clean. No changes.

Rejected findings:
- Bk III ch. 18–19 "chest" (Deucalion) and "ark" (Noah) are both "arca". Portuguese "arca" also means chest and is the usual word for Deucalion's λάρναξ. The source contrasts the two men, not the two vessels.
- Bk II ch. 34 "se acham ser nomes de homens" and Bk III ch. 23 "se acham todos ter legislado" ("are found to be / to have legislated"). This is a stiff Latinate infinitive construction, but it is grammatical.
- Bk II ch. 4 "coeval" → "coeterna" against Bk II ch. 10 "coevo". Each fits its context: in ch. 4, matter that is uncreated, and so as old as God, is eternal.
- Bk II ch. 13 "At the first God created" → "No princípio". This follows the Key Terms row tying ἀρχή to "princípio" and to Gn 1:1.
- Bk III ch. 24 "3,938" → "3938". Number formatting only; the file writes all four-digit totals this way.

### Round 2

Focus: clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of all three books (negation, subject and object, tense and mood, qualifiers, Scripture wording), plus the mechanical audit and a check of the journal's claims against the text. Verdict: 1 defect, in the journal; no change to the chapter files.

Fixed:
- Key Terms, "repentance / repent": the row gave the verb as "arrepender-se", but Bk III ch. 25 "not repenting" reads "não fazendo penitência". The rendering is sound and echoes "com a penitência do povo" in ch. 24, so the row now records it instead of the text being changed.

Rejected findings:
- Bk II ch. 11, "And the evening and the morning were the first day" (and the other five days) → "E houve tarde e manhã: o primeiro dia". This is close to the familiar Bible wording, but it says the same thing as the source, and Theophilus does not argue from the wording of this formula.
- Bk II ch. 13, "by means of the 'first' principle the heavens were made" → "por meio do 'princípio'". The quoted word repeats the opening of the verse just quoted. Since "At the first" is "No princípio" (Round 1), the quoted word in Portuguese is "princípio", and "primeiro" would no longer point back to the verse.
- Bk II ch. 21, "it shall bruise your head, and you shall bruise his heel" → "ela te ferirá a cabeça, e tu lhe ferirás o calcanhar". "Ela" agrees with "descendência", the Portuguese word for "seed", and "lhe" is as neutral as the source's "his".
- Bk II ch. 8 heading, "Opinions Concerning Providence" → "acerca da providência". Headings use sentence case under the Conventions; inside the text, capitalization of "Providence" still follows the source.
- Bk III ch. 10 and 12, the Scripture quotations move between singular and plural address ("Não afligirás … conheceis"; "Soltai … Reparte o teu pão"). Dods's "you" does not mark number, so the source cannot contradict either choice. The Hebrew of the Exodus verse also moves from singular to plural, and the plural of Is 58:6 is the recorded decision "rasgai todo título injusto".

Evidence: `ember-translation-evidence/church-fathers__theophilus__theophilus-to-autolycus/review-2.md` (outside the repo).

### Round 3

Focus: a source-blind cold read of all three books, with each mark then checked against the source; a spelling-only sweep (diacritics, crase, post-1990 hyphenation, punctuation, quote pairing); and the mechanical audit. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed.

Fixed:
- Bk III ch. 7, Euhemerus: "quis negar absolutamente a sua existência e que todas as coisas fossem governadas por uma ação que se regula a si mesma" → "… e sustentar que todas as coisas são governadas …". The source says "would absolutely deny their existence, and have all things to be governed by self-regulated action". In the old wording, "e que …" read as a second object of "negar", so Euhemerus denied the self-regulation that he in fact asserts.

Rejected findings:
- Bk I ch. 13, "e a semente, …, agora lançando raiz, cresceu uma árvore". This mirrors the source's anacoluthon ("and the seed, …, now striking root, a tree has grown up"), and "uma árvore" is the postposed subject of "cresceu".
- Bk III ch. 7, Protagoras: "Pois se os deuses existem, não sou capaz de afirmar acerca deles". The source reads "For whether the gods exist, I am not able to affirm concerning them". A fronted "se" clause is a standard Portuguese indirect question ("whether"), and it has the same shape as the English.

Evidence: `ember-translation-evidence/church-fathers__theophilus__theophilus-to-autolycus/review-3.md` (outside the repo).

### Round 4

Focus: function words (prepositions, articles, demonstratives, connectors, possessive "seu/sua", "consigo"), every heading, and every place where the Portuguese must commit and the source is open (antecedents, gender, tu/vós traced turn by turn in each speech, poem and apostrophe), plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 2 defects, fixed.

Fixed:
- Bk I ch. 6: "às quais a multiforme sabedoria de Deus chamou por nomes próprios" → "a todas as quais …". The source says "all of which", after the Pleiades, Orion, Arcturus and the other stars. Without "todas", the feminine relative attaches to the nearest noun, "outras estrelas", and the naming no longer covers the whole list.
- Bk III ch. 14 (Is 66:5): "e se manifeste na sua alegria" → "na alegria deles". The source reads "be apparent in their joy", the joy of "those that hate you". Coming after "o nome do Senhor", "sua" reads as the Lord's joy.

Rejected findings:
- Bk I ch. 11, "as He will not have those called kings whom He has appointed under Himself" → "assim como ele não quer … abaixo de si". The sense may be about the earthly king, but Dods's capital "He" makes it God, and the canonical source governs.
- Bk I ch. 11, "honrarei o rei [do que aos teus deuses]". The prepositional direct object is grammatical. It also keeps "teus deuses" from being read as the subject of the comparison.
- Bk II ch. 1 heading, "Occasion of Writing This Book" → "Ocasião em que se escreveu este livro". "Ocasião" covers both circumstance and moment, as "occasion" does, and the chapter tells the circumstance.
- Bk II ch. 6 (Hesiod), "when by heaven her love was sought" → "quando o céu buscou o seu amor". Even with "seu" read as heaven's, "sought his love" means "sought his beloved", the earth, which is the same event.
- Bk II ch. 14, "For they are not guided by the word of truth" → "não são guiadas". The feminine follows the subject of the preceding clause, "doutrinas de erro / heresias".
- Bk II ch. 31, "God destroyed their city, and overthrew their tower" → "Deus destruiu a sua cidade e derrubou a sua torre". The city and tower belong to the builders named in the same sentence, so "sua" cannot be read as God's.
- Bk II ch. 32, "Since then the occupation of the world … was at first in three divisions" → "Visto, pois, que". The English can be read as causal or temporal, and neither reading changes the facts stated.
- Bk II ch. 37 (Aeschylus), "'Tis with you … 'Twill follow you" → "Ela está contigo … Ela te seguirá". "Ela" is the justice of the opening lines ("its stern hand"). It cannot be the masculine "destino".

Evidence: `ember-translation-evidence/church-fathers__theophilus__theophilus-to-autolycus/review-4.md` (outside the repo).

### Round 5

Focus: terms, journal and names. A per-paragraph term-frequency diff against every Key Terms row, in both directions; a grep of every falsifiable claim in the journal; every proper name checked against the standard Portuguese form and against sibling pt-BR works in `content/books/church-fathers/`; plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 1 defect, in the journal; no change to the chapter files.

Fixed:
- Names note under Key Terms: it said biblical names follow the Vulgate-based Catholic forms, but Bk II ch. 20 and 24 have "Pisom", "Giom", "Havilá" and Bk III ch. 24 has "Eli". These forms match the siblings ("Giom" in Cyril's *Catechetical Lectures*, "Eli" in Chrysostom's *On the Priesthood*). The text stays, and the note now lists them as exceptions, so a later round does not "correct" them to Fison, Geon, Hevilat, Heli.

Rejected findings:
- Bk III ch. 20, 21, 23 "Manetho" → "Maneton", while Tertullian's *Apology* has "Mâneton". "Maneton" is a current Brazilian form of the name, and the book uses it consistently. Two standard forms in two books is not an objective error.
- Key Terms, "type / pattern (Bk II ch. 15-17)": "pattern" also occurs in ch. 14 ("como modelo da ressurreição"), rendered as the row says. The parenthesis gives a location, not a full list, so it is not false.
- "princípio" also translates "beginning" / "at first" ("No princípio", "a princípio"), not only "governing principle". The row's own note ties ἀρχή to "No princípio" of Gn 1:1, and Bk II ch. 13 argues from that link.
- Bk II ch. 8 heading "Providence" → "providência": already rejected in Round 2 (headings use sentence case).
- Greek words in Bk I ch. 4 and Bk II ch. 12, 17, 28 use tonos where en-US uses oxia (for example U+03AC against U+1F71). The two are canonically equivalent under Unicode normalization and display the same.

### Round 6

Focus: completeness, paragraph by paragraph. Every block of the three books was aligned one to one with the source (29, 147 and 66 blocks) and checked for line, number, Greek, bracket and parenthesis counts, then read in full side by side. All 44 Scripture references were checked for the Portuguese book name and the chapter:verse, and the mechanical audit was run. Verdict: clean. No changes.

Rejected findings:
- Bk II ch. 8 (Euripides), "Apart from God, there's nothing owned by men" → "À parte de Deus, nada há que os homens possuam". "À parte de" ("apart from, except") is grammatical and keeps the line's meaning. Whether to use "Sem Deus" instead is a matter of style.
- Bk I ch. 3, "if I say He is Light, I name but His own work" → "nomeio apenas a sua obra". "Sua" already marks the work as God's, so leaving out "own" drops no content.
- Bk II ch. 7, "from Zeus the Dii and Diogenæ" → "os Dios e os Diógenes". "Diógenes" is the Portuguese form of Διογενεῖς, and the plural is the same as the singular. The family-name sense comes from the article and the list around it.

### Round 7

Focus: clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of all three books (negation, subject and object, tense and mood, false friends, qualifiers, Scripture wording, every number in the Bk III chronologies), plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: clean. No changes.

Rejected findings:
- Bk I ch. 8, "even when you hold so many pledges at His hand" → "mesmo tendo recebido de sua mão tantos penhores". To hold pledges from God's hand and to have received them describe the same state, so no content is lost.
- Bk II ch. 10, "power of the highest" → "poder do Altíssimo". This is reverential capitalization of a divine title, which `.claude/rules/books.md` says not to normalize.
- Bk II ch. 35 (Is 40:28), "there is no searching of His understanding" → "o seu entendimento é insondável". This is close to the familiar Bible wording, but it says the same thing as the source, and Theophilus does not argue from this clause.
- Bk III ch. 2, "What did Plato's system of culture profit him?" → "Que proveito lhe trouxe a Platão o seu sistema de cultura?". The doubled indirect object ("lhe … a Platão") is a pleonasm, but it is grammatical Portuguese.
