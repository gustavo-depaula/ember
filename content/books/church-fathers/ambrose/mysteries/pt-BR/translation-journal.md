# Translation Journal — On the Mysteries (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (trans. H. de Romestin, Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, Vol. 10; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

| English | Portuguese | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| the Mysteries | os Mistérios | capitalized throughout, as in the source |
| Sacrament(s) | Sacramento(s) | capitalized when referring to Baptism/Eucharist as instituted rites |
| the laver / the font | a fonte batismal / a fonte | "laver" (regeneration) rendered as "fonte" rather than the more literal "lavatório," matching how the app's other patristic/liturgical translations render baptismal imagery |
| regeneration | regeneração | |
| catechumen | catecúmeno | |
| chief priest [bishop] | sumo sacerdote [bispo] | editorial bracket from the source kept as-is |
| Godhead | Divindade | |
| the Trinity | a Trindade | |
| consecration | consagração | |
| type / figure / likeness | tipo / figura / semelhança | kept distinct — the three typological terms are never collapsed into one another; ¶25 argues explicitly from the difference |
| shadow / truth | sombra / verdade | Pauline shadow-vs-truth contrast (Col 2:17 register) |
| cleanse / heal | purificar / curar | kept distinct throughout the Naaman passage (¶16-19) and the pool of Bethesda (¶22-24): Naaman is *cleansed* of leprosy, the paralytic is *healed*. "purificar" also carries "purify" (¶34, ¶43) |
| laver / font | fonte batismal / fonte | never "lavagem" — in ¶14 the laver is one of two vessels ("the laver and the cup of salvation"), so an action noun breaks the pair |
| black and comely | negra e formosa | Song 1:4/1:5, Vulgate *nigra sum sed formosa*, Figueiredo/Ave Maria "negra". Not "morena": ¶35 turns on the black↔white antithesis ("how is she now suddenly made white?") |
| meat and drink | alimento e bebida | Song 5:1 as Ambrose quotes it (*cibum meum* / *potum meum*), not the Vulgate's favum/vinum — ¶57 glosses these two words directly, and ¶58 echoes them in "alimento espiritual e bebida espiritual" |
| spices | especiarias | distinct from "ointments"/"odour" → perfume, which is used for both in ¶55-56 |
| food / bread | alimento / pão | never "pão" for "food". ¶47 runs "angels' food → that food → that food which you receive" and only then pivots to "that living **Bread**"; naming the manna "pão" first spends the contrast before it is drawn, and ¶49's "the bread of angels" is where the source itself says bread. Same reach for the bread register that produced "o pão da vida" for "the body of life" in ¶49 |
| the Lord Almighty | o Senhor Todo-Poderoso | Mal 2:7 as the source titles it, not the Portuguese Bible's "Senhor dos exércitos" (Vulgate *Domini exercituum*) — a divine title is not a proper name, so it follows the source rather than being localized |
| mind / soul / spirit | mente / alma / espírito | three distinct words, never merged. ¶11's "tranquillity of mind" must not be "do espírito": the Holy Spirit is the subject of that very sentence, so "espírito" there reads as the Spirit just named. ¶15 pairs "mente e espírito" for "mind and spirit". Idioms excepted: "call to mind" (¶21) is "recordar" |
| quoted Scripture | the source's wording, word for word | the standing rule, and the one this chapter keeps breaking: a quotation renders the wording of *this* English edition even where the Vulgate, Figueiredo or the Ave Maria says something else. Six defects across passes 4-7 were this and nothing else — "Senhor dos exércitos" for "Lord Almighty", an imported "precioso", "favo/vinho" for "meat/drink", "ninguém" for "they", an added "como" turning a metaphor into a simile, a dropped "redness". Check every quotation in both directions: a word in the English with no counterpart in the Portuguese, *and* a word in the Portuguese with no counterpart in the English |
| address to Christ | tu | Ambrose addresses the *reader* as vós throughout, but when he turns to address Christ directly (¶29) he must use tu, because the Song of Songs quotations he is weaving into his own sentences are tu throughout, and ¶29's second "Your" deliberately echoes the first ("the odour of Your garments" → "the odour of Your resurrection"). Mixing the two families across one address breaks the echo |
| Real Presence | Presença Real | used once, in the introduction |

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-02: This is the first pt-BR translation of a work by St. Ambrose in the corpus, so no prior Ambrose glossary existed to draw on. Terms were chosen for consistency with existing pt-BR patristic translations already in `content/books/church-fathers/` (e.g. Gregory Thaumaturgus) and with `augustine-confessions/pt-BR` for shared vocabulary (Trindade, Divindade, regeneração, etc.).
- 2026-09-02: All Scripture references preserved with the same book/chapter/verse numbers as the English source, with only the book name localized to standard Portuguese Catholic usage (e.g. "Mark 7:34" → "Marcos 7:34", "1 Corinthians 5:18" → "1 Coríntios 5:18", "Song of Songs" → "Cântico dos Cânticos"). No reference numbers were corrected or altered, even where the source's citation looks slightly off (e.g. the "things seen are temporal" quote cited as 1 Corinthians 5:18 in ¶8), since fidelity to the source's apparatus — not correction of it — is the goal.
- 2026-09-02: The source itself skips from paragraph 47 to two consecutive paragraphs numbered "49" (no "48" exists, and "49" repeats) — a known quirk of this edition's numbering, not a transcription error introduced by this translation. Preserved exactly as in the source: **47** → **49** → **49** → **50**. Flagging here so a translation review doesn't mistake this for a dropped/duplicated paragraph.
- 2026-09-02: No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source (the unsigned introductory paragraphs are the translator/editor's synopsis, not footnotes, and were translated as part of the text, matching the source's own structure). No translator notes were needed.
- 2026-09-02: Second-person plural verb forms ("vós," -stes/-eis conjugations) used throughout for Ambrose's direct address to the newly baptized, matching the formal, homiletic register of the Portuguese used elsewhere in the corpus for patristic sermons/catecheses (e.g. Augustine's `catechising-of-the-uninstructed`-style address).
- 2026-09-02: "the pool" (John 5, Bethesda) rendered as "o tanque," matching standard Portuguese Gospel translations (João 5:2-4), rather than a more literal "a piscina."
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 2): Paragraph numbers are written `**N.**` (bold text), not `N.` (a Markdown ordered-list marker), in **both** languages. The en-US chapter originally used `N.`, which `marked` renders as `<ol start="47">` — the literal numbers are thrown away and the browser renumbers sequentially, so the source's 47 → 49 → 49 displayed as 47 → 48 → 49. That erased exactly the numbering quirk this journal set out to preserve, and labelled a paragraph 48 when no ¶48 exists. en-US was converted to `**N.**` to match this translation and to render the numbers the edition actually prints. No wording changed in either language.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 3): Corrected 3 defects of the same kind — a word the argument leans on rendered so that the link it carries disappears. ¶9 the Psalm proof-text ("and all their strength by the spirit of His mouth") had been rendered "todo o seu exército pelo **sopro** da sua boca," following modern Brazilian psalters. But the whole paragraph is an argument *from that word*: Genesis says the Spirit moved, David says the heavens were made "by the spirit of His mouth," therefore the Spirit was working. With "sopro" the quoted verse no longer names the Spirit and the inference in the next sentence has nothing to rest on; restored to "pelo **espírito** da sua boca," matching Figueiredo (the Portuguese counterpart of the Douay text this edition translates), and "exército" (host — from Almeida's Hebrew-based reading) to "poder," which is what "strength"/*omnis virtus eorum* says. ¶24 "was a **likeness** of this dove" had been rendered "uma **figura** desta pomba," collapsing the glossary's type/figure/likeness distinction — and ¶25 opens by objecting to precisely this sentence ("como é que dizemos que ali estava a **semelhança** e aqui a realidade"), so the reader looked back for "semelhança" and found a different word. Now "uma semelhança desta pomba"; likeness → semelhança is 11/11 across the chapter. ¶38 "the pleasing comparison to **those that are shorn**" had been rendered "com as **ovelhas** tosquiadas," a noun the source does not supply, which then contradicted the very next clause ("pois sabemos que **as cabras**…"). The source is deliberately vague there because the Vulgate's Song 4:1-2 has goats; restored to "com as que são tosquiadas."
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 4): Corrected 8 defects, all of the "a word carries a link and the rendering drops it" kind. ¶57 Song 5:1 was rendered from the Vulgate ("comi o meu **favo** com o meu mel, bebi o meu **vinho** com o meu leite") while the very next sentence glosses it — "por que ele falou de **alimento** e de **bebida**" — so the two words being explained were absent from the quotation they explain. The en-US source reads "My meat"/"My drink" precisely because Ambrose quotes *cibum meum* / *potum meum* in order to comment on them, and ¶58 then echoes the pair in "alimento espiritual e bebida espiritual." Restored to "comi o meu alimento com o meu mel, bebi a minha bebida com o meu leite." ¶35 "black and comely" was "morena," but the chapter-7 synopsis already said "negra e formosa," and the paragraph's whole point is the black↔white antithesis ("Ela era morena — como se tornou agora, de repente, branca?"), which "morena" (brown/tan) does not carry; changed all 3 occurrences to "negra," matching the synopsis and the Vulgate *nigra sum sed formosa*. ¶16-17 "cleansed" was rendered "curar/curado" in three places, colliding with "healed" → "curado" in ¶18 and contradicting the same word rendered "purificado" in the identical construction in ¶19; cleanse → purificar and heal → curar are now kept apart throughout. ¶14 "the spiritual laver" was "a lavagem espiritual," an action noun where the sentence pairs two vessels ("the laver and the cup of salvation") and where the glossary says fonte; now "a fonte espiritual." ¶37 rendered "a dove's"/"a dove" in the plural ("os das pombas," "das quais") and then reverted to the singular in the same sentence ("à semelhança de uma pomba"); the source is singular in all four places, tying the eyes back to the one dove of the Jordan, so the paragraph is now singular throughout. ¶29 the Psalm quotation had gained "precioso" ("Como o óleo **precioso** sobre a cabeça") from modern Brazilian psalters — a word the source does not have; removed. ¶57 "spices" was "perfumes," which is also what "ointments" is called in the adjacent ¶56 and what "odour" is called twice in ¶55; changed to "especiarias," matching the same English word in ¶55. ¶40 "my Brother" (Christ) was lowercase where the same phrase is capitalized in ¶56 and in the source; capitalized.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 5): Corrected 3 defects, again all of the "a word carries a link and the rendering drops it" kind, and again all found by the term-frequency diff only after the terms were added to the glossary above. ¶47 "So man ate angels' **food**" had been rendered "o homem comeu o **pão** dos anjos," from the Portuguese psalter's Ps 77:25. The paragraph is built on holding one word steady and then swapping it: angels' *food* → all who ate that *food* died → that *food* which you receive → "este **Pão** vivo que desceu do céu." Calling the manna "pão" in the first clause spends the contrast before Ambrose draws it, and leaves "daquele alimento" two clauses later pointing back at a phrase that no longer contains the word. It also flattens the source's own distinction between ¶47 ("food") and ¶49 ("the bread of angels," correctly "pão"). Restored to "o alimento dos anjos." This is the third time the bread register has pulled a rendering off the source in this chapter — cf. "o pão da vida" for "the body of life" in the entry below. ¶11 "peace of soul and tranquillity of **mind**" was "a tranquilidade do **espírito**," in a sentence whose subject is the Holy Spirit ("desceu o Espírito Santo... e que inspira em vós"), so the word read as the Spirit just named rather than as the faculty; "mind" is "mente" in ¶15's "mente e espírito." Now "a tranquilidade da mente." ¶6 Malachi 2:7 "the angel of the **Lord Almighty**" had been rendered "o anjo do Senhor **dos exércitos**," the standard Portuguese wording (and the Vulgate's *Domini exercituum*), but a different divine title from the one the source gives; restored to "o Senhor Todo-Poderoso," on the same principle as ¶29's "precioso" — the quotation follows the edition being translated, not the target-language Bible.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 5, verified clean): Structure re-checked mechanically and unchanged — 59 paragraph markers in identical sequence (including the 47 → 49 → 49 quirk), 11 headings mapping 1:1, all 57 chapter:verse citations identical, NFC-normalized, no ordered-list markers, per-paragraph word-count ratios all within 0.80-1.10. cleanse/heal now separate 12/12 and 12/12 across the chapter with zero crossovers; likeness, type, figure, shadow, dove, Godhead, spices, black and grace all align exactly.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 6): Corrected 3 defects, none of them a word choice — the term-frequency diff and the glossary, which found every defect of passes 3-5, found nothing this time. All three were turned up by checks that count something *other* than vocabulary. **Introduction** "but in what year cannot be fixed" had been rendered "mas o ano não pode ser fixado **com precisão**." The added qualifier is not in the source and it weakens the claim: the source says the year cannot be established at all, the translation says it cannot be established *precisely*, which concedes that it roughly can. Removed. **¶28** the source's one parenthetical editorial gloss, "You went down, then **(into the water)**," had been absorbed into the sentence unmarked ("Descestes, pois, à água"). The content was never missing, but the reader could no longer tell the editor's clarification from Ambrose's own words — while the chapter's three bracketed insertions (`[i.e. the bishop]`, `[of the ark]`, `[of old]`) are all marked in this translation. Restored the marker: "Descestes, pois (à água)". **¶29** "How many souls... have loved **You**, Lord Jesus" was "**vos** amaram, Senhor Jesus," while the rest of the same sentence addresses Christ with tu ("Atrai-nos após **ti**... o perfume das **tuas** vestes... o perfume da **tua** ressurreição"). Reverential vós for Christ is idiomatic on its own, but not two clauses away from tu for the same addressee — and the tu is not negotiable, since it comes from the Song of Songs quotation Ambrose is weaving in, and the sentence's closing "the odour of Your resurrection" is built to echo the quotation's "the odour of Your garments." Changed to "te amaram, Senhor Jesus," so the echo survives.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 7): Corrected 5 further defects, every one of them the same class — **the target-language Bible's wording leaking into a quotation** — which is the class this chapter has now produced eleven times. A pass over all 70 quotations, comparing each in *both* directions (a word in the English with no counterpart in the Portuguese, and a word in the Portuguese with no counterpart in the English), is what found them; reading for sense does not, because every one of these reads perfectly well. **¶29** "Your Name **is** ointment poured out" had become "O teu nome é **como** óleo derramado" — an added "como" turning Solomon's metaphor into a simile, in a paragraph that sets it directly against David's actual simile two sentences earlier ("**Like** the ointment upon the head") and then identifies the two ("This **is** the ointment of which Solomon, too, says"). Removed. **¶36** "the **redness** of His garments is from Bosor" had become "com as vestes **tintas** de Bosra" — "dyed," with the colour word gone, in the one paragraph whose argument is a colour (red garments against "glorious in white apparel," following ¶35's black↔white). Restored to "a vermelhidão das suas vestes vem de Bosra." **¶40** "and indeed **they** will not despise me" had become "e **ninguém** me desprezaria por isso" — the standard Portuguese Song 8:1, with the source's third-person plural subject replaced, "indeed" unrendered and "por isso" added. Now "e, na verdade, eles não me desprezariam." **¶43** "You have prepared in my sight a table **against** them that trouble me" had become "Preparaste diante de mim uma mesa, **diante** dos que me perseguem" — one Portuguese preposition doing the work of two different English ones in a single sentence, and reversing the second: a table set *against* one's enemies became a table merely *in front of* them. Restored to "contra os que me atribulam," which is also Figueiredo's wording. **¶55** "A garden enclosed **is my sister**, my spouse" had become "Jardim fechado **és tu**, minha irmã" — third person turned into second, in the one clause of that quotation where the source leaves the second person.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 7, considered and not changed): "já embranquecida" in ¶35 for "comes up made white" — "já" has no counterpart word in the source, but it carries the resultative force English gets from "made"; without it the participle reads as a standing attribute rather than the change of state the next sentence questions ("Ela era negra — como se tornou agora, de repente, branca?"). "selo" twice in ¶41 for the source's "as a **seal** upon your heart, as a **signet** upon your arm" — the Vulgate has one word (*signaculum*) in both places, and Ambrose's gloss picks up only "seal," so no argument rests on the English pair. "lavadouro" in ¶38 for Song 4:2's "pool" where the Bethesda "pool" of ¶22-23 is "tanque" — different referents, and nothing in the chapter links them.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 6, verified clean): Structure re-checked mechanically after the fixes — 59 paragraph markers in identical sequence, 11 headings 1:1, all 57 chapter:verse citations identical (only book names localized), NFC-normalized, no ordered-list markers, no diacritics missing, quotation marks balanced 69/70 pairs (the one difference is ¶9, where the source's split attribution `"the Spirit," it is said, "moved upon the waters"` is a single quotation in Portuguese with "diz-se que" moved in front — content identical). `pnpm build:corpus` clean at 2012 catalog items.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 6, considered and not changed): The Latin title in the introduction is `De *Patriarchis*` in en-US (only the second word italic, an artifact of the New Advent HTML) and `*De Patriarchis*` in pt-BR. The Portuguese is the correct way to set a Latin work title and follows `docs/content/primitives-guide.md` on italicizing Latin; matching the source's italic scope here would make the translation worse, and the source's own scope is not load-bearing.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 4, considered and not changed): "Bosra" in ¶36 for the source's "Bosor" — the same city (Is 63:1, Bozrah), in the standard Portuguese biblical form, consistent with Naamã / Jerubaal / Melquisedeque / Arão elsewhere; localizing a proper name is not the same as altering the source's citation apparatus.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass): Corrected 3 mistranslations found by review — ¶8 "eterno poder e divindade"/"presente a divindade" capitalized to "Divindade" for consistency with "Godhead" everywhere else in the chapter (¶25, ¶46); ¶29 "the odour of Your garments" had been rendered "o perfume dos teus perfumes" (garments dropped, duplicating "perfumes") — fixed to "o perfume das tuas vestes"; ¶31 "the humility of the servant" had been rendered "a dignidade do servo" — fixed to "a humildade do servo" for consistency with the same English word correctly rendered "humildade" in ¶33; ¶49 (first) "the body of life" had been rendered "o pão da vida" (echoing the manna imagery of the same paragraph) — fixed to "o corpo da vida" to preserve Ambrose's flesh/Body parallel.
