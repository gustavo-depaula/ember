# Translation Journal — On the Mysteries (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (trans. H. de Romestin, Nicene and Post-Nicene Fathers, Second Series, Vol. 10; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-02: This is the first pt-BR translation of a work by St. Ambrose in the corpus, so no prior Ambrose glossary existed to draw on. Terms were chosen for consistency with existing pt-BR patristic translations already in `content/books/church-fathers/` (e.g. Gregory Thaumaturgus) and with `augustine-confessions/pt-BR` for shared vocabulary (Trindade, Divindade, regeneração, etc.).
- 2026-09-02: All Scripture references preserved with the same book/chapter/verse numbers as the English source, with only the book name localized to standard Portuguese Catholic usage (e.g. "Mark 7:34" → "Marcos 7:34", "1 Corinthians 5:18" → "1 Coríntios 5:18", "Song of Songs" → "Cântico dos Cânticos"). No reference numbers were corrected or altered, even where the source's citation looks slightly off (e.g. the "things seen are temporal" quote cited as 1 Corinthians 5:18 in ¶8), since fidelity to the source's apparatus — not correction of it — is the goal.
- 2026-09-02: The source itself skips from paragraph 47 to two consecutive paragraphs numbered "49" (no "48" exists, and "49" repeats) — a known quirk of this edition's numbering, not a transcription error introduced by this translation. Preserved exactly as in the source: **47** → **49** → **49** → **50**. Flagging here so a translation review doesn't mistake this for a dropped/duplicated paragraph.
- 2026-09-02: No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source (the unsigned introductory paragraphs are the translator/editor's synopsis, not footnotes, and were translated as part of the text, matching the source's own structure). No translator notes were needed.
- 2026-09-02: Second-person plural verb forms ("vós," -stes/-eis conjugations) used throughout for Ambrose's direct address to the newly baptized, matching the formal, homiletic register of the Portuguese used elsewhere in the corpus for patristic sermons/catecheses (e.g. Augustine's `catechising-of-the-uninstructed`-style address).
- 2026-09-02: "the pool" (John 5, Bethesda) rendered as "o tanque," matching standard Portuguese Gospel translations (João 5:2-4), rather than a more literal "a piscina."
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 2): Paragraph numbers are written `**N.**` (bold text), not `N.` (a Markdown ordered-list marker), in **both** languages. The en-US chapter originally used `N.`, which `marked` renders as `<ol start="47">` — the literal numbers are thrown away and the browser renumbers sequentially, so the source's 47 → 49 → 49 displayed as 47 → 48 → 49. That erased exactly the numbering quirk this journal set out to preserve, and labelled a paragraph 48 when no ¶48 exists. en-US was converted to `**N.**` to match this translation and to render the numbers the edition actually prints. No wording changed in either language.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 3): Corrected 3 defects of the same kind — a word the argument leans on rendered so that the link it carries disappears. ¶9 the Psalm proof-text ("and all their strength by the spirit of His mouth") had been rendered "todo o seu exército pelo **sopro** da sua boca," following modern Brazilian psalters. But the whole paragraph is an argument *from that word*: Genesis says the Spirit moved, David says the heavens were made "by the spirit of His mouth," therefore the Spirit was working. With "sopro" the quoted verse no longer names the Spirit and the inference in the next sentence has nothing to rest on; restored to "pelo **espírito** da sua boca," matching Figueiredo (the Portuguese counterpart of the Douay text this edition translates), and "exército" (host — from Almeida's Hebrew-based reading) to "poder," which is what "strength"/*omnis virtus eorum* says. ¶24 "was a **likeness** of this dove" had been rendered "uma **figura** desta pomba," collapsing the glossary's type/figure/likeness distinction — and ¶25 opens by objecting to precisely this sentence ("como é que dizemos que ali estava a **semelhança** e aqui a realidade"), so the reader looked back for "semelhança" and found a different word. Now "uma semelhança desta pomba"; likeness → semelhança is 11/11 across the chapter. ¶38 "the pleasing comparison to **those that are shorn**" had been rendered "com as **ovelhas** tosquiadas," a noun the source does not supply, which then contradicted the very next clause ("pois sabemos que **as cabras**…"). The source is deliberately vague there because the Vulgate's Song 4:1-2 has goats; restored to "com as que são tosquiadas."
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 4): Corrected 8 defects, all of the "a word carries a link and the rendering drops it" kind. ¶57 Song 5:1 was rendered from the Vulgate ("comi o meu **favo** com o meu mel, bebi o meu **vinho** com o meu leite") while the very next sentence glosses it — "por que ele falou de **alimento** e de **bebida**" — so the two words being explained were absent from the quotation they explain. The en-US source reads "My meat"/"My drink" precisely because Ambrose quotes *cibum meum* / *potum meum* in order to comment on them, and ¶58 then echoes the pair in "alimento espiritual e bebida espiritual." Restored to "comi o meu alimento com o meu mel, bebi a minha bebida com o meu leite." ¶35 "black and comely" was "morena," but the chapter-7 synopsis already said "negra e formosa," and the paragraph's whole point is the black↔white antithesis ("Ela era morena — como se tornou agora, de repente, branca?"), which "morena" (brown/tan) does not carry; changed all 3 occurrences to "negra," matching the synopsis and the Vulgate *nigra sum sed formosa*. ¶16-17 "cleansed" was rendered "curar/curado" in three places, colliding with "healed" → "curado" in ¶18 and contradicting the same word rendered "purificado" in the identical construction in ¶19; cleanse → purificar and heal → curar are now kept apart throughout. ¶14 "the spiritual laver" was "a lavagem espiritual," an action noun where the sentence pairs two vessels ("the laver and the cup of salvation") and where the glossary says fonte; now "a fonte espiritual." ¶37 rendered "a dove's"/"a dove" in the plural ("os das pombas," "das quais") and then reverted to the singular in the same sentence ("à semelhança de uma pomba"); the source is singular in all four places, tying the eyes back to the one dove of the Jordan, so the paragraph is now singular throughout. ¶29 the Psalm quotation had gained "precioso" ("Como o óleo **precioso** sobre a cabeça") from modern Brazilian psalters — a word the source does not have; removed. ¶57 "spices" was "perfumes," which is also what "ointments" is called in the adjacent ¶56 and what "odour" is called twice in ¶55; changed to "especiarias," matching the same English word in ¶55. ¶40 "my Brother" (Christ) was lowercase where the same phrase is capitalized in ¶56 and in the source; capitalized.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass 4, considered and not changed): "Bosra" in ¶36 for the source's "Bosor" — the same city (Is 63:1, Bozrah), in the standard Portuguese biblical form, consistent with Naamã / Jerubaal / Melquisedeque / Arão elsewhere; localizing a proper name is not the same as altering the source's citation apparatus.
- 2026-09-02 (review pass): Corrected 3 mistranslations found by review — ¶8 "eterno poder e divindade"/"presente a divindade" capitalized to "Divindade" for consistency with "Godhead" everywhere else in the chapter (¶25, ¶46); ¶29 "the odour of Your garments" had been rendered "o perfume dos teus perfumes" (garments dropped, duplicating "perfumes") — fixed to "o perfume das tuas vestes"; ¶31 "the humility of the servant" had been rendered "a dignidade do servo" — fixed to "a humildade do servo" for consistency with the same English word correctly rendered "humildade" in ¶33; ¶49 (first) "the body of life" had been rendered "o pão da vida" (echoing the manna imagery of the same paragraph) — fixed to "o corpo da vida" to preserve Ambrose's flesh/Body parallel.
