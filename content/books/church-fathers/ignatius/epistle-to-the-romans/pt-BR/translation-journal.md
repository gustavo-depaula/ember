Source: en-US (trans. Alexander Roberts and James Donaldson, Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 1; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-02: This is the second Ignatian epistle translated into pt-BR (after `epistle-to-polycarp`, #351/#355). Reused that glossary's register and its choice of "Carta a/aos ___" for the title.
- 2026-09-02 — **address form**: unlike the Polycarp letter (addressed to one bishop, with a mid-text shift to the congregation), this epistle is addressed throughout to a single collective addressee — "the Church which... presides in the place of the region of the Romans." Rendered with plural **"vós"** consistently for the whole letter, no singular "tu" anywhere — there is no personal addressee to shift to/from as there was with Polycarp.
- 2026-09-02: Two Scripture allusions in the source carry no chapter/verse citation ("For the things which are seen are temporal..." in Ch. 3, echoing 2 Corinthians 4:18; "For what shall a man be profited..." in Ch. 6, echoing Matthew 16:26). Left uncited in the translation, matching the source exactly — only quotations the English edition itself cites (1 Corinthians 4:4, 1 Corinthians 15:8-9) were given references, localized to standard Portuguese Catholic usage (1 Coríntios 4:4; 1 Coríntios 15:8-9).
- 2026-09-02: No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source — a single continuous letter with a greeting and ten chapter headings, all original to this edition and translated as part of the text. No translator notes were needed.
- 2026-09-02: "man-pleaser" (Ch. 2) rendered as a paraphrase, "quem agrada aos homens," rather than coining a single Portuguese noun — matches the parallel construction with "pleasing God" ("quem agrada a Deus") in the same sentence.
- 2026-09-02: "mo impeçais" (Ch. 4, "unless you hinder me") uses the clitic contraction *mo* (me + o) to keep the sentence compact, consistent with the formal register elsewhere in the letter.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass corrections** (2 defects found on a third independent review, after two earlier clean passes):
  - Ch. 2: "julgou digno a mim... de ser chamado" used the tonic pronoun "a mim" alone as a direct object, which is not standard Portuguese (unlike Spanish's personal "a," this needs a paired clitic) → "Deus julgou-me digno, o bispo da Síria, de ser chamado..."
  - Ch. 9: "attain to God" had been rendered "chegar a Deus" (arrive/reach), breaking the letter's consistent rendering of this recurring theme ("alcançar a Deus"/"alcançar a Jesus Cristo," used in all 9 other occurrences across the letter) → "se eu alcançar a Deus".
- 2026-09-02: Reviewed and confirmed as intentional (not a defect): straight `"..."` quotation marks are used for the letter's 3 embedded Scripture quotations rather than the source's typographic curly `"..."` — this matches the established convention already in `epistle-to-polycarp/pt-BR`. Re-verified post-merge: `epistle-to-polycarp/pt-BR/ch001.md` does use straight quotes (2 straight, 0 curly), so the claim holds.
- 2026-09-02 — **post-merge review corrections** (4 defects found across three fresh independent passes — completeness/structure, grammar, semantic fidelity — run after #365 merged):
  - Ch. 2: the source's editorial bracket in "such [another] opportunity" had been dropped. Found mechanically: en-US carries 19 bracketed insertions, pt-BR only 17 → "[outra] oportunidade como esta".
  - Ch. 8: same defect for "you have wished [well] to me" → "vós me quisestes [bem]". Bracket parity with the source is now 19/19.
  - Ch. 2: "porque Deus julgou-me digno" used enclisis after the subordinating conjunction "porque", which requires proclisis — and which the letter observes consistently elsewhere after "que", "se", "para que", "nenhum". This was an unintended side effect of the earlier "julgou digno a mim" fix logged above → "porque Deus me julgou digno".
  - Ch. 2: "eu me tornarei de Deus" ("I shall become God's") — `tornar-se` is a linking verb that selects a noun or adjective complement (*tornar-se médico/rico*); a bare `de + noun` is not a licensed complement, making this a calque of the English genitive. Portuguese expresses belonging with `ser de` → "eu serei de Deus". The inchoative force of "become" is carried by the future tense plus the conditional clause, matching the translator's own handling of Ch. 6's inchoative "I shall indeed be a man of God" → "serei de fato um homem de Deus".
- 2026-09-02 — **post-merge review round, no defects found**: after #367 merged, three further fresh independent passes were run over the letter (completeness/structure, Portuguese grammar/orthography, semantic fidelity) and all three returned clean. Verified in that round: 12 headings matching 1:1 in both languages, 11 body paragraphs each, bracket parity at 19/19, both Scripture citations present and localized, no leftover footnote markers, every clitic placement licensed, and each recurring Ignatian phrase ("alcançar a Deus" ×8, "discípulo", "feras", "Síria") rendered consistently. Two candidates were examined and dropped as non-defects: Ch. 1's "lest it should do me an injury" → "para que ele não me cause dano" (the "para que não" cautionary clause is standard Portuguese for English "lest", not a calque), and Ch. 9's "the very last of them" → "o último deles" ("último" is already extremal in Portuguese, so the intensifier has no natural counterpart to lose). The text is considered settled; a future pass should not re-litigate these.
- 2026-09-02: Reviewed and rejected as a **false positive** (no change made): a review pass proposed that Ch. 2's "that I may rise again to Him" → "para que eu ressuscite nele" loses the directional sense of "to Him" and collapses it with Ch. 4's "in Him" → "ressuscitarei nele". Verified against independent Portuguese renderings of this epistle made from the Greek, which likewise split the sunset/sunrise line into a directional first clause and a locative second one ("...para o Senhor e nele ressurgir") — exactly the structure this translation already has, with "rumo a Deus" carrying the directionality. The proposed "ressuscite para ele" would be worse, reading as purposive/beneficiary ("rise for his benefit").
