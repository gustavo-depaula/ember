# Translation Journal — Epistle to the Magnesians (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (trans. Alexander Roberts and James Donaldson, Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 1; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Key Terms

| English | Portuguese | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| Epistle | Carta | consistent with the other translated Ignatius letters (`epistle-to-the-ephesians`, `epistle-to-the-romans`, `epistle-to-the-trallians`, `epistle-to-polycarp`) |
| Magnesians / Magnesia | Magnésios / Magnésia | standard Portuguese Patristics naming, matching the sibling titles "Carta aos Efésios" / "Carta aos Trálios" already in the corpus |
| Mæander | Meandro | the river near ancient Magnesia; standard Portuguese historical/geographic form |
| Theophorus | Teóforo | kept as Ignatius's proper epithet ("God-bearer"), not translated — matches prior Ignatius translations |
| Damas | Damas | bishop of Magnesia; kept unchanged, an already-valid Portuguese name form with no established alternate rendering |
| Bassus | Basso | presbyter of Magnesia; standard Portuguese -us → -o rendering of the Latin cognomen, consistent with `Onésimo`/`Políbio`/`Frontão` in sibling letters |
| Apollonius | Apolônio | presbyter of Magnesia; standard Portuguese form of the Greek name |
| Sotio | Sotio | deacon of Magnesia; kept unchanged rather than rendered "Sócio" — the obvious Portuguese calque collides with the common noun *sócio* ("member/business partner"), the same collision risk documented for `Burrhus` in `epistle-to-the-ephesians` |
| bishop / presbytery / deacon | bispo / presbitério / diácono | consistent with prior Ignatius translations |
| fellow-servant | conservo | matches `epistle-to-the-ephesians` Ch. 2 ("conservo," from Latin *conservus*) |
| the prince of this world | o príncipe deste mundo | matches the established rendering in `epistle-to-the-trallians` Ch. 4 |
| Sabbath / the Lord's Day | sábado / Dia do Senhor | Ch. 9's contrast between the old and new observance; "Dia do Senhor" capitalized as the liturgical proper term |
| Smyrnæans | os de Esmirna | rendered by city name, not the demonym "esmirniotas" — follows the convention documented in the `epistle-to-polycarp` and `epistle-to-the-trallians` journals and matches every other pt-BR occurrence of Smyrna in the corpus |
| to attain to God | alcançar a Deus | Ch. 14, matches the established rendering in `epistle-to-the-trallians` and `epistle-to-polycarp` |
| puffed up | ensoberbecidos | Ch. 12, adjectival use; matches the reflexive verb form "ensoberbecerdes" used for the same English idiom in `epistle-to-the-trallians` Ch. 7 |
| May I enjoy you / May I have joy of you (ὀναίμην ὑμῶν) | ter alegria de vós | Ch. 12. The corpus convention for Ignatius's recurring optative formula, set by `epistle-to-the-ephesians` Ch. 2 ("Que eu tenha sempre alegria de vós, se é que sou digno disso") and `epistle-to-polycarp` ("Que eu tenha alegria de vós para sempre"). Roberts-Donaldson varies the English ("enjoy you" here, "have joy of you" there) for one and the same Greek formula, so the English wording must not be allowed to split the Portuguese rendering. Do **not** use *gozar de vós*: besides breaking this convention, Brazilian *gozar* + person carries a colloquial sexual/mocking reading that the sibling convention exists to avoid. Copied here per the "a convention logged in one book's journal is invisible from inside another" rule in `docs/journal.md`. |

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-02: Fifth pt-BR translation from Ignatius of Antioch (after *Epistle to Polycarp*, *Epistle to the Romans*, *Epistle to the Ephesians*, and *Epistle to the Trallians*). Register and diction kept consistent with those four: dignified, slightly formal modern Portuguese.
- 2026-09-02 — **address form**: this letter addresses the whole Magnesian congregation throughout ("your bishop," "the whole multitude of you"), like the *Epistle to the Ephesians* and *Epistle to the Trallians*. Rendered with **plural "vós"** address throughout, no singular shifts.
- 2026-09-02: No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source — chapter headings are original to this edition (Roberts-Donaldson) and were translated as part of the text. No translator notes were needed.
- 2026-09-02: Bracketed editorial insertions from the source (e.g. "[Church]", "[of their bishop]", "[so is it also here.]", "[I now write to you]") preserved as brackets in the translation, following the established convention. Checked bracket boundaries against the pass-1 defect found in `epistle-to-the-trallians` (a bracket splitting a Portuguese contraction) — none of this letter's insertions land on a contraction.
- 2026-09-02: All three Scripture references (Matthew 27:52, Ch. 9; 1 Timothy 1:1, Ch. 11; Proverbs 18:17, Ch. 12) preserved with the same book/chapter/verse as the English source, book names localized to standard Portuguese Catholic usage (Matthew → Mateus, 1 Timothy → 1 Timóteo, Proverbs → Provérbios), matching the localization already established across the sibling Ignatius letters.
- 2026-09-02 — **Ch. 7, "and is with and has gone to one"**: kept the source's literal triadic repetition of "one" ("que procedeu de um só Pai, e está com um só, e a um só se encaminhou") rather than smoothing it into a clearer but flatter "e está com ele, e a ele se encaminhou." The threefold "one" is Ignatius's own rhetorical device here (echoed by "one temple," "one altar," "one Jesus Christ" earlier in the same sentence) and central to the chapter's argument for unity, so the slightly denser Portuguese preserves the author's emphasis rather than trading it for smoothness. (The verb itself was "voltou" until pass 7 below corrected it — the triadic-repetition decision recorded here is unaffected by that fix.)
- 2026-09-02 — **Ch. 10, "Christianity did not embrace Judaism, but Judaism Christianity"**: rendered with the cognate verb "abraçar" ("o cristianismo não abraçou o judaísmo, mas o judaísmo o cristianismo") rather than a paraphrase like "crer em," since "abraçar" tracks the English sense of adoption/embrace precisely and reads naturally in Portuguese.
- 2026-09-02 — **names**: Bassus and Apollonius given standard Portuguese forms (Basso, Apolônio), following the corpus's established pattern for well-attested Latin/Greek names (cf. Onésimo, Políbio, Frontão in sibling letters). Damas and Sotio kept in their source spelling — Damas is already valid Portuguese orthography, and Sotio's natural calque "Sócio" collides with the everyday Portuguese noun for "member/partner," the same reasoning that kept "Burrhus" unchanged in `epistle-to-the-ephesians` rather than risk the reading "Burro" ("donkey").
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 1** (1 bracket-convention issue found): Ch. 3, the source's editorial insertion "[so to do]" ("Him who has willed us [so to do]") had been absorbed into the sentence with no bracket ("em honra daquele que assim o quis"), leaving the file with 7 bracket pairs against the source's 8. Fixed to "em honra daquele que nos quis [assim agir]", restoring the bracket.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 2**: clean. Independent fresh proofread and fidelity re-audit: reciprocal forms ("respeitai-vos uns aos outros", "amai-vos... uns aos outros", "uns aos outros" in Ch. 13) all correctly reciprocal (the exact bug class the sibling `epistle-to-the-trallians` review found in its Ch. 13 — "ame ao outro" for "love one another"); no false friends found; no ambiguous possessives; the deliberate "one Father... one" triad in Ch. 7 read against the English confirms the literal repetition was kept as a documented decision, not a slip.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 3** (1 heading-voice issue found, adversarial cross-check against sibling headings): Ch. 14, "Your prayers requested" — an impersonal editorial noun-phrase heading, no explicit grammatical subject — had been rendered "Peço as vossas orações" ("I request your prayers"), shifting the heading into Ignatius's own first-person voice. This is the same defect class `epistle-to-the-trallians` pass 5 found in its Ch. 1 heading (third-person editorial apparatus drifting into the letter's own voice): headings sit in a different grammatical voice from the body, and a translator working inside the letter's voice drifts into it without noticing. Fixed to "Pedido de vossas orações", a noun phrase matching the source's impersonal register and consistent with this book's own Ch. 1 ("Motivo da carta") and Ch. 8 ("Cautela contra falsas doutrinas") headings.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 4**: clean. Full structural re-verification: 16/16 headings match the source one-to-one, 8/8 bracket pairs balanced and content-matched, all three Scripture references (Mateus 27:52, 1 Timóteo 1:1, Provérbios 18:17) present at the same lines with correctly localized book names.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 5**: clean. Diacritics, trailing-whitespace, and name-consistency sweep — no encoding issues, no stray whitespace, each proper name (Damas, Basso, Apolônio, Sotio, Policarpo) appears exactly where expected with consistent spelling; spot-checked adjective agreement (masc. "admirabilíssimo bispo", fem. "compactada coroa").
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 6**: clean. Sweep of the plural-"vós" verb conjugations in the file (e.g. trateis, rendais, obedeçais, procureis, façais, deixeis, sereis, caiais, alcanceis, estais, cultivais) — all correctly conjugated for their mood (imperative/subjunctive/indicative) and consistent with the plural address form used throughout. Two consecutive clean passes (5 and 6) after the pass 3 fix.
- 2026-09-02 — **post-merge audit, pass 7** (4 fidelity issues found, all fixed): the first pass run against the merged file rather than the working copy, and the first to compare the two files clause by clause rather than checking structure, brackets, names, and conjugations. Every earlier pass had been *inventory-shaped* — count the headings, count the brackets, sweep the diacritics — and all four defects it missed are invisible to a count.
  - **Ch. 6, misparsed auxiliary "Do"** (high severity). "Do all then, imitating the same divine conduct, pay respect to one another" is Roberts-Donaldson's archaic emphatic imperative — *do* is the auxiliary, *ye all* (which New Advent drops to "all") the subject, and "pay respect" the sentence's only finite verb; the Greek has one verb, ἐντρέπεσθε, and nothing answering to ποιεῖτε. It had been read as the lexical verb *fazer* and rendered "Fazei, pois, todos, imitando a mesma conduta divina, e respeitai-vos uns aos outros" — a transitive imperative with no object and no recoverable antecedent, plus an inserted "e" that splits one English clause into two Portuguese commands and strands the participial adjunct between them. Not good Portuguese on any reading. Fixed to "Todos vós, pois, imitando a mesma conduta divina, respeitai-vos uns aos outros".
  - **Ch. 6, unsourced "em vós"** (low severity). "contemplei em vós, nas pessoas antes mencionadas, toda a vossa multidão" renders one locative adjunct twice: the source has only "in the persons before mentioned", and "of you" is already carried by *a vossa multidão*. Worse than pleonasm — two adjacent unmarked *em*-phrases in apposition read as identifying "vós" *with* the persons before mentioned, which dissolves the synecdoche the sentence exists to make (the three named delegates of Ch. 2 stood in for the body Ignatius could not see). Fixed by dropping "em vós".
  - **Ch. 7, "has gone to one" → "voltou"** (low severity). *Voltou* ("returned") is sound doctrine and a defensible reading of ἐχώρησεν, but the en-US text is this book's canonical source and it says "has gone to". The English triad leaves each limb's "one" unspecified — came forth from one / is with one / has gone to one — and *voltou* asserts destination-identical-to-origin as a lexical fact, collapsing the third limb into the first. Fixed to "e a um só se encaminhou": *encaminhar-se a* is unambiguous directed motion and keeps the fronted "a um só" parallel that Ch. 7's rhetoric depends on. ("a um só foi" was rejected — fronted, *foi* invites a momentary *ser* reading; "se dirigiu" was rejected — *dirigir-se a* also means "to address", a live ambiguity in a sentence about the Son and the Father.)
  - **Ch. 10, "you shall be convicted" → "reconhecidos"** (high severity). ἐλεγχθήσεσθε is judicial — exposed, found out, proven guilty — and the clause is the sanction that motivates "Be salted in Him": it is the *bad* savour that testifies against you. *Reconhecido* is neutral-to-commendatory in Portuguese (*ser reconhecido por algo* = to be credited for something), so the sentence had inverted a warning into a compliment. Fixed to "sereis denunciados pelo vosso sabor" — *denunciar* is the standard idiom for a trait that betrays someone (*o sotaque o denunciou*). (*Convictos* is a false friend here: in Portuguese it reads "convinced".)
- 2026-09-02 — **post-merge audit, pass 8** (5 issues found and fixed; 5 candidates raised and rejected). Run against the pass-7 corrections, half of it re-reading text pass 7 had just touched — which is how it caught that **a fix from pass 1 had itself broken the grammar**:
  - **Ch. 3, *querer* regency — a defect introduced by an earlier review pass** (high severity). Pass 1's only goal was restoring the bracket around "[so to do]", and it rewrote "em honra daquele que assim o quis" as "em honra daquele que nos quis [assim agir]". The bracket came back; the sentence stopped parsing. Portuguese *querer* does not license accusative-plus-infinitive with a distinct subject — that class is the causatives (*mandar, deixar, fazer, ver, ouvir*) — and clitic climbing makes proclitic *nos* the object of the embedded infinitive, which *agir* (intransitive) cannot take. No parse assigns *nos* a governor. Fixed to "em honra daquele que quis [que assim agíssemos]": the bracket stays one well-formed constituent, "us" is carried by the 1pl inflection inside it, and it passes the bracket-removal test the convention exists for — strip the brackets and "daquele que quis" is still grammatical, whereas the alternative "quis que [assim agíssemos]" leaves the stranded fragment "daquele que quis que".
  - **Ch. 12, "May I enjoy you" → "gozar de vós"** (high severity, corpus-consistency). *Gozar de* + person breaks the corpus convention for Ignatius's recurring optative formula ὀναίμην ὑμῶν, which `epistle-to-the-ephesians` Ch. 2 and `epistle-to-polycarp` both render "ter alegria de vós" — and it carries the colloquial Brazilian reading of *gozar* that the convention exists to avoid. Roberts-Donaldson varies the English for the one Greek formula ("enjoy you" here, "have joy of you" in Ephesians), which is exactly how the split got in: translating the English wording faithfully split a rendering the corpus had already unified. Fixed to "Possa eu ter alegria de vós em todos os aspectos"; convention now recorded in the Key Terms table above so it is visible from inside this file.
  - **Ch. 10, "be changed into the new leaven" → "mudai-vos para"** (low severity). In Brazilian Portuguese *mudar-se para* overwhelmingly reads as *to relocate to*, so the default parse was "move over to the new leaven" rather than μεταβάλεσθε εἰς — transformation. Fixed to "transformai-vos no novo fermento".
  - **Ch. 10, "Christ Jesus" reordered** (cosmetic). Rendered "Jesus Cristo" where the source has "Christ Jesus" and where this file's own Ch. 8 keeps "Cristo Jesus". Fixed for internal consistency.
  - **Ch. 6, "those that preside over you" → "presidem sobre vós"** (low severity). *Presidir sobre* is not a licensed regency in Portuguese (VTD *presidir a sessão*, or VTI *presidir a*) — a transparent calque of English "preside over", the defect profile a translation from English invites. Fixed to "e aos que vos presidem", which also strengthens the parallel with "unidos ao vosso bispo".
  - Four journal claims corrected in the same pass: the "Both … three in total" miscount; a wrong chapter citation on the *gozar* entry; a pass-6 entry claiming a sweep of "every" vós-conjugation while enumerating eleven of ~twenty; and — worst — a note this audit itself had written claiming *gozar de* + person was "the established construction across the sibling Ignatius letters". A grep shows *gozar* occurs nowhere else in the Ignatius letters, and everywhere else in the pt-BR corpus (Liguori, Montfort) it takes an abstract object — *gozar de Deus*, *gozam de Deus* — never a person. **The entry asserted a corpus convention without grepping for it, and would have locked the deviation in place against exactly the future review that should have caught it.** A journal entry defending a rendering is only worth what its evidence is worth; assert a cross-book convention only with the grep in hand. (Pass 9 had to correct this very bullet: its first draft claimed *gozar* "occurs nowhere in the corpus except this file", which is false — it is common in Liguori. The moral held; the evidence sentence was itself an ungrepped assertion, written into the paragraph warning against ungrepped assertions. **Scope the claim to the search you actually ran.**)
  - **Rejected candidates**, recorded so later passes do not re-open them: Ch. 7 "do qual nada há de mais excelente" (a legitimate erudite Latinate comparative relative — the Anselmian "aquilo do qual nada maior pode ser pensado" is the same construction — not a mis-set comparative); Ch. 1 heading "Motivo da carta" (idiomatic condensation of "Reason of writing the epistle"; the corpus does both); Ch. 4 heading "à parte do bispo" (the corpus's settled rendering of χωρὶς τοῦ ἐπισκόπου — `epistle-to-the-trallians` Ch. 7 uses the same phrase, and Ch. 4's own body glosses it with "sem ele"); Ch. 9 "a qual alguns negam" and Ch. 15 "os de Esmirna" (both previously documented). **Five of ten candidates in this pass were wrong** — a review pass generates false positives at about the rate it generates findings, and the corpus grep is what separates them.
- 2026-09-02 — **post-merge audit, pass 9** (2 text issues found and fixed, 3 journal issues fixed, 2 candidates rejected). Ch. 8–15 came back clean on a full clause-by-clause re-read — the first time any part of this letter has been declared clean by a *reading* pass rather than a counting one. The two remaining text defects were both in the same category: an English function word mapped onto a Portuguese form that carries syntactic requirements the English one does not.
  - **Ch. 5, uncontrolled gerund** (high severity). Roberts-Donaldson's "Seeing, then, all things have an end" uses *seeing* as a pure causal conjunction (= *since*); "Vendo, pois, que..." is a true Portuguese gerund and an *oração reduzida de gerúndio* needs its implicit subject either coreferential with the matrix subject, overtly expressed (*gerúndio absoluto*), or licensed by an impersonal matrix. The matrix subject here is `estas duas coisas` — inanimate, and it cannot see; the only 1pl in the sentence is `nós` inside the PP `diante de nós`, an oblique that cannot control. Textbook anacoluto. The corpus check cut against the text rather than for it: every sentence-initial gerund in the pt-BR church-fathers set *is* controlled (`epistle-to-polycarp` "Vendo que a Igreja ... eu também me sinto mais encorajado", matrix subject *eu*; this file's own Ch. 14 "Sabendo, como sei, que estais cheios de Deus, exortei-vos"), and Ch. 5 was the sole exception. Fixed to "Visto, pois, que" — *visto que* is already this file's causal conjunction four times over and the corpus's dominant one, and splitting the locução with an interpolated *pois* is precedented in this file's own Ch. 7 ("Assim, pois, como o Senhor nada fez sem o Pai").
  - **Greeting, relative pronoun taking the wrong antecedent** (moderate severity). English "and wish **it** abundance of happiness" is a clause-internal neuter pronoun that can only be the Church. Portuguese "e **a quem** desejo" opens a second relative coordinated with "**em quem** saúdo", and two same-form relatives in coordination share a head — so the default parse takes *Jesus Cristo* and yields Ignatius wishing Christ happiness *in Christ*, with the antecedent re-named inside its own relative clause. The translation manufactured an ambiguity the source does not contain and made the wrong branch the salient one. Fixed to "e **à qual** desejo". (Precisely why that works: *not* "feminine morphology can only reach *a Igreja*" — *Magnésia* in the same clause is feminine too. It works because coordinating the relative with "que está em Magnésia" forces both onto the same head, and the competing head *Meandro* is masculine.) This is the same disambiguation-by-gender technique this journal already documents for Ch. 9's "a qual alguns negam", and it breaks no formula — the Ignatian greeting formula is "abundância de felicidade" (attested in four letters), and two of the four carry no relative pronoun at all, so the relative is local syntax. Note the sibling `epistle-to-the-trallians` greeting uses the same words "e a quem desejo abundância de felicidade" and is correct there: its coordinate partner is "a qual também saúdo", already anchored on the Church. Identical wording, opposite antecedent chain — **a phrase copied from a sibling letter is only as correct as the sentence it lands in.**
  - **Rejected**: Ch. 7 "razoável e próprio à parte". Both limbs of the objection failed against the corpus. *Próprio* for "proper" in the *fitting* sense is corpus-attested in exactly this frame (`dionysius-rome/against-the-sabellians` renders "as is fitting and proper" as "como é conveniente e próprio"), and the competing "own" reading is structurally unavailable — all 16 corpus occurrences of possessive *próprio* carry either a possessive determiner (this file's own "seu próprio lugar", "seu próprio acusador") or an intensified pronoun host, neither of which is present. The proposed substitute *conveniente* would also collide with this file's *convém* for "it is fitting", and the alternative of raising "à parte" onto *procureis* would be a fidelity regression: Greek ἰδίᾳ modifies the appearing, and the English keeps "apart" clause-final inside the subordinate clause.
  - Three journal claims corrected — see the commit history; the worst was in the pass-8 bullet warning against ungrepped assertions, which itself asserted, ungrepped, that *gozar* appears nowhere else in the corpus.
- 2026-09-02 — **known corpus inconsistency, out of this book's scope**: `epistle-to-the-romans` **Greeting** (not Ch. 1) carries the same "presidir sobre" calque — "e que preside sobre o amor", for προκαθημένη τῆς ἀγάπης; published Portuguese has "preside na caridade". (The *também* in that sentence belongs to a different clause, "e que também preside no lugar da região dos romanos"; an earlier draft of this entry merged the two into one quotation.) Not touched here — it belongs to a different book and a different PR — but it should be fixed when Romans is next opened, or the corpus will be internally inconsistent on a regency this book just corrected.
- 2026-09-02 — judgment calls considered and deliberately kept, logged so future passes do not re-litigate them: **"gozar de Deus"** (Ch. 1, for "we shall enjoy God") — *gozar de Deus* is established Catholic devotional Portuguese for the fruition of God, an abstract-object use with no colloquial hazard, and the sibling convention below governs the *person*-object formula, not this one. **"eu possa gozar para sempre"** (Ch. 2, for "whose friendship may I ever enjoy") — the object is *amizade*, not a person, so neither the colloquial reading nor the sibling convention applies; kept as minimal intervention. **"a qual alguns negam"** (Ch. 9) — the English "whom some deny" is ambiguous between Christ and His death, and the feminine *a qual* resolves it to *a morte*, which is the docetist controversy the chapter is actually about. **"os de Esmirna"** for Smyrnæans (Ch. 15) — per the convention already documented in the Key Terms table above.
- 2026-09-02 — **what this book taught the review process**: six pre-merge passes found two defects; the seventh, run post-merge, found four more — and the split is not about effort, it is about *shape*. Passes 1–6 were inventory checks (count brackets, count headings, sweep diacritics, sweep conjugations), and an inventory check cannot see a sentence that is complete, correctly accented, correctly conjugated, and wrong. All four pass-7 defects were well-formed Portuguese sitting at the right place in the right structure. **A translation review needs at least one pass that reads the two texts against each other clause by clause with no checklist in hand** — the counting passes are necessary but they are not sufficient, and running more of them does not substitute for the one that is missing.
