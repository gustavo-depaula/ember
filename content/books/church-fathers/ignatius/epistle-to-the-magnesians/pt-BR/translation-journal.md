Source: en-US (trans. Alexander Roberts and James Donaldson, Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 1; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-02: Fifth pt-BR translation from Ignatius of Antioch (after *Epistle to Polycarp*, *Epistle to the Romans*, *Epistle to the Ephesians*, and *Epistle to the Trallians*). Register and diction kept consistent with those four: dignified, slightly formal modern Portuguese.
- 2026-09-02 — **address form**: this letter addresses the whole Magnesian congregation throughout ("your bishop," "your whole multitude"), like the *Epistle to the Ephesians* and *Epistle to the Trallians*. Rendered with **plural "vós"** address throughout, no singular shifts.
- 2026-09-02: No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source — chapter headings are original to this edition (Roberts-Donaldson) and were translated as part of the text. No translator notes were needed.
- 2026-09-02: Bracketed editorial insertions from the source (e.g. "[Church]", "[of their bishop]", "[so is it also here.]", "[I now write to you]") preserved as brackets in the translation, following the established convention. Checked bracket boundaries against the pass-1 defect found in `epistle-to-the-trallians` (a bracket splitting a Portuguese contraction) — none of this letter's insertions land on a contraction.
- 2026-09-02: Both Scripture references (Matthew 27:52, Ch. 9; 1 Timothy 1:1, Ch. 11; Proverbs 18:17, Ch. 12 — three in total) preserved with the same book/chapter/verse as the English source, book names localized to standard Portuguese Catholic usage (Matthew → Mateus, 1 Timothy → 1 Timóteo, Proverbs → Provérbios), matching the localization already established across the sibling Ignatius letters.
- 2026-09-02 — **Ch. 7, "and is with and has gone to one"**: kept the source's literal triadic repetition of "one" ("que procedeu de um só Pai, e está com um só, e a um só voltou") rather than smoothing it into a clearer but flatter "e está com ele, e a ele voltou." The threefold "one" is Ignatius's own rhetorical device here (echoed by "one temple," "one altar," "one Jesus Christ" earlier in the same sentence) and central to the chapter's argument for unity, so the slightly denser Portuguese preserves the author's emphasis rather than trading it for smoothness.
- 2026-09-02 — **Ch. 10, "Christianity did not embrace Judaism, but Judaism Christianity"**: rendered with the cognate verb "abraçar" ("o cristianismo não abraçou o judaísmo, mas o judaísmo o cristianismo") rather than a paraphrase like "crer em," since "abraçar" tracks the English sense of adoption/embrace precisely and reads naturally in Portuguese.
- 2026-09-02 — **names**: Bassus and Apollonius given standard Portuguese forms (Basso, Apolônio), following the corpus's established pattern for well-attested Latin/Greek names (cf. Onésimo, Políbio, Frontão in sibling letters). Damas and Sotio kept in their source spelling — Damas is already valid Portuguese orthography, and Sotio's natural calque "Sócio" collides with the everyday Portuguese noun for "member/partner," the same reasoning that kept "Burrhus" unchanged in `epistle-to-the-ephesians` rather than risk the reading "Burro" ("donkey").
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 1** (1 bracket-convention issue found): Ch. 3, the source's editorial insertion "[so to do]" ("Him who has willed us [so to do]") had been absorbed into the sentence with no bracket ("em honra daquele que assim o quis"), leaving the file with 7 bracket pairs against the source's 8. Fixed to "em honra daquele que nos quis [assim agir]", restoring the bracket.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 2**: clean. Independent fresh proofread and fidelity re-audit: reciprocal forms ("respeitai-vos uns aos outros", "amai-vos... uns aos outros", "uns aos outros" in Ch. 13) all correctly reciprocal (the exact bug class the sibling `epistle-to-the-trallians` review found in its Ch. 13 — "ame ao outro" for "love one another"); no false friends found; no ambiguous possessives; the deliberate "one Father... one" triad in Ch. 7 read against the English confirms the literal repetition was kept as a documented decision, not a slip.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 3** (1 heading-voice issue found, adversarial cross-check against sibling headings): Ch. 14, "Your prayers requested" — an impersonal editorial noun-phrase heading, no explicit grammatical subject — had been rendered "Peço as vossas orações" ("I request your prayers"), shifting the heading into Ignatius's own first-person voice. This is the same defect class `epistle-to-the-trallians` pass 5 found in its Ch. 1 heading (third-person editorial apparatus drifting into the letter's own voice): headings sit in a different grammatical voice from the body, and a translator working inside the letter's voice drifts into it without noticing. Fixed to "Pedido de vossas orações", a noun phrase matching the source's impersonal register and consistent with this book's own Ch. 1 ("Motivo da carta") and Ch. 8 ("Cautela contra falsas doutrinas") headings.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 4**: clean. Full structural re-verification: 16/16 headings match the source one-to-one, 8/8 bracket pairs balanced and content-matched, all three Scripture references (Mateus 27:52, 1 Timóteo 1:1, Provérbios 18:17) present at the same lines with correctly localized book names.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 5**: clean. Diacritics, trailing-whitespace, and name-consistency sweep — no encoding issues, no stray whitespace, each proper name (Damas, Basso, Apolônio, Sotio, Policarpo) appears exactly where expected with consistent spelling; spot-checked adjective agreement (masc. "admirabilíssimo bispo", fem. "compactada coroa").
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 6**: clean. Full sweep of every plural-"vós" verb conjugation in the file (trateis, rendais, obedeçais, procureis, façais, deixeis, sereis, caiais, alcanceis, estais, cultivais) — all correctly conjugated for their mood (imperative/subjunctive/indicative) and consistent with the plural address form used throughout. Two consecutive clean passes (5 and 6) after the pass 3 fix.
