## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-14: Last remaining work in the Ignatius of Antioch author folder without a pt-BR translation — completes the entire author for Portuguese (all seven authentic epistles and the *Martyrdom* were already translated). Single chapter (`ch001.md`), ~10,800 words, a collection of eight distinct pseudonymous/legendary pieces traditionally transmitted together under this title: the Epistles to the Tarsians, the Antiochians, Hero, and the Philippians; the Epistle of Maria the Proselyte to Ignatius; the Epistle to Mary at Neapolis; two short Epistles to St. John; and an exchange of letters between Ignatius and the Virgin Mary (with her reply). Reused every term already established across the seven authentic epistles and the *Martyrdom* rather than re-deriving it (Teóforo, bispo/presbitério/diáconos, conservo, Neápolis, Policarpo, Onésimo, Damas, Políbio, Filo, Agatopo).
- **Address form**: letters to a congregation (Tarsians, Antiochians, Philippians) use plural **"vós"** throughout, matching the convention for the authentic congregational epistles. Letters to a single named individual (Hero; Maria the Proselyte to Ignatius; Ignatius to Mary at Neapolis; both letters to St. John; Ignatius to the Virgin Mary; her reply) use singular **"tu"** throughout, matching the convention already established in `epistle-to-polycarp` for an individually-addressed letter.
- Heading levels preserved exactly as in the source: `#` for the collection title, `##` for each of the eight individual letters, `###` for "Greeting"/"Chapter N." within the longer letters. The four shortest letters (the two to St. John, Ignatius to the Virgin Mary, and her reply) have no "Greeting"/chapter subheadings in the source — the salutation line runs directly into the body — and this was preserved rather than adding structure the source doesn't have.
- Bracketed editorial insertions from the source (e.g. "[only]", "[Paul]", "[in truth]", "[speed]", "[deemed]") preserved as brackets in the translation, following the established convention.
- Scripture references preserved with the same book/chapter/verse numbers, book names localized to standard Portuguese Catholic usage throughout (Acts → Atos, Galatians → Gálatas, Habakkuk → Habacuque, Romans → Romanos, Colossians → Colossenses, 1/2 Corinthians → 1/2 Coríntios, 1/2 Timothy → 1/2 Timóteo, John → João, Isaiah → Isaías, Genesis → Gênesis, Deuteronomy → Deuteronômio, Mark → Marcos, Malachi → Malaquias, Proverbs → Provérbios, Matthew → Mateus, Ephesians → Efésios, James → Tiago, 1/2 Peter → 1/2 Pedro, Job → Jó, 2 Thessalonians → 2 Tessalonicenses, 1 Timothy → 1 Timóteo, 2 Timothy → 2 Timóteo, Jeremiah → Jeremias, Philippians → Filipenses, Numbers → Números, 1 Samuel → 1 Samuel, Luke → Lucas, Sirach → Eclesiástico).
- The Job quotation (Antiochians, Ch. 10) was translated directly from the English source's own wording ("For what in that case shall I do when the Lord makes an inquisition regarding me?") rather than substituting the familiar Almeida-Bible phrasing of Job 31:14, since the ANF English here is a specific (looser) rendering and the skill's fidelity rule is to translate the source text actually given, not to replace it with an independent Bible translation.
- No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source (only inline editorial brackets, treated as above) — no translator notes were needed.
- The letter titles "Epistle to Hero, a Deacon of Antioch" and "Epistle to Mary at Neapolis, Near Zarbus" were rendered as "Carta a Hero, Diácono de Antioquia" and "Carta a Maria, em Neápolis, Perto de Zarbo" respectively, matching the "Carta a/aos ..." pattern already used for `epistle-to-polycarp` ("Carta a Policarpo").
- 2026-09-14 — **review round 1** (3 parallel independent passes: structural/mechanical; monolingual Portuguese proofread + cross-sibling term check; sentence-by-sentence bilingual fidelity). Found and fixed seven genuine defects, none severe (no reversed meaning, no dropped content):
  - **Antiochians, Greeting** — "recebeu o nome de cristã" had substituted "Christian" for the source's literal "the name Christ" (an interpretive gloss toward the Acts 11:26 reference it cites). Reverted to the literal "recebeu o nome de Cristo", matching the source text as given rather than the translator's inference about what it means.
  - **Antiochians, Ch. 13** — "dignissimo" (missing accent) → "digníssimo".
  - **Hero, Ch. 7** — the bracketed editorial insertion "[to me]" had been dropped (translated as a plain, unbracketed clitic "me"). Fixed by bracketing the clitic itself: "que Deus [me] mostrou a teu respeito", preserving the bracket per the established convention.
  - **Philippians, Ch. 11** — the source's two separate bracketed insertions, "you [now] draw on [others]", had been merged into a single bracket "[agora outros]". Split back into two separate bracket pairs: "arrastas [agora] [outros] à impiedade".
  - **Philippians, Ch. 1** — the Ephesians 4:5-6 quotation had gained an extra clause, "que está **sobre todos**, e por todos, e em todos", not present in the English as given (the source quotes only "who is through all, and in all", two clauses, not the fuller three-clause Eph. 4:6 text). Removed "sobre todos" to match the source's own (partial) quotation exactly.
  - **First Epistle to St. John** — "our new religion and **repentance**" had been rendered "nossa nova religião e **conversão**". "Repentance" and "conversion" are related but not identical (μετάνοια vs. a broader turning); changed to "arrependimento" to match the English term given.
  - **Philippians, Ch. 4 and Ch. 5** — "mutilate and **explain away** the birth of the Virgin" / "why do you mutilate and **explain away** His being born" had both been narrowed to "**neguem**"/"**negas**" (deny outright), losing the specific nuance of "explain away" (offering a false alternative account, not flat denial). Changed both to "desvirtuem"/"desvirtuas" (distort/misrepresent), which carries the "give a false account of" sense "negar" lacks.
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 6** (readability, not a fidelity defect) — "Como poderia tal **ser ser** um mero homem" doubled the word "ser" (noun "being" + verb "to be") in a way that reads as a typo at first glance, even though each occurrence is grammatically distinct. Changed "tal ser" to "tal ente" to remove the collision without changing the meaning.
  - **Maria the Proselyte, Ch. 5** (readability) — smoothed the closing "para que eu esteja em saúde para com Deus" to "para que eu tenha saúde diante de Deus" for more natural Portuguese; not a fidelity defect (the source's own closing here is a prayer request, "Pray... that I may be in health", not the "Fare well" formula used elsewhere, so it was correctly *not* forced into the sibling "Passai bem" pattern).
  - **Considered and rejected as non-defects:**
    - **Anazarbus vs. Zarbus**: flagged as an internal inconsistency ("Anazarbo" in Hero Ch. 9 vs. "Zarbo" in Maria's letter and its reply's title), but the English source itself uses both spellings for what is presumably the same town ("near Anazarbus" once, "near Zarbus" twice) — already documented in the Key Terms table above as a deliberate preservation of the source's own inconsistency, not a translation error.
    - **Hero, Ch. 7** — "that God will show me, Hero, upon my throne" rendered "que Deus me mostrará a ti, Hero, sobre o meu trono" was flagged as a possible pronoun-direction reversal. The English sentence is itself elliptical/garbled (chapter heading: "Solemn charge to Hero, as future bishop of Antioch"); the only reading consistent with the chapter's stated subject — Ignatius foreseeing Hero as his successor on the episcopal throne — is "God will show me [that] you, Hero, [will be] upon my throne", which is what the Portuguese renders. Left unchanged.
    - **Hero, Ch. 7** — "Não sejas de ânimo dobre" (Tiago 1:6, 8) uses the archaic word "dobre" rather than a modern equivalent. This deliberately echoes the standard Almeida Portuguese Bible's own rendering of Tiago 1:8 ("o homem de coração dobre é inconstante"), which readers of a Catholic corpus quoting this exact verse are likely to recognize — kept as the recognizable biblical-register phrasing rather than modernized.
- 2026-09-14 — **review round 2** (2 independent passes: a fresh cold fidelity + grammar re-read; an adversarial re-verification pass with its own bracket/Scripture-reference/proper-name/number/chapter-title audits, not trusting round 1's counts). The adversarial pass came back fully clean (84/84 brackets matched per-line, ~128 Scripture citations independently re-extracted and verified, every proper name checked occurrence-by-occurrence, all numbers and all 55 non-Greeting chapter titles verified). The cold fidelity pass confirmed all seven round-1 fixes read correctly in context with no regressions, and found two further readability defects (not fidelity errors — the English sense was preserved, but the Portuguese punctuation made the sentence briefly misparseable):
  - **Antiochians, Ch. 11** — "Que ninguém **entregue** à ociosidade **coma**" was ambiguous on a first read: "entregue" parses as a present-subjunctive verb ("hand over"), stranding "coma", before the intended reading (a participial phrase, "no one given to idleness") becomes clear. Reworded to "Que ninguém **dado** à ociosidade coma", removing the ambiguity without changing the meaning of "Let no one addicted to idleness eat."
  - **Philippians, Ch. 10** — "a quem as coisas que não são **são** reputadas como se fossem" had two adjacent instances of "são" collide with no punctuation between the end of the relative clause and the start of the main clause, reading like an accidental repetition. Added a comma: "a quem as coisas que não são, são reputadas como se fossem", matching the English's own clause boundary ("to whom the things that are not **are** reckoned as if they were").
  - **Considered and rejected as a non-defect**: Tarsians Ch. 6 renders the English's two identical occurrences of "such a[n] one" differently ("tal ser" the first time, "tal ente" the second, after the round-1 fix). Only the second occurrence had the "ser ser" collision the round-1 fix addressed; the first ("tal ser declarar") has no such collision and reads naturally as-is. Left unchanged — harmonizing the two would not fix a defect, since neither reading is wrong.
- 2026-09-14 — **review round 3** (2 independent passes: a full re-verification of all 11 previously-fixed spots plus a fresh cold read of three previously-unchecked sections — Hero Ch. 1-6, Mary at Neapolis, and the three shortest letters — which came back fully clean; and a fresh independent re-read of the three longest/most theologically dense letters — Tarsians, Antiochians, Philippians — plus a `book.json`/journal-accuracy audit and a mechanical formatting sweep, both of which also came back clean). The second pass found six further genuine defects in the three long letters, all fixed:
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 6** — the abridged John 17:5 quotation, "Glorify Me with Your glory which I had **before the world was**", had gained "junto de ti" ("with you") not present in the English as given — an unconscious fill-in from the familiar full Bible verse. Removed: "que eu tinha antes que o mundo existisse".
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 7** — the English telescopes 1 Cor. 15:13-14 into one sentence ("If the dead rise not, then is not Christ raised; our preaching therefore is vain..."); the Portuguese had re-inserted a second "if Christ has not risen" clause ("e, se Cristo não ressuscitou,") drawn from the familiar Almeida wording of v.14, not present in the ANF text as given. Fixed to "portanto, é vã a nossa pregação..." — a plain consequence, matching the source's own condensed structure.
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 8** — "Depondo, cada um de vós, toda malícia..." used a bare gerund where every surrounding sentence uses a true imperative ("Sede sóbrios", "revesti-vos"), reading as a dangling fragment rather than a command. Fixed to the imperative "Deponde".
  - **Antiochians, Ch. 3** — the ANF source deliberately reverses the standard Isaiah 53:7 word order ("led as a **sheep** to the slaughter; and as a **lamb** before her shearers is dumb" — sheep/slaughter, lamb/shearers, the opposite of the familiar KJV order); the Portuguese had swapped the two animal words back to the familiar order ("como um **cordeiro** ao matadouro... como a **ovelha** muda... perante os tosquiadores"). Fixed to match the source's own (reversed) order: "como uma **ovelha** ao matadouro; e como um **cordeiro** mudo diante dos... tosquiadores".
  - **Philippians, Ch. 11** — "prometes dar-lhe **o que é, na verdade, o que** já é seu" duplicated the relative pronoun ("o que... o que"), an ungrammatical construction on close reading. Fixed to "prometes dar-lhe **aquilo que**, na verdade, já é seu".
  - **Philippians, Ch. 13** — "**Cuidado**, vós, maridos, para que ameis..." used a bare interjection ("Watch out!") where the surrounding imperatives ("Sede sujeitos", "Amai-vos") call for a true command verb. Fixed to "**Cuidai**, vós, maridos...".
- 2026-09-14 — **review round 4** (2 independent passes, both re-verifying all 17 previously-fixed spots with no regressions found, then each doing a fresh adversarial full-file read). Found and fixed 5 further genuine defects — three of them the exact "corrected back to familiar Bible wording" pattern already named as a recurring risk after round 3, confirming it needed a dedicated re-check rather than being a one-off:
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 4** — the ANF source quotes 1 Timothy 2:5 with **singular** "between God and **man**" here (contrast the same letter's own later, correctly-plural citation of the same verse in the Antiochians epistle, Ch. 4: "between God and men") — a source-internal inconsistency across its two Timothy citations, not a typo. The Portuguese had normalized this instance to the familiar plural "os homens," erasing the distinction between the two citations. Fixed to singular "o homem," matching this specific citation as given.
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 4** — the same quotation's parallel "by Him ... and by Him all things consist" had been rendered inconsistently: "por ele foram criadas" (correct) but then "**nele** subsistem" (in Him, not by Him) for the second, identical English construction. Fixed to "por ele subsistem," matching the source's own repeated "by Him."
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 5** — the ANF quotation of 1 Corinthians 15:28 deliberately uses only the pronoun "**He**" ("then shall He also Himself be subject"), not "the Son" (unlike the standard KJV wording of this verse). The Portuguese had inserted "o próprio **Filho**," reverting to the familiar/expected wording instead of the pronoun-only phrasing actually given. Fixed to "ele mesmo," matching the source.
  - **Antiochians Ch. 4 and Philippians Ch. 3** — John 1:14 is quoted three times across this collection, and the ANF source itself alternates between "**became** flesh" (twice: Antiochians Ch. 4, Philippians Ch. 3) and the standard-KJV "**was made** flesh" (once: Philippians Ch. 5) — a deliberate source-internal variation. The Portuguese had normalized all three to the single phrase "(se) fez carne," erasing the "became flesh" divergence in two of the three places. Fixed the two "became flesh" instances to "**tornou-se** carne," leaving the Philippians Ch. 5 "was made flesh" instance as "se fez carne" (already correctly matching its own, standard source wording).
  - **Hero, Ch. 8** — "you shall lead **in and out** the people of the Lord" (a biblical shepherding idiom, cf. Numbers 27:17/Deuteronomy 31:2) had been rendered "tu conduzirás... o povo do Senhor," dropping "in and out" entirely — a content omission, not a wording choice. Fixed by adding the idiom back: "tu conduzirás, **entrando e saindo**,... o povo do Senhor."
- 2026-09-14 — **review round 5** (2 independent passes: a mechanical/structural re-verification — brackets, Scripture references, line/heading counts, proper-name spelling, plus a dedicated full re-read of the Antiochians and Philippians letters specifically, which have accumulated the most fixes — came back fully clean; a second, fully independent adversarial re-read targeting the recurring "corrected back to familiar Bible wording" defect class found 2 more instances of exactly that pattern). Fixed both:
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 6** — the ANF paraphrase of Proverbs 8:27 uses the singular "**the heaven**" ("When He made the heaven, I was present with Him"), deviating from the standard KJV plural "when he prepared the *heavens*." The Portuguese had "Quando fez **os céus**" (plural), reverting to the familiar wording. Fixed to singular: "Quando fez **o céu**".
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 7** — 1 Corinthians 6:9 is quoted with the modal "**can inherit**" ("nor thieves, can inherit the kingdom of God"), not the standard KJV's future "*shall* inherit." The Portuguese had "**herdarão** o reino de Deus" (future tense), reverting to the familiar wording. Fixed to preserve the modal: "**podem herdar** o reino de Deus".
  - **Considered and rejected as a non-defect**: "being one of the company of the senses" (Epistle to Mary at Neapolis, Ch. 1) is rendered "sendo um dos sentidos," dropping "company of." The sense (sight is one of the senses) is unchanged; this is a stylistic compression, not a fidelity error, and was left as-is.
  - Two consecutive review rounds are now needed to close this review (per the corpus's standing convention) — this round found genuine defects, so at least one more clean round is required.
- 2026-09-14 — **review round 6** (2 independent passes: a mechanical re-verification — brackets, all 118 Scripture references, a dedicated full re-read of Hero and Maria the Proselyte, and of the four shortest letters — came back fully clean, including an explicit spot-check confirming all 24 fixes from rounds 1-5 are present and correct; a second, independent exhaustive sweep of every Scripture quotation for ANF-vs-standard-wording deviations found 4 more instances of the same recurring pattern). Fixed all four:
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 3** — the ANF quotation of Romans 6:10 deliberately omits the KJV's opening "For" ("In that He died, He died unto sin once..."). The Portuguese had inserted "**Pois**" (For) at the start, reverting to the familiar standard-Bible wording. Removed: "Quanto a ter morrido, morreu de uma vez por todas...".
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 4** — the same Colossians 1:16-17 quotation already fixed once in this chapter (Ch. 6, round 5, "Quando fez o céu") uses the singular "**heaven**" a second time ("that are in heaven, and on earth"); this second occurrence had been left as the plural "nos **céus**." Fixed to singular: "que há **no céu** e na terra".
  - **Hero, Ch. 9** — 2 Timothy 1:18 is quoted with a deliberate God/Lord distinction ("**God** grant that they find mercy of the **Lord** in that day"); the Portuguese used "**o Senhor**" for both, erasing the distinction. Fixed to "conceda **Deus** que achem misericórdia da parte do Senhor naquele dia".
  - **Antiochians, Ch. 3** — Isaiah 7:14 is quoted here as "a virgin **shall be with Child**" (a state), distinct from the same document's other, later quotation of the same verse in Philippians Ch. 3 ("a virgin **shall conceive** in her womb," rendered "conceberá em seu ventre"). The Portuguese had used "**conceberá**" for this instance too, collapsing two deliberately different ANF wordings of the same verse into identical Portuguese. Fixed to "uma virgem **estará grávida**", distinguishing it from the Philippians instance.
- 2026-09-14 — **review round 7** (in progress: pass A re-verified all 4 round-6 fixes clean, then did an exhaustive quotation-by-quotation audit of every Scripture citation in the file and found one further defect):
  - **Tarsians, Ch. 6** — the abridged John 17:5 quotation, "Glorify Me with **Your** glory which I had before the world was", had dropped the possessive "Your" entirely (rendered "com a glória", not "com a tua glória") — a plain omission distinct from the round-3 fix to this same quotation (which removed an *added* phrase, "junto de ti"). Fixed to restore the possessive: "com a **tua** glória, que eu tinha antes que o mundo existisse".
  - **Pass B** (fresh adversarial read of all non-quoted prose, spot-check of 30+ bracket pairs for translation accuracy of their bracketed content, full re-check of all ~64 chapter titles and all 8 greetings, mechanical/book.json/journal consistency) found two more minor bracket-wording issues, both fixed:
    - **Philippians, Ch. 2** — "not unto one [person] having three names, nor into three [persons] who became incarnate" brackets the same word (person/persons) twice; the Portuguese had rendered the singular as "[ser]" (being) but the plural as "[pessoas]" (persons) — an inconsistent translation of the identical bracketed word in a precise Trinitarian passage where "pessoa" is the exact theological term being used. Fixed to "uma só [pessoa]", matching "[pessoas]" (with the required feminine agreement on "uma só").
    - **Tarsians, Ch. 9** — "those [who continue] in virginity... and the widows [that persevere] in gravity of behaviour" brackets two different English verbs; the Portuguese had rendered both as "[perseveram]", erasing the distinction. Fixed the first to "[permanecem]", leaving the second as "[que perseveram]".
    - Also re-confirmed (per round 1's original adjudication) that Hero Ch. 7's "a ti" — "que Deus me mostrará a ti, Hero, sobre o meu trono" — is a defensible, necessary interpretive reading of the English's own elliptical construction, not a fidelity defect; left unchanged.
- 2026-09-14 — **review round 8** (2 independent passes: a full re-verification of the three round-7 fixes plus a fresh exhaustive sentence-by-sentence read of the entire file; a full mechanical re-verification — brackets, all ~126 Scripture reference tokens, a dedicated re-read of Antiochians/Philippians and of Hero/Maria the Proselyte, plus a spot-check of all 31 documented fixes against the live file and a build/structural-integrity check). **Both passes came back CLEAN — first fully clean round.** Two marginal word choices ("libertino" for "whoremonger", "costela" for "side of Adam") were considered and confirmed as legitimate idiomatic renderings, not defects.
