# Translation Journal — Epistle to the Philadelphians (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (trans. Alexander Roberts and James Donaldson, Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 1; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Key Terms

| English | Portuguese | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| Epistle | Carta | consistent with the other translated Ignatius letters (`epistle-to-the-ephesians`, `epistle-to-the-romans`, `epistle-to-the-magnesians`, `epistle-to-the-trallians`, `epistle-to-polycarp`) |
| Philadelphians / Philadelphia | Filadélfios / Filadélfia | "Filadélfios" for the addressed church/people (matching the demonym pattern of the sibling titles "Carta aos Trálios" / "Carta aos Magnésios"), "Filadélfia" for the city — no prior occurrence of either found in the corpus, so this is the first-attested rendering |
| Theophorus | Teóforo | kept as Ignatius's proper epithet ("God-bearer"), not translated — matches prior Ignatius translations |
| bishop / presbytery / deacon | bispo / presbitério / diácono | consistent with prior Ignatius translations |
| fellow-servant(s) | conservo(s) | matches `epistle-to-the-ephesians` Ch. 2 and `epistle-to-the-magnesians` Ch. 4 ("conservo," from Latin *conservus*) |
| the prince of this world | o príncipe deste mundo | matches the established rendering in `epistle-to-the-trallians` Ch. 4 and `epistle-to-the-magnesians` Ch. 6 |
| in bonds / bound | em cadeias / preso | matches the established rendering in `epistle-to-the-trallians` and `epistle-to-the-ephesians` |
| Antioch in Syria | Antioquia da Síria | matches the established rendering in `epistle-to-polycarp` Ch. 7 |
| Troas | Trôade | the standard Portuguese historical name, per the `epistle-to-polycarp` journal's explicit decision, rather than the untranslated "Troas" |
| Smyrnæans (as a group being referred to, not the letter's own addressees) | os de Esmirna | rendered by city name, not the demonym "esmirniotas" — the convention documented in the `epistle-to-polycarp` and `epistle-to-the-trallians`/`epistle-to-the-magnesians` journals, applied here to "sent... by the Ephesians and Smyrnæans" |
| Ephesians (same clause) | os efésios | ordinary standard demonym, no collision risk — unlike "esmirniota" this is common usage, so left as the demonym rather than forced into the city-name pattern |
| Burrhus | Burrhus | kept unchanged, per the `epistle-to-the-ephesians` journal's explicit reasoning: a literal Portuguese calque ("Burro") reads as the common word for "donkey" |
| Rheus Agathopus | Rheus Agathopus | first appearance of this name anywhere in the corpus (it also occurs in the not-yet-translated `epistle-to-the-smyrnaeans`); kept unchanged rather than inventing an unattested Portuguese form ("Agátopo"/"Reio Agátopo"), following the same conservative approach `epistle-to-the-ephesians` took for its own once-named minor companions (Burrhus, Crocus, Euplus) |
| Philo the deacon, of Cilicia | Filo, o diácono, da Cilícia | "Cilícia" is the corpus's standard place-name form (cf. `butler-lives-of-saints`) |

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-03: Sixth pt-BR translation from Ignatius of Antioch (after *Epistle to Polycarp*, *Epistle to the Romans*, *Epistle to the Ephesians*, *Epistle to the Trallians*, and *Epistle to the Magnesians*). Register and diction kept consistent with those five: dignified, slightly formal modern Portuguese.
- 2026-09-03 — **address form**: this letter addresses the whole Philadelphian congregation throughout ("my brethren," "your safety," "your unity"), like the sibling congregational letters. Rendered with **plural "vós"** address throughout, no singular shifts.
- 2026-09-03: No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source — chapter headings are original to this edition (Roberts-Donaldson) and were translated as part of the text. No translator notes were needed.
- 2026-09-03: Bracketed editorial insertions from the source (Greeting: "[men]"; Ch. 3: "[of Christ.]"; Ch. 4: "[show forth]", "[the will of]"; Ch. 5: "[over you]", "[conscience]"; Ch. 9: "[above the former dispensation]"; Ch. 10: "[for you]", "[to the brethren there]") preserved as brackets in the translation, following the established convention. Checked each bracket boundary against the `epistle-to-the-trallians` pass-1 defect class (a bracket splitting a Portuguese contraction) — none of this letter's insertions land on a contraction.
- 2026-09-03: All three Scripture references (Galatians 1:1, Ch. 1; 2 Timothy 3:6, Ch. 2; John 3:8, Ch. 7) preserved with the same book/chapter/verse as the English source, book names localized to standard Portuguese Catholic usage (Galatians → Gálatas, 2 Timothy → 2 Timóteo, John → João), matching the localization already established across the sibling Ignatius letters.
- 2026-09-03 — **names**: see Key Terms above. Two names (Burrhus, Rheus Agathopus) kept in source form rather than given invented Portuguese equivalents, since no established Portuguese rendering exists for either and — for Burrhus specifically — a literal calque collides with an unrelated common word, the exact reasoning already logged for this name in the `epistle-to-the-ephesians` journal.
- 2026-09-03 — **Ch. 10 heading, "Congratulate the Antiochans"**: rendered "Felicitai os antioquenos", using the standard Portuguese demonym for Antioch ("antioqueno") rather than a periphrasis, matching the register of this book's other imperative headings (Ch. 2 "Mantende...", Ch. 6 "Não aceiteis...").
- 2026-09-03 — **Ch. 6, "neither of these"**: "if either of such persons do not speak concerning Jesus Christ" rendered "se nenhum dos dois falar" — the English "either... do not" is a distributive negative over the two persons just named (the circumcised Christian preacher and the uncircumcised Jewish-law preacher), so "nenhum dos dois" makes the distributive scope explicit rather than risk "nenhum dos dois" being misread as excluding one specific referent.
- 2026-09-03: `book.json` updated with the `pt-BR` language entry and TOC title `"pt-BR": "Carta aos Filadélfios"`; `name` and `author` given pt-BR entries ("Carta aos Filadélfios", "Inácio de Antioquia").
- 2026-09-03 — **review pass 1** (1 bracket-convention issue found): Ch. 1, the source's editorial insertion "[good]" ("the ministry which pertains to the common [good]") had been absorbed into the idiomatic Portuguese noun phrase with no bracket ("ao bem comum"), leaving the file with 13 bracket pairs against the source's 14. Fixed to "ao [bem] comum" — the supplied word is "bem" (Greek supplies "the common" only), and Portuguese noun-adjective order places it before "comum", so the bracket lands on the noun exactly as in the English. All other mechanical counts (12/12 headings, 3/3 Scripture references, 50/50 paragraphs) matched on this pass.
