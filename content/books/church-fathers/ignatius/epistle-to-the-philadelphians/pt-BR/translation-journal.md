# Translation Journal — Epistle to the Philadelphians (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (trans. Alexander Roberts and James Donaldson, Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 1; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Key Terms

| English | Portuguese | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| Epistle | Carta | consistent with the other translated Ignatius letters |
| Philadelphians / Philadelphia | Filadélfios / Filadélfia | "Filadélfios" for the addressed church/people (parallel to "Trálios" for Tralles, "Magnésios" for Magnesia — the established Greek-place-name-to-Portuguese-demonym pattern for these letters), "Filadélfia" for the city |
| Theophorus | Teóforo | kept as Ignatius's proper epithet ("God-bearer"), not translated — matches prior Ignatius translations |
| bishop / presbytery / deacon | bispo / presbitério / diácono | consistent with prior Ignatius translations |
| the prince of this world | o príncipe deste mundo | matches the documented convention from `epistle-to-the-trallians` |
| bound / bonds | acorrentado / cadeias | matches the documented convention from `epistle-to-the-trallians` |
| Smyrnæans | os de Esmirna | rendered by city name, not a demonym — matches the convention documented in `epistle-to-polycarp` and reused throughout the corpus (Trallians, Magnesians, Ephesians) |
| Philo, Rheus Agathopus, Burrhus | Filo, Reu Agatopo, Burrhus | "Burrhus" kept unchanged, NOT "Burro" — `epistle-to-the-ephesians` journal documents that "Burro" collides with the common Portuguese word for "donkey"; `epistle-to-the-magnesians` cites the same precedent for a different name. A first draft of this file used "Burro" and was corrected in review pass 1 (2026-09-03) |
| High Priest (typological, Ch. 9) | Sumo Sacerdote | distinct from "bispo" (the church office) — this chapter's "priests"/"High Priest" are the Old Testament typology, not Ignatius's usual ecclesial vocabulary, so rendered with the standard Portuguese biblical-typology terms (sacerdotes / Sumo Sacerdote) rather than reusing "bispo" |
| Fare well ... our common hope (closing) | Passai bem ... nossa esperança comum | exact phrase match with the closings of `epistle-to-the-ephesians` and `epistle-to-the-trallians` |

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-03: Sixth Ignatian letter translated (after Polycarp, Romans, Ephesians, Trallians, Magnesians), continuing the one-short-work-per-PR pattern for `content/books/church-fathers/`. Single chapter (Greeting + 11 chapters), ~1,580 words.
- **Address form**: this letter addresses the whole Philadelphian congregation throughout ("your bishop," "your unity," "if you are willing"), like Ephesians and Trallians. Rendered with **plural "vós"** address throughout, no singular shifts.
- No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source — chapter headings are original to this edition (Roberts-Donaldson) and were translated as part of the text. No translator notes were needed.
- Bracketed editorial insertions preserved as brackets in the translation, following the established convention. Where Portuguese word order moves the interpolated concept to a different position than the English (e.g. "the common **[good]**" → "ao **[bem]** comum", since Portuguese puts the noun before the adjective), the bracket was kept on the *word that corresponds to the interpolated concept*, not on the same linear position — per the lesson logged for `epistle-to-the-trallians` pass 1, no bracket boundary was allowed to cut through a Portuguese contraction (checked "[do coração]", "[aos irmãos de lá]" — both self-contained).
- Ch. 3: the source's stray period inside the closing bracket ("[of Christ.]") was normalized to "[de Cristo]" (period outside, matching every other bracket in this file and the sibling letters) — a source-formatting artifact, not a textual variant worth preserving.
- Ch. 9's "priests"/"High Priest" (Old Testament typology, the line of Abraham/Isaac/Jacob/prophets/apostles) rendered "sacerdotes"/"Sumo Sacerdote" rather than reusing "bispo" — Ignatius is explicitly contrasting the Jewish priesthood with the Gospel here, not describing the church's threefold ministry, so the corpus's usual bishop/presbyter/deacon vocabulary does not apply to this passage.
- Three Scripture references (Galatians 1:1, 2 Timothy 3:6, John 3:8) preserved with the same book/chapter/verse as the English source, book names localized to standard Portuguese usage (Galatians → Gálatas, 2 Timothy → 2 Timóteo, John → João).
- Proper names in Ch. 11 (Philo, Rheus Agathopus, Burrhus, Troas/Trôade) rendered in their standard Portuguese forms.
- 2026-09-03 — **review pass 1** (3 independent passes: structural, Portuguese proofread + cross-sibling, sentence-by-sentence fidelity). Structural pass clean (12/12 headings, 14/14 bracket pairs, 1/1 paragraph per section, book.json correct). Two genuine defects found and fixed:
  - Ch. 5 — "secure **your safety**" had become "assegurar a vossa **salvação**" (salvation), narrower and more theologically loaded than the English. The corpus already has this exact English word rendered correctly in `epistle-to-the-ephesians` ("established in safety" → "estabelecidos em segurança"). Fixed to "assegurar a vossa **segurança**".
  - Ch. 6 — "if **either** of such persons **do not** speak concerning Jesus Christ" (distributive: applies independently to whichever of the two kinds of teacher fails to speak of Christ) had become "se **nenhum** destes falar" (collective: only triggers if *neither* speaks). Fixed to "se **qualquer um** destes **não** falar", preserving the distributive sense.
  - Cross-sibling check caught the "Burro"/"Burrhus" defect described above (Ch. 11) — documented, not re-explained here.
  - **Lesson**: both fidelity defects were fluent, correctly-accented, grammatically sound Portuguese — exactly the class of defect this corpus's post-merge audits (Trallians, Magnesians) found mechanical/structural passes cannot catch. The sentence-by-sentence fidelity read is what caught them.
- 2026-09-03 — **review pass 2** (2 independent passes: a fresh sentence-by-sentence fidelity read, and an adversarial re-audit verifying pass 1's fixes plus a fresh proofread). The adversarial re-audit came back clean and confirmed all three pass-1 fixes correctly in place. The fresh fidelity read found two further defects:
  - Ch. 7 — "detects the secrets [of the heart]" (the Spirit perceives/discovers hidden things) had become "**revela** os segredos [do coração]" (discloses/makes known) — the wrong direction of the action: perceiving something hidden is not the same claim as publishing it. Fixed to "**descobre** os segredos [do coração]".
  - Ch. 11 — "a man of **reputation**" had become "homem de **boa** reputação", adding a word not in the English and not bracketed as an editorial insertion, unlike every other addition in this file. Fixed to "homem de reputação", matching the source exactly.
  - **Lesson**: two consecutive review rounds each independently found genuine defects the round before had not — this is the same pattern the Magnesians post-merge audit logged ("six passes of counting missed twelve defects that five passes of reading caught"). A round with defects does not mean the fixing round before it was careless; it means fidelity defects are sparse enough that no single reading is guaranteed to be exhaustive, which is why this corpus requires two *consecutive* clean rounds before treating a translation as settled, not just one.
- 2026-09-03 — **review pass 3** (2 independent passes: a fresh fidelity read, and a full re-verification of all five prior fixes plus an independent fresh read). The re-verification pass confirmed all five prior fixes correctly in place and found nothing new. The fresh fidelity read found one further defect:
  - Ch. 9 — "if you **believe in love**" (love as the object of belief, echoing "faith working through love") had become "se credes **com** amor" (believe *with/lovingly*, love as manner rather than object) — a preposition swap that changes what is being claimed about faith and love. Fixed to "se credes **no** amor".
  - Considered and correctly dismissed as a non-defect by the re-verification pass: Ch. 3's "mas **antes** uma pureza extraordinária" — "antes" is a standard Portuguese contrastive connector for "not X, but Y" and adds no propositional content beyond the English "but".
- 2026-09-03 — **review pass 4** (2 independent passes). One confirmed all six prior fixes in place and found nothing new — clean. The other raised two low-confidence items, both **considered and dismissed as non-defects**, same class as the Ch. 3 "antes" precedent above:
  - Ch. 6 — "da boca de um homem circuncidado" for "from a man who has been circumcised": "da boca de" (from the mouth of) is a standard Portuguese idiom for reported/spoken doctrine, adds no propositional content beyond identifying the speaker.
  - Ch. 8 — "por espírito de contenda" for "out of strife": "espírito de" elaborates "contenda" (strife) as a disposition without changing what is being asserted.
  Neither swaps a referent, inverts polarity, or changes what the sentence claims — both are idiomatic amplification within a translator's normal range, not the kind of unmarked-addition defect the brackets convention exists to prevent (contrast: Ch. 11's dismissed "boa reputação" in pass 2, which *did* add a distinct evaluative claim not in the source). Not fixed.
  - **Round adjudicated as clean** (first of the two consecutive clean rounds required to close this review).
