# Translation Journal — Epistle to the Smyrnæans (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (trans. Alexander Roberts and James Donaldson, Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 1; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Key Terms

| English | Portuguese | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| Epistle to the Smyrnæans | Carta aos Esmirneus | title uses the Portuguese demonym "Esmirneus" (as in the standard Vozes/Paulus "Padres Apostólicos" collection), consistent with the sibling title pattern "Carta aos Filadélfios"/"Carta aos Trálios"/"Carta aos Efésios"/"Carta aos Magnésios". This is distinct from the corpus's documented anti-"esmirniota" convention below — that convention governs *third-party* references to the Smyrnaean church from other letters, not this letter's own title, where it is the direct addressee |
| Smyrnæans (as a third party, e.g. the Ephesians/Smyrnæans who sent Burrhus, Ch. 12) | os de Esmirna | rendered by city name, not a demonym — matches the convention already documented in `epistle-to-polycarp`, `epistle-to-the-trallians`, `epistle-to-the-magnesians`, `epistle-to-the-philadelphians` |
| Theophorus | Teóforo | kept as Ignatius's proper epithet ("God-bearer"), not translated — matches prior Ignatius translations |
| bishop / presbytery / deacon | bispo / presbitério / diácono | consistent with prior Ignatius translations |
| fellow-servant | conservo | matches `epistle-to-the-ephesians`/`epistle-to-the-magnesians` |
| beasts in the shape of men / wild beasts | feras em forma de homens / feras selvagens | "feras" is the established corpus term for both figurative and literal beasts (cf. `epistle-to-the-romans` "Que eu goze das feras"); "selvagens" added here to carry the English's explicit "wild", since this letter (unlike Romans) uses the qualifier |
| the Catholic Church | a Igreja Católica | Ch. 8 — this is the earliest datable occurrence of the phrase "the Catholic Church" in Christian literature (Greek καθολικὴ ἐκκλησία); rendered with the standard, capitalized Portuguese theological term rather than a lower-case "igreja universal/geral" gloss, since the phrase is already a fixed proper term in modern Portuguese |
| love-feast (ἀγάπη) | *ágape* | Ch. 8 — the technical term for the early Christians' communal meal, kept as the Greek loanword (already naturalized in Portuguese theological usage) and italicized like the corpus's other inline Latin/Greek technical terms, rather than paraphrased as "banquete de amor" |
| Rheus Agathopus | Reu Agatopo | matches the established rendering in `epistle-to-the-philadelphians` (Philo and Rheus Agathopus appear in both letters) |
| Burrhus | Burrhus | kept unchanged — NOT "Burro", per the established corpus precedent (`epistle-to-the-ephesians`, `epistle-to-the-magnesians`, `epistle-to-the-philadelphians`): the literal Portuguese calque collides with the common word for "donkey" |
| Trôade (Troas) | Trôade | matches `epistle-to-the-philadelphians` |
| Daphnus, Eutecnus | Dafno, Eutecno | standard Portuguese -us → -o rendering of the Latin/Greek cognomen, consistent with `Basso`/`Apolônio`/`Onésimo`/`Políbio`/`Frontão` in sibling letters |
| Tavias | Tavias | kept in its source spelling — a personal name (head of a household saluted in Ch. 13) with no attested standard Portuguese form elsewhere in the corpus or in published Patristics; not altered rather than risk an invented diacritic |
| Alce | Alce | kept unchanged — a woman's name (feminine agreement confirmed by "minha bem-amada" in the same clause) |
| Fare well in the grace of God (closing) | Passai bem na graça de Deus | this letter's closing differs from the "Fare well ... our common hope" formula used in `epistle-to-the-ephesians`/`epistle-to-the-trallians`/`epistle-to-the-philadelphians` — rendered literally for its own (shorter) English wording rather than borrowed from the sibling formula |

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-03: Seventh Ignatian letter translated (after Polycarp, Romans, Ephesians, Trallians, Magnesians, Philadelphians), continuing the one-short-work-per-PR pattern for `content/books/church-fathers/`. Single chapter (Greeting + 13 numbered chapters), ~1,900 words. This is one of Ignatius's seven authentic epistles (the *Martyrdom* and *Spurious Epistles* files under the same author remain untranslated and out of scope for this PR).
- **Address form**: this letter addresses the whole Smyrnaean congregation throughout ("your bishop," "you," never a single named addressee), like Ephesians, Trallians, Magnesians, and Philadelphians. Rendered with **plural "vós"** address throughout, no singular shifts.
- No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source — chapter headings are original to this edition (Roberts-Donaldson) and were translated as part of the text. No translator notes were needed.
- Chapter headings translated as impersonal noun phrases, matching the source's editorial (not first-person) voice — per the lesson logged in `epistle-to-the-trallians` (Ch. 1 heading drifting into "vossa excelência") and `epistle-to-the-magnesians` (Ch. 14 heading drifting into "peço"): Ch. 5's "Their dangerous errors" and Ch. 10's "Acknowledgment of their kindness" are both rendered with explicit third-person "deles" ("Os erros perigosos deles", "Reconhecimento da bondade deles") rather than an ambiguous "seus/sua", which could otherwise misattach to the addressee congregation.
- Bracketed editorial insertions from the source (e.g. "[followers]", "[effecting]", "[in fact]", "[Christ]", "[of having deserved it]", "[now]", "[in reality]") preserved as brackets in the translation, following the established convention.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 1** (3 parallel passes: structural/mechanical, Portuguese proofread + cross-sibling term check, sentence-by-sentence fidelity). All three independently found genuine issues; no overlap. Four defects fixed:
  - Ch. 1 — "in order that all righteousness might be fulfilled **by Him**" had become "para que **nele** se cumprisse toda a justiça" (locative "in Him", passive), dropping Christ as the explicit agent and breaking the parallel with the surrounding list of things Christ truly *did*. Fixed to "para que **por ele** se cumprisse toda a justiça".
  - Ch. 4 — "whom you must **not only not receive**, but... **not even meet with**" had become the garbled "as quais não deveis **não somente** receber, mas... nem mesmo encontrar", where "não deveis" sitting between the two negatives scopes the outer negation over the whole clause and inverts the intended escalation. Fixed to "as quais **não só não** deveis receber, mas, se possível, nem sequer encontrar" — mirroring the already-correct "não só não... mas" pattern used in `epistle-to-the-magnesians` Ch. 1 ("não somente ser chamado cristão, mas sê-lo de fato").
  - Ch. 5 — bracket boundary defect: "[a uma verdadeira crença n]a paixão de Cristo" split the Portuguese contraction "na" (em+a) across the bracket, the same defect class the `epistle-to-the-trallians` journal documents from its own pass 1. Fixed by moving "a" outside the bracket: "retornem a [uma verdadeira crença na] paixão de Cristo".
  - Ch. 13 — "and all **by name**" had become "e a todos **nominalmente**", which in Portuguese skews toward "in name only / nominally" rather than "individually, by their own name" — a weaker/ambiguous match, and the corpus already has a settled rendering for this exact recurring Ignatian phrase (`epistle-to-polycarp`: "Saúdo a todos **pelo nome**"). Fixed to "e a todos **pelo nome**", matching the sibling convention.
- Five Scripture references (Romans 1:3, Matthew 3:15, Isaiah 5:26, Isaiah 49:22, Ch. 1; Romans 8:17, Philippians 4:13, Ch. 4; Matthew 19:12, Ch. 6) preserved with the same book/chapter/verse as the English source, book names localized to standard Portuguese usage (Romans → Romanos, Matthew → Mateus, Isaiah → Isaías, Philippians → Filipenses).
- Ch. 1's "Of this fruit we are by His divinely-blessed passion, that He might set up a standard..." is awkwardly constructed in the English source itself (a difficult Greek clause via Roberts-Donaldson). Rendered literally ("Deste fruto somos nós, por sua divinamente bendita paixão, para que ele erguesse um estandarte...") preserving the source's own syntax rather than smoothing it into clearer but less literal Portuguese — matching the corpus's precedent of preserving the author's/edition's own difficult constructions (cf. `epistle-to-the-magnesians` Ch. 7's kept triadic repetition).
- "standard" (Ch. 1, military/prophetic banner image from Isaiah) rendered "estandarte" rather than the more generic "sinal" or "bandeira", matching the register of the two Isaiah citations it carries.
- "incorporeal spirit" (Ch. 3, the Risen Christ's words to the disciples with Peter) rendered "espírito incorpóreo" — a direct, transparent cognate, no gloss needed.
- Straight quotation marks used for the two embedded quotations (Ch. 3's dominical saying, Ch. 6's citation of Matthew 19:12), matching the corpus convention already documented in `epistle-to-polycarp`/`epistle-to-the-romans`.
- Proper names in Ch. 10, 12, 13 (Philo, Rheus Agathopus, Burrhus, Trôade, Tavias, Alce, Daphnus, Eutecnus) rendered per the Key Terms table above.
