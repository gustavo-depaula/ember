# Translation Journal — Epistle to the Smyrnæans (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (trans. Alexander Roberts and James Donaldson, Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 1; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Key Terms

| English | Portuguese | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| Epistle to the Smyrnæans | Carta aos Esmirneus | title uses the Portuguese demonym "Esmirneus" (as in the standard Vozes/Paulus "Padres Apostólicos" collection), consistent with the sibling title pattern "Carta aos Filadélfios"/"Carta aos Trálios"/"Carta aos Efésios"/"Carta aos Magnésios". This is distinct from the corpus's documented anti-"esmirniota" convention below — that convention governs *third-party* references to the Smyrnaean church from other letters, not this letter's own title, where it is the direct addressee |
| Smyrnæans (as a third party, e.g. the Ephesians/Smyrnæans who sent Burrhus, Ch. 12) | os de Esmirna | rendered by city name, not a demonym — matches the convention already documented in `epistle-to-polycarp`, `epistle-to-the-trallians`, `epistle-to-the-magnesians`, `epistle-to-the-philadelphians` |
| Theophorus | Teóforo | kept as Ignatius's proper epithet ("God-bearer"), not translated — matches prior Ignatius translations |
| bishop / presbytery / deacon | bispo / presbitério / diácono | consistent with prior Ignatius translations |
| fellow-servant | conservo | matches `epistle-to-the-ephesians`/`epistle-to-the-magnesians` |
| beasts in the shape of men / wild beasts | feras em forma de homens / feras selvagens | "feras" is the established corpus term for both figurative and literal beasts (cf. `epistle-to-the-romans` "Que eu goze das feras"); "selvagens" added here to carry the English's explicit "wild", since this letter (unlike Romans) uses the qualifier |
| the Catholic Church | a Igreja Católica | Ch. 8 — this is the earliest datable occurrence of the phrase "the Catholic Church" in Christian literature (Greek καθολικὴ ἐκκλησία); rendered with the standard, capitalized Portuguese theological term rather than a lower-case "igreja universal/geral" gloss, since the phrase is already a fixed proper term in modern Portuguese |
| love-feast (ἀγάπη) | *ágape* | Ch. 8 — the technical term for the early Christians' communal meal, kept as the Greek loanword (already naturalized in Portuguese theological usage) and italicized like the corpus's other inline Latin/Greek technical terms, rather than paraphrased as "banquete de amor" |
| Rheus Agathopus | Reu Agatopo | matches the established rendering in `epistle-to-the-philadelphians` (Philo and Rheus Agathopus appear in both letters) |
| Burrhus | Burrhus | kept unchanged — NOT "Burro", per the established corpus precedent (`epistle-to-the-ephesians`, `epistle-to-the-magnesians`, `epistle-to-the-philadelphians`): the literal Portuguese calque collides with the common word for "donkey" |
| Trôade (Troas) | Trôade | matches `epistle-to-the-philadelphians` |
| Daphnus, Eutecnus | Dafno, Eutecno | standard Portuguese -us → -o rendering of the Latin/Greek cognomen, consistent with `Basso`/`Apolônio`/`Onésimo`/`Políbio`/`Frontão` in sibling letters |
| Tavias | Tavias | kept in its source spelling — a personal name (head of a household saluted in Ch. 13) with no attested standard Portuguese form elsewhere in the corpus or in published Patristics; not altered rather than risk an invented diacritic |
| Alce | Alce | kept unchanged — a woman's name (feminine agreement confirmed by "minha bem-amada" in the same clause) |
| Fare well in the grace of God (closing) | Passai bem na graça de Deus | this letter's closing differs from the "Fare well ... our common hope" formula used in `epistle-to-the-ephesians`/`epistle-to-the-trallians`/`epistle-to-the-philadelphians` — rendered literally for its own (shorter) English wording rather than borrowed from the sibling formula |

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-03: Seventh Ignatian letter translated (after Polycarp, Romans, Ephesians, Trallians, Magnesians, Philadelphians), continuing the one-short-work-per-PR pattern for `content/books/church-fathers/`. Single chapter (Greeting + 13 numbered chapters), ~1,900 words. This is one of Ignatius's seven authentic epistles (the *Martyrdom* and *Spurious Epistles* files under the same author remain untranslated and out of scope for this PR).
- **Address form**: this letter addresses the whole Smyrnaean congregation throughout ("your bishop," "you," never a single named addressee), like Ephesians, Trallians, Magnesians, and Philadelphians. Rendered with **plural "vós"** address throughout, no singular shifts.
- No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source — chapter headings are original to this edition (Roberts-Donaldson) and were translated as part of the text. No translator notes were needed.
- Chapter headings translated as impersonal noun phrases, matching the source's editorial (not first-person) voice — per the lesson logged in `epistle-to-the-trallians` (Ch. 1 heading drifting into "vossa excelência") and `epistle-to-the-magnesians` (Ch. 14 heading drifting into "peço"): Ch. 5's "Their dangerous errors" and Ch. 10's "Acknowledgment of their kindness" are both rendered with explicit third-person "deles" ("Os erros perigosos deles", "Reconhecimento da bondade deles") rather than an ambiguous "seus/sua", which could otherwise misattach to the addressee congregation.
- Bracketed editorial insertions from the source (e.g. "[followers]", "[effecting]", "[in fact]", "[Christ]", "[of having deserved it]", "[now]", "[in reality]") preserved as brackets in the translation, following the established convention.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 1** (3 parallel passes: structural/mechanical, Portuguese proofread + cross-sibling term check, sentence-by-sentence fidelity). All three independently found genuine issues; no overlap. Four defects fixed:
  - Ch. 1 — "in order that all righteousness might be fulfilled **by Him**" had become "para que **nele** se cumprisse toda a justiça" (locative "in Him", passive), dropping Christ as the explicit agent and breaking the parallel with the surrounding list of things Christ truly *did*. Fixed to "para que **por ele** se cumprisse toda a justiça".
  - Ch. 4 — "whom you must **not only not receive**, but... **not even meet with**" had become the garbled "as quais não deveis **não somente** receber, mas... nem mesmo encontrar", where "não deveis" sitting between the two negatives scopes the outer negation over the whole clause and inverts the intended escalation. Fixed to "as quais **não só não** deveis receber, mas, se possível, nem sequer encontrar" — mirroring the already-correct "não só não... mas" pattern used in `epistle-to-the-magnesians` Ch. 1 ("não somente ser chamado cristão, mas sê-lo de fato").
  - Ch. 5 — bracket boundary defect: "[a uma verdadeira crença n]a paixão de Cristo" split the Portuguese contraction "na" (em+a) across the bracket, the same defect class the `epistle-to-the-trallians` journal documents from its own pass 1. Fixed by moving "a" outside the bracket: "retornem a [uma verdadeira crença na] paixão de Cristo".
  - Ch. 13 — "and all **by name**" had become "e a todos **nominalmente**", which in Portuguese skews toward "in name only / nominally" rather than "individually, by their own name" — a weaker/ambiguous match, and the corpus already has a settled rendering for this exact recurring Ignatian phrase (`epistle-to-polycarp`: "Saúdo a todos **pelo nome**"). Fixed to "e a todos **pelo nome**", matching the sibling convention.
- Seven Scripture references (Romans 1:3, Matthew 3:15, Isaiah 5:26, Isaiah 49:22, Ch. 1; Romans 8:17, Philippians 4:13, Ch. 4; Matthew 19:12, Ch. 6) preserved with the same book/chapter/verse as the English source, book names localized to standard Portuguese usage (Romans → Romanos, Matthew → Mateus, Isaiah → Isaías, Philippians → Filipenses).
- Ch. 1's "Of this fruit we are by His divinely-blessed passion, that He might set up a standard..." is awkwardly constructed in the English source itself (a difficult Greek clause via Roberts-Donaldson). Rendered literally ("Deste fruto somos nós, por sua divinamente bendita paixão, para que ele erguesse um estandarte...") preserving the source's own syntax rather than smoothing it into clearer but less literal Portuguese — matching the corpus's precedent of preserving the author's/edition's own difficult constructions (cf. `epistle-to-the-magnesians` Ch. 7's kept triadic repetition).
- "standard" (Ch. 1, military/prophetic banner image from Isaiah) rendered "estandarte" rather than the more generic "sinal" or "bandeira", matching the register of the two Isaiah citations it carries.
- "incorporeal spirit" (Ch. 3, the Risen Christ's words to the disciples with Peter) rendered "espírito incorpóreo" — a direct, transparent cognate, no gloss needed.
- Straight quotation marks used for the two embedded quotations (Ch. 3's dominical saying, Ch. 6's citation of Matthew 19:12), matching the corpus convention already documented in `epistle-to-polycarp`/`epistle-to-the-romans`.
- Proper names in Ch. 10, 12, 13 (Philo, Rheus Agathopus, Burrhus, Trôade, Tavias, Alce, Daphnus, Eutecnus) rendered per the Key Terms table above.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 2**: clean. Two independent passes — a fresh sentence-by-sentence fidelity read (done cold, before consulting this journal) and an adversarial re-verification of all four round-1 fixes plus a full independent mechanical/proofread/cross-sibling sweep. Both found nothing new; the re-verification confirmed all four round-1 fixes read correctly in their full sentences. One borderline lexical observation (Ch. 8's "proper Eucharist" and the later "valid" both rendering as "válido/válida") was considered by pass A and correctly judged not a defect — same sense of legitimacy in both places, no propositional-content change. First of the two consecutive clean rounds required to close this review.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 3** (final confirmation): clean. Two independent passes — a fresh cold fidelity + Portuguese-grammar read (before consulting this journal), and a full re-verification of all four round-1 fixes plus a grep-checked audit of every Key Terms/journal claim (confirmed "Igreja Católica" capitalized in Ch. 8, *ágape* italicized in Ch. 8, "os de Esmirna" genuinely absent from this file since it is the direct addressee rather than a third-party reference) plus its own independent clause-by-clause re-read of the whole chapter. Both clean, both explicitly confirming the four fixes read correctly in context. **Second consecutive clean round — review closed.** Three review rounds total (7 independent passes), four genuine fidelity/mechanical defects found and fixed in round 1, none in rounds 2–3.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 4** (post-merge audit of PR #383, 5 independent passes across two sub-rounds: mechanical/structural + cross-sibling, Portuguese proofread, sentence-by-sentence fidelity; then a cold fidelity + grammar re-read and an archaism adjudication + journal-claim verification sweep). Rounds 1–3 had closed the review, but this audit found **three** further genuine defects, all fixed. Lesson for future reviews: two consecutive clean rounds is a useful stopping heuristic, not a proof of correctness — the three defects below all survived seven prior passes, and each was caught only by a pass with a *specific* remit (appositive punctuation; English-archaism idiom; adverb-level completeness) rather than by another general re-read. Prefer varying each round's remit over repeating the same broad sweep.
  - Ch. 1 — missing appositive comma: "sob Pôncio Pilatos e Herodes **o tetrarca**" needed the comma pair standard for a descriptive appositive in Portuguese (cf. "Herodes, o Grande"). This was also the one place in the chapter where an appositive was not comma-set — every other one already was ("nosso Salvador Jesus Cristo, que sofreu...", "Jesus Cristo, nossa esperança perfeita,", "os vossos diáconos, meus conservos,", "Alce, minha bem-amada,"). Fixed to "sob Pôncio Pilatos e Herodes, o tetrarca,".
  - Ch. 5 — dropped adverb: "For they think **also** the same thing regarding us" had become "Pois pensam o mesmo a nosso respeito", losing the reciprocity the English marks (these people are *also* subject to the same suspicion they direct at others). Fixed to "Pois **também** pensam o mesmo a nosso respeito".
  - Ch. 5 — English-archaism adjudication: "that He was [truly] **possessed of** a body?" had been rendered "que ele estava [verdadeiramente] **possuído de** um corpo?", a passive construction. "Estar possuído de X" is genuine Portuguese idiom, but corpus-wide (`liguori-meditacoes`, `morrow-my-catholic-faith`, `bonne-presse-catechism-in-pictures`) it is used only for (a) an abstract/emotional state overwhelming someone ("possuído de amor ardente", "possuído de tal dor") or (b) literal demonic possession ("uma mulher possuída de um espírito"). "Um corpo" is neither, so the phrase doesn't fall into either idiomatic reading a native speaker would reach for — worse, its surface form pattern-matches sense (b), inviting a passing misreading of "possessed BY a body" (demonic-possession-shaped), which is backward from and undercuts the anti-Docetic point of the clause (that Christ truly had a body). It was also an unexplained inconsistency against this same file's own Ch. 3, which renders the identical English idiom ("possessed of flesh" ×2) actively as "possuía carne" / "como quem possuía carne" — the correct, corpus-consistent pattern (cf. `epistle-to-the-trallians` "possuís uma mente", `epistle-to-the-ephesians` "possui o amor", `epistle-to-the-philadelphians` "o Evangelho possui algo transcendente" — every other "possess X [concrete/abstract non-emotional noun]" in the Ignatian corpus is active). Fixed to "não confessando que ele [verdadeiramente] possuía um corpo?", matching Ch. 3's own construction.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 5** (3 passes with deliberately narrowed, non-overlapping remits, following round 4's lesson: word-level completeness accounting; punctuation + English-archaism audit; adversarial re-verification of round 4's fixes + full mechanical sweep). The verification pass confirmed all three round-4 fixes read correctly and found the whole file mechanically clean. The two narrow-remit passes found **four** further defects, all fixed:
  - Ch. 2 — unpaired parenthetical comma: the English brackets "not, **as certain unbelievers maintain,** that He only seemed to suffer" with commas on both sides; the Portuguese had only the closing one ("não como certos incrédulos afirmam, que..."), so the clause reads as a run-on "not as unbelievers claim" and the parenthetical insertion is lost. Fixed to "não, como certos incrédulos afirmam, que ele apenas pareceu sofrer".
  - Ch. 3 — unwarranted adverb: "and I believe that He is so **now**" (English drops the "still" it used in the preceding clause) had become "e creio que **ainda** é assim agora", adding an "ainda" with no source warrant and redundant against "agora". Fixed to "e creio que é assim agora"; the first "ainda" correctly renders the earlier "still" and stays.
  - Ch. 3 — archaic "found": "they despised death, and **were found** its conquerors" (archaic "found" = *proved to be*, an objective outcome) had become "foram **considerados** seus vencedores", which makes it other people's opinion rather than a demonstrated fact. Fixed to "foram **achados** seus vencedores" — the biblical-register Portuguese passive for "were found" (cf. Almeida's "foi achado"), which keeps both the passive voice and the objective sense.
  - Ch. 11 — "to be **styled from thence**" (= to bear the name of belonging to Antioch) had become "ser **chamado dali**", but "chamar ... de [lugar]" in Portuguese means to *summon* someone from somewhere, so the dominant reading is "not worthy to be summoned from there" — nonsense in context. Fixed to "ser chamado **natural** dali", keeping "chamado" for "styled" and disambiguating with "natural de", the standard Portuguese for "native of".
- Two findings from round 5 were considered and **rejected** as over-corrections, recorded here so later rounds do not re-litigate them:
  - Ch. 4 — "provided **only** he be so in the name of Jesus Christ" → "contanto que o seja em nome de Jesus Cristo". "Contanto que" already carries the sole-condition sense of "provided only"; both proposed insertions ("contanto que **apenas** o seja", "o seja **somente** em nome de") would attach the adverb to "in the name of Jesus Christ" and change the meaning to "*only* in the name of Jesus Christ". Left as is.
  - Ch. 10 — "None of these things shall be lost to you" → "Nenhuma destas coisas se perderá para vós". The proposed "Nada disso ficará sem recompensa" reads the Matt 10:42 / Heb 6:10 reward-echo into the text, but the English source says only "be lost to you"; the existing rendering is exactly as literal as the source it translates. Interpreting the echo would be a translation choice the en-US edition itself declined to make. Left as is.
  - Also fixed: the round-1 entry above said "Five Scripture references" while listing (correctly) seven. Corrected to "Seven".
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 6** (3 passes: adversarial re-verification of round 5's four fixes; cold full fidelity + grammar read; register + cross-sibling terminology audit). The cold read and the register/terminology audit were both clean — the latter confirming vós address with no tu/você slips anywhere, and agreement with the sibling corpus on Eucaristia, bispo/presbitério/diáconos, feras selvagens, conservos, reconfortar, digníssimo, Saúdo, and the "Passai bem" closing. The verification pass confirmed three of round 5's four fixes and found one convention defect in the fourth:
  - Ch. 3 — round 5 had fixed "were found its conquerors" to "foram **achados** seus vencedores". "Achado" is defensible biblical-register Portuguese for this idiom, but the Ignatian sub-corpus has a dominant convention for it: nine instances of "encontrado" (`epistle-to-polycarp` "seja encontrado desertor"/"seja encontrado discípulo"; `epistle-to-the-ephesians` "sejamos encontrados em Cristo Jesus"/"seja encontrado no rol dos cristãos"/"seja encontrado perseverando"; `epistle-to-the-romans` "seja encontrado como tal"/"seja encontrado o pão puro"/"seja encontrado um sacrifício"; `epistle-to-the-trallians` "seja encontrado réprobo") against a single "achados" (`epistle-to-the-magnesians` "sejamos achados discípulos"). Changed to "foram **encontrados** seus vencedores" to follow the 9:1 majority. Noting the Magnesians instance explicitly: the corpus is *not* unanimous here, so this is a majority-convention alignment, not the correction of a unique outlier.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 7**: clean. Two independent passes — an adversarial verification of round 6's "encontrados" change plus a full mechanical re-check and grep-audit of every round 4/5/6 journal claim, and a cold Brazilian-Portuguese proofread that quoted back in full every sentence in the four edited chapters (2, 3, 5, 11) to confirm nothing had been broken in the editing. Both clean; the verification pass additionally found an exact-structure precedent for the new wording in `epistle-to-the-romans` ("para que seja encontrado o pão puro de Cristo" — "ser encontrado" taking a determined noun phrase as predicate, the same slot "seus vencedores" occupies). First of the two consecutive clean rounds.
- 2026-09-03 — **review round 8** (final confirmation): clean. Two independent passes with fresh remits — a cold clause-by-clause fidelity read weighted toward Chapters 6–13 (the least-edited stretch), and a narrow theological-precision + proper-name audit checking every doctrinally loaded clause of this anti-Docetic letter for hedging, over-assertion, or a shift of agent, and every proper name character by character against its other occurrences and against the sibling letters. Both clean. **Second consecutive clean round — review closed.**
- One observation from round 8 was considered and not treated as a defect: Ch. 7's "which the Father, of His goodness, **raised up again**" renders as "que o Pai, por sua bondade, ressuscitou", with no separate word for "again". Portuguese "ressuscitar" is lexically "to raise again / restore to life", so the "again" is absorbed into the verb rather than dropped — the same conclusion round 5's word-level completeness pass reached for "rise again" in the preceding sentence.
- **Audit summary (rounds 4–8, post-merge review of PR #383)**: 5 rounds, 12 independent passes, **8 defects found and fixed** in a chapter that three prior rounds and seven passes had already declared clean and closed — a missing appositive comma and an unpaired parenthetical comma (Ch. 1, Ch. 2), a dropped "also" and an unwarranted "ainda" (Ch. 5, Ch. 3), a passive "possuído de" that inverted the anti-Docetic sense (Ch. 5), an archaic "found" flattened into "considerados" (Ch. 3), "chamado dali" reading as "summoned from there" (Ch. 11), and a stale "Five"/seven miscount in this journal. Four further candidate findings were examined and rejected as over-corrections, each recorded above with its reasoning so later rounds need not re-litigate them. The durable lesson, restated: consecutive clean rounds measure only that the *same kind* of look has stopped finding things. Every defect above was caught by a pass with a specific, narrow remit — punctuation only, word-level accounting only, archaism adjudication only — and none by another broad re-read. Vary the remit each round; when the remits stop being new, the clean rounds stop being evidence.
