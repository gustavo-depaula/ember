## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-05: Eighth pt-BR translation from the Ignatius of Antioch corpus, and the last piece needed to complete his full works in pt-BR (all seven epistles were already translated; only this narrative and the Spurious Epistles remained untranslated). Reused all terms already established in `ignatius/epistle-to-polycarp/pt-BR` and the other epistle journals rather than re-deriving them.
- 2026-09-05: This is a third-person martyrdom narrative (not a letter), written by companions of Ignatius who shift into first-person plural ("we") from Chapter 5 onward as eyewitnesses of the voyage to Rome and the vision after his death. Rendered "we" as **"nós"/verb in the first-person plural** throughout that span, distinct from the address forms (tu/vós) used in the epistles.
- 2026-09-05: Editorial brackets from the source (e.g. "[constant ]", "[Holy ]") were carried over into the translation but the stray trailing space before the closing bracket — an artifact of the source's OCR/editing, not meaningful content — was silently dropped (e.g. "[constante]", "[Sagradas]").
- 2026-09-05: Scripture references preserved with the same book/chapter/verse numbers, book names localized to standard Portuguese Catholic usage: 2 Corinthians 6:16 → 2 Coríntios 6:16; Acts 28:13-14 → Atos 28:13-14; Proverbs 10:24 → Provérbios 10:24.
- 2026-09-05: No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source (only inline editorial brackets, treated as above) — no translator notes were needed.
- 2026-09-05: The dialogue between Trajan and Ignatius (Ch. 2) uses "tu" throughout for both speakers, matching the direct, personal register of an interrogation exchange (not the tu/vós shift pattern used for Ignatius addressing a congregation in his own letters).
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 1**: exhaustive chapter-by-chapter audit (completeness, structure, proper names, Scripture references, the Trajan/Ignatius dialogue line by line, diacritics, markdown format, footnotes). Clean — no issues found.
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 2**: independent line-by-line pass found and fixed three defects:
  - Ch. 5: the bracketed insertion "[as we did]" (qualifying "mourning") had been dropped entirely — added back as "[como nós próprios fazíamos]".
  - Ch. 2: "hurried away" (of Ignatius being taken off to the ship) had been rendered *arrastado* ("dragged along the ground"), which both overstates the English and clashes with the ram/flock simile that follows (a ram is led, not dragged) — changed to *levado às pressas*, matching the same English verb's rendering in Ch. 5 ("apressados").
  - Ch. 1: "he resisted the flood" had been rendered with the preterite *resistiu*, breaking the imperfect-tense aspect used throughout the rest of the sentence for this same durative, habitual resistance (*governava*, *alegrava-se*, *afligia-se*) — changed to *resistia*.
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 3**: independent re-verification of the three round-2 fixes (all correct and natural in context) plus a fresh exhaustive pass (exact bracket-count check, all proper names, the full Trajan/Ignatius dialogue, Scripture references, Ch. 7 dates/numbers, further tense/aspect scan, diacritics). Clean — no issues found.
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 4**: found and fixed two defects:
  - Ch. 2: the 2 Corinthians 6:16 quote ("I will dwell in them, and walk in them") repeats "in them" identically in the English — the deliberate repetition is the point of the exchange (Ignatius answering "do you carry within you Him that was crucified?"). The translation had shifted the second occurrence to *entre eles* ("among them"), breaking the parallel — changed to *neles* both times to match the English's exact repetition.
  - Ch. 2: English italicizes "*we*" for contrastive emphasis in Trajan's question ("Do *we* not then seem to you to have the gods in our mind..." — the only italic-emphasis markup in the whole work). The Portuguese, being pro-drop, had no subject pronoun at all to carry that stress — added an explicit *nós* and kept the italics: "que *nós* temos os deuses em nossa mente".
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 5**: independent re-verification of both round-4 fixes (both correct and natural in context) plus a fresh exhaustive pass: full sentence-by-sentence comparison of all 7 chapters, exact bracket-inventory match (all 28 bracketed insertions present in the same order in both languages), all proper names/places/titles, the full Trajan/Ignatius dialogue question by question, all three Scripture references (placement and internal consistency), a full tense/aspect scan, and quote-style/heading structure (confirmed straight `"..."` quotes are the established pt-BR convention across the Ignatius corpus, not a defect). Clean — no issues found.
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 6**: found and fixed one defect — Ch. 7: "we have made known to you both the day and the time" had rendered "the time" as *a hora* ("the hour"), but no hour-of-day is ever given anywhere in the narrative; the same word "time" recurs one clause later ("according to the time of his martyrdom," i.e. the anniversary/season) referring to date, not clock time — and immediately follows the consular-year dating given in the prior sentence. *A hora* introduced a false implication and was inconsistent with the second occurrence's *o tempo* — changed to *a época* to match the "date/occasion" sense used consistently in both places.
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 7**: independent re-verification of the round-6 fix (correct and natural in context) plus a fresh exhaustive pass: hand-counted bracket inventory by chapter (4+11+2+1+5+3+2 = 28, matching in both languages), all proper names, the full Trajan/Ignatius dialogue, all three Scripture references, tense/mood/aspect, diacritics, markdown structure. Clean — no issues found. (First clean round after round 6's fix; a further clean round is still needed to reach two *consecutive* clean rounds.)
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 8**: independent full re-audit (bracket inventory, proper names, dialogue, Scripture references, tense/aspect, diacritics, markdown structure). Clean — no issues found. **Second consecutive clean round (7, 8) — review closed.**
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 9 (post-merge audit)**: re-opened after merge, three independent passes over the chapter text (Ch. 1–2, Ch. 3–5, Ch. 6–7) plus a manifest check. The translation text came back clean on all three. One defect was found outside the chapter text, in this journal's own Key Terms table: the Cristóforo row described the naming pattern as `"-phorus → -fóforo"`, which would yield *Cristofóforo*; the pattern actually applied (and correct) is `"-phorus → -óforo"`. Fixed.
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 10**: found and fixed one defect — Ch. 2 opened with *Ora, Trajano* for English "For Trajan, in the ninth year of his reign". This file establishes two distinct connective mappings and holds to them everywhere else: "Wherefore" → *Por isso* (6/6) and "For" → *Pois*, including twice inside this same chapter ("for all evil spirits have departed" → *pois todos os espíritos maus se afastaram*; "For there is but one God" → *Pois há um só Deus*) and in Ch. 1 ("For he inwardly reflected" → *Pois refletia interiormente*); *Ora* is reserved for the narrative "Now" (Ch. 6 "Now he enjoined" → *Ora, ele ordenou*; Ch. 7 "Now these things took place" → *Ora, estas coisas se deram*). The chapter-opening "For" is explanatory — it introduces *how* Ignatius attained the desire Ch. 1 closes on — so *Ora* borrowed the word this file had already assigned to a different English connective. Counting sentence-initial connectives made the mismatch mechanical: en-US has 5 "For" and 2 "Now"; pt-BR had 4 *Pois* and 3 *Ora*. Changed to *Pois Trajano*, restoring 5/5 and 2/2 parity.
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 11**: independent re-verification of the round-10 fix plus a fresh full pass. The connective audit was redone occurrence-by-occurrence rather than by totals — all 13 sentence-initial connectives now align one-to-one and in order (Wherefore→*Por isso* 6/6, For→*Pois* 5/5, Now→*Ora* 2/2), including the two easiest to miss: the "For" inside Ignatius's reply to Trajan in Ch. 2 ("For there is but one God" → *Pois há um só Deus*), which sits in quoted speech rather than narration, and the one closing Ch. 6 ("For only the harder portions of his holy remains were left" → *Pois só restaram as partes mais duras*). Also re-checked the bracket inventory (28, per-chapter 4+11+2+1+5+3+2, counted programmatically in both files), the voyage geography, the person shifts, all three Scripture references, Ch. 7 dates and consuls, proper names, tense/aspect, diacritics, markdown structure, and the manifest; plus a factual audit of every checkable claim in this journal against the current file contents. Clean — no issues found. (First clean round after round 10's fix.)
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 12**: split into a full mechanical pass (clean) and a focused semantic pass over archaic ANF vocabulary and pronoun antecedents. The semantic pass found one defect that eleven prior mechanical rounds had missed — Ch. 7: "while others beheld him **again** praying for us" had been rendered *enquanto outros o viram **de novo** orando por nós*. The sentence enumerates three groups seeing three different things in one simultaneous vision during a single brief sleep ("some of us saw... while others beheld... and others still saw..."), and "again" here is the archaic English enumerative idiom *others again* = "others, in their turn" — not a repetition in time. Nothing earlier in the passage has Ignatius praying for them, so there is no prior event for *de novo* ("once more") to point back to; it asserted a repetition the source does not. Changed to *enquanto outros, por sua vez, o viram orando por nós*, which also restores the three-way parallel with *alguns* … *outros, por sua vez* … *outros ainda*.
  - Vocabulary confirmed correct in round 12 (recorded so later rounds need not re-litigate): "wanting to complete" → *ainda faltava* (archaic "wanting" = lacking); "apt to suffer" → *propensos a*; "dropped spiritual grace" → *destilavam* (archaic "dropped" = distilled); "unhallowed sports" → *jogos ímpios*; "settled … who were unwilling" → *tranquilizado … que se opunham*; "the day and the time" → *o dia … a época*.
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 13**: the chapter text came back clean on both a full mechanical pass and a second focused semantic pass (which also confirmed the round-12 fix reads naturally and completes the *alguns / outros, por sua vez / outros ainda* parallel). One defect was found in this journal rather than in the translation: the round-11 entry attributed the Ch. 2 quoted "For there is but one God" to Trajan, but it is spoken by Ignatius, in his reply to Trajan. Corrected. **Note for future rounds:** each round's own journal entry becomes text the next round audits, so entries should stay short and stick to claims that can be checked against the files.
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 14**: full mechanical pass clean. The semantic pass raised one candidate and it was **considered and rejected** — recorded here so later rounds do not re-litigate it. Ch. 2, Ignatius's third reply: "under the feet of those who carry **Him** in their heart" → *sob os pés dos que **o** carregam em seu coração*. The English disambiguates by capitalizing "Him" (Christ); Portuguese has no such cue here, and the clitic's nearest masculine noun is *diabo*. Left unchanged, for three reasons: the devil reading is incoherent in context (Christ casts the devil's malice down beneath these people's feet, so they cannot be the devil's carriers); the phrase directly echoes the Teóforo definition given twice earlier in the same chapter (*Aquele que tem Cristo dentro do seu peito*, *tendo Cristo, o Rei do céu, [dentro de mim]*), which fixes the referent; and every available fix costs more than it buys — reverential capitalization (*O carregam*) appears nowhere in this corpus, and naming Christ outright adds a noun the source deliberately leaves as a pronoun. Four earlier passes had independently read this clitic as Christ.
- 2026-09-05 — **review round 15**: mechanical pass clean; semantic pass found and fixed one defect — Ch. 4: "to give a recompense to the Churches, who came to meet him through their **rulers**" had been rendered *por meio dos seus **governantes***. The referent is fixed by Ch. 3 one paragraph earlier, where the Asian churches welcome Ignatius "through their bishops, and presbyters, and deacons" — so "rulers" here means the churches' own clergy. Portuguese *governante* denotes a civil or political ruler, and in a narrative whose other ruler is Trajan that misdirects the referent outright; it was also a hapax in this corpus, with no sibling using it for churchmen. Changed to *por meio dos que as governavam*, which keeps the English's generic "those who rule them" without naming an office the source does not name, and reuses the *governar* root the text already applies to church governance in Ch. 1 (*governava a Igreja dos antioquenos*).
