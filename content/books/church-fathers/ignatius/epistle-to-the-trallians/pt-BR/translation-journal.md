# Translation Journal — Epistle to the Trallians (pt-BR)

Source: en-US (trans. Alexander Roberts and James Donaldson, Ante-Nicene Fathers, Vol. 1; via New Advent)
Target: pt-BR

## Key Terms

| English | Portuguese | Notes |
|---------|------------|-------|
| Epistle | Carta | consistent with the other translated Ignatius letters (`epistle-to-the-ephesians`, `epistle-to-the-romans`, `epistle-to-polycarp`) |
| Trallians / Tralles | Trálios / Trales | "Trálios" for the addressed church/people, "Trales" for the city — the standard Portuguese Patristics naming (cf. the Vozes/Paulus "Padres Apostólicos" collection), matching the sibling titles "Carta aos Efésios" / "Carta aos Romanos" already in the corpus |
| Theophorus | Teóforo | kept as Ignatius's proper epithet ("God-bearer"), not translated — matches prior Ignatius translations |
| Polybius | Políbio | bishop of Tralles who visited Ignatius at Smyrna; standard Portuguese form of the Greek name |
| bishop / presbytery / deacon | bispo / presbitério / diácono | consistent with prior Ignatius translations |
| sanhedrim | sinédrio | "the sanhedrim of God" (Ch. 3, describing the presbyters) → "o sinédrio de Deus", the standard Portuguese term for the Jewish council, used here as Ignatius's metaphor |
| Docetae | docetas | Ch. 11 heading; the heresy that Christ only seemed to suffer — standard Portuguese theological term |
| Smyrnæans | os de Esmirna | rendered by city name, not the demonym "esmirniotas" — follows the convention documented in the `epistle-to-polycarp` journal ("Church of the Smyrnæans → Igreja de Esmirna") and matches every other pt-BR occurrence of Smyrna in the corpus |

## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-02: Fourth pt-BR translation from Ignatius of Antioch (after *Epistle to Polycarp*, *Epistle to the Romans*, and *Epistle to the Ephesians*). Register and diction kept consistent with those three: dignified, slightly formal modern Portuguese.
- 2026-09-02 — **address form**: this letter addresses the whole Trallian congregation throughout ("your bishop," "your whole multitude"), like the *Epistle to the Ephesians*. Rendered with **plural "vós"** address throughout, no singular shifts.
- 2026-09-02: No editor/scholarly footnotes present in the source — chapter headings are original to this edition (Roberts-Donaldson) and were translated as part of the text. No translator notes were needed.
- 2026-09-02: Bracketed editorial insertions from the source (e.g. "[ministers]", "[against them]", "[of Satan]") preserved as brackets in the translation, following the established convention.
- 2026-09-02: The single Scripture reference (Isaiah 52:5, Ch. 8) preserved with the same book/chapter/verse as the English source, book name localized to standard Portuguese usage (Isaiah → Isaías). Rendered for the sense of the English text ("Ai daquele por cuja vaidade o meu nome é blasfemado entre alguns"), since this is a short didactic citation rather than a lexical argument that turns on a specific Bible edition's wording.
- 2026-09-02: "as sanhedrim of God, and assembly of the apostles" (Ch. 3) rendered "como o sinédrio de Deus e a assembleia dos apóstolos" — a direct, literal rendering; "sinédrio" is transparent in Portuguese and needed no gloss.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 1** (1 formatting issue found): Ch. 10, "contra [a cruz d]o Senhor?" had the bracket boundary cutting through the middle of the Portuguese contraction "do" (de+o). Fixed to "contra [a cruz do] Senhor?", keeping the whole contraction inside the brackets so no word is split.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 2**: clean. Full completeness, structure, footnote, Scripture, diacritics, address-form, and fidelity checks all passed; confirmed the pass 1 fix is correctly in place.
- 2026-09-02 — **review pass 3**: clean. Independent re-check of all the same categories (bracket balance 15/15, one paragraph per section, no footnotes, correct Scripture localization, consistent plural "vós", no fidelity defects). Two consecutive clean passes.
- 2026-09-02 — **post-merge review pass 4** (4 issues found). The structural audit came back clean again (14/14 sections, 14/14 bracket pairs one-to-one, 2/2 parentheticals, Scripture, headings, `book.json`, quote style), as did the full Portuguese proofread (diacritics, agreement, every "vós" conjugation, clitic placement). Four defects surfaced only once the text was read sentence-by-sentence against the source and cross-checked against the sibling letters:
  - **Ch. 5 — "come short of" is not "be deprived of".** "that we come not short of God" had become "para que não sejamos **privados** de Deus". Falling short of a standard and being dispossessed of something you hold are different images, and the corpus itself draws the line: `epistle-to-the-ephesians` uses "privado" for "is **deprived** of the bread of God", where the English actually says it. Fixed to "para que não **fiquemos aquém** de Deus". **A word already in use elsewhere in the corpus for a different English term is the one most likely to be borrowed wrongly** — check what the corpus already spends a word on before spending it again.
  - **Ch. 5 — a qualifier of degree swapped for one of time.** "you who are **but** babes" (archaic "but" = only/merely) had become "que **ainda** sois crianças" ("still"). The same chapter renders "I am still but a learner" as "ainda sou **apenas** um aprendiz", so the translator had the word two sentences later and simply did not reach for it here. Fixed to "que sois **apenas** crianças". **An inconsistency inside one paragraph is worth more than a dictionary check: where the file itself shows the right rendering nearby, the odd one out is the defect.**
  - **Ch. 10 — a negation added to an unnegated rhetorical question.** "Do I therefore die in vain?" had become "**Não** morro, pois, em vão?". Worth being precise about why this is a defect: the meaning is *not* inverted — a negated rhetorical question in Portuguese invites assent to the positive, so both versions still expect "yes, in vain". The defect is the wording. Ignatius's reductio alternates deliberately — unnegated question, then negated question ("Am I not then guilty...") — and negating both flattens the alternation the source builds. Fixed to "Morro, pois, em vão?". **Fidelity is about wording, not only about meaning; a change that survives the meaning check can still be a change.**
  - **Ch. 13 — demonym against a documented corpus convention.** (see also pass 5) "the Smyrnæans" had become "os **esmirniotas**". The word is defensible in isolation, but the `epistle-to-polycarp` journal explicitly records that this corpus renders Smyrna by city name and names "esmirniota" as the form it avoids; every other pt-BR mention of Smyrna in the corpus follows that. Fixed to "os de Esmirna", and the rule is now recorded in this book's Key Terms so it is not re-litigated. **A convention only holds if it is written where the next translator will look — a decision logged in one book's journal is invisible from inside another.**
- 2026-09-02 — **post-merge review pass 5** (3 further issues found). Run after the pass 4 fixes, as an adversarial re-audit plus a fully independent fresh audit. Both confirmed the four pass 4 fixes in place with no new grammar errors; the mechanical categories came back clean for the third time (15/15 bracket pairs, 2/2 parentheticals, Scripture, structure, diacritics, "vós" forms), as did the key-term cross-check against all three sibling letters (bispo/presbitério/diáconos, *alcançar a Deus*, *acorrentado*/*cadeias*, *mansidão*, *o príncipe deste mundo*, *feras*) and every deliberate repetition in the source ("truly" ×5 → *verdadeiramente* ×5, "pure" ×3 → *puro* ×3). What the earlier passes had missed was in the two places a diff is least likely to look — a heading, and a pronoun idiom:
  - **Ch. 1 heading — grammatical person silently changed.** "Acknowledgment of **their** excellence" had become "Reconhecimento da **vossa** excelência", shifting the editor's third-person heading into the letter's own second-person voice. It was the only one of the fourteen headings whose person did not track the source, and it departs from the sibling precedent (Ephesians' "Praise of the Ephesians" → "Louvor **aos Efésios**", not "vosso louvor"). It also risked being read as the honorific title *Vossa Excelência*. Fixed to "Reconhecimento da excelência **deles**" — third person and unambiguous. **Headings are editorial apparatus in a different voice from the text they head; a translator inside the letter's voice will drift into it without noticing.**
  - **Ch. 13 — a reciprocal that stopped being reciprocal.** "love **one another** with an undivided heart" had become "ame **ao outro**" — one man loving one other man, not the congregation loving mutually. The file itself had it right four lines earlier: Ch. 12's "in prayer with one another" is "em oração **uns pelos outros**". Fixed to "ame **aos outros**". **A defect can sit in correct grammar: "ame ao outro" parses cleanly and simply says something narrower than the source.**
  - **Ch. 7 heading — identical source heading, two different renderings across the corpus.** "The same continued" is the same English heading Ephesians Ch. 4 carries, where it is rendered "Continuação"; here it had been elaborated to "O mesmo assunto continua". Neither is wrong alone, but an identical heading should not read differently from one letter to the next. Fixed to "Continuação", matching the earlier precedent. **Consistency questions are invisible inside a single file — they only appear when the same string is looked up across the corpus, which is why the sibling cross-check has to be part of the review and not an afterthought.**
- 2026-09-02 — **post-merge review pass 6** (2 issues found). Two independent runs: the verification audit confirmed all seven earlier fixes in place and reported the file clean; the fresh Portuguese proofread, reading the same file with no knowledge of the prior passes, found two more. Worth noting that this is the pass where the two runs disagreed — a "clean" verdict from one reviewer is evidence, not proof, and both defects below sit in sentences that are perfectly grammatical and survive every mechanical check:
  - **Ch. 1 — a false friend that inverts the emotion.** "so **sympathized** in the joy which I ... possess" had become "de tal modo **se compadeceu** da alegria". *Compadecer-se de* is etymologically the exact calque of *sympathize* (both are "suffer with"), which is presumably why it was chosen — but in living Portuguese it has narrowed to *pity*, and pity is a word for suffering. Applied to *alegria* it says Polybius pitied Ignatius's joy. Fixed to "compartilhou da alegria". **The most dangerous false friend is the one with the correct etymology: the calque is defensible on every ground except what the word now means.**
  - **Ch. 11 — an ambiguous possessive attaching to the nearest noun.** "God ... having promised **their** union" (the union of the head and its members) had become "prometido a **sua** união". Portuguese *sua* marks neither person nor number, so with *Deus* standing immediately before it, the natural parse is "**his** union" — God's. Fixed to "a união **deles**". **English "their" carries number that Portuguese "sua" throws away; every "their" is a place where the translation can quietly reattach to the wrong noun.** This is the same failure the Ch. 1 heading had in pass 5, from the opposite direction — a possessive that does not pin down its referent.
- 2026-09-02 — **post-merge review pass 7**: clean. Two independent runs — a fresh Portuguese proofread with no knowledge of the prior passes, and an adversarial fidelity re-audit — both re-derived their findings from the raw text and both came back with no objective defects. All nine fixes confirmed in place; structure, brackets (15/15), parentheticals (2/2), Scripture, every "vós" form, diacritics, possessive referents, recurring terms and deliberate repetitions all verified. Only style observations remained ("droga mortal", and "puff up" rendered *exaltam* in Ch. 4 vs *ensoberbecerdes* in Ch. 7 — different grammatical contexts, transitive vs reflexive, so both stand). **Nine defects across three rounds, on a text that had already been reviewed clean three times: the passes that found things were the ones that read the text, and the ones that read it against its siblings.**
- 2026-09-02 — **considered and deliberately kept** (recorded so later passes do not re-flag them): "blasphemed **among any**" (Ch. 8, Isaiah 52:5) rendered "entre **alguns**" rather than a universal "entre quaisquer" — the English source phrase is itself archaic and ambiguous, and the partitive is a defensible reading; "strangled" → "sufocados" rather than the literal "estrangulados"; and "through our rising again to Him" → "para com ele" in the Greeting. These are translator judgment calls within range, not defects.
