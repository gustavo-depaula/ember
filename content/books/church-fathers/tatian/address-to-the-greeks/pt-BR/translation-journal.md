# Translation Journal — Address to the Greeks (pt-BR)

Source: en-US/ch001.md (J. E. Ryland, Ante-Nicene Fathers Vol. 2, via New Advent). No `la/` or Greek directory exists, so the English is the only source.
Target: pt-BR

## Key Terms

The only other Tatian work in pt-BR is `tatian/fragments/` ("Taciano", "Logos" untranslated, as here). Conventions follow `athenagoras/plea-for-the-christians/pt-BR` (same ANF volume, same apologetic genre), `athanasius/` and `ambrose/repentance/`: author name without "Santo" (Tatian has no cult; precedent "Atenágoras", "Tertuliano"), headings "Capítulo N. …" in sentence case, Scripture book names in full Portuguese with the source's chapter:verse, curly quotes “ ”, lowercase pronouns for God (ele, dele) with capitalized titles, `vós` for the Greeks addressed as a body, `tu` where the source's address is marked singular (a vocative "O man", or ch. 18's "cure yourself … called a benefactor").

## Translation Decisions

- **Editor apparatus.** The source carries no editor footnotes; nothing dropped.
- **Inline Scripture references** (`John 4:24`, `Romans 1:20`, `John 1:5`, `John 1:3`) sit unparenthesized inside the text, ch. 19's even inside the quotation. Mirrored in place with Portuguese book names.
- **Scripture quotations** follow Ryland's wording: John 1:5 "As trevas não compreendem a luz", keeping "compreender" because the next sentence plays on it ("a luz compreende as trevas"); Ps 8 allusion in ch. 15 "Pois foram feitos um pouco inferiores aos anjos".
- **Ch. 1 verse** and the Homeric/tragic/Orphic quotations in ch. 8 kept as blockquotes with the source's line breaks.
- **Ch. 18** "Why you deify the objects of nature?" (missing "do") rendered as a normal question.
- **Ch. 31** "the one being the oldest of poets … the other the founder of all barbarian wisdom" reverses the order of "Moses and Homer"; mirrored ("um … o outro").
- **Ch. 38** trailing ":—" rendered as ":" (no transplanted English punctuation).
- **Ch. 39** bracketed "[and of the Macedonian, the Ptolemaic, and the Antiochian]" and ch. 40 "[as]", ch. 12 "[of authority]" kept in brackets.
- **Numbers** in running prose written out in words (seiscentos áureos, quatrocentos e sete anos).

## Source edits (OCR-class, en-US)

- Ch. 2 "shut him up and and carried" → "shut him up and carried".
- Ch. 21 "the unshorn Phoœbus" → "Phœbus".
- Ch. 21 "the spouse us came as an old woman to Semele" → "the spouse of Zeus came" (ANF reading).
- Ch. 26 "a pompons appearance" → "pompous".
- Ch. 34 "the tyrant Bhalaris" → "Phalaris".

## Review log

### Round 1

Focus: completeness, paragraph by paragraph (93 source blocks ↔ 93 pt-BR blocks), plus the mechanical audit and Scripture references. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed.

Fixed:
- Ch. 10, last sentence: "representou essa mulher condenada à morte por Orestes" → "representou essa mulher morta às mãos de Orestes". The source says "put to death by Orestes" (killed); "condenada à morte" means "sentenced to death", which the source does not say.

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 29 "the effeminate and the pathic" → "pelos efeminados e pelos depravados". "Depravados" is broader than "pathic" but names the same moral charge without explicit language. This is a register choice, not a mistranslation.
- Ch. 19 "because by proclaiming the truth he convicted the philosophers" → "porque Justino, proclamando…". The source's "he" refers to Justin (Crescens is the one who accuses), so naming him resolves the antecedent correctly.
- Ch. 33 Myron's heifer: "by carrying off the daughter of Agenor it had borne away the prize" → "ela tinha conquistado". In Ryland's English, "it" refers to the heifer, so the feminine follows the source.
- Ch. 41 "Amphion … forbids our collecting further particulars" → "dispensa-nos de reunir mais pormenores". It has the same effect (no further inquiry is needed). Not a change of sense.

### Round 2

Focus: a clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of all 93 blocks, plus the mechanical audit and a Key Terms count check. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed.

Fixed:
- Ch. 7, "foresee future events, not as fated": "não como fatais" → "não como fadados". The Key Terms row for Fate gives "fated" → "fadados", as ch. 11 renders it twice. "Fatais" broke that row and can be read as "deadly".

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 5 "that which comes by participation, making its choice of function" → "assumindo a sua função". "Make choice of" here means taking up a role (Greek αἵρεσιν λαβόν), and "assumindo" says that. Not a dropped qualifier.
- Ch. 33 "But, forsooth, you will not believe that among us there are wise women!" → "não credes". "Will not" expresses refusal, not future time, so the present negation keeps the sense.
- Ch. 35 "that men manifestly ignorant should be reasoned with by a man of like nature" → "sejam instruídos pela razão por um homem". This conveys being addressed with argument. A wording choice, not a change of sense.
- Ch. 4 "God is a Spirit, not pervading matter" → "não um espírito que penetra a matéria". The added "um espírito" only supplies the participle's subject.
- Ch. 8 "deprived of her virility by Hephæstos" → "virilidade". Mirrored from the source. Not an unmistakable OCR slip, so the en-US was left as is.

### Round 3

Focus: a source-blind cold read of the pt-BR, with each mark then checked against the source. After that, a mechanical sweep of spelling, diacritics, crase, hyphenation, punctuation and quote pairing, plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 5 defects, fixed.

Fixed:
- Ch. 25 "herdeiro da doutrina de Ferecides" → "Ferécides". The accent was dropped. Ch. 3 spells it "Ferécides".
- Ch. 24 "Deixamo-vos" → "Deixamos-vos", and ch. 26 "abandonamo-vos" → "abandonamos-vos". The final -s of -mos is dropped only before "nos", never before "vos".
- Ch. 33 "Euctémon" → "Euctêmon". The file uses the Brazilian circumflex on a stressed e or o before a nasal (Sêmele, Fêmio, Epimênides, Dinômenes).
- Ch. 41 "Filámon" (twice) → "Filâmon". A stressed a before a nasal takes â, as "Tâmiris", "Dânao" and "Sâmio" do in the same paragraph.

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 34 "One ought to turn away with disgust from one who…" → "Devia-se desviar com nojo os olhos daquela". Grammarians disagree on agreement with se + auxiliary + infinitive, so this is not an objective error.
- Ch. 30 "a certain 'hidden treasure,' which while we are digging for we are indeed covered with dust" → "que, enquanto o cavamos, ficamos…". The resumptive "o" follows the source's broken relative clause. The sentence is still clear.
- Ch. 38 "the time from Inachus to the taking of Troy" → "o tempo de Ínaco à tomada de Troia". "De … a" means "from … to", so the phrase is grammatical.
- Ch. 3 "more wicked than just persons — one Socrates and a Hercules" and ch. 6 "ὐπόστασις" (ch. 5 has "ὑπόστασις"). Both follow the source as printed.

### Round 4

Focus: function words, headings and forced commitments. This covered pronoun antecedents, demonstratives, possessive "seu/sua", "consigo", connectors, a tu/vós trace, and all 42 headings. The mechanical audit and a Key Terms count check were also run. Verdict: 3 defects, fixed.

Fixed:
- Ch. 4, "the Spirit that pervades matter is inferior to the more divine spirit; and this, even when assimilated to the soul, is not to be honoured equally with the perfect God": "e este" → "e aquele". "Este" points to the nearest noun, "o espírito mais divino". The argument against adoring sun, moon, stocks and stones is about the spirit that pervades matter.
- Ch. 23, "the rich man buys others to kill him": "compra outros para os matar" → "compra outros para que o matem". In the source the others do the killing. The Portuguese had made them the ones killed.
- Ch. 40, "the Greeks, who, without being aware of it, drew his doctrines [as] from a fountain": "tiraram as suas doutrinas" → "tiraram as doutrinas dele". With "os gregos" as subject, "as suas" reads as the Greeks' own doctrines. The source means Moses' doctrines.

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 25, the switch from tu ("Ó homem que rivalizas com o cão… te insulta") to vós ("Vós, que recebeis dos vossos predecessores…"). The source's next sentence is "One of you asserts" and then "O Greeks", so the plural is right. Ch. 26 "Why, O man, do you set the letters…" returns to tu with the singular vocative.
- Ch. 9 heading "They Give Rise to Superstitions" → "Os demônios dão origem às superstições". "They" is the demons of ch. 8, and the chapter opens "Such are the demons". Naming them adds nothing the source does not mean.
- Ch. 12 "And while we say these things … do you who are willing hasten to learn" → "E, visto que dizemos". In this sentence "while" gives the ground for the exhortation, so the causal reading is the same sense.
- Ch. 35 "concerning its doctrines" → "das suas doutrinas". "Suas" is third person, so it cannot refer to "vossas instituições". Its only antecedent is the barbaric philosophy ("ela").
- Ch. 34 "buried them with their maker Pythagoras" → "não as tenhais antes enterrado". "Them" are the statues, whose maker is the sculptor Pythagoras.

### Round 5

Focus: terms, journal and names. A per-chapter term-frequency diff ran against the Key Terms table in both directions. Every journal claim was grepped against the files, and every proper name was checked against standard Portuguese and the sibling pt-BR works. The mechanical audit also ran. Verdict: 2 defects, fixed.

Fixed:
- Journal header: "No other Tatian work has a pt-BR translation" was false. `tatian/fragments/pt-BR` exists. Its conventions ("Taciano", "Logos" untranslated) match this book.
- Ch. 34 "Andron fez uma imagem" → "Ândron". Greek Ἄνδρων is stressed on the first syllable, and a paroxytone ending in -n takes an accent. Without one it reads as an oxytone. The file accents every other such name (Fílon, Míron, Drímon, Selânion, Filâmon, Ânito).

Key Terms table completed (rows were incomplete, not false): "power of the Logos" (chs. 5, 7, 18) also gives "poder do Logos". "Free choice" (ch. 7) gives "livre-arbítrio". "Constitution" (chs. 6, 7, 12, 15) shares "constituição" with "polity".

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 7 "declared him to be God" → "o declararam deus". The lowercase marks a false god made by acclamation, and the predicate noun reads naturally. This is a capitalization choice, not a change of sense.
- Epithet case varies: "Filócoro, o Ateniense" and "Euclo, o Cipriota" against "Ínaco, o argivo" and "Erina, a lésbia". This is style, and no house rule exists.
- "Posêidon", "Júpiter Laciar", "Frineia", "Ferécides", "Aidoneu" and "Core" match the sibling works (Athenagoras, Minucius Felix, Tertullian). "Hirão" is the Portuguese Bible form. "Meleto" is the standard name of Socrates' accuser.
- Ch. 38 "Ægyptiaca" is the source's Latin title, kept unitalicized as printed.

### Round 7

Focus: a clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of all 42 chapters (negation, subject and object, tense and mood, qualifiers), plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed.

Fixed:
- Ch. 11, "Am I a slave, I endure servitude. Am I free, I do not make a boast of my good birth": "Sou escravo, suporto a servidão. Sou livre, não faço alarde" → "Se sou escravo, … Se sou livre, …". The English inversion is conditional. Without "se" the Portuguese states that Tatian is both a slave and free. Ch. 4 keeps the conditional through inversion ("Ordena o soberano…, estou pronto"), so it stays as it is.

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 12 "do you who are willing hasten to learn" → "vós que tendes boa vontade". "Ter boa vontade" means being willing, and the addressees are the same.
- Ch. 17 "There are visitations of demons" → "investidas". The passage is about demons afflicting the sick, and "investidas" names the same assault.
- Ch. 34 "skill in casting metals, of which specimens exist even now" → "da qual ainda hoje existem exemplares". Specimens of his casting are specimens of that skill, so the referent is the same.
- Ch. 38 "the interpreter of their affairs" → "dos seus feitos". The next clause ("narrating the acts of the kings") says what he interprets. Not a change of sense.
- Ch. 9 "after their sorrow" → "depois da sua dor". "Sua" is as open as the source's "their".

### Round 8

Focus: a source-blind cold read of all 42 chapters, with each mark then checked against the source. After that, a mechanical sweep of spelling, diacritics, crase, hyphenation, punctuation and quote pairing, plus the mechanical audit. Verdict: clean, no changes.

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 26 "Why say that wisdom is with you alone, who have not another sun" → "está só convosco, que não tendes outro sol". The 2nd-person-plural verb ties the relative to "convosco". It is not a second thing the Greeks say.
- Ch. 30 "For He has become master of all we have" → "Pois ele se tornou senhor". The source's capital marks God. Lowercase follows the journal's pronoun convention, and the nearest masculine antecedent is "Deus" ("palavras de Deus").
- Ch. 9 "that they might themselves be thought to remain in heaven" → "fossem tidos por permanecer no céu". "Ser tido por" followed by an infinitive is grammatical.
- Ch. 26 "the cask of the Danaïds" → "tonel das Danaides". This is the usual Portuguese spelling of the expression, read with the diphthong "ai" (like "Nereidas"). It is not a missing accent.
- Ch. 1 "now as just, now as not good" → "ora como justa, ora como não boa", and ch. 13 "rises again at last at the end" → "ressurge enfim no fim". Both follow the source's wording.

### Round 9

Focus: function words, headings and forced commitments. This covered prepositions, articles, demonstratives, connectors, possessive "seu/sua", "consigo", gender and antecedent commitments, a turn-by-turn tu/vós trace, and all 42 headings. The mechanical audit also ran. Verdict: 2 defects, fixed.

Fixed:
- Ch. 18, "seek to cure yourself … Why you deify … when you cure your neighbour, are you called a benefactor? Yield to the power of the Logos!": vós ("curar-vos a vós mesmos", "sois chamados benfeitores", "Cedei") → tu ("curar-te a ti mesmo", "divinizas", "curas o teu próximo, és chamado benfeitor", "Cede"). "Yourself" and "a benefactor" mark a single addressee, picking up "he who trusts in the system of matter". The plural changed the number of the addressee. Ch. 19 returns to vós with "yourselves". The journal header's tu/vós line now names this case.
- Ch. 9, "why are not Sardinia and Cyprus honoured in heaven?": "a Sardenha e Chipre honradas" → "honrados". "Chipre" is masculine, and a predicative after a mixed-gender compound subject takes the masculine plural.

Considered and rejected:
- Ch. 11 "How is it that you are fated … Die to the world … lay aside your old nature" and ch. 20 "Even if you be healed by drugs … it behooves you" are rendered with vós. Nothing in the English marks them as singular. Ch. 20's later "O man" is a bare vocative attached to a statement, so it does not reach back to these verbs. The English is the source, and it leaves the number open.
- Ch. 8 "But men form the material of their apostasy" → "da sua apostasia". "Their" is as open in the English (men or demons) as "sua" is in the Portuguese.
- Ch. 31 "To do the former would be foolish … but the other will surprise you" → "Fazer aquilo … mas isto". Aquilo points to the earlier option and isto to the later one, which matches the source.
- Ch. 41 "all the writers that were before him" → "antes dele". This is as open as the source (Homer or Moses).
- Ch. 34 "their offspring Harmonia" → "a filha deles". Harmonia is female, so the gender commitment is correct.
- Ch. 21 "nor can I undertake to persuade my neighbour" → "persuadir o meu próximo a isso". "A isso" only supplies the object implied by the sentence before (homage to the elements).
