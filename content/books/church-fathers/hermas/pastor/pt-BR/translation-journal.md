## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-27 — **resumed translation**: ch001 (the five Visions) was already complete when this journal was created; its renderings were adopted as the governing convention and the Key Terms above were extracted from it. ch002 (Commandments) and ch003 (Similitudes) translated afterwards following the same conventions.
- 2026-09-27 — **address forms**: the Lady (the Church), the shepherd, the young man and Hermas all address one another with singular **tu**. Addresses to a group (the saints, "brethren", the readers) → plural **vós**. God, in the rare direct address, → **tu**.
- 2026-09-27 — **title**: Portuguese "pastor" covers both "pastor" and "shepherd", so the English alternative title "(or 'The Shepherd')" is rendered "(ou 'O Pastor de Hermas')", the name by which the book is known in Portuguese, rather than repeating the same word.
- 2026-09-27 — **TOC titles**: the book.json TOC has three entries titled after each file's first heading ("FIRST VISION", "FIRST COMMANDMENT", "FIRST SIMILITUDE"). They are translated literally ("PRIMEIRA VISÃO", "PRIMEIRO MANDAMENTO", "PRIMEIRA PARÁBOLA") to mirror the source headings, even though each file covers all the Visions, Commandments or Similitudes.
- 2026-09-27 — **Similitude → Parábola**: the Greek παραβολαί; "Parábola" is the usual Portuguese title of the third part of the book. The shepherd's recurring "similitudes" in running text follow suit.
- 2026-09-27 — **Commandment 10 wordplay**: the English uses "grief"/"grieve" for λύπη throughout ("grief is the most wicked of all spirits", "grieves the Holy Spirit"). Rendered "tristeza"/"entristecer" consistently, so "não entristeçais o Espírito Santo" keeps its link with the vice.
- 2026-09-27 — **footnotes**: this New Advent edition has no footnotes; the ANF apparatus was already removed on import. No translator notes added.
- 2026-09-27 — **Scripture**: the few embedded Scripture echoes follow Crombie's wording, not a Portuguese Bible. The inline New Advent references stay mid-sentence where the source has them, with Portuguese book names and the `12:13` format of `clement-rome/first-epistle/pt-BR`: Eclesiastes 12:13 (Mand. 7), Isaías 29:13; Mateus 15:8 and João 12:40; 2 Coríntios 3:14 (Mand. 12.4), Mateus 10:28; Lucas 12:5 (Mand. 12.6), Mateus 18:3 (Sim. 9.29).
- 2026-09-27 — **brackets**: every editorial bracket in the English is kept with the same count per file (ch002: 4, ch003: 38), including the source's odd `[… ]` spacing and brackets that straddle a quotation boundary (Sim. 9.5 "[viremos aqui … Depois de alguns dias, ]").
- 2026-09-27 — **source quotation quirks mirrored**: Sim. 9.14 keeps Hermas's thanksgiving ("Por todas estas coisas dei graças…") inside the shepherd's quotation, as the English does; Sim. 9.30 opens `“Ouve”, respondeu ele, a respeito de todas estas também.` with no reopening quote and no close; Sim. 9.31 has no quotation marks at all. Two paragraphs (Sim. 8.4, Sim. 9.11) have one fewer pair of quote marks than the English because split quotes like `“What, then,” said I, “am I to do?”` read more naturally in Portuguese as one quotation followed by the speech tag.
- 2026-09-27 — **OCR-class typos in en-US, translated by sense** (en-US file left untouched, out of scope): Mand. 11 "the earthly spirit **tees** from him" → "foge" (flees); Sim. 5.4 "and of the **sakes**" → "estacas" (stakes); Sim. 8.7 "he will be **east** out of the tower" → "lançado fora" (cast). Worth fixing in the English.
- 2026-09-27 — **edition oddity left as is**: Sim. 8.8 (second half) and Sim. 8.9 both describe branches "two-thirds withered and one-third green"; the Greek has "two-thirds green" in one of them. Mirrored, so the language files agree.
- 2026-09-27 — **Sim. 9.26 "they who had the stains as servants"**: the Greek means deacons (διάκονοι), but Crombie writes "servants"; rendered "servidores" to follow the English.
- 2026-09-27 — **round-1 review, objective fixes** (5 total, applied directly): Vis. 1.3 and Vis. 3.3 had stray **vós**-forms in otherwise singular-**tu** exchanges ("contra vós, seus pais" → "contra ti, seus pais"; "a vossa vida" → "a tua vida"), fixed to match ch001's own convention; Vis. 3.5 used "doutores" (×2) for "teachers" instead of the corpus term "mestres", fixed; Mand. 5.2 "grande prosperidade" for "great enlargement" introduced a wealth sense not in the source and broke the "enlarged heart" / "lugar espaçoso" breadth metaphor running through the same commandment, fixed to "grande amplidão"; Sim. 9.28 had a gender/number disagreement ("algumas... delas... mais belo... restantes", feminine plural governing a masculine singular adjective), recast to match the fruit-comparison two sentences later ("alguns deles mais belos que os restantes").
- 2026-09-27 — **round-1 review, borderline items left as is**: Vis. 3, Ch. 10–13 have a few tu/vós choices where the English itself is ambiguous between singular-Hermas and collective-church address; checked against the nearest explicit plural markers in each passage and left as translated. Mand. 9 "e não Àquele que não te dá" uses the literary personal "a" before a demonstrative referring to God — archaic but grammatically valid, matches the book's register. Mand. 11 "the empty vessel... is not crushed" → "não se quebra" (break) rather than "esmagar" (crush, used elsewhere for the Spirit) — different verb for a literal jar, a defensible idiomatic choice. Mand. 1 has occasional lowercase "ele/nele" for God beside capitalized "Ele" in the same paragraph — reverential-capitalization drift, left alone per the no-normalization rule in `.claude/rules/books.md`. Sim. 9.2 "eager" → "diligentes" and Sim. 9.12 "fellow-councillor with the Father" → "conselheiro do Pai" (loses the "fellow/co-" nuance, no clean one-word equivalent) are both nuance-narrower-than-ideal but not factually wrong.
- 2026-09-27 — **round-3 review, borderline items left as is**: Mand. 4.1 (end) switches from singular-tu ("afasta-te dele... és participante") to plural-vós ("vos foi imposta a ordem de permanecerdes sós, tanto o homem como a mulher") mid-paragraph. The English "you" stays grammatically singular, but the clause explicitly names "both man and woman", so the injunction is read as addressed to the couple as a pair rather than to Hermas alone — matches the round-1 precedent for ambiguous tu/vós spots, left as translated.
- 2026-09-27 — **round-2 fix, logged late**: Sim. 9.32 negation-scope fix (the fuller/pisoeiro passage). Current text reads "recebê-la-eis dele, e não vos irritareis antes, nem o injuriareis" for the English "will you take it from him, and not rather be angry, and abuse him" — the "não … nem …" correctly extends the negation across both "be angry" and "abuse him". The exact prior (buggy) wording is not recoverable from the current file state alone (this pt-BR file has no prior git history to diff — it is new/untracked in this worktree), but the shape of the round-2 bug described for this book (a "não V1 e V2" construction wrongly negating only V1) means the likely fix was replacing a plain "e o injuriareis" with "nem o injuriareis" so the second verb is no longer left accidentally affirmed. Checked against en-US Sim. 9.32 and confirmed the passage is currently correct.
- 2026-09-27 — **round-3 review, objective fixes** (1 total, applied directly): Sim. 9.13 had a gender-agreement break — "Todas estas… receberam…" and "foram persuadidas" establish feminine agreement (the antecedent is "as pedras que foram rejeitadas"), but the next sentence switched to "Estes, pois, foram rejeitados da casa de Deus e entregues a estas mulheres," which could read as pointing at a different (masculine) antecedent such as "os servos de Deus" mentioned two sentences earlier. Fixed to "Estas, pois, foram rejeitadas da casa de Deus e entregues a estas mulheres," matching the English "These, accordingly, were rejected from the house of God, and were given over to these women" and the established feminine agreement, on the same pattern as the round-1 Sim. 9.28 fix.
- 2026-09-27: The en-US OCR slips recorded in this journal were corrected in the en-US source. The pt-BR text already rendered the corrected reading.
