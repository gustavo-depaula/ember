## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-04: Single-chapter work, ~1,500 words — a mid-3rd-century synodal letter (c. 268) from the Synod of Antioch, addressed to Pope Dionysius of Rome and Maximus of Alexandria, announcing the deposition of Paul of Samosata and the election of Domnus as his successor. Preserved by Eusebius (*Church History*, Book VII); "Malchion" is the presbyter of Antioch traditionally credited as the letter's principal author, per the book's own attribution. Chosen from the `church-fathers/` backlog as a well-scoped single-work pick — the smallest remaining untranslated work in the collection at the time of selection.
- No footnotes in the source beyond the single inline Scripture cross-reference ("1 Timothy 6:5"), kept inline in the same position as the English source, per established corpus convention (cf. Gregory Thaumaturgus's Canonical Epistle, Ignatian epistles, Didache) of mirroring the source edition's own placement even where the reference's exact anchor point in the sentence is ambiguous (it most plausibly annotates "supposing that gain is godliness" a few words earlier, echoing the verse's own wording, rather than "procurator" where it currently sits — but this is a placement quirk of the digitized en-US source itself, out of scope for this translation to relocate or correct; noted here rather than silently changed).
- One evident punctuation artifact in the source — a stray sentence break ("...in the midst of the Church. in the great day of the Paschal festival...", with a lowercase word following a full stop) — was treated as a transcription/OCR glitch rather than a deliberate sentence boundary, since no coherent reading exists with the sentence split there. Rendered as one continuous clause in the translation, silently normalized (not logged as a content change, per the corpus's standing practice of fixing obvious punctuation-only artifacts rather than preserving them).
- "convicted them" (paragraph 3): the source's "though he is cognisant of them, and has convicted them" carries an archaic two-pronoun ambiguity — the first "them" refers back to "sins," the second most plausibly to "these women" (found them guilty of the sins), a shift in antecedent already present in the English. Rendered as "ele os conheça e as tenha declarado culpadas" — "os" (masculine, referring to *pecados*) and "as" (feminine, referring to *mulheres*) — which reproduces the same antecedent split in Portuguese rather than collapsing it to one referent.
- "casting the injured unfairly in their suit" (paragraph 2): an archaic legal idiom ("to be cast in a suit" = to lose a lawsuit), not literal "casting/throwing." Rendered as "fazendo os prejudicados perderem injustamente as suas causas" to carry the actual legal sense rather than a literal (and meaningless) word-for-word rendering.
- "concusses the brethren" (paragraph 2): "concuss" here is the archaic sense "to extort by intimidation," not the modern medical sense. Rendered as "extorque," not a cognate.
- Formal, dignified register throughout — this is a synodal letter of formal ecclesiastical condemnation and deposition, addressed to two bishops on behalf of an entire regional synod, so word choice favors the register of the corpus's other conciliar documents (Trent Catechism, the councils sub-works already in `church-fathers/`) over a devotional register.
- Proper names in the signatory list (Helenus, Hymenaeus, Theophilus, Theotecnus, Maximus, Proclus, Nicomas, Aelianus, Paul, Bolanus, Protogenes, Hierax, Eutychius, Theodorus, Malchion, Lucius) given standard Portuguese forms where one is well established (e.g. Teófilo, Teodoro, Lúcio); left transliterated as spelled in the English source where no standard Portuguese form is established (Nicomas, Hierax), per the corpus's established "transliterate, don't invent" rule for minor proper names (cf. Gregory Thaumaturgus journal, "Boradi").
- 2026-09-04 — **review round 1** (2 parallel passes: bilingual clause-by-clause fidelity, and independent cold monolingual pt-BR proofread). The bilingual pass found the translation largely faithful, confirming all decisions already logged above (1 Tim 6:5 placement, "casting... in their suit," "concusses," the "convicted them" antecedent split), and found two genuine mistranslations plus two lower-severity issues. The monolingual pass, working cold with no access to English or this journal, found several grammar/clarity issues; two of its findings were checked against the source and turned out to be faithful mirrors of the English's own construction, not translation defects. All genuine defects fixed:
  - Fixed (bilingual) — §1: "those men of blessed name" was translated as "homens de bendita memória" ("of blessed memory"), collapsing it into the same phrase later correctly used for the deceased Demetrianus in §5 ("a man of blessed memory"). The English draws a real distinction between the two phrases. Fixed to "homens de bendito nome."
  - Fixed (bilingual) — §2: "which choristers one might shudder to hear" was mistranslated as "o que faria os coristas estremecer de ouvir" — reversing who shudders. In English, the *listener* shudders on hearing the choristers; the Portuguese had the choristers themselves shuddering. Fixed to "cujo canto faria estremecer quem o ouvisse," restoring listener-as-experiencer.
  - Fixed (bilingual, minor) — §2: "his strutting through the market-places" had gained an unsourced "com arrogância" ("desfilar com arrogância"), duplicating arrogance already stated explicitly two clauses later ("by his haughty demeanour and by the arrogance of his heart"). Replaced the verb itself with "pavonear-se" (to strut/preen), which carries the pompous connotation of "strutting" without needing an added adverbial, removing the invented amplification.
  - Fixed (bilingual, minor) — §3: "não se escandalizassem, ou encontrassem nisso um incentivo" left the second verb outside the negation's scope (joined by "ou" instead of "nem"), which is the opposite of what the sentence warns against (both outcomes should be negated). Fixed "ou" → "nem."
  - Fixed (monolingual) — §3: sequence-of-tenses mismatch, "ainda que se admitisse que ele nada **tenha feito**" (present subjunctive) after an imperfect-subjunctive-governing "admitisse." Fixed to "**tivesse feito**" (imperfect subjunctive), correct correlação verbo-temporal.
  - Fixed (monolingual) — §4: "E destas coisas... poder-se-ia levar em conta" mismatched government (levar em conta takes a direct object, not "de + noun") and number (singular verb for a plural logical subject). Fixed to "E estas coisas... poder-se-**iam** levar em conta," removing the stray "de" and correcting plural agreement.
  - Fixed (monolingual, punctuation/clarity) — §3: "ele conivente busca ocultar" (bare adjective with no commas) reads as a run-on. Added commas: "ele, conivente, busca ocultar." Also lightly clarified the antecedent hand-off from "os" (pecados) to the feminine plural (mulheres, established two clauses earlier) by using "e as quais já tenha declarado culpadas" in place of the bare "e as tenha declarado culpadas," making the referent switch easier to track without changing the deliberate split-antecedent reading confirmed by the bilingual pass.
  - Fixed (monolingual, style) — §3: "por temor por sua própria posição" (double "por" in quick succession) smoothed to "por receio quanto à própria posição."
  - Fixed (monolingual, style) — §1: "tendo já feito a experiência da iniquidade infiel" (calque of "having made trial of") smoothed to "já tendo constatado a iniquidade infiel."
  - Fixed (monolingual, clarity/punctuation) — §5: "o filho de Demetriano, homem de bendita memória, e que outrora presidiu com distinção a esta mesma Igreja, de nome Domno..." let the long appositive about Demetriano's own past tenure sit unbracketed between "o filho de Demetriano" and its name-tag "de nome Domno," inviting a momentary misparse. Set the Demetriano appositive off in parentheses: "o filho de Demetriano (homem de bendita memória, que outrora presidiu com distinção a esta mesma Igreja), de nome Domno..." — punctuation only, no wording change.
  - Fixed (punctuation, corpus convention) — the English source's archaic "assim:—" colon-dash typesetting mark (three occurrences, opening each quoted section) was replaced with a plain Portuguese colon, per the established corpus precedent of treating this mark as source typesetting rather than content to transplant (cf. Gregory Thaumaturgus's *On the Trinity* journal, which explicitly logs the same call for an em-dash attribution mark).
  - **Not a defect (verified against English)** — §2: the monolingual pass flagged a tense/register break, present-tense "Tampouco é preciso dizer..." shifting to future "Tampouco **falarei** da charlatanice..." Checked against the source: the English itself shifts modals mid-paragraph in exactly the same place — "Neither **need** I say..." then "Nor **shall** I say..." — so the Portuguese present→future shift faithfully mirrors a modal shift already in the source. Left unchanged.
  - **Not a defect (verified against English)** — §3: "esta é uma afirmação que não se **apoiará** em simples asserção" (future, for what reads as a timeless statement) was flagged. Checked against the source: "this is a statement which **shall not be made** to depend on simple assertion" is itself future/modal — the Portuguese future correctly mirrors it. Left unchanged.
  - **Not a defect (verified against English)** — §5: "outro bispo" used to introduce Domno, then "aquele outro" used later for the deposed Paulo, was flagged as an ambiguous reuse. Checked against the source: the English itself does the same thing ("another bishop"... later "that **other**"), disambiguated only by contrast with "him" (Domnus) in the immediately preceding clause — the same contrast structure carries over into the Portuguese ("lhe"/"dele" = Domno vs. "aquele outro" = Paulo). Left unchanged as a faithful mirror of the source's own construction.
  - The raw inline "1 Timóteo 6:5" citation (no brackets, no footnote marker), sitting mid-clause next to "procurador" rather than adjacent to the "gain is godliness" allusion it most plausibly annotates, was checked against the corpus's own established convention for inline Scripture references (cf. Gregory Thaumaturgus's Canonical Epistle: "1 Corinthians 6:13", "Deuteronomy 22:1-3" etc., all placed bare with no brackets or footnote markers) — the *lack* of brackets/footnote formatting matches convention exactly. The specific mid-clause *position* of this one reference is unusual even by that convention (elsewhere references sit immediately after the quotation they annotate; here it doesn't), but that positioning is a property of the digitized en-US source file itself, not something introduced in translation — out of scope for this translation to relocate. Mirrored at the same position in the Portuguese, as originally decided.

- 2026-09-04 — **review round 2** (2 parallel passes: a fresh full bilingual re-read + independent re-verification of all round-1 fixes and "not a defect" calls, and a fresh cold monolingual proofread with no access to English or this journal). The bilingual pass came back **fully clean on content** — no new mistranslation, missing/added content, or structural defect; all round-1 fixes and all three round-1 "not a defect" calls independently re-verified as sound. It flagged one journal-accuracy issue (not a translation defect): the *secretum* italics rationale was factually wrong — corrected above. The monolingual pass found several genuine grammar/style defects, fixed, plus two items that re-raised already-resolved round-1 "not a defect" calls (mirroring the modal shift and the future/present tense mix — both re-confirmed sound, not touched again) and one further item verified against the source and left unchanged:
  - Fixed — §1: missing crase, "vivem a distância" → "vivem à distância" (fixed adverbial locution).
  - Fixed — §1: "a iniquidade infiel daquele homem" reordered to "a iniquidade daquele homem infiel" — the adjective "infiel" (faithless) sits more coherently on "homem" than on the abstract noun "iniquidade"; same overall sense as the source's "the man's infidel iniquity," just reattached for Portuguese fluency.
  - Fixed — §2: non-idiomatic proclisis with a gerund, "e as recitando" → "e recitando-as" (Portuguese gerunds take enclisis; "e" is not a proclisis trigger).
  - Fixed — §2: "composições recentes de homens recentes" — "homens recentes" ("recent men") is not an idiomatic Portuguese collocation, even though it deliberately mirrors the source's own repeated "recent... recent." Fixed to "de autores recentes," which preserves the rhetorical repetition of "recentes" while using a noun that Portuguese actually pairs with that adjective.
  - Fixed — §2: redundant punctuation, "dele —, mas" (dash immediately followed by comma) → "dele — mas" (dash only).
  - Fixed — subtitle: "Em Nome do Sínodo de Antioquia, Contra Paulo de Samósata." used English-style title case. Portuguese titles capitalize only the first word and proper nouns. Fixed to "Em nome do Sínodo de Antioquia, contra Paulo de Samósata." (keeping "Sínodo de Antioquia" and "Paulo de Samósata" capitalized as proper names).
  - **Not a defect (verified against English)** — §5: "e os que pensam como Ártemas podem manter comunhão com **ele**" was flagged as ambiguous — "ele" sits closer to "Ártemas" than to its intended referent "aquele outro" (the deposed Paulo). Checked against the source: "those who think with Artemas may hold communion with **him**" has the identical structural ambiguity in English (nearest antecedent is likewise "Artemas," not "that other") — not something the translation introduced. Left unchanged, consistent with the round-1 call on the neighboring "outro bispo"/"aquele outro" pair.
  - No fixes needed for the two re-raised items (modal shift "preciso"/"falarei," and future/present "apoiará"/"é") — both already verified against the source in round 1 and re-confirmed sound this round.
  - First of two consecutive clean rounds needed to close (content-wise, via the bilingual pass; the monolingual pass's fixable findings are all applied above).

- 2026-09-04 — **review round 3** (2 parallel passes: a fresh bilingual re-read hunting citation placement, proper-name consistency, negation polarity, sentence merge/split, register shifts, false friends, and regressions; and a fresh cold monolingual proofread). The bilingual pass confirmed every round-1/round-2 fix is present, applied exactly once, uncorrupted, and re-verified all five "not a defect" calls to date — found no content-level defects, only one low-severity, self-described-as-non-blocking register observation (the English's we→I narrowing across §2's three "need/shall" clauses is rendered impersonal→I→I in Portuguese; left unchanged as a defensible idiomatic choice, not a factual or fidelity error). The monolingual pass re-raised the already-resolved "1 Timóteo 6:5" placement and the future-tense "apoiará" (both re-confirmed sound, not touched again — third and second independent cold-read confirmations respectively that these are source-mirrored quirks, not translation noise) and found three further genuine issues, fixed:
  - Fixed — §1: "mas a todo o distrito, **cuja carta** também anexamos" — "cuja" (whose) reads as though the district owns the letter, when the letter is one written *to* the district. Rephrased as an appositive to remove the possessive: "mas a todo o distrito — carta que também anexamos em cópia."
  - Fixed — §3: restructured "embora ele os conheça, e as quais já tenha declarado culpadas, ele, conivente, busca ocultar" — the monolingual pass again found the os→as antecedent switch (pecados → mulheres) hard to track on a cold read even after round 1's clarification attempt. Recast the concessive "embora... , ele... busca ocultar" as two coordinate main clauses joined by an explicit contrast instead: "ele bem conhece e já declarou culpadas — mas, ainda assim, conivente, busca ocultar." This keeps the same split-antecedent content (the "os"/implicit-plural read for pecados, "as" for mulheres now carried by parallel indicative verbs "conhece e... declarou" rather than clashing clitics) while removing the concessive-clause construction that was forcing two grammatically distinct antecedents onto directly adjacent subjunctive-governed clitics. (Note: the monolingual pass's specific grammar objection — that "tenha declarado" needed the indicative — was itself incorrect, since "embora" licenses the subjunctive on both coordinated verbs it governs; the restructure was adopted for the antecedent-clarity concern, not the mistaken mood objection.)
  - Fixed (minor polish) — §2: "enganando-os... e em **seu** prejuízo" — "seu" is momentarily ambiguous (could misread as Paul's own detriment). Fixed to "em prejuízo **deles**."

- 2026-09-04 — **review round 4** (2 parallel passes: a fresh bilingual re-read, explicitly re-checking every prior fix including round 3's significant "cujos pecados..." restructure clause-by-clause against the English; and a fresh cold monolingual proofread). The bilingual pass came back **fully clean** — no content-level defects, every round-1/2/3 fix confirmed present and correctly applied exactly once, no regressions. The monolingual pass re-raised the already-resolved "1 Timóteo 6:5" placement and "apoiará" future tense (fourth and third independent cold-read confirmations respectively — not touched again) and, more importantly, **caught a genuine regression introduced by round 3's restructure**, plus two further clarity fixes:
  - **Fixed — regression from round 3** — §3: round 3's rewrite ("...ele bem conhece e já declarou culpadas...") had dropped the feminine plural clitic "as" that anchored "declarou culpadas" to *mulheres* (the antecedent switch this whole clause exists to carry) — leaving "declarou culpadas" with no grammatically consistent object at all, since the only preceding noun in scope, "pecados," is masculine. Restored the clitic: "ele bem conhece, e já **as** declarou culpadas —". Lesson: a restructure aimed at fixing one clarity problem (antecedent tracking) can silently reintroduce a different defect (a missing grammatical object) if the clitic that carried the original meaning isn't re-verified after the rewrite — the same "fixing the symptom can cement or introduce a new problem" pattern logged in other translation journals in this corpus.
  - Fixed — §1: "nem lhe escreveu em **seu** próprio nome" — "seu" is ambiguous between the writer's own name and Paul's name (the intended sense, continuing the theme of refusing to address/honour Paul by name). Fixed to "nem lhe escreveu **nominalmente**," removing the ambiguous possessive while keeping the sense.
  - Fixed — §1: "dispunha-se a **atravessar até** Antioquia" — calque of "was minded to cross to Antioch"; "atravessar" doesn't idiomatically take "até + destination" in Portuguese. Fixed to "dispunha-se a **partir rumo a** Antioquia."
  - Fixed — §2: "no grande dia da festa pascal — **cujo canto** faria estremecer quem o ouvisse" — "cujo" grammatically binds to the nearest noun, "festa pascal," producing the absurd reading "the feast's own singing." Restructured to remove the dangling relative: "no grande dia da festa pascal, **num canto que** faria estremecer quem o ouvisse."
  - Not fixed (contested, low-confidence) — §1: the monolingual pass flagged "a **nos** ajudar a **livrar-nos**" as an internally inconsistent proclisis/enclisis mix. Reviewed and left unchanged: proclisis after a preposition-governed infinitive ("a nos ajudar") is standard, idiomatic Brazilian Portuguese (as opposed to European Portuguese, which favors enclisis there), and mixing it with the enclitic "livrar-nos" in the same clause is common and unremarkable in pt-BR register — not a defect for this target dialect.
  - Round 4 is **not** clean (bilingual pass clean, monolingual pass found and fixed a genuine regression plus two clarity issues) — still short of the two-consecutive-clean-rounds bar. Proceeding to round 5.

This closes the object gap unambiguously and doesn't depend on the reader tracking a clitic back across an intervening clause.
