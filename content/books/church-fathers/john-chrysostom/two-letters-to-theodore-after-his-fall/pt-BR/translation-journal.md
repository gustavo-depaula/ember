## Decisions

- Chrysostom addresses Theodore as tu throughout, as in the sibling Chrysostom works; en-US mixes "you" with stray "thou/art/hast". God is addressed directly only inside Scripture quotations. The Psalm prayer in Letter 1 ¶1 ("Tende piedade de nós, Senhor") keeps the Vós of the Portuguese liturgical register; Ps 6:5 in Letter 1 §3 ("quem te louvará?") and Gen 3:12 in Letter 2 §2 cont. ("a mulher que me deste") keep the tu of the standard Portuguese Bible wording.
- Paragraph numbers are `**N.**`. en-US writes them as `N. ` list markers; pt-BR follows `.claude/rules/books.md`. Unnumbered paragraphs stay unnumbered, so Letter 1 still starts at §2 as in the edition.
- Scripture references stay inline after the quotation, with no added punctuation, and Portuguese book names (Sirach → Eclesiástico, 2 Chronicles → 2 Crônicas, Haggai → Ageu). The edition's Roman chapter refs "Daniel ii", "Daniel iv", "1 Samuel xv" are written with Arabic numerals (Daniel 2, Daniel 4, 1 Samuel 15).
- Quotations follow the Stephens English, not a standard Portuguese Bible (e.g. Isa 13:9 "mais preciosos do que o ouro não fundido", Jer 3:2 "como uma ave abandonada", Mal 3:2 "soda dos lavandeiros").
- Citations are mirrored as the edition gives them, including partial ranges (Matthew 11:28 for 11:28-30, 2 Corinthians 4:17 for 4:17-18) and Vulgate numbering (Haggai 2:10).
- Quotes are curly “ ”, as in en-US and the sibling works; a quotation nested inside a quotation (Letter 2 §4, Sir 5:8) uses ‘ ’.
- Letter 1 §10: en-US "then you will know its topics for another season, what I will say now" has lost words. Rendered by sense: "então conhecerás a sua amargura. Deixando estes assuntos para outra ocasião, o que direi agora…".
- Letter 1 §9: "those bitter deed and intolerable punishments" rendered by sense as "aqueles amargos tormentos e castigos intoleráveis".
- Letter 1 §13: "this abominate enmity" → "esta abominável inimizade".
- Letter 1 §19: "manifold in shame and character" read as "shape" → "múltiplo na forma e no caráter"; "(right action )" kept as the edition's parenthesis, "(a boa ação)".
- Letter 2 §1: "no one would indite a private individual" (= indict) → "acusaria", as in *Sobre o Sacerdócio*.
- Letter 2 §3 ¶2: "He has not forbidden to marry, but He has forbidden to commit adultery, may you be preserved from ever engaging yourself in marriage!" kept as the edition's abrupt exclamation.
- No footnotes in the source; nothing dropped.

## Review log

### Round 1

Focus: completeness paragraph by paragraph (33 ↔ 33), Bible book names and chapter:verse numbers (75 ↔ 75). Verdict: chapter text clean; one false journal claim.

Fixed:
- Key Terms row "judgment-seat / tribunal | tribunal / sede do juízo": positionally it paired "tribunal" with "sede do juízo", but "tribunal" is always "tribunal". "sede do juízo" renders only "the judgment seat" and "seat of judgment" in Letter 2 §2 cont. The row now says this.

Considered and rejected:
- Letter 1 §2 "the woman who had found the one coin" → "a mulher que achou a única moeda": the English "the one coin" is open to the same "only coin" reading as the Portuguese, so the rendering keeps the source's ambiguity.
- Letter 1 §5 "had seen magic, and astronomy" → "a magia, a astrologia": in 1889 English, "astronomy" covers the Chaldean star-lore Daniel's story describes. "Astrologia" is the correct modern word for it, not a change of meaning.
- Letter 2 §2 "how great an evil it is not to master pleasure, not to upset the ruling principle in nature" → "não dominar o prazer, subverter o princípio que governa": read literally, the second "not" makes the evil be *not* upsetting the ruling principle, which is nonsense. The pt-BR gives the sense the argument needs.
- Letter 1 §12 "the Lord of Sabaoth" → "o Senhor dos Exércitos": this is the standard Portuguese rendering of the title, with the same meaning.
- Letter 1 §12 "rolled up like a scroll" → "enrolado como um livro": "livro" here keeps the traditional Portuguese Bible wording of Isa 34:4, where it means the rolled book.
- Letter 1 §3 "who will confess you?" → "quem te louvará?": in Ps 6:5, "confess" means to give praise. "Louvar" is that meaning, not a different verb.

### Round 2

Focus: a clause-by-clause bilingual read of all 33 paragraphs against en-US. Verdict: 4 defects, all fixed. Three are Scripture quotations pulled toward the Almeida wording, which contradicts the Decisions entry "Quotations follow the Stephens English". The fourth is a garbled phrase.

Fixed:
- Letter 1 §12, Mal 4:1: "e não lhes deixará nem raiz nem ramo" → "e não restará nem raiz nem ramo". The source has "there shall be left neither root nor branch", with no agent and no "them".
- Letter 1 §18, Phm 14: "o teu benefício" → "a tua bondade". The source has "your goodness".
- Letter 2 §2 cont., Mt 11:28: "eu vos aliviarei" → "eu vos darei descanso". The source has "I will give you rest". Chrysostom's lead-in ("Is recovery from fatigue a grievous… thing? For it is to this that Christ calls us") argues from that word, so the verse has to contain "descanso".
- Letter 2 §5: "e mais nada ainda do que uma sombra" → "e menos ainda do que uma sombra". The source has "more naught even than a shadow". The old phrase reads as "and nothing more" and then breaks off.

Considered and rejected:
- Letter 1 ¶1 "it is seasonable for me to utter these words now, yea much more than for the prophet" → "É oportuno que eu pronuncie agora estas palavras, e muito mais do que o profeta no seu tempo": the elliptical comparison carries the same sense, that the words suit the writer more than they suited the prophet.
- Letter 1 ¶1 "until He has received our petition" → "até que Ele tenha atendido o nosso pedido": in a prayer context, "received" means accepted, which is what "atender" means.
- Letter 1 §4 "the flame which he had kindled by so many evil doings" → "a chama que se acendeu com tantas más ações": the sinner who kindled it is still clear from "más ações". There is no change of meaning.
- Letter 1 §13 cont. (Mt 23:37) and §18 (Phm 16): the source's "?" is rendered "!". Both are rhetorical exclamations, so this is punctuation, not meaning.
- Letter 1 §17 cont. "that he was laid to rest" → "que tinha ido para o descanso": this is a death euphemism, and the next sentence confirms it ("estava morto").
- Letter 1 §18 "Onesimus the unprofitable runaway thief" → "o escravo inútil, fugitivo e ladrão": "runaway" in this context means a runaway slave (the same sentence names "his master"), and "fugitivo" alone would lose that.
- Letter 1 §16 "let us breathe freely again" → "deixa-nos respirar": the clause is addressed to Theodore, so "let us" here means "allow us". The imperative is a correct reading.

### Round 3

Focus: a source-blind cold read of all 33 paragraphs, with each mark then checked against en-US, and a mechanical sweep of spelling, diacritics, crase, hyphenation, punctuation and quote pairing. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed. The mechanical sweep found nothing.

Fixed:
- Letter 2 §5: "Vês que até aqui aquele que cuida das coisas do Senhor" → "Vês que mesmo aqui". The source has "even here", meaning in this world, which answers the preceding "ganharão as coisas deste mundo". "Até aqui" reads as "up to this point".

Considered and rejected:
- Letter 1 §5 "um jovem cativo lhas fez resolver": this mirrors the source's causative "a captive youth having caused to be solved for him".
- Letter 1 §3 "E em que respeitam estas coisas à alma humana?": "respeitar a" means "dizer respeito a". The question is as odd as the source's "where do these things concern the human soul?".
- Letter 1 §17 cont. "quando o companheiro percebeu que este era um artifício gasto, lançou abertamente fora a vergonha": the source says the same ("when his companion perceived… he openly threw off shame"). Its referent shift is mirrored, not introduced.
- Letter 1 §16 "vejamos-te": the -s drops only before "nos", so the form is correct.
- Letter 2 §3 cont. "“nada equivale à alma” é dito de um poeta pagão": "dito" is a noun here (source: "is the saying of a heathen poet"). The rendering is defensible.

### Round 4

Focus: function words (prepositions, articles, demonstratives, connectors, possessive "seu/sua", "consigo" vs "com ele"), headings, and every place where the Portuguese must commit and the source is open (gender, antecedent, tu/vós), plus the mechanical audit and book.json. Verdict: 1 defect, fixed. The mechanical audit, headings, the Key Terms counts and the tu/vós trace found nothing.

Fixed:
- Letter 1 §19: "fornecendo-se uma à outra … corta uma delas e a despedaça … vencer a que resta" → "fornecendo-se um ao outro … corta um deles e o despedaça … vencer o que resta". The pair is "a indolência" and "o desespero", and a mixed-gender pair takes the masculine. No feminine noun is in scope ("estas duas coisas" comes only later).

Considered and rejected:
- Letter 1 §5 "make obeisance to His prophet, and command offerings and incense to be sacrificed to Him" → "mandar que lhe fossem sacrificadas oferendas": "lhe" could mean God or the prophet. The en-US capital "Him" is the edition's typography, and Dan 2:46 has the offerings made to Daniel, so the open pronoun is not a mistranslation.
- Letter 1 §3 "Shall we not weep then, I pray you, for such a man" → "peço-te": the paragraph's interlocutor is a single objector ("responder-lhe-ei"), and tu is the letter's address throughout. Theodore is spoken of in the third person here, but a singular "you" is still correct.
- Letter 1 §13 "the God of all" → "o Deus de todas as coisas" (and §13 cont. "Deus de todos"): both render the same open title (Greek τῶν ὅλων, "of the universe"). Neither says something the source does not.
- Letter 2 §3 cont. "For as they dash away" (antecedent "a runner") → "assim como estes passam correndo": the plural with a singular antecedent mirrors the source.

### Round 7

Considered and rejected:
