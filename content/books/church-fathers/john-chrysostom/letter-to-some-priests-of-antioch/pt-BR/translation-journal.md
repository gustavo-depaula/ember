## Translation Decisions

- **Romans 13:8 quotation** ("owe no man anything but to love one another") rendered as "a ninguém devais coisa alguma, senão o amor mútuo" — a direct, natural rendering of the sense. Checked the corpus for an existing pt-BR precedent of this exact verse; none found (only Romans/Hebrews/John 13:8 quoted elsewhere, different verses). This citation is a passing paraenetic reference, not a lexical proof-text the way Ambrose's or the Psalter quotations elsewhere in the corpus are (the argument doesn't hinge on a specific word choice in the verse), so no specific printed Portuguese Bible edition was required to match.
- **Financial/debt metaphor preserved literally throughout** ("dívida", "pagar", "saldar", "enriquecidos", "mais pobre/mais rico") since the entire paragraph's argument is built on the payment-of-love-as-debt conceit; flattening any one instance to a non-financial synonym would break the extended metaphor's internal logic.
- **No footnotes in source** — nothing to drop or keep.
- **No Latin phrases** in this letter — entirely English (translated from Greek by Stephens), no inline Latin to preserve.
- Proper names (Castus, Valerius, Diophantus, Cyriacus, Antioch) rendered in standard Portuguese forms: Casto, Valério, Diofanto, Ciríaco, Antioquia — matching the forms already used for Antioch elsewhere in `church-fathers/pt-BR` chapters (e.g. Malchion's Epistle, Africanus to Origen).

## Review Log

### Round 1 (clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity + mechanical checks)

**One defect found and fixed — a sense inversion from a mis-parsed archaic comparative.** "For he who pays it is **not less bereft** of it, as in the case of money when it is transferred to the receiver; but payment of love makes him who pays richer than before." "Not less bereft... as in the case of money" is an elliptical comparative — "not less bereft of it than [happens] in the case of money" — asserting the payer of love genuinely gives it up (is bereft), *to the same degree* as a payer of money is. The first draft read "not less bereft" as a flat negation ("is not deprived of it at all") and rendered "não fica dela privado" — the opposite claim, and one that also flattens the sentence's actual paradox: Chrysostom's point is that the giver of love truly gives it away (like money changes hands) **but** — contrary to money — is left richer, not poorer, for it. The "but" only sets up a contrast if the first clause affirms deprivation. Fixed to "não fica **menos** privado dela **do que** sucede no caso do dinheiro" (not less deprived of it than happens in the case of money), preserving the comparative and the paradox.

Mechanical checks: 4 paragraph blocks (title, editorial intro, salutation, body) in both files; 2 quoted spans (4 quote marks) in both (curly “ ” in en-US, straight " in pt-BR, matching the corpus's usual quote-style-per-language convention); 5 semicolons, 2 colons, 1 parenthetical in both; all 7 proper-name/reference tokens (Castus/Casto, Valerius/Valério, Diophantus/Diofanto, Cyriacus/Ciríaco, Antioch/Antioquia ×2, Chrysostom/Crisóstomo, Romans 13:8/Romanos 13:8) present 1:1 (Antioch/Antioquia 2:2).

Reviewed and rejected as faithful (not defects):
- "objects of their love" (plural) → "objeto do seu amor" (singular) — natural Portuguese collective singular for an abstract referent shared per-lover; no meaning lost.
- "derive equal enjoyment" → "obtêm igual satisfação" — a lexical register shift (enjoyment/prazer vs. satisfação) but not a fidelity error; meaning fully preserved.
- Romans 13:8 quotation rendered as a natural sense-for-sense translation rather than matched to a specific printed Portuguese Bible edition — this citation is a passing paraenetic reference, not a lexical proof-text the argument's logic depends on (see Translation Decisions above).

### Round 2 (cold monolingual pt-BR proofread + logical-connective check)

**One defect found and fixed — a logical connective flattened by repetition.** The source uses two distinct connectives back to back opening consecutive sentences — "**Therefore**, even if the letter..." then "**Hence** I also in my turn..." — the first draft rendered both as "Por isso", collapsing the source's lexical variety into a repeated connective a cold Portuguese reader notices immediately (two sentences in a row opening identically). Fixed the second to "Daí também eu, por minha vez, ...".

Checked and confirmed as faithful, not a defect: sentences 11 and 12 of the body both open with "Pois" ("For although you need no exhortation..." / "For even if you do not need any one...") — this mirrors the source, which itself opens both sentences with "For". Repetition here is inherited from the original, not introduced in translation.

Verb-conjugation sweep: all 2nd-person-plural verbs consistently in the *vós* paradigm throughout (chameis, recebestes, alcançastes, precisásseis, escrevais, informeis) — no vós/você mixing. The closing "we exhort you... and crave" 1st-person-plural shift in the source (after a letter otherwise in the 1st-person singular) is preserved as "exortamo-vos... e pedimos", not flattened to singular — this is a deliberate epistolary-plural shift in Chrysostom's own text, not a typo to normalize.

Diacritics spot-check: no missing or malformed accents found (espécime, íntimos, exílio, caráter, saciedade, vós, dívida, aliança — all checked clean).

### Round 3 (adversarial re-verification: mechanical invariants + pronoun gender/number agreement)

Re-derived all round-1 mechanical counts after the round-1 and round-2 fixes, to confirm neither fix broke an invariant: 2 curly-quote pairs (en-US) / 4 straight quote marks (pt-BR) — unchanged; 5 semicolons, 2 colons, 1 parenthetical — unchanged in both files.

**One defect found and fixed — an object pronoun that switched gender referring to the same antecedent within one sentence.** The round-1 fix introduced "aquele que **a** paga... privado dela" (feminine `a`/`dela`, agreeing with *dívida* from two sentences earlier) but the same sentence's second clause read "...torna mais rico... aquele que **o** paga" (masculine `o`), an internal gender mismatch — both pronouns stand for the same antecedent (the debt/payment being discussed) and cannot disagree. Checked the source: "him who pays it" and "he who pays" both use English's genderless "it"/absolute "pays" and don't resolve the question either way, so the object pronoun in the second clause was translator-added, not source-mandated. Removed it: "aquele que **paga**" (used absolutely, no object pronoun), which is both grammatical Portuguese and matches the English's own absolute "who pays" in that clause — and sidesteps the gender question entirely rather than resolving it wrong.

No other pronoun-agreement issues found on a full sweep of the chapter's object/possessive pronouns (a, o, dela, disso, isso, vos, vossa).

### Round 4 (corpus-grep verification of journal claims + independent full re-read) — text clean

### Round 5 (cold monolingual pt-BR-only read, no English visible)

**One defect found and fixed — a word order that invites a momentary misparse.** "...mas o pagamento do amor torna mais rico do que antes aquele que paga" fronts the predicate adjective and comparative ("mais rico do que antes") ahead of the postposed object ("aquele que paga"). A cold reader parses "mais rico do que antes" as a complete, self-contained comparison and then hits "aquele que paga" dangling with no clear syntactic role, before backtracking to see it as `tornar`'s object. Reordered to plain SVO: "torna aquele que paga mais rico do que antes" — same meaning, no fidelity change (the English "makes him who pays richer than before" is itself SVO), just removes the ambiguity the earlier word-order choice introduced across rounds 1 and 3's edits to this clause.

No other parseability issues found on a full cold read of both paragraphs.

### Round 6 (targeted re-verification of the four-times-edited clause + crase/preposition sweep) — text clean

The "he who pays it... richer than before" sentence has been touched by rounds 1, 3, and 5 (a sense inversion, a pronoun-gender mismatch, and a word-order misparse, in that order) — per the corpus's own lesson that "the round after a big fix round is the one most worth running", this round re-verified it from scratch rather than assuming the prior fixes composed correctly. Re-read cold: "Pois aquele que a paga não fica menos privado dela do que sucede no caso do dinheiro, quando este é transferido a quem o recebe; mas o pagamento do amor torna aquele que paga mais rico do que antes." Parses correctly and unambiguously now: the payer of love is deprived of it to the same degree money changes hands (first clause), but — contrary to money — the act of paying love enriches the payer (second clause, plain SVO). Single negation (não... menos), not a cancelling double negative — re-checked against the Ignatius double-negative defect class specifically and confirmed this is not that shape.

Crase and contraction sweep across the full chapter (à, às, ao, aos, pelo, pela, dela, nela): all correct — "à taça do vosso amor" (a + a), "pela estação/dificuldade/escassez" (por + a, feminine), "pelo pagamento" (por + o, masculine), no missing or spurious crases found.

Mechanical invariants re-confirmed unchanged after the round-5 edit: 5 semicolons, 2 colons, 1 parenthetical, 4/4 quote marks, all proper names 1:1, both in both files.

No defects found. **First round of a new clean streak** (round 4 was also clean, but round 5 found a defect and broke it — two *consecutive* clean rounds are needed to close, so one more clean round is required after this one).
