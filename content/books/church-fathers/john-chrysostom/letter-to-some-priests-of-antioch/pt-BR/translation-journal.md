## Translation Decisions

- **Romans 13:8 quotation** ("owe no man anything but to love one another") rendered as "a ninguém devais coisa alguma, senão o amor mútuo" — a direct, natural rendering of the sense. Checked the corpus for an existing pt-BR precedent of this exact verse; none found (only Romans/Hebrews/John 13:8 quoted elsewhere, different verses). This citation is a passing paraenetic reference, not a lexical proof-text the way Ambrose's or the Psalter quotations elsewhere in the corpus are (the argument doesn't hinge on a specific word choice in the verse), so no specific printed Portuguese Bible edition was required to match.
- **Financial/debt metaphor preserved literally throughout** ("dívida", "pagar", "saldar", "enriquecidos", "mais pobre/mais rico") since the entire paragraph's argument is built on the payment-of-love-as-debt conceit; flattening any one instance to a non-financial synonym would break the extended metaphor's internal logic.
- **No footnotes in source** — nothing to drop or keep.
- **No Latin phrases** in this letter — entirely English (translated from Greek by Stephens), no inline Latin to preserve.
- Proper names (Castus, Valerius, Diophantus, Cyriacus, Antioch) rendered in standard Portuguese forms: Casto, Valério, Diofanto, Ciríaco, Antioquia — matching the forms already used for Antioch elsewhere in `church-fathers/pt-BR` chapters (e.g. Malchion's Epistle, Africanus to Origen).

## Review Log

### Round 1 (clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity + mechanical checks)

**One defect found and fixed — a sense inversion from a mis-parsed archaic comparative.** "For he who pays it is **not less bereft** of it, as in the case of money when it is transferred to the receiver; but payment of love makes him who pays richer than before." "Not less bereft... as in the case of money" is an elliptical comparative — "not less bereft of it than [happens] in the case of money" — asserting the payer of love genuinely gives it up (is bereft), *to the same degree* as a payer of money is. The first draft read "not less bereft" as a flat negation ("is not deprived of it at all") and rendered "não fica dela privado" — the opposite claim, and one that also flattens the sentence's actual paradox: Chrysostom's point is that the giver of love truly gives it away (like money changes hands) **but** — contrary to money — is left richer, not poorer, for it. The "but" only sets up a contrast if the first clause affirms deprivation. Fixed to "não fica **menos** privado dela **do que** sucede no caso do dinheiro" (not less deprived of it than happens in the case of money), preserving the comparative and the paradox.

Mechanical checks: 4 paragraph blocks (title, editorial intro, salutation, body) in both files; 2 quoted spans (4 quote marks) in both (curly “ ” in en-US, straight " in pt-BR, matching the corpus's usual quote-style-per-language convention); 5 semicolons, 2 colons, 1 parenthetical in both; all 7 proper-name/reference tokens (Castus/Casto, Valerius/Valério, Diophantus/Diofanto, Cyriacus/Ciríaco, Antioch/Antioquia ×2, Chrysostom/Crisóstomo, Romans 13:8/Romanos 13:8) present 1:1 (Antioch/Antioquia 2:2).

Reviewed and rejected as faithful (not defects):
- "objects of their love" (plural) → "objeto do seu amor" (singular) — natural Portuguese collective singular for an abstract referent shared per-lover; no meaning lost.
- "derive equal enjoyment" → "obtêm igual satisfação" — a lexical register shift (enjoyment/prazer vs. satisfação) but not a fidelity error; meaning fully preserved.
- Romans 13:8 quotation rendered as a natural sense-for-sense translation rather than matched to a specific printed Portuguese Bible edition — this citation is a passing paraenetic reference, not a lexical proof-text the argument's logic depends on (see Translation Decisions above).

### Round 3 (adversarial re-verification: mechanical invariants + pronoun gender/number agreement)

**One defect found and fixed — an object pronoun that switched gender referring to the same antecedent within one sentence.** The round-1 fix introduced "aquele que **a** paga... privado dela" (feminine `a`/`dela`, agreeing with *dívida* from two sentences earlier) but the same sentence's second clause read "...torna mais rico... aquele que **o** paga" (masculine `o`), an internal gender mismatch — both pronouns stand for the same antecedent (the debt/payment being discussed) and cannot disagree. Checked the source: "him who pays it" and "he who pays" both use English's genderless "it"/absolute "pays" and don't resolve the question either way, so the object pronoun in the second clause was translator-added, not source-mandated. ### Round 5 (cold monolingual pt-BR-only read, no English visible)

**One defect found and fixed — a word order that invites a momentary misparse.** "...mas o pagamento do amor torna mais rico do que antes aquele que paga" fronts the predicate adjective and comparative ("mais rico do que antes") ahead of the postposed object ("aquele que paga"). A cold reader parses "mais rico do que antes" as a complete, self-contained comparison and then hits "aquele que paga" dangling with no clear syntactic role, before backtracking to see it as `tornar`'s object. ### Round 8 (post-merge audit) — reverses round 1: the "bereft" sentence was inverted, not fixed

**Major defect found and fixed — the round-1 "fix" installed a sense inversion, and rounds 3, 5 and 6 then polished the wrong reading.** Rounds 1/3/5 each edited the same clause without ever re-testing round 1's underlying parse; the thrash itself was the signal that the parse, not the wording, was wrong.

Round 1 read "For he who pays it is **not less bereft** of it, as in the case of money when it is transferred to the receiver" as an elliptical comparative ("no less bereft *than* in the case of money"), concluding the lover genuinely gives love up. That reading is untenable on three independent grounds:

1. **The "For" (γάρ) is causal.** The preceding sentence says "But this is not what commonly happens in the covenant of love" — i.e. in love the payer does *not* become poorer. A "For" clause states the *ground* for that claim. Under the comparative reading the clause asserts the payer is deprived exactly as a money-payer is, which contradicts the sentence it is supposed to support instead of grounding it.
2. **It contradicts the explicit premise two sentences earlier**: "they are both alike enriched by the payment". Both parties enriched leaves no room for the payer being bereft.
3. **It contradicts the clause it governs**: "but payment of love makes him who pays richer **than before**" — one cannot be both stripped of a thing and richer in it than before.

The most likely underlying text is "is not **left** bereft of it, as in the case of money when it is transferred to the receiver" — a flat negation, idiomatic Victorian English, and the only reading compatible with all three points. `left` → `less` is exactly the OCR-class corruption this same file demonstrably carries elsewhere ("Presybters" in the salutation). Either way the *sense* is not in doubt: Chrysostom's whole point is that love, unlike money, does not leave its giver poorer.

Fixed to "não fica privado dela, **ao contrário do que sucede** no caso do dinheiro" — "ao contrário do que sucede" rather than a bare "como sucede", because under negation "não X, como sucede com Y" can be misread as "not X *in the way* Y is". The explicit contrastive removes the ambiguity that round 5 was chasing by word order.

**Two further defects fixed:**
- **Concessive mood/tense.** "ainda que não precisásseis disso" and "ainda que não precisásseis de ninguém" used the imperfect subjunctive, which in Portuguese marks a counterfactual or past-referring concession; both English clauses ("even when you need it not", "even if you do not need any one") are present-tense statements of a present state. The same sentence's parallel concessive already used the present subjunctive ("embora não necessiteis"), so the file also contradicted itself. Fixed to "ainda que disso não preciseis" / "ainda que não preciseis". Round 2's verb sweep checked person/number (the *vós* paradigm) but not tense, which is how this survived.
- **Dropped intensifier.** "out of a very large number" → "dentre um grande número delas" lost the "very"; fixed to "dentre um número muito grande delas".

Verified and left alone (not defects): straight `"` quotes in pt-BR against curly `“ ”` in en-US — grepped 8 sibling `church-fathers/*/pt-BR` books, all use straight quotes (410, 290, 118, 90, 66, 32, 18 marks; curly essentially absent), so the journal's per-language quote-convention claim holds. Also re-confirmed faithful: collective singular "objeto do seu amor", "obtêm igual satisfação", the feminine pronoun chain "a paga… dela" (antecedent *dívida*/*aliança*, internally consistent).
