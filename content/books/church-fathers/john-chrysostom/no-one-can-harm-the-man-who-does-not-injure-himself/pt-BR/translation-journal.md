## Decisions

- Address: the readers as a body are vós (“não façais tumulto”, “não me faleis”, “eu vos suplico”); the imagined single objector is tu (“dize-me”, “Vês”, “Foste privado do teu dinheiro?”), as in the sibling homily journals.
- Pronouns for God are capitalized (Ele, Se, Sua, Ti), as in the sibling Chrysostom works.
- Paragraph numbers are `**N.**`; en-US writes them as `N. ` list markers.
- Scripture references stay inline where the edition puts them, with Portuguese book names (Jó, 1 Timóteo, Lucas, 2 Coríntios, Colossenses, Romanos, 1 Reis, Mateus, Jonas, Daniel). Quotations are translated from the Stephens English, not from a Portuguese Bible; unreferenced quotations (Prov 27:7, Ps 81:16, Ps 105:37, Dan 3:38 LXX, Jonah 3:10 repeated) stay unreferenced.
- Quotes are curly “ ”. The §1 quotation “All things” closes after two words in the edition while the speech runs on; mirrored as given.
- Editorial oddities kept: §6 “the better sort of men indeed cling to both the one and the other”; §17 “tramped on the flame” rendered “pisaram a chama”.
- No footnotes in the source; nothing dropped.

## Review log

### Round 1

Focus: completeness, paragraph by paragraph (title + 23 paragraphs aligned 1:1), plus Scripture names and chapter:verse numbers, and the mechanical audit (structure, footnotes, diacritics, markdown, `book.json`).

Verdict: chapter text clean; one false claim in the journal fixed.

Fixed:
- Terms table, row “harm / harmed”: “noun prejuízo” → adds that the seven “what harm did X do / was done to” questions (§2, §4 ×5, §11) are rendered “que dano fez / fizeram”. The row claimed prejuízo for every noun use, which would exempt those seven sentences from checking or invite a later round to “correct” them; “sustain/receive/suffer no harm” do use prejuízo.

Considered and rejected:
- §14 “arrested the threatened disturbance of their city” → “detiveram a ruína ameaçada da sua cidade”. The disturbance meant is the overthrow announced in the preceding quotation (“Nineveh shall be overthrown” → “será destruída”); “ruína” names that and is not a mistranslation.
- §15 Dan 1:10 “the children which are of your sort” → “dos jovens da vossa idade”. “Of your sort” here means the youths of their own standing and age, as in the Hebrew; “da vossa idade” is a defensible reading, not an addition.
- §13 “the clouds of birds which covered the whole face of the earth by the number of their carcasses” → “com o número dos seus corpos”. “Corpos” for the dead quails keeps the sense; a style choice.
- §9 “a foul and ugly face” → “um rosto feio e disforme”. Both adjectives are rendered; the pairing is a style choice.

### Round 2

Focus: clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of the whole chapter against en-US (the source), plus the mechanical audit and a per-paragraph term-frequency diff for injure/harm/damage.

Verdict: two defects, both fixed.

Fixed:
- §5, Paul's sufferings: “foi lançado ao mar” → “foi submerso no mar”. The source says “he was immersed in the sea” (the night and day in the deep, 2 Cor 11:25); “lançado” adds someone throwing him. §11 “because he was cast into the sea” keeps “foi lançado ao mar”, as its source says.
- Terms table, row “harm / harmed”: “§4 ×5” → “§4 ×4” plus the §4 Abel question. The fifth §4 question reads “what harm did death itself inflict on Abel” and is rendered “que dano infligiu”, not “fez / fizeram”; the row miscounted it.

Considered and rejected:
- §15 “they counted their sojourn there as an aggravation of their trouble” → “agravamento da sua provação”. “Trouble” here is the captivity just described; “provação” keeps the sense. A word choice, not a mistranslation.
- §11 “perpetually overlooked” → “perpetuamente desprezado”. “Desprezar” also means to disregard or neglect; the sense stands.
- §14 “were delivered from every kind of evil” → “foram livres de toda espécie de mal”. “Livre” as the short participle of livrar is accepted usage; meaning unchanged.
- §12 “the indolent and supine man” → “indolente e negligente”. “Supine” here is the moral sense, inert and careless; “negligente” renders it.

### Round 3

Focus: source-blind cold read of the pt-BR, each mark then verified against en-US; then a mechanical sweep of spelling, diacritics, crase, hyphenation, punctuation and quote pairing; plus the mechanical audit and `book.json`.

Verdict: clean. No changes to the chapter, `book.json` or the Terms table.

Considered and rejected:
- §10 “For it prepares you to thrust the sword against yourself, and renders you liable…, and makes your punishment intolerable” → “Pois prepara-te para cravar a espada contra ti mesmo, e torna-te sujeito…, e faz o teu castigo intolerável”. On a cold read the three verbs can look like imperatives, but the subject is “a riqueza” of the sentence before, and “torna-te sujeito a uma conta mais pesada” makes no sense as an exhortation; the indicative reading is the only coherent one.
- §14 “they so thoroughly abolished… as to have the happiness of hearing the declaration” → “eles a aboliram… tão completamente, …, um náufrago estrangeiro, que tiveram a felicidade de ouvir”. The “que” looks like a relative on “náufrago”, but it is the consecutive of “tão completamente”, and the plural verb resolves it to the Ninevites. Grammatical; the distance follows the source's long period.

### Round 4

Focus: function words, headings and forced commitments (este/aquele, former/latter, seu/sua, consigo, adversatives, gender of referents, tu/vós traced turn by turn), plus the mechanical audit and `book.json`.

Verdict: two concord defects, both fixed.

Fixed:
- §13: “pois aquelas não se gastaram” → “pois aqueles não se gastaram”. Source “the former did not wear out” refers to “clothes, and shoes”; with vestes (f) and calçados (m) the pronoun must be masculine, or it leaves the shoes out.
- §5: “prejudicam os que as sofrem” → “os que os sofrem”. The antecedent is the whole list (“loss of money, … banishment, … death”), which includes “o banimento”; a mixed-gender antecedent takes the masculine.

Considered and rejected:
- §10 “a demand … for satisfaction for his transgression against himself” → “pela falta cometida contra ele”. “Ele” inside the participial phrase points to the creditor-servant; the fellow servant committed the fault, so no other reading fits. “Contra si” is not required here.
- §10 “he never even gave him a thought: yet the parasites … were pampered” → “nunca lhe dedicou sequer um pensamento; os parasitas … eram mimados”. The “yet” sets the flatterers' plenty against the neglect; the semicolon juxtaposition and the following “mas o pobre” carry that contrast. No meaning lost.
- §7 “if you please, let us investigate” → “se vos apraz, examinemos” (vós) amid tu address to “ó homem”. It is an aside to the audience joined to the first-person plural, as in §1–2; consistent with the Decisions entry on address.
- §3 “that most malicious and ferocious demon” → “o mais malicioso e feroz de todos”. Elative and relative readings both fit; style.

### Round 5

Focus: Terms table, as a per-paragraph frequency diff in both directions; every falsifiable claim in this journal; proper names against standard Portuguese and the sibling church-fathers works. Also the mechanical audit and `book.json`.

Verdict: one defect, fixed.

Fixed:
- §17: “mas antes sofreram dano” → “mas antes foram prejudicados”. The source reads “were yet no wise profited, but rather damaged”. The Terms row renders the verb “damage” as prejudicar, as in §3 “injured and damaged me” → “me lesou e me prejudicou”. The fix also matches §14 “nor … were the Ninevites harmed” → “os ninivitas foram prejudicados”.

Considered and rejected:
- provação also renders “troubles” in §5 (“when suffering such great troubles” → “ao sofrer tão grandes provações”) and “trouble” in §15 (already rejected in Round 2). In both places the “trouble” is a trial the sufferer endures, so the word does not clash with “trial” → provação. The Terms row stays as it is.
- injustiça also renders §2 “what injustice is” and §6 “of unrighteousness and iniquity” (“de injustiça e de iniquidade”). Injustiça is the plain equivalent of both words and keeps the same sense as the §1 “wrong” row, so there is no conflict.
- “hurt” → ferir (§16 “hurt by another” → “ferido por outro”; §17 “able to hurt” → “poderiam ferir”), and ferir also renders §13 “smite their heads” → “lhes feria a cabeça”. Hurt is not one of the thesis verbs, and ferir keeps it apart from lesar and prejudicar. No row is needed.
- §11 “Nowhere was he harmed at all” → “Em parte alguma ele sofreu qualquer prejuízo”. This keeps the prejudicar/prejuízo family the “harm” row assigns. Using the noun for the passive verb is a style choice.

### Round 6

Focus: completeness, paragraph by paragraph (title + 23 paragraphs aligned 1:1), checking every quotation, parenthetical and Scripture reference, Portuguese book names and chapter:verse numbers. Also the mechanical audit and `book.json`.

Verdict: clean. No changes to the chapter, `book.json` or the Terms table.

Considered and rejected:
- §4 “Cain laid snares for Abel, yet he himself was not ensnared” → “mas este não caiu na cilada”. “He himself” is Abel, the parallel to “Joseph… yet he himself was not injured”; “este” names Abel without ambiguity. Nothing is dropped.
- §17 “the fire… undid their bonds” → “desfez as suas cadeias”. The bonds are what they were tied with (“bound, and cast into the furnace” → “atados”); “cadeias” covers bonds in general, so this is a word choice, not a mistranslation.

### Round 7

Focus: clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of the whole chapter against en-US (negation, subject/object, tense and mood, false friends, dropped qualifiers, quotation wording), plus the mechanical audit and `book.json`.

Verdict: clean. No changes to the chapter, `book.json` or the Terms table.

Considered and rejected:
- §1 “will make a recantation, and apology, and crave pardon” → “farão retratação e apologia, e pedirão perdão”. In Portuguese “apologia” means a defence, not an expression of regret, so it can look like a false friend. But the three verbs together already carry the regret: “retratação” renders the recantation and “pedirão perdão” renders the craving for pardon. “Apologia” in its sense of a spoken account of oneself does not contradict either. The rendering is defensible and is not a mistranslation.
- §13 “even when the memorials of God's benefits in Egypt were fresh in their minds” → “quando as lembranças … ainda estavam frescas”. “Even” has no word of its own, but “ainda” plus the clause after it (“e ainda gozavam efetivamente de muitos outros”) keeps the concessive sense. Nothing is lost.
