## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-06: This is a companion piece to `ignatius/martyrdom/pt-BR` — same genre (a letter from one church to another recounting a bishop's martyrdom, with an eyewitness first-person-plural passage), overlapping Roman/municipal vocabulary (proconsul, stadium, Kalends), and it reuses every term that document already established rather than re-deriving them (Trajano/Domiciano's `-us`→`-ico`/`-o` pattern, the demonym-adjective convention, lowercase `calendas`).
- 2026-09-06: Chapter titles (the `## Chapter N. ...` headings) are original to this ANF edition, not part of the ancient text — translated like any other heading, following the book's own English wording rather than inventing new emphases.
- 2026-09-06: Scripture references (1 Corinthians 2:9, Matthew 10:23, Matthew 26:55, Matthew 6:10, Acts 21:14, Romans 13:1-7, Titus 3:1) are kept inline exactly where the source places them — mid-sentence in three cases, not moved to end of paragraph or converted to footnotes — with book names localized to standard Portuguese Catholic usage (1 Coríntios, Mateus, Atos, Romanos, Tito).
- 2026-09-06: "For" as a sentence-initial connective → *Pois*; "Now" → *Ora*; "Wherefore" → *Por isso* — same three-way mapping established in `ignatius/martyrdom/pt-BR`'s journal, and it held here without needing an exception.
- 2026-09-06: Ch. 6's "the Irenarch (whose office is the same as that of the Cleronomus)" — both are obscure Greek municipal titles with no standard Portuguese equivalent. Rather than leaving one transliterated and one untranslated (which would look like an inconsistency), both were adapted with the same `-arch`/`-us`→`-arca`/`-ônomo` Hellenizing pattern already in use for Asiarch→Asiarca, so a reader sees two parallel coined titles rather than one translated and one left in Latin script.
- 2026-09-06: Ch. 9's "Away with the Atheists" is the pagan crowd's own slur (Christians refusing the gods were branded atheists) — kept literal as *"Fora com os ateus"* rather than softened, since Polycarp then ironically redirects the same words at the crowd itself; softening either occurrence would break that irony.
- 2026-09-06: No editor/scholarly footnotes in the source — only the inline Scripture citations handled above. No translator notes were needed.
- 2026-09-06: The narrator shifts to first-person plural ("we," eyewitnesses) starting in Ch. 15 (the burning) through the end — rendered as *nós*/first-person-plural verb forms throughout that span, distinct from the *tu* forms used in direct speech (Polycarp addressing the proconsul, and vice versa).
- 2026-09-06 — **review round 1**: exhaustive check (structural line-by-line diff against en-US — identical heading/line count; bracket count 20/20; all 8 Scripture references present with matching book/chapter/verse; blockquote count and placement; *tu*-form consistency through the whole proconsul dialogue, no `você` anywhere; diacritics present throughout) plus a semantic read chapter by chapter. Found and fixed one defect:
  - Ch. 17: "he did his utmost that not the least memorial of him should be taken away **by us**" — "by us" names the agent (the Christians would be the ones taking the relic), but the translation had rendered it as "lembrança dele **nos** fosse tirada" ("taken away **from** us"), reversing who acts on whom. Changed to "para que nem a menor relíquia dele fosse **por nós** levada", which also renders "memorial" more concretely as *relíquia* to match the immediately following "become possessors of his holy flesh" (a physical remnant, not an abstract memory).
- 2026-09-06 — **review round 2**: fresh sentence-by-sentence semantic pass over all 22 sections (re-verified the round-1 fix reads correctly and naturally in context), plus re-confirmation of the mechanical checks (heading/line count identical to en-US, 20/20 bracketed insertions, all 8 Scripture references present inline at the same points, 3/3 blockquotes, no `você` anywhere in the *tu*-form dialogue). Found and fixed one defect:
  - Ch. 13: "gathering together wood **and fagots**" had rendered "fagots" as *feixes de **sarmentos*** — *sarmento* specifically means a dry grapevine cutting, a detail absent from the English (which just means bundled sticks/kindling in general) and not otherwise supported by context. Changed to *feixes de **gravetos***, a neutral term for bundled twigs/kindling that doesn't add an unsupported specific.
- 2026-09-06 — **review round 3** (independent pass, run by a separate agent with no access to this journal's round-2 entry): full structural re-verification (heading/paragraph/blockquote/bracket alignment, all Scripture references inline and unmoved), a fresh sentence-by-sentence semantic comparison of all 22 sections, a battery of archaic-idiom checks ("even" = namely in Ch. 19, "even as" = just as in Ch. 1 and Ch. 22, "again" = "in turn" vs. temporal in Ch. 11 — all confirmed correctly disambiguated), full *tu*-form conjugation check across every dialogue exchange, and independent re-verification of the round-1 Ch. 17 fix (confirmed correct: *nós* is the agent doing the taking, matching "by us"). Clean — no issues found. (This pass ran on the text as it stood after round 1's fix but before round 2's *sarmentos*→*gravetos* fix; it did not have occasion to flag that wording, which is not a contradiction — round 2 already caught and fixed it independently.)
- 2026-09-06 — **connective-mapping check** (mechanical, run after round 3): counted every sentence-initial "For"/"Now"/"Wherefore"/"But"/"And" in en-US (16/4/2/11/20) against pt-BR's *Pois*/*Ora*/*Por isso*/*Mas*/*E* (14/4/2/11/20). The 2-count gap on "For" is not a defect: two of the sixteen are the two-word idioms "For this reason" (Ch. 2) and "For this end" (Ch. 17), correctly rendered as the equivalent Portuguese idioms *Por essa razão* and *Para esse fim* rather than literal *Pois* — every other bare "For" maps 1:1 to *Pois*. All five connectives fully reconciled; nothing to fix.
- 2026-09-06 — **review round 4**: independent re-verification of the round-2 *gravetos* fix (reads naturally: "gathering together wood and fagots" → "lenha e feixes de gravetos", no unsupported specific left in) plus a final full pass — every chapter/section number, Scripture citation, and count of digits in en-US matched 1:1 against pt-BR (line-by-line diff of all numerals); no missing/wrong diacritics found on a targeted scan of common nouns; re-read Ch. 2, Ch. 9–11, and Ch. 14 (the densest and highest-stakes passages — the martyrs' ecstasy, the interrogation, and the death prayer) sentence by sentence against en-US once more. Clean — no issues found. **Second consecutive clean round (3, 4) — review closed.**
