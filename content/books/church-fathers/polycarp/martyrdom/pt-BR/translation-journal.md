## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-06: This is a companion piece to `ignatius/martyrdom/pt-BR` — same genre (a letter from one church to another recounting a bishop's martyrdom, with an eyewitness first-person-plural passage), overlapping Roman/municipal vocabulary (proconsul, stadium, Kalends), and it reuses every term that document already established rather than re-deriving them (Trajano/Domiciano's `-us`→`-ico`/`-o` pattern, the demonym-adjective convention, lowercase `calendas`).
- 2026-09-06: Chapter titles (the `## Chapter N. ...` headings) are original to this ANF edition, not part of the ancient text — translated like any other heading, following the book's own English wording rather than inventing new emphases.
- 2026-09-06: Scripture references (1 Corinthians 2:9, Matthew 10:23, Matthew 26:55, Matthew 6:10, Acts 21:14, Romans 13:1-7, Titus 3:1) are kept inline exactly where the source places them — mid-sentence in three cases, not moved to end of paragraph or converted to footnotes — with book names localized to standard Portuguese Catholic usage (1 Coríntios, Mateus, Atos, Romanos, Tito).
- 2026-09-06: "For" as a sentence-initial connective → *Pois*; "Now" → *Ora*; "Wherefore" → *Por isso* — same three-way mapping established in `ignatius/martyrdom/pt-BR`'s journal, and it held here without needing an exception.
- 2026-09-06: Ch. 6's "the Irenarch (whose office is the same as that of the Cleronomus)" — both are obscure Greek municipal titles with no standard Portuguese equivalent. Rather than leaving one transliterated and one untranslated (which would look like an inconsistency), both were adapted with the same `-arch`/`-us`→`-arca`/`-ônomo` Hellenizing pattern already in use for Asiarch→Asiarca, so a reader sees two parallel coined titles rather than one translated and one left in Latin script.
- 2026-09-06: Ch. 9's "Away with the Atheists" is the pagan crowd's own slur (Christians refusing the gods were branded atheists) — kept literal as *"Fora com os ateus"* rather than softened, since Polycarp then ironically redirects the same words at the crowd itself; softening either occurrence would break that irony.
- 2026-09-06: No editor/scholarly footnotes in the source — only the inline Scripture citations handled above. No translator notes were needed.
- 2026-09-06: The narrator shifts to first-person plural ("we," eyewitnesses) starting in Ch. 15 (the burning) through the end — rendered as *nós*/first-person-plural verb forms throughout that span, distinct from the *tu* forms used in direct speech (Polycarp addressing the proconsul, and vice versa).
- 2026-09-06 — **review round 1**: exhaustive check (structural line-by-line diff against en-US — identical heading/line count; bracket count 20/20; all Scripture references present with matching book/chapter/verse [count corrected in round 9: there are **7**, not 8 — this entry and round 2's miscounted, and the error went unnoticed for seven rounds]; blockquote count and placement; *tu*-form consistency through the whole proconsul dialogue, no `você` anywhere; diacritics present throughout) plus a semantic read chapter by chapter.   - Ch. 17: "he did his utmost that not the least memorial of him should be taken away **by us**" — "by us" names the agent (the Christians would be the ones taking the relic), but the translation had rendered it as "lembrança dele **nos** fosse tirada" ("taken away **from** us"), reversing who acts on whom. Changed to "para que nem a menor relíquia dele fosse **por nós** levada", which also renders "memorial" more concretely as *relíquia* to match the immediately following "become possessors of his holy flesh" (a physical remnant, not an abstract memory).

Eight defects [stated as "Nine" until round 12 counted the bullets below: eight are defects, the last two record a `book.json` check and a structural re-verification that both came back clean.   - Ch. 2: "beds full of **spikes**" had been `leitos cheios de **espinhos**` — botanical thorns, which swaps the iron instrument of torture for a plant and softens the described cruelty. Now `leitos cheios de pontas aguçadas`.

Now `carrascos` / `carrasco` — added to Key Terms above.
  - Ch. 17: "**lest**, forsaking Him that was crucified, they begin to worship this one" had been `não abandonem aquele que foi crucificado, **e** comecem a adorar a este`, where the negation reads as scoping only over the first verb — leaving the second clause affirming precisely the martyr-worship the chapter exists to deny. Now `nem comecem`, which negates both. (Note this is the *third* defect the audits have found in Ch. 17, after round 1's agency reversal — the chapter's piled-up subordinate clauses are the hardest passage in the document.)
  - Ch. 21: `Xantico` → `Xântico`. **The round-1–4 reviews never caught this because this journal's own Key Terms row asserted the accent was unnecessary**, and later passes checked the text against the journal rather than the other way round.   - **The lesson this round adds.** Rounds 9 and 11 both closed on the finding that the journal must be audited against the text. What separates the two is method. A symmetric miscount — one stated identically for both languages — is consistent with every possible text, so it survives any amount of cross-checking; and a *recount* is only as good as the pattern it anchors on, which is how a regex keyed to sentence-ending punctuation silently dropped the one `But` that follows an inline Scripture citation.
