## Translation Decisions

- 2026-09-07: Translating from the Latin (`la/`) as the canonical source, per the "never translate from a translation" rule. `book.json` lists `en-US` (Maurer, 1949) as a secondary public-domain translation only.
- 2026-09-07: Maurer's en-US text inserts bracketed scholarly citations not present in the Latin (e.g. "*(Aristotle, Metaphysics, 1017a22–35.)*" after "as the Philosopher says in Metaphysics 5.7"). These are Maurer's own editorial apparatus, not Aquinas's text and not in the Latin source — dropped from pt-BR, matching the Latin exactly (same policy as dropping editor footnotes).
- 2026-09-07: Preserving the Latin's own heading structure per chapter rather than the en-US restructuring. Chapters 1–3 in the Latin carry a separate italicized sub-heading line under the `# Caput N` heading (e.g. `***De nominis entis et essentiae significatione***`); Chapters 4–6 have no such sub-heading in the Latin at all. pt-BR mirrors this exactly — sub-heading line for chapters 1–3 only, translated; bare `# Capítulo N` for chapters 4–6. (en-US instead folds a descriptive title into every chapter's H1, including 4–6, which have none in the Latin — an en-US-only restructuring, not followed here.)
- 2026-09-07: `ens`/`esse` distinction — this pair is the technical core of the whole treatise (Ch. 5 turns entirely on God being *ipsum esse*, not merely *ens*). Rendered strictly as *ente* (ens, a being) vs. *ser* (esse, the act of being/to-be) throughout, never swapped or blended, even where Portuguese idiom might prefer "existência" — consistency across chapters matters more than local elegance here.
- 2026-09-07: `materia signata` (Ch. 2) and `materia designata` (Ch. 2, later in the same chapter) are used interchangeably by Aquinas for the same concept (matter under determinate dimensions, the principle of individuation). Both rendered "matéria designada" in pt-BR rather than inventing two Portuguese terms for one Latin concept.
- 2026-09-07: No footnotes (author or editor) exist in the Latin source files — none to drop or preserve.
- 2026-09-07: Scripture/authority citations embedded in the prose (e.g. "ut in II *Metaphysicae* dicitur", "ut Avicenna expresse dicit") kept as inline attributions exactly as structured in the Latin, translated into Portuguese, without adding modern citation apparatus.
- 2026-09-07 (review round 1): A reviewer flagged that italicized Latin formulas Aquinas quotes from other authorities (*quod significat essentiam rei*, *quod quid erat esse*, *intellectu quoquo modo capi potest* in Ch. 1; three more in Ch. 2) were translated into Portuguese rather than left in Latin. **Kept as translated, not reverted** — the source text is Latin in its entirety, so translating every sentence (quoted formulas included) is simply what "translating the book" means here; the "keep Latin phrases inline" rule in the general guidelines targets recognizable liturgical/fixed Latin embedded in a *non-Latin* source (e.g. the Magnificat quoted inside French prose), not routine scholastic citations inside an already-Latin treatise. Confirmed against precedent: Maurer's own en-US reference translation renders all of these into English rather than leaving them in Latin (e.g. "what it is to be a thing," "the essence of a thing," "can in any way be captured by the intellect") — the same policy adopted here for pt-BR. Bare technical terms with no vernacular equivalent (*usia*) are still left untranslated, as before.
- 2026-09-07 (review round 15 fixes): Four defects, all glossary-consistency drifts (prologue/ch001/ch002/ch004/ch006 clean). Ch003 (2): the same "una" (invalid Portuguese, should be "uma") spelling bug already fixed once in ch002 (round 13) recurred twice more here — "nunca poderia ser una... é una enquanto está em Sócrates" — fixed to "uma"/"uma"; a book-wide grep afterward confirmed no other stray "una" remains. Ch005 (2): "participar da brancura de uma mesma razão" used "razão" for a *ratio* in the same shared-notion sense this very chapter renders "noção" three paragraphs earlier ("noção de natureza") — fixed to "noção" for internal consistency; and "não podem... ser significadas nem por si mesmas nem por diferenças acidentais" inflected the invariable "per se"→"por si" glossary term into "por si mesmas" — fixed to bare "por si". **Pattern across rounds 13-15**: the recurring defects are no longer new categories of error, but old fixed bugs (una/uma, per se/por si, ratio/razão-vs-noção) resurfacing at OTHER unfixed occurrences the same round's reviewers hadn't specifically checked — confirming each fix needs a same-round grep-style sweep for sibling occurrences, not just a spot fix, and that "chapter X was clean last round" does not mean chapter X has no instances of a bug fixed elsewhere.
- 2026-09-07 (review round 14 fix): One defect (prologue, ch001, ch002, ch003, ch005, ch006 all clean). Ch004's single most important sentence — "esse est aliud ab essentia vel quiditate" (stating the ens/esse... no, esse/essentia distinction that grounds the whole treatise) — was rendered "o ser é outra coisa que a essência ou quididade." Bare Portuguese "outra coisa que X" reads as a relative clause ("another thing, which is X"), nearly inverting the intended "something other than X"; the idiom requires "outra coisa senão X". The very same paragraph gets the identical construction right two sentences earlier ("sejam outra coisa senão a própria forma"), confirming the inconsistency. Fixed to "outra coisa senão a essência ou quididade". Swept the whole book for the bare "outra coisa que" pattern afterward — the three other hits are unrelated correct constructions ("outra coisa que a receba" = "another thing that receives it", a genuine relative clause; "outra coisa" alone; "qualquer outra coisa além dela") and were left unchanged.
- 2026-09-07 (review round 13 fixes): Three defects, all in prologue/ch002 (ch003, ch004, ch005, ch006 all clean this round). Prologue (1): a distributive-singular Latin interrogative ("quid...significetur et quomodo...inveniatur, et quomodo se habeat") governs three parallel singular subjunctives about one implied subject ("what X is"), but pt-BR pluralized the second and third ("se encontram"/"se relacionam") as if their subject were the nearby "e"-joined "essência e ente" rather than the singular "o que" — fixed to singular "se encontra"/"se relaciona", matching en-US's own "how **each** may be found... how **each** is related." This is the inverse of the usual postposed-compound-subject bug: here a genuinely singular (distributive) subject was wrongly pluralized by proximity to an unrelated "e"-joined pair. Ch002 (2): "per se loquendo" had drifted into "propriamente falando" with no trace of the "por si" glossary term at all — fixed to "por si, propriamente falando"; and "una" (feminine "one") appeared twice where standard Portuguese requires "uma" ("una" isn't valid pt-BR, only Italian/Spanish/archaic) — fixed both. **Lesson**: the "postposed compound subject → plural" rule chased down across rounds 5–12 can be over-applied to a case where the real subject is a singular pronoun/pro-form that merely sits near an unrelated coordinated pair — worth checking what the verb's subject actually IS, not just scanning for the nearest "e"/"ou" coordination.
- 2026-09-07 (review round 12 fixes): Three more defects. Ch002 (1): one more postposed-compound-subject slip — "não se põe este osso e esta carne"→"não se põem" (the very next clause in the same sentence already correctly used plural "são" for the parallel construction, confirming the singular was the outlier). Ch003 (1): "convém ao gênero ser predicado por si mesmo" mistranslated the Latin's "praedicari convenit generi **per se**" — *per se* modifies *convenit* ("it belongs essentially/per se to genus [to be predicated]"), not "praedicari" as if genus predicated itself; also drifted from this book's own established "per se"→"por si" glossary term into "por si mesmo" — fixed to "convém por si ao gênero ser predicado", placing "por si" against the verb it actually modifies. Ch006 (1): an "ou"-joined subject ("a forma ou a matéria") had been pluralized ("estão") against this book's own established convention that "ou"-joined subjects (as opposed to "e"-joined ones) take a singular verb (cf. ch005 "a natureza ou quididade delas é absoluta") — fixed to "está". Note this is the *inverse* mistake of the recurring "e"-joined-subject bug: a translator (or fixer) generalizing the "postposed compound subject → plural" rule too far, to a case where Portuguese "ou" keeps it singular. prologue/ch001/ch005 clean this round; ch004 clean.
- 2026-09-07 (review round 11 fixes): Five more postposed-compound-subject agreement defects (prologue/ch001/ch002/ch003 all clean), the same bug class introduced/fixed in round 10 — it turns out that bug was more widespread than the two spots fixed then. Ch005 (2): "se encontra nelas gênero, espécie e diferença"→"se encontram"; "não se toma da mesma maneira o gênero e a diferença"→"não se tomam". Ch006 (2): "se toma de outro modo o gênero, a diferença e a espécie"→"se tomam"; and, notably, the treatise's own **closing sentence** — "nele esteja o fim e a consumação deste discurso"→"nele estejam" — which round 8 had explicitly examined and defended as acceptable "concordância por atração" (agreement by proximity), but which round 11 flagged against this book's own established stricter house rule (rounds 5, 6, 10: postposed compound subject → plural verb, no exception). Sided with the house rule for consistency across the whole book rather than allowing one sentence to use a different, if independently defensible, agreement convention. Ch004 (1): a genuine "in tantum...ut" subjunctive result clause ("ut res materialis trahatur") had been rendered indicative ("é atraída") instead of matching the chapter's own subjunctive pattern for identical result clauses elsewhere in the same paragraph — fixed to "seja atraída". **Lesson**: this book's postposed-compound-subject rule needed a dedicated full-book grep-style sweep, not just fixing the two instances a single round happened to catch — round 10 fixed 2 occurrences, round 11 found 5 more that had been sitting in already-"clean"-declared chapters (ch005, ch006) the whole time.
- 2026-09-07 (review round 10 fixes): Three defects found (ch005, ch006 both clean). Ch002 (1): another false-friend, same class as round 9's "chatura" — "coparte" is a real Portuguese word, but in Brazilian Portuguese it is specifically legal/procedural jargon (a co-litigant in a lawsuit), not a general term for "an equal/coordinate part of a whole" (Latin *compars*). Fixed to "parte equiparada". Ch003 and ch004 (1 each): postposed compound-subject agreement errors — "é a unidade e a comunidade" and "se encontra ordem e grau" both paired a singular verb with a plural subject that happens to follow the verb rather than precede it; fixed to "são"/"se encontram", per this book's established rule (rounds 5–6) that a compound subject takes a plural verb regardless of word order. **Pattern across rounds 9–10**: real-word-but-wrong-sense errors (false friends) and postposed-subject agreement errors are a distinct blind spot from the mood/tense errors that dominated rounds 1–8 — worth a dedicated pass for each in any future review of this text, since they don't show up under a pure mood re-derivation.
- 2026-09-07 (review round 9 fix): One defect found (prologue, ch001-ch005 all clean): ch006's "como se diz que a chatura é a curvatura do nariz" (Latin: "simitas est curvitas nasi") used a real but wrong Portuguese word — "chatura" means exclusively "boringness/tediousness" (from colloquial "chato" = annoying/boring), not "flatness/snubness". The adjectival use two sentences later ("o chato é o nariz curvo", for "simum est nasus curvus") is fine, since "chato" does carry the flat/snub sense in that concrete context — only the abstract noun was a false friend. Fixed to "o achatamento é a curvatura do nariz" (an unambiguous standard Portuguese noun for flatness). This is the second round in a row where a plain wrong-word/spelling error, not a mood or agreement rule, was the defect — reinforcing round 8's lesson that a dedicated "is this actually the right/attested word" pass catches a different class of error than clause-by-clause mood re-derivation.
- 2026-09-07 (review round 8 fixes): Round 7 was fully clean, but round 8 — a maximally skeptical fresh re-read explicitly framed as "the decider" — found four more genuine defects, resetting the clean-streak counter. Ch002 (1, but 3 occurrences): "precindir" is not a Portuguese word (missing an "s" — the correct form, from Latin *praecidere*, is "prescindir") — a spelling error present in three places, all now fixed to "prescindida"/"prescinde"/"prescinde". Ch004 (1): "não pode... dizer que não é qualquer matéria... que impeça" mixed indicative "é" and subjunctive "impeça" within the single embedded proposition being denied under "não pode dizer que" — this book's established convention renders the whole denied proposition subjunctive, so fixed to "não seja... que impeça" (both subjunctive). Ch006 (1): the identical gender-agreement bug fixed once already in round 6/8 for "diferenças da cor, que são causadas" recurred earlier in the very same paragraph for the parent sentence "os princípios de que são causad-" (elided subject also "diferenças", feminine) — round 6 had fixed only the second occurrence and missed this first, sibling one; now fixed to "causadas". **Lesson**: even a "decider" round with three independent skeptical agents can still miss things (a plain misspelling nobody had flagged in 7 prior rounds, and a sibling occurrence of an already-fixed agreement bug in the very next sentence over) — there is no round count that guarantees exhaustiveness on its own, only the discipline of re-deriving fresh each time.

This is the first round with no findings anywhere in the book, including ch002, which had needed fixes in every prior round (1, 3, 4, 5, 6). - 2026-09-07 (review round 6 fixes): Corrected five more defects found by three fresh parallel review agents, each explicitly re-deriving prior fixes from scratch rather than trusting them (all held up). Ch002 (1): a Latin "sicut...ita" parallel deliberately mixes tenses (imperfect "praedicabatur"/"implicabat" for the backward-referencing genus/species half, present "praedicatur" for the newly-asserted species/individual half) — pt-BR had collapsed both to the present, breaking the internal consistency of a sentence that correctly kept "implicava" imperfect right next to it; fixed "se predica" → "se predicava" to match. Ch003 (1): "lhe convêm também a noção de gênero ou de diferença" — number-agreement error, plural verb with the singular subject "a noção" (gênero/diferença are a genitive complement, not a compound subject) — fixed to "convém", matching this exact construction's three earlier correct occurrences in the same chapter. Ch004 (1): "tal coisa, que é seu próprio ser" broke the subjunctive mood of the two other occurrences of the identical still-hypothetical-entity relative clause immediately before and after it in the same sentence-cluster — fixed to "que seja seu próprio ser". Ch006 (2): "diferenças da cor, que são causados" — gender-agreement error, masculine "causados" with the feminine antecedent "diferenças" — fixed to "causadas"; and the fixed technical phrase "unum per se" (glossed in this journal as "um por si") was rendered as "algo uno por si" in two of its four occurrences in this chapter — unified to "um por si" throughout. **Pattern holding across rounds 4–6**: nearly every remaining defect is an *internal* mismatch between two or more occurrences of the identical or structurally parallel Latin construction, not a wrong rule applied in isolation — the fix is never "learn a new mood/agreement rule" but "find every sibling occurrence and make them agree."
- 2026-09-07 (review round 5 fixes): Corrected six more defects, found by three fresh parallel review agents (ch003 clean; ch005 clean for the third round running). Ch002 (4, all internal mood inconsistencies): "não é" for "não seja" (an "X é claro" declarative, matching two other instances of the identical construction two sentences later in the same paragraph — the earlier "não seja" had over-generalized from the Latin's fronted-clause subjunctive rather than reading the governing "é claro" as a Portuguese fact-assertion); "alcança" for "alcance" ("acontece que" reporting a demonstrated fact, same rule already applied elsewhere in this chapter, takes the indicative); "se disser" for "se diz" (an open hypothetical needing future subjunctive, matching two other identical constructions elsewhere in the same chapter); and a **correction of round 4's own fix** — "significa" restored from "signifique" for the second half of what looked like a parallel "oportet ut X...Y" pair, but the Latin's second clause ("nomen autem significans...significat partem formalem") is actually an independent "autem" declarative outside the "oportet ut" scope, correctly indicative in the Latin and therefore correctly indicative in Portuguese; round 4 mistook it for a forced parallel. Ch004 (1): number-agreement fix, "diferem" for "difere" — the Latin elides a repeated "essentia" under one verb ("differt essentia substantiae compositae et substantiae simplicis"), but the Portuguese restructuring into two full "a essência..." noun phrases joined by "e" makes a genuine plural compound subject requiring "diferem", not "difere". Ch006 (1): "for" for "é" — another open-hypothetical "si + present subjunctive" needing Portuguese future subjunctive, the same construction type fixed twice already in ch004. **Lesson**: a review agent's suggested fix is not automatically correct — round 4's "significa"→"signifique" change in ch002 was itself a mood error, caught and reverted by round 5. Fixes from any round need to hold up under the next round's fresh read, not just be applied and trusted.
- 2026-09-07 (review round 4 fixes): Corrected eleven more verb-mood defects across ch002, ch003, and ch004, found by three fresh parallel review agents (ch005 and ch006 both came back clean for the second round in a row). The recurring failure mode this round was **internal inconsistency on identical or structurally parallel Latin constructions** — the same "non potest dici quod X conveniat" or "oportet ut X...Y" pattern rendered correctly in one place and with the wrong mood a few sentences later — rather than a single systematic rule being wrong. Ch002 (5): "se ponha" for "se põe" (negated-declarative quod-clause), "acontece" for "aconteça" (indirect question — Portuguese indirect questions take the indicative, unlike Latin), "se podem designar" for "se possam designar" (first of two identical relative-clause occurrences, the second was already correct), "possa sentir" for "pode sentir" (subjunctive result clause), "signifique" for "significa" (second half of a parallel "oportet ut...signifique...signifique" pair, the first half was already correct). Ch003 (3): "não é" for "não seja" (plain-fact relative clause), "convenha"/"convenha" for "convém"/"convém" (two of three "não se pode dizer que X convém" instances — the third, inside a "resta que" logical-conclusion construction, correctly stays indicative), "sobrevém" for "sobrevenha" (a "resta que" conclusion clause wrongly matched the subjunctive of a nearby but grammatically different "não se pode dizer que" clause instead of its own indicative-taking construction). Ch004 (3): "impeça" for "impede" (subjunctive after "não pode dizer que"), "se encontrarem" for "se encontram" and "se propuser" for "se propõe" (both future-subjunctive open/hypothetical conditions, matching the pattern already correct elsewhere in the book, e.g. ch003's "se se perguntar"). The general lesson carried forward: Latin mood does not map onto Portuguese mood by a fixed rule per construction type — cum-causal maps to Portuguese indicative, "non potest dici quod" maps to Portuguese subjunctive, indirect questions map to Portuguese indicative regardless of the Latin's own subjunctive — and the same governing phrase repeated later in a paragraph or chapter must be checked individually for whether it's the same construction throughout, not assumed consistent from checking one instance.

(6) The Ch. 6 esse-secundum fix from round 1 was grammatically faithful but awkward (fronting "a coisa subsistente" as subject of "pode" and then attaching an impersonal "entender-se que seja" after it) — smoothed to the impersonal construction "sem o qual se pode entender que a coisa subsistente seja".
