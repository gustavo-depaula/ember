## Translation Decisions

- Translated from the Latin (`la/`). Paragraph structure mirrors the Latin exactly: 60/13/32/21/28 non-heading paragraphs in chapters 1–5, including the Latin's short enumeration paragraphs that begin in lowercase ("primo quidem…", "secundo…"), kept lowercase.
- Headings mirror the Latin's bare `# Caput N` → `# Capítulo N`; toc titles "Capítulo N" (no descriptive titles exist in any language).
- Work citations follow the sibling convention ("no II da *Física*", "no XVI de *Sobre os Animais*"): "no II de *Sobre a Alma*", "no XI da *Metafísica*", "no IX da *Ética*", "no livro *Sobre a Geração dos Animais*", "nas *Categorias*" (*praedicamenta*), "na sua *Retórica*". The Latin's spelled-out ordinals (*in secundo de anima*) become Roman numerals, and titles are italicized though the Latin leaves them roman, as in *De Principiis*. No pt-BR sibling had rendered *De anima*; "Sobre a Alma" chosen to match "Sobre os Animais".
- Quotations from Aristotle, Temístio, Avicena, Algazel and the Averroist adversary are translated, italic where the Latin italicizes (*De Ente* policy for quotations inside an all-Latin source). Editorial ellipses "(...)" and Aquinas's parenthetical glosses inside quotations kept as in the Latin.
- The Latin "translations" of Aristotle Aquinas quotes are often awkward (Moerbeke's word-for-word Greek); rendered literally enough that Aquinas's glosses still find their words in the quote, smoothed only where Portuguese would be ungrammatical.
- ch005 final paragraph alludes to 1 Tm 6,20 ("falsi nominis scientia" → "ciência de falso nome") and Ef 3,8 ("aliorum sum minimus" → "o mínimo dos outros"); rendered literally, no reference added since the Latin gives none.
- ch002 *depravator* → "corruptor"; ch005 *perversor* → "pervertedor" — two different Latin words, kept distinct.
- No footnotes in the source; none added.
- Source edits: none. Two Latin punctuation gaps were left as printed and resolved only in the pt-BR: ch003 ¶ "Tertio, sequeretur…" ends without a full stop; ch004 "species conservantur in intellectu est enim locus specierum" lacks a comma/semicolon before "est enim".

## Review log

### Round 1

Focus: completeness, paragraph by paragraph, against `la/`; Scripture names and references. Verdict: ISSUES 3. All three fixed. No dropped, added, merged or split paragraphs. Every quotation, parenthetical and Latin gloss is present. The text has no explicit Scripture citations; the two ch005 allusions are present.

Fixed:
- ch001 ¶57 ("Ergo, ex mixtione elementorum necdum intellectus, sed nec anima vegetativa producitur"): "não é produzido nem sequer o intelecto, nem mesmo a alma vegetativa" → "não só não é produzido o intelecto, mas nem mesmo a alma vegetativa". *necdum … sed nec* means "not only not X, but not even Y". "Nem sequer o intelecto" turned the intellect into the least-expected case and inverted the argument.
- ch003 ¶19, Ethics quotation ("intellectivi enim gratia quod unusquisque esse videtur"): "pois cada um parece ser em vista do intelectivo" → "pois é em vista do intelectivo, que é o que cada um parece ser". *quod* refers to the intellective: each man seems to *be* it. Aquinas's gloss on the next lines ("non … quod homo sit solus intellectus") depends on that identification.
- ch003 ¶30 ("cui aliqualiter communicat materia non totaliter comprehendens ipsam"): "do qual de algum modo comunica a matéria, sem o abranger totalmente" → "com o qual a matéria de algum modo comunica, sem a abranger totalmente". *ipsam* is feminine, so it means the soul, not the masculine *ser*. *materia* is the subject of *communicat*; the old word order made it read as the object.

Considered and rejected (not defects):
- ch002 ¶12 "qua nihil possit inveniri subtilius et pulchrius" → "mais sutil e mais bela que a qual nada se possa encontrar": the word order is awkward, but the sentence is grammatical and complete.
- ch001 ¶36 "*cuius enim causa unumquodque*" → "*pois cada coisa é em vista de algo*": a loose rendering, but it keeps the "for the sake of" sense that Aquinas glosses next.
- ch004 ¶10 "secundum phantasmata illustrata" → "nos fantasmas iluminados": same meaning.
- ch005 ¶19 "isto é" added before Aquinas's unmarked gloss "quantitas vel multitudo sine ordine": it only marks a gloss that the Latin already contains.
- ch005 ¶23 "pronuntiare praesumunt, quod ipse dicit, hoc sensisse omnes…" → "presumem pronunciar que todos … pensaram aquilo que ele diz": the sentence is restructured, but the meaning is unchanged.

### Round 2

Focus: a clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of all five chapters against `la/`, plus the mechanical audit (`book.json`, paragraph counts, italics, Key Terms frequency in both directions, spelling). Verdict: ISSUES 1, fixed. The journal's claims and Key Terms rows match the text.

Fixed:
- ch005 ¶23, Themistius quotation ("sed quod quidem dixi pronunciare quidem de eo quod videtur philosophis, singularis est studii et sollicitudinis"): "pronunciar-se, de fato, sobre o que parece aos filósofos acerca do que eu disse" → "como eu disse, pronunciar-se, de fato, sobre o que parece aos filósofos". *quod quidem dixi* is the parenthetical "as I said" (Greek ὅπερ ἔφην). The old wording made "what I said" the subject of the philosophers' opinion.

Considered and rejected (not defects):
- ch005 ¶4 "quod multum dicitur non solum numero…" → "*múltiplo* se diz…": the italic marks a word cited as a word. It adds no content.
- ch005 ¶8 "quia est una quidditas per quam intelligitur" → "pela qual se entendem": the singular verb becomes plural to agree with the two things understood. The meaning is the same.
- ch004 ¶19 "secundum hoc intelligantur" → "são entendidos" (masculine, so the phantasms): ¶20–21 argue about phantasms being made intelligible, so the phantasms are the right subject.
- ch001 ¶34 "si quis etiam in aliis rebus consideret" → "se alguém considerar também as outras coisas": the preposition *in* is dropped, but the sense ("look at other things too") is the same.

### Round 3

Focus: a source-blind cold read of the pt-BR, with each mark then checked against `la/`, plus a mechanical sweep of spelling, diacritics, crase, italics and quotation pairing, and the `book.json` audit. Verdict: ISSUES 1, fixed. The sweep found nothing.

Fixed:
- ch002 ¶13 ("Unde miror ex quibus Peripateticis hunc errorem se assumpsisse glorientur"): "me admiro de quais peripatéticos se gloriam de ter tomado esse erro" → "me admiro de quais peripatéticos eles se gloriam de ter tomado esse erro". Without a subject, "quais peripatéticos" reads as the subject of "se gloriam", so the Peripatetics seem to be the ones boasting. In the Latin the subject is the Averroists, and *ex quibus Peripateticis* names where they claim to have taken the error from.

Considered and rejected (not defects):
- ch003 ¶9 "si sic transformant, ut consueverunt definire" → "se transformam assim, como costumaram definir": *se* is the conjunction "if" and the verb is active, as in the Latin. The reflexive misreading is not the natural one after "costumaram definir".
- ch005 ¶11 "participantes ab una forma separata unius veritatis cognitionem" → "que participassem de uma só forma separada o conhecimento de uma só verdade": "participar algo de alguém" is heavy but grammatical, and it keeps the Latin construction.
- ch004 ¶10–12 "Primeira, … / Segundo, … / Terceiro, …": the shift mirrors the Latin "Primum … Secundo … Tertio".
- ch001 ¶5 "(pelas coisas que se seguem)", ¶54 "ainda, além disso, acerca disso", ch003 ¶30 and ch004 ¶8 (anacolutha): each follows the Latin as printed.

### Round 4

Focus: function words (prepositions, articles, demonstratives, connectors, possessive *seu/sua*), headings and section labels, and every point where the Portuguese must commit and the Latin is open (gender of a referent, pronoun antecedent, second-person address). Also the mechanical audit and `book.json`. Paragraph numbers below count non-heading paragraphs from 1. Verdict: CLEAN. Nothing changed.

The text has no dialogue. Every second person (ch003 ¶4, ¶10, ¶11; ch004 ¶6; ch005 ¶8, ¶14, ¶22) is a generic interlocutor or a quoted voice and is rendered with *tu* throughout. The ch005 ¶28 challenge to the adversary is in the third person in both languages.

Considered and rejected (not defects):
- ch005 ¶22 "nec intellectus agens, de quo Aristoteles loquitur, est unus qui est illustrans, nec etiam possibilis qui est illustratus" → "nem o intelecto agente de que Aristóteles fala é um só, que é o que ilumina, nem tampouco o possível, que é o iluminado": the two *qui est* clauses are parallel descriptions (agent = illuminating, possible = illuminated), and the pt keeps that parallel. Either reading leads to the same conclusion, that the agent intellect is not the single first illuminator.
- ch003 ¶28 "forma materialis, et non sit denudata … non est intellecta" → "forma material, e que não está despojado … não é entendido": the Latin participles agree with *forma*, the pt ones with *intelecto*. Same referent, and grammatical either way.
- ch005 ¶24 "lex eorum" → "a sua lei" and ¶28 "eius errori … ignorantiae" → "ao seu erro … à sua ignorância": each "seu/sua" has one plausible possessor in context (the Latins; the boasting adversary).
- ch003 ¶3 "Virtus … huius demonstrationis" → "A força … dessa demonstração": the force of an argument, not the faculty sense that the Key Terms row *virtus → virtude* covers.

### Round 7

Focus: a clause-by-clause bilingual fidelity read of all five chapters against `la/`, checking negation, subject and object, tense and mood, false friends and dropped qualifiers. Also the mechanical audit and `book.json`. Paragraph numbers count non-heading paragraphs from 1. Verdict: CLEAN. Nothing changed.

Considered and rejected (not defects):
- ch004 ¶8 "*ut sciens dicitur qui secundum actum*" → "*como se diz sábio aquele que o é segundo o ato*": *sciens* is "the one who knows" (Aristotle's ἐπιστήμων). "Sábio" is the usual Portuguese Thomist rendering of it, and the gloss that follows ("como se diz que o sábio está em ato enquanto tem o hábito") uses the same word, so the gloss still finds its word in the quote.
- ch002 ¶1, Themistius, "*sed investitur toti potentia intellectui qui factivus*" → "*mas reveste-se de todo o intelecto em potência aquele que é factivo*": the factive intellect is the subject, and it covers the whole potential intellect. The building simile that follows (the builder inside the wood) confirms that reading.
- ch002 ¶5, Theophrastus, "*potentia autem omnia bene*" → "*isto está bem: que … em potência é todas as coisas*": *bene* renders the Greek καλῶς ("rightly said") and qualifies the whole statement, not *omnia*.
- ch004 ¶14 "nisi post nostrum addiscere aut intelligere" → "aprender ou entender": the sentence's pattern elsewhere is *addiscere aut invenire*, but the Latin prints *intelligere* here. It is not an unmistakable scan slip, so it is mirrored.
- ch001 ¶39 "Nihil autem potest esse sine eo, quod inest ei per se" → "aquilo que lhe convém por si": the previous clause has just said "per se ei convenit esse in materia", and the pt reuses its verb for the same predicate. The argument is unchanged.
