Rodada 4

Rodada 4 · Do princípio

The goal is the rule, not the library

Rounds two and three redesigned a catalogue. The problem was never that the catalogue is ugly; it is that someone holding a rule doesn't see how a single practice gets into it.

A rule is made of few thingsOf 439 prayers, 81 are hymns, 87 are office variants and 89 are novenas. Nobody schedules A solis ortus cardine for 7h30. What goes on the string is the small set the corpus already marks as daily, Marian, Eucharistic, meditation, reading, the Office. The page should show that set, not the alphabet.
The best suggestions are already yoursThe app records what you pray outside the plan and shows it as “Além do plano”. Something prayed six times this month without being in the rule is the surest candidate there is, and needs no editor to rank it. Next surest: the part of your day that holds nothing yet.
One sign, wherever a practice appearsThe open bead at the foot of the string means “not on your string yet; tap to hang it”. If the same bead sits beside a practice everywhere it shows up, the sheet teaches the gesture once and the page is only one of the places it works.
What this round takes from you, and what it leaves open
  • Kept: the open bead in the sheet; a page built like Orações (title, one row of tabs, holy-card rows); a first tab of suggestions.
  • Changed: the tabs are the kinds of thing a rule holds, read from the categories the manifests already carry, so there is one row and no “Toda devoção”. Orações itself can take the same first tab afterwards; I've left it out of this round.
  • No editor's list in the first tab. Every row there states a fact about you or the calendar. A hand-picked “classics” section can come later, once you've chosen it.
  • Invented for the mock: the counts in “Você já reza” and the gaps in the day. The data for both exists (completions outside the plan; the hours the rule's times fall in).
