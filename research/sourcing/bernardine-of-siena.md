# St. Bernardine of Siena, O.F.M. (1380–1444) — sourcing

Two bodies of work:

**A. Latin works**, written by him as model sermons and treatises. Standard critical edition: *S. Bernardini Senensis Opera omnia*, Quaracchi (Ad Claras Aquas, Florence: Collegium S. Bonaventurae), 9 vols, 1950–1965. The older collected edition is Jean de La Haye's (Paris/Lyon 1635, reissued Lyon 1650; Venice 1745), in 5 tomes. Contents, by Quaracchi:
- *Quadragesimale de christiana religione* (Quaracchi vols I–II), the Lenten cycle on faith, sin, restitution and moral theology.
- *Quadragesimale de evangelio aeterno* (vols III–V), including the famous sermons on contracts and usury (*De contractibus et usuris*).
- Sermones de festivitatibus (on the saints and on the Virgin, including the Marian sermons), extraordinary sermons and the *Seraphim* cycle (Padua 1423, a reportatio), plus *opuscula* and letters (vols VI–IX). I did not confirm the exact allocation per volume.
- **Spurious** pieces in La Haye, for instance the *Commentarii in Apocalypsim* (La Haye tome V), which is not by Bernardine. Quaracchi excludes them.

**B. Italian sermons** (*prediche volgari*), taken down by hearers as he preached:
- **Siena 1427** (Piazza del Campo, 15 Aug–end of Sept 1427): 45 sermons recorded by Benedetto di Maestro Bartolomeo. Edited by Luciano Banchi, Siena 1880–1888, 3 vols. Critical edition: Carlo Delcorno, Rusconi 1989 (C).
- **Florence 1424 and 1425** (S. Croce): edited by Ciro Cannarozzi OFM, Pistoia/Firenze 1934–1958 (5 vols).
- **Siena 1425** and other cycles, partly edited (Cannarozzi 1958).
- Derived anthologies: *Novellette ed esempi morali* (exempla extracted from the sermons; Carabba 1916), and Zambrini's 19th-century selections.

Nothing by him is in the corpus.

## Prediche volgari, Siena 1427

Original sources, best first:
1. **it.wikisource, *Prediche volgari*, ed. Luciano Banchi (Siena: Tip. edit. all'insegna di S. Bernardino, 1880–1888)**: https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Prediche_volgari, proofread page by page against `Indice:Bernardino da Siena - Prediche volgari I.djvu` and the later volumes. It has 50 subpages: Introduction, *Vita di San Bernardino*, Prologue, **Prediche I–XLV**, and indexes. Status SAL 75% (October 2024). By the Wikisource search index the subpages total 427,111 words, which includes Banchi's footnotes on manuscript variants ("Il Cod. Pal. e il Cod. Sen. 6…"). Tier A (Banchi died 1887). Each sermon has its rubric, e.g. "Predica XXXVIII. Dei mercatanti e de' maestri e come si deve fare le mercanzìe". **This is the base text.**
2. Banchi's three volumes as scans: https://archive.org/details/lepredichevolgar01bern, `…02bern`, `…03bern` (Toronto copies, OCR 138k + 158k + 173k = 468k words), and the Google copy `lepredichevolga01bancgoog`. The OCR is good, modern type. Use it to check the Wikisource text.
3. ***Prediche volgari … per la prima volta messe in luce***, Siena 1853: https://archive.org/details/predichevolgari00berngoog (104k OCR words). An earlier, partial printing; I did not establish its exact scope (probably a selection of the 1427 cycle). A second witness.
4. Delcorno's critical edition (Rusconi 1989): tier C.

English, best first:
1. **Saint Bernardine of Siena, *Sermons*, selected and edited by Don Nazareno Orlandi, translated by Helen Josephine Robins, Siena: Tipografia Sociale, 1920** (dedicated to Fr. Paschal Robinson): https://archive.org/details/MN5033ucmf_1 (108k OCR words). The OCR is fair; the table of contents is garbled. A second scan is `bwb_C0-ANN-935` (1920, poor OCR on the opening pages). Tier A (published 1920; Robins died 1951, so PD in the EU since 2022 as well). Its sermons follow Banchi's titles ("How he who hath an office must administer justice", "Of the twelve damsels whom the Virgin Mary had round about her", "Of alms…", "How we ought to love God, and how he took farewell of the people"…), so it is **a selection of about 30 of the 45**, and possibly abridged within sermons. Check each against the Italian before pairing.
2. No other PD English. Iris Origo, *The World of San Bernardino* (1962), is a study and tier C.

Structure: 45 sermons, each one chapter. The footnotes are Banchi's apparatus of variants and Bible references; keep the Bible references and drop the variants.

## Novellette ed esempi morali

- it.wikisource, Lanciano: Carabba 1916 ("Scrittori nostri"): https://it.wikisource.org/wiki/Novellette_ed_esempi_morali_(Bernardino_da_Siena). 62 subpages: thematic sections ("Ad ogni morbo la sua medicina", "Amore coniugale", "Carità, elemosina, amicizia" …), an introduction and a bibliography. SAL 100%. Tier A. The editor's introduction (1916) is also old enough. Size about 40k (an estimate from 62 short pages, not counted).
- It is an anthology of exempla cut from the sermons, so it partly duplicates the Prediche. Import it as a separate small book or skip it.
- English: Ada Harrison, *The Examples of San Bernardino* (London 1926) is the obvious match, but I could not locate it or confirm its status. Unverified.

## Florence 1424/1425 and Siena 1425 sermons

- Cannarozzi's editions (Pistoia 1934; Firenze 1940, 1958) are on archive.org only as lending copies (`lepredichevolgar0001bern`, `…0002bern`, `…0001unse`, `…0002unse`, 1934; "access-restricted"). Cannarozzi's death date is unknown to me. The Italian 20-year rule for critical editions (L. 633/1941 art. 85-quater) may have freed the text, but that is **unverified**. Treat it as C until checked. No English.

## Latin Opera omnia

- **La Haye edition, Lyon (Huguetan & Ravaud) 1650**, Google scans on archive.org: tome III `bub_gb_uE16JD0lB5IC` (625k OCR words), tome IV ("Sermones eximii de Christo Domino, Eucharistiae sacramento, Deipara Virgine…") `bub_gb_oYwF_SmrvjUC` and `bub_gb_kzjBUYR3Ba0C` (312k each), tome V `bub_gb_Z3KUpVKF3vkC` and `bub_gb_oolTj78b940C` (the Apocalypse commentary, spurious). I did not locate tomes I–II as separate items. Tier A. The OCR is **very poor** (two-column folio, long s, abbreviations: "fign i (icantur", "ChnAi eAcharitas"). It is unusable without fresh OCR.
- Quaracchi 1950–65: no digital copy found. It is a modern critical edition; tier C in practice, although the same 20-year Italian rule could apply (unverified).
- No clean Latin text on documentacatholicaomnia, thelatinlibrary or Corpus Corporum that I could find.
- English: only scattered excerpts (e.g. the Office of Readings passage on the Holy Name, under liturgical copyright). Nothing usable.

## Portuguese

No Portuguese translation of the *Prediche volgari*, the *Novellette* or the Latin works found (pt.wikisource, dominiopublico, archive.org, web search; Paulus has sermon series for Bernard of Clairvaux and Anthony of Padua, not Bernardine). pt-BR from our own translation of the Italian.

## Recommendation

- Layout: `bernardine-of-siena-opera-omnia/prediche-siena-1427/` (it/ + en-US/ for the Robins sermons), `…/novellette-ed-esempi/`, later `…/opera-latina/<work>`.
- **Import first: the 45 Siena 1427 sermons** from Wikisource (clean, proofread, Banchi apparatus), with Robins 1920 English for the sermons it covers. That is his most important vernacular work and the only one cleanly available.
- Then (optional) the *Novellette*.
- Cannot be sourced cleanly: the Latin corpus, which is the larger and doctrinally weightier half (the 1650 folio OCR is unusable and Quaracchi is not online), and the Florence sermons (Cannarozzi, tier unverified). The Latin would need a fresh-OCR project on the 1650 or 1745 folios, which is large.
- **Total available now**: about 460k Italian words (Prediche 1427 ~420k, Novellette ~40k) and about 100k English words (Robins). Unavailable: the Latin, about 1.5M words (estimated from the 1650 OCR), and the Florence and Siena 1425 cycles.

Raw downloads (Banchi OCR, Robins OCR, La Haye OCR) are in `research/sourcing/bernardine-of-siena/raw/`.
