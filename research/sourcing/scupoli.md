# Lorenzo Scupoli, C.R. (Theatine, c.1530–1610) — sourcing

Works, as gathered in the standard collected editions (Paris, Imprimerie Royale 1660, after Rome 1657; reprinted in Venice 1770 and others). The 1770 editor's preface lists them, and so does the footnote in the 1842 Naples edition:

1. **Il Combattimento spirituale**: 33 chapters in 1589, grown by the author to **66 chapters** in the final form (1610).
2. **Aggiunta al Combattimento spirituale** (the "Supplement"): 38 chapters in the Rivingtons English, 2 parts in the Italian editions.
3. **Della pace interiore, o Sentiero del Paradiso** ("Path of Paradise", "Of Inward Peace"): about 25–30 short chapters.
4. **Dei dolori mentali di Cristo nella sua passione.** **Doubtful.** The 1770 editor says it was attributed to Scupoli on no good basis, having first appeared anonymously in Naples, and that Santo Milani first joined it to the *Combattimento* (Milan 1599). It needs flagging, not silent attribution.
5. **Modo di consolare e aiutare gl'infermi a ben morire**: a short manual for assisting the dying.

Authorship of the *Combattimento* itself was long disputed: it was attributed to the Spanish Benedictine Juan de Castañiza in the Latin *Pugna spiritualis* and in early English versions "by J. Castaniza". The settled view gives it to Scupoli.

Nothing by Scupoli is in the corpus (`_corpus-books.txt`, `content/books/`).

## Il Combattimento spirituale, with the Aggiunta and the Sentiero

Original sources (I found no clean Italian text online; all of these are OCR of scans), best first:
1. **Naples 1842, "quarta edizione riscontrata e corretta, divisa in due parti"**: https://archive.org/details/CombattimentoSpiritualeV1, text at `…/CombattimentoSpiritualeV1_djvu.txt` (50k words). Tier A. It is set in modern type with no long s, so the OCR is fair ("Coiabattimmto Smrìtuale" in the dedication, but body text mostly readable). It appears to hold the Combattimento with part of the sequels. I did not locate vol. 2.
2. **Venice 1770 ("colla giunta…"), following the Paris Imprimerie Royale 1660 text**: https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_v6CR_PzdBAcC (99k OCR words). Another copy is `bub_gb_zOJIBIPW5H0C`. Tier A. It contains all five works and is the best *witness*, because the editor collated against the Rome 1657 edition. The OCR has long s throughout ("Paradifo", "fua").
3. **Venice (Pezzana) 1776**: https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_CxAqi_JGjJoC (101k). Long-s OCR, poor.
4. Other early editions for collation: 1594 (`bub_gb_9h7Xm2EGXgwC`, the "seconda impressione"), 1684/1685 (`bub_gb_IcTWrbnMb2sC`, `bub_gb_mcbfSanudIoC`, `bub_gb_ICZT0VbUcFEC`), 1741 (`bub_gb_EIvxRVhgZdwC`), 1756 (`bub_gb_QJeUPljssCcC`).
5. A course handout from the Università Salesiana reproducing the Italian (https://collin.unisal.it/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/TB1420-U002-APP-Combattimento-Spirituale-Lorenzo-Scupoli.pdf). The host did not resolve when I tried, so its completeness, edition and tier are **unverified**.

English, best first:
1. **"A New Translation", Rivingtons (London/Oxford/Cambridge) 1875**, in the *Library of Spiritual Works for English Catholics*. **Transcribed on en.wikisource**: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_Spiritual_Combat, with subpages `/The_Spiritual_Combat` (66 chapters, 45.5k words), `/Supplement` (38 chapters, 11.0k) and `/Interior_Peace` (8.6k), about 65k in all. The Wikisource scan index is `Index:Spiritualcombat.djvu`. Tier A (1875, anonymous translator). The series was Anglo-Catholic, but a keyword check of the 1875 OCR shows the Catholic content kept: 38 mentions of the Virgin/Mary, confession and the saints present. Still, spot-check the sacramental and Marian chapters against the Italian. Scans: https://archive.org/details/TheSpiritualCombat (73k OCR) and the 1876 reprint `TheSpiritualCombat1876`.
2. ***The Spiritual Combat, together with the Treatise of Inward Peace*, "a new translation from the Italian", London: Burns & Oates, imprimatur Westminster 18 Oct 1905**: https://archive.org/details/spiritual-combat-catholic-edition (66k OCR words; the uploader marks it Public Domain Mark 1.0). Tier A (1905, anonymous). This is an explicitly Catholic translation: 66 chapters, the Supplement and *Of Inward Peace; or, the Path to Paradise*. The OCR is decent, with errors in the table of contents. **Use it for doctrinal fidelity and to cross-check the Rivingtons text.**
3. Haydock edition (Manchester: R. W. Dean for T. Haydock, early 19th century), adding *The Peace of the Soul*, *Reflections on Death* and *Penitence*: https://archive.org/details/the-spiritual-combat-haydock-edition (90k words, poor OCR on the title pages). Tier A. This is the older English Catholic version (the 1698/1742 "Castaniza" lineage), in archaic style.
4. 1817 printing of the same old Catholic version: https://archive.org/details/the_spiritual_combat_1817 (57k). Dornin edition (US, early 19th century): https://archive.org/details/the-spiritual-combat-dornin-edition (55k, OCR very poor). Both tier A.
5. **Not recommended**: 1846 (James Burns, "for the use of members of the English Church", `TheSpiritualCombat1846`) and 1882 (`SpiritualCombat`, which says outright that it is adapted "into harmony with the Book of Common Prayer and Anglican Divinity"). These are adapted Anglican texts and fall outside the original's faithful line. Tier A, but unfaithful.
6. Lester & Mohan, *The Spiritual Combat and a Treatise on Peace of Soul* (Newman 1945; TAN reprint): **unverified**, possibly B if the copyright was never renewed. I did not check. TAN sells it, so treat it as C. *Unseen Warfare* (the Nicodemus/Theophan Orthodox adaptation, Kadloubovsky & Palmer, Faber 1952) is C and a different work.

Structure: Combattimento chapters 1–66, Aggiunta (2 parts, 38 chapters in English), Sentiero/Pace interiore (chapters), then the Dolori mentali and the Modo di aiutare. No footnotes in the main editions.

## Dei dolori mentali di Cristo (doubtful) and Modo di aiutare gl'infermi a ben morire

- Italian: only in the 1770 Venice volume and similar collected editions (long-s OCR).
- English: no PD translation found in the Catholic or Rivingtons editions checked. A German version of the *Modo* is in the 1865 *Der geistliche Kampf … von der Weise, die Kranken zu …* (`bub_gb_ATo9AAAAcAAJ`).
- Sourcing these two means OCR, with no English to align against.

## Portuguese

Best first:
1. **D. Thomaz Bequeman (Theatine), *Combate espiritual, I. & II. parte*, Lisboa: Officina de Valentim da Costa Deslandes, 1707**. Catalogue record: https://ribac.cimac.pt/Record/bme_29338. Reprinted **Lisboa: Regia Officina Typographica, 1783** (https://ribac.cimac.pt/Record/bmr_36307). Tier A (18th century). This is the classic Portuguese version. **I found no digitised copy** (not on purl.pt/BND, archive.org or Google Books in my searches), so it is scans not located, a library copy only. If digitised, it would be a PD Portuguese witness in 18th-century spelling; it would need modernisation, not translation.
2. Modern Brazilian editions (e.g. Touche Livros, https://www.touchelivros.com.br/combate-espiritual/; others sold by Martins Fontes and similar). Translators not established. Tier C.
3. Nothing found for the Aggiunta, the Sentiero, the Dolori mentali or the Modo as separate Portuguese texts, though Bequeman's "II. parte" may contain the Aggiunta/Sentiero (unverified).

archive.org has several *Combate espiritual* items, but every one I checked is **Spanish** (1771 Madrid, 1850 Barcelona, 1865), not Portuguese.

## Recommendation

- Layout: `scupoli-opera-omnia/combattimento-spirituale/`, `…/aggiunta/`, `…/sentiero-del-paradiso/`, `…/dolori-mentali-di-cristo/` (flagged doubtful), `…/modo-di-aiutare-gl-infermi/`.
- **Import first: the Combattimento with the Aggiunta and the Sentiero in English**, from en.wikisource (Rivingtons 1875), cross-checked against the Catholic Burns & Oates 1905. The Wikisource text is clean and split by chapter.
- **The Italian original needs OCR work.** No clean text exists online. Re-OCR the 1842 Naples edition (modern type) for the Combattimento, and the 1770 Venice edition (Paris 1660 text) for the sequels, collated against each other. Until then the corpus would hold this work in English only, against the policy of an Italian canonical source, so put the Italian OCR on the import plan.
- Not cleanly sourceable: the Dolori mentali (doubtful anyway) and the Modo, both Italian scans only with no English.
- **Total**: about 80k Italian words (all five works, estimated from the 99k long-s OCR of the 1770 edition minus front matter) and about 65k English words (Rivingtons 1875).
