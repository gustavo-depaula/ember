# The ecumenical councils (21): sources

Scope: the 21 councils the Catholic Church counts as ecumenical, Nicaea I (325) to Vatican I (1869–70). Vatican II is out of scope (LEV, tier C). The corpus already holds the **first seven in English only** (`content/books/church-fathers/councils/`, Percival, NPNF2 vol. 14, 1900, via New Advent, ~153k words incl. the local synods), plus NPNF's local synods (Ancyra, Neocaesarea, Gangra, Antioch, Laodicea, Sardica, Carthage, Constantinople 382/394, Trullo). It has no Greek or Latin for any of them and nothing from Constantinople IV onward.

What a "council" is here: its **decrees** (creed/definition, canons, constitutions, bulls promulgated in session), as in the standard collections (Mansi, Hardouin, COD). Not the full acts (Mansi runs to thousands of columns per council); the acts are noted as an optional later layer.

Raw downloads used to measure: `research/sourcing/councils/`.

## Sources, by kind (ranked, with reasons)

### Latin, all councils

1. **Latin Wikisource** (la.wikisource.org), one page per council, almost all with `Fons = Documenta Catholica Omnia`. Clean HTML/wikitext, easy to fetch through the API (`action=raw`). The text is the LLT/COD Latin: medieval councils from COD, the first seven in the *ancient Latin versions* COD prints ("transl. latina"). Exceptions with a real PD edition named: **Trent** = *Sacrosanctum Concilium Tridentinum*, Padua: Manfrè 1738 (scan linked: archive.org `bub_gb_R9lFeEQ_eEgC`); **Vatican I** = *Acta et decreta sacrosancti oecumenici concilii Vaticani*, Freiburg: Herder 1892 (archive.org `actaetdecretasac00unse`). Quality: COD/LLT text with **punctuation largely stripped and lowercase `deus`/`dominus`** in the medieval councils (e.g. Lateran IV: "Firmiter credimus et simpliciter confitemur quod unus solus est verus deus aeternus et immensus omnipotens…"), and no canon numbers in some pages. Needs re-punctuating and numbering against a printed edition, which is a real but mechanical job. Best first source because it is clean and complete per COD.
   - Page titles (with typos as on the wiki): `Concilium Nicaenum I`, `Concilium Contantinopolitanum I`, `Concilium Ephesinum`, `Concilium Chalcedonense`, `Definitio fidei (Concilium Chalcedonense)`, `Concilium Contantinopolitanum II`, `Concilium Constantinopolitanum III`, `Concilium Nicaenum II`, `Concilium Constantinopolitanum IV`, `Concilium Lateranum I`–`IV`, `Concilium Lugdunense I`, `II`, `Concilium Viennense`, `Concilium Constantiense Documenta`, `Concilii Basiliensis Documenta` (source given as ldysinger.com), `Concilium Lateranense V Documenta`, `Concilium Tridentinum`, `Concilium Vaticanum I`, `Pastor Aeternus` (+ `/Praefatio`, `/Caput I`–`IV`).
2. **Documenta Catholica Omnia** (`https://www.documentacatholicaomnia.eu/01_10_Concilia_Oecumenica.html`, one page per council, e.g. `01_10_1215-1215-_Concilium_Lateranum_IIII.html`). Same LLT/COD Latin as Word `.doc` files (the `.doc.html` page links `../03d/…LT.doc`; I downloaded Lateran IV: 13,854 words, "Ex Libris … ex Mansi" claimed on the landing page, but the text and its header *Concilia oecumenica et generalia Ecclesiae catholicae* are the LLT/COD text). Also "Altera Lectio" Latin PDFs (scans/other editions) for most councils, Greek PDFs (Nicaea I canons GR and GR–FR, Constantinople I GR, Chalcedon *Documenta Omnia GR*, Nicaea II GR), and for Trent a Latin *Canones et Decreta* PDF plus a 1781 edition scan. The site states "© 2006 Cooperatorum Veritatis Societas quoad hanc editionem iura omnia asservantur" (a claim on its own compilation, not on the decrees). Use as the cross-check for Wikisource and for the Greek PDFs. The site's TLS certificate chain is broken (`curl -k` needed).
3. **Mansi**, *Sacrorum conciliorum nova et amplissima collectio* (Florence/Venice 1759–98; Paris reprint 1901–27), Latin and Greek, full acts. Tier A. Page scans only (archive.org, e.g. `sacrorumconcilio0003phil`, `bub_gb_GxXJEL5GvCgC`, `sacrorumconcilio0026joan`); OCR unusable for Greek, poor for two-column Latin. Best PD *authority* to check readings and canon numbering against; not an import source.
4. **Schroeder 1937, "Text of the canons" appendix** (Latin, Greek text from Lauchert 1896, Latin for Constantinople IV from Catalani, later councils from Catalani/Hefele-Leclercq/Friedberg, Basel and Lateran V from Hardouin). Disciplinary decrees only. archive.org `schroeder-disciplinary-councils` has full OCR (`…_djvu.txt`, 323k words for the whole book), but **its Greek was OCR'd as Latin and is lost**; Latin OCR is fair. A PD witness for the disciplinary Latin.
5. **Hardouin**, *Acta conciliorum* (1714–15) and **Collectio Regia** (Paris 1644): scans (e.g. Paderborn digital). Tier A, scans only.

**Tier of the LLT/COD Latin (sources 1–2): B, with reasoning.** The decrees themselves are medieval/ancient texts. COD is a 20th-century critical edition (Istituto per le scienze religiose, Bologna, 1962; 3rd ed. 1973), and Tanner's 1990 edition re-uses its text. A critical edition's *text* of a public-domain work carries at most a neighbouring right that has run out (Italy art. 85-quater L.633/1941: 20 years; Germany §70 UrhG: 25 years), and its punctuation-stripped form here contains none of COD's apparatus, introductions or translations. It is freely redistributed by DCO and Wikisource with no publisher claiming it. **Unverified**: whether the Bologna institute (now FSCIRE) or Brepols would object. If that is unacceptable, every council can be rebuilt from Mansi/Hardouin (tier A) at the cost of OCR work, and Trent and Vatican I already come from tier A editions.

### Greek (first seven councils, and Florence)

1. **Greek Wikisource**, *Κανόνες Α΄ Οικουμενικής Συνόδου* … *Ζ΄*, and *Κανόνες Πενθέκτης Οικουμενικής Συνόδου*, plus the local synods the corpus already has in English: *Κανόνες της εν Αγκύρα / Νεοκαισαρεία / Γάγγρα / Αντιοχεία / Λαοδικεία / Σαρδική / Καρθαγένη Συνόδου*, *Αποστολικοί Κανόνες*. The text is from the *Pedalion* (Nicodemus the Hagiorite and Agapius, 1800), tier A. Each page starts with Nicodemus's prolegomena in Katharevousa ("ΠΡΟΛΕΓΟΜΕΝΑ. Ἡ Ἁγία Οἰκουμενικὴ πρώτη Σύνοδος συνεκροτήθη…"), which has to be stripped to leave the canon texts. Wordcounts: Nicaea I 2,097; Constantinople I 1,310; Ephesus 1,794; Chalcedon 2,747; Trullo 11,032; Nicaea II 3,202; Ancyra 2,304; Neocaesarea 845; Gangra 1,464; Antioch 3,965; Laodicea 2,877; Sardica 3,038; Carthage 15,959 (Greek version of the African code). **This would complete the English-only council books already in the corpus, canons only.**
2. **Schaff, *Creeds of Christendom* II (1877)** on CCEL (`https://ccel.org/ccel/schaff/creeds2/`): clean HTML Greek and Latin of the Nicene and Niceno-Constantinopolitan creeds (`creeds2.iv.i.ii.*`), the Chalcedonian definition (`creeds2.iv.i.iii`), and the Constantinople III definition (`creeds2.iv.i.v`). Tier A. Best source for the doctrinal *horoi*, which the Pedalion lacks.
3. **DCO Greek PDFs** (listed above): fill the Chalcedon and Nicaea II gaps (definitions, letters). Whether they are scans or typeset text, and from which edition, is unverified.
4. **Lauchert, *Die Kanones der wichtigsten altkirchlichen Concilien*** (Freiburg 1896), archive.org `diekanonesderwi00churgoog`: critical Greek of the canons, tier A, Google scan (Greek OCR unusable). The edition Schroeder followed; the best check on the Pedalion readings.
5. **Rhalles–Potles, *Syntagma*** (Athens 1852–59), archive.org `SyntagmaTonTheionKaiHieronKanononTonTeHagionKaiPaneuphemonApostolon`: canons with Byzantine commentaries, tier A, scan.
6. **Schwartz, *Acta Conciliorum Oecumenicorum*** (1914–40) for Ephesus and Chalcedon (Cyril's letters, Leo's Tome, the full acts): critical, tier A (Schwartz d. 1940), scans only. Constantinople II (ACO IV, Straub 1971) and the Riedinger ACO² volumes for Constantinople III and Nicaea II are tier C.
- The Greek *decree* of Constantinople II survives only in fragments, so its Latin acts are the original for practical purposes. For Florence, the Greek text of *Laetentur caeli* is in Mansi XXXI (scan) and in Gill's critical edition (1953, C).

### English

1. **NPNF2 vol. 14, Percival 1900**, already in the corpus for councils 1–7. Nothing to add for those.
2. **H. J. Schroeder OP, *Disciplinary Decrees of the General Councils: Text, Translation and Commentary*** (St. Louis: B. Herder, 1937). Nicaea I through Lateran V, disciplinary decrees with a historical essay per council, notes, and the Latin/Greek texts in an appendix. Fordham's Internet Medieval Sourcebook, which hosts Lateran I, II and IV from it, states: *"B. Herder's list was bought by TAN books, of Rockford IL. TAN confirmed that US copyright was not renewed after the statuary 28 years and that the text is now in the public domain in the US."* Schroeder died in 1942, so it is also life+70 PD. **Tier A** (US non-renewal confirmed by the rights holder, and life+70).
   - archive.org `schroeder-disciplinary-councils` (full OCR, good English: spot-checked p. 133, clean) and `DisciplinaryCouncils` (a second scan). A 1972 reprint exists (`disciplinarydecr0000cath`, lending only).
   - Fordham: `https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/basis/lateran1.asp` (7.8k words incl. notes), `lateran2.asp` (8.3k), `lateran4.asp` (23k). Clean HTML, Schroeder's notes kept. Other council pages under `basis/` returned HTTP 500 when checked.
   - Limitation: **disciplinary only**. Schroeder includes Lateran IV c.1 (*Firmiter*), the Constance decrees *Haec sancta* and *Frequens*, and the Basel reform decrees, but not purely doctrinal texts such as the Florentine union decrees or the Constance condemnations of Wyclif and Hus.
3. **Trent: J. Waterworth, *The Canons and Decrees of the Sacred and Oecumenical Council of Trent*** (London: Dolman, 1848). Tier A.
   - Hanover Historical Texts Project, `https://history.hanover.edu/texts/trent/trentall.html`: one page, complete from the Bull of Indiction to the bull of confirmation (1564), Waterworth's page numbers in brackets, "Scanned by Hanover College students in 1995". **93,302 words counted.** Best: clean and complete, dogmatic *and* reform decrees.
   - DCO mirror: `04z/z_1545-1563__Concilium_Tridentinum__Canona_Decretaque_[Hanover_Historical_Texts]__EN.doc.html`.
   - archive.org `cu31924029369760` (1848, Cornell; 207k words OCR including Waterworth's long historical essays) and `thecanonsanddecr00unknuoft` (1888 reprint): to check Hanover's scanning errors against.
   - CCEL Schaff *Creeds* II, `creeds2.v.i.i.*`: the **dogmatic** decrees and canons only, Latin and Waterworth side by side, clean HTML. Best Latin+English pairing for the dogmatic sessions.
   - Alternatives: T. A. Buckley, *Canons and Decrees … Literally Translated* (London 1851), archive.org `canonsanddecree00buckgoog` (161k OCR), H. J. Schroeder OP, *Canons and Decrees of the Council of Trent* (Herder 1941, Latin and English; translator d. 1942 → life+70 A; US renewal unverified; TAN sells a reprint), archive.org `CanonsAndDecreesOfTheCounSchroederH.J.O.P.5861_201903`. Schroeder 1941 is the more modern English if Waterworth's archaisms bother; Waterworth is the brief's choice and is safest.
4. **Vatican I** (1870-era English). Tier A for all of these.
   - *Decrees and Canons of the Vatican Council* (1875 pamphlet), archive.org `a678655400slsnuoft`: both constitutions (*Dei Filius*, *Pastor aeternus*) in English, 7.5k words OCR, decent.
   - H. E. Manning, *The Vatican Council and its Definitions* (1871), archive.org `thevaticancounci00mannuoft` / `cu31924029359332`: the texts plus Manning's pastoral (72k total).
   - Schaff, *Creeds* II (1877), printed pp. 234–271: Latin and English of both constitutions. It is missing from CCEL's HTML edition, but present in the archive.org scans `creedschristendo02scha` (OCR 232k words; two-column interleaving garbles it).
   - *Dogmatic Canons and Decrees* (New York: Devin-Adair 1912), archive.org `dogmaticcanonsan00unknuoft` (46k): authorized translations of Trent's dogmatic decrees, *Ineffabilis Deus*, the Syllabus, and Vatican I.
   - `cihm_91774` (1875), *History of the Vatican Council … with the Latin and English text*.
   - For the minor session decrees (Sessions I–II, the profession of faith) the English is probably only in Manning or the Acta; unverified.
5. **Hefele, *A History of the Councils of the Church*** (Edinburgh: Clark, 1871–96, 5 vols, to 787 only), archive.org `historyofcouncil0Nhefeuoft`: narrative with canon translations, tier A. Fallback English for the first seven, and the historical introductions. Hefele–Leclercq, *Histoire des conciles* (French, 1907–52; Leclercq d. 1945 → life+70 A in the EU; US status of the later volumes unverified) goes through Trent, with French translations of every decree. A fallback for Constance–Lateran V, scans.
6. **Avoid (tier C): Tanner**, *Decrees of the Ecumenical Councils* (Sheed & Ward / Georgetown 1990). It is what papalencyclicals.net, EWTN and most of DCO's `Documenta_Omnia__EN.pdf` / `[Tanner_Translation]__EN.doc` files carry for Constantinople IV through Lateran V. Also avoid Gill's *Council of Florence* (1959) and Loomis's *Council of Constance* (1961).

## Notes per council

- **Councils 1–7.** English is done. Add `grc/` (canons, from Pedalion via el.ws, prolegomena stripped; creeds and horoi from Schaff/DCO) and optionally `la/` (the ancient Latin versions via COD). The existing chapter split is one `ch001.md` per council, so the Greek must follow the same single-chapter shape or the corpus books must be split by canon. The Ephesus and Chalcedon "decrees" in NPNF include Cyril's letters and Leo's Tome, whose Greek and Latin only Schwartz (ACO, scans) and Mansi give cleanly. Expect gaps there.
- **Constantinople IV (869–70).** Ecumenical in the Western reckoning only. The authoritative text is Anastasius Bibliothecarius's Latin (27 canons and the *horos*). The Greek is an abridgement (Mansi XVI).
- **Lateran I–IV, Lyons I–II.** The Latin is short and clean (see table). The English is Schroeder's, complete for the canons. Lateran IV is fully on Fordham, the best single item. Lyons I includes the bull deposing Frederick II, which Schroeder gives. Lyons II's constitution *Fideli ac devota* (the Procession) is doctrinal, and its presence in Schroeder needs checking.
- **Vienne.** 26.9k Latin words (it includes the *Vox in excelso* suppression of the Templars and the Clementine decrees). Schroeder translates only a selection, so English coverage is partial.
- **Constance.** la.ws *Documenta* is 14.1k words. Whether it holds all of COD's text (the condemned articles of Wyclif and Hus, sessions 8 and 15, are long) is unverified. English: Schroeder for the reform decrees. The doctrinal articles have no PD English beyond Deferrari's Denzinger (see `denzinger.md`; tier unverified).
- **Basel–Ferrara–Florence–Rome.** Two Latin blocks: the Basel sessions (DCO `…Documenta_Concilii_Basiliensis__LT.doc`) and Ferrara–Florence–Rome (la.ws, 19.7k, source ldysinger.com). Which sessions of Basel count is itself a question: COD prints sessions 1–25 with the Pope's approval only for parts. English is the biggest gap. Schroeder covers Basel's reforms. **No PD English of the bulls of union with the Greeks, Armenians (*Exultate Deo*), Copts (*Cantate Domino*), Syrians, Chaldeans and Maronites has been found.** Options: a tier-A French translation (Hefele–Leclercq vol. VII), or translate from the Latin with the pipeline.
- **Lateran V.** 24k Latin (la.ws). Schroeder covers the reform decrees. Doctrinal items (*Apostolici regiminis* on the soul) should be checked in Schroeder.
- **Trent.** Best covered: Latin 1738 (la.ws, 51k) plus Waterworth (Hanover, 93k). Structure: 25 sessions, each with decree(s), canons, and a reform decree, plus the bulls. Natural chapter split: one chapter per session (Session XXV split in two, plus the bulls). Note the English is longer than the Latin because the Hanover file includes the bull of indiction and confirmation. Check that la.ws also has them.
- **Vatican I.** Small: four sessions. Session III = *Dei Filius*, Session IV = *Pastor aeternus*, plus the opening decrees and the profession of faith of Session II. la.ws `Concilium Vaticanum I` (5.7k words, from the *Acta* 1892) runs from Session I and mentions *Dei Filius* in its text; *Pastor Aeternus* is also on separate subpages. Whether every session decree is complete there is unverified, so check it against the *Acta* (archive.org, A) and Schaff.

## Recommendation

**Layout:** extend the existing `church-fathers/councils/` for 1–7 (add `grc/`, optionally `la/`) and start a new collection `councils/` (or `church-fathers/councils/` if the collection should stay single) for 8–20. The ids would be `councils-constantinople-iv`, `councils-lateran-i` … `councils-vatican-i`, one book per council, `la/` canonical and `en-US/` beside it. Trent and Vatican I are big enough to split by session. The medieval councils fit one chapter per council or per constitution group.

**Import order:**
1. **Trent**: la.ws Latin (A) + Waterworth Hanover (A). Complete, clean, highest value. Cross-check the Latin against DCO's *Canones et Decreta* and Schaff (dogmatic sessions), and Waterworth against the 1848 Cornell OCR.
2. **Vatican I**: *Acta et decreta* 1892 Latin via la.ws (A) + the 1875 pamphlet or Schaff English (A). Tiny.
3. **Lateran IV, Lateran I–II**: la.ws/DCO Latin (B) + Schroeder via Fordham (A). Then Lateran III, Lyons I–II, Constantinople IV from the Schroeder archive.org OCR.
4. **Greek canons for the first seven** (and the local synods) from el.wikisource Pedalion. They complete existing books.
5. **Vienne, Constance, Lateran V, Basel–Florence**: the Latin is ready (B). The English is partial (Schroeder), and the doctrinal parts would need pt-BR/en from the Latin or wait for a PD translation.

**Cannot be sourced cleanly:**
- PD English of the Florentine union decrees and the Constance articles. Tanner is C; Deferrari is unverified.
- Critical Greek of Ephesus and Chalcedon beyond canons and horoi: ACO scans only.
- Any council's full acts: Mansi scans only.
- The B-tier status of the COD-derived Latin rests on neighbouring-right expiry and no one claiming it. If the user wants strictly A, rebuild the medieval councils from Mansi/Hardouin scans.

**Total size (decrees only):** Latin ≈ 250k words (all 20; Trent 51k, Vienne 27k, Lateran V 24k, Basel–Florence ~20k + Basel sessions est. 25k, Constance 14k, Lateran IV 13.5k, others 1–8k each). Greek canons ≈ 23k (first seven + Trullo) and ≈ 34k more for the local synods. New English ≈ 93k (Trent) + 7.5k (Vatican I) + ~60–80k Schroeder translations for 8–18 (est.), so ≈ 170k. The first-seven English (~153k) is already in.

Notes:
- António Pereira de Figueiredo, *Portuguezes nos concilios geraes* (Lisboa 1787, archive.org `portuguezesnosco00figu`) is a catalogue of Portuguese attendees, not a translation. Joaquim José Pacheco e Sousa, *Concilio Tridentino vindicado* (1808, `conciliotridenti00pach`) is apologetics. Neither is a source.
- The Reycend volumes are the only tier-A Portuguese of any council found. Their text is 18th-century European Portuguese. That suits a reference for terminology (*seja excommungado*, *Sacrosanto*) but not a drop-in pt-BR text.

**Recommendation:** for Trent, use Reycend 1781 (A, complete) as the Portuguese reference, and re-OCR the Portuguese pages from the BNP PDF (purl.pt/360) if it is to be imported, with modernised spelling. Montfort's partial modern text is a C-tier wording check only. Vatican I and the other councils have no tier-A/B Portuguese, so translate them from the Latin.
