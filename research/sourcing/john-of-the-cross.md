# St. John of the Cross (1542–1591): sourcing report

Not in the corpus. `_corpus-books.txt` and `content/books/` have no John of the Cross, Carmelite or *Juan de la Cruz* entry (the only "cross" hit is `montfort-friends-cross`).

**Canon used.** The table of contents of the two critical editions that are free to use: Gerardo de San Juan de la Cruz, *Obras del místico doctor* (Toledo, 1912–14, 3 vols), and Silverio de Santa Teresa, *Obras de San Juan de la Cruz* (Biblioteca Mística Carmelitana 10–14, Burgos, 1929–31, 5 vols). These were cross-checked against the modern canon in Eulogio Pacho's edition (Editorial Monte Carmelo; its index is on documentacatholicaomnia.eu). The modern canon is:

- the four great treatises: *Subida*, *Noche*, *Cántico* in two redactions (CA and CB), *Llama* in two redactions (A and B);
- the poems;
- the *Avisos espirituales*: the *Dichos de luz y amor* (the Andújar autograph), *Puntos de amor* (Beas), and the smaller sets copied by Magdalena del Espíritu Santo, by María de Jesús, from Antequera and in the Gerona edition;
- the *Cautelas*, the *Cuatro avisos a un religioso* and the *Monte de perfección* (the drawing with its sayings);
- the *Epistolario* (33 letters in Pacho);
- a *Censura y parecer* on a nun's spirit, plus some administrative documents (licences, election acts).

**Doubtful.** The *Grados de perfección*, and the *Suma de la perfección*/*Olvido de lo criado* quatrain. Pacho keeps them; Gerardo prints the *Grados*.

**Spurious.** Gerardo vol. III prints the following, and Silverio and every modern edition reject them:

- the *Coloquios entre Cristo y su esposa* / *Espinas de espíritu*;
- the *Tratado breve del conocimiento oscuro de Dios*;
- the treatises of Cecilia del Nacimiento;
- the *Apuntamientos* of Diego de Jesús;
- José de Jesús María's work.

Many of the poems Gerardo lists as "atribuidas" are rejected too. None of these should be imported as John's.

## Sources, ranked

### Spanish witnesses (all works)

1. **Silverio de Santa Teresa, BMC 10–14, Burgos 1929–31**, archive.org (Princeton Theological Seminary copies, good OCR, djvu.txt present):
   - t. I: Preliminares (1929). [obrasdesanjuande01john](https://archive.org/details/obrasdesanjuande01john)
   - t. II: Subida y Noche (1929). [obrasdesanjuande02john](https://archive.org/details/obrasdesanjuande02john)
   - t. III: Cántico, CA and CB (1930). [obrasdesanjuande03john](https://archive.org/details/obrasdesanjuande03john)
   - t. IV: Llama A and B, Cautelas, Avisos, Cartas, Censura, Poesías, Dictámenes appendix (1931). [obrasdesanjuande04john](https://archive.org/details/obrasdesanjuande04john)
   - t. V: Procesos (1931). Not his works. [obrasdesanjuande05john](https://archive.org/details/obrasdesanjuande05john)
   - Other uploads of the same scans: `BMC10…`–`BMC14…` (e.g. `BMC11ObrasDeSanJuanDeLaCruzTomoIISubidaYNocheOscura`), and an `obrasdesanjuande0000psil` 1943 copy.

   **Why first:** it is the critical text every 20th-century translation (Peers included) follows, and it prints both redactions of the Cántico and the Llama. The OCR is the cleanest of the old editions. It uses modern accentuation, and errors are scattered (`ios`→`los`, the odd `I` for `!`). Footnotes carry the manuscript variants ("E. p.: …", "A y B no traen…") and must be stripped. Each page has a running head (`LIBRO PRIMERO. — CAP. XV`) and a page number to remove.

   **Tier A, with evidence:**
   - t. II–III are published ≤1930.
   - t. IV–V are 1931, and Silverio died in 1954 (72 years ago), so A by the brief's life+70 rule.
   - The text itself is the saint's; Spain's art. 129 protection of an edition of a public-domain text lasts 25 years and has long lapsed.
   - Caveat: Spain applies life+80 to authors who died before 1987, so Silverio's own *notes and introductions* stay protected in Spain until 2035. Import the saint's text and strip the apparatus.
   - The volumes carry "ES PROPIEDAD".
2. **Gerardo de San Juan de la Cruz, *Obras del místico doctor*, Toledo (Peláez), 1912–14**, two independent scans:
   - University of Toronto: [obrasedicincr01john](https://archive.org/details/obrasedicincr01john) (Vida + Subida), [obrasedicincr02john](https://archive.org/details/obrasedicincr02john) (Noche; Cántico B then A; Llama B then A), [obrasedicincr03john](https://archive.org/details/obrasedicincr03john) (Cautelas, Cuatro avisos, Grados, Avisos de Andújar and other sets, Dictámenes, 25 letters + fragments, Censura, documents, Poesías auténticas + atribuidas, then the spurious works listed above).
   - Princeton: [obrasdelmisticod01john](https://archive.org/details/obrasdelmisticod01john), [02](https://archive.org/details/obrasdelmisticod02john), [03](https://archive.org/details/obrasdelmisticod03john).
   - A third upload: `SANJUANDELACRUZObrasCompletasTomoI1912`.

It is the best **second witness**: an independent pre-Silverio critical text with both redactions, and two scans of it can be compared. Its errata lists are at the end of each volume.

## Notes per work

- **Subida.** Prologue; Argument (stanzas); Book I (15 ch.), II (32 ch.), III (45 ch.), unfinished: it breaks off in III.45 in all witnesses. Split one file per chapter, with the Prologue and Argument (the *Noche* poem) as their own files. Silverio numbers the chapters as the manuscripts do. Lewis/Zimmerman 1922 follows the old numbering, which differs in Book II (the vulgate splits and merges some chapters), so align chapter by chapter before pairing en-US with es. Footnotes: Silverio has dense variant apparatus (strip it); Lewis has scripture references.
- **Noche.** Prologue; poem; Book I (14 ch.), Book II (25 ch., breaks off at stanza 3). The 1916 Lewis/Zimmerman edition inserts the Gerardo variants into the English, which makes it the best match for a critical Spanish base. Its 61k includes Spanish-title matter and an introduction.
- **Cántico A and B.** Two works, two corpus books, or one book with `a/` and `b/` sub-works.
  - CA (Sanlúcar ms.): 39 stanzas, prologue, declarations in the order the poem first had.
  - CB (Jaén ms.): 40 stanzas reordered, an "Argumento" and added annotations before stanzas.
  - Lewis translates the old printed CB (Madrid 1630 tradition). Zimmerman notes the personal allusions to Ann of Jesus "disappeared" from the printed texts except Brussels 1627, so Lewis's CB is not line-for-line Silverio's CB.
  - Pairing: CB en-US is approximate, and CA has **no free English**.
- **Llama A and B.** Prologue + 4 stanzas, each declared verse by verse; B is a careful rewrite of A. Silverio t. IV prints both ("(PRIMERA REDACCIÓN)", "(segunda redacción)"), and Gerardo t. II prints B ("de la segunda escritura", p. 373) and then A (p. 618). Zimmerman 1912 says the work survives in two versions and that he restored "a long and important passage … omitted in all former editions", taken from Gerardo (1910). I could not settle which redaction Lewis's base text is (unverified). Check it against Silverio's A and B before pairing; whichever it matches gets the en-US, and the other has no free English.
- **Poems.** About 20 pieces. Silverio t. IV "Poesías" separates authentic from attributed poems, and Gerardo t. III does the same ("Poesías auténticas" p. 157, "atribuidas" p. 197). Make one file per poem; the nine *Romances* on *In principio erat Verbum* form one poem in 9 parts. The poem texts are also embedded at the head of the Subida/Noche (Noche poem), Cántico and Llama, so don't duplicate them: link or repeat deliberately. English: Lewis 1912/1919 "Poems" (≈7k words without the index); O'Connor for the Cántico.
- **Avisos / Dichos de luz y amor.**
  - Structure: the autograph of Andújar (prologue + numbered sayings), then the *Puntos de amor* (Beas) and the copied sets.
  - Silverio and Pacho number them differently, and Lewis's "Spiritual Maxims" (≈15k) is a different, longer compilation: the 1703 hundred maxims, partly culled from his treatises, plus later additions per Zimmerman's intro. So it doesn't pair one-to-one with the Spanish.
  - Options: import the Spanish by Silverio's sets with no en-US pairing, or keep Lewis's Maxims as a separate English-only item. Our pipeline can produce the pt-BR from the Spanish either way.
- **Cautelas / Cuatro avisos / Grados.** These are short. Lewis's "Instructions and Precautions" contains both of the first two. The *Grados de perfección* is doubtful; only Gerardo prints it among the authentic works, and it has no English. A single "opuscula" book would suit them.
- **Epistolario.** 33 letters + fragments in Pacho's numbering, 25 + fragments in Gerardo's (t. III), and Silverio t. IV in between (count not verified). Lewis has 17 letters in 1864 and 18 in 1912/1919 (Zimmerman: "The older editions contained but ten letters, the first by Mr. Lewis seventeen, the second … eighteen"). One file per letter, numbered by Silverio, with Pacho's number noted. About 15 letters will be Spanish-only.
- **Censura y parecer, documents.** Tiny; append to the opuscula book. The documents (licences, acts) are administrative; skip them or put them in an appendix.

## Recommendation

**Layout:** `content/books/john-of-the-cross-opera-omnia/` with sub-works:

- `subida/`
- `noche/`
- `cantico-a/`
- `cantico-b/`
- `llama-a/`
- `llama-b/`
- `poesias/`
- `avisos/` (Dichos de luz y amor + the other sets)
- `opuscula/` (Cautelas, Cuatro avisos, Grados [doubtful], Monte de perfección, Censura)
- `epistolario/`

Each has `es/` (canonical) and `en-US/` where a free translation exists.

**Base texts:** the Spanish is Silverio (archive.org djvu.txt), corrected against Gerardo (two scans) and the clean Pacho/DCO text, which you can consult for corrections but whose text you can't copy in. The English is Lewis/Zimmerman (1922 Ascent, 1916 Dark Night, 1919 Canticle, 1919 Living Flame volume), with Lewis 1891 and 1864 as fallbacks.

**Import order:**
1. *Subida* + *Noche*: Silverio t. II has both, and both pair with Lewis/Zimmerman.
2. *Cántico B* + *Llama* (once the Lewis redaction is identified).
3. *Poesías*, *Cautelas*/*Avisos a un religioso*, *Epistolario* (from t. IV + the Lewis 1919 Flame volume, which holds all their English).
4. *Cántico A*, the other *Llama* redaction, and the *Dichos* in the Spanish order, Spanish only.

**Cannot be sourced cleanly:**
- **English of Cántico A and of one Llama redaction.** Only Peers (vols. II–III, lending scans; tier disputed, see above) or Kavanaugh (C) exist. Recommend Spanish-only for these.
- **An English *Dichos de luz y amor* in the autograph order.** Lewis's Maxims is a different compilation.
- **BVMC's HTML transcriptions** (BAE 1862, Montserrat ms.) could not be opened (403), so they are untested.
- **Clean Spanish HTML/plain text** of the prose under tier A/B: none found online. The only clean digital prose is Pacho's (C). The free base is OCR (Silverio: good), so expect a proofreading pass.
