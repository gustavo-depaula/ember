# G. K. Chesterton (1874–1936): sourcing report

Chesterton wrote in English, so the **English text is the canonical source** (`en-US/`); there is no separate translation column. The "English source" column below gives the **second and third witnesses** (other transcriptions or editions) for cross-checking.

**Not in the corpus.** `_corpus-books.txt` and `content/books/` hold no Chesterton. Only the `catholic-encyclopedia` mentions him.

## Copyright: how the tiers work for Chesterton

- **Life + 70.** Chesterton died 14 June 1936. Every work published in his lifetime has been public domain since 1 Jan 2007 in Brazil (Lei 9.610 art. 41), the UK and the EU, and earlier in Australia and Canada. Under the brief's definition ("author dead 70+ years") all of these are **tier A**.
- **US, works published 1930 or earlier:** public domain. In this report **A** means public domain everywhere.
- **US, works published 1931–1936:** protected for 95 years, until 1 Jan of the publication year + 96. I mark these **A†**: A by the brief, but not US-PD yet.
  - **Renewals checked** in the NYPL transcription of the Copyright Office renewal records (`github.com/NYPL/cce-renewals`, renewal years 1956–1992). Extract: `chesterton/raw/renewals_chesterton.txt`. Renewals were found for:
    - *Autobiography* (R336640, 2 Apr 1964, Dorothy Edith Collins)
    - *The Scandal of Father Brown* (R320553, 16 Aug 1963, Oliver Chesterton)
    - the 1932 US *Collected Poems* (R248444, 16 Dec 1959)
    - several magazine stories
    - *The Thing* (R198755) and *Generally Speaking*, both now moot because they were published in 1929.
  - **No renewal found** for *St Thomas Aquinas*, *Chaucer*, *Christendom in Dublin*, *Sidelights*, *All I Survey*, *All is Grist*, *Avowals and Denials*, *The Well and the Shallows*, *The Way of the Cross* or *As I Was Saying*.
  - **But non-renewal probably does not free them.** These books were first published in London. The UK term was revived to life+70 on 1 Jan 1996, so on the URAA restoration date they were still protected in their source country. That likely restored their US copyright regardless of renewal. Only a work first published in the US (or there within 30 days of the UK edition) would be **B** (unrenewed US work), and I could not verify first-publication dates. Treat all 1931–36 works as US-protected until their dates.
  - **The hosts agree.** Standard Ebooks on *Scandal*: "This book was published in 1935, and will therefore enter the U.S. public domain in 5 years on January 1, 2031." Wikisource lists *St. Thomas Aquinas* as `copyright-until 2029`. Gutenberg.org (US) carries none of the 1931–36 books. Project Gutenberg Australia and Roy Glashan's Library carry them under local law: "Project Gutenberg of Australia eBooks are created from printed editions which are in the public domain in Australia, unless a copyright notice is included … check the copyright laws for your country before downloading."
- **Posthumous works (1937 and later)** — *The Paradoxes of Mr Pond* (1937), *The Coloured Lands* (1938), *The Common Man* (1950), *The Surprise* (1952), *A Handful of Authors* (1953), *The Glass Walking-Stick* (1955), *Lunacy and Letters* (1958), *Where All Roads Lead* (1961 as a book), *The Spice of Life* (1965), and the later Collins/Smith collections:
  - **Brazil:** public domain (posthumous works follow the same 70-year term).
  - **UK:** term for posthumous works is unverified (old 50-years-from-publication rules apply).
  - **US:** protected 95 years from publication.
  - Overall: **unverified / effectively C for a US-hosted corpus**. Except *Pond*, none has a clean online text.
- **Modern editions are tier C:** Ignatius Press *Collected Works* (37 vols, 1986–), Penguin, Dover and others. Use the first-edition texts instead.

## Notes per work (priority set)

- **Heretics / Orthodoxy.** 20 and 9 chapters, each with a preface. No notes. Gutenberg #130 was proofread by Martin Ward against a second scan, which makes it the best base. The Wikisource transcriptions are scan-backed, so a disputed reading can be checked against the page image.
- **The Everlasting Man.** Introduction plus Part I "On the Creature Called Man" (8 chapters) and Part II "On the Man Called Christ" (6 chapters), a conclusion, and two appendices. PG #65688 is the H&S first edition. My 107.9k count includes front and back matter; the text itself is about 95–100k.
- **St. Francis of Assisi.** 10 chapters. The PG transcriber regularised hyphenation and moved the publisher's list to the end.
- **The Catholic Church and Conversion.** 7 chapters plus Belloc's series preface (Belloc died 1953, so the preface is PD in Brazil; in the US it was published 1926, so also PD). PG #76305 is the only clean text.
- **The Thing.** 36 short essays (in the US edition "Why I Am a Catholic" and "Is Humanism a Religion?" are among them). The best open OCR is the Princeton copy of Dodd Mead 1930. It needs a cleanup pass: running heads, page numbers and hyphenation. The DLI 1946 copy serves as an independent second OCR for diffing. Dodd Mead 1930 versus Sheed & Ward 1929: confirm that the essay lists match before using the US text as canonical.
- **The Resurrection of Rome.** Two independent DLI scans exist, so two OCRs can be diffed, which is ideal. Running heads still need removing.
- **The Superstitions of the Sceptic.** An I.D.C. Club booklet of a lecture followed by the Coulton–Chesterton correspondence. Coulton died 1947, so his letters are PD in Brazil and, published 1925, PD in the US. The OCR of the opening page is garbled; the body is good.
- **Father Brown.** 5 collections of 12, 12, 8, 10 and 8–10 stories (later editions of *Scandal* add "The Vampire of the Village"), plus 2 uncollected stories. Each story is a natural chapter. Standard Ebooks texts are the cleanest (CC0, modernised typography). Use PG or PGA where the original typography matters.
- **St. Thomas Aquinas.** An introductory note plus 8 chapters (PGA ToC: "On Two Friars", "The Runaway Abbot", "The Aristotelian Revolution", "A Meditation on the Manichees", "The Real Life of St. Thomas", "The Approach to Thomism", "The Permanent Philosophy", "The Sequel to St. Thomas"). The PGA/RGL text is clean HTML. It is the obvious companion to `aquinas-opera-omnia`.
- **The Well and the Shallows.** About 30 essays plus the opening "My Six Conversions" series.
- **Autobiography.** 16 chapters. Clean RGL HTML.

## Portuguese

**Summary.** Chesterton reached Portuguese late, and almost every translation on the market is a modern commercial edition (tier C). The only Portuguese Chesterton text I found with an explicit free licence is a novel, not one of the Catholic works. For the priority set (Orthodoxy, Heretics, Everlasting Man, St Francis, St Thomas, The Thing, Father Brown) the corpus pt-BR has to come from our own pipeline. The commercial editions below are useful only as a *reference* for terminology and title conventions.

**Where I looked:**
- **pt.wikisource:** nothing. There is no `Autor:` page and a search for "Chesterton" returns 0 hits.
- **dominiopublico.gov.br:** the search sits behind a Cloudflare JS challenge, so I could not query it, and web searches surfaced no Chesterton item there. Status unverified.
- **archive.org** (language:por): only pirated scans of modern editions, plus PG #46860.
- **BN Portugal** (id.bnportugal.gov.pt): modern editions only.
- **Permanência, Obras Católicas, Montfort:** nothing in searches. They publish Chesterton quotations and articles about him, not whole translated books.

**Older translations are unverified.** Older Portuguese and Brazilian translations (1930s–1960s, e.g. Porto houses such as Tavares Martins, or Rio publishers) may exist in BN Brasil and PORBASE. Most would still be in copyright, because their translators died after 1955. Brazilian copyright runs for life + 70 for the translator as well.

**Bottom line for pt-BR:** translate from the English canonical text with our pipeline. The only import candidate is Fuzitaki's CC0 *O Napoleão de Notting Hill*. Check the licence of Abreu's *O Homem Eterno* (baixelivros) by hand as a possible B-tier second witness.
