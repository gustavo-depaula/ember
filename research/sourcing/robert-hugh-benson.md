# Mgr. Robert Hugh Benson (1871–1914): sourcing report

Benson wrote in English, so the original is the English text. He died on 19 October 1914, which puts him more than 70 years dead. Everything he published is pre-1930. **Every work is tier A worldwide**, posthumous volumes included (*Loneliness?* 1915, *Spiritual Letters* 1915, *Poems* 1915, *Sermon Notes* 1917, *A Book of Essays* 1916, *The Upper Room* 1914). He was received into the Church in September 1903. Only *The Light Invisible* (1903) and a few early pieces belong to his Anglican years.

**Bibliography used:**
- the DNB 1927 supplement (Wikisource)
- C. C. Martindale, *The Life of Mgr. R. H. Benson* (1916), list of works
- the "By the same author" lists printed in his books (e.g. the front of `vexillaregisaboo00unknuoft`)

**Where the texts are:**
- **Project Gutenberg** has 14 Benson texts, proofread. These are the best witnesses, counted from PG plain text and saved in `robert-hugh-benson/pg/`.
- **archive.org** has scans of everything else, with very clean OCR: dictionary score 0.97–0.99, because these are early-1900s books. Saved in `robert-hugh-benson/ia/`.
- **Wikisource** has *Lord of the World*.
- **Gutenberg mirrors on IA** (`…gut` ids) duplicate the PG texts.

## Notes

- **Structure.** The novels split by chapter, and the sermon books by sermon. *A Book of the Love of Jesus* collects short medieval devotions: a natural source of short prayers or practices in Middle English, modernised by Benson. *Vexilla Regis* is a war-time prayer book of intercessions, made of short prayers. *Spiritual Letters* splits one letter per chapter.
- **Gutenberg first.** For the 14 PG titles, use PG and check it against an IA scan. For the rest, the IA OCR is very good (0.97–0.99), so a light clean-up pass removing running heads is enough.
- **Fit for the app.** The core is the Catholic apologetic and spiritual works (*Friendship of Christ*, *Christ in the Church*, *Paradoxes*, *Confessions of a Convert*, *Religion of the Plain Man*) plus *A Book of the Love of Jesus*. The novels (*Lord of the World*, *Come Rack! Come Rope!*, *By What Authority?*) are the most read Catholic fiction of the period.

**Notes.**
- Benson in Portuguese is **entirely recent and tier C**. Brazil has *Lord of the World* from Ecclesiae (2013), Sétimo Selo (2021) and Avis Rara/Faro (2022), Quadrante's two devotional titles (1996/2002), Ecclesiae's *Paradoxos* (2022) and Sétimo Selo's *Mirror of Shalott* (2025). Portugal has the 2025 Paulus *Senhor do mundo*. BNP's older Benson holdings are French only: *Le maître de la terre* and *La lumière invisible* (1931).
- No digitised Portuguese Benson text was found anywhere.

**Recommendation:** translate from the English (Gutenberg / archive.org texts above). For *O Senhor do Mundo*, the Ecclesiae 2013 edition is the most-read Brazilian text and the obvious reference for names and coined terms (Felsenburgh, the *volors*, the vocabulary of the Humanity religion). For *Confissões de um converso* and *A amizade com Cristo*, use Quadrante's. Nothing is importable as tier A or B.
