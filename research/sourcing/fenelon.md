# François de Salignac de La Mothe-Fénelon (1651–1715): sourcing report

Bibliography followed: the *Table des écrits de Fénelon* that closes the *Œuvres complètes* (Paris, Leroux & Jouby / Gaume, 1848–52, 10 vols, the Saint-Sulpice edition of J.-E.-A. Gosselin), transcribed on Wikisource (`https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Œuvres_complètes_de_Fénelon_(1848-1851)`, saved as `research/sourcing/fenelon/oc1848_table.txt`). It sorts the works into six *classes*, listed below with the volume and page they occupy in that edition. That edition reprints the Versailles edition (Lebel, 1820–30: 22 vols of works + 11 vols of *Correspondance*, 1827–29), the first complete one. The modern critical editions are Jacques Le Brun's Pléiade (1983–97, 2 vols) and the *Correspondance* ed. Jean Orcibal et al. (Droz, 18 vols, 1972–2007), both **tier C**. Nothing by Fénelon is in the corpus.

**The condemned book.** The *Explication des maximes des saints sur la vie intérieure* (Paris, Aubouin, 1697) was **condemned by Innocent XII** (brief *Cum alias*, 12 March 1699; 23 propositions censured), and Fénelon submitted publicly (mandement of 9 April 1699, printed in the OC, T. II p. 405ff). The 1848–52 table lists all the defences of the book but **not the book itself**. It does not appear in any OCR volume I could read (vols I, III–X), so the Saint-Sulpice editors apparently left it out (unconfirmed, because vol. II's text was unavailable on archive.org). If imported, it must be labelled as condemned, with the brief of condemnation and Fénelon's act of submission beside it.

Word counts: totals per volume are **measured** on the archive.org OCR of the 1851 OC (`research/sourcing/fenelon/oc1851_NN.txt`). Per-work figures are **estimates** from the page spans in the table × about 820 words/page (vol. VI: 615k words / ~750 pp.), unless marked "measured".

## Sources per work, ranked

**French, best first**

Some works are **proofread** there (*Réflexions saintes*, *Testament*), and most pages are raw OCR. Best for the works that have been proofread, and the only source of **tome 2**.
2. **OC 1851 on archive.org** (Toronto scans `oeuvrescomplte01…10fnuoft`, `oeuvrescomplte07fn`, `oeuvrescompltesd09fnel`; a duplicate series `oeuvresdefenelon01…10fn`). ABBYY OCR, two columns: **fair to good**, about 0.6M words per volume. Vol. II returned "Item not available" for both copies, so use #1 for it. ## Notes per work

- The *Œuvres spirituelles* are posthumous editorial gatherings (1718, 1738), not a work Fénelon composed as such. The 1848 edition breaks them into *Manuel de piété* and *Instructions et avis*. Keep the OC grouping.
- *Télémaque* has 18 or 24 *livres* depending on the edition (the 1717 authentic edition has 24, Didot 18). Follow the edition chosen as base.
- Correspondence is enormous and includes letters **to** Fénelon (Beauvilliers, Chevreuse, Bossuet, Mme Guyon…), so the numbers are inflated by about 30% of non-Fénelon text. The *Lettres spirituelles* (OC vol VIII p. 439ff, about 200k words) are what matters for a prayer app.
- Doubtful or second-hand attributions: the *Entretiens de Fénelon et de M. de Ramsai* and the *Essai philosophique sur le gouvernement civil* ("selon les principes de feu M. de Fénelon") were written up by Ramsay, so mark them "after Fénelon". The *Abrégé des vies des anciens philosophes* (1726, posthumous) has had its authorship questioned (unverified), so flag it.

## Recommendation

- Layout `fenelon-opera-omnia/<class>/<work>`: `spiritualite/` (manuel-de-piete, reflexions-saintes, instructions-et-avis, lettres-spirituelles, sermons-et-entretiens, education-des-filles), `theologie/` (existence-de-dieu, ministere-des-pasteurs, lettres-metaphysique, malebranche…), `quietisme/` (maximes-des-saints [flagged condemned], defenses…), `jansenisme/`, `mandements/`, `litterature/` (telemaque, dialogues-des-morts, fables, eloquence, odyssee, vies-des-philosophes), `politique/`, `correspondance/`.
- **Import first**: *Lettres spirituelles* (FR OC VIII; EN Lear ×2), *Réflexions saintes* (FR Wikisource proofread; EN *Pious Reflections*), *Instructions et avis* / *Manuel de piété* (EN Metcalf), *Éducation des filles*, *Existence de Dieu*. Then *Télémaque* and *Dialogues des morts* (clean Wikisource FR + many PD EN). The *Maximes* should go in only as a flagged historical document with the condemnation.
- **Lowest priority / heavy**: the Quietist and Jansenist controversies (about 1.85M words, no English), political mémoires, the non-spiritual correspondence.
- **Cannot be sourced cleanly**: tome II of the 1851 edition on archive.org (use the Wikisource djvu); English for the controversies and most letters; any modern critical text (Orcibal, Pléiade: tier C).

**Total size**: OC 1848–52 OCR measures about **6.2 M words** (vols I, III–X measured at 5.63M; vol II estimated at 0.6M), of which about 10% is editorial (Gosselin's *Histoire littéraire*, notes, tables) and about 0.7M is letters written to Fénelon. Fénelon's own text is therefore about **4.9 M words**, plus the *Maximes* (37k). Usable PD English is about **0.75 M words**.

## Portuguese

Fénelon reached Portuguese almost only through *Télémaque*. The standard study, Marie Eulalie Monteiro Pereira, *Les réécritures portugaises des Aventures de Télémaque de Fénelon* (PhD thesis, Coimbra 2024, `https://estudogeral.uc.pt/handle/10316/115661`; text saved as `research/sourcing/fenelon/pt/pereira2024_thesis.txt`), finds only **four original translations, all from 1765–88**. Every later edition, including the Paris and Lisbon reprints of the 19th c., the Portuguese edition of 1923 and its 2011 reissue, is a revised reprint of those four. Before *Télémaque*, the only Portuguese Fénelon was the *Dialogos sobre a eloquencia* (1761). I found **no Portuguese translation of any spiritual, theological or controversial work** (Lettres spirituelles, Manuel de piété, Réflexions saintes, Existence de Dieu, Maximes, Éducation des filles…), in old or modern catalogues.

Where I searched: archive.org (advancedsearch, by creator, title and `language:por`); Europeana API (BNP, BSB/MDZ, Gallica, Coimbra and the rest, filtered on `proxy_dc_language:por`); Biblioteca Nacional Digital (Portugal, `bndigital.bnportugal.gov.pt`); Alma Mater (Universidade de Coimbra); Brasiliana Guita e José Mindlin (USP); pt.wikisource full-text search; Open Library (`language=por`). **Not reachable**: dominiopublico.gov.br, BN Digital Brasil and HathiTrust (all Cloudflare 403), Gallica direct (403; its records were read through Europeana), Google Books (API quota exhausted). The session's web-search budget also ran out, so Catholic sites (Permanência, Obras Católicas, Montfort) and the publishers' catalogues (Vozes, Paulus, Ecclesiae, Cultor de Livros, Cristo Rei) were **not checked**. Treat "none found" for those as unverified.

**pt-BR reference**: for *Télémaque*, use the **Paris 1837 Sousa–Nascimento–Fonseca text** (Gallica `bpt6k315042b`/`bpt6k315041z`). It is complete, PD, OCR'd and the most polished of the old versions. The **1765 MDZ scan** serves as a second witness. All of these are 18th-c. Lusitanian prose in pre-reform spelling, so they guide vocabulary and proper names but cannot be imported as pt-BR. **For everything that matters to Ember (letters, Manuel de piété, Réflexions saintes, Instructions et avis), there is no Portuguese reference at all**, and the pt-BR must come from the pipeline, translated from the French OC.
