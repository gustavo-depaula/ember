# Cardinal Henry Edward Manning (1808–1892): sourcing report

Manning wrote in English, so the original is the English text. He died in 1892, so every lifetime and pre-1930 edition is **tier A**. He was received into the Church on **6 April 1851**. The brief asks for his Catholic works, which are listed in full below. His Anglican works are summarised in a short table at the end.

**Bibliography used:**
- the DNB and *Catholic Encyclopedia* (1913) articles (both on Wikisource)
- the Burns & Oates "Cardinal Manning's Works" lists printed in his later volumes
- the archive.org author index (`manning/ia-search.txt`, 355 items)

**Where the texts are:**
- **Wikisource**, transcribed: *The Eternal Priesthood*, *Petri Privilegium*, *The True Story of the Vatican Council*, *Christ and Antichrist*, *Rome and the Revolution*.
- **CCEL** (`ccel.org/ccel/manning_henry`): *The Grounds of Faith* (Catholic) and the four volumes of Anglican *Sermons*.
- **archive.org**, for everything else: mostly University of Toronto scans with clean OCR (dictionary score 0.95–0.98).

How I measured: `_scripts/ia_count.py` counted the full text, including front matter and index. `_scripts/ws_check.py` summed each Wikisource page and its subpages; subpage nesting may double-count a little. The files are in `manning/ia/`.

## Notes

- **Structure.** The spiritual books (*Eternal Priesthood*, *Internal Mission*, *Glories of the Sacred Heart*, *Sin and its Consequences*, *Love of Jesus to Penitents*) divide cleanly into chapters or sermons. The Vatican-council pastorals carry long appendices in Latin: the decree texts and the conciliar documents. That accounts for their lower dictionary scores (0.90–0.91), not bad OCR. On import, either keep the Latin as an appendix chapter (`la` blocks) or drop it as documents available elsewhere (e.g. *Pastor Aeternus* in `papal-magisterium`).
- **Wikisource** is the best witness for *The Eternal Priesthood*, *Petri Privilegium* and *The True Story*. For each, check against the archive.org scan.
- ***Internal Mission* and *The Holy Ghost the Sanctifier*** are different books, as Manning's own notice says. Keep both.

## Recommendation

- **Layout:** `manning-opera-omnia/<work>`, with `en-US` canonical and the Catholic works only (an `anglican/` subfolder later if wanted).
- **Import first (spiritual):** *The Eternal Priesthood* (Wikisource), *The Internal Mission of the Holy Ghost*, *The Temporal Mission of the Holy Ghost*, *The Glories of the Sacred Heart*, *Sin and its Consequences*, *The Love of Jesus to Penitents*, *The Grounds of Faith* (CCEL), *Sermons on Ecclesiastical Subjects*.
- **Then the ecclesiological and historical works:** *Petri Privilegium*, *The True Story of the Vatican Council*, *The Vatican Decrees*, *Religio Viatoris*, *Miscellanies*.
- **Cannot be sourced cleanly:** no Manning work is blocked by copyright. The complete pastoral letters and the *Dignity of Labour* pamphlet were not located individually; several are reprinted in *Miscellanies*.

**Total size:** Catholic works ≈2.08M words (spiritual books ≈0.63M, sermons ≈0.32M, Vatican/papacy ≈0.57M, collections and essays ≈0.55M). Anglican works ≈0.73M. Letters ≈0.07M plus biographies.

**Notes.**
- Manning reached Portuguese readers mostly through French (Estante Virtual lists the French *Conférences prêchées à Londres sur le pouvoir temporel*; BNP holds no other Manning in Portuguese). Today's Brazilian Manning is one small house, Cristo e Livros (three titles), plus Nebli's *Sacerdócio eterno*.
- **The one tier-A Portuguese Manning is the 1887 *O sacerdocio eterno*,** which BNP holds in print and hasn't digitised. Nebli's reprint probably carries the same text.

**Recommendation:** translate from the English. For *The Eternal Priesthood*, get the 1887 Portuguese (a BNP reproduction, or Nebli's reprint as a reading copy). It is tier A, so if a scan is obtained it can be imported or used to seed the pt-BR text, after modernising the 1887 European spelling. Nothing is importable from an online source today. *O Papa e o Anticristo* is a good modern terminology reference but is tier C.
