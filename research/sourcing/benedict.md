# St. Benedict of Nursia (c. 480–547): sourcing

Benedict wrote one work: the **Regula monasteriorum** (Prologue + 73 chapters). Everything else printed under his name (PL 66) is spurious. His life is Book II of St. Gregory's *Dialogues*, sourced in `gregory-the-great.md`.

Nothing of Benedict is in the corpus yet: no `benedict` directory in `content/books/`, and `_corpus-books.txt` lists none.

## Regula: every source found, ranked

### Latin

1. **la.wikisource, *Regula sancti Benedicti*.** https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Regula_sancti_Benedicti (raw wikitext: `?action=raw`). Complete Prologue + ch. 1–73, with chapter headings as `==Caput N: …==` (that makes the split easy). It gives no edition. The orthography (*Obsculta*, *conple*, *oboedientiae*, *diabulum*) is that of the modern critical texts (Hanslik CSEL 75 / de Vogüé SC 181–2 / Neufville), with æ ligatures added. It has small typos (*pastotis*, *delinquentiump*, *inoboetientibus*). 12,837 words by the wiki count. Tier **B**: the medieval text itself has no author copyright, and this transcription circulates freely with no claim. A strict reading could say the orthography derives from a copyrighted critical edition, so it is not A. **Best for a clean import.** Fix the typos against #3 or #4.
2. **The Latin Library**, https://www.thelatinlibrary.com/benedict.html. This is the same transcription as Wikisource (identical headings and typos), so it is not an independent witness. It is complete: 13,464 words with page chrome. Tier B. An archive.org mirror exists (`regula_spn_benedicti`, "Preuzeto: thelatinlibrary.com"). Downloaded: `benedict/rb-latinlibrary.html`.
3. **Wölfflin, *Benedicti Regula monachorum*** (Leipzig: Teubner, 1895), the first critical text. https://archive.org/details/benedictiregula00wlgoog (djvu.txt available). It is a Google scan with OCR, and the apparatus pollutes the text layer. Tier **A**. Use it as an independent PD witness for spot-checking.
4. **Hunter Blair, *The Rule of St. Benedict*, Latin and English on facing pages** (Fort Augustus Abbey Press, 1906 / Sands 1907 / 1914). Archive.org IDs: https://archive.org/details/ruleofstbenedict00bene_1 (1906), https://archive.org/details/TheRuleOfStBenedict (1907, Google scan), and `ruleofstbenedict0000unse_h4o5` (1914). The Latin is the older vulgate text (*Ausculta… comple*, *obedientiae*). The OCR is mediocre: drop caps break (*"U SCULT A"*), sidenotes and stray glyphs intrude, and the two languages interleave page by page. About 47–48k words for both languages together. Tier **A**: published 1906/07, and Hunter Blair died in 1939. Use it as a PD cross-check witness, but it is not clean.
5. **Schmidt, *Regula S. P. Benedicti iuxta antiquissimos codices recognita*** (Regensburg: Pustet, 1880). https://archive.org/details/VitaEtRegulaSSPBenedicti2 (djvu.txt). Tier **A**. OCR not inspected.
6. **Migne PL 66, 215–932** (the Rule embedded in the commentaries of Paul the Deacon / Martène). DCO PDF: https://www.documentacatholicaomnia.eu/04z/z_0480-0547__Benedictus_Nursinus__Regula__MLT.pdf.html. DCO also has a Word file (`…Regula__LT.doc.html`), source not stated. Tier **A**, but it is a scan PDF with the commentary interwoven, so it is the last resort.
7. **IntraText LAT0011**, http://www.intratext.com/X/LAT0011.HTM. IntraText's Latin RB (a concordance hypertext, CC licence). The page responds; the text edition was not checked.

### English

1. **Boniface Verheyen OSB**, *The Holy Rule of Our Most Holy Father Benedict* (Atchison, KS: Abbey Student Press; 1st ed. 1902 Latin–English, 2nd 1906, 8th 1935, 1949 ed.). CCEL: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/benedict/rule, plain text https://www.ccel.org/ccel/b/benedict/rule/cache/rule.txt. Complete, clean e-text, with Scripture references added in parentheses. It uses the archaic *thou*/*hath* register. 21,541 words (including CCEL header). CCEL says **"Rights: Public Domain"**, and Verheyen (1844–1923) died over 70 years ago, so the tier is **A**. On "the 1949 edition": that is just the printing CCEL transcribes. It came out 26 years after his death, and no reviser is credited (archive.osb.org bibliography: "Atchison, Kan.: Abbey student press, 1949", bound with the American-Cassinese *Declarations*). Any posthumous retouching would be a 1949 US publication with no renewal found, which is tier B at worst. **Recommended en-US text.** Downloaded: `benedict/ccel-rule.txt`.
2. **Cardinal F. A. Gasquet**, *The Rule of Saint Benedict* (London: Chatto & Windus, King's Classics, 1909; reissued 1925 in The Medieval Library). It is a more fluent translation than Verheyen's, with a long introduction. HathiTrust full view of the 1909 edition (rights "pdus"): https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=mdp.39015065773759. Archive.org 1925: https://archive.org/details/ruleofsaintbened0000bene (djvu.txt, about 26.9k words with the introduction, good OCR apart from hyphenation and running heads). Tier **A**: Gasquet died in 1929. Baronius Press sells a reset reprint, but that gives it no rights over the 1909 text. **Best second English text**, OCR only.
3. **Leonard J. Doyle**, *St. Benedict's Rule for Monasteries* (Collegeville: Liturgical Press, ©1948, from Butler's 3rd ed. 1935). Project Gutenberg #50040: https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/50040 (clean, about 21.9k words in the body). Downloaded: `benedict/doyle-pg50040.txt`. It is the most modern of the free texts. Tier **B**: Gutenberg cleared it as a US publication never renewed. But Liturgical Press **still sells it** (litpress.org Products/644, ISBN 9780814606445, and the 2001 anniversary edition, ISBN 9780814627358), and osb.org prints "© Copyright 1948, 2001, by the Order of Saint Benedict". Doyle died in 1970, so it is not PD outside the US until 2041. That makes it **B with C-risk**: use it for cross-checking only, not as the shipped English.
4. **Hunter Blair 1906/1914** (Latin #4 above): the English facing page. Tier A, mediocre OCR. A third PD English witness.
5. **The 1638 English Rule, ed. 1875 by "one of the Benedictine Fathers of St. Michael's, Hereford"** (London: Washbourne). Archive.org `ruleourmostholy00benegoog` ("NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT"). The same English, lightly revised, appears in the 1895 Rome edition *The Life of Our Most Holy Father S. Benedict … with the Rule* (Baker 1898), on CCEL: https://www.ccel.org/ccel/gregory/life_rule (text file `benedict/ccel-life_rule.txt`, 40k words, of which the Rule is roughly the second half). Tier **A**. Clean on CCEL, in 17th-century diction.
6. **Ernest F. Henderson**, *Select Historical Documents of the Middle Ages* (London: Bell, 1892; reprints to 1912), pp. 274–314, translated from PL 66. It is **abridged** ("The few omissions made consist almost wholly of pious expressions and exhortations"). Archive.org `selecthistorical00hend` (djvu.txt). Fordham Sourcebook excerpts: https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/source/rul-benedict.asp. Tier **A**, but not usable as the main text.

Unusable (tier **C**): McCann (Burns Oates 1952; the Stanbrook 1937 private-press edition is unverified), RB 1980 (Fry, Liturgical Press), Kardong, White (Penguin), Venarde (Dumbarton Oaks).

## Notes

- **Structure.** Prologue + 73 chapters. Use one chapter file per chapter (74 files), or group the liturgical code (ch. 8–20) as some editions do. One file per chapter keeps alignment with the daily-reading tradition (the Rule read three times a year) trivial. Chapter numbering is identical across the Latin and all English versions above.
- **Text-family mismatch.** Wikisource follows the critical text; Verheyen translated the older vulgate text (he worked before Butler). The differences are slight at chapter level and don't affect alignment, but Verheyen's added Scripture references follow Vulgate psalm numbering ("Ps 94[95]:8").
- **Spurious works in PL 66** (all on DCO, Benedict author page https://www.documentacatholicaomnia.eu/20_40_0480-0547-_Benedictus_Nursinus,_Sanctus.html): *Epistola ad S. Remigium* (letter to St. Remigius), *Sermo in mortem S. Placidi* (part of the Placidus legend forged at Monte Cassino in the 12th century), *Ordo monasticus [attributus]*, and "Opuscola" (PL 66, 933–934). None is authentic, and there is no English. Leave them out, or at most note them in the book description. Exact authorship ascriptions here are from general knowledge and unverified.
- DCO also lists *Testimonia veterum de Regula* and the Gregory *Dialogi* II excerpt. These are dossier material, not Benedict's work.

## Recommendation

- **Layout:** `church-fathers/benedict/rule/`, with `la/` (Wikisource, typos corrected against Wölfflin 1895), `en-US/` (Verheyen via CCEL), and `pt-BR/` (pipeline). This matches the existing `church-fathers/<author>/<work>` layout (Gregory, Cassian). There is no separate opera omnia because there is one work.
- **Import first:** the Rule. It is short (about 13k Latin words), and both the Latin and the English are clean digital text with no OCR work.
- **Cross-check witnesses:** Latin against Wölfflin 1895 / Hunter Blair; English against Gasquet 1909 and Doyle (Doyle for checking only).
- **Pair it** with Gregory's *Dialogues* Book II (Life of Benedict), which the Gregory report sources. The CCEL Rome-1895 *Life and Rule* volume contains both in PD English.
- Nothing is tier-C-only or scan-only.

**Total size:** about 13k words of Latin and about 22k of English (Verheyen), so the pt-BR target is about 22k words.

Raw downloads: `research/sourcing/benedict/`.

## Portuguese

The general web-search budget ran out partway through this pass. After that, searching went only through the sites' own catalogues: archive.org advancedsearch, pt.wikisource API, the IntraText index, osb.org / archive.osb.org, BNP permalinks, and the Martins Fontes Paulista catalogue API, which covers most Brazilian Catholic publishers. Domínio Público and Lírio Católico sit behind a Cloudflare challenge and could not be searched. pt.wikisource has no Rule. The bibliography to use is **Geraldo J. A. Coelho Dias OSB, "A Regra de S. Bento, norma de vida monástica: sua problemática moderna e edições em português", *História* (FLUP) 3, 2002, pp. 9–48**: https://repositorio-aberto.up.pt/handle/10216/7969 (scan-only PDF; local tesseract OCR in `benedict/pt/dias-2002-ocr.txt`). It counts **44 Portuguese editions from 1516 to 2000** (military orders 3, monastic congregations 6, private-devotion pocket editions 17, modern 3 in Portugal plus "1+11" in Brazil), lists 11 medieval Portuguese manuscripts, and describes the 1586 *princeps* and the 1992 Singeverga edition in detail.

**Recommendation.** Use **Enout (IntraText POR0014)** as the pt-BR reference while translating: it is complete, clean, and the translation Brazilian monasteries actually read. Do not ship it, because it is a 1980 translation still sold by Lumen Christi. The only tier-A Portuguese texts are the 1586 Pinto translation (a scan) and its 1632 reprint (poor OCR, archaic). Neither is importable as a modern pt-BR text. The pt-BR Rule should come from our own pipeline, with Enout as the cross-check.
