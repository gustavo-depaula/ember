# St. Peter of Alcántara (1499–1562): sourcing report

Not in the corpus. Nothing in `_corpus-books.txt` or `content/books/` mentions Alcántara or Pedro, under any slug.

**Authority for the canon.** The canon below follows the *Dictionnaire de Spiritualité* article (vol. XII/2, cols. 1489–1495, Beauchesne 1986). **The Granada / Alcántara attribution.** The *Tratado* condenses Luis de Granada's *Libro de la oración y meditación* (Salamanca 1554). Its weekly meditations (I.2) and Passion meditations (I.4) are taken, often verbatim, from Granada.
- **Case for Granada.** Justo Cuervo OP argued that Granada was "the true and only author". His texts are *Biografía de Fr. Luis de Granada… donde se demuestra que el venerable padre, y no San Pedro de Alcántara, es el verdadero y único autor del Libro de la oración* (Madrid 1895, archive.org `biografadefrlu00cuer`) and *Fr. Luis de Granada verdadero y único autor del Libro de la Oración* (1918, `frluisdegranadav00cuer`). He relied on the printers' "advertencias" in the Blavio (Lisbon) and Portonariis (Salamanca 1574) editions.
- **Rebuttal.** León Amorós OFM, "San Pedro de Alcántara y su «Tratado de la Oración y Meditación»", *Archivo Ibero-Americano* 22 (1962) 163–221, is open access at <https://revistasfranciscanas.org/index.php/ArchivoIberoAmericano/article/view/385> (PDF downloaded to `peter-of-alcantara/amoros1962.pdf`, 26k words). He concludes that Alcántara is the author of the Blavio text "tal como ha llegado hasta nosotros". On his reading:
  - Alcántara compiled the meditations from Granada but wrote the doctrinal core himself: chs. I.5–12 (the six parts of prayer, the eight *avisos*) and all of Part II (devotion, its aids, its hindrances, temptations, admonitions).
  - Teresa quotes that doctrinal core in *Moradas* IV.
  - The printers' notices are commercial manoeuvres.
- **Consensus.** The DSp agrees: the work borrows from Granada, but "Pedro aporta mucho de sí mismo". The Church (canonisation; John XXIII, 1962) and modern editors (Sanz Valdivieso 1996, the UNED critical-edition thesis) treat it as Alcántara's.
- **Recommendation.** Import it under Alcántara, with a front-matter note that Part I's meditations are drawn from Granada. If Granada's *Libro de la oración* is ever imported, cross-reference the two rather than dedupe.

## Notes per work

### Tratado de la oración y meditación
**Structure** (identical across the DCO text, the 1632 TCP, the 1844 and 1905 editions and Bouix's French):
- Prólogo (in 1632 a dedication).
- **Part I**, 12 chapters:
  - I fruit of prayer
  - II matter of meditation, with 7 meditations for the days of the week (Mon sins, Tue miseries of life, Wed death, Thu judgement, Fri hell, Sat glory, Sun benefits)
  - III time and fruit
  - IV seven Passion meditations (Mon Supper/Eucharist … Sun Resurrection/Ascension)
  - V six parts of prayer
  - VI–XI preparation, reading, meditation, thanksgiving, oblation, petition (XI ends with the "oración para alcanzar el amor de Dios")
  - XII eight *avisos*
- **Part II**, 5 chapters: I what devotion is; II nine aids; III ten (1632: nine) hindrances; IV nine temptations with remedies; V eight admonitions.

**Split.** One file per chapter, with the 7 + 7 daily meditations as their own files: about 33 files.

**No apparatus** beyond scripture and patristic citations. The DCO text has inline numbers for some citations.

### Breve introducción + De tres cosas
- **Text.** A short rule of life: A-B-C of spiritual exercises, examen, frequent communion, and so on. Only in early-print scans, where the OCR is garbage (1699 Seville ff. 139–152: archive.org `A253022`; 1622 *Compendio*). For Spanish it must be keyed from a scan, or taken from the 1594/1587 printings via vision.
- **English.** Hollings 1905 App. I–II is clean enough to use (tier A).
- **Authenticity.** Not in the DSp canon. Import, if at all, as an appendix to the *Tratado* labelled "traditionally attributed".

### Constitutions, *Miserere*, *Camino de perfección*
None found online. They exist in manuscript (AHN, RAH, Barcelona) and in modern editions (BAC 570; AIA articles). The AIA archive at revistasfranciscanas.org is open access and was digitised in 2025. Its 1962 issues are the most likely free source of editions of the constitutions and the *Miserere* (Amorós's article was there), but I did not find them in this pass. Mark **gap / unverified**.

## Recommendation

**Layout.** `alcantara-opera-omnia/` with:
- `tratado-oracion-meditacion/`: `es/` from DCO; `en-US/` from Willoughby. Use the TCP 1632 keyed text, normalised against the 1844 modern-spelling reprint, or Hollings 1905 if a smoother modern English is preferred (see below). About 33 chapter files.
  - Optionally include Willoughby's *Life* as front matter in `en-US` only. It is about him, not by him, and has no Spanish original.
- `breve-introduccion/`: appendix, "traditionally attributed". `en-US` from Hollings App. I–II. `es` needs keying from a 1587/1699 scan.
- `cartas/`: the two Silverio letters now (es + en for the Teresa letter); the rest when a free edition is found.

**Import first.** The *Tratado*: es DCO + en Willoughby/Hollings, cross-checked against the folkscanomy OCRs and Bouix's French.

**Choosing the English.** The two English versions translate different Spanish redactions: Willoughby's III has nine hindrances where the Spanish and Hollings have ten. Hollings matches the DCO Spanish more closely, and its appendices also cover the *Breve introducción*. So **Hollings 1905 is the better en-US match for the es text**, with Willoughby/TCP as the cross-check. Spot-check before deciding.

**Cannot be sourced cleanly now:**
- 10 of the 12 letters, the three sets of constitutions, the *Miserere* commentary and the *Camino de perfección*: tier C editions or manuscript only.
- The Spanish *Breve introducción*: scans only.

**Do not import as Alcántara:** *Paz del alma* (Bonilla), *Tratado de los votos* (Savonarola), the *Dictamen* (Ibáñez), or the devotional prayers printed after the *Tratado*.

## Total size
- **Sourceable now:** about **35k words Spanish** (Tratado ~31.5k, Breve introducción ~2.5k est., 2 letters ~1.2k) and about **38.5k words English** (Tratado ~35.3k, Short Introduction + Three Things ~3.5k, Teresa letter ~0.7k). Willoughby's *Life* adds 13k English if wanted.
