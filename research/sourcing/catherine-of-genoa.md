# St. Catherine of Genoa (Caterina Fieschi Adorno, 1447–1510) — sourcing

Catherine wrote nothing with her own hand. Everything goes back to one compilation, the *Libro de la vita mirabile et dottrina santa de la beata Caterinetta da Genoa* (Genoa: Bellone, 1551), drawn from her disciples' notes (Cattaneo Marabotto, her confessor; Ettore Vernazza). Its three parts are the works:

1. **Vita mirabile e dottrina santa**: the Life, with her sayings and teaching (in the 1743 edition it runs pp. 1–258, the Trattato pp. 259–282 and the Dialogo from p. 283. I did not confirm the chapter count from the poor OCR).
2. **Trattato del Purgatorio** (*Dimostratione et dechiaratione del purgatorio*), 17–19 chapters by edition. Its doctrine is accepted as hers. The wording is the disciples'.
3. **Dialogo spirituale** between the Soul, the Body, Self-love, the Spirit, Humanity and God, in 3 parts. **Doubtful as to authorship.** Part I reflects her teaching. Parts II–III are generally ascribed to Battista Vernazza (Ettore's daughter), as von Hügel (1908) and Bonzi argued. Mark it "attributed".

The critical edition is Umile Bonzi da Genova, *S. Caterina Fieschi Adorno*, vol. II, *Edizione critica dei manoscritti cateriniani* (Marietti 1961–62). It is tier C, reference only.

Nothing by or about her is in the corpus apart from encyclopedia and lives-of-saints entries.

## Vita mirabile e dottrina santa

Original sources, best first:
1. **1743 Padova (Comino) edition**, *Vita mirabile, e dottrina celeste di Santa Caterina Fiesca Adorna … scritta già da Cattaneo Marabotto ed Ettore Vernazza*: https://archive.org/details/wotb_6743872, full text at https://archive.org/download/wotb_6743872/wotb_6743872_djvu.txt (145k OCR words for Vita, Trattato and Dialogo together, with the editor's matter). Tier A. The OCR is **poor**: long s read as f ("fempre", "Crocififfo"). It needs either a regex pass (ſ→s, done with care) or fresh OCR. This is the most legible early edition found.
2. **1590 Venice edition**, *Vita della beata Caterina Adorni da Genova, con un dialogo…*: Google scans at https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_vVGeonabZpoC (104k OCR words), with other copies at `bub_gb_WA6YU7K0tkwC` (113k), `bub_gb_LPgJlBuCSiwC` and `bub_gb_716a7xJ29xcC`. Tier A. The OCR is worse than 1743 (u/v, long s, broken words). Multiple scans of the same edition allow cross-checks.
3. The 1551 *editio princeps*: I did not locate a digital copy. Bonzi 1962: tier C.
4. Not her text: Parpera, *B. Caterina di Genova … illustrata* (1682), and Marcellino da Civezza's anthology (1873) are later re-workings.

English, best first:
1. ***Life and Doctrine of Saint Catherine of Genoa*, "translated from the Italian"**, New York: Catholic Publication Society 1874, with an introduction by Isaac T. Hecker (signed "I. T. Hecker"). CCEL transcribes the 1907 Christian Press Association reprint: https://ccel.org/ccel/catherine_g/life/life, plain text at https://www.ccel.org/ccel/c/catherine_g/life/cache/life.txt. Tier A (1874/1907, and CCEL marks it "Proofed"). The whole file is 79k words: introduction, Life chapters I–XLII (a "XXXIV" heading appears to be missing in the CCEL text, so check it), Spiritual Dialogue parts I–III, and Treatise on Purgatory chapters I–XVII. It is the only complete PD English of all three works, and clean.
2. The 1874 volume as a scan: https://archive.org/details/lifedoctrineofsa0000vari (OCR 86k). Use it to check the CCEL transcription, including the missing chapter XXXIV.
3. Friedrich von Hügel, *The Mystical Element of Religion as studied in Saint Catherine of Genoa and her Friends* (Dent 1908; 2nd ed. 1923): https://archive.org/details/TheMysticalElementOfReligionV1. Tier A for the 1908 edition (von Hügel died 1925). It is a study, not a translation, but it translates and sorts the authentic sayings, which helps with attribution.
4. Balfour & Irvine (Sheed & Ward 1946) and Hughes, *Purgation and Purgatory; The Spiritual Dialogue* (Paulist CWS 1979): **tier C**.

Structure: the 1874 English has 42 chapters for the Vita, and the Italian division (1551/1743) is not confirmed to match it. Check the chapter concordance at import.

## Trattato del Purgatorio

Original sources, best first:
1. **documentacatholicaomnia.eu**, Word file, modernised Italian: https://www.documentacatholicaomnia.eu/03d/1447-1510,_Caterina_da_Genova,_Trattato_Sul_Purgatorio,_IT.doc. I downloaded and converted it: 5,569 words, **complete**, with an index of 19 chapters with headings ("I. – Perfetta uniformità delle Anime purganti al volere di Dio" … "XIX. – La Santa conclude…") and numbered paragraphs. It does not name its edition. The chapter titles are a modern editor's (likely an early-20th-century devotional edition), so the tier is **unverified**: DCO posts it with no licence terms, which is probably B. Cross-check it against the 1743 scan.
2. The 1743 and 1590 editions above, which have the Treatise after the Vita (scans, OCR poor). Tier A.

English, best first:
1. The 1874 *Life and Doctrine* (CCEL), Treatise chapters I–XVII. Tier A, proofread.
2. ***Treatise on Purgatory*, translated with a preface by Cardinal Manning**, London: Burns & Lambert 1858: https://archive.org/details/TreatiseOnPurgatory (Google scan, 9.4k OCR words with the preface), also `TheTreatiseOnPurgatory` and `TreatiseOnPurgatory1858`. Tier A. It is an independent translation, which LibriVox recorded (https://librivox.org/treatise-on-purgatory-by-st-catherine-of-genoa/), a sign that it is accepted as PD.
3. Balfour/Irvine 1946 and Hughes 1979: tier C.

## Dialogo spirituale (attributed)

- Original: in the 1590 and 1743 editions only (scans, poor OCR). I found no clean Italian text online.
- English: 1874 *Life and Doctrine* (CCEL): Part I (21 chapters), Part II (11), Part III (13). Tier A. A LibriVox recording exists (`spiritualdialogue_ab_librivox` on archive.org), probably read from this text (not checked). Hughes 1979: tier C.
- Attribution: import it as "attributed to St Catherine of Genoa" and note the scholarly view that Parts II–III are by Battista Vernazza.

4. archive.org `tratado-do-purgato-rio` ("TRATADO DO PURGATÓRIO", user upload dated 2026, metadata language mislabelled "spa"). The text download returned HTTP 502 when I tried, so the source edition and translator are unknown. Unverified, probably a copy of a modern edition (C).

Nothing for the Vita or the Dialogo. pt-BR from our own translation.

## Recommendation

- Layout: `catherine-of-genoa-opera-omnia/` with `vita/`, `trattato-del-purgatorio/`, `dialogo-spirituale/` (it/ + en-US/).
- **Import first: the Trattato del Purgatorio.** It is short, the most read, has a clean Italian text (DCO, tier to confirm) and two PD English translations (1874 CCEL, 1858 Manning).
- Second: the English Vita and Dialogo from CCEL. The English is clean, but the Italian exists only as long-s OCR (1743, 1590). To be complete in the original language they need fresh OCR of the 1743 Comino scan, or a careful long-s fix of its djvu text.
- Not cleanly sourceable: any Italian Vita or Dialogo better than OCR of the early printings. The critical text (Bonzi 1962) is tier C.
- **Total**: about 90k Italian words (Vita ~55k, Trattato ~5.5k, Dialogo ~30k, the Vita and Dialogo figures estimated from the 1743 OCR) and about 79k English words (the whole 1874 volume).

Raw downloads are in `research/sourcing/catherine-of-genoa/raw/`.
