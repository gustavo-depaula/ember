# St. Vincent de Paul (1581–1660): sourcing report

**Reference bibliography:** Pierre Coste CM (ed.), *Saint Vincent de Paul. Correspondance, entretiens, documents*, Paris, J. Gabalda / Lecoffre, 1920–1925, 14 volumes. It is the critical edition and the canon of his works. Vincent wrote no treatises: his works are letters, conferences (taken down by his hearers) and documents (rules, regulations, contracts, his few memoranda).

| Coste vol. | Contents | Date |
|---|---|---|
| I–VIII | **Correspondance**: about 3,000 letters, nos. 1 → ≈3,300, 1607 → 1660. Coste includes letters **to** Vincent as well, with notes | 1920–1924 |
| IX–X | **Entretiens aux Filles de la Charité** (≈120 conferences, 1634–1660) | 1923 |
| XI | **Entretiens aux Missionnaires** I (conference extracts, *répétitions d'oraison*, counsels) | 1923/24 |
| XII | **Entretiens aux Missionnaires** II (conferences on the Common Rules 1658–59, etc.) | 1924 |
| XIII | **Documents**: biographical documents, the rules of the Confraternities of Charity, the Common Rules of the Congregation of the Mission, regulations, writings on the Daughters of Charity | 1924 |
| XIV | Tables générales | 1925 |

**Supplements after Coste:**
- *Mission et Charité* nos. 19–20 (1970): Lettres inédites, ≈200 items. JesusMarie presents it as "tome XV". Tier C/unverified.
- John E. Rybolt CM (ed.), unpublished texts in 4 volumes, French/Latin/Italian with English translation, DePaul Vincentian Studies Institute, 2020s. Free PDFs on via.library.depaul.edu; tier B/unverified.

**Doubtful or not his:** JesusMarie's *Petit Catéchisme* and *Avis et maximes* are a compilation and a catechism of uncertain attribution; don't import them as his works without a source. The *Maximes et pratiques de St. Vincent de Paul* (1885) are extracts. Abelly's *Vie* (1664) and Ansart's *Spirit of St Vincent de Paul* are about him, not by him.

## Original-language sources, ranked

### Coste, all volumes
1. **JesusMarie.com HTML, Coste I–XIV (+ "XV")**: http://jesusmarie.free.fr/vincent_de_paul.html, files `vincent_de_paul_coste_{1..15}.htm`. This is the "édition numérique par le Père Claude Lautissier CM" (famvin CD-ROM, http://www.famvin.org/fr/edition%20SV/Doc_CDRom.html, which now returns 404), keyed and corrected by Daughters of Charity: "Numérisation et correction terminées … 19 avril 1992 … Sœurs Marie-Catherine Desmard et Geneviève Rabiller, Filles de la Charité. Claude Lautissier". The text is clean, with modern accents. Coste's letter numbers, headnotes ("Lettre 1. — L. a. — L'original…") and footnotes are inline. Text-only words: I 197k, II 208k, III 220k, IV 196k, V 202k, VI 192k, VII 182k, VIII 176k, IX 226k, X 244k, XI 149k, XII 154k, XIII 276k, XIV (index) 243k, XV 75k.
   - **Rights:** each file ends "**© CONGRÉGATION de la MISSION, Province de Paris, 88, rue du Cherche-Midi, 75006 PARIS**". The underlying Coste text is PD: published 1920–25, and Coste died 1935, so 70 years have passed. But the digital transcription is **claimed**, so by the brief's definition it is not tier B. → **unverified/claimed**. Two options: (a) use it only to cross-check our own OCR, or (b) ask the CM Paris Province for permission. A mere transcription likely carries no copyright in the US (no originality, *Feist*); this is a policy call for Gustavo.
   - **Quirks:** vol. I contains a stray block of Pascal's *Pensées* ("BLaise PASCAL * Jésus-Christ 728…") pasted into the middle of the letters, so strip it. There are page markers ("— p. 329 —") and some OCR-ish typos (`premièrernent`, `plrocureur`, `RELATlFS A SAlNT VlNCENT`).
2. **archive.org scans of Coste**, all tier A:
   - Toronto: `correspondancee{01..11}vinc` and `correspondanceen{01..11}vinc`, vols 1–11 (two scans each).
   - Wellcome Library: `b31366818_00{01,02,03,08,09,10,11,12,13,14}`. This is the only archive set with vols **XII–XIV**.
   - `saintvincentdepa00{01,02,03,04,06,07,09,10,13,14}unse` (1920–25; date per volume).
   - Djvu OCR is **good** (sample from XI: "Ne recevoir aucun présent de qui que ce soit, pour petit ou pour grand qu'il puisse être."), with hyphenation and occasional `ia` for `la`. Raw: XI 147k, XII 157k, XIV 242k.
   - **Best clearly-licensed witness.**
3. Pre-Coste editions as third witnesses (fewer texts, older readings), tier A:
   - Pémartin, *Lettres de S. Vincent de Paul*, 4 vols, Dumoulin 1880–82: `lettresdesvincen01vinc` (180k raw), `…02vinc`, `lettresdesvince0{0,1}paulgoog`.
   - *Conférences de S. Vincent de Paul aux Filles de la Charité*, 1881 (2 vols): not checked online.
   - *Lettres choisies*, 1908: `lettreschoisiesd00vinc`.
   - *Saint Vincent de Paul; textes choisis*, 1913: `saintvincentdep00paulgoog`.
4. Gallica (Coste and Pémartin are there): blocked by Cloudflare from this machine, so **unverified**.

### Structure
- **Letters**: Coste numbers, chronological, each with a heading ("1. — A MONSIEUR DE COMET") and Coste's source note and footnotes. Group into chapters by Coste volume or by year (≈3,000 items).
- **Conferences**: Coste numbers, each a conference with date and subject. Import one chapter per conference (≈120 to the Daughters of Charity, ≈200 entries to the Missionaries).
- **Documents**: numbered items, a mix of Latin and French (ordination letters, contracts, rules). The Common Rules (1658) and the Charity regulations are the spiritually important pieces.

## Recommendation

**Layout:** `content/books/vincent-opera-omnia/`, with:
- `correspondance/`: chapters by Coste volume or by year.
- `entretiens-filles-de-la-charite/`: one chapter per conference.
- `entretiens-missionnaires/`.
- `documents/`, with the Common Rules and the Charity regulations possibly split out as their own books: `vincent-common-rules`, `vincent-reglement-charite`.

**Import first:**
1. **Entretiens aux Filles de la Charité (IX–X)**: his best-known spiritual teaching, self-contained, ≈470k words. French from the Coste OCR on archive.org, cross-checked against the JesusMarie/Lautissier text.
2. **Entretiens aux Missionnaires (XI–XII)**, with the Common Rules from XIII.
3. **Correspondance (I–VIII)**: large. Consider filtering out the letters *to* Vincent, or keeping them marked as such, since Coste interleaves them.
4. Leave out vol. XIV (index) and the 1970 supplement (tier C/unverified).

**English:** no usable PD English exists for any major work. Two options: (a) ask DePaul's Vincentian Studies Institute / New City Press for permission to use CCD, and the CM Paris Province for the Lautissier transcription; or (b) run fr-FR → en-US through our own pipeline as well as pt-BR. Flag this to Gustavo before importing.

**Cannot be sourced cleanly:**
- English (tier C only).
- The clean French HTML is under a © claim.
- The archive.org OCR is tier A and good but needs proofreading.
- Vols XII–XIV exist only in the Wellcome scans.

**Total size (est.):** Coste I–XIII ≈ **2.6M words** with footnotes (JM count). Vincent's own text ≈ **2.0M** after removing notes, Coste's headnotes and letters addressed to him (rough estimate). PD English ≈ **0.03M** (extracts only); the CCD tier-C English ≈ 2.5M.

Downloads used for counting: `research/sourcing/vincent-de-paul/jm/` (JesusMarie Coste HTML and text), `ia/` (archive djvu samples).

## Portuguese

No old (pre-1930) Portuguese translation of Vincent's own writings turned up. The modern one is the Brazilian translation of Coste, made by the Província Brasileira da Congregação da Missão (PBCM) with the Vincentian Family. It is sold, so it serves only as a reference. Searched: archive.org (advancedsearch on creator/title "Vicente de Paulo" / "Vincent de Paul" with `language:por`: nothing by him); pt.wikisource (only a hymn to him); Biblioteca Nacional Digital (Portugal) `bndigital.bnportugal.gov.pt` (only music for his feast and a *Novena de S. Vicente de Paulo*, item 83125, which is devotional matter *about* him, not his text); famvin.org/pt, filles-de-la-charite.org/pt-pt, pbcm.org.br (articles that quote him, no full texts). Not checkable from here: dominiopublico.gov.br (HTTP 403 on its search form), bndigital.bn.gov.br (Cloudflare block), Google Books (API quota exhausted, HTML search behind a captcha). The web-search budget also ran out partway through, so the 19th-century Portuguese printings (Common Rules, maxims) are **unverified, not ruled out**.
