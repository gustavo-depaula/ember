# St. Ignatius of Loyola (1491–1556): sourcing report

Bibliography: the *Monumenta Historica Societatis Iesu* (MHSI), series *Monumenta Ignatiana*: Series I *Epistolae et instructiones* (12 vols, Madrid 1903–1911); Series II *Exercitia spiritualia et eorum Directoria* (Madrid 1919; new ed. Rome 1955/1969); Series III *Constitutiones Societatis Iesu* (4 vols, Rome 1934–1948, which includes the *Diario espiritual* and the *Deliberatio de paupertate* among the *Monumenta constitutionum praevia*); Series IV *Scripta de S. Ignatio* (2 vols, Madrid 1904/1918, which includes the *Acta* = Autobiography), superseded by *Fontes narrativi* I (1943). The modern one-volume reference is the BAC *Obras de San Ignacio de Loyola* (Iparraguirre–Dalmases–Ruiz Jurado; tier C as an edition, but the 16th-c. text itself is public domain).

Ignatius wrote in **Spanish** (with some Italian and Latin). The *Ejercicios* exist in the Spanish *Autógrafo* (a copy with his own corrections) and two Latin versions: the *Versio prima* (Ignatius's own early Latin, c. 1534/1541) and the *Vulgata* (André des Freux, printed Rome 1548, the approved text). The Autobiography is Spanish to n. 79 and Italian from there to the end (Câmara's amanuenses changed in Genoa). Most of the ~7,000 letters were drafted by his secretaries (above all Juan de Polanco) and sent in his name; MHSI prints them all.

Nothing by Ignatius is in the corpus (`_corpus-books.txt` and `content/books/` checked; `church-fathers/ignatius/` is Ignatius of Antioch).

## Notes per work, with every source ranked

### Ejercicios espirituales (Spanish Autograph)
Structure: Anotaciones [1–20], then four Weeks, with the paragraph numbering [1]–[370] that every modern edition uses (Principio y fundamento [23], Examen [24–43], meditations, Dos banderas [136], Elección [169–189], Contemplación para alcanzar amor [230–237], Tres modos de orar [238–260], Misterios [261–312], Reglas de discreción [313–336], de distribuir limosnas, de escrúpulos [345–351], para sentir con la Iglesia [352–370]). Chapter split: Annotations / Week I / Week II / Week III / Week IV / Three methods of prayer / Mysteries / Rules — 8–12 files, keeping [n] as anchors. No footnotes in the base text.

Original-language sources:
1. **DCO "Ejercicios Espirituales" (ES PDF, 47 pp., text layer)** — B. Complete, [n] numbering, 16th-c. orthography preserved (*annotación, exercicios, rescibir*), from a modern critical edition of the Autógrafo (the "Concordancia ignaciana" text, same family as the BAC). Clean, born-digital; best base. DCO hosts it without a licence claim (its copyright page is a generic Wikipedia extract).
2. **MHSI *Exercitia spiritualia… et eorum Directoria*, Madrid 1919** (archive.org `monumentaignatia01igna`, `_djvu.txt` 511k words for the whole volume) — A. The critical edition: Autógrafo, Vulgata, Versio prima and Roothaan's version on facing columns, with apparatus. OCR good (sampled: "El hombre es criado para alabar, hazer reuerencia…"), but the four columns interleave page by page, so extraction needs per-page column work. The cross-check witness.
3. **Facsimile of the Autograph, Rome 1908** (`ejerciciosespiri00igna`) — A; manuscript images only, OCR useless; for ruling on single readings.
4. 1833 Spanish edition (`ejercicios-espirituales-de-san-ignacio-de-loyola_202606`) — A; pre-critical, probably not the Autógrafo; fallback only.
(es.wikisource has no text of the *Ejercicios*.)

English, ranked:
1. **Elder Mullan SJ, 1914, from the Autograph** — A (1914). CCEL "Proofed": https://www.ccel.org/ccel/i/ignatius/exercises/cache/exercises.txt (32,770 words counted); also IntraText ENG0036 and sacred-texts. Follows the Autograph closely; the standard PD English.
2. **John Morris SJ, *The Text of the Spiritual Exercises… translated from the original Spanish*, Burns & Oates 1880** (4th ed. 1908) — A. https://archive.org/details/textofspirituale00ignauoft (OCR 31,900 words, clean). Independent second translation; ideal cross-check.
3. **W. H. Longridge, 1919, from the Spanish, with commentary and the Directorium** — A (Longridge d. 1930; the brief's rule "dead 70+ years" applies; 1919 publication). https://archive.org/details/spiritualexercis00igna_4 (155k words with commentary). Anglican; text good, commentary not wanted.
4. **Charles Seager, 1847, from Roothaan's literal Latin**, pref. Wiseman — A. https://archive.org/details/a588350800loyouoft. Translation of a translation; fallback.

### Exercitia spiritualia — the Latin versions
The *Vulgata* (1548) is the text the Church approved (Paul III, *Pastoralis officii*) and the one used for four centuries; the *Versio prima* is Ignatius's own. Worth a `la/` text beside `es-ES/`, but as a separate work, since the Latin versions do not match the Autograph sentence for sentence.
1. **MHSI 1919, Vulgata and Versio prima columns** — A; critical; OCR good, columns interleaved (see above).
2. **Roothaan's edition, 1881 (Vulgata + Roothaan's literal Latin version from the Autograph, notes)** — A. https://archive.org/details/exercitiaspiritu00igna_0 (106k words OCR). Clean single-column print; best extraction source for the Vulgata.
3. Dozens of early prints of the Vulgata on archive.org (1599 `bub_gb_4U7WeLIXzmIC`, 1644, 1676, 1838 `bub_gb_1d8omgVldrwC`) — A; Google Books OCR of early type, poorer.
English: Seager 1847 (above) translates Roothaan's version, not the Vulgata; no PD English of the Vulgata itself was found.

### Autobiografía (Relato del Peregrino)
101 numbered paragraphs, divided by modern editors into 11 chapters (es.wikisource uses I–XI, split at "VIII (continuación)" where the Italian begins). Also Câmara's preface and Nadal's prologue. Short: one file per chapter.

Original-language sources:
1. **MHSI *Scripta de S. Ignatio* I, Madrid 1904, "Acta S. Ignatii a P. Consalvio"** — A. https://archive.org/details/monumentaignati00ignagoog (Google scan of the Harvard copy). The only one here with the text as dictated: Spanish to fol. 19, then **Italian** to the end (verified: "In Ruano…", n. 79ff.). OCR fair (old Google OCR; abbreviations, Latin chapter summaries and footnotes to strip). Required for the canonical text of §79–101.
2. **es.wikisource**, https://es.wikisource.org/wiki/Autobiografía_de_San_Ignacio_de_Loyola (13 pages, 19,673 words counted; saved in `ignatius-of-loyola/ws-autobio/`) — chapters I–VIII are the Spanish original, modernized spelling; the rest is a modern Spanish translation of the Italian by user "Sio2" (stated on the index page), so CC BY-SA and not the original. Clean; use for §1–79 only.
3. **DCO "Autobiografía" PDF** — B; same text as Wikisource (identical wording checked at n. 1), 19,300 words.
4. MHSI *Fontes narrativi* I (1943) is the modern critical edition — not found online; would be tier B/C.

English:
1. **J. F. X. O'Conor SJ, *The Autobiography of St. Ignatius*, Benziger 1900** — A. Gutenberg #24534 (clean, 26,056 words incl. front matter) and archive `stignatiusautobi00ignauoft`. Translated from the Latin version of Annibal du Coudret, so it can drift from the Spanish/Italian.
2. **E. M. Rix, *The Testament of Ignatius Loyola*, London 1900** — A. https://archive.org/details/testamentofignat00igna (and `cu31924029423146`). Independent PD translation, with Câmara's preface; cross-check.
3. Modern translations (Young 1956 *St. Ignatius' Own Story*; Tylenda 1985/2001 *A Pilgrim's Journey*; Olin 1974) — C (Loyola Press / Ignatius Press / Fordham); not usable.

### Diario espiritual
Two notebooks: 2 Feb–12 Mar 1544 (the election on the poverty of the churches) and 13 Mar 1544–27 Feb 1545 (Masses and tears). 490 dated entries in the DCO numbering. Ignatius's own sigla (marginal signs for tears before/during/after Mass, crossed-out words) need a decision: render as small markers or drop. Two chapters (the two notebooks), or one per month.
1. **DCO "El diario espiritual" PDF** — B. Complete (ends "27. … viernes, O C Y en misa mucha abundancia…"); Spanish original with the symbol conventions of Thió de Pol (*La intimidad del Peregrino*, 1990) and BAC; contains an editorial introduction about the symbols to strip. 18,570 words.
2. **MHSI *Constitutiones* I, *Monumenta constitutionum praevia*, Rome 1934 (ed. A. Codina)** — A (16th-c. text; the 1934 edition's editorial protection has long lapsed). https://archive.org/details/monumentaignatia01igna_0 (OCR 267k words for the volume; the Diario is printed among the monuments, with the *Deliberatio de paupertate* as Monumentum 17). Critical; the cross-check.
English:
1. **William J. Young SJ, "The Spiritual Journal of Ignatius Loyola", *Woodstock Letters* 87 (July 1958) 195–267** — B: a Jesuit house journal "for Ours" (private circulation), now posted openly by Boston College's Jesuit Online Library; no copyright notice was found in the OCR of the issue (downloaded to `ignatius-of-loyola/en/`), and a 1958 US publication without notice is public domain. Translation from the MHSI text. PDF scan with OCR in scrambled column order; needs re-OCR or careful cleanup. ~20,000 words (est.).
2. Munitiz 1987 (*Inigo: Discernment Log-Book*), Simon Decloux etc. — C.

### Constituciones de la Compañía de Jesús
Structure: *Examen general* (8 chapters) + *Constituciones* in 10 Parts, each Part in chapters, with the *Declaraciones* (lettered [A], [B]…) interleaved; standard numbering [1]–[827]. Chapter split: Examen + 10 Parts (11 files), declarations kept as indented blocks.
1. **DCO "Constituciones" PDF** — B. Complete (Examen + 10 Parts, declarations, numbered "Cons 1:171"-style), 74,800 words; contains a few modern editorial notes (e.g. a reference to CIC 701/CCEO 502) to strip.
2. **MHSI *Constitutiones* II, *Textus hispanus*, Rome 1936** — A as to the text (Ignatius); https://archive.org/details/monumentaignatia02igna_0 (OCR 533k words: it prints the successive texts a, A, B, D side by side). Critical witness.
3. Latin: **1838 London reprint of the Rome 1558 Latin edition with English translation** (below) and MHSI III *Textus latinus* (1938) https://archive.org/details/monumentaignatia03igna; also *Institutum Societatis Iesu* II (Florence 1893): https://archive.org/details/Institutum2 — all A.
English:
1. **Anonymous (Protestant) translation, *Constitutiones Societatis Jesu… with a translation*, London 1838 (Tegg/Rivington)** — A. https://archive.org/details/constitutionesso00jesuuoft (also `constitutioness00jesugoog`, `constitutioness01jesugoog`). Latin–English facing; translated from the Latin 1558, not the Spanish; the translator's preface is polemical, the translation itself plain. Only PD English found.
2. Ganss 1970 (Institute of Jesuit Sources) and Padberg 1996 — C.

### Minor foundational texts and Directories
DCO "Documentos" (8,850 words): *Cinco capítulos* (1539), *Fórmula del Instituto* 1540 (*Regimini militantis*) and 1550 (*Exposcit debitum*), plus the deliberations. DCO "Directorios" (6,100 words): the autograph Directory, notes dictated to Victoria and others. MHSI *Regulae Societatis Iesu (1540–1556)* (1948, `monumentaignatia04igna`) gathers the rules (many by Ignatius, some by others — treat as partly doubtful). All short; one combined book of "Documentos de fundación" is the natural unit. English: the Formulas are in the 1838 volume; no PD English of the Directories beyond what Longridge appends.

### Cartas e instrucciones
~6,800 items, 1524–1556, in Spanish, Italian and Latin. Most were written by secretaries in his name ("ex commissione"); MHSI marks those. Autograph or personally dictated letters are a few hundred. A complete import is a very large job; a selection by MHSI flags (autograph/own letters, the great instructions: on obedience 1553, to the fathers at Trent 1546, on perfection to Coimbra 1547) is the realistic first unit, with one letter per file.
Original-language sources:
1. **MHSI *Epistolae et instructiones*, Madrid 1903–1911, 12 vols** — A. On archive.org (Google scans, old OCR, fair; Latin headnotes and footnotes): vol. 1 `monumentaignati04ignagoog` / `monumentaignatia0022igna`; vol. 2 `monumentaignati05ignagoog`; vol. 3 `monumentaignati03ignagoog`; vol. 4 `monumentaignati00unkngoog`; vol. 5 `monumentaignati02ignagoog`; vol. 6 `monumentaignati01ignagoog`. Volumes 7–12 (1909–1911) were not found on archive.org; probably on HathiTrust/Google Books (unverified). ~250–300k OCR words per volume.
2. **Cartas de San Ignacio de Loyola, Madrid 1874–1889, 6 vols (ed. Antonio Cabré, Miguel Mir, Juan José de la Torre)** — A. archive.org `cartasdesanignac0001vari` … `0004vari`, `cartasdesanignac0000dela` (v.5), `cartasdesanignac0006john` (v.6); 164k–190k OCR words each, ~1.0 M total incl. notes and appendices. Spanish (Italian/Latin letters translated into Spanish), modernized spelling, good OCR. Easier to extract than MHSI, but incomplete and partly in translation.
English:
1. **D. F. O'Leary, ed. Alban Goodier SJ, *Letters and Instructions of St. Ignatius Loyola*, vol. I (1524–1547), Herder/Manresa Press 1914** — A. https://archive.org/details/LettersAndInstructionsOfStIgnatiusV1 (34,000 words, clean OCR). Only this first volume appeared.
2. Letters translated in *Woodstock Letters* (various issues, BC Jesuit Online Library) — B, scattered.
3. W. J. Young, *Letters of St. Ignatius of Loyola*, Loyola Univ. Press 1959 (archive.org, lending only) — unverified (renewal not checked; treat as C); Rahner, *Letters to Women* 1960 — C; Palmer–Padberg–McCarthy 2006 — C.

## Recommendation

Layout: `ignatius-opera-omnia/<work>` with `es-ES/` canonical (and `it/` for the Italian part of the Autobiography and the Italian letters, `la/` for the Vulgata and the Latin letters):
- `ignatius-opera-omnia/ejercicios-espirituales` (es-ES from DCO, verified against MHSI 1919; en-US Mullan, cross-checked with Morris)
- `ignatius-opera-omnia/exercitia-spiritualia-vulgata` (la from Roothaan 1881 / MHSI 1919)
- `ignatius-opera-omnia/autobiografia` (es-ES §1–79 from Wikisource; §79–101 Italian from MHSI 1904 — the work is bilingual in the original, so one book with mixed-language canonical chapters, or `it/` chapters for the tail; en-US O'Conor, cross-check Rix)
- `ignatius-opera-omnia/diario-espiritual` (es-ES DCO, checked against MHSI 1934; en-US Young 1958 after re-OCR)
- `ignatius-opera-omnia/constituciones` (es-ES DCO; en-US 1838, noting it translates the Latin)
- `ignatius-opera-omnia/documentos` (Cinco capítulos, Fórmulas, Directorios, Deliberaciones)
- `ignatius-opera-omnia/cartas` (selection first, then the full MHSI)

Import first: the *Ejercicios* (es-ES + Mullan) — the defining Catholic manual of prayer, clean on both sides. Then the Autobiography and the Diario.

Cannot be sourced cleanly: a PD English of the Diario other than Young 1958 (tier B, needs re-OCR); PD English of the letters beyond 1547; MHSI letter vols 7–12 as text online (not located); a clean (non-OCR) text of the Italian portion of the Autobiography and of the Vulgata — both need OCR cleanup from the MHSI/Roothaan scans.

Total size: works other than letters ≈ 230,000 words in the originals (Ejercicios 28k, Vulgata 30k, Autobiography 20k, Diario 18k, Constituciones 75k, Latin Constitutions 45k, documents/directories/rules ~40k); English available ≈ 190,000 words (Mullan 33k, O'Conor 26k, Young ~20k, 1838 Constitutions ~45k, O'Leary letters 34k, plus cross-check translations). Letters add ~2 M words (MHSI, estimated).
