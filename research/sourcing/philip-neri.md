# St. Philip Neri (1515–1595) — sourcing

Philip left almost no writings. Before he died he burned his papers. What survives:

1. **Letters**: about 26–30 authentic letters (to Francesco Vai, his nephews and nieces, Carlo Borromeo, Catherine de' Ricci, nuns at S. Vincenzo in Prato and others). The modern count, about 30, comes from Cistellini's critical edition (*San Filippo Neri, gli scritti e le massime*, 1996, tier C).
2. **Sonnets / poems**: 3 Italian sonnets preserved by Bacci; by 1902, two more and a fragment were known (Antrobus's note). One Latin poem is sometimes added.
3. **Maxims and sayings** (*Ricordi e detti*): not written by him but gathered from the canonization processes and the early lives (Gallonio 1600, Bacci 1622). The standard devotional arrangement gives one per day of the year (Turin, *Ricordi e detti di S. Filippo Neri*). Authentic in substance but transmitted by others, so mark them as a compilation.

The great Lives (Gallonio, Bacci, Capecelatro) are *about* him, not by him, and belong to hagiography if wanted at all.

Nothing by him is in the corpus.

## Letters

Original sources, best first:
1. **Alfonso Capecelatro, *La vita di S. Filippo Neri, libri tre*, Milano 1889 (also Napoli 1879), vol. I, appendix "Documenti", N. IV ("[Vedi pag. 344.]")**: https://archive.org/details/lavitadisfilippo01capeuoft, text at `…/lavitadisfilippo01capeuoft_djvu.txt` (lines ~18656–19990 of 168k words; letters I–XXVI+). Tier A (Capecelatro died 1912). Each letter carries an editorial argument, e.g. "I. A Messer Francesco Vai. — Prato. Esorta questo suo figliuolo spirituale a ritornarsene in Roma…". The OCR is fair, modern type, some errors ("n:)n", "l)adre"). It is the same sequence as the English in Antrobus.
2. Other copies of Capecelatro: `lavitadisfilipp00nerigoog`, `lavitadisfilipp01nerigoog` (Google scans).
3. Antonio Biscioni, *Lettere di santi e beati fiorentini* (Firenze 1736; repr. 1839): https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_6QBEAAAAYAAJ. It includes letters of Philip, a Florentine (I did not check the count). It is an independent witness for cross-checking. Tier A.
4. Cistellini (1996): tier C, for the canon only.

English, best first:
1. **Pietro Giacomo Bacci, *The Life of Saint Philip Neri*, ed. Frederick Ignatius Antrobus, London: Kegan Paul 1902, 2 vols, vol. II, "Letters of Saint Philip", pp. 355–409**: https://archive.org/details/lifesaintphilip00baccgoog (OCR 133k words for vol. II), also `LifeOfSaintPhilipNeriV2`, `lifeofsaintphili02bacc`. 26 letters headed "LETTER I … XXVI", each with an argument ("TO MESSER. FRANCESCO VAI, PRATO. He exhorts this his spiritual son…"). In the OCR the heading "LETTER XXII" is missing (it jumps XXI→XXIII), so check against the scan. Tier A (Antrobus died 1903; translators of the earlier 1847 Oratorian text are long dead). OCR good.
2. Capecelatro's Life in English, tr. Thomas Alder Pope (London 1882; 2nd ed. 1894), 2 vols: `a549672601capeuoft`, `a549672602capeuoft` (those djvu files hold only ~7k words, so the OCR is broken; use `bwb_T3-CBS-627_1`/`_2` or `capecelatro-the-life-of-saint-philip-neri-apostle-of-rome-v-1`). I did not confirm whether Pope translates the Documenti. Tier A (1882/1894).

## Sonnets

- Italian and English both in **Antrobus 1902 vol. II pp. 410–412**: two sonnets in Italian ("Se l'anima ha da Dio l'esser perfetto…", "Amo, e non posso non amarvi, quando…"), each followed by a verse translation by **H. I. D. Ryder**, Provost of the Birmingham Oratory (died 1907, tier A). The third sonnet, and the two found later, are mentioned in vol. I (OCR line ~1976: "three sonnets of his, composed in youth… Since Bacci's time two more sonnets have been found"). Their full texts are not located in a PD source, so they are unverified.
- Cross-check the Italian against Bacci's *Vita* (Italian, many editions: Roma 1622 `bub_gb_gzPavP7g2ioC`; 1745 `vitadisfilippone00bacc`; 1851 `vitadisfilippon00baccgoog`), all OCR.

## Maxims and Sayings

- **English: F. W. Faber, *The Maxims and Sayings of St Philip Neri*, 1847 (preface dated "St. Wilfrid's, Feast of St. Bridget, 1847"), transcribed on en.wikisource**: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/The_maxims_and_sayings_of_St_Philip_Neri. A single page, 10,564 words by the Wikisource search count, with maxims numbered by day under month headings (JANUARY 1–31 …). Tier A (1847, Faber died 1863). Clean. The Oxford Oratory sells a 2024 reprint, and Good Press an e-book, both of the same PD text.
- **Italian original**: the *Ricordi e detti di San Filippo Neri* (Turin), which Faber translated. **Not located online.** The sayings themselves are scattered through Bacci's *Vita* (Book I–II) and Giuseppe Crispino's *La scuola del gran maestro di spirito S. Filippo Neri nella quale co' fatti, e co' detti del medesimo…* (1675): https://archive.org/details/bub_gb_Y2ufeLONOyMC (OCR, long s). Reconstructing the Italian would mean matching each maxim to Bacci, which is research, not import.

## Portuguese

No Portuguese translation of the letters, sonnets or maxims found (pt.wikisource, dominiopublico, archive.org, web search). Devotional sites quote isolated maxims ("Estejam quietos, se puderem") without a source edition. pt-BR from our own translation (of the Italian for the letters and sonnets, of Faber's English for the maxims unless the Turin *Ricordi e detti* is found).

## Recommendation

- Layout: `philip-neri-opera-omnia/` with `lettere/` (one file per letter), `sonetti/` and `massime/` (by month). The whole author is under 25k words, so a single book `philip-neri-scritti` with three parts is also reasonable.
- **Import first: the *Maxims and Sayings*** from Wikisource (clean, tier A, English). The Italian original is not available; pt-BR would be translated from the English. Flag this exception to the original-language rule, or leave pt-BR out until the Turin *Ricordi e detti* is found.
- Then the **letters**: Italian from the Capecelatro 1889 "Documenti" (OCR cleanup), English from Antrobus 1902 (OCR cleanup). They are the same 26-letter sequence, so they align one to one.
- Sonnets: two with Italian and English from Antrobus. The others are unverified.
- **Total**: about 11k Italian words and about 25k English words (Faber maxims 10.6k, letters ~13k, sonnets <1k).
