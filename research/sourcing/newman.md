# St. John Henry Newman (1801–1890): sourcing report

Newman wrote in English, so the **original is the English text**: there is no separate translation column. Where the table says "cross-check", it means other witnesses of the same English text (another edition or transcription) to check transcription errors against. Newman died in 1890. Everything he published in his lifetime, and the posthumous editions up to 1930, is **tier A** as a text.

**Bibliography used:** Newman's own *uniform edition* (Longmans, 36 volumes, 1868–1881, reissued to the 1910s), as indexed by the Newman Reader (`https://www.newmanreader.org/works/index.html`, saved as `newman/nr-index.html`). It groups the works as *Anglican Period*, *Catholic Period* and *Miscellaneous Works*. I added the posthumous volumes and the letters. Newman was received into the Church on **9 Oct 1845**. Each row gives the period of composition (**ANG**/**CATH**, or **MIX** for collected volumes with material from both).

**How I measured:**
- Newman Reader: crawled all ~1,215 pages (`_scripts/crawl_count.py`; raw HTML in `newman/nr-html/`; counts in `newman/nr-counts.tsv`). The counts include a little per-page navigation text (about 1–3%).
- archive.org: counted the `_djvu.txt` full text (`_scripts/ia_count.py`; files in `newman/ia/`). "OCR q" is the share of lowercase tokens found in an English dictionary. 0.97 and up is clean, below 0.90 is poor. The archive.org figures include front matter and indexes.
- Gutenberg: counted between the START/END markers (`_scripts/pg_count.py`; files in `newman/pg/`).

## The main source and its copyright notice

**Newman Reader** (`newmanreader.org`, run by the National Institute for Newman Studies, NINS) is a hand-made HTML transcription of the Longmans uniform edition. It keeps the original page numbers, and every work names the impression it follows (e.g. *Development*: "Fourteenth impression, Longmans 1909"; *Apologia*: "Oxford Edition … Frowde 1913"; *Apologia (1865)*: "New impression, Longmans 1908"). The transcription is clean and the pages are regular, so it is easy to parse. The encoding is mixed: some pages are Latin-1 and some UTF-8, so decode per page.

**Every page ends with: "Newman Reader — Works of John Henry Newman Copyright © 2007 by The National Institute for Newman Studies. All rights reserved."** The underlying text is public domain: it transcribes 1868–1913 impressions, and Newman died in 1890. A plain transcription adds no copyrightable authorship in the US. But the site does claim rights in its pages, so I mark it **A\*** = *text public domain; host claims copyright in its transcription*.

The safe route is to treat Newman Reader as a convenience copy, and to verify or ground the import against a tier-A witness without any such claim: the Gutenberg text where one exists, otherwise the archive.org scans of the *same* Longmans impression. The page numbers in Newman Reader make that alignment easy. If the user prefers zero friction, the archive.org/Gutenberg texts alone suffice, but their OCR needs cleanup.

Other witnesses:
- **Project Gutenberg** (13 Newman texts): tier A, proofread, the cleanest public-domain digital text.
- **CCEL** (`ccel.org/ccel/newman`): *Callista*, *Dream of Gerontius*, *Apologia*, *Tracts for the Times*.
- **Wikisource**: only the "Tracts for the Times" index (6 subpages, about 20k words, a partial set of tracts), *Lead, Kindly Light* and *Anima Christi*.
- **archive.org**: scans of almost every Longmans volume, mostly University of Toronto copies (`…newmuoft`), with OCR quality 0.94–0.98.

## Notes per group

- **Parochial and Plain Sermons.** Eight volumes, 191 sermons. One sermon is one chapter. Newman Reader gives each sermon its own page (`sermonN.html`), which maps 1:1 onto corpus chapters. These are Anglican-period texts but Newman reissued them as a Catholic in 1868 (ed. W. J. Copeland) without changing the text. The "Catholic period" marking matters here: the user may want an `ANG` tag in the book description.
- **Catholic sermons** are *Discourses to Mixed Congregations*, *Occasions* and *Sermon Notes*. *Faith and Prejudice* was first published in 1956 by Sheed & Ward. Newman's words are public domain in the EU/UK (he died 1890). In the US the 1956 edition may carry a renewed copyright through 2051. I could not check the renewal, so it is **unverified**: do not import before checking.
- **Meditations and Devotions** is the most devotional work and the most natural fit for a prayer app. It has three parts: Month of May, Stations of the Cross/Litanies, and Meditations on Christian Doctrine with the *Hope in God—Creator* meditations. Each meditation is a short chapter.
- **Gerontius.** Gutenberg #48927 includes Egan's 1903 introduction and notes, which are public domain but are not Newman's. Use the poem from Newman Reader or CCEL, and use Gutenberg to check it.
- **Apologia.** There are two textual versions. The 1864 version is serial pamphlets with polemical Parts 1–2. The 1865 version is Newman's revised book, with the polemics cut and Notes A–G added. Import the **1865** text as the work; the Oxford 1913 edition has both if a variant is ever wanted.
- **Development.** The 1845 and 1878 texts differ substantially, because Newman rearranged the book as a Catholic. Newman Reader and Gutenberg carry 1878, the canonical one.
- **Letters.** The critical *Letters and Diaries* (32 vols) is **tier C** (OUP; NINS digital collection behind registration). Pre-1930 selections (Mozley 1891, Keble 1917, Ward 1912) are tier A but cover only parts of the life, and Mozley's covers only the Anglican years. A complete Newman letters corpus cannot be sourced cleanly.
- **Latin works.** Only *Dissertatiunculae* (1847) is Newman's own Latin, and Gutenberg has it proofread. *Hymni Ecclesiae* is a compilation of breviary hymns. The 1847 *Theses de fide* and the Newman–Perrone paper were first printed in the 20th century: not found online, tier unverified.

## Recommendation

- **Layout:** `newman-opera-omnia/<work>`, with `en-US` as canonical. Slugs like `parochial-plain-sermons-1` … `-8` (or one book with 8 parts), `meditations-and-devotions`, `dream-of-gerontius`, `apologia`, `grammar-of-assent`, `idea-of-a-university`, `development`, `loss-and-gain`, `callista`, `discourses-mixed-congregations`, `sermons-various-occasions`, `sermon-notes`, `difficulties-of-anglicans`, `letter-to-pusey`, `letter-to-duke-of-norfolk`, `present-position`, `dissertatiunculae` (with `la` as canonical).
- **Import first (Catholic, devotional):** *Meditations and Devotions*, *Dream of Gerontius*, *Discourses to Mixed Congregations*, *Sermons on Various Occasions*, *Apologia (1865)*, then *Parochial and Plain Sermons* (the most read), *Grammar*, *Idea*, *Development*, *Loss and Gain*, *Callista*, and *Letter to the Duke of Norfolk* (conscience).
- **Text pipeline:** parse Newman Reader for structure and use Gutenberg/archive.org as the tier-A ground truth. This matters because of the NINS "All rights reserved" footer. If the user is uneasy about that footer, rebuild from the archive.org Longmans scans; their OCR is 0.94–0.98 and the Newman Reader page numbers align with them.
- **Cannot be sourced cleanly:** the complete letters (*Letters and Diaries*, C), *Faith and Prejudice* (unverified US status), and the 20th-century first printings of Latin papers.
- **Already in the corpus:** Newman's Athanasius translations, in their NPNF revision (`church-fathers/athanasius/`), and the Oxford *Catena Aurea* he edited. Don't re-import them.

**Total size.** Newman Reader holds ≈5.6M words. Of that, Newman's own works are ≈4.9M once the editorial and compilation volumes (Athanasius translations ≈0.7M, prefaces, *Sayings*, *Addresses*) are removed. The Catholic-period works alone are ≈1.7M. The pre-1930 letters add ≈1.1M (estimate).

**Notes.**
- **No tier-A or tier-B Portuguese Newman exists in any source I could reach.** All Portuguese Newman is post-1960. The Brazilian editions are almost all 2016 or later: Ecclesiae, Cultor de Livros, Molokai, Veritatis Splendor and Edições Livre, a wave around the 2010 beatification and the 2019 canonisation. Portugal has its own translations: Verbo 1974, Assírio & Alvim 2005, Alêtheia 2010 and Morais 1962.
- The Verbo *Apologia* is the only Portuguese Newman with an online full text (archive.org OCR). It is a careful annotated translation (Mello Moser was a Lisbon anglicist), but it is European Portuguese, abridged in its apparatus, and tier C.

**Recommendation:** translate Newman to pt-BR from the English. As a reference, use the Verbo 1974 *Apologia* (OCR at archive.org) to check difficult passages, and Morão's *Gramática do assentimento* (2005) for Newman's technical vocabulary (*assentimento real/nocional*, *sentido ilativo*), which the Portuguese-speaking literature has settled on. Nothing is importable.
