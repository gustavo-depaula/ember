# Sourcing: complete works of the authors missing from the corpus

One report per author in this folder (`<author>.md`), written to `_brief.md`: every work, several ranked sources per work in the original, English and Portuguese, each with its copyright tier and evidence, size, structure and an import order. Raw downloads stay local (`.gitignore`).

Tiers: **A** public domain · **B** free in practice (freely distributed with no one claiming it, given away by its author or order, or US 1931–63 never renewed) · **C** sold or licensed, unusable.

## Overview

Words are the original-language text of the author's own works, measured or estimated in each report. "English" is how much of it has an A/B English translation.

## Decisions (2026-11-01)

1. **pt-BR is translated from the original language** (es, fr, it, la…), with the English as a reference, as the pipeline already does for Latin. It needs `t2.py`/`r2.py` to take `languages[0]` as the source. Translation waits until the imports are done.
2. **Critical editions of 1912–1963 are used with their editors' notes kept** (as footnotes), e.g. Silverio's John of the Cross and Teresa, Sala Balust's John of Ávila.
3. **Chesterton's 1931–36 books are imported** (St Thomas Aquinas, The Well and the Shallows, the Autobiography, The Scandal of Father Brown…): public domain in Brazil.
4. **`trent-catechism` keeps its Vozes 1951 pt-BR for now.** A translation of our own may come later as a separate book (the corpus has no linked-editions feature: a second translation is its own book id).
5. **Bridget of Sweden is imported** from the best Latin source.
6. **Where no usable English exists (Vincent de Paul…), the pipeline will also translate into English**, later.
7. **Medieval vernaculars get their own language folders:** `dum/` (Middle Dutch, Ruusbroec) and `gmh/` (Middle High German, Suso, Tauler).

8. **Works and editions.** A **work** is what the author wrote; an **edition** is one concrete text of it (one language, one source: an editor, a translator, or the author's own redaction). A **book** is one source edition plus the translations made from it, at most one per language: our pt-BR translations stay in the book of the text they were translated from (`pt-BR/` beside `es-ES/`), and an imported translation may stay there too when its chapters follow the source's (as Montfort's `en-US/` does). Every other edition of the same work is its own book, linked by a `work` field. `book.json` gains `"work": "<author>/<work>"` and `"editions": { "<lang>": { editor | translator, from?, year, sources } }`; the corpus build groups books by work; the reader and book page get an edition picker (look to be agreed with the user first). Switching edition does not keep the reading position: editions need not share chapters. Near-duplicates (`augustine-confessions` and `church-fathers/augustine/confessions`; the New Advent and opera omnia Summas; the Vozes `trent-catechism` pt-BR) move under shared works.

Order: build works/editions first, then import the first wave into it.

## Before anything is lost

Three tier-A/B sources survive only on the Wayback Machine and should be mirrored now: Bossuet (Abbaye de Port-Valais transcription), Mary Magdalene de' Pazzi (O.Carm. *Tutte le opere*, already saved in `mary-magdalene-de-pazzi/raw/`), and the Bari Franciscan sources.
