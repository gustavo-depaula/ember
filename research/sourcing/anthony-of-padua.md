# St. Anthony of Padua (of Lisbon) OFM, Doctor Evangelicus (c.1195–1231): sourcing report

**Bibliography.** By current criticism Anthony wrote one work, the *Sermones dominicales et festivi*, which he wrote down at Padua in 1229–31 as a homiletic textbook for the friars. He gave the series no titles. The parts are:
- **Sermones dominicales**: general prologue (*Prologus*), 53 Sunday sermons, an epilogue and 7 short prologues. They come with Anthony's own **Tabula**, an index of the scriptural "authorities" in each sermon. The critical edition splits the Tabula up and prints each piece at the head of its sermon.
- **Sermones mariani** (*in honorem et laudem beatissimae Mariae Virginis*): a prologue and 4 sermons (Nativity, Annunciation, Purification, Assumption).
- **Sermones festivi** (*in solemnitatibus sanctorum*): 20 sermons. Several early Lives say he wrote them in his last year, at the request of Cardinal Rinaldo of Ostia.

That canon comes from the critical edition: B. Costa, L. Frasson, I. Luisetto, P. Marangon, *S. Antonii Patavini… Sermones dominicales et festivi ad fidem codicum recogniti*, 3 vols, Padua: Edizioni Messaggero, 1979. It is restated, with a full survey of manuscripts, prints and spurious texts, by J. F. Meirinhos, "S. António de Lisboa, escritor. A tradição dos *Sermones*: manuscritos, edições e textos espúrios", *Mediaevalia* 11–12 (1997) 139–182 (open PDF, Univ. Porto: https://repositorio-aberto.up.pt/handle/10216/20086). Treccani's DBI entry agrees.

Printed history:
- Paris, Badius, 1520 (*Sermones dominicales*), plus two 1521 Badius volumes: the spurious *Quadragesimales* and *Sermones de Sanctis*.
- Venice, ed. Maffei, 1574/75.
- **de la Haye, *Opera omnia* (with Francis), Paris 1641**; reprinted Lyon 1653, Stadt am Hof (Pedeponti) and Augsburg 1739, and Paris 1880 (Horoy).
- Pagi, *Sermones festivi*, Avignon 1684 (reprinted 1734).
- Azzoguidi, *Sermones in Psalmos*, Bologna 1757 (spurious).
- Josa, Bologna/Padua 1883–85: some festive sermons and the Marian series.
- **Locatelli and continuators, 13 fascicles in 3 vols, Padua 1895–1913**: the first complete edition, from the Paduan manuscripts.
- Critical edition, 1979.

Not in the corpus: checked `_corpus-books.txt` and `content/books/`. Two neighbours are already there. `francis-of-assisi-opera-omnia/` presumably holds Francis's note *To Brother Anthony* (see `francis-of-assisi.md`; the *Sacrum commercium*, which two late manuscripts give to Anthony, is also covered there). `kempis-imitation-of-christ/` holds the *Imitatio*, which a few tradition sources once gave to Anthony.

## Notes per work, sources ranked

### Sermones dominicales et festivi (the authentic corpus)
Structure for the chapter split: one file per sermon, in the critical edition's order. That gives the Prologus, then Septuagesima through the 24th Sunday after Pentecost, Advent and Epiphany (53 Sunday sermons), with the 4 Marian sermons placed among them, then the 20 festive sermons (Nativity, Stephen, John, Innocents, Circumcision, Epiphany, Conversion of Paul, Purification, Chair of Peter, Ash Wednesday, Annunciation, Cena Domini, Easter, Philip and James, Finding of the Cross, Rogations, Ascension, Pentecost, John the Baptist, Peter and Paul). Each sermon has numbered paragraphs, headings by theme (*I – De…*), inline scriptural references, and ends with a closing prayer (*Rogemus ergo…*). These prayers are short and liturgically usable; they are worth flagging for the prayer side of the app.

Original, ranked:
1. **Documenta Catholica Omnia**, 79 Latin PDFs plus a theme index (`Sermo_079_Sermonum_Index_et_Themata`), listed at `http://www.documentacatholicaomnia.eu/03d/` (files `1195-1231,_Antonius_Patavinus,_Sermo_NNN_…,_LT.pdf`).
   - **Text**: the 1979 critical text, with its paragraph numbers, theme headings and biblical references in brackets. The PDF metadata names "Fr. Anthony Trimakas" as the maker (2005–06). The site states no licence.
   - **Size**: 365,961 words counted with `pdftotext`: Sundays 281,184, Marian 11,965, festive 70,181, index 2,631. A few files split the series oddly (e.g. `Sermo_033` has 201 words; it is the Marian prologue).
   - **Quality**: clean, digital-born, not OCR. Line breaks need rejoining and hyphenation needs fixing.
   - **Tier, honestly: B/C, a decision for the user.** By the brief's letter it is C, because Edizioni Messaggero still sells the 1979 edition. Against that: a critical edition of a public-domain work is protected in Italy for only 20 years from publication (L. 633/1941 art. 85-quater), so that right ended in 1999. In the US, a restored medieval text is not original authorship. The editors' apparatus is not in these PDFs; only paragraph numbers, headings and Bible references are. I have not had this legal reading confirmed.
   - **la.wikisource** has `Antonii Patavini Sermones` (source given as "Scribd", i.e. the same text). Only the Prologus and Sermons I–II are transcribed, so it is not a separate witness.
2. **Locatelli, *S. Antonii Pat. thaumaturgi incliti Sermones dominicales et in solemnitatibus*, Padua 1895–1913** (13 fascicles, 3 vols). Tier **A**: public-domain text, the first complete edition, from the Paduan MSS; Meirinhos calls its editorial method dated. Online I found only part of it:
   - BSB/MDZ fascicles: `bsb00107655` / `bsb11517983` (vol. 1, fasc. 1: XXIV + 71 pp.), `bsb11826894` (vol. 1, fasc. 2: pp. 73–168) and `bsb00107656` / `bsb11517984` (vol. 2: pp. 362–458).
   - Access: page images with MDZ hOCR per page (`https://api.digitale-sammlungen.de/ocr/<id>/<page>`). The scans are marked NoC-NC / CC BY-NC-SA, but the text itself is public domain.
   - Not found on archive.org or Google Books full view. HathiTrust probably has it, but its catalogue search was blocked (Cloudflare 403), so that is unverified.

   This is the public-domain fallback and the cross-check for the DCO text.
3. **de la Haye, *Opera omnia* S. Francisci… nec non S. Antonii Paduani, Paris 1641**:
   - **Copies**: BSB `bsb10634728` (1,048 scans, MDZ hOCR, fair 17th-c. OCR with long-s ſ/f confusions); Google Books (Complutense, UCM5319058342, via Europeana). The 1739 reprints are BSB `bsb10634730` (Pedeponti) and `bsb10813112` (Augsburg).
   - **Contents**: the sermon block runs pp. ~100–463. I spot-checked it: a passage on p. 105 matches *Dominica III de Adventu* of the critical text word for word. But the block also includes the spurious *Quadragesimale* and the spurious *de sanctis in communi* (pp. 417–463), so it is a mixed witness.
   - **Size**: ≈475k OCR words for the sermon block, an estimate from a 1-in-20 page sample at ~1,300 words per page, marginal notes included.
   - **Tier**: A.
4. **Horoy**: his Anthony tomus I (`mediiaevibiblio02bottgoog`) holds only the *Expositio in Psalmos* and the prolegomena. No later Anthony volume of Horoy was found online. Austrian National Library (ONB) and Europeana list the series only up to the Francis volume.

English:
- **Paul Spilsbury**, *The Sermons of St Antony of Padua*, translated from the 1979 critical edition. It is complete: Sundays, Marian and festive sermons, with notes.
- Formerly hosted at franciscan-archive.org (`/antonius/opera/ant-hdNN.html`, now 404). DCO keeps a 1,210-page print of those pages, `…_Antonius_Patavinus,_Sermones,_EN.pdf`: 511,803 words without the URL headers, notes included.
- Every page says: "The copyright in this translation belongs to the author, Revd Dr S.R.P. Spilsbury". It is now sold by Edizioni Messaggero as *Sermons for Sundays and Festivals* (4 vols, ISBN 9788825018790…). Tier **C**: unusable.
- No public-domain English translation of the sermons was found. I searched archive.org and the web, and checked Gilliat-Smith 1926, which is a Life with no sermon text. Older English "readings from the sermons" pages online are excerpts of Spilsbury.

### Sermones in Psalmos / Expositio in Psalmos (dubia → spurious)
These are 278 moral "sermons" on the Psalter, from a Bologna manuscript once revered as Anthony's autograph. Azzoguidi published them in 1757, with Sicco Polentone's *Vita* and the breviary legend.

A. Callebaut's work rejected the attribution, and the text is now given to Cardinal Jean Halgrin d'Abbeville († 1237). Sources: Meirinhos 1997 pp. 178; Treccani ("fortemente contestata… non pochi critici danno per certa l'Expositio all'Abbeville").

Sources:
1. **Horoy VI (Paris 1880)**, cols. 575–1263, archive.org `mediiaevibiblio02bottgoog`. The same volume is bound with Honorius V in `mediiaevibiblio09bottgoog`. 246,605 OCR words counted, Google scan, OCR fair to good, two columns with sermon headings (*SERMO I…*). Tier A.
2. **Azzoguidi, Bologna 1757**, 2 vols, HathiTrust full view `ucm.5319074461`, `ucm.5319074470` (record 009302815). Tier A, scans.

If imported, put it under a `dubia/` path with the attribution stated.

### Expositio mystica and Concordantiae morales (dubia → spurious)
**Expositio mystica in Sacram Scripturam**:
- de la Haye printed it first, in 1641 (pp. 464–656), from a now-lost Mirecourt MS. It is a Genesis-to-Apocalypse moral gloss, apparently compiled from the sermons via the Badius print, and includes a short Canticle commentary (pp. 580–584).
- Wadding, Azzoguidi and Azevedo already doubted it; Meirinhos and the 1979 introduction reject it.
- Size ≈180k OCR words, estimated from sampled BSB pages.

**Concordantiae morales SS. Bibliorum**:
- Wadding published it in Rome in 1624 ("nunc primum… ex mss. Bibliothecae Aracoeli") from a lost codex marked "sancti Antonii". de la Haye reprinted it (1641, pp. 657–792).
- It differs from the sermons; per Meirinhos and the 1979 introduction it is by a mid-13th-c. Franciscan.
- Size ≈120k OCR words, estimated.
- English: **J. M. Neale, 1856 (2nd ed. 1867)**, archive.org `moralconcordance00neal` (53,216 OCR words) and `MN5031ucmf_5` (microfilm). It is "translated, verified, and adapted to the English Church": Neale omits sections and corrects references. Tier A, but an Anglican adaptation, usable only as a labelled companion.

### Quadragesimale and the sermons De sanctis (spurious)
- The *Sermones quadragesimales et de tempore* (one sermon a day for Lent) are by Henri de Montjardin OFM (c.1350). Bartholomew of Pisa first gave them to Anthony, c.1385. Prints: Badius 1521, Maffei 1575, de la Haye 1641, Pergula 1649/50.
- The 36 *Sermones de sanctis in communi* are mostly by Pierre de Remis OP, per Schneyer. Prints: Badius 1521, HathiTrust `ucm.5322477580`; de la Haye pp. 417–463.
- Pagi's 1684 *Sermones festivi* add spurious *de sanctis in communi* and *de diversis* (pp. 400–462); not located online.
- All of these exist only as scans. They are inside the BSB 1641 OCR, which makes them extractable. Low priority, `dubia/` if at all.

## Recommendation

Layout: `anthony-of-padua-opera-omnia/` with `la/` canonical:
