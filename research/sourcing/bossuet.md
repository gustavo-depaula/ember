# Jacques-Bénigne Bossuet (1627–1704): sourcing report

Bibliography followed: the *Œuvres complètes de Bossuet*, ed. F. Lachat (Paris, Louis Vivès, 1862–66, **31 vols**), still the standard complete edition, whose volume contents are listed below (read from the title pages of the archive.org OCR and from the Abbaye Saint-Benoît transcription's index). It is checked against the earlier Versailles edition (Lebel, 1815–19, 43 vols) and corrected by the two critical editions that superseded Lachat for their genres: the **Œuvres oratoires**, ed. J. Lebarq (Desclée, 1890–97, 6 vols), revised by Ch. Urbain & E. Levesque (Desclée, 1914–26, **7 vols**), and the **Correspondance**, ed. Ch. Urbain & E. Levesque (Hachette, *Grands Écrivains de la France*, 1909–25, **15 vols**). Nothing by Bossuet is in the corpus.

Size: the Lachat OCR measures **8.15 M words** across the 31 volumes (`research/sourcing/bossuet/lachat_NN.txt`, measured), including Lachat's *Remarques historiques*, Latin texts (Bible commentaries, *Defensio declarationis*), letters *to* Bossuet and tables. Per-work figures below are **measured** where marked "m", otherwise estimated from the share of a Lachat volume (±15%).

## Sources per work, ranked

**French, best first**

1. **Abbaye Saint-Benoît de Port-Valais transcription of the Lachat edition** (clean HTML, typed, not OCR), published at `http://www.abbaye-saint-benoit.ch/bossuet/` and later at `https://www.bibliotheque-monastique.ch/bibliotheque/bibliotheque/bossuet/`. **Both sites are now dead.** The text survives only in the **Wayback Machine** (CDX: about 1,110 captured pages for the first site, 634 for the second, covering vols I–XXXI including the Latin XXI–XXII; the URL lists are saved in `research/sourcing/bossuet/asb_cdx.json` and `bm_cdx_pages.txt`, and the full index in `asb_index_links.txt`). Quality is **excellent**, with one systematic loss: the ligature **œ is dropped** (`cur` for `cœur`, `uvre`), so a dictionary repair pass is required. Scripture references are kept, and Lachat's *Remarques historiques* are separate pages. No licence is stated on the pages I read. The text is Lachat 1862–66 (tier A). Fetching is slow: Wayback throttles at about 20 pages before refusing, so a polite crawl is needed (the measured samples in `asb_pages/`).
2. **Œuvres oratoires, critical ed. Lebarq rev. Urbain & Levesque** (1914–26, 7 vols; archive.org `uvresoratoiresd01boss` … `uvresoratoiresd07boss`, about 1.7–2.2 MB of text per volume; the 1890–97 first edition is `oeuvresoratoires01boss` … `05boss`, plus `oeuvresoratoires00bossuoft`). This is the **best text for the sermons, panegyrics and funeral orations** (manuscript-based, chronological, with variants), and it corrects Lachat's interpolations. Tier A (published ≤1930). The OCR is single-column with good quality.
3. **Correspondance, ed. Urbain & Levesque** (Hachette GEF 1909–25, 15 vols; archive.org `correspondancede01boss` … `correspondanced15boss`, two scan series). This is the **best text for the letters** (dated, annotated). Tier A.
4. **Lachat OCR on archive.org** (`uvrescompletesde0001boss` … `0031boss`; vols 10 & 11 from the Google copies `oeuvrescompltes08lachgoog` and `oeuvrescompltes17lachgoog`). It is complete, with **fair** OCR (two columns, decorative headbands render as junk lines `LULU UV UV`). Use it as the page-image witness for checking the ASB typing.
5. **Wikisource**: *Discours sur l'histoire universelle* (1681 ed., proofread, *Texte entier* 129.8k words), the *Oraisons funèbres* (6 great ones + Monterby, validated), djvu of the *Politique* (1709) and of Lachat t. 7 (1859 reprint). These are the cleanest texts where they exist.
6. **Versailles edition 1815–19** (43 vols; archive.org `oeuvresrevuessur01bossuoft` … `43bossuoft`, complete) is a second complete witness. Also Gutenberg FR #77228 (*Concupiscence*) and #77452 (*Oraisons funèbres*).

**English, best first**

1. **Tier A complete translations**: *History of the Variations* (anon., Dublin: Coyne 1836; Kenedy reissues 1885/1902, liberius.net 394/395); *Exposition of the Doctrine* (1686; New York 1829); *Universal History* (Elphinston 1767/1778; 1821); *Conference with Claude* (1687; 1842); *Treatise of Communion under Both Kinds* (1685/87); *Maxims and Reflections upon Plays* (1699); *History of France* (1762).

## Notes per work

- **Élévations**: 25 *semaines*, each of numbered *élévations* (posthumous; Bossuet's nephew printed it in 1727). The ASB pages split one page per week, the natural chapter unit.
- **Méditations sur l'Évangile**: *Le Sermon de Notre-Seigneur sur la montagne* (journées), *La dernière semaine du Sauveur* (journées I–XCVII), *Jérémie et Jonas…*, then *La Cène* (parts 1–2, journées). Use one chapter per journée, or per block of journées.
- **Sermons**: Lachat orders them by the liturgical year; the critical *Œuvres oratoires* orders them chronologically. Choose the critical text and keep a liturgical index (feast → sermon), because a sermon-of-the-day surface fits Ember.
- **Lachat interpolations**: Lachat sometimes printed the "Deforis" reconstructions of sermons; Lebarq removed them, which is another reason to prefer the critical edition for oratory.
- **Gallicanism**: the *Defensio declarationis cleri gallicani* defends the 1682 Four Articles, later repudiated, so if it is imported, present it with a historical note (low priority anyway).
- **Quietism**: Bossuet's side of the Fénelon controversy. Pair it with `fenelon-opera-omnia/quietisme` if it is ever imported.

## Recommendation

- Layout `bossuet-opera-omnia/<group>/<work>`: `spiritualite/` (elevations-sur-les-mysteres, meditations-sur-l-evangile, traite-de-la-concupiscence, opuscules, lettres-de-direction, catechismes-de-meaux, prieres-ecclesiastiques), `oratoire/` (oraisons-funebres, sermons, panegyriques), `doctrine/` (exposition, connaissance-de-dieu, histoire-universelle, politique), `controverse/` (variations, avertissements, communion-deux-especes, conference-claude, defense-de-la-tradition, projet-de-reunion), `ecriture/`, `quietisme/`, `gallicanisme/`, `dauphin/` (histoire-de-france, logique, libre-arbitre…), `correspondance/`.
- **Base texts**: ASB HTML (Wayback) for everything except oratory and letters, with the œ repaired and checked against the Lachat OCR images; *Œuvres oratoires* 1914–26 for sermons, panegyrics and funeral orations (with Wikisource for the 6 great orations); Urbain-Levesque for the correspondence; Wikisource for the DHU.
- **First batch**: Élévations, Méditations, Concupiscence and Opuscules: about 0.5M words of French with almost no English except the 1962 Franchère selections (B) and the 1900 *Sermon on the Mount* (A), so most of the `en-US` would come from the pipeline.
- **Cannot be sourced cleanly**: no PD English for the *Élévations*, *Concupiscence*, *Connaissance de Dieu*, the letters of direction, most sermons, or any Quietism or Latin work. The best clean French (ASB) is **Wayback-only**, so mirror it early into `research/sourcing/bossuet/` (polite crawl) before the captures become unreachable.

**Total size**: Lachat OCR **8.15 M words** (measured). Bossuet's own text, net of Lachat apparatus (about 8%), letters to Bossuet (about 0.4M) and tables, comes to about **7.2 M words**, of which about 1.0M is Latin. Usable English is about **1.3 M words** (tier A/B, mostly controversy and history; devotional English is scarce).

## Portuguese

Bossuet is very thin in Portuguese and almost undigitized. The only Portuguese Bossuet I could open is the *Discurso sobre a historia universal*, and that copy holds only Part II. Inocêncio's *Diccionario bibliographico portuguez* (XVI, 113, as quoted by the Livraria Castro e Silva listing) records Portuguese editions of the *Discurso* in 1772 and 1830, besides the Coimbra 1790 printing. I found no Portuguese edition, old or modern, of the *Élévations*, *Méditations sur l'Évangile*, *Traité de la concupiscence*, the spiritual *Opuscules*, the letters of direction, the Meaux catechisms, the funeral orations, the sermons, the *Exposition*, *Connaissance de Dieu*, *Politique* or the *Variations*.

Where I searched: archive.org (advancedsearch by creator/title, `language:por`, and Portuguese title words for each major work); Europeana API (BNP, BSB/MDZ, Gallica, Coimbra and the rest, `proxy_dc_language:por AND who:bossuet` → 1 hit); Biblioteca Nacional Digital (Portugal); Alma Mater (Universidade de Coimbra; `bossuet` → 1 item); Brasiliana Guita e José Mindlin (USP); pt.wikisource full-text search (only literary mentions); Open Library (`language=por` → 0). **Not reachable**: dominiopublico.gov.br, BN Digital Brasil and HathiTrust (Cloudflare 403), Gallica direct (403), Google Books (API quota exhausted). The web-search budget also ran out, so the Catholic sites (Permanência, Obras Católicas, Montfort) and the publishers' catalogues (Vozes, Paulus, Ecclesiae, Cultor de Livros, Cristo Rei) were **not checked**. Every "none found" below is therefore "none found in the catalogues above", not proof of absence. ## Mirror

Both Port-Valais sites are mirrored from the Wayback Machine in `research/sourcing/bossuet/mirror-port-valais/` (gitignored), with the original paths kept under one folder per host. Each folder holds a `manifest.tsv` (url, timestamp, bytes, sha256) and the `cdx.tsv` listing. The crawl script is `_scripts/mirror/wayback_mirror.py`. From each URL's 200 captures the largest was kept. Images were skipped, because they are only FrontPage buttons.
