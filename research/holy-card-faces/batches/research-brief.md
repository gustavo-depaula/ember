# Research brief for a batch

The instructions a research agent gets for batch N (the saints are in the table in [README.md](README.md)). The agent writes only `dossiers/batch-N.md` and `batches/batch-N.json`, plus source copies under `consult/batch-N/` (gitignored; never /tmp). It does not generate images, commit, or start helper agents, and finishes both outputs in one run.

Read first: `../README.md` (method and lessons), `README.md` (pipeline and schema), `../dossiers/first-batch.md` (dossier style). Look at the cards in `content/saints/` and at the `face` lines of the other `batches/*.json`, so faces repeat neither existing cards nor other batches.

For each saint:

1. **Face.** Research with WebSearch/WebFetch: photographs or portraits from life where they exist, contemporary descriptions, else iconographic tradition (Dionysius of Fourna's Painter's Manual via Didron's French edition on archive.org, christianiconography.info, the Golden Legend, early mosaics and paintings on Wikimedia Commons). Write one face line: age, then **bone structure** (face shape — long, round, square, heart, triangular; nose; eyes; brows; cheekbones; jaw; mouth), then hair, beard and gaze. Hair and beard alone are not a face: vary the structure deliberately across the batch and against existing cards. Idealise: keep distinguishing marks, never unflattering details. Avoid the stock faces (a thirties man with brown curls and a short beard; a white-bearded elder; a beardless youth) unless the sources demand them, and avoid a Christ-like young long-haired man with a pointed beard. People of Africa, Asia and the Americas look authentically so, in period dress. On a multi-figure card, describe each figure and how they differ.
2. **Subject.** Dress, attributes, pose (half-length like the existing cards; multi-figure cards side by side), and a real background place. Modest and devotional: no gore, no text or lettering besides the initial.
3. **Refs.** Three unedited original cards of the most similar kind (for technique only): agnes aloysius_gonzaga alphonsus_liguori ambrose anne anthony_padua athanasius augustine benedict bernard_clairvaux bonaventure bridget_sweden catherine_siena cecilia charles_borromeo clare_assisi dominic elizabeth_hungary francis_assisi francis_xavier george gregory_great ignatius_loyola jerome john_of_the_cross lucy margaret_mary mary_magdalene monica moses_the_black nicholas patrick philip_neri philomena rose_lima sebastian teresa therese thomas_aquinas vincent_de_paul (multi-figure: include philip_james).
4. **Name and patron.** en-US "St. X" / pt-BR as used in Brazil ("São/Santa/Santo"; plural "Sts."/"Santos"). `patronOf` is short ("Patron of youth" / "Padroeiro da juventude") or a role ("Bishop and martyr" / "Bispo e mártir").
5. **Excerpt.** One short line in en-US and pt-BR, verified, never recalled from memory. Preferred: verbatim from the feast's formulary in `content/of/formularies/sanctoral/MM-DD.json` (or its subfolder; antiphons, a self-contained phrase of the collect, the gospel acclamation verse), else the matching Common. Or a short saying of the saint from a public-domain source (ancient or pre-1930), cited, with a faithful rendering. Saints who died after about 1930: formulary text only (copyright). Check verbatim-ness with a script; record provenance in `excerptSource`.
6. **Feast** from `content/of-data/calendar/sanctorale`.
7. **catalogMatch**: a substring matching exactly one unticked line of `docs/plans/holy-cards-catalog.md` (check it).
8. **basis** (one or two sentences, our words) and 2–4 `sources` ({title, url}), preferring pictures actually consulted.

Validate the JSON against the schema in `README.md` ("batch": N, "date", "dossier": "dossiers/batch-N.md"). End the dossier with the look-alike risks that remain, and the final message with a brief summary and doubts.

## Cards that aren't a single saint

Same outputs and schema, with these differences. The card's `id` comes from the batch table in `README.md`.

- **Initial.** The first letter of the English name, skipping "The" and "St."; titles of Our Lady take **M** (as on `fatima`).
- **Name and patron line.** `name` as the catalog line has it, tidied to en-US / pt-BR usage. `patronOf` becomes a short caption of what the card shows, as on the existing feast cards (`epiphany`: "Christ revealed to the nations" / "Cristo revelado às nações"; a Rosary mystery: "The first Sorrowful Mystery" / "O primeiro mistério doloroso").
- **Feast.** The fixed date. A moveable feast, a season, a part of the Mass or an object has none: omit `feast` and say so in the dossier (the app needs `feast` to be optional before those cards ship; see `README.md`).
- **Scenes** (feasts, second feasts of saints, Rosary mysteries, Marian titles). `subject` is the Gospel or traditional scene, composed for the arched window, figures half-length or small in a landscape. `face` describes each principal figure. **Christ and Our Lady keep one face across the set:** before writing theirs, read `recurring-figures.md` and use its lines; if a figure isn't there yet, look at the cards that show it (`annunciation`, `visitation`, `nativity_christ`, `nativity_bvm`, `assumption`, `fatima`, `transfiguration`, `epiphany`, `holy_family` once made), write one line, and add it to `recurring-figures.md` (the one file besides the outputs that a research agent may edit). A Marian title shows its own image faithfully (Guadalupe's tilma, Aparecida's small dark terracotta statue in its mantle and crown, Our Lady of Sorrows' seven swords, …) with no lettering.
- **Pictorial Lives saints and feasts.** Everything as for a saint, except: `feast` is the book's date (the day key in `content/practices/saint-of-the-day/data/saint-of-day-index.json`); `name` follows the book's en-US and pt-BR names, tidied ("St. Maud"); the excerpt is one verbatim sentence (or self-contained clause) of that day's `reflection` in both languages, from the index; `excerptSource` names the day key. The book is public domain.
- **Seasons, Ember Days, parts of the Mass, objects and vestments.** `face` is `-`; add `"frame"` and, for seasons, `"box"`, copied from the variants in `README.md` (the season's liturgical colour: violet, rose, white, green; red has no season). `subject` paints the moment or the object, no lettering; figures small and serene (a priest seen from behind or in profile, servers, a congregation). Excerpt: for a part of the Mass, its own words from `content/of/order/order-of-mass.json`; for an object, the words of the Mass in which it is used, or Scripture; for a season, an antiphon or a phrase of the collect of its first Sunday. `refs`: `exaltation_cross annunciation thomas_aquinas` unless a closer card exists.
- **catalogMatch on an inline line** (Rosary mysteries, seasons, parts of the Mass, objects share a line): the item's text right after its `[ ] `, e.g. `"The Scourging at the Pillar"`, `"Advent — Sunday"`.
