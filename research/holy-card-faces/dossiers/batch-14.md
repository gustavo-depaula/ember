# Batch 14: the last calendar saints and feasts, the first feasts of the Lord

Damasus, John of Kanty, Aparecida, the Conversion of St. Paul, the Chair of St. Peter, the Beheading of St. John the Baptist, the Circumcision, the Holy Name of Jesus, the Presentation of the Lord, the Holy Relics. Eight are scenes; Our Lady, the Christ Child and St. Joseph now have lines in `batches/recurring-figures.md`, taken from `annunciation`, `nativity_christ` and `epiphany`. Excerpts were checked with `consult/batch-14/verify.py` (verbatim unless noted as recast).

## Damasus

Face: a Roman of Spanish stock, about seventy-five, a broad-browed rectangular face, a long straight nose with a broad bridge, wide-set dark eyes under level heavy brows, flat cheekbones, a square jaw, short grey hair, a short rounded grey-and-white beard.

- The Lund Gospels miniature (Uppsala Cod. C 83, c. 1150; `consult/batch-14/damasus_Pope_Dam.jpg`), which I checked: Jerome presents the Vulgate to Damasus; he has short grey hair, a short rounded grey beard and moustache, a long nose, wide-set eyes, a red chasuble, a pallium with small crosses and a low white conical cap. No likeness from life exists.
- Wikipedia and the formulary's description: born about 305 (Rome or Hispania, family from Lusitania), pope 366–384, promoted the cult of the martyrs and adorned their tombs with verses, set Jerome to revise the Latin Bible. Hence San Callisto and the codex. The card keeps him in 4th-century dress, without a tiara; any marble slab is blank, since his epigrams would be lettering.
- Excerpt: 12-11 collect, opening clause.

## John of Kanty

Face: about seventy-five, a long, narrow, sculpted face, very high bald forehead and crown with a wreath of short grey-white hair, prominent high cheekbones, straight nose, heavy-lidded half-closed grey eyes, ruddy, full prominent lips, a short close grey beard.

- Sakralne Dziedzictwo Małopolski (the Kraków archdiocesan inventory; pages saved in `consult/batch-14/`): "starszy, łysiejący mężczyzna z wianuszkiem siwych włosów, o takim samym krótkim zaroście. Ma wysokie, łyse czoło, rumianą twarz z prostym nosem i wydatnymi ustami"; another portrait: "pociągła twarz o wyrazistych rysach, półprzymkniętych oczach, wydatnych kościach policzkowych i krótkim, siwym zaroście … w togę doktorską o czerwonym podszyciu". The face line follows these two descriptions closely.
- Wikipedia: 1390–1473, professor at the Kraków Academy until his death on Christmas Eve, buried in St. Anne's; gave his clothes to the poor. Hence the Collegium Maius courtyard in snow and the cloak given to a student.
- Excerpt: 12-23 collect, "by showing compassion to all, may gain forgiveness in your sight" / "praticando a misericórdia para com todos, alcançar o vosso perdão", recast as a wish in both languages.

## Aparecida

The face is the image itself: the statue's small, round, dark cinnamon-brown terracotta face, eyes lowered, joined hands, in the triangular blue gold-embroidered mantle and jewelled gold crown. Three distinct caboclo fishermen: Domingos (fifty, square, grey stubble, straw hat), João (twenty-five, long lean face, thin moustache, the net), Filipe (forty, round face, short black beard).

- pt.wikipedia: 36 cm terracotta Immaculate Conception; "A cor de canela que apresenta hoje deve-se à exposição secular à fuligem produzida pelas chamas das velas"; mantle and crown given by Princess Isabel in 1888; found in 1717 in the Paraíba do Sul by Domingos Garcia, João Alves and Filipe Pedroso, the body first and the head at the next cast, then the great catch.
- The card shows the image as venerated (mantle and crown), not as found, over the canoe and net at dawn, as the task asked. The real mantle carries small flags; the subject forbids them because of their lettering.
- Excerpt: the Brazilian formulary (`10-12/brazil.json`) is pt-BR only. Its entrance antiphon is Isaiah 61:10; the en-US is the opening clause of the same verse as the entrance antiphon of the Immaculate Conception (12-08), the feast she represents. The two translations differ in wording ("Com grande alegria rejubilo-me no Senhor" / "I rejoice heartily in the Lord") but are the same verse and meaning.
- Letter M, as on `fatima`. Feast 12 Oct from `of-data/calendar/sanctorale/10-12/brazil.json` (solemnity in Brazil).

## The Conversion of St. Paul

Paul keeps the `paul` card's face (bald crown, dark wavy hair at the sides, long dark pointed beard, straight fine nose), a little younger. The scene follows Acts 9:3-7 and the Western fall from the horse (Caravaggio, Santa Maria del Popolo): struck to the ground, shielding his eyes from the light, the companions shrinking back, Damascus and Mount Hermon beyond. The sword at his belt links the persecutor to his later emblem.

- Excerpt: 01-25 communion antiphon, whole (the `paul` card already uses 2 Timothy 4:7).

## The Chair of St. Peter

Peter keeps the `peter` card's face and dress (blue tunic, golden-ochre mantle). Enthroned frontally on the ivory-panelled chair, teaching gesture, keys and open codex, before Bernini's glory and the dove window in the apse of St. Peter's.

- Excerpt: 02-22 communion antiphon, Christ's sentence (the `peter` card uses John 21:17).

## The Beheading of St. John the Baptist

John keeps the `john_baptist` card's face (long face, curly shoulder-length dark-brown hair, short beard), made gaunter by prison. The prison scene at Machaerus, which Josephus names, with the Dead Sea through the bars; the sword is only a shadow and a gleaming hilt in the doorway. No charger, no head, no blood.

- Excerpt: 08-29 entrance antiphon (Ps 119:46-47), first clause; the `john_baptist` card already uses "He must increase", the communion antiphon here.

## The Circumcision of Our Lord

A Pictorial Lives feast (1 Jan, index key 01-01). Luke 2:21 says only that the Child was circumcised and named Jesus on the eighth day. The card paints the naming in a Bethlehem house, with nothing of the rite shown: Mary holding the Infant, an elder raising his hand over the Child, Joseph bowing; a silver bowl and a lamp on the table. The elder has a round face and short grey beard, to stay apart from Simeon on `presentation_lord`. Art often sets the scene in the Temple (Mantegna, Bellini); the house keeps it apart from the Presentation.

- Excerpt: the book's 01-01 reflection, "May this year be one of fervor and of progress!" / "Seja este ano um ano de fervor e de progresso!", verbatim.

## The Most Holy Name of Jesus

The Name cannot be written on the card (no lettering), so the card shows the Child, about three, blessing in an empty sunburst, adored by two angels and small kneeling faithful above Bethlehem: the "every knee should bend" of Philippians 2:10, after Gaulli's Triumph of the Name of Jesus in the Gesù.

- Letter M, by the brief's rule (first word after "The" is "Most"). If an H reads better, the name would become "The Holy Name of Jesus"; left to review.
- Excerpt: 01-03 entrance antiphon, opening clause.

## The Presentation of the Lord

Luke 2:22-38: Simeon holding the Child with veiled hands, Mary and Joseph (with the two turtledoves), Anna the prophetess of eighty-four, in the Herodian Temple. Simeon: a long, narrow face, hooked nose, hollow cheeks, long white beard in two locks, as tradition paints him; the unavoidable white-bearded elder, distinguished by structure. Anna: a small heart-shaped face under a dark veil. The same card serves the fourth Joyful Mystery (catalog line: "The Presentation — shares the Presentation of the Lord"); `catalogMatch` includes the date to stay unique.

- Excerpt: 02-02 communion antiphon, opening clause.

## The Holy Relics

A Pictorial Lives feast (8 Nov). The index has no reflection for 11-08; the excerpt is the relative clause of the chapter's first paragraph ("the bodies of the martyrs and other Saints, who were living members of Jesus Christ and temples of the Holy Ghost"), made a sentence in both languages. Name tidied from "The Feast of the Holy Relics" to "The Holy Relics", letter H.

- The scene is a real rite: the Pilgrimage of the Relics (Heiligdomsvaart) at Maastricht, where the reliquary chest of St. Servatius, the Noodkist, is carried in procession (a 1962 photo on Wikipedia shows it under a canopy) and then set on the choir steps of the Basilica of St. Servatius. Bearers, servers and a priest are small and differ in age and build; the shrine is the subject.

## Look-alike risks that remain

- Damasus and Peter, Apollinaris, Sylvester, Clement I: short-bearded grey popes and bishops. The rectangular face, straight short hair and rounded close beard (not curly, not square) and the red paenula with the crossed pallium should hold; reject a tiara.
- John of Kanty and Bernardine of Siena, Bede, Paul VI: bald old clerics. The short grey beard, ruddy cheeks, full lips and the red-lined doctor's gown separate him.
- Conversion of Paul, Chair of Peter, Beheading: they must match `paul`, `peter`, `john_baptist`; on the face sheet compare with those cards, not against them. Paul on the ground may lose his bald crown to foreshortening; redo if he reads as a generic bearded man.
- John the Baptist is already Christ-like on his card; the prison gauntness and wild hair must not drift further toward Christ.
- Circumcision, Presentation, Holy Name: the Child, Mary and Joseph must look like `nativity_christ` and `epiphany`; the Circumcision elder must not become Simeon.
- Aparecida: the model may paint a living dark-skinned Virgin instead of the small statue, or add the flags; reject either. The fishermen must be colonial Brazilians, not Galilean apostles.
- Holy Relics: the Noodkist may come out as a generic Gothic shrine; acceptable if it is a gilded house-shaped chest.
