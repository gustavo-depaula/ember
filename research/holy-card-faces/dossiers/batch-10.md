# Batch 10 of new cards: faces

Faces, dress and settings for ten saints: Clement I, John Damascene, Peter Canisius, Thomas Becket, Sylvester I, Pancras, Callistus I, Martin I, Hilary and Peter Damian. The card data is in `../batches/batch-10.json`. As in `first-batch.md`, each "Face:" line uses only marks with a source quoted below it, and says so where a mark is an artistic choice. Pictures and texts consulted are in `../consult/batch-10/` (Wikipedia extracts `wp-*.txt`, lead images `lead-*.jpg`, web notes `notes-web.txt`).

Excerpts: nine come from the collect of the feast's formulary; Pancras has a calendar entry (05-12/pancras) but no formulary, so his card takes the Common of Martyrs in Easter Time for one martyr. Every line was checked verbatim against the JSON with a script (the verbatim parts and the small recasts are named in `excerptSource`).

## The four popes

The brief asks that Clement, Sylvester, Callistus and Martin differ from each other and from the existing popes. The existing ones: Leo (heavy, round, clean-shaven, 60), Gregory (bald crown, curled reddish-gold beard, 55), Pius X, Linus (hexagonal, dark greying hair, rounded salt-and-pepper beard, 50), Cletus (narrow, bony, 70, long forked silver beard), Alexander (clean-shaven oval, 30), Fabian (lean, long, clean-shaven, 65, tiara), John XXIII, Paul VI, John Paul II. All four new popes are dressed as early bishops of Rome: bare-headed, a plain bell chasuble and a narrow white wool pallium, no tiara, in four different colours.

## Clement I

Face: a man of about sixty with a diamond-shaped face, a high-bridged aquiline nose, deep-set dark eyes under straight black brows, a thin firm mouth; close-cropped black hair receding and greying; a short black beard following the jaw, grizzled at the chin.

- Christian Iconography: his attributes are "Anchor" and "Papal tiara"; in the apse mosaic of San Clemente "St. Clement holds the anchor that is his attribute" (local `notes-web.txt`).
- Wikipedia: he appears in the pallium, with an anchor at his side or tied to his neck, and often a palm; the apocryphal account has him "tied to an anchor and thrown from a boat into the Black Sea" under Trajan.
- No likeness survives, and I found no fixed facial type. The face is an artistic choice, made Roman (the formulary: "the third after Peter to govern the Roman Church") and set apart from the other popes by structure and the short dark beard. The tiara is left out, per the batch instruction.
- Background: Ostia, the harbour of Rome, suits the anchor without showing the drowning.
- Excerpt: 11-23.json collect, the relative clause "bore out by his witness what he celebrated in mystery and confirmed by example what he preached with his lips".

## John Damascene

Face: a Levantine man of about sixty-five, olive complexion, a long narrow face, a long thin high-bridged nose, heavy-lidded almond dark eyes, lean cheeks; a long wavy iron-grey beard; the gaze lowered to the scroll.

- The Athonite fresco (local `lead-John_of_Damascus.jpg`), which I checked: a long, lean face, a long, wavy grey-white beard, a white head-cloth crossed with black stripes, a purple mantle over an ochre tunic, writing with a reed pen on a long scroll. The card keeps all of it, with the scroll blank.
- Wikipedia: born in Damascus to an Arab Christian family, a monk and priest at Mar Saba near Jerusalem (local `wp-John_of_Damascus.txt`). The beard is iron-grey rather than white to keep him from the white-bearded elders (Anthony, Polycarp, Hilary in this batch).
- The small icon of the Mother of God recalls his defence of the holy images, the subject of his best-known treatises.
- Excerpt: 12-04.json collect, final clause, as a wish.

## Peter Canisius

Face: a man of about sixty-five with an oblong face broadening at the jaw, a long straight nose with a fleshy tip, hooded pale-brown eyes, full lower cheeks; short grey hair combed forward; a short close grey beard.

- The traditional portrait (local `lead-Peter_Canisius.jpg`), which I checked: an elderly Jesuit, grey hair combed forward over a broad forehead, heavy-lidded eyes, a long nose, full lower face with a short grey beard, black cassock with a narrow white collar. The card follows it.
- Wikipedia: 1521–1597, died at Fribourg; his catechisms were his most widely read work (local `wp-Peter_Canisius.txt`), hence the small thick book.
- Excerpt: 12-21.json collect, "those who seek the truth may joyfully find you, their God" (en-US recast as a wish; pt-BR joins "concedei" to its object).

## Thomas Becket

Face: a tall, spare man of about fifty, pale, with a long, narrow, well-formed face, a long fine nose, bright dark eyes under straight dark brows, a firm narrow chin; clean-shaven; dark-brown hair touched with grey.

- William FitzStephen, his clerk, describes him as slender, unusually tall, pale, with dark hair, a long nose and well-formed features, flushing when moved (search results and Wikipedia; local `notes-web.txt`).
- The British Library miniature, Cotton MS Claudius B II (local `lead-Thomas_Becket.jpg`), which I checked: clean-shaven, mitre, red chasuble, pale face. The card keeps these.
- Age: born c. 1120, killed 29 December 1170.
- Per the batch instruction, no violence: an archbishop with pallium, crozier and book before the Norman cathedral of Canterbury (the Gothic choir came after 1174).
- Excerpt: 12-29.json collect, final clause.

## Sylvester I

Face: a man of about sixty-five with a long, narrow face tapering to a small chin, very large almond dark eyes under high arched brows, a long thin straight nose, a small mouth and chin; white hair in a clerical ring; a short pointed white beard.

- The fresco in Santa Maria Antiqua, 757–767 (local `lead-Pope_Sylvester_I.jpg`), which I checked: frontal, a long face, large eyes, a long straight nose, a tonsure, a short dark pointed beard, a dark-red chasuble and a white pallium, holding a jewelled book. The card follows the face and the book, aged and whitened for the end of a reign of 314–335.
- Wikipedia: very little is known of his life; the legends give him a dragon and the conversion of Constantine (local `wp-Pope_Sylvester_I.txt`). The card uses neither. The Acts of Sylvester (Wikipedia, a Roman compilation of about 460) have him found "hiding on Mt Soracte to the north of Rome" during the persecution, and a monastery of San Silvestro stood there by the eighth century; the card uses the mountain as a quiet background.
- Excerpt: 12-31.json collect, final clause (pt-BR's subject is "o vosso povo", hence the singular).

## Pancras

Face: a boy of fourteen with a round face still full with childhood, a short straight nose, large round dark-brown eyes under straight thick brows, a small dimpled chin; olive complexion; short tight dark curls; a candid gaze lifted a little.

- Wikipedia: a Roman citizen from near Synnada in Phrygia, orphaned, brought to Rome, beheaded at fourteen around 304; buried on the Via Aurelia; "in medieval iconography, Pancras was depicted as a young soldier" (local `wp-Pancras_of_Rome.txt`). The soldier type is not followed.
- The statue in Barcelona Cathedral (local `lead-Pancras_of_Rome.jpg`), which I checked: a curly-haired boy in a green tunic and red cloak, with a book and a palm. The card follows the book and palm and gives him a Roman tunic.
- Patron line: in Brazil São Pancrácio is invoked above all for work and employment; Wikipedia gives "children, jobs and health".
- Excerpt: mart10.json entrance antiphon, first clause. The pt-BR "o homem" is the Common's; a different line would need another source.

## Callistus I

Face: a stocky man of about fifty-five, a wide rectangular face with a heavy jaw, a broad flat-bridged nose, small shrewd hazel eyes under thick straight brows, full cheeks, a wide mouth; coarse black-and-grey hair in a blunt Roman fringe; a short, dense, square black-and-grey beard.

- The medallion portrait from the series of popes at St Paul Outside the Walls, a fresco from before the 1823 fire (local `lead-Pope_Callixtus_I.jpg`), which I checked: a broad face, receding hair with curls at the side, a broad nose and a short full beard. The card follows the breadth and the beard, gives him a Roman fringe instead of the receding hair so he does not repeat batch 7's bald, curly-fringed Sixtus II, and makes him sturdier, as a man who had been a slave and a labourer in the mines of Sardinia (Wikipedia, local `wp-Pope_Callixtus_I.txt`).
- Wikipedia: Santa Maria in Trastevere was his titulus; he was perhaps "thrown down a well" in a popular uprising; he was buried on the Via Aurelia. The card shows a modest house-church and a well-head in Trastevere, without the martyrdom. The catacomb of St Callixtus is avoided because Fabian's card already uses it.
- Excerpt: 10-14.json collect, final clause.

## Martin I

Face: a man of about sixty, thinned by hardship, with a long rectangular face, a straight Roman nose, large dark sorrowful eyes under thick straight black brows, hollow cheeks; short dark hair streaked with grey; a full dark beard cut square.

- The statue by Carlo Laurenti at Todi (local `lead-Pope_Martin_I.jpg`), which I checked: a long, full beard, a tiara, seated in blessing. The card keeps a full beard, shorter and squared, and drops the tiara.
- Wikipedia: born near Todi, abbot before his election, arrested in the Lateran in 653, tried at Constantinople and exiled to Chersonesus in the Crimea, where he died in 655 (local `wp-Pope_Martin_I.txt`). The thin face follows that history; it is an artistic choice.
- Excerpt: 04-13.json collect ("not … daunted by threats or broken by suffering").

## Hilary

Face: a robust man of about fifty-five with a broad, trapezoid face, a high broad forehead with receding white hair, a strong straight nose with a broad bridge, wide-set grey eyes under heavy arched white brows, ruddy cheeks; a long, broad, flowing white beard.

- Christian Iconography: "Portraits usually give St. Hilary episcopal vestments, a mitre and crozier, and a beard, usually white and often long" (local `notes-web.txt`). The card keeps the white beard; the tradition demands it here.
- The fourteenth-century miniature of his consecration (local `lead-Hilary_of_Poitiers.jpg`), which I checked: a young, beardless, fair-haired bishop. Not followed, as against the dominant tradition.
- Age: born c. 310, died 367; the book is his De Trinitate. The baptistery of Saint-Jean at Poitiers is a fourth-century building.
- Excerpt: 01-13.json collect.

## Peter Damian

Face: a lean, ascetic man of about sixty-four with a long face and a craggy brow ridge, deep-set burning dark eyes turned upward, a long, slightly hooked nose, sharp cheekbones over sunken cheeks, thin lips; short wiry grey hair; a long, narrow, tapering grey beard.

- The marble bust (local `lead-Peter_Damian.jpg`), which I checked: a long, tapering, wavy beard, deep-set eyes raised upward, a lean face, short hair. The card follows it.
- Wikipedia: "represented in art as a cardinal bearing a knotted rope (the disciplina) in his hand"; a monk and prior of Fonte Avellana, Cardinal-Bishop of Ostia, 1007–1072 (local `wp-Peter_Damian.txt`). The rope is a short plain white knotted cord, held quietly; the cardinal's scarlet goes over the white Camaldolese habit.
- Excerpt: 02-21.json collect, middle clause.

## Look-alike risks that remain

- Long beards: John Damascene (iron-grey, wavy, olive skin, head-cloth), Hilary (white, broad), Peter Damian (grey, narrow, tapering) and Martin (dark, squared, medium) all carry beards to or near the breast. They are separated by colour, cut and headdress, and Hilary's white beard sits near Anthony, Polycarp, Cletus and Nicholas; check the face sheet for Hilary against those.
- Sylvester and Cletus: both old popes with pointed white beards on a long face; Sylvester's is short and neat with very large eyes, Cletus's long and forked. Worth a side-by-side.
- Callistus against Linus, Sixtus II (batch 7) and Peter Chrysologus (batch 7): broad-faced, bearded, around sixty. Callistus has a heavy rectangular jaw, a blunt fringe (Sixtus is bald with curls, Linus receding) and a square-trimmed beard (Linus's and Chrysologus's are rounded).
- Sylvester against Norbert (batch 7): both long, tapering faces; Norbert is clean-shaven and forty-five.
- Thomas Becket against Fabian and Charles Borromeo: long, lean, clean-shaven prelates. Becket is younger, dark-haired and in a mitre with the pallium.
- Pancras against Aloysius and the other beardless youths: the round, childish face and tight curls should keep him fourteen and not a young man.
