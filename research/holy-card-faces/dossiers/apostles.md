# Apostles, evangelists and St Joseph: faces

Facial marks for the saints who share the stock male face (man in his 30s, brown curly hair, short brown beard), so that each card can be edited toward its own tradition. The rules follow `faces.md`: keep only the flattering marks (age, hair, beard, build) and leave out harsh literal detail.

Each **Face:** line uses only features that a quoted source attests. Where no source gives a hair colour, the line gives none, and the colour is ours to choose (see "Look-alikes" at the end). Tags:
- **[Byz]**: the Byzantine *Painter's Manual* (Hermeneia) of Dionysius of Fourna (compiled on Athos c. 1730–34), quoted from Paul Hetherington's translation, *The 'Painter's Manual' of Dionysius of Fourna* (London: Sagittarius Press, 1974). The apostle list is on pp. 52–54, §353–358 of the Greek. The Hermeneia also gives the date of each saint's feast.
- **[GL]**: the *Golden Legend* (Jacobus de Voragine, 13th c.), in Caxton's English translation from the Fordham Medieval Sourcebook.
- **[West]**: Western art-historical practice, from Richard Stracke's *Christian Iconography* site (christianiconography.info).

Where the sources:
- Hermeneia record: https://archive.org/details/paintersmanualof0000dion
- A scanned PDF of the Hetherington translation (OCR'd for this note; a local copy is in the gitignored `../consult/`): https://maypoleofwisdom.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/775213623-the-painter-s-manual-of-dionysius-of-fourna.pdf

A caveat on the Hermeneia's list. Hetherington's note 1 on p. 52 says the Hermeneia follows the "hieratic or liturgical series" of apostles. That series "has added Matthew, Mark, Luke and Paul to the eight apostles common to both series, ignoring the Gospel tradition which provides James the Less, Matthias, Judas Iscariot and Thaddaeus". So James the Less, Matthias, Jude/Thaddaeus and Barnabas are described only in the Manual's list of the **Seventy** (§355–358), and there the Eastern church treats some of them as different people from the Western apostle of the same name (see James the Less and Jude).

## Andrew

**Face:** an old man with unruly curly grey-white hair and a long beard that divides into two points.

- [Byz] "Andrew, an old man with curly hair and his beard divided into two points; he holds a cross and a bound scroll. November 30th." (Hetherington p. 52, §353)
- [West] "the four common identifying characteristics of portraits of St. Andrew: the wild hair, the long beard, a cross, and a book … Wild hair is an individualizing characteristic of St. Andrew portraits from at least as early as the 6th century." Attributes: "Wild hair, long beard". https://www.christianiconography.info/andrew.html
- Grey-white hair is our reading of "old man". Neither source gives a colour.

## Bartholomew

**Face:** a mature man of straight, upright build with fair skin, large eyes, black curly hair and a long black beard just starting to grey. (Western, [GL])

- [GL] The devil describes him: "He hath his hairs black and crisp, his skin white, eyes great, his nostrils even and straipht, his beard long and hoar a little, and of a straight and seemly stature." (*Golden Legend*, Life of S. Bartholomew, Caxton, vol. 5) https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/basis/goldenlegend/GoldenLegend-Volume5.asp
- [West] "The Golden Legend describes St. Bartholomew as having black curly hair, large eyes, fair skin, and a long beard starting to gray. Of these features the only one followed consistently in the images is the curly hair, and that only in Western images after the Legend was published, in the 13th century." https://www.christianiconography.info/bartholomew.html
- [Byz] differs: "Bartholomew, a young man with an incipient beard. June 11th." (p. 52, §354). We follow the Western [GL] description because the cards are Western holy cards and it separates him from James the Greater, whom the Hermeneia describes in the same words.

## James the Greater

**Face:** a youthful man with a young, light beard. His pilgrim's hat and staff carry his identity. (Both [Byz] and the Western pilgrim type)

- [Byz] "James, a young man with an incipient beard. April 30th." (p. 52, §354). April 30 is the Eastern feast of James son of Zebedee.
- [West] "In the Pudentiana mosaic and other apostle groups, James the Greater often has a long white beard …, although he looks more youthful when portrayed as a pilgrim." He is shown as a pilgrim "always carrying a pilgrim's staff … in a broad-brimmed pilgrim's hat", often with a cockle shell. https://www.christianiconography.info/jamesGreater.html
- We choose the youthful pilgrim type. The long-white-beard option would clash with Andrew and Matthew.

## James the Less

**Face:** a young man with long hair that has never been cut, falling to his shoulders, and a pointed beard. In the West he was said to resemble Christ in body and face.

- [Byz] Among the Seventy: "James Alphaeus, a young man with a pointed beard; one of the 12." (p. 53, §355)
- [GL] "He was called also the brother of our Lord, because he resembled much well our Lord in body, in visage, and of manner … He never drank wine, mead, ne cider, ne never ate flesh, ne never rasor touched his head, ne he never bathed." (*Golden Legend*, Life of S. James the Less, vol. 3) https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/basis/goldenlegend/GoldenLegend-Volume3.asp
- [West] "his face resembles that of Jesus; this practice was condemned by Molanus …, who noted however that it seemed to have its origin in a comment attributed to St. Ignatius that James looked so much like Jesus that they could have been twins." Also: "Later images sometimes put beards on all the apostles except John the Evangelist and James the Less." Attribute: a club, often a fuller's club. https://www.christianiconography.info/jamesLess.html
- The Eastern church treats James the brother of the Lord as a different person: "James the Brother of God, an old man with a long beard. October 23rd." (p. 53, §355). The West identifies the two, but we don't use this entry.
- A risk: pushing the likeness to Christ too far could make him read as Christ. Keep it to the uncut hair and the pointed beard.

## Philip

**Face:** a beardless youth with a somewhat long face and a tousled forelock parted in two.

- [Byz] "Philip, a young man, beardless. November 14th." (p. 52, §354)
- [West] "He may be portrayed as a man in middle or advanced age …, but sometimes in the West and consistently in the East he is a beardless youth with a somewhat long face and a tousled or bifurcated forelock". https://www.christianiconography.info/philip.html

## Matthew

**Face:** an old man with a long beard. The long beard should be flowing and undivided, to set him apart from Andrew's forked beard.

- [Byz] "Matthew the evangelist, an old man with a long beard, holding a Gospel. November 17th." (p. 52, §353)
- [West] No facial tradition is given. Attributes: angel (often a boy), book, sometimes a money-bag, a sword or a halberd. https://www.christianiconography.info/matthew.html

## Mark

**Face:** a greying man with a rounded beard.

- [Byz] "Mark the evangelist, grey-haired, with a rounded beard, holding a Gospel. April 25th." (p. 52, §353)
- [Byz] The Seventy list has a different entry: "Mark, nephew of Barnabas, a young man, beardless." (p. 53, §357). The East treats John Mark as a separate person. We don't use it, because there are already two beardless youths (Philip, Thomas).
- [West] No facial tradition is given. Attributes: lion, pen, book, desk. https://www.christianiconography.info/mark.html
- The Hermeneia uses "grey-haired" as a separate category from "old man". We render it as greying middle age, which is our interpretation.

## Luke

**Face:** a young man with curly hair and a short beard. This is essentially the current stock face, so Luke can keep it.

- [Byz] "Luke the evangelist, a young man with curly hair and a short beard, painting the Mother of God. October 18th." (p. 52, §353). Hetherington's note 3: the Luke-painting-the-Virgin type "would seem to originate only in the 15th century".
- [West] "Usually St. Luke is shown bearded and with a full head of hair". Attributes: ox, book, painting of the Virgin. https://www.christianiconography.info/luke.html
- A different entry, "Luke, an old man with a long beard", appears in the Seventy list (p. 54, §357). It isn't tagged as the evangelist, so we ignore it.

## Thomas

**Face:** a beardless young man.

- [Byz] "Thomas, a young man, beardless. October 6th." (p. 52, §354)
- [West] "most medieval images give Thomas a full beard and a full head of hair. But mosaics influenced by Orthodox iconography make him a beardless youth". Attributes: spear, carpenter's square, sometimes a sword. https://www.christianiconography.info/thomas.html
- We choose the beardless youth. The Western full beard would put him back on the stock face.

## Matthias

**Face:** an old man with a full head of hair and a rounded beard.

- [Byz] Among the Seventy: "Matthias, an old man with a rounded beard." (p. 53, §355)
- [West] No facial tradition is given. Attributes: "Axe or spear; Book or scroll". https://www.christianiconography.info/matthias.html
- "Full head of hair" is not attested. It is a design choice to separate him from Simon, who is bald.

## Barnabas

**Face:** a greying man with a long beard.

- [Byz] Among the Seventy: "Barnabas, grey-haired, with a long beard." (p. 54, §358)
- [West] No facial tradition is given. Attributes: "Book or scroll; Fire". "The fire on his hand most likely refers to the manner of his death." https://www.christianiconography.info/barnabas.html

## Simon (the Zealot)

**Face:** an old man, bald, with a rounded beard.

- [Byz] "Simon Zelotes, an old man, bald, with a rounded beard. May 1st." (p. 52, §354)
- [West] No facial tradition is given. One tradition of great age: "The alternative version … says Simon was crucified during the reign of Trajan (98-117), when Simon was 120 years old." https://www.christianiconography.info/simon.html
- **Attributes, for the joint card** [West]:
  - "In most portraits after the 13th century St. Simon's attribute is a bucksaw". This comes from the Golden Legend's statement that he and Jude were "butchered" (*trucidarunt*) by pagan priests.
  - "Rarely, an image will have the saint executed with a sword instead of a bucksaw", and one portrait has "a one-handed sword with bucksaw-like serrations".
  - "In England, Simon's attribute can be a boat." This refers to legends of his journey to Britain.
  - Source for all three: https://www.christianiconography.info/simon.html

## Jude (Thaddeus)

**Face:** a young man with a young, light beard. This follows the Hermeneia's "Judas the brother of the Lord". The alternative is the Thaddaeus entry: a greying man with a wide beard.

- [Byz] Among the Seventy: "Judas the brother of the Lord, a young man with an incipient beard." (p. 53, §355). Also: "Thaddaeus, grey-haired, with a wide beard." (p. 54, §358). The East keeps Thaddaeus of the Seventy distinct from the apostle Jude, whose Eastern feast is June 19 according to christianiconography.info. The West identifies them: "In medieval texts and images, the above names are assumed to belong to the same person and are used interchangeably."
- Hetherington gives no note saying which Hermeneia entry is the apostle. Our choice of the "brother of the Lord" entry rests on the Western identification of Jude as "Jude the brother of James" (Luke 6:16). We chose it because a young Jude contrasts with the old, bald Simon on the joint card.
- [West] No facial tradition is given.
- **Attributes, for the joint card** [West], all from https://www.christianiconography.info/judeThaddeus.html:
  1. "HALBERD OR CLUB … in all other images I have seen the attribute is either a halberd … or a club. In many cases the club is significantly broader at one end." (It warns that James the Less's fuller's club can cause confusion.)
  2. "IMAGE OF JESUS … In later Latin Christianity there is an iconographic type in which St. Jude looks directly at the viewer while holding a plaque with Jesus' face up to his chest." This comes from the Abgar / Edessa image story in the *Acts of Thaddaeus* and in Eusebius.
  3. "FLAME In that western type of image a flame burns from the top of the saint's head." This may refer to the Golden Legend's "Lo! how we be burnt by flames", or to Jude 1:23.
  4. The carpenter's square appears rarely, "perhaps owing to a confusion with St. Thomas". Avoid it.
- The 19th/20th-century devotional type combines items 2 and 3: a frontal gaze, the medallion of Christ's face on the chest and a flame above the head. It is the most recognisable choice. Add the halberd or club if the card holds an object.

## Joseph (spouse of Mary)

**Face:** a man in vigorous middle age with a full head of dark hair and a full, rounded beard. (Western post-16th-century type, beard shape from [Byz])

- [Byz] "The just Joseph, son of Jacob, betrothed to the Mother of God; an old man with a rounded beard." (pp. 27–28, §187)
- [West] "A common approach previously had been to follow the Protevangelium in making him an old man – balding, graying, often leaning on a cane." Later: "Molanus's spirited insistence that St. Joseph should be portrayed as a young man … After Molanus many images abandon the traditional portrayal and give the saint, for example, a full head of dark hair … This is by no means a consistent pattern in any period, however. Indeed, in 1895 the same studio in Germany produced … a Marriage of the Virgin with a quite young Joseph with all his hair and … a Holy Family with … a Joseph seriously balding and carrying a cane." One 17th/18th-century Holy Family pictures him "as a man in vigorous middle age". Attributes: flowering staff or lily stalk, sometimes a dove at its top, and carpenter's tools. https://www.christianiconography.info/joseph.html
- Both the old and the middle-aged Joseph are attested for the holy-card period. Middle-aged, dark-haired Joseph is the closest to the stock face, so make him visibly older than it (late 40s, heavier and rounder beard). If he still reads as stock, switch to the old, balding, greying type.

## Look-alikes

Grouped by the sources' own descriptions, with the pairs that would still look alike:

| Group | Saints | Separator |
|---|---|---|
| Beardless youth | Philip, Thomas | Both are attested only as "young, beardless". Philip's long face and parted forelock (christianiconography.info) is the only sourced difference, so **flag**. If they still read alike, the sourced fallback is the Western middle- or old-aged Philip. |
| Young, light/new beard | James the Greater, Jude | The descriptions are identical in the Hermeneia. **Flag.** In practice the pilgrim hat (James) and the flame and medallion of Christ (Jude) keep them apart. Use different hair colours (not attested). |
| Young with a beard | Luke (curly, short beard), James the Less (long uncut hair, pointed beard) | Different enough. |
| Rounded beard | Mark (greying), Matthias (old, full hair), Simon (old, bald), Joseph (middle-aged, dark) | **Flag Mark and Matthias**: they differ only in degree of age. Make Mark clearly younger and steel-grey, and Matthias white. |
| Long beard | Matthew (old, undivided), Barnabas (greying), Andrew (old, forked, wild hair), Bartholomew (black, curly, just greying) | **Flag Matthew and Barnabas**: they differ only in degree of age. |
| Bald, old | Simon, compared with Peter's existing card (balding, curly fringe, square beard) and Paul's (bald, long pointed beard) | Simon's rounded beard and fully bald crown differ from Peter's only moderately. **Flag Simon and Peter.** |

No saint in this set lacks an attested facial tradition, since the Hermeneia covers all of them. For Matthew, Mark, Matthias, Barnabas, Simon and Jude, however, the only facial source is Byzantine. Western practice records attributes for them but no face.
