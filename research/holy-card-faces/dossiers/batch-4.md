# Batch 4: faces

Faces, dress and settings for ten saints who don't have a card yet: three modern popes, five modern saints and two from tradition. The generation inputs are in `../batches/batch-4.json`; this file holds the evidence behind them. Pictures consulted are in `../consult/batch-4/` (the contact sheets `sheet-sources*.jpg` put them side by side; `sheet-existing.jpg` is the face crop of existing cards they must not resemble).

Excerpts: for every saint who died after about 1930 the excerpt is copied from the feast's Mass formulary in `content/of/formularies/`, because their own writings are in copyright. Where only part of a collect is used, the phrase is verbatim and only its first letter was capitalised. Bakhita's feast has no proper formulary in the repo, so hers comes from the Common of Virgins. Juan Diego's is the Virgin's line in the *Nican Mopohua* (1649), translated from the Nahuatl.

## The three popes

The three must differ from each other and from `leo_great` (a heavy-set, fleshy, dark-eyed pope in tiara and gold cope before Rome). Each gets a distinct build, a distinct colouring and distinct dress:

| | build and age | colouring | dress | place |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| John Paul II | sturdy, athletic, ~60 | fair greying hair, grey-blue eyes | all white, pellegrina, the crucifix staff | Kraków, Wawel |
| John XXIII | stout, round, ~80 | dark eyes, laughing | red mozzetta, embroidered stole, red ermine **camauro** | Sotto il Monte |
| Paul VI | slender, long-faced, ~70 | blue deep-set eyes, dark brows, aquiline nose | ermine-edged red mozzetta, apostle stole, Gospel book | Sea of Galilee, Mount Tabor |

## John Paul II

Face: a sturdy, broad-shouldered, athletic man of about sixty, clean-shaven, with a broad Slavic face, high cheekbones and a strong square jaw, light grey-blue eyes, fair, greying ash-blond hair at the temples under the zucchetto, and a warm, open half-smile.

- The Wikipedia lead photograph (Adamello, 1984; local copy `Pope_John_Paul_II.jpg`) and the 1991 portrait (`Pope_John_Paul_II_1991_cropped.jpg`), which I checked: a broad face with high cheekbones, light eyes, fair-grey hair, white cassock and zucchetto; in the 1984 picture he holds the crucifix staff. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_Paul_II, https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pope_John_Paul_II_1991_(cropped).jpg
- He was elected at 58. "In his 20s, he was an athlete, standing 5 feet 10.5 inches at 175 pounds"; "an indefatigable hiker and kayaker" who skied in the Tatras. https://www.skiinghistory.org/lives/john-paul-ii
- The staff: "a contemporary single-barred crucifix, designed by the Italian artist Lello Scorzelli in 1963"; Paul VI first used it in 1965 and John Paul II carried it throughout his pontificate. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_ferula
- Choice: the vigorous years, not the stooped old man of the 2000s. The age sets him apart from John XXIII, the athletic build from Leo, and the fair colouring from both.
- Dress and attributes: white cassock, pellegrina and zucchetto, a gold pectoral cross, the ferula, a hand raised in blessing.
- Background: Kraków from the Vistula with Wawel Hill (he was its archbishop), the Tatras on the horizon.

## John XXIII

Face: a stout, heavy-set man of about eighty, clean-shaven, with a large round face, full cheeks and a double chin, a broad, prominent nose, small twinkling dark-brown eyes with laughter lines, and a broad, kindly smile.

- The De Agostini portrait (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Pope_John_XXIII.jpg`) and the 1959 photograph (`Pope_John_XXIII_-_1959.jpg`), which I checked: a round, heavy face, a large nose, a wide smile, white zucchetto, red mozzetta and a red stole embroidered in gold with figures of saints. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII
- Age: 1881–1963, pope from 76 to 81.
- The camauro: "Pope John XXIII revived the hat with the assistance of papal tailor Gammarelli, wearing it for the first time in a December 1958 audience"; it is red velvet trimmed with white ermine. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camauro. It is the one piece of dress that no other card has, so it carries his identity.
- Background: Sotto il Monte, the village near Bergamo where he was born into a family of sharecroppers (Wikipedia).
- The nose: keep it broad and kindly, not caricatured.

## Paul VI

Face: a slender, ascetic man of about seventy, clean-shaven, with a long, narrow face, a high bald forehead and close dark-grey hair at the temples, a pronounced aquiline nose, deep-set clear blue eyes under strong dark brows, thin lips and a grave, gentle, thoughtful expression.

- The 1969 official portrait (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Pope_Paul_VI.jpg`) and `Papa_Paolo_VI.jpg`, which I checked: a long, lean face, an aquiline nose, deep-set light eyes, dark brows, a red mozzetta edged in white ermine and a red stole embroidered with apostles. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI
- Age: 1897–1978, pope from 65 to 80. Seventy is the portrait's age.
- Holy Land: in January 1964 he went on pilgrimage to the Holy Land, the first pope to do so; Wikipedia carries a photograph of him on Mount Tabor in 1964. That gives a real place that no other card uses and keeps him out of Rome, which is Leo's background.
- Patron line: he led and closed the Second Vatican Council, so "Pope of the Second Vatican Council".
- Not John XXIII: both wear a red mozzetta in their portraits. The camauro, the stout round face and the smile mark John; the white zucchetto, the long thin face, the nose and the grave expression mark Paul.

## Maria Goretti

Face: a girl of eleven, visibly a child, with a round, soft face, a lightly sun-warmed complexion, long, wavy chestnut-brown hair parted in the middle and loosely tied back, large dark-brown eyes and a serene, trusting gaze.

- Wikipedia: "The only known photograph of Maria Goretti, dated to early 1902" (local copy `Photograph_of_Saint_Maria_Goretti_1902.jpg`), which I checked: a slim girl with dark hair drawn back, in a white blouse and long white apron, standing among the farm's poultry. The traditional portrait (lead image, local copy `Maria_Goretti.jpg`) gives long, wavy chestnut hair and a soft oval face. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Goretti
- Wikipedia: she is shown as "a wavy-haired young girl in farmer's clothes or a white dress, with a bouquet of lilies in her hands"; attributes "lily; farmer's clothing; martyr's palm". Her family shared "La Cascina Antica" at Le Ferriere near Nettuno.
- She died on 6 July 1902, aged eleven. The collect calls her martyred "in her youth".
- Choice: farmer's clothes rather than the white dress, which makes her read as a real peasant child and not another virgin princess. No wound, no assailant.
- Background: the Pontine plain at Le Ferriere, the Cascina Antica, the sea near Nettuno.

## Josephine Bakhita

Face: a Sudanese woman of about fifty with deep dark-brown skin, a round, full face, a broad nose and full lips, large, gentle dark eyes and a warm, open smile.

- The photograph of c. 1910 (local copy `Photograph_of_Saint_Josephine_Bakhita_c._1910.jpg`) and the colour portrait (Wikipedia lead image, `Josephine_Bakhita.jpg`), which I checked: a round face, full lips, a gentle smile, the black Canossian habit with a round bonnet gathered in ruffles and tied under the chin, a white collar edge, and a medal on a cord. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephine_Bakhita
- Wikipedia: she wore the Canossian habit "and medal of Our Lady of Sorrows"; in Schio she was "Sor Moretta" or "Madre Moretta", remembered for her "gentleness, calming voice, and the ever-present smile". She lived c. 1869–1947, 42 years in Schio. Patron of Sudan.
- Left out: the 114 scars cut into her body when she was a slave. They were on her breast, stomach and arm, not her face, and a devotional card does not show them.
- The broken chain is the usual modern emblem of her freedom, not an early tradition; keep it small at the lower edge.
- Excerpt: no proper Mass for her in the repo (`content/of/formularies/sanctoral/02-08.json` is Jerome Emiliani, and there is no `02-08/` folder). The line is the first sentence of the communion antiphon in the Common of Virgins, `common/virgins/virg0.json`: en-US "Whoever loves me will be loved by my Father, and I will come to him…" (cut after "Father"), pt-BR "Aquele que me ama será amado por meu Pai." The two languages continue differently after that sentence, so only the shared first sentence is used.

## Martin de Porres

Face: a slender Afro-Peruvian man of about forty-five with warm medium-brown skin, close-cropped, tightly curled black hair, clean-shaven, a broad nose and full lips, heavy-lidded dark eyes lowered in humility, and a gentle, quiet expression.

- Wikipedia: a 17th-century portrait in Lima "was painted during his lifetime or very soon after his death, hence it is probably the most true to his appearance"; he "is often depicted as a young mixed-race friar wearing the old habit of the Dominican lay brother". His father was a Spanish nobleman and his mother "Ana Velázquez, a freed slave of African and Native descent". 1579–1639, at the convent of the Rosary in Lima. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_de_Porres
- The Huaycán portrait (lead image, local copy `Martin_de_Porres.jpg`), which I checked: a thin, clean-shaven face with close black hair and heavy-lidded, downcast eyes, a pale tunic with a black scapular and a long rosary. The 2016 forensic reconstruction from his skull (`San_Martín_de_Porres_-_Reconstrucción_Facial_3D.jpg`): a broad nose, full lips, close dark hair, medium-brown skin.
- Attributes (Wikipedia): the broom, and "a dog, a cat and a mouse eating in peace from the same dish".
- Age: shown in middle age, between the young friar of the images and his death at 59.
- pt-BR name: the Brazilian missal calls him "São Martinho (de Lima)"; the card uses "São Martinho de Lima".

## Juan Diego

Face: an Indigenous Mexican man in his mid-fifties with warm copper-brown skin, straight black hair to the shoulders, a thin black moustache and a short, sparse black beard on the chin, high cheekbones, dark almond-shaped eyes and a humble, wondering gaze turned down toward the image.

- Miguel Cabrera's *Fiel retrato* (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Juan_Diego.jpg`) and the *Verdadero retrato del siervo de Dios Juan Diego* (`Juan-Diego.jpg`), which I checked: long straight black hair, a thin moustache and a sparse chin beard, a white tilma knotted at the shoulder; in the first the Virgin appears above him, in the second he kneels with roses on the ground. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Diego
- Wikipedia: born 1474, so 57 at the apparitions of December 1531; the image appeared on his cloak (*tilmahtli*) when he brought the bishop the flowers he had gathered on Tepeyac.
- The beard is sparse and black, not the stock brown curls; the long straight hair and the copper skin carry the difference.
- Excerpt: *Nican Mopohua* v. 119, the Virgin to Juan Diego when he is afraid for his uncle: "Cuix amo nican nica nimonantzin?" (Nahuatl text as transcribed with notes by D. K. Jordan, https://pages.ucsd.edu/~dkjordan/nahuatl/nican/nican5.html, local copy `nican5.html`). The Nahuatl, printed by Laso de la Vega in 1649, is public domain; the English and Portuguese lines are our own literal translations, not Jordan's. The words are Our Lady's, not his, but they are the line that devotion to him carries. The formulary collect (`12-09.json`) is the alternative: "by following the counsels our Mother gave at Guadalupe".
- Background: the hill of Tepeyac in December, with nopal and scrub, and the valley and lake of Mexico beyond.

## Charbel Makhlouf

Face: a lean old monk of about seventy with a long, narrow face and high cheekbones, a fine straight nose, a long, full white beard falling to the chest and a white moustache, heavy white brows, and eyes lowered and almost closed in recollection; the black hood shadows the forehead.

- No picture was taken of him in life. On 8 May 1950 a Maronite priest from Scranton photographed four priests at Annaya and found a fifth, white-bearded figure in the print; "all the old monks convincingly said that it was St Charbel", and "all subsequent photos and portraits of St Charbel were based on the photo". https://saintcharbel.net.au/miracles/st-charbel-appears-in-a-photo-after-his-death-in-annaya-mount-lebanon/, https://aleteia.org/2025/07/24/the-only-photograph-of-st-charbel-was-a-miracle/
- The standard image (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Charbel_Makhlouf.jpg`), which I checked: a black hood drawn down to the brows, eyes lowered almost shut, a long, broad white beard, a narrow face. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charbel_Makhlouf
- Wikipedia: 1828–1898; he lived from 1875 until his death in the Hermitage of Saints Peter and Paul above the monastery of Saint Maron at Annaya; attributes "religious habit and prayer rope"; patron of Lebanon.
- Not the stock white-bearded elder: the hood hides all hair and the upper forehead, the eyes are nearly closed, and the face is narrow. He never looks out of the card.
- Excerpt: the formulary gives "Sharbel" in English; the card name uses the usual "Charbel".

## Josemaría Escrivá

Face: a robust, clean-shaven priest of about sixty-five with a broad, round face and full cheeks, dark hair greying at the temples combed straight back from a high forehead, dark eyes behind heavy dark-rimmed rectangular spectacles, and a wide, cheerful smile.

- The Wikipedia lead photograph (local copy `Josemaría_Escrivá.jpg`) and the official Opus Dei prayer card (local copy `escriva-prayercard.pdf`, page render `escriva-prayercard-1.png`), which I checked: a round face, dark hair combed back, dark-framed glasses, a broad smile, a black cassock with a Roman collar. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josemar%C3%ADa_Escriv%C3%A1, https://multimedia.opusdei.org/docs/St%20Josemaria%20Prayercard.pdf
- Age: 1902–1975; the familiar photographs are from his sixties.
- Excerpt: the gospel acclamation of his Mass (`06-26.json`, Mk 1:17). The collect speaks of "the universal call to sanctity" but its two languages are built differently, so no phrase lines up.
- Background: Madrid, where he founded Opus Dei on 2 October 1928, with ordinary street life kept small to suggest holiness in daily work.

## Catherine of Alexandria

Face: a young noblewoman of eighteen with a fine oval face, fair olive skin, a straight classical nose, dark chestnut hair braided and coiled up beneath the crown with a few loose strands, large dark-brown eyes and a clear, intelligent, composed gaze.

- Golden Legend (Caxton, vol. 7, local copy `../consult/gl/v7.txt`): daughter of King Costus of Cyprus, "so fair of visage and so well formed in her members that all the people enjoyed in her beauty"; schooled in "the arts liberal"; "this holy virgin was at that time eighteen years of age, dwelling in her palace" at Alexandria when she confronted the emperor, who set fifty rhetoricians against her. The torment was "four wheels of iron, environed with sharp razors", and "the blessed virgin prayed our Lord that he would break these engines". https://sourcebooks.fordham.edu/basis/goldenlegend/GoldenLegend-Volume7.asp
- Caravaggio, *Saint Catherine of Alexandria* (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Catherine_of_Alexandria.jpg`), which I checked: dark auburn-brown hair, rich dark dress, the spiked wheel, a sword and a palm. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_of_Alexandria
- Attributes, modestly: crown, book, palm, the spiked wheel shown broken; the sword sheathed and point-down, or left out.
- Background: the harbour of Alexandria with the Pharos.
- Not Philomena, who already has a crown: Catherine's hair is dark and coiled up, not in long braids; her dress is green and crimson with ermine, not blue and red; she holds a book, and the broken wheel is hers alone.

## Pairs that may still look alike

- **John XXIII and Leo the Great**: both heavy, clean-shaven, old popes. John's camauro, laughing eyes and broad smile against Leo's tiara, gold cope and commanding gravity. Check this pair on the contact sheet first.
- **John XXIII and Paul VI**: both in red mozzetta and embroidered stole in their portraits. The headgear (camauro against white zucchetto) and the face shape (round and smiling against long and grave) must carry it.
- **John Paul II and Charles Borromeo / Paul VI**: all clean-shaven clerics. John Paul is fair, broad and in white; Borromeo is dark, thin and in cardinal's red; Paul is thin, dark-browed and in red with ermine.
- **Escrivá and Kolbe**: both spectacled priests. Escrivá is clean-shaven and round-faced with dark rectangular frames and a black cassock; Kolbe has a long dark beard and round wire glasses.
- **Escrivá and Vincent de Paul / Philip Neri**: black-cassocked priests; Escrivá is the only one without a beard and with glasses.
- **Charbel and Padre Pio / the white-bearded elders**: Pio's beard is rounded and he smiles under a brown hood; Charbel's hood is black and drawn low, his beard long, his eyes nearly closed.
- **Martin de Porres and Moses the Black**: both men of African descent. Moses is bearded and powerful in a brown cloak; Martin is slender, clean-shaven, in the black-and-white Dominican habit with the broom.
- **Juan Diego and Moses the Black / Francis Xavier**: bearded dark-haired men. Juan Diego's beard is sparse, his hair long and straight, and the tilma image makes him unmistakable.
- **Bakhita and the nuns** (Faustina, Thérèse, Margaret Mary): the ruffled Canossian bonnet and her colouring separate her.
- **Maria Goretti and Agnes, Lucy, Philomena**: she is a child of eleven in farm clothes without veil or crown; if the draft ages her to a young woman, redo it.
- **Catherine and Philomena**: see her entry.
