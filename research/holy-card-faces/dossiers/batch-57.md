# Batch 57: the traditional parts of the Mass, from the Memento of the dead to the Leonine Prayers

The other eight traditional parts with no card. The research, the table of the whole Ordo, the decisions and the convention are in `dossiers/batch-56.md`; `consult/batch-56/build.py` writes this batch file too and checks its excerpts against the Divinum Officium files. **RS** and **F** as there.

## The cards

- **`memento_dead`** — Low Mass, after the Consecration. The priest's hands joined and raised before his face, his eyes on the Sacrament on the altar (RS IX.2). Excerpt: the prayer's first sentence, without "N. and N.". The widow, the votive candles, the wall cross and the churchyard in the window are the painter's setting, not a rubric.
- **`nobis_quoque`** — the priest raises his voice a little and strikes his breast with the right hand, the left on the corporal (RS IX.3). Excerpt: the opening, up to the "holy Apostles and Martyrs". The reredos of martyrs with palms is decoration, standing for the saints the prayer names.
- **`libera_nos`** — the priest signs himself with the paten, the left hand below the breast, and then kisses it (RS X.1–2). Excerpt: the opening. The fraction and commingling that close the prayer are `agnus_dei`.
- **`domine_non_sum_dignus`** — the priest bowed a little, the two halves of the Host and the paten in his left hand, striking his breast three times (RS X.4; F l. 5166–5174). No bell: "there is no authority in the missal for ringing … at Domine non sum dignus", though the custom is tolerated (F l. 6416–6418 and note). Excerpt: the prayer, once.
- **`ablutions`** — Low Mass. The server pours wine and water over the priest's thumbs and forefingers into the chalice at the epistle corner (RS X.5; F l. 5352–5362). Excerpt: Quod ore sumpsimus, whole.
- **`placeat`** — after Ite, missa est the priest bows his head with joined hands on the altar (RS XII.1); the chalice is veiled (RS X.5) and the Missal closed (RS XI.1). The people kneel for the blessing that follows (F l. 6694–6695). Excerpt: the opening.
- **`last_gospel`** — Low Mass. The priest at the gospel corner reads from the altar card with joined hands, the server standing at the epistle side (RS XII.1; F l. 2988–2989); all stand (F l. 6697). Excerpt: John 1:1, from `Ordo/Prayers.txt` `[Ultima Evangelium]`. The reading is painted, not the genuflection at Et Verbum caro factum est, which would repeat `profession_of_faith`.
- **`leonine_prayers`** — after Low Mass. The priest kneels on the lowest step with the prayer card, the server at the epistle side (F l. 5479–5486, 2995–2997, 6540–6543). Excerpt: the opening of the prayer to St. Michael, from the Ordo's `# Leonine Prayers`. The statue of Our Lady and the St. Michael window are decoration, for the Hail Marys, the Hail Holy Queen and the prayer to St. Michael.

## Decisions

1. **The Leonine Prayers are a card of the Mass section** though they are said after Mass, because the Ordo carries them (`!*R`, Low Mass) and they are what a Low Mass ends with. Fortescue: "these prayers are not part of the Mass" (l. 5486–5488).
2. **The excerpt is St. Michael's prayer**, the best known of the five. The alternative is the collect, "O God, our refuge and our strength", which has no European spelling in the Portuguese.
3. **White and gold throughout**, the Memento of the dead too. A Requiem in black would say "the dead" at a glance, but it would break the Mass-part convention a second time (after the Universal Prayer's Good Friday).
