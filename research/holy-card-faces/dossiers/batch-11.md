# Batch 11 of new cards: faces

Faces, dress and settings for ten cards: Raymund of Penyafort, Ansgar, Jerome Emiliani, the Seven Holy Founders of the Servites, Gregory of Narek, Casimir, Turibius of Mogrovejo, Isidore of Seville, Adalbert of Prague and Fidelis of Sigmaringen. The card data is in `../batches/batch-11.json`. As in `first-batch.md`, each "Face:" line uses only marks with a source noted below it, and says where a mark is an artistic choice. Pictures and texts consulted are in `../consult/batch-11/` (Wikipedia extracts `wp-*.txt`, Catholic Encyclopedia extracts `ce-*.txt`, lead images `lead-*.jpg` with a contact sheet `leads-sheet.jpg`, face crops `faces-crop.jpg` and `crop-turibius.jpg`).

Excerpts: eight come from the collect of the feast's formulary. Ansgar (02-03) and Adalbert (04-23) have calendar entries but no formulary, so they take the Common: Pastors, for Missionaries (`past11.json`) and Martyrs, for One Missionary Martyr (`mart12.json`). Every line was checked against the JSON with a script; four en-US lines recast "that … we may" as a wish, named in `excerptSource`.

## Raymund of Penyafort

Face: a Catalan of about eighty, small shield-shaped face, short straight nose, small bright black eyes, bushy white brows, a long upper lip; white Dominican crown; a close-cropped white beard.

- The painting on Wikipedia (local `lead-Raymond_of_Penyafort.jpg`), which I checked: a tonsured old friar with a short grey beard, kneeling on the water, his black cappa rigged to a staff as a sail, a key in his hand, a lighthouse and coast behind. The card follows the beard, the sail, the staff and the key, standing him upright.
- Wikipedia (local `wp-Raymond_of_Penyafort.txt`): born near Barcelona c. 1175, died "at the age of 100" in Barcelona in 1275; the legend says he spread his cappa on the water, rigged it to his staff and sailed from Mallorca to Barcelona in six hours after the king forbade him to leave the island. Patron of canon lawyers (Wikipedia summary).
- The structure is our choice, set against Albert the Great (heavy, broad, clean-shaven) and Vincent Ferrer (full oval), the other Dominicans; the beard is kept short so he is not a white-bearded elder.
- Excerpt: 01-07.json collect, final clause.

## Ansgar

Face: a Frank of about sixty, clean-shaven, a broad square-browed face with a wide cleft chin, pale deep-set eyes, a short broad-bridged nose; silver-flaxen hair in a Carolingian fringe.

- Wikipedia (local `wp-Ansgar.txt`): born near Amiens of a noble Frankish family, raised at Corbie; Rimbert's Life says he "wore a rough hair shirt, lived on bread and water"; archbishop of Hamburg-Bremen, missionary to Denmark and to Birka in Sweden; died 865.
- Bendixen's painting (local `lead-Ansgar.jpg`), which I checked: a clean-shaven bishop holding a model of a church. The card keeps the clean-shaven face and the church, as a wooden church; the pallium is kept and the mitre left out for the ninth century.
- No likeness survives; the face is our choice, spare and weathered for his travels in the North.
- pt-BR name: "Santo Oscar", as in our calendar entry and the catalog.
- Excerpt: past11.json entrance antiphon (Common of Pastors, for Missionaries), first clause.

## Jerome Emiliani

Face: a Venetian of about fifty, a long oval face, balding domed forehead, long straight nose, large dark eyes lifted, brows sloping down at the ends; a full rounded dark-brown beard threaded with grey.

- Giandomenico Tiepolo's painting in Ca' Rezzonico (local `lead-Jerome_Emiliani.jpg`, `faces-crop.jpg`), which I checked: a balding man with a full dark beard looking up, his hand on an orphan boy's shoulder, holding a chain. The card follows it.
- Wikipedia (local `wp-Jerome_Emiliani.txt`): born in Venice, a soldier at the defence of Castelnuovo in 1508, then a magistrate; in the plague and famine of 1528 he cared for orphans; founded the Somascans with the motherhouse at Somasca; died there of the plague, 8 February 1537. Pius XI named him patron of orphans in 1928. He was never ordained, so the card gives him a plain black gown, not clerical vestments.
- The chain: the tradition of his release from captivity after the fall of Castelnuovo, as in Tiepolo's painting; shown broken, without the prison.
- Excerpt: 02-08.json collect, the address.

## The Seven Holy Founders

No portraits survive. The sources give the names (Wikipedia: Bonfilius Monaldi, Alexis Falconieri, Manettus dell'Antella, Amadeus of the Amidei, Hugh dei Lippi Uguccioni, Sostene, Bonajuncta), the vision of 1233 and the withdrawal "to Monte Senario, eleven miles north of Florence" (Catholic Encyclopedia, local `ce-servites.txt`), and a few facts that shape the faces: Alexis begged alms in the streets of Florence where he had lately been a prominent citizen and lived to about 110 (died 1310); his humility is the reason for his lowered eyes, and the missing tonsure follows the common tradition that he stayed a lay brother (not in the sources consulted); Manettus and Sostene governed the first provinces; Bonfilius was the first prior. Ages are set for the years around the order's approval (1249).

Faces (our choice, made different in shape and age, per the brief's "three in front, the rest behind"):

- Front: Alexis (45; long, narrow, hollow-cheeked, clean-shaven, no tonsure, eyes lowered); Bonfilius (55, centre; broad, square, heavy jaw, short rounded grey beard); Bonajuncta (35; round, plump, snub nose, short black beard).
- Behind: Amadeus (50; bald dome, long rectangular, clean-shaven); Manettus (45; heart-shaped, auburn beard); Hugh (35; angular, Roman nose, clean-shaven, curly black hair); Sostene (35; fair oval, light-blue eyes, thin moustache and chin tuft).

The black habit and the small picture of the sorrowing Virgin mark the order's devotion; Florence appears only as walls and towers, since the Duomo's dome is later. Excerpt: 02-17.json collect, the opening petition.

## Gregory of Narek

Face: an Armenian of about fifty, olive, a broad flat-planed oval face, a long high-bridged nose, very large level dark eyes under thick arched black brows; short black hair, a short dense black beard.

- The miniature in Matenadaran Ms. 1568, dated 1173 (Commons file description; local `lead-Gregory_of_Narek.jpg`, `faces-crop.jpg`), which I checked: short black hair cut round, very large eyes, a straight nose, a short black beard and moustache, a book in his hands, in a red mantle over blue. The card follows the face.
- Wikipedia (local `wp-Gregory_of_Narek.txt`): c. 950 – 1003/1011, a monk at Narekavank on the southern shore of Lake Van, son of a bishop of the Artsruni kin; author of the *Book of Lamentations*, "kept in Armenian homes". Hence the red-bound book of prayers.
- Dress: the miniature shows him bare-headed; the card puts him in the black robe and black pointed hood of Armenian celibate priests and monks so that he reads at once as Armenian. This is a choice of later Armenian monastic dress, not of the miniature.
- Excerpt: 02-27.json collect, the petition.

## Casimir

Face: a prince of twenty-four, a long, narrow, pale face, a long nose with a slightly drooping tip, heavy-lidded eyes, a small mouth; chestnut hair parted in the middle to the shoulders; clean-shaven.

- The painting of 1594 (local `lead-Saint_Casimir.jpg`, `faces-crop.jpg`), which I checked: a long face, a long nose, heavy lids, shoulder-length brown hair, a crowned red cap, an ermine-lined red robe, lilies and a rosary. The card follows it.
- Wikipedia (local `wp-Saint_Casimir.txt`): 1458–1484; "He is usually depicted as a young man in long red robe lined with stoat fur. Sometimes he wears a red cap of the Grand Duke of Lithuania"; patron of Lithuania and Lithuanian youth. Catholic Encyclopedia (local `ce-casimir.txt`): he knelt for hours at night before locked church doors.
- The beardless young face is what the sources give; the long, pale structure keeps him from Louis IX (long oval, forty) and Wenceslaus (broad Slavic).
- Excerpt: 03-04.json collect, the address.

## Turibius of Mogrovejo

Face: a Castilian of about sixty-two, clean-shaven, a long face broad at the jaw, a bald dome with a grey fringe, a long large straight nose, heavy-lidded pale eyes under high arched brows, a small tight mouth; weathered.

- The seventeenth-century portrait (local `lead-Turibius_of_Mogrovejo.jpg`, `crop-turibius.jpg`), which I checked: kneeling at an altar in a red cappa over a rochet; bald with a grey fringe, a long straight nose, heavy lids, arched brows, a small mouth; Andean people behind him. The card follows the face.
- Wikipedia (local `wp-Turibius_of_Mogrovejo.txt`): born 1538 at Mayorga, died 1606 at Saña; "Alone and on foot he traversed his entire archdiocese three times … baptising and confirming almost half a million"; a trilingual catechism in Spanish, Quechua and Aymara. Hence the confirmation of an Andean child. The patronage of the Latin American episcopate is commonly given but I did not verify it, so `patronOf` reads "Archbishop of Lima".
- Excerpt: 03-23.json collect, the petition.

## Isidore of Seville

Face: a Hispano-Roman of about seventy, a large, heavy, long face, a strong nose with a gentle hook, heavy-lidded eyes lowered to the page; a full rounded grey beard with a dark moustache.

- Murillo's portrait (local `lead-Isidore_of_Seville.jpg`), which I checked: an aged archbishop reading, with a grey beard, in a mitre and white cope. The card keeps the age, the beard and the book, and dresses him as a seventh-century bishop, bare-headed, in chasuble and pallium.
- Wikipedia and Catholic Encyclopedia (local `wp-Isidore_of_Seville.txt`, `ce-isidore.txt`): born at Cartagena, archbishop of Seville for more than thirty-two years, "the aged Archbishop Isidore" presided at the Fourth Council of Toledo in 633, died 4 April 636; author of the *Etymologies*. The legend of the bees is not in these sources and is left out.
- Excerpt: 04-04.json collect, final clause, as a wish.

## Adalbert of Prague

Face: a Czech of about forty, an angular high-cheekboned face with a pointed chin, blue-grey eyes, straight fair brows; ash-blond hair with a tonsure; a short fair beard ending in a point.

- Wikipedia (local `wp-Adalbert_of_Prague.txt`): born Vojtěch of the Slavník clan c. 952–956, bishop of Prague from 982, founder of Břevnov, killed by the Prussians in April 997; patron of the Czech lands, Poland and Prussia; the Gniezno bronze doors (c. 1175) show his life in eighteen reliefs.
- The lead painting (local `lead-Adalbert_of_Prague.jpg`), which I checked, shows a long white beard and a high mitre, a later convention for a man who died at about forty. The card does not follow it: the age is the historical one and the face is our choice.
- No martyrdom, no spears: the palm only, before the castle hill of Prague with an early round church.
- Excerpt: mart12.json entrance antiphon (Common, for One Missionary Martyr).

## Fidelis of Sigmaringen

Face: a Capuchin of about forty-four, a fine-boned narrow face, a narrow straight nose, gentle hazel eyes, delicate cheekbones; tonsure; a combed dark-brown beard to the chest.

- The Pfärrenbach wall painting (local `lead-Fidelis_of_Sigmaringen.jpg`), which I checked: a bearded Capuchin with a palm and a sword. The card keeps the habit, the dark beard and the palm and drops the sword, per the batch instruction.
- Wikipedia (local `wp-Fidelis_of_Sigmaringen.txt`): born Mark Rey in 1577, a doctor of law at Freiburg known as a modest, meek man; a Capuchin missionary in the Grisons; killed at Seewis im Prättigau on 24 April 1622. Hence the lawyer's fine face and the alpine village.
- Excerpt: 04-24.json collect, final clause.

## Look-alike risks that remain

- Fidelis and Lawrence of Brindisi: both Capuchins with long dark beards in brown habits; the faces differ (narrow and fine against broad-browed) and Lawrence holds a raised crucifix. Check on the face sheet.
- Fidelis and Josaphat (long, narrow, ascetic, dark beard, forty): different dress, but the faces may converge.
- Turibius and Pius V: both bald, clean-shaven, arched grey brows in their sixties; Turibius is broad at the jaw with a straight nose and weathered skin, Pius gaunt and aquiline.
- Gregory of Narek and Ephrem, John Damascene: all Eastern monks in dark robes; Gregory is broader-faced with a dense short black beard and the pointed hood.
- Ansgar and Bruno, Boniface: clean-shaven or northern missionary faces; the cleft chin, pale crinkled eyes and silver-flaxen fringe should carry him.
- Casimir and Louis IX: long pale royal faces; Casimir is younger, beardless with shoulder-length chestnut hair and the ermine robe.
- The Servites: seven faces on one card may collapse into a few; the central three are the ones to check first.
