# Batch 12 of new cards: faces

Faces, dress and settings for ten cards: Peter Chanel, John of Ávila, Nereus and Achilleus, John I, Gregory VII, Augustine of Canterbury, Marcellinus and Peter, Romuald, Paulinus of Nola and the First Martyrs of the Church of Rome. The card data is in `../batches/batch-12.json`. As in `first-batch.md`, each "Face:" line uses only marks with a source noted below it, and says where a mark is an artistic choice. Pictures and texts consulted are in `../consult/batch-12/` (Wikipedia lead images `*.jpg`, the Catholic Encyclopedia article on Gregory VII `catholic-encyclopedia-gregory-vii.html`, and `build.py`, which writes the JSON and checks every excerpt and catalog match).

Excerpts: nine come from the collect of the feast's formulary. Gregory VII (05-25) has a calendar entry but no formulary; his card takes his last words as quoted in the Catholic Encyclopedia (1909), with a faithful pt-BR rendering. `build.py` checks each fragment against the JSON or the saved page; recasts ("that … we may" as a wish) are named in `excerptSource`.

## Peter Chanel

Face: a Frenchman of about thirty-seven, clean-shaven, a soft square-jawed face with full cheeks, a straight rather strong nose, large heavy-lidded dark-brown eyes, softly arched dark brows, a dimple in the chin; thick dark-brown hair parted on one side with a wave over the forehead.

- The devotional portrait on Wikipedia (local `Peter_Chanel.jpg`), which I checked: a young clean-shaven priest with a full, gentle face, dark wavy hair parted on one side and covering the tops of the ears, a black soutane with a black cravat and a black cloak, a crucifix held in both hands. The card follows it.
- Wikipedia: born 1803, killed on Futuna on 28 April 1841 at 37 by Musumusu at the instigation of King Niuliki; "Protomartyr of Oceania". The brief asks for no violence: the card shows only the island, the palms and the lagoon.
- Character: Marist writers stress his "serene, gentle" spirit and "unfailing gentleness and kindness" (Marist Fathers Australia, reflection PDF), hence the mild gaze.
- Excerpt: 04-28.json collect, the address.

## John of Ávila

Face: a Castilian priest of about seventy, a long, gaunt face narrow at the jaw, a cropped grey fringe brushed forward, high cheekbones over hollow cheeks, large dark down-turned eyes under arched brows raised in the middle, a small thin mouth, a close-clipped grey beard following the jaw.

- The portrait attributed to El Greco, c. 1580, inscribed "M. IVAN D. AVILA" (local `John_of_Avila_elGreco.jpg`), which I checked: an old priest with a long, hollow-cheeked face, short cropped grey hair with a fringe, large, sad, heavy-lidded eyes, a short grey beard and moustache, a black cloak and a narrow white collar, the right hand laid on his breast. The card keeps the gesture and all these marks, dropping the dark shadows under the eyes.
- Wikipedia: born 1499 at Almodóvar del Campo, died 10 May 1569 at Montilla at seventy, "Apostle of Andalusia", of converso descent. Hence Montilla as the background.
- Not Peter Damian (craggy brow, longer beard) or Charbel (long white beard): the clipped beard and the El Greco fringe carry him.
- Excerpt: 05-10.json collect, the petition, verbatim.

## Nereus and Achilleus

Face: Nereus (left), about forty-five, a blocky, flat-topped head, heavy brow, deep-set dark eyes, a strong nose with a slight bend, wide square jaw, cropped black hair greying at the temples, dark stubble. Achilleus (right), about twenty-eight, clean-shaven, a long face with high rounded cheekbones and a squared chin, a thin straight nose, large light-brown eyes, tight light-brown curls.

- Damasus's epitaph (The Cult of Saints, E07153; summarised on Wikipedia): they "had enlisted in the army" and carried out a cruel office, then, "suddenly converted", fled the tyrant's camp and "flung away their shields, their decorations, and their bloody weapons", rejoicing to bear Christ's triumph. The card shows them laying down shield and sword, with palms, in soldiers' red tunics.
- The Catholic Encyclopedia (1911): among the ruins of their basilica in the Domitilla catacomb were two ciborium pillars carved with their beheading, one inscribed "Achilleus". The later Acts, which make them Domitilla's chamberlains, are legendary; the card follows Damasus.
- Rubens's altarpiece (local `Nereus_and_Achilleus.jpg`), which I checked: an older dark man and a younger man with palms on either side of Domitilla. No likeness survives: the pairing of an elder and a younger man follows Rubens, the structures are our choice.
- Background: the Via Ardeatina and the catacomb of Domitilla, where they were buried.
- Excerpt: 05-12.json collect, final petition.

## John I

Face: a Tuscan of about sixty-five, worn thin, a rounded-triangular face with a broad low forehead and wide flat temples tapering to a short narrow chin, a nose with a wide flat bridge, round pale-brown eyes drooping at the outer corners, thin grey brows raised in patient sorrow, a small soft mouth, a grey tonsure fringe and a short grey beard trimmed to a point.

- Wikipedia: born in Tuscany; sent by Theodoric to Constantinople in 526 to plead for the Arians; on his return imprisoned at Ravenna, where he died "of neglect and ill treatment" on 18 May 526. "Depicted in art as looking through the bars of a prison." Hence the barred window and Ravenna with Theodoric's mausoleum (built about 520) behind.
- The reliquary bust at Coimbra (local `Pope_John_I.jpg`), which I checked: a baroque bust with a tiara and a short dark beard, a conventional face. We keep only the short beard; the card drops the tiara, which no sixth-century pope wore, for the paenula and pallium of his time.
- The structure is our choice, set against the other popes: Martin I (emaciated, long, narrow), Sylvester (long, tapering, huge eyes), Callistus (heavy, rectangular), Linus (hexagonal), Fabian (clean-shaven, fine-boned).
- Excerpt: 05-18.json collect, final clause.

## Gregory VII

Face: a small, spare Tuscan of about sixty-five with an olive complexion, a short compact face broad across brow and cheekbones with a firm, jutting small chin, a short beak-like nose, small black piercing eyes under straight bristling brows, a thin tight-set mouth, a very short iron-grey beard.

- Appearance: Will Durant, *The Age of Faith* (1950), summing up the chroniclers: "He was small of stature, homely of feature, keen of eye, proud of spirit, strong of will." A web summary of the sources adds that Hugh of Cluny remarked his small stature, the hostile Benzo called him a "homuncio", and an annalist noted his swarthiness; I did not see those texts directly. The card keeps small, swarthy and keen-eyed and idealises "homely" into a hard, compact, determined face.
- The Exultet roll of southern Italy, c. 1087, made in or near his lifetime (local `Pope_Gregory_VII.jpg`), which I checked: an enthroned pope in a tall conical tiara, a red chasuble and a jewelled pallium, blessing. The card takes the conical tiara, which sets him apart from every other pope card.
- Catholic Encyclopedia (1909, local copy): he withdrew "to the castle of Salerno by the sea, where he died"; "His last words were: 'I have loved justice and hated iniquity, therefore I die in exile.'" That line is the excerpt (no formulary in the repo). Background: Canossa, where Henry IV stood penitent in the snow in January 1077 (Wikipedia); the card shows only the castle on its rock, no figures.
- Distinct from Gregory the Great (bald crown, curled side-hair, tawny beard) and from the tall, long-faced popes.

## Augustine of Canterbury

Face: a Roman monk of about fifty-five, a slender, finely boned long face with gently hollowed cheeks, a narrow high-bridged nose, large grave dark-brown eyes under long level brows, a small firm mouth, a greying monastic crown, a short square-cut dark beard streaked with grey.

- Bede, *Ecclesiastical History* I.25 (Medieval Sourcebook): the missionaries met Æthelberht on Thanet "bearing a silver cross for their banner, and the image of our Lord and Saviour painted on a board; and singing the litany". The card gives him both, and Thanet's shore behind.
- Wikipedia: prior of St Andrew's monastery on the Caelian in Rome; died probably 26 May 604; no description of his appearance survives. The face is our choice.
- Not Augustine of Hippo (olive Berber complexion, close-cropped hair, short dark round beard, forty-five): Canterbury is slenderer and older, with a tonsure, a square-cut greying beard and the black habit.
- Excerpt: 05-27.json collect, final clause.

## Marcellinus and Peter

Face: Marcellinus (left), a priest of about fifty-five, a wide, low-browed face with flat cheekbones and a rounded chin, a short straight nose, small warm deep-set eyes, a grey-brown tonsure, a short curly pepper-and-salt beard. Peter (right), an exorcist of about thirty, a narrow fine-boned face with a pointed chin, a thin nose with a slight hook, large dark intense eyes under straight black brows, short black hair and a sparse short black beard.

- Wikipedia: Marcellinus a priest, Peter an exorcist, beheaded about 304 under Diocletian; Damasus "heard the story of these two martyrs from their executioner"; buried on the Via Labicana "at the two laurel trees" at the third mile. A fourth-century fresco in their catacomb shows them "with short beards"; later art gives them tonsures and palms. The short beards follow the fresco; the contrast in age and structure is our choice.
- The Seligenstadt statue (local `Marcellinus_and_Peter.jpg`), which I checked: Marcellinus as a priest in a chasuble holding a chalice and a palm. The card gives him the chalice and Peter the exorcist's book.
- Excerpt: 06-02.json collect, the petition (the pt-BR address has a typo, "Deus1", so the card uses the petition).

## Romuald

Face: an old hermit of about seventy-five, a long face under a massive bald dome, a strongly furrowed forehead, white brows knit downward over deep-set penetrating grey eyes, a long, slightly hooked nose, sunken cheeks, a long wavy white beard in strands to mid-chest.

- Fra Angelico's fresco at San Marco, Florence (local `Romuald.jpg`), which I checked: a bald dome, a long white beard, brows knit, a severe, searching look, a long nose, the white Camaldolese cowl. The card follows it.
- He died in 1027, very old; he founded the hermitage of Camaldoli in the Casentino. His vision of a ladder with monks in white climbing to heaven is the subject of Andrea Sacchi's *Vision of St Romuald*; the card sets it faintly in the sky over the cells and firs.
- The sources demand a white-bearded elder. The knit brows, the hooked nose and the severity keep him from Anthony Abbot (triangular, crow's-feet), Charbel (narrow, gentle), Francis of Paola (broad, square) and Matthew (long straight hair).
- Excerpt: 06-19.json collect, final clause.

## Paulinus of Nola

Face: a Gallo-Roman aristocrat of about seventy, clean-shaven, a fine-boned heart-shaped face, a high-bridged patrician nose, large mild light hazel eyes with crow's-feet, thin arched silver brows, a gentle full-lipped smile, short white hair in a Roman fringe.

- Wikipedia: born about 354 at Bordeaux of a senatorial family, suffect consul and governor of Campania; he and his wife Therasia gave away their wealth after their only child died; bishop of Nola from about 410 until his death on 22 June 431 at about seventy-seven; rebuilt the shrine of St Felix at Cimitile with great porticoes and fountains. Tradition credits him with introducing church bells and tells that he sold himself into slavery to ransom a captive, hence the bell and the broken chain.
- The Linz Cathedral window (local `Paulinus_of_Nola.jpg`), which I checked: a nineteenth-century bishop in a mitre holding a chain, with a crozier; the face is generic. The card keeps the chain and drops the mitre, anachronistic for 431, for the paenula and stole of the time.
- No likeness survives. Clean-shaven is the fashion of a fourth-century Roman senator; the gentle poet's face is our choice, set against Albert the Great (heavy, broad, jowled) and John XXIII (round), the other clean-shaven old men.
- Excerpt: 06-22.json collect, final clause.

## The First Martyrs of the Church of Rome

Faces: the freedman (left), a Syrian of about fifty-five, a long bony olive face, a gently hooked nose, heavy-lidded dark eyes, a wide thin mouth, short grey hair and a short neat grey beard. The matron (centre), about thirty, a wide-cheeked, softly square face, straight nose, large dark calm eyes under strong brows, full lips, dark-brown hair parted under the palla. The youth (right), a Greek boy of about sixteen, a round face, short straight nose, bright black eyes, short tight black curls.

- Tacitus, *Annals* 15.44 (Church and Brodribb, Wikisource): after the fire of 64 Nero blamed the Christians; "covered with the skins of beasts, they were torn by dogs and perished, or were nailed to crosses, or were doomed to the flames and burnt, to serve as a nightly illumination"; he offered his gardens for the spectacle and exhibited in the circus. None of that is shown: the card gives only the setting, the gardens and the Circus of Gaius and Nero with the obelisk that now stands before St Peter's, with a haze over the city after the fire.
- The three faces are our choice: a freedman from the East, a Roman matron and a Greek household servant, the kinds of people Paul greets in Romans 16. The brief asks for three figures in front, modest; a few smaller faithful stand behind.
- Excerpt: 06-30.json collect, the address.

## Look-alike risks that remain

- Romuald and Charbel, Anthony Abbot, Francis of Paola: all old hermits with long white beards. Romuald's knit brows, hooked nose and massive bald dome, and his white cowl, have to carry him; check on the face sheet.
- John of Ávila and Peter Damian, Fabian: long, gaunt old faces. Ávila's clipped beard, cropped fringe and El Greco hand on the breast should hold.
- Gregory VII and Callistus, Linus: short, broad popes' faces. The conical tiara, olive colouring and piercing eyes are the distinguishing marks; if the model makes him broad and heavy, redo with "small and spare".
- John I and Sixtus II, Martin I: sixth- and third-century popes in paenula and pallium with grey beards. John I's drooping eyes and short narrow chin are subtle; the barred window and Ravenna help.
- Augustine of Canterbury and Bruno, Norbert, Josaphat: long, fine monastic faces. The square-cut beard and the silver cross should carry him.
- Peter Chanel and John Bosco, Vincent of Saragossa: clean-shaven square or full faces. Chanel's side-parted wave and his youth separate him from Bosco; Vincent wears a dalmatic.
- Nereus and Achilleus and John and Paul, Cosmas and Damian: paired Roman martyrs. The soldiers' red tunics and laid-down arms set them apart; Achilleus must not drift into Sebastian (a reference card).
- Marcellinus's Peter and the young long-haired Christ type: his hair is specified short and his beard sparse; reject a draft that drifts.
- The First Martyrs' youth and Pancras (fourteen, round face): both round-faced Roman boys; the youth is older, with tight black curls and a servant's tunic.
