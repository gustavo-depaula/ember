# Batch 9 of new cards: faces

Faces, dress and settings for ten saints of October and November: Bruno, Denis, Hedwig, the North American Martyrs, Paul of the Cross, John of Capistrano, Anthony Mary Claret, Albert the Great, Margaret of Scotland and Gertrude. The card data is in `../batches/batch-9.json`. As in `first-batch.md`, each "Face:" line uses only marks with a source quoted below it, and says so where a mark is an artistic choice. Pictures and texts consulted are in `../consult/batch-9/`.

Excerpts: eight come from the collect of the feast's formulary, checked against the JSON with a script (the verbatim parts are named in `excerptSource`). Paul of the Cross (10-19/paul) and Gertrude (11-16/gertrude) have calendar entries but no formulary in the repo; both died long before 1930, so their cards carry a short saying from a public-domain edition, with the pt-BR line our rendering.

Structure across the batch, so no two faces share a skeleton: Bruno long and rectangular; Denis broad, short and square; Hedwig long oval with high cheekbones; Brébeuf large and strong-boned, Jogues narrow oval; Paul long oval with a high, wide forehead; Capistrano narrow, bony, triangular; Claret large and round; Albert broad, heavy and jowled; Margaret softly square; Gertrude narrow and fine-featured.

## Bruno

Face: a clean-shaven man of about fifty with a long, rectangular face, strong low brows over deep-set grey eyes, a thin long nose, flat lean cheeks, a squared chin, the head shaven to the Carthusian ring of short dark-brown hair just greying; eyes lowered.

- Christian Iconography: "In images St. Bruno will be seen tonsured and in the habit of the Carthusian order … In most cases there is no attribute, but some paintings place a bishop's mitre on the ground beside him, and some identify him by a halo of golden stars." The stars are the seven stars of St Hugh's dream over the Chartreuse (local `ci-bruno.html`).
- Houdon's plaster Saint Bruno, 1766 (local `bruno-houdon.jpg`), which I checked: shaven head, clean-shaven, long lean face, arms crossed on the breast, head bowed. The card takes the pose and the long face.
- Mignard's Saint Bruno in prayer (local `lead-Bruno_of_Cologne.jpg`): young, clean-shaven, white hooded cowl, ecstatic upward gaze. Not followed: the upward gaze and youth would repeat aloysius_gonzaga's mood.
- Age: born c. 1030–35, came to the Chartreuse in 1084 (formulary description: "Born at Cologne about the year 1035 … founded the monastery of the Carthusians").
- Stars: kept small and faint in the sky above the peaks, not as a halo.
- Excerpt: collect of 10-06.json, "that amid the changes of this world we may constantly look to you alone" / "em meio à agitação deste mundo, nos dediquemos inteiramente ao vosso serviço".

## Denis and Companions

Face: a man of about sixty with a broad, short, square face, wide cheekbones and jaw, a short broad nose, round wide-set dark eyes under heavy arched brows, a full mouth; thick, tightly curled pepper-and-salt hair and a full, rounded, curly beard.

- No likeness exists. The statue on the Portal of the Virgin at Notre-Dame (local `lead-Denis_of_Paris.jpg`), which I checked: thick short curls, a short curled beard, a broad calm face, a mitre. He holds that head in his hands; the card keeps the head on the shoulders and takes only the curls and the breadth.
- Christian Iconography: "Portraits have him dressed as a bishop and holding his head in his hands" (local `ci-denis.html`). Deliberately not followed, per the batch instruction.
- Attributes on the card: bishop's vestments of an early, plain cut (a bell chasuble, a low mitre), a Gospel book and a martyr's palm; red for martyrdom.
- Background: Montmartre, the hill of the martyrs, over the Seine and the small Roman town of Lutetia. The companions (Rusticus and Eleutherius) are in the name only; a three-figure card would crowd the window.
- Excerpt: opening of the 10-09.json collect, "O God, who" / "Ó Deus, que" dropped.

## Hedwig

Face: a tall woman of about sixty, long oval face, high cheekbones over softly hollowed cheeks, a long straight narrow nose, large heavy-lidded brown eyes under thin arched brows, a small firm mouth, fine lines at the eyes; wimple and veil.

- The Hedwig Codex, 1353 (Getty; local `lead-Hedwig_of_Silesia.jpg`), which I checked: a tall figure in a white veil and wimple and a grey mantle, holding a small statuette of the Virgin and Child to her breast, a pair of shoes and a book with a string of beads in the other hand, with the duke and duchess kneeling as donors. The card follows it closely, minus the book.
- Wikipedia: attributes "holding a church or a pair of shoes in her hands"; "she went barefoot even in winter, and when she was urged by the Bishop of Wrocław to wear shoes, she carried them in her hands"; buried at Trzebnica, the Cistercian abbey she founded (local `wp-Hedwig_of_Silesia.txt`).
- Christian Iconography (Padua): she "wears a more modest crown, as Duchess of Silesia" — hence the thin circlet over the veil (local `ci-hedwig-padua.html`).
- Age: 1174–1243; widowed 1238. The face is an artistic choice for a widow near sixty, long and quiet like the Codex figure.
- Patron line: in Brazil Santa Edviges is the "padroeira dos pobres e endividados", the reason she is well known there.
- Excerpt: collect of 10-16.json; pt-BR "cuja vida" becomes "Sua vida".

## John de Brébeuf and Isaac Jogues

Face (Brébeuf): a tall, powerful man of about fifty-five with a large, long, strong-boned face, a bald dome with a grey fringe, a big straight nose, deep-set grey eyes under heavy brows, broad cheekbones, a clipped salt-and-pepper beard cut to a blunt point.

Face (Jogues): a slighter man of about thirty-nine with a narrow oval face, dark-brown hair receding high, gently arched brows, soft lowered brown eyes, a thin straight nose, a short dark pointed beard.

- Parkman, *The Jesuits in North America* (local `parkman-flat.txt`): "a tall, strong man, with features that seemed carved by Nature for a soldier, but which the mental habits of years had stamped with the visible impress of the priesthood. This was Jean de Brébeuf"; "the Ajax of the mission". Of Jogues: "Nature had given him no especial force of intellect or constitutional energy, yet the man was indomitable".
- Brébeuf's traditional portrait (local `lead-Jean_de_Brébeuf.jpg`), which I checked: bald crown, short grey pointed beard and moustache, long strong face, black soutane and cloak.
- Jogues, portrait by Donald Guthrie McNab, 1895 (local `lead-Isaac_Jogues.jpg`), which I checked: receding hair, lowered eyes, a short dark beard, hands joined — with a red wound on the head. The card keeps the face and leaves out the wound, and hides his mutilated fingers in his sleeves.
- Ages: Jogues 1607–1646, Brébeuf 1593–1649.
- Background: Sainte-Marie among the Hurons on Georgian Bay, Brébeuf's mission; no fire and no torture.
- Name: en-US "Sts. John de Brébeuf and Isaac Jogues" (the companions are in the feast title and the collect), initial J.
- Excerpt: the 10-19.json collect's last clause; the en-US line drops "through their intercession".

## Paul of the Cross

Face: a tall, slender, clean-shaven man of about seventy with a long oval face, a high, wide forehead, a long nose with a gently rounded drooping tip, calm grey eyes with heavy lids lowered, softly hollowed cheeks, a small rounded chin; thin grey hair.

- Fr. Pius a Spiritu Sancto, *The Life of St. Paul of the Cross* (Sadlier, 1886; local `paul-lifeofstpaulofcr0000puis.txt`): "He was tall of stature and of slender build, had a placid modest countenance, clear calm eyes, an elevated and wide forehead, a distinct, sonorous, and penetrating voice. His appearance struck one as grave and majestic".
- The traditional portrait (local `lead-Paul_of_the_Cross.jpg`), which I checked: an old clean-shaven man with grey hair, a long face and long nose, eyes lowered, clasping a wooden crucifix, in the black habit with the heart badge lettered "JESU XPI PASSIO". The card keeps the crucifix and the pose; the badge is a plain white heart and cross, without lettering.
- Age: 1694–1775. Seventy rather than his full eighty, to keep him apart from the stock elder.
- Background: Monte Argentario, where he founded the first retreat (the Presentation), above the Tyrrhenian.
- Excerpt: *Flowers of the Passion: Thoughts of St. Paul of the Cross gathered from the letters of the Saint*, ed. Louis-Thérèse de Jésus-Agonisant, tr. Ella A. Mulligan (Benziger, 1893), "The Passion and Prayer", p. 65: "If you cannot spend much time at prayer, no matter: to act well is always to pray well." (local `paul-flowers-flat.txt`). The card has the last clause. It is a translation of a French anthology of his Italian letters, so the wording is Mulligan's, not his.

## John of Capistrano

Face: a small, lean man of about seventy, clean-shaven, with a narrow, bony, triangular face, a domed bald head with a small white tuft, prominent cheekbones over sunken cheeks, a long thin slightly hooked nose, small deep-set lively dark eyes, a small pointed chin; cheerful and alert.

- Bartolomeo Vivarini, *St John of Capistrano*, 1459, three years after his death (Louvre MI 488; local `capistrano-vivarini-1459.jpg`), which I checked: an old, bald, clean-shaven, hollow-cheeked friar with a small tuft of white hair, in a grey habit and cord, holding a white banner with a red cross and a book.
- A contemporary: "A man small of body, withered, emaciated, nothing but skin and bone, but cheerful, strong and strenuous in labor" (quoted at https://archiv.katolikus.hu/hun-saints/john.html). The card idealises this to lean and bright.
- Christian Iconography: "In one he stands as if victorious holding a cross or crucifix in one hand and in the other a cross-topped banner … Images of this type will often show a fallen soldier at the saint's feet" (local `ci-johnCapistrano.html`). The card takes the banner and cross and leaves out the soldier and the battle.
- Dress: grey-brown Observant habit, as Vivarini paints it; greyer than francis_assisi and anthony_padua.
- Age: 1386–1456; seventy at Belgrade.
- Excerpt: last clause of the 10-23.json collect, verbatim in both languages.

## Anthony Mary Claret

Face: a stocky, clean-shaven Spaniard of about forty-five with a large, round, fleshy face, olive-dark skin, a short broad nose, full cheeks, small dark penetrating eyes under thick straight black brows, a full lower lip, a heavy rounded chin; short black hair receding at the temples.

- Photograph c. 1860 (Commons `Antonio María Claret, c.1860.jpg`; local `lead-Anthony_Mary_Claret.jpg`), which I checked: seated in cassock and mozzetta with a pectoral cross and zucchetto; a large, round, heavy face, thick dark brows, dark eyes, full lips, short dark hair receding. There is also a Jean Laurent photograph (Commons `Padre Claret, de Jean Laurent.jpg`), not downloaded.
- Age and place: archbishop of Santiago de Cuba 1850–1857 (aged 42–49; Wikipedia, local `wp-Anthony_Mary_Claret.txt`). The card sets him there, with a book (he wrote and printed popular devotional books) and a rosary.
- Excerpt: 10-24.json collect petition, recast from "we may earnestly devote ourselves…" to "May we…"; pt-BR verbatim from "nos apliquemos".

## Albert the Great

Face: an old man of about seventy-five, clean-shaven, with a large, heavy, broad face, a wide square jowled jaw, a big slightly fleshy nose, small keen heavy-lidded grey eyes under low straight white brows, a wide thin firm mouth; white tonsure ring under the mitre.

- Tommaso da Modena, fresco in the chapter house of San Niccolò, Treviso, 1352 (local `albert-tommaso-1352.png`), which I checked: a clean-shaven old bishop with a heavy, broad jaw, a strong nose, a thin, firm mouth, a jewelled white mitre over the Dominican black-and-white habit, an open book. The card keeps this face.
- The Spanish Baroque painting in the Wikipedia lead (local `lead-Albertus_Magnus.jpg`) gives him a full white beard; not followed, because it is late and would make the stock white-bearded elder.
- Christian Iconography (Murano fresco): "identified here and in other images by a book, a black-over-white Dominican habit, and a mitre and crozier" (local `ci-albert-venice.html`).
- Distinct from thomas_aquinas (young, round, Dominican) by the mitre, the age and the heavy jaw.
- Setting: Cologne, where he taught and died (1280); the cathedral choir was begun in 1248, in his lifetime.
- Excerpt: end of the 11-15.json collect petition, verbatim in both languages.

## Margaret of Scotland

Face: a woman of about forty-five with a gentle, broad-browed, softly square face, wide-set clear blue eyes under light straight brows, a short straight nose, fair rosy full cheeks, a wide gentle mouth, a rounded firm chin; golden-blonde plaits under the veil.

- The genealogical roll (13th c.; local `lead-Margaret_of_Scotland.jpg`), which I checked: crowned over a white veil and wimple. Only the dress is usable.
- St Giles' Cathedral window (local `margaret-stgiles-window.jpg`), which I checked: long golden hair, a crown, a book at her side. The golden hair comes from this later tradition; the face is an artistic choice.
- Wikipedia: Malcolm "so admired her piety that he had her books decorated in gold and silver. One of these, a pocket gospel book with portraits of the Evangelists, is in the Bodleian Library" (local `wp-Saint_Margaret_of_Scotland.txt`). That is her attribute on the card, in place of the loaves, which elizabeth_hungary already has.
- Age: c. 1045–1093; forty-five near the end of her reign.
- Setting: St Margaret's Chapel on the castle rock of Edinburgh, and the Queen's Ferry over the Forth, named for her.
- Excerpt: 11-16.json collect, en-US recast as a wish ("May we reflect…"), pt-BR verbatim from "concedei-nos".

## Gertrude the Great

Face: a woman of about forty with a pale, fine-featured, narrow face, a slender slightly aquiline nose, large luminous grey eyes under fine arched brows, delicate cheekbones, a small curved mouth, a narrow rounded chin; eyes lifted a little.

- Christian Iconography: she "is portrayed with a burning or shining heart on her breast. The heart is often embellished by a small figure of the Christ Child"; the crozier belongs to her abbess, Gertrude of Hackeborn, with whom she is confused (local `ci-gertrude.html`).
- Wikipedia: "Images of Gertrude show her clothed as a nun with the ample sleeves typical of the cowl. In the Baroque period she was depicted wearing a Benedictine habit, though this detail is not historically certain." (local `wp-Gertrude_the_Great.txt`).
- The Venetian altarpiece (local `lead-Gertrude_the_Great.jpg`), which I checked: a nun in black with a white wimple, a pale, narrow face, lifted by angels. Nothing is known of her looks; the face is an artistic choice for a nun of forty (1256–1302).
- Distinct from margaret_mary (Sacred Heart, Visitation habit): black Benedictine cowl, the Child within the heart, the quill and book of the writer of the *Herald*.
- Name: calendar "Saint Gertrude, virgin" / "Santa Gertrudes, virgem"; the card uses "St. Gertrude the Great" / "Santa Gertrudes Magna" to tell her from Gertrude of Nivelles. Catalog match "Saint Gertrude" is unique among unticked lines.
- Excerpt: *The Herald of Divine Love* II.5, in *The Life and Revelations of Saint Gertrude* (Burns & Oates / Benziger, 1870s), p. 83: "I beseech Thee, by the merits and prayers of all here present, to pierce my heart with the arrow of Thy love." (local `gertrude-thelifeandrevela00gertuoft-flat.txt`). Her own words, in the first person.

## Look-alike risks that remain

- **Paul of the Cross and alphonsus_liguori**: both old, clean-shaven, 18th-century Italian founders in black. Paul is tall and long-faced with a high forehead and no hat, head bowed over a crucifix; the heart badge must come through clearly.
- **Bruno and john_of_the_cross / bernard_clairvaux**: tonsured, clean-shaven monks. Bruno's all-white Carthusian cowl and mountain valley separate him from Bernard's white Cistercian habit; check the face is rectangular and fifty, not young.
- **Albert and thomas_aquinas**: same habit. Albert must read old and heavy-jawed under a mitre.
- **Capistrano and francis_assisi / anthony_padua**: three Franciscans. Capistrano's age, baldness and grey habit, and the banner, should carry it.
- **Denis**: the model may still try to put his head in his hands. Reject any draft that does.
- **Claret and charles_borromeo**: both prelates; Charles is gaunt with a hooked nose, Claret round and heavy.
- **Hedwig and bridget_sweden / monica**: veiled older women. The ducal circlet, statuette and shoes carry Hedwig.
- **Gertrude and margaret_mary**: the glowing heart. The Child in it, the black cowl and the quill must show.
- **Jogues**: the model may add his head wound or mutilated hands from the McNab portrait; keep both hidden.
