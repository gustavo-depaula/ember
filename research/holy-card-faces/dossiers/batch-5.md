# Batch 5 of new cards: faces

Faces, dress and settings for ten saints: the five of Brazil's own calendar (Anchieta, Benedito, Frei Galvão, Inácio de Azevedo and companions, the martyrs of Cunhaú and Uruaçu) and five of the general calendar (Blase, Vincent, Fabian, Angela Merici, Frances of Rome). The card data is in `../batches/batch-5.json`. As in `first-batch.md`, each "Face:" line uses only marks with a source quoted below it, and says so where a mark is an artistic choice. Pictures and texts consulted are in `../consult/batch-5/`.

Excerpts: the Brazilian formularies (`content/of/formularies/sanctoral/MM-DD/brazil.json`) have pt-BR only. For those cards the pt-BR line is verbatim and the en-US line is our faithful rendering; `excerptSource` says so.

## Brazil's own calendar

## José de Anchieta

Face: a lean man of about thirty, slight and a little stooped, with an olive, sun-tanned complexion, short straight dark-brown hair, a broad forehead, a long straight nose, a sparse, thin dark beard along the jaw, and blue-grey eyes lowered to the sand with a cheerful, kindly half-smile.

- Simão de Vasconcelos, the only written portrait: "Foi o Padre José de Anchieta de estatura medíocre, diminuto em carnes, em rigor do espírito robusto e atuoso, em cor trigueiro, os olhos parte azulados, testa larga, nariz comprido, barba rara, mas no semblante inteiro, alegre e amável." Quoted by https://www.anchieta.es.gov.br/noticias/82241/voce-sabe-quem--foi-jose-de-anchieta. The early-edition opening is in the local crop `anchieta-vasconcelos1663-opening-crop-4.png`.
- The stoop: "Desde jovem, Anchieta padecia de tuberculose óssea, que lhe causou uma escoliose" (https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_Anchieta). Render it only as a slight forward bend over the sand.
- Age: born 1534. During the 1563 peace mission to the Tamoios at Iperoig he was 29, a hostage, and composed the *De Beata Virgine Dei Matre Maria*, traditionally written in the sand of the beach. The opening line is "Eloquar? an sileam, sanctissima Mater Iesu?" (Joaquim Ribeiro, *Anchieta e o poema da Virgem*, local copy `anchieta-ribeiro-poema-virgem.pdf`).
- Benedito Calixto, portrait (1902, Museu Paulista; local copy `anchieta-calixto-1902-museu-paulista.jpg`), which I checked: an old Anchieta, clean-shaven, with white hair, in a black soutane with a crucifix and rosary, holding a book and a staff on a beach with gulls and a mountain behind. Calixto's *Poema à Virgem* (1901, local copy `anchieta-calixto-1901-poema-virgem.jpg`): a small black-robed figure bent over a staff, writing lines of verse in the sand of a long beach at dawn, with gulls and a wooden cross on the dunes. The card takes Calixto's scene and dress but Vasconcelos's face at 29. Calixto's white-haired old man would read as the Curé of Ars.
- Dress and attributes: the black Jesuit soutane with a sash, a small crucifix, a staff to write with, and a book.
- Background: the beach of Iperoig (Ubatuba) with the Serra do Mar behind.
- Excerpt: the collect of 06-09/brazil.json, "a fim de que, a exemplo do Bem-aventurado José de Anchieta, apóstolo do Brasil, sirvamos fielmente o Evangelho, tornando-nos tudo para todos…". The collect still calls him *Bem-aventurado*. He was canonised in 2014, so the card says *São*.

## Benedito (Benedict the Moor)

Face: an Afro-Sicilian man of about sixty with deep ebony-brown skin, a round, full face, close-cropped tightly curled black hair greying at the temples around a small Franciscan tonsure, clean-shaven, full lips, and dark eyes lowered to the roses with a broad, warm smile.

- Wikipedia: born 1526 at San Fratello to enslaved Africans, died in Palermo in 1589. He was called "the Black", "the African", "the Moor", *Æthiops* and *Niger*. He was cook, novice master and guardian (superior) though a lay brother, and "returned to kitchen duty in his later years". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_the_Moor
- Image types, Vitruvius (arquitextos 238.04): the Italian model shows him in the Franciscan habit "segurando o menino Jesus nos braços"; the Portuguese model has "flores na mão esquerda, fazendo alusão ao 'milagre dos pães'" (loaves for the poor, hidden in his habit, turned into flowers). https://vitruvius.com.br/revistas/read/arquitextos/20.238/7662
- Age and fuller face: an artistic choice. He died at about 63 and returned to the kitchen in his last years; the older, rounder, smiling face keeps him apart from martin_de_porres (batch 4: a slender, clean-shaven Afro-Peruvian lay brother of forty-five with a lowered, quiet face).
- Pictures checked: the polychrome statue attributed to José Montes de Oca, c. 1734 (NGA; local copy `benedito-nga.jpg`): beardless, close-cropped curly black hair, a young-looking slender face, a gilded habit, an open book. The bronze statue in Brazil (local copy `benedito-estatua-commons.jpg`): beardless, with a thick ring of curled hair, holding the Child. Both are clean-shaven, and the card follows them.
- Why not the Child: anthony_padua is already a Franciscan in a brown habit holding the Child, and elizabeth_hungary already has loaves and roses in her apron. Benedito keeps the Portuguese type (roses in the fold of his habit) and differs from Elizabeth by the habit, the setting and the missing bread.
- Dress: the brown Franciscan habit with a hood and white cord, as Brazilian images show.
- Background: the friary of Santa Maria di Gesù at Palermo, where his cell is still shown, with Monte Pellegrino and the bay.
- Excerpt: the entrance antiphon of 10-05/brazil.json, "Humilhou-se em tudo e perante Deus encontrou misericórdia; pois grande é o poder de Deus e pelos humildes ele é honrado." The data cites "Lc 1, 28", which looks wrong: the wording is close to Sir 3:18–20. Only the first clause is on the card.

## Frei Galvão (Antônio de Sant'Anna Galvão)

Face: a sturdy man of about sixty, clean-shaven, with a full, round, gentle face, a thick crown of short greying dark hair around the Franciscan tonsure, dark eyebrows, dark kindly eyes and a warm, quiet smile.

- The portrait by an unidentified painter, c. 1850 (Commons `File:Anônimo - Frei Galvão.jpg`; local copies `galvao-anonimo-commons.jpg`, `galvao-freigalvao-commons.jpg`), which I checked: clean-shaven, a full, rounded face, a thick crown of dark hair, dark eyes, a slight smile, a dark habit with a white cord, seated by a table. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:An%C3%B4nimo_-_Frei_Galv%C3%A3o.jpg. Also `galvao-mosteiro-luz-1875.jpg`, the Mosteiro da Luz in 1875.
- Age: 1739–1822. The Mosteiro da Luz was built from 1774 and the pills date from his mature years. The portrait's face is middle-aged; the card greys the hair a little.
- The pills: he wrote on a slip of paper "Post partum Virgo, inviolata permansisti: Dei Genitrix intercede pro nobis" (a versicle of the Marian office), rolled it like a pill and gave it to a sick man with kidney stones. The Concepcionist sisters of the Mosteiro da Luz still make and give them away. https://museuartesacra.org.br/acervo/frei-galvao-e-as-pilulas/
- Dress: the portrait's habit is dark, nearly black-brown. The card uses a dark greyish-brown so that he does not read as anthony_padua or francis_assisi (both warm brown).
- Background: the Mosteiro da Luz, São Paulo, which he designed and built and where he is buried.
- Name: officially *Santo Antônio de Sant'Anna Galvão*; in Brazil he is universally *Frei Galvão*, and his church is the "Igreja de São Frei Galvão". pt-BR "São Frei Galvão"; en-US "St. Anthony Galvão", so the initial is A.
- Excerpt: the communion antiphon of 10-25/brazil.json (Mt 20:28), first clause.

## Inácio de Azevedo and companions (the Forty Martyrs of Brazil)

Face (Inácio): a nobleman of about forty-four, with a long, lean face, a high forehead with receding dark hair, a full dark-brown beard of medium length touched with grey, and dark earnest eyes raised toward the icon.

Face (companion): a slender boy of about seventeen, beardless, with short fair-brown hair and a fresh, ardent face.

- *Via Spiritus* 15 (2008), "Aspectos de devoção e iconografia dos Quarenta Mártires do Brasil" (local copies `azevedo-viaspiritus-2008.pdf`/`.txt`): "Inácio de Azevedo sozinho ou acompanhado pelos restantes companheiros de martírio e vestidos com a tradicional sotaina usada pelos jesuítas, segura a imagem de Nossa Senhora de S. Lucas … Em todas as imagens que conhecemos, Inácio de Azevedo tem sempre barba para mostrar a sua meia-idade à data da morte em comparação com a extrema juventude de quase todos os seus companheiros. Um barco é, para além do retrato da Madonna di San Luca, o outro atributo iconográfico praticamente obrigatório." https://ler.letras.up.pt/uploads/ficheiros/8299.pdf
- The same article: "o jovem mártir António Correia (1553-1570)", venerated in Porto. He is the model for the companion.
- Pictures checked: the oval portrait on Commons (local copy `azevedo-commons-inaciodeazevedo.jpg`): a full dark beard, dark hair, a black soutane, holding the framed Madonna, with the sea behind. The agent also saved `azevedo-braga-17c.jpg`, `azevedo-kusel-1675.png` and `azevedo-rijksmuseum-print.jpg` (17th-century prints).
- Facts from 07-17/brazil.json: born in Porto in 1526 "de família ilustre", martyred at sea off the Canaries on 15 July 1570 with 39 companions, beatified in 1854. The ship was the *Santiago*, taken by Huguenot corsairs off Tazacorte, La Palma (pt.wikipedia, Quarenta Mártires do Brasil).
- Beard: to keep him off francis_xavier (thirties, short curly dark beard), the beard is longer and greying and the hairline receding.
- No violence: the ship, the calm sea, La Palma and the palms say "martyrs at sea".
- Title: *Beato*/"Bl." The group is not canonised. The initial is I (Ignatius).
- Excerpt: the collect of 07-17/brazil.json, "concedei-nos professar constantemente, para vossa maior gloria, a fé que recebemos de nossos antepassados". The card drops "para vossa maior gloria".

## André de Soveral, Ambrósio Francisco Ferro, Mateus Moreira and companions (martyrs of Cunhaú and Uruaçu)

Face (André): an old priest of about seventy-three, clean-shaven, with a weathered, sun-browned face, high cheekbones, bushy white eyebrows, a bald crown with a short fringe of white hair, and deep-set dark eyes lifted to the Host.

Face (Mateus): a sturdy man of about forty with weathered olive skin, short black hair, a neat black moustache and short chin beard, and a fervent upward gaze.

- **Canonised.** "Os Santos Mártires, padre Ambrósio Francisco Ferro, padre André de Soveral, o leigo Mateus Moreira e seus 27 companheiros foram beatificados em Roma, no dia 5 de março de 2000 e canonizados no dia 15 de outubro de 2017" by Pope Francis in St Peter's Square. https://arquidiocesedemanaus.org.br/2017/10/15/o-brasil-tem-30-novos-santos-papa-canoniza-martires-de-cunhau-e-uruacu/ and https://www.consolata.org.br/santos-martires-de-cunhau-e-uruacu
- **Discrepancy with the repo:** `content/of-data/calendar/sanctorale/10-03/brazil.json`, the formulary title and the collect ("vossos mártires bem-aventurados André, Ambrósio e companheiros") and the catalog line still say *Beatos*. The card says *Santos*/"Sts."; the calendar data should be updated separately.
- Name: in Brazil they are "Santos Mártires de Cunhaú e Uruaçu" (the shrine, the archdiocese of Natal, Vatican News), so that is the pt-BR name. en-US "Sts. André de Soveral and Companions", so the initial is A.
- André: born c. 1572 in the captaincy of São Vicente, educated by the Jesuits, killed at about 72–73 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_de_Soveral). Killed at Mass in the chapel of Nossa Senhora das Candeias at the Engenho Cunhaú: the attack came "depois do momento da elevação do Corpo e Sangue de Cristo" (https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_M%C3%A1rtires_de_Cunha%C3%BA_e_Urua%C3%A7u). The date is 16 July 1645 in the English and Portuguese Wikipedias and 16 June in some Brazilian sites; the card doesn't depend on it.
- Mateus Moreira, a layman and sacristan, killed at Uruaçu on 3 October 1645: "Louvado seja o Santíssimo Sacramento", said "mesmo arrancado seu coração" (pt.wikipedia; Consolata). This is the excerpt. It is tradition, not the formulary.
- No portrait exists for any of them. The official painting by Gilvan Lira (2000) is modern and copyrighted, and I did not use it. Pictures saved: the shrine at Uruaçu (`cunhau-martires-uruacu.jpg`, with the painting at the back, too small to read) and the chapel (`cunhau-capela.jpg`).
- Faces: artistic choices from age and state of life. André is clean-shaven and bald, not the stock white-bearded elder. He differs from john_vianney (long white hair) by the bald crown and the chasuble at the altar. Mateus has a moustache and chin beard, not the stock short full beard.
- The white chasuble and the elevation make the Eucharistic point of the excerpt. Keep the scene peaceful.

## General calendar

## Blase

Face: a man of about forty-five, clean-shaven, with a round, fresh face and ruddy cheeks, light-brown hair showing under the mitre, blue-grey eyes and a gentle smile turned down toward the boy.

- christianiconography.info: "The saint is always portrayed in episcopal vestments with a bishop's mitre and crozier … His martyrdom is more often symbolized, if at all, by an iron comb … The most frequent attribute, especially in later years, is a candle." https://www.christianiconography.info/blaise.html (local copy `ci-blaise.html`)
- Golden Legend (Caxton), local copy `gl-blaise.htm`: "S Blase was so sweet, holy and humble in manners"; he lived in a cave where "the birds of heaven brought to him meat", and hunters found "great multitude of beasts which were about him". He healed a boy with "a bone of a fish athwart" in his throat. To the widow whose pig the wolf took "he began to smile"; she brought him "a candle", and he told her to offer one yearly in his church. https://www.christianiconography.info/goldenLegend/blaise.htm
- Pictures checked: Memling, Lübeck Passion Altarpiece (1491; local copy `blaise-memling-lubeck.jpg`): a clean-shaven, middle-aged bishop with a mild, lowered gaze, a jewelled mitre, a crozier and a tall lit candle, with the iron comb at his feet. Pere Fernández (`blaise-pere-fernandez.jpg`): clean-shaven, mitre, holding the iron comb. Also `blaise-bicci-indianapolis.jpg`, `blaise-louvre-OAR504.jpg`, `blaise-mergolo.jpg`.
- Clean-shaven, as in the Western panels (Memling, Fernández). Batches 1–4 took most bearded prelate types (linus: rounded salt-and-pepper; cornelius: short grey; chrysogonus: square black-and-grey; josaphat: dark squared), and stanislaus is a clean-shaven bishop of fifty with greying dark hair and a grave, lowered look. So Blase is younger and fresher, with light-brown hair, ruddy cheeks and a smile toward the boy. The smile comes from the Golden Legend; the colouring is an artistic choice.
- Attributes: the two crossed candles of the throat blessing, the boy with the fish bone and his mother, birds and a deer from the cave. The red chasuble is for the martyr.
- Background: the hills of Sebaste in Cappadocia/Armenia (modern Sivas), with his cave.
- Excerpt: the collect of 02-03.json, in both languages. In en-US "they may rejoice" becomes "we may rejoice" so it stands alone.

## Vincent of Saragossa

Face: a young man of about twenty-two, clean-shaven, with straight chestnut-brown hair parted in the middle and falling thickly to the jaw, covering the ears (no fringe, no tonsure, no curls), a pale oval face, a straight nose, and heavy-lidded brown eyes gently lowered in grave recollection.

- christianiconography.info: "The gridiron is associated with St. Lawrence, so in images Vincent's attribute is usually the rack, represented as two crossed wooden beams. In portraits he typically wears a dalmatic and holds a martyr's palm and a closed book … The provost of Valencia ordered that St. Vincent's body be tied to a millstone and thrown from a ship at sea … a ship is used as the saint's attribute." https://www.christianiconography.info/vincent.html (local `ci-vincent.html`; Golden Legend `gl-vincent.htm`)
- Pictures checked:
  - Nuno Gonçalves, *Saint Vincent Panels* (c. 1470, MNAA Lisbon; local copy `vincent-nuno-goncalves-panels.jpg`): Vincent, patron of Lisbon, appears twice as a beardless young deacon with straight chestnut hair falling to the jaw, a pale oval face and a grave, lowered gaze, in a red-and-gold dalmatic with a red cap, holding a book.
  - Bernat Martorell, *Altarpiece of Saint Vincent* (local copy `vincent-martorell.jpg`): the saint in a brocade dalmatic with palm and book, standing before a large X-shaped rack.
  - Also `vincent-giner-prado.jpg`, `vincent-clerc-vignerons.jpg` (vinedressers) and `vincent-commons-main.jpg`.
- The ravens: tradition says ravens guarded his body and later escorted the ship carrying his relics to Lisbon, which has them in its coat of arms.
- Not lawrence or stephen: lawrence is dark, short-haired and tonsured with a beard shadow, holding the gridiron; stephen is a boy with light-brown curls. Vincent gets the jaw-length straight bob of the Lisbon panels, is a little older and graver, and has the rack, millstone and ravens, not a gridiron.
- Background: the harbour of Valencia, where he died, with the ship.
- Excerpt: the collect of 01-22.json. The two languages differ in wording (the pt-BR adds "o diácono" and "do martírio"); each fragment is verbatim.

## Fabian

Face: a lean, clean-shaven man of about sixty-five, with thick, short silver hair showing below the tiara, a long, fine-boned face with hollow cheeks, gentle downcast grey-blue eyes and a humble, quiet expression.

- Eusebius, *Church History* VI.29 (McGiffert, NPNF 1890; local copy `eusebius-he6.htm`): "They say that Fabianus having come, after the death of Anteros, with others from the country, was staying at Rome … several renowned and honorable men were in the minds of many, but Fabianus, although present, was in the mind of none. But they relate that suddenly a dove flying down lighted on his head, resembling the descent of the Holy Spirit on the Saviour in the form of a dove." https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/250106.htm
- Catholic Encyclopedia (1909): pope 236–250; he divided Rome into seven districts under deacons; he was martyred on 20 Jan. 250 under Decius and buried in the catacomb of St Callistus. https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/05742d.htm (local `cathen-fabian.htm`)
- Picture checked: Giovanni di Paolo, *Saints Fabian and Sebastian* (National Gallery, London; local copy `fabian-giovanni-di-paolo-ng.png`): an old, lean, clean-shaven pope with a long face, hollow cheeks and a mild, lowered gaze, in a tiara and white-and-gold cope, blessing and holding a red book.
- Not leo_great (heavy-set, clean-shaven, full face) and not gregory_great (reddish beard, mitre): Fabian is lean and ascetic, with a dove on the tiara.
- Excerpt: the formulary's collect ("O God, glory of your Priests …") says little on its own, so the card quotes Eusebius. en-US is McGiffert verbatim; pt-BR is our rendering.
- Background: the Appian Way and the catacomb of St Callistus.

## Angela Merici

Face: a slender woman of about sixty with a fine-boned oval face, soft lines at the eyes, warm dark-brown eyes, dark eyebrows, fair skin, and a tender, motherly smile as she looks down at the child.

- Catholic Encyclopedia (1907): born 1474 at Desenzano on Lake Garda; "She became a tertiary of St. Francis"; she taught "all the little girls of Desenzano"; in "a vision … it was revealed to her that she was to found an association of virgins"; on 25 Nov. 1535 "Angela chose twelve virgins and laid the foundation of the order of the Ursulines"; she died in 1540 at Brescia and was buried at Sant'Afra. https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/01481a.htm (local `cathen-angela.htm`)
- Wikipedia: attributes "Cloak, ladder"; "Her body was clothed in the habit of a Franciscan tertiary." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merici. The ladder is her vision at Brudazzo near Desenzano, of virgins climbing a ladder to heaven among angels.
- Portrait: Moretto painted her funeral portrait in 1540 (https://www.visitdesenzano.it/en/holy-angela-merici-ac8, http://www.angelamerici.it/index_dettagli.php?get_id=11). I did not see a reproduction I could check, so the face is not taken from it. The age (about sixty) comes from the founding in 1535; the face is an artistic choice.
- Not monica (older, white veil, blue mantle, eyes raised): Angela wears the brown tertiary habit and white wimple, looks down at a child, and has the ladder in the sky. Not clare_assisi: no monstrance, and she is older.
- Background: Desenzano and Lake Garda.
- Excerpt: the collect of 01-27.json. Both clauses are verbatim, turned into a wish.

## Frances of Rome

Face: a woman of about forty-five with an olive Roman complexion, a gentle oval face, fine dark eyebrows, large dark eyes lifted a little toward the angel, a straight nose and a serene, faintly smiling mouth.

- christianiconography.info: "Italian images put her in a simplified version of the Benedictine habit, portraying a black or dark blue tunic and a long or mid-length white veil, usually without a wimple or scapular … she was accompanied during the last 24 years of her life by an archangel whom only she could see … Many of her images therefore include the archangel, who is usually pictured as small in stature." https://www.christianiconography.info/francesRome.html (local `ci-francesRome.html`)
- The angel: a boy of about nine, with hair "like fine golden yarn" to his shoulders, in "a dalmatic like the deacon at Mass"; the light of his hair let her read her Office at night. https://catholicmagazine.news/st-frances-of-rome-the-angel-of-rome/
- Catholic Encyclopedia (1909): born in Rome in 1384 of a noble family and married at twelve to Lorenzo de' Ponziani; "remarkable for her charity to the poor"; she founded the oblates of Tor de' Specchi (1433) attached to Santa Maria Nuova, and had "the bodily vision of her guardian angel". Her body lies in Santa Maria Nuova in the Forum, now Santa Francesca Romana. https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/06205c.htm (local `cathen-frances.htm`)
- Age: the angel was with her in her last 24 years (from about 1416, aged 32). She founded the oblates at 49. The card puts her at about 45.
- Not bridget_sweden (black habit, white wimple with a red-banded crown, quill): Frances has a long plain veil without wimple, a book of hours, a bread basket and the golden-haired angel.
- Excerpt: the collect of 03-09.json. The clauses are verbatim, turned into a wish.

## Pairs that may still look alike

Checked against content/saints and the faces in batches 1–4.

- **Benedito and martin_de_porres (batch 4)**: both clean-shaven Black lay brothers with close curls. This is the closest pair. Martin is slender, about forty-five, quiet, with a lowered gaze, in the Dominican white-and-black habit with a broom. Benedito is about sixty, rounder, greying and broadly smiling, in the brown Franciscan habit with roses.
- **Benedito and moses_the_black**: Moses has a full black beard, a stern gaze, a sword and a desert. Make sure the model does not give Benedito a beard.
- **Benedito and anthony_padua / elizabeth_hungary**: the Franciscan with the Child, and roses in the apron. Benedito has no Child and no visible loaves.
- **Frei Galvão and anthony_padua**: both clean-shaven friars with a thick ring of hair. Galvão is about sixty, round-faced and greying, in a darker grey-brown habit, seated at a table with the paper pills. Check this pair on the contact sheet.
- **Anchieta and francis_xavier / ignatius_loyola / inacio_azevedo**: four Jesuits in black. Anchieta is young with only a sparse beard, bending over the sand; Xavier has a short curly beard; Ignatius is bald.
- **Azevedo and josaphat (batch 2)**: both lean men of about forty with a full dark beard. Azevedo's beard is greying, his hairline recedes, and he wears a Jesuit soutane with the icon and the ship. Josaphat wears a sakkos and crown-mitre.
- **André de Soveral and john_vianney / paul_vi (batch 4)**: clean-shaven old priests. Soveral is sun-browned and weathered, with high cheekbones and white brows, in a chasuble at the elevation in a colonial chapel. Vianney has long white hair; Paul VI is pale and narrow in papal dress.
- **Blase and stanislaus (batch 2) / nicholas / ambrose**: mitred bishops. Blase is clean-shaven, younger and ruddy, with light-brown hair and a smile, holding the crossed candles. Stanislaus is greying and grave, with a sword. The candles must carry most of the difference.
- **Fabian and leo_great / paul_vi / cletus**: popes. Leo is heavy and full-faced; Paul VI has a high bald forehead; Cletus has a long forked silver beard and no tiara. Fabian is lean and clean-shaven, with thick short silver hair, the tiara and the dove.
- **Vincent and alexander (batch 3) / lawrence / stephen**: young, clean-shaven men. Alexander has short chestnut hair with a straight fringe; Lawrence is tonsured; Stephen has light curls. Vincent's hair is centre-parted and falls to the jaw over the ears. The dalmatic, rack, millstone and ravens separate him further.
- **Frances of Rome and bridget_sweden**: a black habit with a white veil. Frances has no wimple, and has the angel.
- **Angela Merici and monica / scholastica**: veiled women of middle age. Angela has the brown tertiary habit, looks down at a child, and has the ladder.

Face lines in `../batches/batch-5.json` were given explicit bone structure on 2026-09-29 (face shape, nose, eyes, brows, cheekbones, jaw, mouth before hair, beard and gaze), because lines of age, hair and beard alone produced one shared underlying face; the structures were chosen so that no two faces across batches 1–5 and the existing cards share one. The "Face:" lines above keep the sourced marks but not the added structure.
