# Batch 13 of new cards: faces

Faces, dress and settings for ten cards: Anthony Zaccaria, Augustine Zhao Rong and companions, Henry, Apollinaris, Eusebius of Vercelli, Pontian and Hippolytus, John Eudes, Joseph Calasanz, John Leonardi and Columban. The card data is in `../batches/batch-13.json`. As in `first-batch.md`, each "Face:" line uses only marks with a source noted below it, and says where a mark is an artistic choice. Pictures and texts consulted are in `../consult/batch-13/` (Wikipedia lead images `*.jpg`, a crop `leonardi-head.jpg`, article extracts `*.txt`, and `build.py`, which writes the JSON and checks every excerpt, feast and catalog match).

Excerpts: seven come from the collect of the feast's formulary. Calasanz, Leonardi and Columban have calendar entries but no formulary in the repo, so their cards take an entrance antiphon of the matching Common: For Educators (Calasanz; the file has no pt-BR, so the pt-BR is our faithful rendering), For One Pastor (Leonardi) and For Missionaries (Columban), both verbatim in the two languages. Recasts ("that … we may" as a wish) and dropped phrases are named in `excerptSource`.

## Anthony Zaccaria

Face: a pale, slender Lombard of about thirty-six, a long oval face in three-quarter view, receding dark hair at the temples, a long straight nose with a slightly lowered tip, deep-set dark eyes under straight brows, a firm closed mouth, a dense dark-brown beard ending in a short squared point.

- The traditional portrait on Wikipedia (local `zaccaria.jpg`), which I checked: a man in profile in a black high-collared cassock, the hair short and receding at the temples, a long prominent nose, deep-set eyes, a full dark moustache and beard ending in a point, the gaze lifted to the right. The card keeps the profile-leaning pose and these marks.
- Formulary description (07-05.json): born at Cremona 1502, studied medicine at Padua, founded the Clerics Regular of St Paul (Barnabites), died 1539. Hence Cremona with the Torrazzo; the monstrance is for the Forty Hours he promoted, the lily and crucifix are his usual attributes.
- The sources demand a thirties man with a dark beard, the stock face the brief warns about; the receding hairline, the long nose and the pointed, squared beard in three-quarter view have to carry him.
- Excerpt: 07-05.json collect (en-US recast as a wish; pt-BR the opening words verbatim).

## Augustine Zhao Rong and Companions

Faces: Augustine (centre), a former soldier of sixty-nine, a broad, square, wide-jawed Han face, shaved forehead and grey queue, thin drooping moustache and sparse chin beard. Lucy Yi Zhenmei (left), about forty-five, a smooth oval face, fine arched brows, black hair in a low bun. Chi Zhuzi (right), eighteen, clean-shaven, narrow and angular, shaved forehead and black queue.

- Wikipedia: born 1746 in Wuchuan, Guizhou; a Qing soldier from twenty; converted while guarding Fr (later Bishop) Dufresse in 1785; the first Chinese-born diocesan priest; arrested, tortured and died in prison in Chengdu, 1815. Hence a sixty-nine-year-old with a soldier's square build.
- The Vatican biographies of the 120 martyrs (canonised 1 October 2000) list "Blessed Chi Zhuze, aged 18" among the lay martyrs of July 1900; Wikipedia's list of the Martyrs of Guizhou (February 1862) names "Lucy Yi Zhenmei, lay catechist". Neither gives a portrait. The three were chosen to show a priest, a woman catechist and a young layman across the three persecutions; the brief asks for three figures in front.
- The stained glass on Wikipedia (local `zhao.jpg`), which I checked, gives Zhao a Western face, a moustache and goatee and a biretta; set aside. Dress is Qing-era: long scholar's robe and small cap for the priest (Chinese priests of the period wore Chinese dress), side-fastened jacket and skirt for Lucy, a cotton jacket and the queue, compulsory for Han men under the Qing, for the two men.
- Background: the terraced hills and limestone peaks of Guizhou and Sichuan, where all three lived; our choice.
- No violence: palms only.
- Excerpt: 07-09.json collect, the address with the wonderful providence and the names dropped.

## Henry (Emperor Henry II)

Face: a German prince of about forty-five, a short, broad, heart-shaped face, a short straight nose with a rounded tip, wide-set light hazel eyes, a small full mouth, chestnut hair cut level at the ears, a short rounded chestnut beard.

- The coronation page of the Regensburg Sacramentary (1002–14), made for him in his lifetime (Wikipedia lead image, local `henry.jpg`), which I checked: Henry crowned by Christ, in a golden tunic with a blue mantle, brown hair cut at the ears, a short dark beard and moustache. The card takes the dress, the hair and the beard; the Ottonian page gives no structure, so the heart shape is our choice.
- Wikipedia: born 973, Duke of Bavaria, king 1002, emperor 1014; founded the Diocese of Bamberg in 1007, fell ill and died at Bamberg on 13 July 1024 at fifty-one; buried in Bamberg Cathedral; canonised 1146/47. Hence the cathedral model and Bamberg (the present cathedral is thirteenth-century but stands on his site).
- Excerpt: 07-13.json collect, the final clause, en-US recast as a wish.

## Apollinaris

Face: a Syrian-born bishop of about sixty, olive, a short wide-cheeked face under a flat square forehead, large round dark eyes, a long narrow nose, short white hair combed forward in a fringe, a short white beard to a neat point.

- The apse mosaic of Sant'Apollinare in Classe, c. 549 (Wikipedia lead image, local `apollinaris.jpg`), which I checked: frontal, both hands raised in the orans gesture, a white tunic with dark-striped cuffs, a gold-green paenula with small leaves, a white pallium with a black cross; short white hair combed forward, large eyes, a long nose, a short white beard coming to a point; white lambs among lilies. The card follows it closely.
- Wikipedia quotes the Roman Martyrology ("a bishop who, according to tradition ... honoured the Church of Classis near Ravenna by a glorious martyrdom") and calls him a Syrian saint. Hence the olive complexion, our reading of that tradition.
- Not Sylvester (long, narrow, large almond eyes, also mosaic-like): Apollinaris is short and wide-cheeked, with the forward fringe.
- Excerpt: 07-20.json collect, the opening clause.

## Eusebius of Vercelli

Face: a Sardinian of about sixty-five, dark, an oblong face under a heavy brow ridge, deep-set black eyes, a broad-bridged nose, deep lines from nose to mouth, tight grey-black curls, a dense iron-grey beard cut square.

- Wikipedia: born in Sardinia in 283; bishop of Vercelli; founded a priestly community there on the model of Athanasius's Life of Anthony; exiled after Milan (355) to Scythopolis, Cappadocia and the Thebaid for refusing to condemn Athanasius; died 370/371. The Codex Vercellensis, the oldest Old Latin Gospels, kept in his cathedral, "was believed to have been written by Eusebius, though now scholars tend to doubt it": the card shows it as the traditional attribute, a purple codex with no readable text.
- No likeness survives. The lead image, a Sebastiano Ricci painting, is a baroque generic bishop; not used. The face is our choice: a dark islander, made hard and constant, the virtue the collect names.
- Background: Vercelli on the Piedmont plain with the Alps and Monte Rosa, his see.
- Excerpt: 08-02.json collect, the opening clause.

## Pontian and Hippolytus

Faces: Pontian (left), a Roman of about fifty, round and compact, a short upturned nose, round brown eyes, cropped greying hair, a short rounded dark beard. Hippolytus (right), about sixty-five, long, lean and hollow-cheeked, a bald crown with long grey hair at the sides, a thin arched nose, bushy grey brows, a long flowing grey beard.

- Wikipedia (Pope Pontian): bishop of Rome 230–235; in 235, under Maximinus Thrax, "both Pope Pontian and the Antipope Hippolytus of Rome were arrested and exiled to labor in the mines of Sardinia"; Pontian resigned, the first pope to do so; "the two may have reconciled with one another in Rome or in Sardinia before their deaths"; Pope Fabian brought both bodies back to Rome. Wikipedia (Hippolytus): the leading Roman theologian of his time and a rival of the popes, "reconciled to the Church before he died as a martyr".
- The seated marble statue found in 1551 on the Via Tiburtina, long taken for Hippolytus, "was heavily restored", and Guarducci showed it was originally a female figure: no likeness. The philosopher's cloak and scroll mark the writer; both faces are our choice, Pontian's round and humble against Hippolytus's long and learned one. The hand on the shoulder shows the reconciliation.
- Dress: third-century tunics and cloaks, the exile's coarse cloak for Pontian with the pallium as the only mark of the pope (as on the other early popes' cards). Background: the mining hills of south-west Sardinia (the Iglesiente, worked since antiquity) above the sea.
- Excerpt: 08-13.json collect, first clause (en-US "O Lord, we pray," dropped).

## John Eudes

Face: a Norman priest of about sixty-five, a long pointed-oval face under a large bald dome, a grey fringe, a long nose with a rounded tip, heavy-lidded pale-grey eyes under raised thin brows, marked folds by the mouth, a faint knowing smile, a small grey moustache and narrow goatee.

- The seventeenth-century portrait on Wikipedia (local `eudes.jpg`), which I checked: a bald old priest with a narrow grey fringe, heavy-lidded eyes, marked nasolabial folds, a small grey moustache and short goatee, a faint smile, a black cassock and cloak with a wide white rabat, holding a flaming heart with a cross (inscribed) and a crucifix. The card keeps all of these and drops the inscription.
- Wikipedia: founded the Order of Our Lady of Charity (1641) and the Congregation of Jesus and Mary (1643); formulary: chosen "to proclaim the unfathomable riches of Christ". He founded his seminary at Caen, hence the Abbaye aux Hommes.
- Not Anselm or Romuald (bald domes with long beards): the goatee and the rabat carry him.
- Excerpt: 08-19.json collect, the address.

## Joseph Calasanz

Face: a spare Aragonese of about eighty-five, a long face broad at the temples, bald under the biretta, prominent cheekbones over hollow cheeks, a long slightly hooked nose, large bright grey-brown eyes under high-arched white brows, a faint kind smile, a short trimmed white beard.

- The portrait in the Museo del Prado (Wikipedia lead image, local `calasanz.jpg`), which I checked: an old man in a black biretta and black habit and cape with a white collar edge, bald, with a long face, large gentle eyes under raised arched brows, a short white beard and moustache, a faint smile, the right hand laid on the breast. The card keeps the gesture and marks.
- Wikipedia: born 1557 at Peralta de Calasanz in Aragon; founded the Pious Schools, free schools for poor boys, in Rome; died 25 August 1648 at ninety; buried at San Pantaleo. Hence the children with slate and primer, and San Pantaleo by Piazza Navona.
- Not the stock white-bearded elder: short, trimmed beard, and a long, bright-eyed face.
- Excerpt: Common for Educators, entrance antiphon; he has no formulary in the repo.

## John Leonardi

Face: a Tuscan of about sixty-five, ruddy, a broad, full, rectangular face, bald with grey hair at the sides, wide cheekbones, a short broad nose, round dark eyes turned upward, a short full grey beard trimmed round along the jaw.

- The altarpiece on Wikipedia (local `leonardi.jpg`, head crop `leonardi-head.jpg`), which I checked: in a black cassock and cloak, eyes raised to the Virgin and Child, a quill in his hand and a book held by a boy; his head is balding with grey hair at the sides, a broad face and nose, and a short grey beard and moustache. The card takes the upward gaze, the vision and the marks.
- Wikipedia: born 1541 at Diecimo in the Republic of Lucca; trained ten years as a pharmacist's assistant in Lucca before ordination (1572); founded the Clerics Regular of the Mother of God; kept from Lucca by its government; died in Rome on 9 October 1609 of influenza caught nursing his brothers. Hence the mortar and majolica jar and the Lucca background.
- Excerpt: Common of Pastors, For One Pastor, entrance antiphon; he has no formulary in the repo.

## Columban

Face: a weathered Irishman of about seventy, a long, big-boned face with a jutting chin, the front of the head shaved ear to ear, long red-grey hair behind, deep-set pale-blue eyes, bristling brows, a long bony nose, a short grizzled red-grey beard.

- Wikipedia: born in Leinster in 543; trained at Bangor; founded Annegray, Luxeuil and Fontaine in the Vosges, then Bobbio (614), where he died on 21 November 615. The Frankish bishops "complained about the distinct Irish tonsure". "In art, Columbanus is represented bearded, bearing the monastic cowl, holding in his hand a book with an Irish satchel, and standing in the midst of wolves. Sometimes he is depicted in the attitude of taming a bear." The card takes the beard, cowl, satchel, book and bear.
- The Wikipedia article on the tonsure: "The Celtic tonsure, the exact shape of which is unclear from the sources, but in some way involved shaving the head from ear to ear." The card shaves the front from ear to ear and leaves long hair behind, the usual reconstruction.
- No likeness survives. The red colouring and big-boned structure are our choice, to keep him Irish and apart from Patrick.
- Background: Bobbio in the Trebbia valley with its humpbacked bridge (a later medieval bridge, but the place as it looks).
- Excerpt: Common of Pastors, For Missionaries, entrance antiphon; he has no formulary in the repo.

## Look-alike risks that remain

- Anthony Zaccaria and Ignatius, Francis Xavier (his refs) and the stock thirties bearded man: the portrait demands a young dark-bearded priest. The receding temples, profile-leaning pose and squared pointed beard must hold; reject a draft with curls or long hair.
- Augustine Zhao Rong and Lawrence Ruiz, Paul Miki, Andrew Kim, Andrew Dung-Lac: East Asian men. Zhao's age, grey queue and square soldier's face separate him; Chi Zhuzi at eighteen must not drift into Andrew Kim (soft oval) — his is narrow and angular.
- Henry and Stephen of Hungary, Wenceslaus, Louis of France: crowned rulers. Henry's heart shape, level-cut Ottonian hair and the Bamberg model carry him; check on the face sheet against Wenceslaus (broad Slavic).
- Apollinaris and Sylvester, Clement I: mosaic-styled early bishops. The forward white fringe and short wide face are the distinguishing marks.
- Eusebius and Cyril of Jerusalem, Denis: square-bearded bishops in paenula. The heavy brow ridge, tight curls and dark complexion should hold.
- Hippolytus and Jerome (a ref), Romuald, Anselm: long grey beards and bald crowns. The philosopher's cloak and scroll help; if he reads as Jerome, shorten the beard in a redo.
- Pontian and Cajetan, Peter Chrysologus: round faces. The exile's cloak and the pair composition separate him.
- John Eudes and Anselm, Romuald, Paul VI: bald domes. The goatee and rabat should carry him.
- Joseph Calasanz and Alphonsus Liguori, Pius V: old, thin clerics. The biretta, short white beard and the children hold him apart.
- John Leonardi and Philip Neri (a ref), Peter Canisius: bearded Italian priests of the same century. The broad rectangular face, round-trimmed beard, upward gaze and the pharmacy props separate him.
- Columban and Patrick, Boniface: missionaries from the British Isles. The Irish tonsure with long hair behind, the red-grey colouring, cowl and bear are the marks; reject a draft with a Roman crown tonsure or a mitre.
