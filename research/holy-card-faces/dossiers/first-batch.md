# First batch of new cards: faces

Faces, dress and settings for ten saints who don't have a card yet. The cards will be generated new, not edited, so each entry also gives dress, attributes and a background. Each "Face:" line uses only marks with a source quoted below it. Where a mark is an artistic choice made to keep two cards apart, the entry says so.

The existing stock faces to avoid are a man in his thirties with brown curls and a short beard, a white-bearded elder, and a beardless youth. "Dionysius" is the *Painter's Manual* in Didron's French (1845), as in `prelates-and-young.md` (local copy `../consult/prelates-src/didron-fr.txt`). Pictures and texts consulted for this batch are in `../consult/first-batch/`.

## Moderns: faces from photographs and witnesses

## Faustina Kowalska

Face: a woman of about thirty with a round, open face, fair skin with a light dusting of freckles, reddish-gold eyebrows (her hair is hidden by the veil), light grey-green eyes, a straight nose and a calm, steady gaze.

- Sister Sabina, who received her as a postulant, recalled that "she was red-haired and heavily freckled." https://catholicexchange.com/a-star-and-the-moon-part-ii-the-lives-of-alicja-lenczewska-and-st-faustina-kowalska/
- The painted portrait on Wikipedia, based on her photographs (local copy `Faustina_Kowalska.jpg`), which I checked: a round, smooth face, light eyes, straight nose, black veil with a white band framing the face, a white collar and a crucifix on a cord. The 1931 photograph shows "the black habit and veil of the Congregation of the Sisters of Our Lady of Mercy and a crucifix necklace." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faustina_Kowalska
- Age: she lived from 1905 to 1938 and died at 33.
- Dress and attributes: the black habit and veil with the white band, a crucifix. The attribute is the Divine Mercy image: "Jesus appeared wearing a white garment with red and pale rays emanating from his heart", signed "Jesus, I trust in You" (Wikipedia). Show the image beside her or as the vision above her.
- Background: the convent of Our Lady of Mercy at Kraków-Łagiewniki, where she made her final vows in 1933 and is buried. Vilnius, where the first image was painted, is the alternative.
- Freckles: keep them faint. The freckles and red hair set her apart; a heavy rendering would not suit a devotional card.

## Padre Pio (Pio of Pietrelcina)

Face: a man of about sixty, with short, thick white hair receding from a high forehead, a full, rounded white beard of medium length, strong dark eyebrows, deep-set dark eyes and a warm, faintly amused half-smile.

- The official portrait photograph, c. 1947, when he was about 60 (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Padre_Pio.jpg`), which I checked: a white beard, white hair, bushy eyebrows darker than the beard, a slight smile, the brown Capuchin habit with its hood. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padre_Pio
- The 1919 photograph with the stigmata, local copy `Padre_Pio_of_Pietrelcina_with_the_stigmata.jpg`: at 32 he had a black beard and dark hair. Use the older face, which is the one devotion knows. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Padre_Pio_of_Pietrelcina_with_the_stigmata.jpg
- Mittens: "He often wore red mittens or black coverings on his hands and feet, saying that he was embarrassed by the marks" (Wikipedia). A Capuchin writer describes "brown fingerless mittens" that he "never removed … except on the altar when offering Mass each day." http://bryanshortall.blogspot.com/2020/01/talking-sense-about-he-mitten-of-st.html. Brown fingerless mittens match the habit and are the usual choice on cards.
- Dress and attributes: the brown Capuchin habit with its hood and white cord, brown fingerless mittens, a rosary, and a crucifix or the chalice.
- Background: San Giovanni Rotondo on the Gargano hills, with the friary church of Santa Maria delle Grazie, where he lived from 1916 until his death in 1968.
- Not the stock white-bearded elder: the beard is shorter and rounded, the brows are dark and heavy, and the expression smiles.

## John Vianney (Curé of Ars)

Face: a small, thin, clean-shaven old priest of about seventy, with long snow-white hair falling to his collar, a high, pale, smooth forehead, a narrow, wasted face with lines around the mouth, and deep-set, luminous eyes, lowered or raised in prayer.

- The Bishop of Birmingham (Ullathorne), who saw him in 1854, quoted by Monnin: "His face was small, wasted, and sallow; many expressive lines were marked around the mouth. His hair was white as snow; his expansive forehead pale, smooth, and clear; whilst his eyes were remarkably deep in shadow … As he opened his eyes, they sent forth a light … so preternaturally bright and tranquil." Monnin, *Life of the Curé d'Ars*, https://archive.org/details/LifeOfTheCureDArs (local copy `monnin.txt`)
- Monnin on the old man: "He walked with his head bowed down, and his eyes on the ground; his long and flowing hair fell around his neck, and encircled his face with a white aureola." On the young priest: "His face was pale and angular, his stature low … There was nothing in his appearance, except its asceticism, and the singular brightness of his eyes."
- He refused to be photographed. Cabuchet modelled a wax bust in secret in 1858 (https://www.arsnet.org/en/ars-great-witnesses/emilien-cabuchet/), and Camille Dolard photographed him on his deathbed in 1859 (local copy `Cure_d__Ars_1.jpg`). The standard image, as in the stained glass on Wikipedia (local copy `John_Vianney.jpg`), follows these: clean-shaven, long white hair, a thin smiling face. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Vianney
- Age: 1786–1859, died at 73.
- Dress and attributes: a worn black soutane, a surplice and a violet stole (the confessor's stole), a rosary, a crucifix. Wikipedia lists "Cassock, surplice, preaching bands, stole, rosary, crucifix". He is shown clean-shaven with the French clergy's white collar bands.
- Background: the village church of Ars-sur-Formans in the Dombes, with the confessional.
- Left out: "sallow" and "shrunken". Render him as thin and pale, not sickly.

## Maximilian Kolbe

Face: a man in his early forties, balding, with close-cropped hair, a long, dark-brown beard that narrows to a point on the chest, small round wire-rimmed spectacles, a straight nose and a direct, earnest gaze through the glasses.

- The 1936 photograph from Japan (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Maximilian_Kolbe.jpg`), which I checked: round wire spectacles, a high, balding forehead with short hair, a long, full dark beard to mid-chest, a dark Conventual habit with a short cape and a white cord. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximilian_Kolbe
- The beard: "Most photos, paintings, and icons of St. Maximilian Kolbe show him with his signature long beard. But the very last photo of him shows him clean-shaven." He grew it as a missionary and shaved it as the Nazis closed in. https://www.thecatholicthing.org/2023/08/15/st-maximilian-kolbes-beard/
- Age: 1894–1941; he died at 47 in Auschwitz as "prisoner 16670".
- The two crowns: at nine, Mary offered him a white crown and a red one: "The white one meant that I should persevere in purity and the red that I should become a martyr. I said that I would accept them both" (Wikipedia).
- Dress: two choices, each with its own face. (a) The dark Conventual Franciscan habit with its short cape and white cord, bearded and spectacled, as in most photographs and icons. (b) The striped camp uniform with the number 16670, clean-shaven, spectacled, as in his last years. Recommend (a): the beard and glasses are what people recognise. On either, put the Immaculata above him offering the white and red crowns.
- Background: for (a), Niepokalanów, the friary and publishing house he founded near Warsaw in 1927; for (b), the barbed wire and blocks of Auschwitz, kept sober.
- Not Padre Pio: Kolbe is younger, his beard is dark and long, he wears round glasses, and his habit is dark with a cape rather than brown with a hood.

## Francis de Sales

Face: a sturdy man of about fifty with a large, almost completely bald head, a broad, full forehead with high, arched brows, clear blue eyes, ruddy cheeks, a thick auburn beard of medium length and a gentle, kindly expression.

- His nephew and historian, Bishop Charles-Auguste de Sales, in 1634: "His body was straight and robust, his figure full, with large shoulders, bright complexion, his head was large and well-formed and almost completely bald, his hair auburn, his brow wide and full, well curved high eyebrows, blue eyes, well-defined but not prominent nose, ruddy cheeks, round mouth, a thick and medium length beard." Quoted in Jules Chambelland, "St. Francis de Sales and His Portraits" (*Annales Salésiennes*, 1937). https://hosted.desales.edu/files/salesian/PDF/ChambellandPortraits.pdf (local copy `chambelland.txt`). The same article says his secretary Martel Ange painted him from life in 1606 through a hole in the wall of his bedroom.
- The portrait at the Château de Bussy-Rabutin (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Francis_de_Sales.jpg`), which I checked: a bald dome, a full reddish-brown beard, blue eyes, mozzetta and pectoral cross. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_de_Sales
- Wikipedia: "tall and well built with blue-grey eyes". He lived from 1567 to 1622 and died at 55.
- Dress and attributes: a bishop in rochet and violet mozzetta with a pectoral cross, as in the portraits (a mitre and crozier are optional). Attributes: the Heart of Jesus with the crown of thorns (Wikipedia gives "Heart of Jesus" and "Crown of Thorns"), and a book or pen (the *Introduction to the Devout Life*).
- Background: Annecy, its lake and the Savoy Alps, where he lived as Bishop of Geneva and founded the Visitation.

## Ancients and medievals: faces from tradition

## Ignatius of Antioch

Face: an old man, bald on the crown with a fringe of grey hair, a long grey beard narrowing to a point, a lean, ascetic face and a steady gaze. Keep the beard grey and tapering, not broad and white.

- Dionysius, p. 317: "Saint Ignace le Porte-Dieu : vieillard à large barbe" (an old man with a broad beard).
- The 11th-century mosaic at Hosios Loukas (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Ignatius_of_Antioch.jpg`), which I checked: bald on top, long grey-white beard tapering to a point, a thin face, omophorion with crosses and a Gospel book. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignatius_of_Antioch
- christianiconography.info: "Portraits in the East sometimes include two lions devouring the saint, arranged on each side"; in the West he sometimes holds a heart inscribed "YHS", from the Golden Legend's story that the name of Jesus was written on his heart. https://www.christianiconography.info/ignatiusAntioch.html
- Dress and attributes: bishop's vestments (on a Latin card, chasuble and pallium or mitre; on the Eastern model, the white omophorion with black crosses), a book of his letters, and two lions at his feet. The heart inscribed IHS is an optional Western attribute.
- Background: the Flavian Amphitheatre in Rome, where tradition places his martyrdom by beasts.
- Near the stock white-bearded elder, and near Athanasius and Nicholas. Separate him by the tapering grey beard (Athanasius: broad, full, white; Nicholas: short, rounded) and by the lions.

## Leo the Great

Face: a heavy-set, clean-shaven pope of about sixty, with a full, fleshy face, a strong jaw, dark eyes and a calm, commanding expression.

- Raphael, *The Meeting of Leo the Great and Attila* (1513–14, Stanza di Eliodoro): "Initially, Raphael depicted Leo I with the face of Pope Julius II but after Julius' death, Raphael changed the painting to resemble the new pope, Leo X." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Meeting_of_Leo_the_Great_and_Attila. I checked the fresco (local copy `Leoattila-Raphael.jpg`): a clean-shaven, full-faced pope in tiara and cope on a white horse, Peter and Paul with swords in the sky, and Rome with the Colosseum and an aqueduct behind him. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Leoattila-Raphael.jpg
- The other types: Dionysius, p. 319: "Saint Léon, pape de Rome : vieillard, grande barbe" (an old man with a large beard). Algardi's relief in St Peter's (1646–53, local copy `Alessandro_Algardi_Meeting_of_Leo_I_and_Attila_01.jpg`) and Herrera the Younger's painting (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Pope_Leo_I.jpg`) also give him a white beard. Raphael's clean-shaven Leo is the best-known Western image and the only one that does not fall into the stock white-bearded elder. I recommend it, but it is a choice: no source gives Leo I's own features.
- Dress and attributes: papal tiara, gold cope over alb, a papal cross; the meeting with Attila, with Peter and Paul holding swords in the sky.
- Background: Raphael's Roman campagna with the Colosseum and aqueduct. The historical meeting of 452 was in northern Italy, near Mantua.

## Martin of Tours

Face: a young man of about twenty, clean-shaven, with fair, wavy hair cut to the jaw, a kind face and his gaze lowered to the beggar.

- Sulpicius Severus, *Life of St Martin*, ch. 3: at the gate of Amiens in winter, "Taking, therefore, his sword with which he was girt, he divided his cloak into two equal parts, and gave one part to the poor man." He was baptised shortly after, at about eighteen to twenty, after nearly three years as a soldier. https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3501.htm
- Simone Martini, *Division of the Cloak* (c. 1320, Lower Church, Assisi; local copy `Simone_Martini_-_Division_of_the_Cloak_(scene_1)_-_WGA21367.jpg`), which I checked: a beardless young horseman with fair, chin-length hair, bent toward the beggar, cutting his cloak with his sword at a city gate. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Simone_Martini_-_Division_of_the_Cloak_(scene_1)_-_WGA21367.jpg
- christianiconography.info: "Martin is on horseback and uses his sword to divide his cape in two"; his attributes are the cloak and the goose; later images show him in episcopal vestments. https://www.christianiconography.info/martin.html
- Left out: Sulpicius, ch. 9, reports his opponents' view that as bishop he was "despicable in countenance" with "disgusting" hair. That is a caricature by his enemies.
- Dress and attributes: a young Roman cavalry officer, with helmet or bareheaded, on a white horse, cutting his red military cloak with his sword for a half-naked beggar. The bishop (mitre, crozier, goose) is the alternative, but the cloak scene is what everyone recognises.
- Background: the city gate of Amiens in winter snow.
- Close to the stock beardless youth and to George (a beardless young soldier with close-cropped hair). Separate him by the fair, wavy, jaw-length hair, the downward, compassionate gaze and the horse.

## Agatha

Face: a young noblewoman of about twenty, with long fair (blonde) hair under a sheer veil, a serene oval face, large dark eyes and a composed, steadfast expression.

- christianiconography.info: "Agatha typically appears with long blonde hair and often wears 'a diaphanous veil.'" In the West she is "readily identified by the presence of a platter holding her severed breasts"; she sometimes holds "the pincers used to remove them", with a palm. The earliest image, the 6th-century mosaic at Poreč, presents her "as a noblewoman in a veil." https://www.christianiconography.info/agatha.html
- Sant'Apollinare Nuovo, Ravenna (6th c., local copy `Sancta_Agathe_Ravenna.jpg`), which I checked: a veiled noblewoman in jewelled gold dress offering a crown, with a lamb beside her in the procession of virgins. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sancta_Agathe_Ravenna.jpg. Also the 7th–8th-century fresco possibly from Santa Maria Antiqua (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Saint_Agatha.jpg`): large dark eyes, dark hair under a pearl-studded cap. The dark hair there conflicts with the later Western blonde; blonde is the Western card tradition.
- Golden Legend: "Saint Agatha the virgin was right fair, noble body and of heart, and was rich of goods." After her death the people of Catania "took the cloth that lay upon her tomb, and held it abroad against the fire" of the mountain, and the fire stopped. https://www.liturgies.net/saints/agatha/goldenlegend.htm
- The veil: Catanese tradition says it was white and turned red when it stopped the lava a year after her death. https://www.siciliapreziosa.it/storie/storia-velo-di-santagata-che-fermo-lava-etna/
- Attributes, modestly: a palm, a martyr's crown, and the pincers (tongs) held at her side. Her red veil and Etna behind her identify her without the breasts on a dish. If the dish is used, show it covered with a cloth, or with the two small rounded forms of the traditional painted cards, never anatomically.
- Background: Catania by the sea, with Etna smoking behind it.
- Look-alikes on existing cards: Lucy (a Sicilian virgin martyr with a dish) and Agnes. Keep Agatha's hair blonde and her veil red, and give her the tongs and Etna.

## Perpetua and Felicity

Face (Perpetua): a young noblewoman of about twenty-two, with dark hair bound up under a white veil and a jewelled diadem, a composed, oval face and a direct, fearless gaze.

Face (Felicity): a slightly younger woman with a simpler, rounder face, dark hair under a plain veil without jewels, and a gentle, lowered gaze.

- *Passion of Perpetua and Felicity*: Perpetua was "respectably born, liberally educated, a married matron … about twenty-two years of age", with "a son an infant at the breast". Felicity was a slave ("fellow-servant"), pregnant when arrested; in prison "she brought forth a little girl". https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0324.htm
- Perpetua's vision: "a golden ladder of marvellous height, reaching up even to heaven", with iron weapons on its sides, and "beneath the ladder itself was crouching a dragon of wonderful size". At the top, in a garden, sat "a white-haired man … in the dress of a shepherd". In the arena the women faced "a very fierce cow", and Perpetua "bound up her dishevelled hair" before she guided the gladiator's sword to her throat. Same source.
- Sant'Apollinare Nuovo, Ravenna (local copies `Sancta_Perpetua_Ravenna.jpg`, `Sancta_Felicitas_Ravenna.jpg`), which I checked: both veiled, with diadems, carrying crowns. christianiconography.info: "the one from Ravenna does put Felicity in plainer garments to remind the viewer that she was a slave"; "In second-millenium images the two saints hold long swords pointed down." https://www.christianiconography.info/perpetuaFelicity.html
- Complexion is **not attested**. They were Carthaginians, but no source describes their colouring. A warm Mediterranean complexion for both fits Carthage without claiming more.
- Dress and attributes: Perpetua in a Roman matron's stola and palla with a diadem; Felicity in a plain tunic and veil. Both hold palms, or the long swords pointed down. In the sky, the golden ladder with the dragon at its foot (Perpetua's vision). The cow is optional and small, in the arena.
- Background: the amphitheatre of Carthage, with the Gulf of Tunis beyond.
- Keep Felicity different from Perpetua by the plainer dress, the absent jewels and the softer, rounder face, as Ravenna does.

## Pairs that may still look alike

- **Padre Pio and Kolbe**: both are bearded, balding Franciscan friars. Pio is older, with a white, rounded beard, dark brows, a brown hooded Capuchin habit and mittens. Kolbe is in his forties, with a long dark beard, round spectacles and a dark caped habit. The glasses and the beard colour carry most of the difference.
- **Kolbe and Francis de Sales**: both are bald and bearded. Kolbe's beard is long, dark and pointed, and he wears glasses. Francis's is shorter, auburn and full, his face ruddy, and he wears a mozzetta.
- **Ignatius and the white-bearded elders** (Athanasius, Nicholas, the stock elder): only the grey, tapering beard and the lions separate him. Check this pair on the contact sheet.
- **Leo**: Raphael's clean-shaven type avoids the collision. The Greek and Baroque bearded type would fall into the stock elder.
- **Martin and George / the beardless youth**: both are young soldiers. Martin's fair jaw-length hair, the horse and the beggar must do the work.
- **Vianney and Borromeo**: both are clean-shaven clerics. Vianney is old, with long white hair and a surplice and stole; Borromeo is middle-aged and thin, with a prominent nose, in a cardinal's red.
- **Agatha, Lucy and Agnes**: young virgin martyrs. Agatha's red veil, blonde hair, tongs and Etna.
- **Perpetua and Felicity** against each other: jewels and matron's dress against plain dress, as in Ravenna.
