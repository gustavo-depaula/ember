# Batch 28 — the Pictorial Lives, 14 to 24 May

Ten cards from the unclaimed lines of "From the Pictorial Lives of the Saints" after batch 27: `pachomius`, `peter_dionysia`, `john_nepomucene`, `paschal_baylon`, `venantius_camerino`, `peter_celestine`, `hospitius`, `yvo`, `julia_corsica`, `donatian_rogatian`. No line in the stretch is skipped: none of the ten has a suppressed cult, though John Nepomucene and Venantius come close to the rule (see Doubts). 20 May (Bernardine of Siena) is carded already. St. Germanus (28 May) is the next unclaimed line, left for batch 29.

The card data is in `../batches/batch-28.json`, written by `../consult/batch-28/build.py` from `cards.py` (adapted from batch 27's). The script checks each excerpt verbatim in both languages against the card's chapter and, when the excerptSource says so, against its Reflection; that each `lifeChapter` exists in en-US and pt-BR, is on the card's day and is the index's chapter for it; which chapters have a `**Reflection**`/`**Reflexão**` paragraph (all ten, in both languages); that every sanctoral formulary whose title names one of the ten is listed, and would have to be the card's `proper` if it had en-US or pt-BR collect text; that each pt-BR name is the book's form; feasts against the index days; that each catalogMatch hits one unticked line no other batch claims; that no id collides; that no other card uses the chapter; initials and refs. Consulted material is in `../consult/batch-28/`: Wikipedia summaries, extracts and lead images (`*.json`, `*.txt`, `*.jpg`; sheet `wiki-sheet.jpg`, face crops `faces-crop.jpg`), the book's engravings (`book-*.jpg`, sheet `book-sheet.jpg`), existing cards compared against (`existing-sheet.jpg`, `existing-sheet-2.jpg`), and every face line of batches 1–27 (`faces-all.txt`). `fetch.sh` is the fetcher, `list.txt` its input, `sheet.py` makes the image sheets.

**Dates, names, initials.** Feasts follow the book (index days 05-14 to 05-19, 05-21 to 05-24). Names are the book's, with its pt-BR forms (São Pacômio, São Pedro e Santa Dionísia, São João Nepomuceno, São Pascoal Baylon, São Venâncio, São Pedro Celestino, Santo Hospício, Santo Ivo, Santa Júlia, São Donaciano e São Rogaciano), without the book's "Abbot", "Martyr", "Recluse", "Confessor", "Virgin". One en-US tidy: the book's "St. John Nepomucen" becomes **"St. John Nepomucene"**, the usual English form (Wikipedia: "also called John Nepomucene"); the pt-BR keeps the book's "São João Nepomuceno". "St. Yvo" keeps the book's spelling (Wikipedia: "also known as Yvo, Yves, or Ives"). None is "Blessed" in the book, so no titles change (Pachomius, Peter Celestine canonized 1313, John Nepomucene 1729, Paschal 1690, Yvo, Julia, the others by ancient cult). Ids `venantius_camerino`, `julia_corsica` keep them apart from namesakes; the pairs follow `cosmas_damian`. Initials: P, P, J, P, V, P, H, Y, J, D.

**lifeChapter and reflection.** Each card has its own chapter, the index's chapter for the day, in both languages, and each chapter has a `**Reflection**` paragraph, which is the card's reflection. None is shared with another card.

**proper.** None. No formulary in the repo with en-US or pt-BR collect text is proper to any of the ten. Three are, but only in other languages: `sanctorale.05-16.german-speaking` (Johannes Nepomuk, de only), `sanctorale.05-17.spain` (San Pascual Bailón, es only), `sanctorale.05-19.france` (Saint Yves, fr only). The script's only other title hit is `sanctorale.10-09`, St. Denis of Paris, another saint. The universal formularies on the ten book days are other saints (Matthias, John I, Christopher Magallanes, Rita).

**Excerpts.**
- From the saint's own Reflection: Pachomius (the whole of it, his own saying on simplicity), Peter and Dionysia (the whole of it, on the martyrs' patience), John Nepomucene (first sentence), Paschal (the whole of it), Venantius (first sentence), Hospitius (the whole of it, with its line of Philippians), Yvo (the first clause, "St. Yvo was a Saint amidst the dangers of the world", with a full stop), Julia (first sentence), Donatian and Rogatian (the whole of it, the book's words from Ecclesiasticus, without a reference, like `mammertus`'s Judith).
- The saint's own words from the chapter, where the Reflection is borrowed: **Peter Celestine** ("I desired nothing but a cell, and a cell they have given me", said under guard; the Reflection quotes the *Imitation of Christ*), as batch 27 did for Antoninus and Epiphanius.

## St. Pachomius (14 May)

- Wikipedia (`Pachomius.txt`, `Tabenna.txt`): born c. 292 in the Thebaid of pagan parents, conscripted at twenty-one, baptized, disciple of Palaemon, founder of the cenobitic community at Tabenna, died 348. Lead image: a modern Coptic icon, a white-bearded abbot in a dark habit, an angel bringing him a tablet. The name was read in Greek as "broad-shouldered".
- The Hermeneia (`../hermeneia-ocr.txt`, l. 7406): "Saint Pachomius, an old man, bald, with a beard divided into five points", with the angel holding out the habit. The card keeps the bald head and the five-pointed beard, at about fifty-five (he died at about fifty-six).
- The book: the conscript on the Nile, Palemon, the angel who gave him a rule at Tabenna, the monk who made two mats "to give his work to the devil". The engraving shows a hooded monk writing at a rock beside an angel with a book. The card has the rule as a small scroll in his hands, and no angel.
- **Against `macarius_alexandria`** (batch 19: wide, low, rounded-square, narrow creased eyes), `john_egypt` (batch 24: elfin triangle under a bulging bald forehead), `paul_hermit` (long, tapering, hooked nose, long hair), `john_climacus` (lean, bony rectangle, vertical furrows) and `anthony_abbot`: Pachomius is long and straight-sided, broad-shouldered, with a low, flat, broad nose, large wide-set almond eyes, full lips and the five-pointed beard.

## Sts. Peter and Dionysia (15 May)

- Wikipedia (`Lampsacus.txt`, the article "Peter, Andrew, Paul, and Denise"): martyrs of Lampsacus on the Hellespont under Decius; Dionysia "a sixteen-year-old Christian girl" who rebuked the apostate Nicomachus, was protected by an angel, escaped and was beheaded. Wikipedia joins her to Andrew and Paul; the book to Peter, who died first. No lead image.
- The book: Peter, "young though he was", bound to a wheel and thanking Christ "with a smiling countenance", then beheaded; Dionysia's rebuke, her angel, her escape and death. The engraving shows Peter on the wheel and Dionysia standing apart. The card shows both with palms before the Hellespont, and no wheel.
- **Peter against `forty_martyrs_sebaste`'s youngest** (small, narrow heart), `benjamin` (soft, round cheeks, receding chin) and `jonas_barachisius` (square-jawed): a face broad at the temples tapering in straight lines to a pointed, jutting chin, a long, thin aquiline nose, a smile. **Dionysia against `dorothy`** (small round, dimples), `euphrasia` (narrow oblong, high forehead), `genevieve` (broad, square-jawed) and the original virgin martyrs (soft ovals): a diamond, widest at the cheekbones, with up-tilted eyes, a short nose and a wide mouth.

## St. John Nepomucene (16 May)

- Wikipedia (`JohnNepomuk.txt`): born c. 1345 at Pomuk, vicar-general of the Archbishop of Prague, tortured and thrown from the Charles Bridge into the Vltava by Wenceslaus IV on 20 March 1393 in a dispute over the Kladruby abbey; "by the middle of the 15th century" his killing "was reinvented as a story about the importance of the seal of confession"; canonized 1729 as a martyr of the seal. Lead image: a reverse-glass painting with the fur almuce, a palm and the five stars.
- The book tells the legend: born 1330, chaplain to the court, the empress's confessions, silence under the rack, thrown from the bridge on 16 May, the heavenly light on the water, the incorrupt tongue. The engraving shows him in rochet and almuce, finger to his lips, before the king, the bridge through the arches.
- The card follows the traditional image (rochet, almuce, biretta, crucifix, palm, five stars, finger to the lips) before the bridge and castle of Prague. The patron line is only "Priest and martyr", not "martyr of the seal of confession", because Wikipedia gives that motive as a later legend; the excerpt and reflection are the book's.
- **Against `philip_james`'s James and `cosmas_damian`** (brown-haired, bearded young men) and `peter_chanel` (soft, square-jawed, clean-shaven): John is in his late forties, soft and long-oval, full-cheeked, with a fleshy round nose tip and large, prominent, upturned eyes.

## St. Paschal Baylon (17 May)

- Wikipedia (`Paschal.txt`): born 1540 at Torrehermosa in Aragon, shepherd till twenty-four, lay brother of the Reformed Franciscans from 1564, nearly killed by Calvinists on the way to Paris, died 17 May 1592, canonized 1690; Leo XIII made him patron of Eucharistic congresses and confraternities (1897); art gives him the monstrance. Lead image: an anonymous canvas inscribed with his name, a lay brother praying with a rosary: a long, bony face, a strong nose, dark brows, a thin moustache and a chin tuft.
- The book: the shepherd of Aragon, the lay brother, the Huguenots, the angel who showed him the Host while the bell rang for Mass in the valley. The book dates his death to 15 May 1592 (Wikipedia: 17 May); the card shows no date. The engraving shows a young shepherd kneeling under a tree with his flock.
- The card keeps the canvas's features, in the friar's habit, adoring a monstrance in the sky, with a flock and a village church in the valley behind for the book's vision.
- **Against `francis_assisi`, `anthony_padua`, `bonaventure`, `benedito`** (broad, full, laughing), `frei_galvao` (round, clean-shaven), `fidelis` (fine, narrow, full beard), `john_capistrano` (small triangle) and `bernardine_siena` (thin, pointed): Paschal is long and angular with bony cheekbones, deep nose-to-mouth creases, a hooked nose and a thin moustache.

## St. Venantius (18 May)

- Wikipedia (`Venantius.txt`): "Christian tradition holds" a boy of fifteen, tortured and beheaded at Camerino under Decius (d. 251 or 253); the torments are those of "his 11th century Acts"; patron of Camerino, his basilica outside its walls; in the General Roman Calendar 1670–1969. Lead image: a German wall painting of his torments.
- The book tells the same: the angel who saved him from scourging and fire, the judge who fell dead, the lions at his feet, the spring he drew from a rock for his thirsty guards, his beheading in 250. The engraving shows an angel lifting him from the flames. The card shows the spring beside him and Camerino on its ridge, and none of the torments.
- **Against `pancras`** (round, child's fullness), `benezet` (batch 26: narrow, long, bony, freckled), `forty_martyrs_sebaste`'s youngest (small, narrow heart) and `aloysius_gonzaga`: Venantius is a boy with a face wide and flat across the cheekbones, a broad, low-bridged nose, wide-set hooded eyes, a wide mouth, a small square chin and a blunt black fringe.

## St. Peter Celestine (19 May)

- Wikipedia (`Celestine.txt`, `Celestines.txt`): Pietro da Morrone, a hermit in a cave on Monte Morrone and on the Maiella, founder of the Celestines, elected pope in 1294, resigned on 13 December 1294, confined by Boniface VIII at Fumone, where he died on 19 May 1296 "at about the age of 81"; buried at Collemaggio. The order's tradition is that in the founder's time they wore "a coarse brown cloth". Lead image: Niccolò di Tommaso's fourteenth-century panel, a long, narrow, clean-shaven face in the tiara.
- The book: his childhood visions, three years in a mountain solitude, the rule of the Celestines, his election and resignation after four months, the boarded cell under guard ("I desired nothing but a cell, and a cell they have given me"), his death at Lauds. The engraving shows a hooded hermit in a cave cell with a cross, a road below.
- The card: the hermit's coarse habit, the tiara set aside, the psalter open, his hermitage in the Morrone cliff above the Sulmona valley. The face keeps the panel's: clean-shaven, long, narrow and straight-sided, with small, close-set, level eyes and a long upper lip.
- **Against `celestine_i`** (batch 25: massive hooked nose, broad jaw), `romuald` (bald dome, knit brows, long wavy beard), `francis_paola` (broad, square, spade beard), `peter_damian`, `pius_v` (gaunt, aquiline, white beard) and the clean-shaven old men (`hugh_cluny` full square-oval, `antoninus_florence` domed over a short face, `albinus_angers` pear, `john_kanty` high cheekbones): Peter Celestine's cheekbones are barely marked; the face falls flat and parallel to a narrow chin.

## St. Hospitius (21 May)

- Wikipedia (`Hospitius.txt`, `CapFerrat.txt`): a recluse in a ruined tower on Cap Ferrat (Cap Saint-Hospice), near Villefranche, "according to tradition" a monk from Egypt; he foretold the Lombard incursion about 575, and one of the Lombards, whose arm he healed, told his story to Gregory of Tours; died 21 May 581. No lead image; `CapFerrat.jpg` is the Pointe Saint-Hospice.
- The book: the tower near Villafranca, a league from Nice, the iron chain, bread and dates, the Lombard's stiffened arm, his death on 21 May 681. **The book's 681 is a century later than Wikipedia's 581** (the story comes from Gregory of Tours, who died in the 590s); the card shows no date.
- The engraving shows a bearded, long-haired recluse seated before a ruined arch, Lombard soldiers coming with swords. The card shows him in haircloth with the chain, bread and dates in his hand, the ruined tower on the rocky point over the bay; no soldiers.
- **Against `simeon_stylites`** (small, narrow, chiselled, eyes lifted), `john_egypt` (triangle), `macarius_alexandria`, `paul_hermit`, `jerome` and `anthony_abbot`: Hospitius's high cheekbones run flat and straight across a lean face, over sunken cheeks, with narrow, down-slanting eyes and a long, thin, straight nose.

## St. Yvo (22 May)

- Wikipedia (`Ivo.txt`): Ivo of Kermartin, 1253–1303, born near Tréguier, law at Paris and canon law at Orléans, ecclesiastical judge ("official") at Rennes and then Tréguier, "advocate of the poor", parish priest at Louannec, perhaps a Franciscan tertiary; patron of Brittany and of lawyers; attributes a purse and a rolled paper. Feast 19 May; the book keeps 22 May, and the repo's only formulary for him is France's 19 May (fr only).
- Lead image (`Ivo.jpg`): a panel of about 1450 from the workshop of Rogier van der Weyden, which Commons titles **"A Man Reading (Saint Ivo?)"**, with the question mark: a lean, clean-shaven man in a fur-lined gown and black cap reading a document. It is a century and a half after his death, and the identification is uncertain. The card borrows its dress and its lean, long-chinned, clean-shaven face as a type, not as a likeness.
- The book: noble family near Tréguier, studies at Paris and Orléans, vow of chastity, priesthood against his humility, judge of Rennes, "the advocate and lawyer of the poor", his hospital and his corn, his death in 1303. The engraving shows a priest in a surplice among the sick and poor under a bridge.
- **Against `fidelis`** (the other lawyer-saint, fine-boned with a full beard), `casimir` (long, narrow, young), `john_avila` (long, gaunt, narrow jaw), `ludger` (long oblong, small eyes, large ears) and `peter_celestine` in this batch: Yvo is widest at the cheekbones with hollows beneath, a bony brow ridge, a long, square-tipped chin, a low-set fleshy nose tip and a pouting lower lip.

## St. Julia (23 May)

- Wikipedia (`Julia.txt`, `Nonza.txt`): a Carthaginian girl sold into the service of Eusebius after Genseric took Carthage in 439, crucified on Cap Corse, the account from Victor of Vita as edited by Ruinart; relics at Santa Giulia in Brescia; with Devota, patroness of Corsica (declared 1809); the traditional site at Nonza. Lead image: a nineteenth-century window at Meaux, a veiled woman with a cross and a palm.
- The book tells the same: the slave's patience, the governor Felix, her answer that she was free while she served Christ, her death on a cross, the monks of Gorgona, her relics at Brescia. The engraving shows her standing apart from her master's pagan feast.
- The card: a slave's plain tunic and veil, a small wooden cross and a palm, Nonza's rock over the sea with a merchant ship at anchor. No torments.
- **Against `saragossa_martyrs`'s Engratia** (batch 26: long oval, square-tipped chin, strong straight nose), `euphrasia`, `apollonia` (short, wide, Fayum eyes), `mary_egypt` and the original virgin martyrs: Julia's profile is softly convex, from a sloping forehead through a gently curved nose to a rounded, prominent chin, with heavy dark brows and a wide mouth.

## Sts. Donatian and Rogatian (24 May)

- Wikipedia (`Donatian.txt`): "les enfants nantais", martyred at Nantes under Maximian about 288–290, known from a fifth-century Passion; sons, it seems, of the first magistrate of the city; Donatian the younger, baptized, converted Rogatian; pierced by a lictor's spear and beheaded; their basilica stands on the site of the family villa. Lead image: the statue of Donatian at the portal of Nantes cathedral.
- The book: Donatian converts his elder brother, Rogatian cannot be baptized because the bishop is in hiding, the kiss of peace, their night of prayer, the rack, their hands pierced and cut off, about 287. The engraving shows two young people in prison, one kneeling in chains. The card shows the two brothers side by side, Donatian's hand on Rogatian's shoulder, the Loire at Nantes behind; no torments.
- **Against `faustinus_jovita`** (batch 21: long rectangular bearded elder; short, round, snub-nosed younger), `nereus_achilleus`, `adrian_eubulus` and `philip_james`: Rogatian is wide, flat and square with a low hairline, small deep-set eyes and a close beard; Donatian is a beardless oval with high rounded cheekbones, a slightly upturned nose, blue eyes, a dimpled chin and short, straight fair hair.

## Doubts

- **John Nepomucene.** His cult is not suppressed (canonized 1729, the German-speaking lands keep his Mass), but Wikipedia says the confessional motive the book tells, and the reflection praises, was "reinvented" in the fifteenth century; he was killed in 1393 in a dispute between the king and the archbishop. The card keeps his traditional image and patron line "Priest and martyr", and the reflection is the book's. If "rests on a discredited story" should cover this, the line is skipped and St. Germanus (28 May) comes in.
- **Venantius.** Wikipedia gives his torments only as "Christian tradition" from eleventh-century Acts; his feast left the General Calendar in 1969, but he is still the patron of Camerino with his basilica, so the card stands, like `vitalis_ravenna` in batch 27, showing only the spring and a palm.
- **"St. John Nepomucene"** is our en-US tidy of the book's "Nepomucen". Alternative: keep the book's spelling, as batch 27 kept "Mammertus".
- **Yvo's face** rests on a portrait whose identification is only "Saint Ivo?"; if that is too weak, the face stays but the basis should call it wholly our choice.
- **Peter Celestine's face** follows a fourteenth-century panel that shows him clean-shaven; later images (not consulted) often give him a white beard.

## Look-alike risks that remain

- **Pachomius and Hospitius against the desert elders** (`anthony_abbot`, `macarius_alexandria`, `paul_hermit`, `john_egypt`, `john_silent`): check Pachomius's five-pointed beard, bald dome, flat broad nose and large almond eyes; Hospitius's long hair, flat high cheekbones and down-slanting eyes. Reject a white-bearded elder for either: both are dark-bearded, greying.
- **John Nepomucene against the brown-bearded stock face** (`philip_james`'s James, `cosmas_damian`): the Baroque type demands it. Reject a thirties man with curls, or a Christ-like face; he is near fifty, soft and full-cheeked, with prominent upturned eyes, short straight hair under the biretta.
- **Peter Celestine against `hugh_cluny`, `antoninus_florence` and `john_kanty`**: the flat, straight-sided narrow face with small close-set eyes is the check; reject marked cheekbones or a domed bald cranium.
- **Yvo against `peter_celestine`** (both clean-shaven and long-faced in this batch): Yvo is fifty, dark-haired, wide at the cheekbones with hollows beneath and a long square-tipped chin; Celestine is eighty, white, narrow and flat-cheeked.
- **Venantius against `pancras` and `aloysius_gonzaga`**: he must read as a boy of fifteen with a wide, flat face and a blunt fringe, not a round child or a slender youth.
- **Donatian against `philip_james`'s Philip**: short, straight fair hair, blue eyes and a dimpled chin, not brown waves.
- **Dionysia and Julia against the original virgin martyrs** (`agnes`, `lucy`, `philomena`, `cecilia`: soft ovals with long hair): reject a soft oval; Dionysia is a diamond with up-tilted eyes, Julia a convex profile with a prominent chin, both veiled.
- **Paschal**: reject a young shepherd's face; he is a man of forty-five in the habit, with the monstrance, as the patron line says.
