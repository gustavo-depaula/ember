# Batch 2 of new cards: faces

Faces, dress and settings for ten more cards, four of them group cards. As in `first-batch.md`, each "Face:" line uses only marks with a source quoted below it, and where a mark is an artistic choice made to keep two cards apart, the entry says so.

The existing stock faces to avoid are a man in his thirties with brown curls and a short beard, a white-bearded elder, and a beardless youth. "Dionysius" is the *Painter's Manual* in Didron's French (1845), https://archive.org/details/manueldiconograp00dion (local copy `../consult/prelates-src/didron-fr.txt`). Pictures and texts consulted for this batch are in `../consult/batch-2/`, with contact sheets `_sheet1.jpg` to `_sheet4.jpg`.

Excerpts come from the feast's formulary in `content/of/formularies/sanctoral/` unless the entry cites a public-domain text; those are rendered into Portuguese by us.

## Martyrs of Asia and Africa: group cards

## Charles Lwanga and companions

Face (Charles): a tall, athletic Muganda man of about twenty-five, with chestnut-brown skin, close-cropped black hair, clean-shaven, a handsome oval face with high cheekbones, a broad, calm brow and a steady, protective gaze.

Face (Kizito): a boy of thirteen, smaller and rounder in the face, with dark-brown skin, close-cropped hair, large bright eyes and an eager, trusting look.

- The *Dictionary of African Christian Biography*, after Faupel's *African Holocaust*: "Like Kaddu, his colouring was chestnut brown"; he entered royal service "then aged about eighteen" (c. 1878) and was burned in 1886. https://dacb.org/stories/uganda/lwanga-charles/
- Parish biographies call him "the most handsome man of the Kingdom" and the court's strongest athlete (NCR, "Black Saints: Charles Lwanga", https://www.ncronline.org/blogs/ncr-today/black-saints-charles-lwanga). These are late sources, so the face uses only "athletic" and "handsome" in the idealising sense every card has.
- Wikipedia, *Uganda Martyrs*: Kizito was "13–14", the youngest of the Catholic martyrs; Lwanga is listed as "patron saint of African youth and Catholic action". The window at the Munyonyo shrine (local copy `Lwanga_Kizito.jpg`), which I checked, shows Charles pouring baptismal water on a kneeling Kizito, both in bark-cloth coloured robes. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St._Kizito_being_baptised_by_St._Charles_Lwanga_at_Munyonyo.jpg
- No photograph of Lwanga exists. The "Karoli Lwanga and his followers" image on Wikipedia (local copy) is a modern painted panel, too small to use for a face.
- Dress: a long white kanzu with a bark-cloth mantle knotted on one shoulder. The dress of the pages is not attested for these two in particular; white kanzu and bark-cloth are what Ugandan images give them.
- Pose: Charles's hand on Kizito's shoulder carries the protector role without showing the prison or the fire.
- Background: the Lake Victoria shore at Munyonyo, where the king's lakeside residence was and where Kizito was baptised, with banana groves and round thatched houses. Namugongo, the place of execution, is the alternative, but it would invite the pyre.
- Look-alike: Moses the Black is the only other African man on a card; he is bearded, older and in a desert. Charles is young and clean-shaven.

## Paul Miki and companions

Face (Paul): a Japanese man of about thirty-three, clean-shaven, with short-cropped black hair, a lean oval face with high cheekbones, fine straight brows, narrow dark eyes and lips slightly parted as if preaching, his gaze raised.

Face (Louis Ibaraki): a Japanese boy of twelve with round cheeks, glossy black hair tied back in a short tail, bright eager eyes and a small, joyful smile.

- Butler, *Lives of the Saints* (1864), on the martyrs of Japan: "one was Paul Miki, a noble Japanese, and an eminent preacher, at that time thirty-three years old"; among the twenty-six were "three boys who used to serve the friars at mass; two of them were fifteen years of age, and the third only twelve" (the twelve-year-old is Louis Ibaraki). They "were fastened to crosses by cords and chains … The crosses were planted in a row, about four feet asunder". https://www.ewtn.com/catholicism/library/martyrs-of-japan-5735
- Franciscan Media: "Brother Paul Miki, a Jesuit and a native of Japan"; "26 martyrs of Japan were crucified on a hill, now known as the Holy Mountain, overlooking Nagasaki." https://www.franciscanmedia.org/saint-of-the-day/saint-paul-miki-and-companions/
- The painting of c. 1635 (Wikipedia lead image of the Twenty-six Martyrs, local copy), which I checked: a row of crosses on a hill above the bay with ships. Too small for faces. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Martyrdom-of-Paul-Miki-and-Companions-in-Nagasaki-(made-c1635).png
- The Bamberg statue (Wikipedia lead image of Paul Miki) is a modern white figure with a cross and lance, no usable face.
- No portrait exists. The face is a choice within the sources: a Japanese man in his thirties, a preacher. Short hair rather than a samurai topknot, because he was a Jesuit religious; whether Japanese Jesuit brothers shaved the head in 1597 I could not confirm.
- Dress and attributes: a plain black Jesuit cassock, a crucifix and palm, a plain wooden cross behind his shoulder. Louis in a boy's kimono and hakama (a choice; his dress is not recorded).
- Background: Nishizaka hill above Nagasaki bay in February, pines, tiled roofs, a Portuguese carrack in the bay, and a distant line of small crosses with no figures on them.
- Look-alikes: Francis Xavier and Ignatius wear the same black. Paul is Japanese and clean-shaven, and has the boy beside him.

## Andrew Kim Taegon, Paul Chong Hasang and companions

Face (Andrew): a slender Korean man of twenty-five with a smooth oval face, fine black brows, narrow dark eyes, a thin black moustache and a small tuft of beard on the chin, his black hair gathered up under the gat, and a serene, direct gaze.

Face (Paul): a sturdier Korean layman of about forty-five, with a broader face, lines at the eyes, a fuller short black beard with moustache, and a warm, earnest gaze.

- The oil portrait by Louis Chang Bal (Tjyang Louis), c. 1920, reproduced by the Paris Foreign Missions in *Le catholicisme en Corée* (1924) (local copy `Kim_Jang_Bal_1920.jpg`), which I checked: a black gat tied under the chin, a white hanbok coat, a thin moustache and a small chin tuft, a book in one hand and a palm in the other. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:V%C3%A9n%C3%A9rable_Andr%C3%A9_Kim,_photographie_d%E2%80%99une_peinture_%C3%A0_l%E2%80%99huile_de_Tjyang_Louis_-_1920.jpg. UCA News reports a second version of the same 1920 portrait as "one of the oldest portraits" of him.
- The engraving "Père André Kim" in Launay, *La Corée et les missionnaires* (1901), p. 267 (local copy `Kim_Launay_1901.jpg`), which I checked: the same broad-brimmed gat with chin ties and a moustache, a heavier face than the painting. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Launay,_Dallet_-_La_Cor%C3%A9e_et_les_missionnaires,_1901-p267.jpg
- Neither is from life (he died in 1846), but both predate modern devotional art and agree on the gat, hanbok and moustache.
- Paul Chong Hasang (1795–1839, died at 44–45) was a layman who went to Beijing repeatedly and wrote to the Pope asking for a diocese in Korea; Wikipedia gives his attributes as "Hanbok and gat, crucifix, palm of martyrdom". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Chong_Hasang. No portrait: he is made older and fuller-bearded than Andrew, and his coat is grey-blue against Andrew's white, to tell them apart.
- Andrew wears a red stole over the hanbok as the only sign of priesthood, as many Korean images do.
- Background: the Han River at Saenamteo near Seoul, where Andrew was beheaded in 1846, with hills, pines and tiled roofs.
- Name: the card says "Sts. Andrew Kim Taegon and Companions" for length; Paul Chong is one of the companions shown.

## Andrew Dung-Lac and companions

Face (Andrew): a Vietnamese man of about forty-four with a lean, high-cheekboned face, a warm golden complexion, clean-shaven, black hair under a black khăn đóng, calm dark almond eyes and a gentle, resolute expression.

Face (Peter Thi): an elderly Vietnamese priest of about seventy-six, thin and slightly stooped, with a sparse white moustache and a wispy white chin beard, and deep lines around kind, narrowed eyes.

- Wikipedia: born c. 1795, beheaded in Hanoi on 21 December 1839 with "Fr. Peter Thí, whom he had visited for confession"; he took the name Lạc while hiding after his 1835 arrest. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_D%C5%A9ng-L%E1%BA%A1c
- Peter Trương Văn Thi was born in 1763 at Kẻ Sở, arrested with Andrew on 10 November 1839, ransomed, rearrested and beheaded with him at Ô Cầu Giấy. https://www.vinhsonliem.org/2017/05/20/phero-truong-van-thi-1763-1839/
- The statue "Thánh An-rê Dũng Lạc" (local copy `DungLac_1.jpg`) and the Westerville window (Wikipedia lead image, local copy), which I checked: clean-shaven, a round black khăn đóng, a long tunic with a stole, palm and book. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Anre_Dung_Lac.jpg
- No portrait from life. Peter Thi's features are a choice: an old man, to separate him from Andrew, and Vietnamese, with a thin white beard unlike the stock elder's.
- Dress: long dark tunics under white surplices with red stoles, the khăn đóng; palms, a crucifix and a rosary.
- Background: the edge of Hanoi near the Cầu Giấy gate, rice paddies, bamboo, a river and a village church with curved tiled roofs.

## Bishops and a philosopher

## Cornelius and Cyprian

Face (Cornelius): a man of about sixty, bald on the crown with a close fringe of grey hair, a short, neat grey beard, large dark almond eyes, a long straight nose and a calm, grave face.

Face (Cyprian): a man of about fifty with a warm olive-brown North African complexion, thick, tightly curled black hair, a full black beard parted into two points at the chin, dark eyes and an expression both grave and serene.

- The 12th-century apse mosaic of Santa Maria in Trastevere (Wikipedia lead image of Pope Cornelius, local copy), which I checked: bald crown with grey hair around it, a short grey beard, large dark eyes, a dark chasuble with the white pallium. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pope_Cornelius_%E2%80%93_Santa_Maria_in_Trastevere.jpg
- Cornelius's attribute: "In iconography, Cornelius' attribute was the horn, in reference to the Latin origin of his name, from cornu" (Wikipedia, Pope Cornelius). The triple cross is a usual papal attribute on later cards.
- Dionysius, p. 320, among the hierarchs: "Saint Cyprien : jeune, cheveux frisés, barbe grande et séparée en deux" (young, curly hair, a large beard parted in two). The list is of bishops, so this is likely Cyprian of Carthage, but Dionysius also lists Cyprian of Antioch (2 October, p. 386: "vieillard, barbe arrondie; suivant d'autres : jeune, chauve, barbe séparée en deux"). Treat it as supporting, not decisive.
- The Greek icon of Cyprian of Carthage (Wikipedia lead image, local copy), which I checked: dark curly hair, a dark full beard, omophorion and book. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Cyprian_von_Karthago2.jpg
- Pontius, *Life of Cyprian* 6: "His countenance was grave and joyous … His dress was not out of harmony with his countenance." https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0505.htm
- Complexion: not attested. Olive-brown fits a Carthaginian without claiming more, as with Perpetua and Felicity.
- Dress: both bareheaded, as in the mosaic and the icon. Cornelius in a red chasuble with pallium, triple cross and horn; Cyprian in a red cope, a book and a palm.
- Background: the Appian Way behind Cornelius (buried in the catacomb of Callixtus), the bay of Carthage behind Cyprian.
- Excerpt: Cyprian, *On the Unity of the Church* 6, "He can no longer have God for his Father, who has not the Church for his mother" (ANF vol. 5, https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/050701.htm), which fits the collect's prayer for the unity of the Church.
- Not Bartholomew (black curly hair and beard): Cyprian's beard is forked and his skin darker, and he stands beside the grey pope.

## Justin

Face: a man of about sixty with thick, wavy salt-and-pepper hair brushed back from a high forehead to the nape, a full, broad, square-cut beard, dark streaked with grey, heavy dark brows, deep-set eyes and a searching, thoughtful gaze.

- Dionysius, p. 397, calendar for June: "Saint Justin le Philosophe : vieillard, grande barbe" (an old man with a large beard).
- Theophanes the Cretan, Stavronikita (16th c., Wikipedia lead image, local copy), which I checked: thick dark hair, a full dark beard, a red tunic under a rose philosopher's cloak, a scroll. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saint_Justin_Martyr_by_Theophanes_the_Cretan.jpg
- The two disagree on age. He died c. 165, probably in his sixties. Grey-streaked dark hair and beard sit between them and keep him off both the stock elder and Bartholomew.
- The philosopher's cloak: in the *Dialogue with Trypho* 1, Trypho greets him because he wears the philosopher's dress ("those who array themselves in this dress"). https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/01281.htm
- Dress: a lay philosopher's blue-grey pallium over an ochre tunic, no vestments; a scroll or book (the *Apology*) and a palm.
- Background: Rome, where he taught and was beheaded under the prefect Rusticus, with a colonnaded portico and the Palatine.
- Excerpt: the communion antiphon (1 Cor 2:2), which answers the collect's "folly of the Cross".

## Irenaeus

Face: a man of about sixty with a long, narrow face and a high forehead, straight ash-brown hair to the ears, a long, straight ash-brown beard falling to the chest and silvering at the chin, light grey eyes and a gentle, peaceable gaze.

- Dionysius has no entry for Irenaeus of Lyon (the one Irenaeus in his calendar is a young deacon).
- The Orthodox icon "Saint Irenaeus of Lyons" (local copy `Irenaeus_icon.jpg`), which I checked: a long, narrow face, a long straight brown beard with grey, a mitre and a book. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saint_Irenaeus_icon.jpg
- The window at Lyon (Wikipedia lead image, local copy), which I checked: a greying beard, balding, a mitre, a pink chasuble. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saint_irenee_saint_irenee.jpg
- Wikipedia: a Greek from Smyrna who heard Polycarp, second bishop of Lugdunum after Pothinus; declared Doctor of the Church in 2022 with the title *Doctor unitatis*; the circumstances of his death are unknown. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irenaeus. Hence "Doctor of Unity" as the card's title rather than "Bishop and martyr".
- Colour and age are a choice: ash-brown silvering, not white, to stay off the stock elder, Athanasius and Ignatius of Antioch.
- Dress: a white mitre, red chasuble, crozier and a large book (*Against Heresies*).
- Background: Lyon at the confluence, Fourvière hill with the basilica and the Roman theatre.
- Excerpt: *Against Heresies* IV.20.7, "For the glory of God is a living man; and the life of man consists in beholding God" (ANF vol. 1, https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0103420.htm), shortened by dropping "For".

## Boniface

Face: a sturdy Anglo-Saxon man of about fifty with a broad, ruddy, weathered face, fair flaxen hair cut short below the mitre, a short, square-trimmed flaxen beard flecked with grey, clear blue eyes and a resolute, open gaze.

- The Fulda Sacramentary (c. 975, local copy `Fulda_Boniface.jpg`, crop `_fulda_crop.jpg`), which I checked: in both scenes a beardless, nimbed cleric; below, he holds a book over his head against the sword. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:St_Boniface_-_Baptising-Martyrdom_-_Sacramentary_of_Fulda_-_11Century.jpg
- Later images give him a beard: Cornelis Bloemaert's engraving (Wikipedia lead image, local copy), a long white beard with mitre, sword and a book pierced by it; Henschel's statue at Fulda (1842, local copy `Boniface_Fulda_statue.jpg`), bareheaded with cross and book; in the small photograph he appears bearded.
- christianiconography.info: his attributes are a "book pierced by a sword" and an "axe" for the oak "sacred to Thor"; "a great many images dispense with the attributes and simply present the saint in his episcopal regalia with a book, mitre, and crozier." https://www.christianiconography.info/boniface.html
- Willibald, *Life*, ch. 6 (tr. Robinson, 1916): at Geismar he set out "to fell a certain oak of extraordinary size, which is called, by an old name of the pagans, the Oak of Jupiter"; it fell and "burst into four parts", and he "built from the timber of the tree a wooden oratory, and dedicated it in honor of Saint Peter". https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintboni00robiuoft (local copy `willibald_robinson.txt`)
- Age: born c. 675; Geismar was c. 723, when he was about fifty; he was about seventy-nine at Dokkum. I chose the Geismar age and flaxen colouring: the white-bearded old bishop of Bloemaert is the stock elder, and the Fulda clean-shaven type would collide with Stanislaus in this same batch. Anglo-Saxon fairness is a choice, not attested.
- Dress: mitre, green chasuble with pallium (he was archbishop), crozier; the pierced book (no blood) and the axe by the felled oak.
- Background: the wooded hills of Hesse with the small wooden oratory.
- Excerpt: Willibald, ch. 8, his words at Dokkum: "Men and brethren, be of stout heart, and fear not them who kill the body, since they are not able to slay the soul" (Robinson 1916, public domain), shortened.

## Josaphat

Face: a lean man of about forty with a long, narrow, ascetic face, hollow cheeks, deep-set dark eyes, a straight nose, a dark-brown moustache and a full dark-brown beard of medium length squared at the chin, and a steady, gentle gaze.

- The drawing after his portrait, 1620s–1630s (local copy `Josaphat_1620s.jpg`), which I checked: a long face with a full beard and moustache under a Byzantine crown-mitre, omophorion over the robe. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:%D0%9D%D0%B5%D0%B2%D1%8F%D0%B4%D0%BE%D0%BC%D1%8B_%D0%B0%D1%9E%D1%82%D0%B0%D1%80._%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%8E%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%BA_%D0%B7_%D0%BF%D0%B0%D1%80%D1%82%D1%80%D1%8D%D1%82%D0%B0_%D0%AF%D0%B7%D0%B0%D1%84%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B0_%D0%9A%D1%83%D0%BD%D1%86%D1%8D%D0%B2%D1%96%D1%87%D0%B0._1620%E2%80%941630-%D1%8F_%D0%B3%D0%B3.jpg
- The woodcut of 1625 (local copy `Josaphat_1625.jpg`), which I checked: crown-mitre, a full dark beard and moustache, sakkos and omophorion with crosses, a hand cross; the frame says he was martyred at Vitebsk in 1623.
- The icon on Wikipedia (local copy `JKuncewicz.jpg`) shows him as a monk in a black klobuk with a brown beard, a lean face. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josaphat_Kuntsevych
- Wikipedia: born c. 1580, Archbishop of Polotsk from 1618, killed at Vitebsk on 12 November 1623; he "wore a hair shirt and a chain around his waist". Hence the ascetic face; keep it lean, not gaunt.
- Dress: the Byzantine-rite archbishop, as in the portraits: gold sakkos, white omophorion with crosses, crown-mitre, enkolpion, hand cross, palm. The axe of his martyrdom is left out.
- Background: Polotsk on the Western Dvina with the Cathedral of Holy Wisdom.
- Excerpt: the communion antiphon. The en-US text in the formulary reads "Whoever loses his life for my sake, will find it in eternity, say the Lord"; the card drops the attribution and the stray comma. The pt-BR text has no "in eternity"; both are kept as the formulary gives them.

## Stanislaus

Face: a man of about fifty, clean-shaven, with a full, broad face, heavy-lidded dark eyes lowered in thought, a straight nose, short dark hair greying at the temples below the mitre, and a grave, gentle expression.

- Stanisław Samostrzelnik's miniature (c. 1530s, Wikipedia lead image, local copy, crop `_stan_crop.jpg`), which I checked: clean-shaven, a full face with heavy lids and lowered eyes, a jewelled mitre and cope, an angel holding the crozier. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Stanis%C5%82aw_Samostrzelnik,_%C5%9Aw_Stanis%C5%82aw.jpg
- Tadeusz Kuntze, *The Raising of Piotrowin* (18th c., local copy), which I checked: a clean-shaven bishop with dark hair in mitre and cope raising the shrouded Piotr. The late-Gothic panel in the Ciołek Palace, Kraków (local copy `Stanislaus_Ciolek.jpg`), is also clean-shaven. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Tadeusz_Kuntze_-_Wskrzeszenie_Piotrowina.jpg
- Wikipedia: born 1030, killed 11 April 1079 aged 48, "while he was celebrating Mass in the Skałka outside the walls of Kraków"; "usually depicted as a bishop holding a sword … and sometimes with Piotr rising from the dead at his feet"; patron of Poland and Kraków. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislaus_of_Szczepan%C3%B3w
- Dress: mitre, red chasuble, crozier, a clean sword held by the hilt; Piotr small in a white shroud, optional.
- Background: Wawel hill with the cathedral above the Vistula, and the Skałka church.

## Pairs that may still look alike

- **Stanislaus and Leo the Great**: both clean-shaven, heavy-faced prelates. Leo wears the tiara and is in his sixties; Stanislaus has dark hair, lowered heavy-lidded eyes, a mitre and a sword. Check them side by side.
- **Stanislaus and Charles Borromeo**: both clean-shaven; Borromeo is thin with a prominent nose and wears a cardinal's red, not a mitre.
- **Irenaeus and Josaphat**: both long-faced bishops with long beards. Irenaeus is older, ash-brown silvering, in a Latin mitre; Josaphat is dark-brown, younger, in the Byzantine crown-mitre and omophorion.
- **Irenaeus and Ignatius of Antioch**: Ignatius is bald with a grey tapering beard; Irenaeus keeps his hair and his beard is straight and brown-grey.
- **Justin and Mark / Peter**: grey-haired, bearded. Justin's hair is long, wavy and salt-and-pepper, his beard broad and square, and he wears no vestments.
- **Cyprian and Bartholomew**: black curly hair and beard. The forked beard, darker skin and cope separate Cyprian.
- **Cornelius and the balding bishops** (Ignatius of Antioch, Athanasius): Cornelius's beard is short and neat and he wears the pallium with the horn.
- **Boniface and Francis de Sales / Gregory the Great**: bearded mitred bishops. Boniface's beard is flaxen and square-trimmed, his face ruddy and weathered, and the axe and oak mark him. His fair colouring is a choice and must be kept, or he falls back into the stock.
- **The two Andrews**: both "A" initials and both Asian priests with companions. Andrew Kim is young with a moustache and chin tuft in a white hanbok and gat; Andrew Dung-Lac is in his forties, clean-shaven, in a khăn đóng and surplice, beside an old man.
- **Paul Miki and Francis Xavier / Ignatius**: same black Jesuit dress; Paul is Japanese, clean-shaven, with a boy beside him.
- **Kizito and Louis Ibaraki**: both boy companions; they differ by ethnicity, dress and setting, but check that neither card reads as a generic "man and boy".

Face lines in `../batches/batch-2.json` were given explicit bone structure on 2026-09-29 (face shape, nose, eyes, brows, cheekbones, jaw, mouth before hair, beard and gaze), because lines of age, hair and beard alone produced one shared underlying face; the structures were chosen so that no two faces across batches 1–5 and the existing cards share one. The "Face:" lines above keep the sourced marks but not the added structure.
