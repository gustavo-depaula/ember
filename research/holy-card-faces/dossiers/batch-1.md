# Batch 1 of new cards: faces

Faces, dress and settings for ten new cards (two of them group cards), consumed through `../batches/batch-1.json`. Each "Face:" line uses only marks with a source quoted below it. Where a mark is an artistic choice made to keep two cards apart, the entry says so.

The stock faces to avoid are a man in his thirties with brown curls and a short beard, a white-bearded elder, and a beardless youth. "Dionysius" is the *Painter's Manual* in Didron's French (1845), https://archive.org/details/manueldiconograp00dion (local copy `../consult/prelates-src/didron-fr.txt`); the English is my translation. Pictures and texts consulted for this batch are in `../consult/batch-1/` (the Wikipedia/Commons lead images, a contact sheet of them in `refs.jpg`, contact sheets of the existing cards in `others.jpg` and `women.jpg`, and Forbes's *Pope Pius the Tenth* in `forbes-pius-x.txt`).

Excerpts: formulary text is copied from `content/of/formularies/sanctoral/MM-DD.json`. Sayings are from public-domain sources, cited per entry; where the pt-BR (or both languages) is "our rendering", it is translated from the Latin or English quoted there.

## Moderns: faces from photographs

## John Bosco

Face: a sturdy man of about fifty with a square, clean-shaven face, broad high cheekbones, thick wavy dark-brown hair with the first grey, brushed back from the forehead, lively dark eyes and a broad, warm, fatherly smile.

- The 1880 Turin photograph (Commons, local copy `John_Bosco.jpg`), which I checked: a broad, square, clean-shaven face, thick wavy hair brushed back, deep smile lines, a black cassock with a small collar and bow. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Don_Bosco_@_Torino,_1880_(original).jpg
- The Salesian Bulletin on his photographs: "curly hair", a "square face", "a typical peasant figure" with "two large cheekbone folds very pronounced"; in the Nice photograph of 1885, at almost seventy, "a man already old and tired, with a face marked by the fevers", with "a gaze that … is still alive, intelligent and shrewd". https://salesianbulletin.org/2023/01/29/getting-to-know-don-bosco-images/
- Left out: the tired, fever-marked face of the last photographs and the dull right eye. Fifty is the age of the Valdocco years and of the familiar image.
- Dress and attributes: the black cassock; a boy at his side (his work for poor boys, "father and teacher of the young" in the collect); Mary Help of Christians, to whom he built the basilica.
- Background: Turin, the Basilica of Mary Help of Christians at Valdocco (consecrated 1868), with the Alps.
- Excerpt: his motto. In the *Life of Dominic Savio* (1859), ch. 8, Dominic reads the card on Don Bosco's wall: "*Da mihi animas caetera tolle*. He looked at them attentively and I helped him to translate them as follows: *Give me souls, and take away everything else*." https://www.sdb.org/en/Don_Bosco_Resources/Writings/Books/Life_of_the_young_Dominic_Savio. The pt-BR "Dai-me almas e ficai com o resto" is the rendering Brazilian Salesians use; "*cetera tolle*" is literally "take the rest".
- Not Vianney (thin, long white hair) nor Borromeo (thin, prominent nose, cardinal's red): Bosco is broad, dark-haired and smiling.

## Pius X

Face: a man of about seventy with a broad, handsome, open face and strong clear-cut features, clean-shaven, thick short silver-white hair under the white zucchetto, clear light eyes under straight brows and a kind, winning smile.

- The official photograph by Histed (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Pope_Pius_X.jpg`), which I checked: a broad, full, clean-shaven face, short white hair, white zucchetto and cassock, mozzetta, pectoral cross on a cord. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Pius_X,_by_Ernest_Walter_Histed_(retouched).jpg
- F. A. Forbes, *Pope Pius the Tenth* (1918), quoting a member of the French government in Venice: "A man of personal fascination and splendid presence … with handsome open face and strong clear-cut features, softened by eyes in which shines the light of perpetual youth." As cardinal: "a noble manly figure, full of dignity and sweetness, blessing the crowd with the winning smile that was characteristic of him." A missionary bishop after an audience: "I could see nothing but those eyes, and the light of them I shall never forget." https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/35953 (local copy `forbes-pius-x.txt`)
- Eye colour is not settled: some popular accounts call them blue, others dark. The face line says only "clear light eyes".
- Dress and attributes: white cassock and zucchetto, red mozzetta and stole, pectoral cross. The ciborium, the Host and the child in a First Communion veil stand for *Quam singulari* (1910), which admitted children to Communion at the age of reason; hence "Pope of the Eucharist", as he is called in Brazil.
- Background: the Vatican gardens and the dome of St. Peter's. Venice (he was its Patriarch) is the alternative, and would set the card apart from Leo's and Peter's Roman views.
- Excerpt: the collect, "to safeguard the Catholic faith and to restore all things in Christ" / "restaurar todas as coisas em Cristo", which is also his motto, *Instaurare omnia in Christo*.

## Teresa Benedicta of the Cross (Edith Stein)

Face: a woman of about fifty with an oval, pale face, strong straight dark eyebrows, deep-set dark-brown eyes, a long straight nose and full, closed lips; a serious, thoughtful, steady gaze with a faint gentleness about the mouth.

- The "passport" photograph taken at the door of the Cologne Carmel before she left for Echt, c. 1938–39 (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Edith_Stein.jpg`), which I checked: heavy straight dark brows, deep-set dark eyes, a strong straight nose, a full mouth, a grave expression, the black veil and white wimple. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Edith_Stein_(ca._1938-1939).jpg
- Wikipedia: born 12 October 1891, killed 9 August 1942 at fifty; entered "the Discalced Carmelite monastery St. Maria vom Frieden … in Cologne-Lindenthal in October 1933"; attributes: "Discalced Carmelite nun's habit (sometimes with a Yellow badge), cross, a book or scroll with Hebrew letters, burning bush, martyr's palm." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edith_Stein
- Dress and attributes: the Discalced Carmelite habit (brown habit and scapular, black veil, white wimple, white mantle), a cross, a book (the philosopher), a palm. The yellow badge and the camp are left out to keep the card devotional.
- Background: Cologne with its cathedral, where she entered Carmel. Echt is the alternative.
- Excerpt: her writings are in copyright, so the formulary. The collect has no self-contained short sentence; the card uses its petition clause, "that the whole human race may acknowledge Christ as its Savior" / "que todas as pessoas reconheçam o Cristo Salvador". The two languages differ slightly ("human race" / "todas as pessoas"); both are verbatim.
- Not Teresa of Ávila or Thérèse (same habit): she is older, with heavy dark brows and a grave rather than smiling face.

## Ancients and medievals: faces from tradition

## Scholastica

Face: a woman of about forty with a soft, round, fair face, grey-blue eyes, gently arched light-brown brows, rosy cheeks and a warm, quiet smile; her gaze is lifted a little upward.

- No portrait or description survives. Gregory the Great, *Dialogues* II.33–34, is the only early source: her visit to Benedict, her prayer, the storm, and "the soul of his sister … in the likeness of a dove to ascend into heaven". https://www.tertullian.org/fathers/gregory_02_dialogues_book2.htm
- Wikipedia: "a ninth-century tradition makes her the twin sister of Benedict"; attributes "in Benedictine religious habit, with crozier and crucifix; with dove flying from her mouth"; her community lay about five miles from Monte Cassino. The lead image is the Kleinmariazell altar (1765, local copy `Scholastica.jpg`), which I checked: black habit, white wimple, the dove above. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scholastica
- The face is an artistic choice: round, fair and smiling, younger than Hildegard and Rita, so the three black-habited nuns of this batch differ. The twin tradition does not help, since the Benedict card is an old bearded man.
- Dress and attributes: black Benedictine habit and veil, white wimple, the Rule, an abbess's crozier, the white dove. The dove rises from her rather than "from her mouth".
- Background: Monte Cassino from the valley, with rain clouds for the storm.
- Excerpt: Gregory's comment on the storm, *Dialogues* II.33, Latin "iusto valde iudicio illa plus potuit, quae amplius amauit" (https://la.wikisource.org/wiki/Dialogi_(Gregorius_Magnus)/Liber_II); the 1608 English has "of right she did more which loved more". Card: "She could do more, because she loved more." / "Pôde mais aquela que mais amou." The collect ("serve you with pure love") was the alternative.

## Martha, Mary and Lazarus

Face (Martha): a woman of about thirty-five, strong and wholesome, with a round face, rosy cheeks, chestnut hair tucked under a white head-cloth, steady brown eyes and a frank, kindly look.

Face (Mary): a younger woman of about twenty-five, with a delicate oval face, dark hair smoothly parted under a blue mantle, large dark eyes lowered to the scroll and a still, listening expression.

Face (Lazarus): a lean man of about forty with a pale, gaunt, gentle face, short straight black hair, a close-trimmed black beard and dark eyes raised in quiet gratitude.

- No tradition fixes their faces. The Painter's Manual has them only in scenes: "Marthe apporte un plat sur cette table … elle regarde le Christ", while "Marie, assise près de ses pieds, le regarde et l'écoute" (p. 181), and both sisters prostrate at the raising of Lazarus.
- The decree of 26 January 2021: "In the household of Bethany the Lord Jesus experienced the family spirit and friendship of Martha, Mary and Lazarus"; the Latin Church's uncertainty about Mary's identity with the Magdalene "has been resolved in recent studies and times". https://www.vatican.va/roman_curia/congregations/ccdds/documents/rc_con_ccdds_doc_20210126_decreto-santi_en.html. So Mary of Bethany is not given the Magdalene's loose hair and ointment jar, which the existing `mary_magdalene` card has.
- Vermeer, *Christ in the House of Martha and Mary* (Wikipedia lead images for Martha and Mary, local copies `Martha.jpg`, `Mary_of_Bethany.jpg`), which I checked: Martha standing with a basket of bread and a head-cloth, Mary seated, chin in hand, listening.
- Golden Legend (Caxton), *Life of S. Martha*: "hostess of our Lord", she "as a noble hostess ministered and served our Lord", and later sailed with Lazarus and Mary to Provence (local copy `../consult/gl/v4.txt`). The keys at her girdle are the usual Western housekeeper's attribute; her Provençal dragon (the Tarasque) is left out to keep the card on Bethany.
- The faces are artistic choices that separate the three by age, colouring and role: Martha serving (keys, jug), Mary listening (the Scriptures; the pt-BR collect asks to be "nutridos pela meditação da Palavra" with Mary), Lazarus raised (a linen cloth over his shoulder, pale). Lazarus is given straight black hair and a close beard so he is not the stock brown-curled man.
- Background: Bethany on the Mount of Olives, Jerusalem in the distance.
- Excerpt: the communion antiphon, Martha's confession: "You are the Christ, the Son of God, who is coming into this world." / "Tu és o Cristo, Filho do Deus vivo, que vieste a este mundo."

## Basil the Great and Gregory Nazianzen

Face (Basil): a lean, ascetic man in his late forties with thick dark hair only threaded with grey, high-arched black brows, a long, narrow face with hollow cheeks and a long black beard narrowing to a point; a grave, searching gaze.

Face (Gregory): an older man of about sixty, bald on the whole crown with a short fringe at the sides, a broad, full, square-cut beard of smoky dark grey streaked with black, heavy grey-black brows and a gentle, meditative look.

- Dionysius, p. 316: "Le grand Basile : cheveux gris, grande barbe, sourcils arqués" (grey hair, a large beard, arched brows); "Saint Grégoire le Théologos : vieillard, chauve, large barbe de couleur fuligineuse, et les sourcils de même" (an old man, bald, a broad beard of a sooty colour, and the brows the same). Elsewhere (p. 320) King Amasias has "barbe en pointe ; il ressemble au grand Basile", so Basil's beard is pointed.
- The 11th-century mosaic of Basil in St Sophia, Kiev (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Basil_of_Caesarea.jpg`), which I checked: dark hair, a long dark beard narrowing to a point, a long lean face, high brows, the omophorion with crosses and a jewelled book. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Basil_of_Caesarea.jpg
- The Chora fresco of Gregory (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Gregory_of_Nazianzus.jpg`), which I checked: bald, a broad, squared grey beard, a thin face, polystaurion and book. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Gregor-Chora_(cropped).jpg
- Ages: Basil 330–379 (died at about 49); Gregory 329–390. Gregory's *Oration* 43.23 says Basil had "prudence of old age, even before his hair was gray". Dionysius's "grey hair" for Basil is kept only as threads of grey; the dark hair follows Kiev and separates him from Gregory.
- Gregory's beard is "sooty": charcoal grey with dark streaks, not white. This keeps him out of the white-bearded elders (Athanasius, Nicholas).
- Dress and attributes: Greek bishops, as on the Athanasius and Chrysostom cards: phelonion and white omophorion with black crosses; Basil with the scroll of his Liturgy, Gregory ("the Theologian") with the Gospel book.
- Background: Cappadocia, near Caesarea, with Mount Argaeus.
- Excerpt: Gregory on their friendship at Athens, *Oration* 43.20: "We seemed to have one soul, inhabiting two bodies." https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/310243.htm. pt-BR is my rendering. The communion antiphon ("We proclaim Christ crucified") is the formulary alternative.

## Anthony the Abbot

Face: a very old but hale man with a lean, deeply sun-browned face, the black hood framing it so that no hair shows, a medium-length grey-white beard parted at the chin into two points, bushy white brows and clear, bright, undimmed eyes with a calm gaze.

- Dionysius, p. 330: "Saint Antoine : vieillard, barbe courte et séparée en deux, le menton un peu chauve. Il porte un manteau" (an old man, a short beard parted in two, the chin a little bare; he wears a mantle). Didron's note: in Greece he is never shown with the pig, as in the West from the 15th century.
- Michael Damaskinos's icon (16th c., Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Anthony_the_Great.jpg`), which I checked: the hood drawn over the head, a long dark-grey beard, a lean brown face, a scroll. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saint_Anthony_(Damaskinos).png
- Athanasius, *Life of Antony* 14: when he came out after twenty years, "he had the same habit of body as before, and was neither fat … nor lean from fasting"; ch. 93, at his death at 105: "his eyes were undimmed and quite sound and he saw clearly … He remained strong both in hands and feet; … he appeared more cheerful and of greater strength" (local copy `athanasius-antony.html`). Ch. 2 is the Gospel that sent him out: "If you would be perfect, go and sell that you have and give to the poor". https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/2811.htm
- The beard: Dionysius's short parted beard is lengthened to medium so it reads at card size; the hood hides the hair, which is what separates him from Jerome and Benedict.
- Dress and attributes (Western card): black hooded mantle with a blue tau, tau staff with a bell, a small pig (the Antonine hospital brothers' pigs), a book. The fire of "St Anthony's fire" is left out.
- Background: the Egyptian desert of the Red Sea mountains, a cave, a spring, palms.
- Excerpt: the communion antiphon, the same Gospel words (Mt 19:21).

## Polycarp

Face: a very old man of about eighty-five with a full head of soft, thin, silvery-white hair falling to the nape, a short, neat, rounded snow-white beard, a lean, gentle face with a quiet smile and eyes raised to heaven.

- Dionysius, p. 318: "Saint Polycarpe de Smyrne : vieillard, barbe arrondie" (an old man, a rounded beard).
- *Martyrdom of Polycarp* 9: "Eighty and six years have I served Him, and He never did me any injury: how then can I blaspheme my King and my Saviour?" Ch. 15: "the fire, shaping itself into the form of an arch, like the sail of a ship when filled with the wind, encompassed as by a circle the body of the martyr", and he appeared "as gold and silver glowing in a furnace". https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/0102.htm
- An Orthodox fresco (Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Polycarp.jpg`), which I checked: bald crown, white hair at the sides, a long white beard, omophorion and book. It contradicts Dionysius's rounded beard; the card follows Dionysius.
- The full head of white hair is an artistic choice. Nicholas, Athanasius, Ignatius of Antioch and Jerome are all bald on top; Polycarp is the one old bishop with hair.
- Dress and attributes: bareheaded, white chasuble and pallium, Gospel book (disciple of John), palm, and the arch of flame from ch. 15 as a golden sail behind him, without burning.
- Background: Smyrna, its harbour and Mount Pagos.
- Excerpt: the first half of ch. 9. pt-BR is my rendering.

## Hildegard of Bingen

Face: a woman of about sixty-five with a long, noble, narrow face, a strong straight nose, fine lines at the eyes, thin silver-grey brows, deep-set brown eyes raised upward toward the light, and firm, composed lips.

- No portrait from life. The Rupertsberg *Scivias* frontispiece (c. 1175, Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Hildegard_of_Bingen.jpg`), which I checked: Hildegard seated in a dark habit and veil, tongues of fire descending on her head, writing on a wax tablet with a stylus, while Volmar copies at the window. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hildegard_von_Bingen.jpg
- The *Protestificatio* of *Scivias*: in 1141, at forty-two, "maximae coruscationis igneum lumen aperto caelo veniens totum cerebrum meum transfudit" (a fiery light of great brilliance, coming from the open heaven, poured through my whole brain). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scivias
- Age: 1098–1179. She founded Rupertsberg at about fifty and wrote the *Liber divinorum operum* in her late sixties and seventies. The older face is chosen for that, and to keep her apart from Scholastica (forty, round) and Edith Stein (fifty, dark brows).
- Not used: the white silk veils and gold crowns she let her nuns wear on feast days, which Tenxwind of Andernach criticised. They were for her virgins, not her own dress, and would suggest a queen.
- Dress and attributes: black Benedictine habit and veil, white wimple, crozier, wax tablet and stylus, tongues of fire from an opening heaven.
- Background: the Rhine at Bingen, Rupertsberg, vineyards.
- Excerpt: there is no Mass formulary for her in the corpus (the calendar has `09-17/hildegard.json`, the formularies only Bellarmine). The card uses the opening of her sequence to the Holy Spirit, "O ignis Spiritus paracliti, vita vite omnis creature" (http://www.hildegard-society.org/2014/11/o-ignis-spiritus-paracliti-sequence.html): "O fire of the Spirit, the Comforter, life of the life of every creature." / "Ó fogo do Espírito Consolador, vida da vida de toda criatura." Both are my rendering.

## Rita of Cascia

Face: a woman of about fifty-five with a pale, fine-boned, slightly long face, a small dark thorn-mark in the middle of the forehead kept small and neat, dark-brown eyes lifted in sorrowful love to the crucifix, a slender straight nose and gentle, closed lips.

- The portrait on the casket made for her body (the *cassa solenne*, 1457; Wikipedia lead image, local copy `Rita_of_Cascia.jpg`), which I checked: a long, pale, older face with the forehead wound, a white veil over a brown habit, holding the crucifix. https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Santa_Rita_da_Cascia.jpg
- Wikipedia: "a small wound appeared on her forehead, as though a thorn from the crown that encircled Christ's head had loosened itself and penetrated her own flesh"; attributes "Forehead wound, rose, bees, grape vine"; the rose found blooming in winter at the end of her life; 1381–1457. It also notes that the black Augustinian habit of popular images "is historically inaccurate; she would have worn the brown robes and white veil" of her monastery. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rita_of_Cascia
- Dress: the black habit is kept because it is the image Brazilian devotion knows. The brown habit and white veil of the casket are the accurate alternative, and would separate her further from the other nuns.
- The wound: a small, neat mark with a single golden ray from the crucifix, never a wound drawn realistically.
- Background: Cascia in the Umbrian hills, the monastery of St Mary Magdalene, a vine (her dry vine that blossomed).
- Excerpt: the collect's opening. The en-US drops "we pray" ("Bestow on us, we pray, O Lord, the wisdom and strength of the Cross"); the pt-BR is verbatim.

## Pairs that may still look alike

- **Scholastica, Hildegard, Rita** (all in black habits with white wimples) and **Margaret Mary** (black veil, white wimple): the faces carry the difference (Scholastica forty, round, smiling, light-eyed; Hildegard sixty-five, long face, grey brows, looking up at flames; Rita fifty-five, pale, thorn-mark, looking at the crucifix), together with dove, wax tablet and crucifix. Check this group on the contact sheet. The brown habit and white veil for Rita would remove most of the risk.
- **Edith Stein, Teresa of Ávila, Thérèse**: same Carmelite habit. Stein's heavy dark brows, fifty years and grave look must carry it.
- **Pius X and Leo the Great**: both are clean-shaven popes with full faces. Leo wears the tiara and has dark eyes and a stern look; Pius a white zucchetto, white hair showing, light eyes and a smile. **Pius X and John Vianney**: both clean-shaven with white hair; Vianney is thin with long hair.
- **Polycarp, Nicholas, Philip Neri**: short, rounded white beards. Polycarp is bareheaded with a full head of white hair and the flame arch; Nicholas is mitred and bald; Philip Neri is in a black cassock.
- **Anthony, Jerome, Benedict**: old men with grey-white beards. Anthony's hood, his forked beard and his brown desert face should separate him.
- **Gregory Nazianzen, Athanasius, Ignatius of Antioch**: bald old bishops in the white omophorion. Gregory's beard must stay smoky dark grey and square, not white (Athanasius) or tapering (Ignatius). This is the riskiest face in the batch.
- **Basil, Paul, John Chrysostom**: long dark pointed beards. Basil has a full head of dark hair (Paul is bald) and a long beard (Chrysostom's is short and thin).
- **Lazarus and the stock thirties man**: kept apart only by straight black hair, pallor and a close beard. If the draft gives him brown curls, redo it.
- **Mary of Bethany and Mary Magdalene**: kept veiled, dark-haired and with a scroll rather than loose hair and a jar.
- **John Bosco**: no close look-alike among the clean-shaven priests, but the boy beside him may come out like Aloysius; he should be a plainly dressed working boy.
