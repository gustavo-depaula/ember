# Prelates and young saints: faces

Faces for the cards that collapse into the stock white-bearded mitred prelate and the stock beardless youth. Each "Face:" line uses only marks with a source quoted below it. Where nothing is attested the line says so and suggests nothing.

"Dionysius" below is the Athonite *Painter's Manual* (Ἑρμηνεία, c. 1730), quoted from Didron's French translation, *Manuel d'iconographie chrétienne grecque et latine* (Paris 1845), full text at https://archive.org/details/manueldiconograp00dion (local copy `../consult/prelates-src/didron-fr.txt`). Didron's manuscript and Hetherington's English edition can differ in wording; the English here is my translation of Didron's French. Consulted texts are saved in `../consult/prelates-src/`.

## Prelates

## Ambrose

Face: a man in early middle age with short dark-brown hair receding at the temples, a short, close dark-brown beard and moustache, a broad forehead, a long straight nose and an intense, direct gaze. No white beard.

- The 5th-century mosaic in the sacello of San Vittore in Ciel d'Oro, Sant'Ambrogio, Milan. Wikimedia Commons: "Late antique mosaic of Saint Ambrose in the church St. Ambrogio in Milan. This might be an actual portrait, made while Ambrose was living." https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:AmbroseOfMilan.jpg (local copy `../consult/prelates-src/AmbroseOfMilan.jpg`, which I checked: short dark beard and moustache, short dark hair.)
- Simone Piazza, "Il volto di Ambrogio", in *La mémoire d'Ambroise de Milan* (Éditions de la Sorbonne): "Il mosaicista ha raffigurato Ambrogio con i capelli bruni e non biondi…"; "il santo ha barba e baffi"; "la fronte spaziosa, la lunga canna nasale … sono «segni particolari»"; "i capelli bruni con la forte stempiatura ai lati della fronte"; "l'età piuttosto giovanile, la stempiatura laterale, i capelli bruni senza tonsura, barba e baffi anch'essi bruni e abbastanza corti." https://books.openedition.org/psorbonne/29310?lang=en
- The Greek tradition differs. Dionysius, p. 319: "Saint Ambroise de Milan : vieillard, barbe en pointe" (an old man with a pointed beard). Prefer the Milan mosaic, which may be a portrait from life and is the Western type.
- Left out: Piazza's note that the right eyebrow sits higher than the left.

## Augustine

Face: a mature man with a North African (Berber) complexion, a short dark beard and close-cropped hair, as in his oldest surviving portrait. No white flowing beard.

- The oldest surviving portrait is a 6th-century fresco in the Lateran: "The earliest portrait of Saint Augustine in a 6th century fresco, Lateran, Rome." https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Augustine_Lateran.jpg (local copy `../consult/prelates-src/Augustine_Lateran.jpg`). From my own look at the image: a short dark beard and short, close-cropped hair. No written source describes these features, so treat them as observed only.
- Origin. Wikipedia: "Scholars generally agree that Augustine and his family were Berbers, an ethnic group indigenous to North Africa, but were heavily Romanized." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustine_of_Hippo
- Midwest Augustinians: "we don't know what he looked like"; "Augustine's traditional depictions in European art have largely followed Western aesthetic norms and show the saint with European features"; recent art stresses his North African origin. https://www.midwestaugustinians.org/news/becoming-the-face-of-st-augustine
- christianiconography.info covers only his vestments and attributes, not his face. https://www.christianiconography.info/augustine.html

## Athanasius

Face: an old man, bald on top, with a broad full white beard. Keep it broader and fuller than Nicholas's short rounded one.

- Dionysius, p. 316, "Les saints évêques, les traits de leur figure": "Saint Athanase d'Alexandrie : vieillard, chauve, large barbe" (an old man, bald, with a broad beard).
- Complexion is **not attested**. Wikipedia says only that "Some Western scholars consider his command of Greek … evidence that he may have been a Greek born in Alexandria", and that he also knew Coptic. His ethnicity is disputed, so no skin tone is suggested. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athanasius_of_Alexandria
- The same manual has a different "Saint Athanase : vieillard, barbe séparée en cinq" (p. 327), but he is one of the Forty Martyrs, not the bishop. Don't use it.

## John Chrysostom

Face: a lean, ascetic man, younger than the other hierarchs, with a high bare forehead, hollow cheeks from fasting, dark hair, and a short, thin, pointed dark beard.

- Dionysius, p. 316: "Saint Jean Chrysostome : jeune, peu de barbe" (young, little beard).
- A museum label quoted by christianiconography.info (Met, 12th-c. Byzantine wall painting): he "is usually depicted with a pointed beard, sunken cheeks, and a high forehead. He is most often, but by no means always, portrayed with a full head of hair." https://christianiconography.info/metropolitan/march2018/chrysostomByzantine.html
- Orthodox Arts Journal, "Designing Icons (pt. 2): Icon Prototypes": "sunken cheeks and wispy beard". https://orthodoxartsjournal.org/designing-icons-pt-2-icon-prototypes/
- Dark hair: sources don't agree on how much hair he has, but none makes him white-haired. "Dark" rests on the "young" in Dionysius, not on an explicit colour. Render the hollow cheeks as an ascetic's leanness, not as emaciation.

## Gregory the Great

Face: a mature man, bald on the crown and high forehead, with the hair at the sides worn long and neatly curled, a moderate tawny (reddish-gold) beard, a slim, slightly aquiline nose and a well-formed chin.

- John the Deacon (9th c.) saw a portrait painted for Gregory's monastery of St Andrew shortly after his death. As summarised on Wikipedia: "'rather bald' and had a 'tawny' beard like his father's … The hair that he had on the sides was long and carefully curled. His nose was 'thin and straight' and 'slightly aquiline'. 'His forehead was high.' He had thick, 'subdivided' lips and a chin 'of a comely prominence' and 'beautiful hands'." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_I (local copy `../consult/prelates-src/gregory.txt`)
- Beardless? Not confirmed for the West. The Greek manual has "Saint Grégoire le Dialogos : jeune, peu de barbe" (young, little beard; Dionysius p. 318), and the portrait John the Deacon saw has a beard. Prefer John the Deacon, the closest source to Gregory's lifetime.
- Wikipedia notes that Ribera's painting exaggerates these features ("total baldness, outthrust chin, beak-like nose"). Keep them moderate.

## Nicholas of Myra

Face: an elderly man, bald on the crown and high forehead, with a short, full, rounded white beard, slightly divided into two lobes at the chin.

- Dionysius, p. 316: "Saint Nicolas : vieillard, chauve, barbe arrondie" (an old man, bald, with a rounded beard).
- Wikipedia: "Iconographically, Nicholas is depicted as an elderly man with a short, full, white, fluffy beard and balding head." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Nicholas
- Orthodox Arts Journal: "short white beard, double lobed at the bottom". https://orthodoxartsjournal.org/designing-icons-pt-2-icon-prototypes/

## Patrick

Face: no attested facial tradition. There is no portrait and no description of his appearance. Since the Middle Ages the convention has been a generic bearded bishop, so leave the face as it is and let the shamrock, snake and vestments identify him.

- christianiconography.info: "Medieval portraits showed him without a beard and sometimes dressed as a religious"; "After the Middle Ages he was most often portrayed as … a bearded bishop with a mitre, crozier, and chasuble." https://www.christianiconography.info/patrick.html
- Caution: Dionysius's "Saint Patricius : jeune, peu de barbe" (p. 319) is Patricius of Prusa, not Patrick of Ireland.

## Alphonsus Liguori

Face: an elderly bishop with a fresh complexion, a full forehead, clear blue eyes, an aquiline nose, a small smiling mouth, and a thick beard kept short. Optionally, spectacles. Not a long flowing beard.

- Antonio Tannoia, his companion and first biographer, describes him in *The Life of St. Alphonsus Maria de Liguori* (Eng. tr. 1855, ch. XXXVII): "He was of middle size, with a rather large head, and of a fresh complexion. He had a full forehead, pleasing azure blue eyes, an aquiline nose, a small mouth and smiling lips. His beard was thick and his hair black; he kept them short, and often cut them himself. He was short-sighted and made use of glasses, but never in the pulpit or when he spoke to women." https://archive.org/details/thelifeofstalpho00tannuoft (local copy `../consult/prelates-src/alph_thelifeofstalpho00tannuoft.txt`)
- Berthe's *Life* (1906, vol. 2) repeats the portrait ("his complexion fair. He had a broad forehead … His hair was black and his beard, which he cut …"). https://archive.org/details/lifeofstalphonsu02bert
- Tannoia's black hair is his hair in his prime. On a card of the aged bishop, grey hair and a grey beard kept short fit his age at death: "ninety years ten months and five days" (Tannoia).
- Left out: the hunched posture after his 1768–69 illness, which is "as he is typically depicted in art" (National Catholic Register, https://www.ncregister.com/blog/saints-and-art-alphonsus-liguori).

## Charles Borromeo

Face: a thin, clean-shaven face in middle age, with a slender but prominent nose. No beard at all.

- christianiconography.info: "The saint is usually pictured with a thin, clean-shaven face and a thin but prominent nose." https://www.christianiconography.info/charlesBorromeo.html
- Being clean-shaven is his own programme: he issued the pastoral *De barba radenda* urging his clergy to shave (Catholic Encyclopedia, "Beard"). https://www.newadvent.org/cathen/02362a.htm
- He died at 46, so he should not look like an old man.

## Young saints

## Stephen

Face: a beardless youth with a tonsure and a serene, radiant, "angelic" expression. Clean-shaven.

- Wikipedia: "Stephen is often shown as a young, beardless man with a tonsure, wearing a deacon's vestments." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Stephen
- Acts 6:15: at his trial his face looked "like that of an angel". This is the one scriptural facial mark for any saint on this list.
- Dionysius, p. 321, "Les saints diacres": "Saint Etienne, premier martyr et chef des diacres : jeune, imberbe" (young, beardless).
- christianiconography.info: "As a deacon, he is tonsured and wears a dalmatic." https://www.christianiconography.info/stephen.html

## Lawrence

Face: a young man, a little older than Stephen, with a tonsure and the first light shadow of a beard ("budding beard").

- Dionysius, p. 321: "Saint Laurent : jeune, barbe naissante" (young, budding beard), listed with Stephen "jeune, imberbe". The Greek manual deliberately separates the two deacons this way.
- christianiconography.info: "a 5th-century fresco that portrays him as a youthful man, tonsured and wearing the dalmatic of a deacon … The youth, the tonsure, and the dalmatic will stay with him through sixteen more centuries." https://www.christianiconography.info/lawrence.html
- Wikipedia: born "in Huesca … Hispania Tarraconensis", ordained deacon when "only 32". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lawrence (His Iberian birth does not attest a complexion, so none is suggested.)

## Aloysius Gonzaga

Face: a slender young man, just past twenty, with large dark eyes, a gently aquiline nose, a fine-boned ascetic face with a faint flush in the cheeks, and his head inclined slightly forward.

- Cepari/Goldie, *Life of Saint Aloysius Gonzaga* (1891), from the beatification and canonization processes: "From the testimony of those who knew him, Aloysius was of middle height, rather tall than short. His head was bent slightly forward, his eyes were large and dark, his nose somewhat aquiline. He was strongly built, but the austerities of his life soon undermined his robust health. Yet even his emaciated features, and his cheeks faintly tinged by the deep and intense emotions of his soul, could not conceal the natural vigour of his constitution." https://archive.org/details/lifeofsaintaloys00cepauoft (local copy `../consult/prelates-src/cepari.txt`)
- The same book says a portrait of him "when about 17 years and a half", by an unknown painter of the school of Veronese, is kept in his room (the Cappelletta) at the Roman College.
- christianiconography.info: "He died soon after, at the age of 23." https://www.christianiconography.info/aloysiusGonzaga.html

## Sebastian

Face: a young man with a handsome, youthful face and at most a faint budding beard.

- christianiconography.info: "his youthful and handsome body is pierced by numerous arrows". https://www.christianiconography.info/sebastian.html
- Dionysius, p. 324: "Saint Sébastien : jeune, barbe naissante" (young, budding beard).
- The earliest Western image is different. Wikipedia on the San Pietro in Vincoli mosaic (c. 682): "a grown, bearded man in court dress". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sebastian. The youthful type is the standard from the Renaissance on.
- Hair colour and style: no attested tradition.

## George

Face: a beardless young soldier with close-cropped hair.

- Dionysius, p. 321, "Les saints martyrs": "Saint Georges : jeune, imberbe" (young, beardless).
- christianiconography.info, on the c. 1440 reliquary bust in Zadar: "Military status expressed by close-cropped hair." https://www.christianiconography.info/george.html
- Wikipedia: "Of Cappadocian Greek origin". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George

## John the Evangelist

Face: a young man, beardless or with only a short light beard. Of the young saints, he is the one who may carry a slight beard.

- christianiconography.info: "In most portraits and other images in the West, St. John is a young person with either a short beard or none at all … he was an adolescent at the time of the Last Supper and partly because of his perpetual virginity." In the East, "his white or grizzled beard will be bifurcated and he will have a high forehead." https://www.christianiconography.info/john.html
- Wikipedia: "either as an aged man with a white or gray beard, or alternatively as a beardless youth … the second was more common in the art of Medieval Western Europe, and can be dated back as far as 4th century Rome." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Apostle
- Hair: no attested tradition in these sources.

## Anthony of Padua

Face: a young man in his mid-thirties with a broad Franciscan tonsure, a long, narrow face, large eyes, an aquiline nose and a well-defined chin.

- christianiconography.info: "The earliest images are like the second picture at right, with a closed book and the tonsure and habit of the Franciscan order"; a "broad tonsure" appears in every period. https://www.christianiconography.info/anthonyPadua.html
- The Basilica of St Anthony, from the 1981 study of his skeletal remains: "His head was elongated so his face must have been straight and narrow, with a prominent chin, aquiline nose, large eyes and healthy, regular teeth." https://www.santantonio.org/en/content/his-face
- Age: the skull is that of "a 36-year-old male" (National Catholic Register on the 2014 reconstruction). https://www.ncregister.com/news/what-did-st-anthony-of-padua-look-like
- Left out: the page says the 2014 digital reconstruction came out "more massive and corpulent" than the traditional thin face. Keep the traditional lean face.
- Beard: no source here settles it. Cards and the Franciscan tradition normally show him beardless, but I did not find that in writing.

## Pairs that may still look alike

- **Nicholas and Athanasius**: both are old, bald and white-bearded in Dionysius. The only difference is the beard: rounded, short and double-lobed for Nicholas, broad and full for Athanasius. Push that contrast, and rely on the attributes (Nicholas: three gold balls or the three boys; Athanasius: Greek vestments and a book).
- **Ambrose and Augustine**: both have a short dark beard. Ambrose's temples recede and his nose is long; Augustine has the North African complexion. The beehive or scourge (Ambrose) and the flaming heart (Augustine) must carry the rest.
- **Patrick and any bearded bishop**: he has no facial tradition, so only his attributes set him apart.
- **Stephen and Lawrence**: the same age, tonsure and dalmatic. The only attested difference is Stephen beardless and Lawrence with a budding beard, which is subtle at card size. The stones and the gridiron remain the real identifiers.
- **Stephen, George and Sebastian**: all three are beardless or nearly so. George's short-cropped soldier's hair and Stephen's tonsure help. Sebastian has no attested hair tradition.
- **Aloysius and Anthony**: both are lean young clerics with aquiline noses and large eyes. Aloysius has a full head of hair and a Jesuit cassock and surplice; Anthony has the broad tonsure, a Franciscan habit and is visibly older (36 against 23).
- **Borromeo and Alphonsus**: both are clean-faced or short-bearded bishops. Borromeo is fully clean-shaven, thin and middle-aged; Alphonsus is aged, with a short beard, blue eyes and optional spectacles.
