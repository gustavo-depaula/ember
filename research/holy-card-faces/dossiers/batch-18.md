# Batch 18 — the Sorrowful Mysteries and five second feasts

Ten scene cards: the five Sorrowful Mysteries (`agony_garden`, `scourging`, `crowning_thorns`, `carrying_cross`, `crucifixion`) and five second feasts from the Pictorial Lives (`peter_chair_rome`, `discovery_cross`, `john_latin_gate`, `apparition_michael`, `peter_chains`). Christ, Our Lady (matured to about forty-eight on the Way of the Cross and at Calvary), Peter and John use their lines from `recurring-figures.md`. I added two lines there: St. Michael, described from the `michael_archangel` card (which has no `meta.face`), and St. Helena, written for this batch so that her own card on 18 August matches. A script checked every excerpt verbatim against its source file in both languages, and every Pictorial Lives name against the index. Each catalogMatch hits exactly one unticked line or inline item of `docs/plans/holy-cards-catalog.md`.

**No `feast`** on the five mysteries. They can't ship until `feast` is optional in the app (see `README.md`). The Pictorial Lives cards take the book's dates: 18 Jan, 3 May, 6 May, 8 May and 1 Aug.

**Initials.** The mysteries take A, S, C, C and C. The feasts take P (Peter's Chair), D (Discovery), J (John), A (Apparition) and P (Peter's Chains).

**The Passion, kept reverent.** No gore anywhere. Wounds are small, clean marks, as on devotional cards: a few thin red lines at the Scourging, two or three tiny drops under the crown, clean nail marks on the Cross, and the side not yet opened. The Scourging shows Christ alone at the column after the scourging, as in Velázquez's *Christ after the Flagellation*, with no tormentors striking. At the Crowning the soldiers are small and in shadow. The titulus on the Cross is a blank board.

## The Agony in the Garden

Christ kneels at the rock, and the strengthening angel of Lk 22:43 holds out the cup. Peter, James and John sleep small in the background. The angel is new: a soft round face with dark-auburn curls and no armour, kept apart from Michael and from batch 15's heart-shaped angel.

- Excerpt: "Nevertheless, let your will be done, not mine." / "Não se faça, todavia, a minha vontade, mas sim a tua." (Palm Sunday C, the Passion). The English file has "'Father,' he said 'if…'", so only the second half is verbatim in both languages.

## The Scourging at the Pillar

Christ is alone at a short marble column in Pilate's courtyard, with the rods on the ground and a soldier far off walking away.

- Excerpt: "By his wounds we have been healed." / "Por suas chagas fomos curados." (Friday of Lent week 5, communion antiphon, 1 Pt 2:24).

## The Crowning with Thorns

Christ is seated in the scarlet cloak with the reed and a thin crown, majestic and meek. The Good Friday Reproaches ("you put on my head a crown of thorns") would fit better but have no pt-BR text in the formulary, and the Good Friday Passion has no en-US text. So the excerpt is Heb 2:9, which answers the thorns with glory.

- Excerpt: "We see in Jesus one who was for a short while made lower than the angels and is now crowned with glory and splendour because he submitted to death" / "Jesus, a quem Deus fez pouco menor do que os anjos, nós o vemos coroado de glória e honra, por ter sofrido a morte." (Ordinary Time week 27 Sunday B, second reading). The two translations differ in construction; both are verbatim.

## The Carrying of the Cross

Christ under the Cross meets his Mother, with John behind her. This is the Fourth Station and the common Rosary-card image. I chose it over Veronica or Simon so that the recurring faces carry the scene.

- Excerpt: Lk 9:23, "…take up his cross every day and follow me" / "…tome sua cruz cada dia, e siga-me." (Thursday after Ash Wednesday, gospel).

## The Crucifixion

This is the Stabat Mater composition. The Cross fills the upper centre, with Mary at the left and John at the right, both facing it, at the moment of the last words: eyes raised, side not yet pierced. It differs from batch 17's `mother_of_church` on purpose. That card shows Mary and John in the foreground turning away together, with three tiny crosses far off at dusk. Here there is one Cross, close and central, with an eclipsed sun.

- Excerpt: "Father, into your hands I commit my spirit" / "Pai, nas tuas mãos entrego o meu espírito." (Palm Sunday C, Lk 23:46).

## St. Peter's Chair at Rome (18 Jan, Pictorial Lives)

This card must not repeat `chair_peter` (22 Feb), which shows Peter enthroned in Bernini's golden glory. The Catholic Encyclopedia ("Chair of Peter") records that Rome kept 18 January as the day Peter first held a service with its faithful. The Roman Acts place "the seat where he first sat" at the Ostrianum on the Via Salaria, by Senator Pudens's villa, where he baptised. So this card shows Peter in daylight on a plain wooden chair in a villa garden, with a baptismal basin, teaching Pudens and his daughter. The listeners' faces are new and small: a square-faced clean-shaven senator and a heart-faced girl.

- Excerpt: the whole of the day's reflection (one sentence).

## The Discovery of the Holy Cross (3 May, Pictorial Lives)

The miracle of identification: the third cross touched to the dying woman, who sits up healed, at the excavation on Calvary, with the other two crosses, the blank title and the nails. This is the moment of the book's own engraving. The `exaltation_cross` card already shows a crowned woman and a white-bearded mitred bishop raising the Cross upright, so this card keeps the Cross low and Macarius small, lean, dark-grey-bearded and without a mitre.

**Helena's face is new.** Eusebius gives her age as about eighty on her return from Palestine, and the book says "near eighty". Her coins show a strong straight nose, a firm chin, and hair drawn back to a bun at the nape (the braided crown is posthumous), with a diadem. Hence: seventy-eight, a long dignified face, a Roman nose, silver hair in a low bun, a slender diadem, and a purple mantle. She must not look like the crowned woman on `exaltation_cross`, who is middle-aged and in red. The existing queens are also different: `elizabeth_portugal` wears a tertiary habit, `margaret_scotland` a blue gown, `hedwig` a widow's wimple.

- Excerpt: "In every pious undertaking the beginning merely does not suffice." (the reflection's first sentence).

## St. John Before the Latin Gate (6 May, Pictorial Lives)

**Decided: John keeps the young, beardless face of `john_evangelist`, not an aged one.** In 95 he would have been about ninety, and the book's engraving shows him old and bearded, as Eastern icons do. But the Western devotional tradition this set follows paints the Latin Gate with the beardless young evangelist in nearly every image (Ad Imaginem Dei survey; Balestra at San Stae). An old John would also break the recurring-figures line he shares with the Passion cards in this very batch. He wears a white tunic for modesty and rises from calm golden oil, with the flames small under the hearth and no burns or steam. The executioners are astonished. The Porta Latina has its single arch between two round towers.

- Excerpt: "O singular happiness, to have stood under the cross of Christ!" / "Ó singular felicidade, ter estado sob a cruz de Cristo!" (a sentence of the reflection, which also ties the card to the Crucifixion).

## The Apparition of St. Michael the Archangel (8 May, Pictorial Lives)

Michael, dressed exactly as on his card, appears on a ledge over the Gargano grotto and points to it. The bishop of Siponto kneels below with his mitre set down, and the white bull of the legend lies at the cave mouth. There is no dragon, so the card doesn't repeat `michael_archangel`. The bishop has a new face: broad and low-browed, with a short rounded beard.

- Excerpt: the reflection's first sentence.

## St. Peter's Chains (1 Aug, Pictorial Lives)

This follows Acts 12 and the book: the angel in light wakes Peter, the chains fall from his hands, the soldiers sleep, and the gate stands open (Raphael's *Liberation of St. Peter* fixed the type). Peter keeps his card's face at about sixty-five, even though he was nearer fifty in AD 44, so that he is recognisable. The angel is new: a long, narrow face, a fine aquiline nose, and ash-blond straight hair to the jaw.

- Excerpt: the whole reflection (one sentence with the quoted promise, Mt 18:19).

## Look-alike risks that remain

- **Christ on five cards in a row.** His line is fixed, but the generator may drift toward the stock bearded man. Check the face sheet against `transfiguration` and the batch 17 mysteries.
- **Our Lady and John appear on both `carrying_cross` and `crucifixion`**, and on batch 17's `mother_of_church`. The three compositions differ, but check that they don't come out as three near-identical pairs.
- **Three new angels** (the Gethsemane angel, the Chains angel, Michael): round, auburn and rose-gold; long, ash-blond and white; golden curls in armour. Reject if the first two come out golden-curled like Michael or like `guardian_angels`.
- **Helena against the Exaltation's crowned woman.** Both stand beside the Cross. Helena must read as old (silver hair, lined), in purple, with a diadem rather than a crown.
- **`peter_chair_rome` against `chair_peter`.** Both show Peter seated with the keys. The setting (daylight garden, listeners, plain chair) carries the difference; reject a golden apse or dove.
- **The titulus** must stay blank, and the Discovery's title board too. Reject any INRI.
- **`john_latin_gate` modesty.** Make sure the tunic stays on and that no pain or burning is shown.
