# Batch 7 of new cards: faces

Faces, dress and settings for Norbert, Ephrem, John Fisher and Thomas More, Cyril of Alexandria, Elizabeth of Portugal, Camillus de Lellis, Lawrence of Brindisi, Peter Chrysologus, Peter Julian Eymard, and Sixtus II with a deacon. The card data is in `../batches/batch-7.json`; each face line there adds explicit bone structure to the sourced marks below. "Dionysius" is the *Painter's Manual* in Didron's French (1845), https://archive.org/details/manueldiconograp00dion (local copy `../consult/prelates-src/didron-fr.txt`); the English is my translation. Pictures and texts consulted are in `../consult/batch-7/`.

Excerpts: Fisher and More (22 June) and Eymard (2 August) have no formulary in `content/of/formularies/sanctoral/`, so they carry a cited pre-1930 saying. The rest are verbatim clauses of the feast's collect or gospel acclamation, checked with a script against the formulary JSON; the two adjusted en-US lines (Ephrem, Elizabeth) say how in `excerptSource`.

## Norbert

- Gaspar de Crayer, *Saint Norbert* (1664; local copy `norbert-crayer.jpg`, Commons `SSACRAM_62.JPG`), which I checked: clean-shaven, an oval face, short dark hair, dark eyes; a white habit, rochet and shoulder cape; a monstrance in one hand and a double-barred archiepiscopal cross in the other, an olive branch, the heretic Tanchelm at his feet, and a putto carrying the mitre. The card keeps the dress, monstrance, cross and olive branch, and leaves out Tanchelm.
- Wikipedia: born about 1080, founded Prémontré in 1120, archbishop of Magdeburg from 1126, died 6 June 1134. So he was mid-forties as archbishop.
- Structure: a long, smooth, egg-shaped face with high-arched brows and a tonsure. That is my choice, to keep him apart from john_baptist_de_la_salle (oval, full cheeks), the other clean-shaven man in white.
- Background: the valley of Prémontré in the forest of Coucy.
- Excerpt: the collect's petition clause.

## Ephrem

- Dionysius: "Saint Éphrem de Syrie : vieillard, cheveux rares, peu de barbe ; il porte un manteau". Hetherington's English reads "beardless by nature and with sparse hair" (local `hermeneia-ocr.txt`, in copyright, consult only).
- Syriac icon (Commons `Mor_Ephrem_icon.jpg`, local `ephrem-icon.jpg`), which I checked: a black monastic hood and habit, seated writing with a quill in a codex on a lectern, a dove over him. Its short dark beard contradicts Dionysius. The card follows Dionysius (sparse hair, only a wisp of beard) because it keeps him apart from the white-bearded elders.
- Wikipedia: born at Nisibis, deacon, died at Edessa in 373; called the "Harp of the Spirit". The lyre, the deacon's orarion and the Edessa background (the citadel with its two columns, the pools of Abraham) come from this.
- Excerpt: the opening of the collect, en-US without "we pray".

## John Fisher and Thomas More

- Fisher: Holbein's chalk drawing, about 1527 (Royal Collection; local `fisher-holbein.jpg`), which I checked. It shows a long, gaunt, clean-shaven face with a high forehead, very prominent cheekbones and hollow cheeks, a long, straight nose, large, wide-open eyes, deep lines from nose to mouth, a thin mouth and a cap. He was about 58 then and 65 at his death in 1535. The card idealises the gauntness into leanness and keeps the wide eyes. Pope Paul III made him a cardinal while he was in the Tower, hence the red hat lying beside him.
- More: Holbein, 1527, Frick Collection (local `more-holbein.jpg`), which I checked: clean-shaven with a faint stubble, a straight-sided face, a long, straight nose, grey-blue eyes looking off to the side, a small crease between the brows, dark hair to the ears under a black cap, a fur-collared black gown, red velvet sleeves, and the collar of SS with the Tudor rose.
- Erasmus to Hutten, 23 July 1519 (local `erasmus-hutten.pdf`; https://thomasmorestudies.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Erasmus-to-Ulrich-von-Hutten.pdf): "His complexion is fair, his face being rather blonde than pale … His hair is auburn inclining to black … his beard thin, his eyes a bluish grey … an expression of kind and friendly cheerfulness with a little air of raillery." The "hint of wry humour" in the face line comes from this letter.
- He grew a beard in the Tower (the scaffold anecdote in Wikipedia). The card uses Holbein's clean-shaven face, the one everyone recognises.
- Background: the Thames and the Tower of London, where both were imprisoned.
- Excerpt: More to Margaret Roper from the Tower, 1534 (Rogers 206), in the Center for Thomas More Studies text (local `more-letters-205-206.pdf`): "And therefore, mine own good daughter, never trouble thy mind for anything that ever shall hap me in this world. Nothing can come but that that God will." The Office of Readings version ("Nothing can come but what God wills") is ICEL's translation and was not used. The pt-BR line is my rendering.

## Cyril of Alexandria

- Dionysius: "Saint Cyrille d'Alexandrie : cheveux gris, grande barbe séparée en deux. Il porte sur sa tête un voile orné d'une croix". Didron's note: "Saint Cyrille est très-reconnaissable à cette croix qui est constamment figurée sur son voile."
- Chora church fresco, 14th century (Commons `Chora-Kirche_2013-03-21zh_(cropped).jpg`, local `cyril-chora.jpg`), which I checked: a white cap with black crosses, a long brown beard tapering to a point, strongly knitted brows, a strong nose, a white polystavrion and omophorion, and a book.
- Emmanuel Tzanes, 1654 (local `cyril-tzanes.jpg`), which I checked: a very long, dark, pointed beard and a long face.
- I chose the single tapering point of the Chora fresco and Tzanes over Dionysius's two-pointed beard, because Cyprian (batch 2) already has a beard parted into two points.
- The icon of the Theotokos is for Ephesus (431). The background is Alexandria with the Pharos.
- Excerpt: the collect's phrase about him, with "An"/"Um" added.

## Elizabeth of Portugal

- There is no portrait from life. Her tomb (1330, now in Santa Clara-a-Nova, Coimbra; local `elizabeth-tomb.jpg`), made while she was alive and to her instructions, shows her recumbent in a Poor Clare habit with a crown and a pilgrim's staff. Wikipedia: "often depicted in royal garb with a dove or an olive branch, usually holding a bunch of roses in her skirt or in the scapular of her religious habit", and she was a Franciscan tertiary.
- The Zurbarán on Commons (local `elizabeth-zurbaran.jpg`) is doubtfully identified; its own description says it may be St Casilda. I did not use it.
- She died in 1336, aged 65. The card makes her about fifty-five, a widow in a habit with a crown and a staff, with no children. The existing elizabeth_hungary card is a young crowned queen in pink, giving roses and bread to children.
- The face is my choice: a long, rectangular face with an aquiline nose and grey-green eyes.
- Background: Coimbra on the Mondego with Santa Clara, where she lived and is buried.
- Excerpt: the collect's petition clause. It is verbatim in pt-BR; in en-US, "that we may become" is reordered as "May we become".

## Camillus de Lellis

- Painting (Wikipedia lead image `Lellis2.jpg`, local `camillus-wiki.jpg`), which I checked: short-cropped dark hair receding at the temples, a short dark beard and moustache, a long face, a strong straight nose, grey-brown eyes, the black habit with the red cross, holding a dying man.
- Engraved portrait (Wellcome M0010962, local `camillus-wellcome.jpg`): an older, close-cropped, clean-shaven, strongly lined face with a long nose, and a red cross on the breast. A later engraving (local `camillus-engr2.jpg`) has similar features.
- Wikipedia: born 1550, a soldier and gambler before his conversion, founded the Ministers of the Sick in 1582 and died in 1614. His great height is in the biographers (Cicatelli); I did not re-check it in a primary text.
- Background: the ward of Santo Spirito in Sassia, the Roman hospital where he served.
- Excerpt: the final clause of the collect.

## Lawrence of Brindisi

- Painting (Commons `San_Lorenzo_da_Brindisi.jpg`, local `brindisi-2.jpg`), which I checked: an old Capuchin, bald, with a very long grey beard, writing in a book with a quill.
- Wikipedia: born 1559, died 1619. At the battle of Székesfehérvár (1601) he rode before the imperial army holding a crucifix. He knew Hebrew and preached widely.
- The card shows him at 42, at that battle, so the long beard is dark brown and not white. This keeps him out of the white-bearded elder and apart from padre_pio (a white rounded beard). The structure (broad brow, rounded cheekbones, thick-tipped nose) is my choice.
- Background: the plain by Székesfehérvár, with no fighting.
- Excerpt: the gospel acclamation verse.

## Peter Chrysologus

- Wikipedia: "A contemporary portrait of Peter Chrysologus, found in the mosaics of the Church of San Giovanni Evangelista in Ravenna". That mosaic does not survive, and I found no reproduction.
- Engraved roundel (Commons `Pedro_crisologo01.jpg`, local `chrysologus-1.jpg`), which I checked: short hair receding, a short beard, a broad face, holding a book.
- The broad, round face, speaking mouth and gold chasuble ("golden-worded") are my choices within that tradition. He is bareheaded with a pallium, as bishops are in the Ravenna mosaics.
- Background: Sant'Apollinare in Classe and the pines. The bell tower is later than his time, but it is the recognisable Ravenna skyline.
- Excerpt: the collect's petition clause.

## Peter Julian Eymard

- Hand-coloured photograph from before 1868 (Commons `Eymard_(kleur).jpg`, local `eymard-colour.jpg`), which I checked: clean-shaven, a very high forehead receding at the temples, a long face, a straight nose, deep-set eyes and a thin mouth.
- The La Seyne portrait (local `eymard-laseyne.jpg`): kneeling with joined hands in a cassock with white-edged French bands, hair long behind the ears, gazing upward.
- He died in 1868 at 57. He founded the Congregation of the Blessed Sacrament in Paris in 1856 and began exposition there in 1857.
- Excerpt: his invocation "Our Lady of the Most Blessed Sacrament, Mother and Model of Adorers, pray for us who have recourse to thee!", as printed in *Month of Our Lady of the Blessed Sacrament* (New York, 1903; local `eymard-month-1903.txt`). That book says the title is his. The pt-BR line is my rendering.

## Sixtus II and companions

- Wikipedia: pope 257–258, seized while celebrating in the cemetery and beheaded on 6 August 258, "martyred along with six deacons: Januarius, Vincentius, Magnus, Stephanus, Felicissimus and Agapitus". Lawrence followed four days later and has his own card.
- Raphael, Sistine Madonna (detail, Commons `Raphael_-_Saint_Sixtus.JPG`, local `sixtus-raphael.jpg`), which I checked: a bald dome with a grey fringe, a hooked nose, a short, tousled grey beard, a face lifted upward, and a gold cope. There is no early portrait of Sixtus. Raphael's is the image best known in the West, and the card follows it.
- Agapitus: his face is my choice. He is about thirty, clean-shaven with a square, cleft chin and close black hair, kept apart from lawrence (a tonsured, softer young deacon) and vincent_saragossa (centre-parted hair to the jaw). The brief allowed one deacon, and I named him from the Tridentine commemoration.
- Background: a catacomb chamber on the Appian Way lit by lamps. It does not repeat Cornelius's open Appian Way with cypresses.
- Excerpt: the collect's petition clause.

## Look-alike risks that remain

- **Sixtus and cornelius_cyprian (Cornelius)**: both are balding popes with grey fringes and short grey beards. Sixtus has the hooked nose, curly, tousled beard, upturned head and gold cope, and sits in a catacomb. Check them on the face sheet.
- **Eymard and john_vianney / paul_vi**: thin, clean-shaven French or Italian priests with high foreheads. Vianney's hair is long and snow-white; Eymard's is brown going grey, and he kneels before a monstrance. Paul VI is in papal white.
- **Fisher and paul_vi / fabian**: long, lean, clean-shaven older faces. Fisher's black cap, rochet and red hat, and More beside him, should carry him.
- **Norbert and dominic / john_baptist_de_la_salle**: clean-shaven men in white or with bands. Norbert has the monstrance and double cross; Dominic has a small beard in the existing card's tradition. Verify on the sheet.
- **Elizabeth of Portugal and elizabeth_hungary**: both are crowned queens with roses. They are separated by age, habit and veil, the pilgrim staff, and the absence of children, but the roses are shared by tradition.
- **Cyril and cornelius_cyprian (Cyprian) / josaphat**: long dark beards. Cyril's white cross-marked cap is unique, and his beard is a single point.
- **Lawrence of Brindisi and anthony_padua / francis_assisi / bonaventure**: all Franciscans in brown. Only Lawrence has a long, full beard, and he holds a raised crucifix.
- **Camillus and vincent_de_paul / philip_neri**: older priests in black with short beards. The red cross on the breast and the sick man make the distinction.
- **Peter Chrysologus and nicholas / polycarp**: round-faced older bishops with rounded beards. Chrysologus is bareheaded, with a short grey beard and a speaking gesture; Nicholas is bald with a fuller beard and a mitre.
- **Against the parallel batches 6, 8 and 9** (read after these faces were written): Fisher is close to pius_v (gaunt, long, bald under the zucchetto) and robert_bellarmine (long, rectangular, prominent straight nose). Fisher keeps Holbein's wide-open pale eyes and hollow cheeks under a black cap, in a rochet. Eymard is close to paul_of_the_cross (slender, clean-shaven, high forehead). Eymard has the long hair behind the ears, French bands and monstrance. Camillus shares "long, rectangular" with bruno and bellarmine, but he alone is bearded and very big-framed.
