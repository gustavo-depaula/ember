What is yours to decide

The Latinist critic and both judges found nothing to correct. The stylist, reading only for the mouth, marked eight of the ten verses as not yet native: “várias construções fazem ouvir o trabalho da tradução durante a oração.”

Almost every fix it offered buys fluency by leaving the Latin. That is the project’s first rule against its first style priority, and no pipeline can settle it. Switch the wording above to the stylist’s, pray it, switch back, and say where the line is. Your answer becomes the brief for every psalm after this one.

Vós ou tu

Decided on 20 September 2026: vós, for the whole psalter. What follows is the record of what was weighed — each pair identical but for the address — and the costs of vós that the translation now has to manage. The tu switch above still works, for hearing the road not taken.

The endings at the cadence
Vós closes every perfect in -astes, -estes; tu in -aste, -este. Both sing. The difference is the sibilant, verse after verse.
Length
Vosso is a syllable longer than teu, and Psalm 118 has a possessive in nearly every verse. Listen for whether the vós lines drag.
Psalm 90 changes whom it speaks to
Under vós, 90:9 turns audibly from God to the just man in mid-verse. Under tu the two halves sound like one addressee.
Psalm 4 does the reverse
Under vós, atendei (to God) and esperai (to the crowd) share a form. Under tu, God is singular and the crowd plural, as in the Latin.
A trap only vós has
Ouvi a minha oração is also “I heard my prayer”. The vós imperative of every -ir verb collides with the first-person past. It is why exaudire became atender here.
The ear you already have
The Pai-Nosso and the Ave-Maria are vós. Which line sounds like prayer, and which like a Bible being read?
