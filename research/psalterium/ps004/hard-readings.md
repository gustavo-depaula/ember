# Ps 4 — hard readings

Where the Latin is not self-explanatory, and what the parallels in `dossier.json` show. The rule stands: LXX and Hebrew explain Jerome, they never correct him. Lexicon: Lewis & Short via `lexicon.py` (classical-first; Blaise and Souter not consulted — not digitised here). Written by Claude; unreviewed by a Latinist.

| Verse | Latin | What the parallels show | Consequence for the Portuguese |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4:2a | *exaudívit me Deus* … *dilatásti mihi* | Third person, then second, inside one verse. Rahlfs has the same switch (*εἰσήκουσέν* … *ἐπλάτυνάς*). Hebrew and *iuxta Hebraeos* have an imperative (*exaudi me*). | Keep the switch. Do not harmonise to "ouvistes-me". |
| 4:2a | *dilatásti mihi* | L&S *dilato*: spread out, enlarge, extend. Dative *mihi* = LXX *μοι*, Heb. *hirḥabtā lî*: "you made it wide for me" — the opposite of *tribulatio* as a tight place. | The image is space. *me alargastes* / *me destes largueza*, not "aliviastes". |
| 4:3 | *gravi corde* | = LXX *βαρυκάρδιοι*, "heavy-hearted". The Hebrew says something else entirely ("my glory to shame"; *iuxta Hebraeos*: *incliti mei, ignominiose*). | Translate the Latin: *pesados de coração*. The DO pt-PT "duros de coração" drifts to a different idiom. No verb in the Latin — do not supply "sereis". |
| 4:4 | *mirificávit* | L&S: ecclesiastical Latin, "to exalt, make wonderful, magnify", citing this verse. LXX *ἐθαυμάστωσεν*. | One controlled rendering — the verb recurs (15:3, 16:7, 30:22). |
| 4:4 | *sanctum suum* | LXX *τὸν ὅσιον αὐτοῦ*, Heb. *ḥāsîd*. | *o seu santo*. Glossary term: *sanctus* (= *ὅσιος*) recurs across the psalter. |
| 4:5 | *Irascímini* | Deponent; imperative and indicative are the same form, as in LXX *ὀργίζεσθε*. Parallel with *nolíte* makes it imperative; so does Eph 4:26, which Hetzenauer's margin cites. | *Irai-vos*. |
| 4:5 | *quæ dícitis … compungímini* | Relative clause fronted: "the things you say in your hearts, on your beds be pierced (for them)". *compungi* in late Latin = be stung by conscience (L&S). Rahlfs lacks the relative (*λέγετε … καὶ*); the Latin follows the reading with *ἃ*. Hebrew: "speak in your hearts on your beds, and be silent". | Keep the Latin's syntax, not the Hebrew's. *compungi-vos* keeps the sting; "arrependei-vos" loses it. |
| 4:6 | *Quis osténdit nobis bona?* | *ostendit* is present or perfect by form; LXX *δείξει* is future. *bona* plural, LXX *τὰ ἀγαθά*. | Present: *Quem nos mostra os bens?* — plural kept. |
| 4:7 | *Signátum est* | L&S *signo*: mark, seal, stamp (as a coin). LXX *ἐσημειώθη*. Hebrew is an imperative, "lift up" (*iuxta Hebraeos*: *leva*). | A stamped mark, perfect passive: *Foi marcada / Está selada*. Not "levantai". |
| 4:8 | *A fructu* | The Latin = *ἀπὸ καρποῦ*. Rahlfs prints *ἀπὸ καιροῦ* ("from the time"), which is what the Hebrew and *iuxta Hebraeos* have (*in tempore*). | *fruto* stands. A reviewer who checks Rahlfs will think it an error; this note is the answer. |
| 4:8 | *sui … multiplicáti sunt* | *sui* = LXX *αὐτῶν*, "their"; subject of *multiplicáti sunt* is "they" — the *multi* of v. 6, masculine plural. | *do seu trigo … se multiplicaram*. The subject stays unnamed, as in the Latin. |
| 4:9 | *in idípsum* | = LXX *ἐπὶ τὸ αὐτό*, "together, at once"; *iuxta Hebraeos*: *simul*. Not in L&S as a lemma. Recurs at 33:4, 40:7, 61:10, 73:6, 121:3, so it is a formula. Augustine's reading of *idipsum* as God the Selfsame (Conf. IX) is a reception fact — from memory, verify before citing. | Needs one fixed rendering, decided with all six places in view. Draft: *a um só tempo*. |
| 4:10 | *singuláriter in spe* | = *κατὰ μόνας ἐπ' ἐλπίδι*. *iuxta Hebraeos*: *specialiter securum*. The adverb can lean on God ("you alone") or on *me* ("apart, singly"); the Latin leaves it open. | Keep it open and adverbial: *singularmente*. |

**Pointing.** 4:5 carries `‡`: the app prays it with the mediant after *peccáre*; with flexes, mediant after *vestris*. 4:6 carries `†`, which the app drops. Both verses need a Portuguese that breathes at all three places.
