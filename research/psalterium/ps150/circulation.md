# Ps 150 — Brazilian circulation checked (2026-09-23)

I fetched these sources. None of this comes from memory. The Hebrew-based texts show what Brazilian ears already know. They are never evidence of sense (rule 1).

- **liturgiadashoras.online/salmo-150/** (WebFetch; short quotes only, because the page is © CNBB — *"A tradução da Liturgia das Horas nesta página pertence à © Conferência Nacional dos Bispos do Brasil"*). It is the CNBB Liturgia das Horas text, and it is made from the Hebrew:
  - *"Louvai o Senhor Deus no santuário, louvai-o no alto céu de seu poder!"*
  - *"Louvai-o com o toque da trombeta"*; *"louvai-o com a harpa e com a cítara"*
  - *"Louvai-o com a dança e o tambor"*; *"louvai-o com as cordas e as flautas"*
  - *"Louvai-o com os címbalos sonoros, louvai-o com os címbalos de júbilo"*
  - *"Louve a Deus tudo o que vive e que respira, tudo cante os louvores do Senhor"*
- **WebSearch** `"todo espírito louve o Senhor"` found no Brazilian text with that wording. The line people know in Brazil comes from the Hebrew: *"Todo ser que respira louve ao Senhor"* (ARA, via bible.com / YouVersion 1608 PSA.150.6) and *"Tudo que tem fôlego louve ao Senhor"* (bibliaon.com, ibasp.org.br). Both are Protestant, Hebrew-based versions.

- **dicio.com.br/sonoro/** (WebFetch, for the Latinist's v1 remark on *benesonántibus*): sense 2 *"Que tem som claro e agradável: voz sonora"*; sense 4 *"[Figurado] Melodioso, harmonioso, suave ao ouvido: canto sonoro"*.

**What this shows.** Brazilian ears already know *Louvai … louvai-o*, *címbalos sonoros* (also MS1932 and DM1962), *dança* for the Hebrew's *machol*, *flautas* for *ugab*, and a closing line on "what breathes". Matos Soares 1932, a Vulgate-family version, also has *Tudo o que respira*, and it has *em coro* and *órgão*. The Latin's *omnis spíritus* has no current Brazilian wording of its own. *Todo espírito* is Douay-Rheims' "every spirit" in Portuguese, and it is not a line anyone in Brazil already knows.
