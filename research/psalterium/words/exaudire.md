# *exaudire*

2026-09-20. Word study; the data is [`exaudire.concordance.md`](exaudire.concordance.md) (generated — every occurrence with its Greek and the pt-PT antecedent). **Not decided** — the recommendation at the end is Claude's, for Gustavo to take or leave.

## What the word is in the psalter

- **74 occurrences in 44 psalms.** 33 of them are the bare imperative *exáudi* — nearly half. 14 open their verse (*Exáudi, Deus, oratiónem meam*). It is the psalter's verb of petition, and it is also the Office's: *V. Dómine, exáudi oratiónem meam* (Ps 101:2) is one of the common versicles of the hours (`content/do/horas/Latin/Psalterium/Common/Prayers.txt:85`; the phrase occurs in 12 files of the DO Office data), so its rendering will be said far more often than the psalms themselves.
- **Sense.** Lewis & Short: classically "to hear clearly"; in prayer, "to hearken, listen to; to regard, grant" — citing Ps 142:1 for it. So *ex-audire* is still a verb of **hearing**, with the favourable outcome carried by the context of prayer, not by a different root.
- **Two Greek verbs stand behind it:** *εἰσακούω* 50×, *ἐπακούω* 25× (6 verses did not match by number; 1 simple *ἀκούω*). The split does not follow the Latin form — imperatives, pasts and futures each divide about two to one. The Latin psalter chose not to distinguish them. One Portuguese verb for all 74 is therefore the faithful policy, not a simplification.

## Its neighbours — the verbs it must not be confused with

| Latin | n | What it does | pt-PT antecedent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *audire* | 47 in 36 psalms | plain hearing — God's and men's alike (*audívimus*, *audíte*, *áudiam*) | *ouvir* 26× |
| *inténdere* | 23 (14 × *inténde*) | "turn toward, give heed" | *atender* 6×, *ouvir* 7× |
| *áuribus percípere* | 8 (7 × *pércipe*) | "take in with the ears" | — |
| *atténdere* | 2 | "pay heed" | — |

They are not loose synonyms scattered across the book: they stand **beside** *exaudire* as its parallel, inside one verse, nine times —

> 16:1 *Exáudi, Dómine, justítiam meam: \* inténde deprecatiónem meam.*
> 54:2 *Exáudi, Deus, oratiónem meam… \* inténde mihi, et exáudi me.*
> 60:2 *Exáudi, Deus, deprecatiónem meam: \* inténde oratióni meæ.*
> 140:1 *Dómine, clamávi ad te, exáudi me: \* inténde voci meæ…*
> 53:4 · 38:13 · 83:9 · 142:1 *exáudi… \* áuribus pércipe…*
> 9:38 *…exaudívit Dóminus: \* præparatiónem cordis eórum audívit auris tua.*

Whatever renders *exaudire* has to leave a **different** verb free for *inténde* in the same breath, and a third for *audire*.

## The constraints

1. **The address is *vós*.** The *vós* imperative of *ouvir* is *ouvi* — also "I heard". With 33 imperatives, *ouvir* fails at the word's most frequent form. (Under *tu* it would have been the obvious choice: Matos Soares 1932 has *ouviu-me… ouve… me ouvirá*.)
2. **It must stay apart from *audire*.** *Ouvir* is the natural home of *audire* and should be kept for it.
3. **It must stay apart from *inténde*.** The antecedent's instinct for *inténde* is *atende* — and it is a good one: *atender a* is exactly "turn toward, give heed".
4. **The ear Brazil already has.** The versicle circulates as both *Senhor, ouvi a minha oração* and *Senhor, escutai a minha oração* (*E chegue até vós o meu clamor*) in missals and devotionaries found online. None of those is an official liturgical book; the wording of the Brazilian *Liturgia das Horas* was **not verified**.
5. **Cadence.** *atendei* / *escutai*: both three syllables, both oxytone — no difference. *atendestes* / *escutastes*, *atenderá* / *escutará*: the same.

## The candidates

| | For | Against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **ouvir** | The antecedents' word (pt-PT 40×, Matos Soares 1932); one of the two living forms of the versicle | *ouvi* = "I heard" under *vós*; takes *audire*'s verb |
| **atender** | Says "hear and grant" outright | Leaves the hearing family, so 9:38 (*exaudívit… audívit auris*) and 142:1 (*exáudi… áuribus pércipe… exáudi*) lose their play on hearing; **collides with *inténde***, its own parallel in five verses; says more than the Latin word does |
| **escutar** | A hearing verb, as *exaudire* is — *ouvir* / *escutar* mirrors *audire* / *ex-audire*; the other living form of the versicle; collides with nothing (the antecedent uses it 4× for *exaudire*, once for *audire*); safe at every form under *vós* | "Listen" does not by itself say "grant" — but neither does the Latin outside the context of prayer |

## Recommendation

**exaudire → *escutar*; audire → *ouvir*; inténdere → *atender*; áuribus percípere → *dar ouvidos* (to be studied in its turn).**

This **reverses draft 2 of Ps 4**, which took *atender* to escape the *ouvi* trap without having looked at *inténde*. How it prays:

## Not known

- The Hebrew behind the two Greek verbs was not checked. General knowledge says *ἐπακούω* tends to render "answer" and *εἰσακούω* "hear" — which would explain why the Hebrew-family Bibles have *responde-me* at 4:2 — but that is unverified here.
- Blaise and Souter (Christian Latin) are not online and were not consulted; Lewis & Short is classical-first.
- The pt-PT file's verse ids drift from the Latin's in about one row in five, so its tallies are indicative, not exact.
- Official Brazilian liturgical wording of the versicle.
