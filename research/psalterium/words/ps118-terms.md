# The law-vocabulary of Ps 118

2026-09-20/21. Word study for *Beáti immaculáti in via*; written before a verse was translated. The data: [`../ps118/terms.md`](../ps118/terms.md) (counts, generated by `ps118/terms.py --psalter`), [`../ps118/renderings.md`](../ps118/renderings.md) (what each version did, verse by verse, generated by `ps118/renderings.py`), and the concordances `lex`, `testimonium`, `mandatum`, `iustificatio`, `iudicium`, `verbum`, `sermo`, `eloquium`, `via`, `semita`, `praeceptum` in this folder (`sh research/psalterium/ps118/concordances.sh` regenerates them). The ruling at the end is Claude's, made under the delegation recorded in `DECISIONS.md`; it is provisional until Gustavo has read it.

The `pt-PT` column of those concordances and the "pt-PT" block of the parallels are DO's present Portuguese — the text this project replaces. It is not counted as evidence anywhere below (D12); where it is mentioned it is only "what is there now".

What the counts say:

- **182 law-words in 176 verses.** Only six verses have none of the eight (118:3, 37, 90, 91, 122, 151 — and 3, 37, 151 have *via* instead); twelve have two. Every stanza has between 6 and 11. Whatever is chosen is said about once a verse, all day long at the little hours.
- **Almost half of them (81) stand at a cadence** — before the asterisk or at the end, alone or followed only by *tuus*. Because Portuguese puts the possessive first (*os vossos mandamentos*), the law-word itself will be the cadence word far more often than in the Latin, where *tuis / tuas / tuum* usually is. Stress and sound at the close decide a great deal here.
- ***mandáta* is the commonest (38), not *lex* (28).** *justificatiónes* is second (29) — and 29 of its 30 occurrences in the whole psalter are in this psalm. It is a word that exists, for the pray-er, only here. Whatever renders it is heard as "the word of Ps 118".
- ***præcéptum* never occurs in Ps 118.** That frees *preceito* for another term, if the eight places where *præcéptum* does stand can do without it (§5).
- ***sermo* is rare (5) and always plural; *elóquium* is frequent (20) and mostly singular.** The task of keeping three "word" terms apart is mostly the task of finding a good word for *elóquium*.
- The Latin is not mechanically regular. *verbum* is missing from 9 stanzas, *justificátio* from 2, *lex* from Beth only. The psalm's variation is real variation, and rule 6 (what repeats in the Latin repeats identically) keeps it for free so long as each term has exactly one word.

## 2. The Greek behind them

From the Rahlfs text in `consult/parallels/ps118.md`, read verse by verse against the Latin (the concordances match by verse, not by word, so their tallies were checked by eye for Ps 118).

| Latin | Greek | Does the Greek distinguish what the Latin distinguishes? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *lex* | νόμος, always | yes, one to one |
| *testimónia* | μαρτύρια, always | yes, one to one |
| *mandáta* | ἐντολαί, always | yes, one to one |
| *justificatiónes* | δικαιώματα — 27 of 29 | nearly. Two departures: **118:20** the Greek has κρίματα (the Latin *justificatiónes*), and **118:62** *judícia justificatiónis tuæ* stands for κρίματα τῆς **δικαιοσύνης** σου — the phrase that is *judícia justítiæ tuæ* in 118:7, 106, 160, 164. The singular *justificátio* is therefore not the same thing as the plural: it is "justice", once. |
| *judícia* | κρίματα (plural and most singulars); κρίσις for the singular in 118:84, 137, 154 | the Latin merges κρίμα and κρίσις into one word; nothing to keep apart |
| *verbum* | λόγος | **no.** |
| *sermo* | λόγος (always plural, λόγοι) | **no** — *verbum* and *sermo* are one Greek word. Both render plural λόγοι (*verba* 118:74, 101, 139, 147, 160, 161; *sermónes* 118:9, 16, 17, 42, 130). In 118:42 the Greek has λόγον … λόγους in one verse and the Latin varies: *verbum … sermónibus*. The distinction is the Latin's own. |
| *elóquium* | λόγιον, 19 of 20 (118:154 has λόγον in Rahlfs) | **yes** — λόγιον (an utterance, an oracle) against λόγος. This is the distinction that goes back through the Greek to the Hebrew. |
| *via* | ὁδός | yes; *sémita* is τρίβος |

So the three "word" terms are of unequal standing. *verbum / elóquium* is a real pair (λόγος / λόγιον). *verbum / sermo* is a variation the Latin translator made inside one Greek word. Rule 1 says translate the Latin as Latin, so all three stay distinct; but if one distinction ever has to be given up, it is *sermo*'s, and the merge would be into *palavra* — which is what the Greek says.

Elsewhere in the psalter the three meet in single verses, which is the practical reason they need three words: **55:11** *In Deo laudábo verbum, in Dómino laudábo sermónem*; **102:20** *faciéntes verbum illíus, ad audiéndam vocem sermónum ejus*; **147:4** *Qui emíttit elóquium suum terræ: velóciter currit sermo ejus*; and here **118:42**.

## 3. Lewis & Short

(`lexicon.py`; letters J and T fetched into `consult/`.)

- ***justificátio*** — late Latin. (1) "justification". (2) "(as if from *justa-facio*) due formality": *juxta omnes cærimónias et justificatiónes*, Vulg. Num 9:3. That second sense is the psalm's: an ordinance, a thing laid down as right to do. The Portuguese cognate has only the first sense (and, in daily speech, a third: an excuse).
- ***elóquium*** — classical poets: eloquence; "in late Lat., declaration, communication in general … *elóquia pulchritúdinis*, fine words, Vulg. Gen 49:21". An utterance: the thing spoken, from *é-loqui*, to speak out.
- ***sermo*** — "a speaking or talking with any one; talk, conversation, discourse"; "ordinary speech, speaking, talking" (*sermo est orátio remíssa et finítima cottidiánæ locutióni*); "a language"; late, a single word. The everyday word for talk, as against *orátio*.
- ***testimónium*** — "witness, evidence, attestation"; what serves as proof. Nothing legal-code about it: the word is a calque of μαρτύριον and keeps its plain sense.
- *verbum*, *mandátum*, *judícium*, *lex* — plain; not looked up beyond confirming that *mandátum* is the noun of *mandáre* (118:4 *Tu mandásti mandáta tua* plays on it; so does the Greek, ἐνετείλω τὰς ἐντολάς).

## 4. What the versions did

Tallies from `ps118/renderings.md` (per verse; a verse with two law-words credits both, so read them as ±2).

| Latin | Douay-Rheims (Vulgate) | Matos Soares 1932 (Vulgate) | Diurnal Monástico 1962 (Hebrew — diction only) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *lex* 28 | law 28 | lei ~23 | lei 25 |
| *testimónia* 22 | testimonies 22 | **testemunhos 6, preceitos 6, mandamentos 5**, leis 2, ensinamentos 2, ordens, decretos | testemunhos 12, preceitos 7, decretos |
| *mandáta* 38 | commandments 34, precepts 3 | mandamentos ~30, leis 4, preceitos 2 | mandamentos 21, **preceitos 15** |
| *justificatiónes* 29 | **justifications 29** | ***leis* (*justas leis*, *leis justas*) ~10, preceitos 9, (*justos*) decretos 5, ordens 2, determinações, *justos mandamentos*** | **decretos 23**, preceitos 5 |
| *judícia* 23 | judgments 21 | juízos 14, *leis* 4, preceitos 2 | **sentenças 10, decretos 8** |
| *verbum* 17 | word 17 | palavra 11, promessa 3 | palavra 16 |
| *sermo* 5 | word 5 | palavra 4 | palavra 5 |
| *elóquium* 20 | word 19, saying 1 | palavra 15, promessa 3 | **promessa 15**, palavra 2 |

- **Douay-Rheims held five of the eight rigidly** (law, testimonies, commandments, justifications, judgments) and **merged the three "word" terms into one** (*word*). It is the model of consistency for the first five and no help for the last three.
- **Matos Soares did not hold anything but *lei*, *mandamentos* and *palavra*.** His own footnote says why: "A lei de Deus é chamada com nomes diferentes, mas que tem o mesmo sentido." He treats the terms as interchangeable, so *justificatiónes* is seven different things in his text and *testimónia* is six. He never once writes *justificações*. For this psalm he marks the outer bound of rule 2 by being *too loose*, not a pattern to follow: rule 6 asks for more than he gave.
- **The Diurnal** follows the Hebrew's eight terms, not the Latin's, so its distribution cannot be copied; but it shows which nouns a Brazilian monk of 1962 could say 176 times: *lei, testemunhos, preceitos, mandamentos, decretos, sentenças, palavra, promessa*. Note what is **absent**: *justificações*, *estatutos*, *juízos* (he has *sentenças*), *oráculos*.
- The CNBB Bible (Hebrew family; `consult/bolls-CNBB-19-119.json`, tallied by `ps118/cnbb_tally.py`) — current Brazilian Catholic diction: *preceitos* 27, *lei* 25, *testemunhos* 23, *estatutos* 23, *palavra* 23, *promessa* 19, *normas* 18, *mandamentos* 16. A Florianópolis diocesan article on this psalm (Pe. Ney Brasil, fetched) lists the eight as "*lei, testemunho, palavra, preceito, estatuto, norma, mandamento, decreto*". So in Brazilian Catholic usage today the family is *lei / testemunhos / preceitos / estatutos / mandamentos / normas / decretos / palavra / promessa* — and again never *justificações*.
- What is in DO's Portuguese now (no weight): *justificações* 27, *testemunhos* 20, *mandamentos* 37, *juízos* 20, *palavra* for all three word-terms.
- **Web, for *justificatiónes* specifically.** Its one famous place outside the psalter is Luke 1:6, *in ómnibus mandátis et justificatiónibus Dómini* (Latin verified in DO's Gospel for the Vigil of St John Baptist; the Greek there is ἐντολαῖς καὶ δικαιώμασιν — the same pair as Ps 118). Three Portuguese versions fetched from bibliaon.com all have "**mandamentos e preceitos do Senhor**" (Almeida Revisada, Almeida Revista e Corrigida, and the site's own text). Those are Protestant editions; the Catholic ones (Ave Maria, Matos Soares NT) could **not** be fetched (HTTP 403) and are **unverified**. It is evidence of what Portuguese calls δικαιώματα beside *mandamentos*, not of Catholic usage in particular.

## 5. The candidates, term by term

Every candidate was tried for: stress at the cadence (last or second-last syllable), a plural, the feel of *os vossos … / as vossas …* said many times, collision with another Latin term, and accidental rhyme with its neighbours. The rhyme test, by `checks.py`'s own measure (stressed vowel onward): *lei* (-ei), *testemunhos* (-unhos), *mandamentos* (-entos), *preceitos* (-eitos), *juízos* (-ízos), *palavra* (-avra), *fala* (-ala), *dito* (-ito), *caminho* (-inho), *vereda* (-eda) — no two alike. (*julgamentos* for *judícia* would rhyme with *mandamentos* every time they met: one more reason against it.)

### *lex → lei*, *testimónia → testemunhos*, *mandáta → mandamentos*, *judícia → juízos*, *via → caminho*

The glossary's pencilled words, confirmed. Each is the cognate, each is what Douay-Rheims' rigid English corresponds to, each is in Matos Soares as his commonest word for it, all are paroxytone (or, for *lei*, a stressed monosyllable: *a vossa lei* closes well). *os vossos mandamentos* is long (8 syllables against *mandáta tua*'s 5) and will push many cola over the Latin's count; there is no shorter faithful word (*ordens* is *mandáre*'s and *ordinátio*'s; *preceitos* is wanted elsewhere), so the length is accepted psalm-wide.

- *testemunhos*: in current speech a *testemunho* is a person's witness. *Os vossos testemunhos* is heard, rightly, as "what you have attested" — the Latin is no more legal than that (L&S). The Hebrew-family *preceitos* (DM1962) is another word's meaning. Kept.
- *juízos*: DM1962's *sentenças* is the live alternative (in Brazil *juízo* is also good sense, and the law-court). But *judícium* is 66× in the psalter and must serve *judícium et justítiam* (118:121), *fácies judícium* (118:84), *in judício* (1:5) — *juízo* can, *sentença* cannot. Kept. The singular (7× here) stays *juízo*.
- *mandáre*: the glossary has *dar ordem* (open, Ps 90:11), noting that it loses the link with *mandáta*. In 118:4 (*Tu mandásti mandáta tua*) and 118:138 the link is the point. 118:4 is translated with *mandar*; the glossary row is widened to say so.

### *justificatiónes* — the hard one

| candidate | for | against |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **justificações** | the cognate; Douay-Rheims' policy; the reader crossing from the Latin column finds the same word | In Portuguese it means an excuse (*apresentar justificações*), a proof, or the theological act of being made just. *Ensinai-me as vossas justificações* is heard as "teach me your excuses / your reasons". The late-Latin sense "ordinance" (L&S 2) does not exist in the Portuguese word: it would need the footnote rule 5 forbids. Five syllables, 29 times, 16 of them at a cadence (*-ções* is at least oxytone). Matos Soares, translating this very Latin, never uses it; nor does any Brazilian version seen. |
| **preceitos** | Three syllables, paroxytone, a plural that is its normal form, native with every verb the psalm gives it — *guardar, ensinar, aprender, meditar, cumprir os vossos preceitos* (118:5, 12, 71, 16, 112), *cantáveis … os vossos preceitos* (118:54). It is what Portuguese Bibles call δικαιώματα beside *mandamentos* in Luke 1:6 (§4). Matos Soares' second commonest word for it (9×). A *preceito* is a rule of conduct laid down — L&S's "due formality" without the stiffness. | It is the cognate of *præcéptum*. But *præcéptum* is **never in Ps 118**, is always singular, and in 6 of its 8 places renders πρόσταγμα, "decree" (2:6, 7:7b, 80:5, 93:20, 98:7, 104:10, 148:6; 18:9 is ἐντολή) — for which *decreto* is the better word anyway: 148:6 *pôs um decreto, e não passará*; 2:6 *proclamando o seu decreto*; 98:7 *guardavam os seus testemunhos, e o decreto que lhes deu*. The *just-* root is lost (so it is with every candidate but the first and the last). In the Hebrew-family Brazilian Bibles *preceitos* names a different term of this psalm; that matters to nobody praying from the Latin. |
| **decretos** | DM1962's word for the matching Hebrew term (23×): proven sayable in a Brazilian choir. Three syllables, paroxytone. Collides with nothing in the Latin psalter (no *decrétum*). Matos Soares 5× (*justos decretos*). | A *decreto* in Brazil is first an act of government (*decreto presidencial*, *decreto-lei*), and *os decretos de Deus* are his sovereign decisions rather than rules one keeps: *ensinai-me os vossos decretos*, *cumprir os vossos decretos* are a shade off. And it is the natural word for *præcéptum* / πρόσταγμα (above), which would then have to take *preceito* — workable, the mirror image of the ruling. |
| estatutos | the Almeida / CNBB word for the Hebrew term | Hebrew-family, Protestant-coloured; today *estatuto* is the by-laws of a club or a statute of the Republic (*Estatuto da Criança*). Four syllables. |
| justiças | keeps the root; Ps 18:9 *Justítiæ Dómini rectæ* is δικαιώματα too | Collides inside the psalm: *judícia justítiæ tuæ* (118:7, 106, 160, 164) against *judícia justificatiónis tuæ* (118:62), and *justítia* → *justiça* is a glossary word (9× here). |
| ordens / ordenações | MS1932 2× | *ordem* is *mandáre*'s (*dar ordem*) and 118:91 has *ordinatióne tua*; *ordenações* is heavy and historical (*Ordenações Filipinas*). |
| leis justas, justos decretos | MS1932's favourite: it explains the word | two words for one, and the noun is another term's (*lex*). Beyond rule 2. |

***preceitos*** and ***decretos*** are the two real candidates; they differ in what is left over. With *preceitos* here, *præcéptum* becomes *decreto* — which suits πρόσταγμα better than *preceito* did. With *decretos* here, *præcéptum* keeps its cognate. I rule for *preceitos* because the psalm's verbs are about learning, keeping, doing and singing these things, which is what one does with *preceitos* and not quite with *decretos*; and because this word is said 29 times in one psalm and must be the warmer of the two.

The singular **118:62** *judícia justificatiónis tuæ* is not this term (it is δικαιοσύνη, §2). The agent who reaches it should not write *do vosso preceito*; *os juízos da vossa justificação* (the one place the abstract cognate is right) or a decision there.

### *verbum / sermo / elóquium* — the second hard one

*verbum → palavra* is fixed from outside: *secúndum verbum tuum* (118:25, 65, 107) is the Nunc dimittis' *segundo a vossa palavra*.

**What *sermo* and *elóquium* have to be able to do**, from the concordances: *sermo* — God's words that one keeps, forgets, hopes in (Ps 118), but also a man's talk: 54:22 *Mollíti sunt sermónes ejus super óleum*, 108:3 *sermónibus ódii*, 138:4 *non est sermo in lingua mea*, 18:4 *Non sunt loquélæ, neque sermónes*. *elóquium* — what God utters (11:7 *Elóquia Dómini, elóquia casta*; the formula *secúndum elóquium tuum*, 7× + *propter* + *juxta*), but also a man's: 18:15a *elóquia oris mei*, 103:34 *elóquium meum*. Both must take *vosso/vossa* and a plural, and close a colon.

| candidate | for *sermo* | for *elóquium* |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **fala** (f.) | L&S's sense exactly: talk, ordinary speech. Native in every place tried: *as suas falas são mais macias que o azeite* (54:22), *não há fala na minha língua* (138:4), *com falas de ódio me cercaram* (108:3), *veloz corre a sua fala* (147:4), *guardando as vossas falas* (118:9). Paroxytone, two syllables. | Etymologically perfect (*é-loqui* : *falar*), but then nothing as good is left for *sermo*. |
| **dito** (m.) | possible (*os vossos ditos*) | λόγιον is exactly what scholarship calls a *dito* (the *lógia* of Jesus are "os ditos de Jesus" in Portuguese — general knowledge, unverified). *Os ditos do Senhor são ditos puros* (11:7), *os ditos da minha boca* (18:15a), *o dito do Senhor o inflamou* (104:19), *segundo o vosso dito* (118:41 …), *no meu coração escondi os vossos ditos* (118:11). Paroxytone, two syllables. Cost: it is a small word — *dito popular*, *dito e feito* — and in the formula *segundo o vosso dito* it is thinner than *promessa*. |
| **promessa** | — | DM1962 (15×), MS1932 (3×): in the formula *secúndum elóquium tuum* it is what the line means, and it prays beautifully. But it is an interpretation (rule 2: do not resolve), it comes from the Hebrew family, and it fails wherever the *elóquium* is kept, hidden, spoken or a man's: *elóquium tuum custodívi* (118:67), *elóquia tua non custodiérunt* (118:158) — one does not keep another's promise; *pronuntiábit lingua mea elóquium tuum* (118:172); 18:15a, 103:34. |
| oráculo | — | the dictionary sense of λόγιον, and "oráculo do Senhor" is a formula of the Brazilian lectionary (unverified). Proparoxytone: it would put the stress on the third-last syllable at ten cadences; and *os oráculos da minha boca* (18:15a) is absurd. |
| dizer(es) | — | *segundo o vosso dizer* is good; the plural *dizeres* means the wording on a sign. |
| discurso | what is there now in DO 3× | *guardarei os vossos discursos*: a speech from a platform. |
| palavra(s) for all three | what Douay-Rheims, Matos Soares and DM1962 all did for *sermo*; it is also what the Greek says | 55:11, 102:20, 118:42 set *verbum* beside *sermo*, 147:4 sets *elóquium* beside *sermo*: one word would erase a variation the Latin makes (rule 2: repetitions and variations are the Latin's to make). |

Ruling: ***sermo → fala***, ***elóquium → dito***. *fala* goes to *sermo* because there the match of sense is exact and the word is at its most natural; *dito* to *elóquium* because it is the one remaining plain word that means "a thing uttered", works for God and for man, and has a good plural. I am less sure of *dito* than of anything else in this study: the fall-back, if it sounds too slight when the psalm is prayed, is *promessa* inside Ps 118 only (accepting the misfit in 118:67, 158, 172), not *palavra*.

### *sémita → vereda*

τρίβος, a beaten track; 14× in the psalter, often beside *via* (24:4, 26:11, 76:20), so it needs its own word. *vereda*: paroxytone, plain, alive in Brazil, works in the plural (*luz para as minhas veredas*, 118:105) and for the sea (8:9 *as veredas do mar*). Alternatives: *senda* (literary), *trilha* (the modern word for exactly τρίβος, but it is a hiking trail today).

## 7. What the blind readers heard (added after 118:1–32 was translated)

Three readers of another model family read draft 1 of 118:1–32 (`ps118/critic/v1.*.json`); none saw this study.

- **Latinist:** no remark on any law-term, in either draft. The gate is clean on draft 2.
- ***falas* (sermo)** — the **stylist refused it three times** (118:9, 16, 17): it "sounds like turns in a conversation or lines in a play", and *guardar as vossas falas* "sounds made up"; he asked for *palavras*. The **ambiguity reader**, who had the Portuguese alone, understood it at once and rightly (*cumprindo aquilo que Deus diz*) and did not list it as unknown. Kept under D2 — *palavras* is another Latin word's rendering — but this is the ruling most exposed to the ear. If Gustavo's ear agrees with the stylist, the honest retreat is the merge into *palavras* (what the Greek says), not another invented word.
- ***ditos* (elóquium)** — one place (118:11); neither reader stumbled. The singular formula *segundo o vosso dito* has not been tried on a reader yet: the next agent's 118:38, 41 will be the test.
- ***preceitos* (justificatiónes)** — the ambiguity reader lists it as a word "an ordinary hearer might not know", at every one of its ten places; but the same list has *iniquidade*, *sondam*, *fitar*, *deleitei*, *vivificai-me*, *dormitou*, so its bar is low. The stylist did not remark on it. Kept: it is the word of Brazilian church speech (*dia de preceito*) and of this very psalm in the psalters Brazilians already pray. *decretos* would be better known and less apt.
- ***testemunhos*** — heard at 118:2 (*os seus testemunhos*) first as "accounts of religious experience", and from 118:14 on (*os vossos testemunhos*) as "declarations given by God", which is right. The price of the cognate; kept.
- ***juízos*** — heard as God's sentences first, his criteria of what is just second. Both are *judícia*.
- Verbs: ***confúndi → ser confundido* failed** — heard first as "I shall not be *confused*" in both verses (118:6, 31). Draft 2 has *ser envergonhado / não me envergonheis*; the glossary row says what that costs (the Te Deum's last line; *erubéscere* → *corar*). *cobiçar* + infinitive failed the stylist → *concupíscere → ansiar por*. *opróbrio* failed both readers → *afronta*. *exercitar-se em* was heard as practising before studying — as open as *exercéri in*; kept.
