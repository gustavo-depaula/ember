Fetched, not from memory. Hebrew-based texts: evidence of what Brazilian ears know, never of sense (rule 1).

- **Search result summary** (WebSearch, "Glorifica o Senhor, Jerusalém" salmo 147): Paulus "Liturgia diária das horas" (Sexta-feira, dia 4 and 14), liturgiadashoras.online "Salmo 147(147B)", musicasparamissa.com.br settings titled *"Salmo 147 (147B) – Glorifica o Senhor, Jerusalém!"* and *"Glorifica o Senhor Jerusalém, celebra teu Deus, ó Sião!"*. The search summary says the psalm is used for Corpus Christi and on Fridays in the Liturgy of the Hours.
- **liturgiadashoras.online/salmo-147147b/** (fetched): the page states *"A tradução da Liturgia das Horas nesta página pertence à © Conferência Nacional dos Bispos do Brasil"*; begins *"Glorifica o Senhor, Jerusalém!"*. Full text not quoted by the fetch tool (copyright).
- **paroquiadivino.org.br, "Salmo – Sl 147,12-13.14-15.19-20 (R. 12)"** — the responsorial psalm of the Brazilian lectionary (fetched): refrain *"Glorifica o Senhor, Jerusalém; celebra teu Deus, ó Sião!"*; v13 *"Pois reforçou com segurança as tuas portas"*; v14 *"A paz em teus limites garantiu e te dá como alimento a flor do trigo"*.

Result: the Brazilian ear knows the psalm as *Glorifica o Senhor, Jerusalém … celebra teu Deus, ó Sião* and the Corpus Christi line as *a flor do trigo* — both from the Hebrew (שַׁבְּחִי "praise/glorify"; חֵלֶב חִטִּים "the fat = finest of wheat"). The Latin has *Lauda* (→ *louvar*, the psalter's verb for *laudáre*) and *ádipe fruménti* (→ *a gordura do trigo*, as 80:17 *ex ádipe fruménti* already reads). Neither Brazilian wording can be taken without leaving the Latin; *flor do trigo* is kept as an option in `adipe`.
