minor

“cujo auxílio é o Deus de Jacó” cujus Deus Jacob adjútor ejus

adjutor is a personal agent noun (helper), and 'auxílio' makes it the abstract 'help'. That is a small shift from a concrete agent to an abstraction.

proposedBem-aventurado aquele cujo auxiliador é o Deus de Jacó

kept as an option under Beátus, cujus Deus Jacob adjútor ejus — the adjútor row keeps auxílio 14/14; the same request refused at 118:114 and 9:10 (D24, D27)

7a

minor

“Ele guarda a verdade para sempre” Qui custódit veritátem in sǽculum

The Latin keeps the relative chain from v. 6 ('qui fecit... qui custodit'), and the translation turns it into a new main clause. This is defensible because the prayed verse starts fresh, but a relative would be closer and would match 'que fez' in 145:5.

proposedQue guarda a verdade para sempre, † faz juízo para os que sofrem injustiça: * dá alimento aos famintos.

kept as an option under Qui custódit veritátem in sǽculum — he calls the finite form defensible; a relative heading a prayed verse reads as an exclamation; the ambiguity reader heard Ele as God

This is a faithful, close translation. Tenses are kept exactly, including the futures suscipiet/disperdet and regnabit, and nothing is added or dropped. The two minor points are the abstract 'auxílio' for the agent noun adjutor and the break in the relative 'Qui' at 145:7a. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse.

2b

fails: cadence

“Não confieis nos príncipes: *”

'príncipes' is proparoxytone, so the mediant lands on a dactyl and the tone has to drag the stress back. The half-verse also ends on a weak fall before the colon that carries the argument.

proposedNos príncipes não confieis: *

❧ taken

5

fails: sayable, native

“Bem-aventurado aquele cujo auxílio é o Deus de Jacó, † a sua esperança está no Senhor, seu Deus:”

After the 'cujo' clause, 'a sua esperança está...' starts a new, unconnected sentence. The ear loses who 'sua' refers to, so the beatitude breaks in two at the flex. A relative that matches 'cujo' keeps it one statement, just as the Latin's parallel 'ejus... ejus' does.

proposedBem-aventurado aquele cujo auxílio é o Deus de Jacó, † e cuja esperança está no Senhor, seu Deus:

kept as an option under spes ejus in Dómino, Deo ipsíus — the Latin has neither conjunction nor relative; the ambiguity reader did not stumble

7a

fails: native, plain

“faz juízo para os que sofrem injustiça”

In Brazilian ears 'fazer juízo' means 'to behave sensibly' ('toma juízo'). It does not mean 'to render judgment'. 'para os que' is also flabbier than a plain dative.

proposedjulga a causa dos que sofrem injustiça

kept as an option under facit judícium injúriam patiéntibus — 102:6's identical wording; 'causa' supplied; 'justiça' is justítia's and chimes with injustiça

9

fails: native, sayable

“e exterminará os caminhos dos pecadores”

'exterminar' is used of living things (pests, peoples) and sounds odd applied to 'caminhos'. 'e ex-' also produces a vowel clash and a heavy, hissing four-syllable verb just before the final cadence.

proposede destruirá os caminhos dos pecadores

❧ taken

10

fails: plain, sayable

“pelos séculos, Sião, o teu Deus,”

Without an 'ó', 'Sião' reads for a moment as the thing in apposition, as if Sião were 'o teu Deus'. The vocative has to be marked so the phrase parses on first hearing, and the mediant should rest on the address.

proposedO Senhor reinará pelos séculos, o teu Deus, ó Sião, * de geração em geração.

❧ taken — the 'ó' taken; the Latin order refused because it rhymes Sião / geração

The psalm is mostly plain and strong. The anaphoric 'O Senhor...' run in 7b–9 chants cleanly, with good paroxytone cadences throughout. The weak spots are a few idiomatic missteps ('faz juízo', 'exterminará os caminhos'), a proparoxytone mediant in 2b, and two syntactic joins (5, 10) that a listener cannot parse on first hearing.

worst line145:7a

best line145:7b

major

“e voltará à terra” et revertétur in terram suam

The possessive 'suam' is dropped. The Latin says he returns to 'his own earth', the earth he came from, and the translation has lost that word.

proposede voltará à sua terra

minor

The Latin continues the relative chain from 'Deo ipsíus … qui fecit' (v. 5-6) with a second 'Qui'; turning it into an independent clause with a supplied 'Ele' breaks the syntactic dependence the Latin keeps. Defensible for readability, but a relative is closer.

Verse by verse

2a

The Alleluia title (LXX αλληλουια Αγγαιου καὶ Ζαχαριου; MS1932 'Aleluia: De Ageu e de Zacarias'; DRB 'Alleluia, of Aggeus and Zacharias') is not in DO's Latin, which opens on 'Lauda, ánima mea'; as in Pss 110–113 and 134–135 it is not reproduced (D7). 'na minha vida' for 'in vita mea' as 62:5, 103:33; the antiphon 'Laudábo * Deum meum in vita mea' ([Day3 Laudes1], [Daya6 Vespera], monastic Saturday Vespers) will read 'Louvarei o meu Deus na minha vida'. 'Senhor … Senhor' at the flex and mediant is the Latin's Dóminum / Dóminum. The Offertory (Pasc3-0, Pasc7-5) has 'quámdiu ero'; the Portuguese is the same.

4

'ejus' (singular) against 'eórum' (plural) kept: 'o seu espírito' / 'os pensamentos deles'. cogitátio → pensamento (row); 55:6 'todos os seus pensamentos'. períre → perecer (row). 'in illa die' → 'naquele dia'.

5

The formula 'que fez o céu e a terra' (row) follows 'seu Deus' directly; 'tudo o que neles há' as 95:11 'et ómnia quæ in eis sunt'. 145:6 is carried inside the DO verse 145:5; the inline (6) marker is not reproduced.

7a

esca → alimento where God gives food (110:4, 135:25, 103:27); esuriéntes → famintos (106:5, 106:36).

7b

The row 'compedíti · sólvere' names this verse: 'solta os acorrentados', = 101:21. illumináre → iluminar (18:9 'ilumina os olhos'); the Latin says the Lord gives light to the blind, not that he opens their eyes (the Hebrew, DM1962 and the CNBB LH 'abre os olhos aos cegos'). The fourfold anaphora 'O Senhor' of 7b–9 is kept.

8b

díligere → amar (row).

9

suscípere (persons) → amparar (D19), the Latin future kept ('amparará') beside the present 'guarda'; pupíllus → órfão (row); the Latin order 'o órfão e a viúva' kept (the CNBB LH inverts it). via → caminho; 1:1 'no caminho dos pecadores'.

10

'O Senhor reinará' puts the subject first where the Latin has the verb first (natural order; 9:37 'O Senhor reinará para sempre'). The vocative, marked with 'ó', is placed before the apposition to avoid the Sião / geração rhyme (decision sion).
