Verse by verse

1

*oves páscuæ tuæ → as ovelhas da vossa pastagem*, as the *páscua* row foresaw (78:13, 99:3b, 94:7 should agree). *super* → *contra* (ἐπί of hostility; DRB *against*). The verse opens the Introit of Pent13-0 as its psalm verse (missa/Latin/Tempora/Pent13-0.txt).

2

Reads alone as the Thursday antiphon (with *Dómine*) and as the Preces versicle (*Meménto* for *Memor esto*); both halves stand alone. *Memor esto* → *Lembrai-vos de* (row; 118:49), the same at 73:18 and 73:22.

2b

*in quo habitásti in eo* has a Hebrew-style resumptive pronoun ('in which you dwelt in it'). Portuguese cannot say it, so *em que habitastes* renders both. This is syntax, not a repetition the ear could keep. *mons Sion* is nominative, an exclamation in apposition, and stays so (*o monte Sião*, as 47:3).

4

*gloriári* absolute → *se gloriaram* (row, 31:11 absolute; 51:3 the wicked man's boasting). *odérunt* → present *odeiam* (the *odísse* row), as 73:23.

6

*a derrubaram*: the Latin's feminine *eam* has no stated antecedent (the doors? the forest *silva*? the house of God?), and the Portuguese *a* is just as open (*selva* is feminine too). Left so. *silva → selva* (D44). *lignórum → de árvores* (1:3a *lignum → árvore*). *excídere → cortar*, *dejícere → derrubar*: two verbs kept two.

8

*Quiéscere faciámus → Façamos cessar* (DRB *abolish*, MS1932 *Façamos cessar*); *a terra → da terra*, put before the object so that it is not heard as *Deus da terra*. The Greek has 'let us burn' (κατακαύσωμεν), and the Latin is followed.

10

The formula *Úsquequo … in finem?* → *Até quando … para sempre?* (D27), here with *Deus*. *adversárius → adversário*: new, and plain.

11

*sinus → seio* (34:13b *ao meu seio*). *déxtera → a direita* (row).

13

The run of *Tu* (73:13, 14, 15 ×2, 16, 17 ×2) is kept exactly where the Latin has it, and nowhere else.

14

*draco → dragão* (90:13).

15

*dirúmpere → romper* (row, 2:3). *fontes, et torréntes* without articles, as the Latin (*torrens → torrente*, row). *Ethan* is a proper name in the Latin and the Greek (Ηθαμ); the Hebrew איתן means 'ever-flowing', which the Latin did not read. Spelled *Etã*, the Portuguese form of the name.

16

*Tuus est dies, et tua est nox* → *Vosso é o dia, e vossa é a noite*, the predicate first, as the Latin has it and as Portuguese allows.

17

*términos terræ → os limites da terra* (row, 2:8; ὅρια here, not the πέρατα of 64:8 *os confins*). *plasmáre → moldar* (row, 118:73). The Latin's resumptive *ea* is kept (*vós os moldastes*).

18

*Memor esto hujus* → *Lembrai-vos disto*. *pópulus insípiens* has no article in the Latin or the Greek (λαὸς ἄφρων), so *um povo insensato* (MS1932 the same).

19

*béstiæ → feras* (the *oves / boves / pécora* row reserves *feras* for béstiæ). The Latin repeats *ánimas* in both colons, and so does the Portuguese. The Introit of Pent13-0 and the Gradual of Quad4-4 adapt this verse with 73:20 and 73:22 (*Réspice, Dómine, in testaméntum tuum … ne obliviscáris in finem*; grep).

20

*Réspice in → Olhai para* (the formula *Réspice in me → Olhai para mim*). *testaméntum → aliança* (row).

21

*pauper et inops → o pobre e o carente* (D38). *laudáre → louvar*.

22

*Exsúrge, Deus → Levantai-vos, Deus* (row). *júdica causam tuam → julgai a vossa causa* (118:154 *a minha causa*).

23

*voces inimicórum tuórum*: the Greek has 'your suppliants' (τῶν ἱκετῶν σου), and the Latin's enemies are followed. *ascéndit → sobe* (46:6 *Subiu Deus*); MS1932's *aumenta* interprets. *semper → sempre* (row).
