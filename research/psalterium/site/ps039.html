not taken — *além do número* is not Portuguese; *todo* is what the idiom needs to say 'past counting' (grammar, D2). *sem número* stays an option.

Imperfect subjunctive rendered as present subjunctive; tense shifted.

proposedque eu fizesse a vossa vontade

3

fails: native, plain

“e da lama do lodo”

Lama and lodo are near-synonyms, so the phrase sounds like a stammer rather than an image, and the l-l-d-l-d run is sticky in the mouth. The Latin fæx is the sediment at the bottom, which is a distinct second noun.

proposede da lama do fundo

❧ taken — In substance: the Latinist's *borra*, which keeps fæx as a noun; *do fundo* would turn it into a place.

5

fails: native

“e que não olhou para as vaidades e as loucuras falsas”

'Loucuras falsas' sounds like a calque: in Portuguese a madness is not 'false'. 'Olhou para' is flat for respexit (turned his gaze toward).

proposede que não se voltou para as vaidades e as loucuras enganosas

not taken — respícere → olhar (12:3, 21:2, 24:16); *voltar-se* is convérti's. *voltou os olhos para* stays an option.

6

fails: native, sayable

“Vós fizestes muitas coisas, Senhor, meu Deus, as vossas maravilhas”

'Muitas coisas' is then corrected by the apposition 'as vossas maravilhas', so the colon sounds like a speaker restarting halfway through. 'Coisas' is also filler that the Latin does not have: multa agrees with mirabilia.

proposedMuitas fizestes vós, Senhor, meu Deus, as vossas maravilhas

❧ taken

8

fails: sayable, native

“está escrito de mim que eu faça a vossa vontade”

'Escrito de mim' is ambiguous to the ear (it can sound like 'written by me'), and the colon is very long for one breath.

proposedNa cabeça do livro está escrito de mim: que eu faça a vossa vontade (or: está escrito a meu respeito que eu faça a vossa vontade)

kept as an option under scriptum est de me ut fácerem voluntátem tuam — *a meu respeito* is option 2 (two syllables more on a colon already +3). The blind reader heard *de mim* as 'about me'. A colon after *de mim* adds a break the Latin lacks.

10

fails: sayable, plain

“não reterei os meus lábios”

The re-te-rei stutter trips the tongue, and 'reter os lábios' is not idiomatic.

proposedeis que não conterei os meus lábios: Senhor, vós o sabeis

not taken — The row prohibére → reter (118:101, 33:14, and names this verse). The blind reader heard 'I will not keep silent' — the sense. *conter* noted in the row as the stylist's second proposal (after 33:14).

11b

fails: plain

“da congregação numerosa”

This sounds administrative and heavy at the final cadence.

proposedda numerosa assembleia (or, if the word must differ from 39:10: do grande concílio)

not taken — D34: concílium by its Greek; here συναγωγή → *congregação*; *assembleia* is ecclésia's, one verse before.

12

fails: native

“não ponhais longe de mim as vossas compaixões”

'Pôr longe' is a stiff calque of longe facias. The plural 'compaixões' is rare and sounds strange.

proposedVós, porém, Senhor, não afasteis de mim as vossas compaixões

not taken — The Latin says *longe*; 21:20 *não ponhais longe de mim*, the same Greek. miseratiónes → compaixões (row), kept apart from misericórdia in the next colon.

14

fails: native, no rhyme

“Senhor, olhai para me auxiliar”

'Olhai para me auxiliar' first sounds like 'look at me', then turns into a purpose clause, which is awkward. Its -ar also echoes the -ar of 'libertar-me' at the mediant.

proposedSenhor, olhai em meu auxílio

❧ taken — In part: *olhai para auxiliar-me* ends the mishearing, *que me liberteis* the rhyme. *em meu auxílio* refused: it is adjutórium's (69:2a) and would pre-empt D40.

15

fails: sayable, native

“Sejam envergonhados e desonrados juntos os que buscam”

'Juntos' dangles after the two participles and jams against 'os que', so the reader loses the subject.

proposedSejam juntos envergonhados e desonrados os que buscam a minha alma

❧ taken — In part: a comma after *juntos*; the adverb stays after the verbs as 34:26.

16

fails: native

“Que bom, que bom”

This sounds sincere and cheerful, not like the jeering of enemies. The mockery of Euge, euge is lost to the ear.

proposedos que me dizem: Bem feito, bem feito!

not taken — The row euge (34:21, 34:25; 39:16 named to copy): *Bem feito* was heard as 'deserved punishment' at 34:25. The recurring complaint is added to the row.

18b

fails: concrete

“o meu auxílio e o meu protetor”

It pairs an abstract noun with a person-noun. Adjutor is a person, like protector, so the pair limps.

proposedVós sois o meu socorro e o meu protetor (or: o meu amparo e o meu protetor)

not taken — D19 row adjútor → auxílio (open for Gustavo); *socorro* is as abstract, *amparo* is suscípere's (39:12). The stylist's pairing point is the row's hard case, added to its evidence.

Overall the psalm prays well: the opening (Aguardando, aguardei), the new song and the closing plea (mendigo e pobre... não tardeis) have a plain, noble ring. Most of the problems are small calques (loucuras falsas, pôr longe, reter os lábios, olhar para me auxiliar) and one clumsy apposition in 39:6. The weakest spot in tone is 'Que bom, que bom', which empties the enemies' taunt of its menace.

best line39:18

“as loucuras enganosas” insánias falsas

'falsas' is simply 'false'; 'enganosas' (deceitful) slightly shifts the adjective toward an active sense the Latin does not specify, when the cognate is available.
