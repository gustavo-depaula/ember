Verse by verse

1

The three genitives stay distinct, as the glossary has them: ímpii → ímpios, peccatóres → pecadores (and 1:5–6 justi → justos). consílium → conselho (as Ps 118:24), via → caminho (D15). The verse ends on a colon, as the Latin does, because 1:2 continues the sentence. First colon +2 by the checker, the price of 'Bem-aventurado'. DM1962 has this psalm (monastic Prime): it was heard for diction only — it reads the Hebrew ('dos que zombam') and says 'Feliz'.

2

The Latin has no verb in the first colon; 'está' is the copula Portuguese needs (D2). volúntas → 'vontade', the Latin's word (Douay-Rheims 'his will is in the law'), not the Hebrew's 'delight' (DM1962 'se compraz'). Draft 2 takes the natural order in the first colon (decision 'v2order'). lex → lei, meditári → meditar em (D15; words/ps118-terms.md §5): 'na sua lei meditará' is the vocabulary of 118:16 'Nos vossos preceitos meditarei'. The future is kept (meditábitur; Matos Soares 1932 makes it a present). die ac nocte → 'dia e noite', the living phrase. The ambiguity reader asked whether it is the man or the Lord who meditates: he heard the man, rightly.

3a

'E será': the subject stays in the verb ending, as in the Latin; the man of 1:1–2 is still in the ear. fructum suum … in témpore suo: both possessives kept ('o seu fruto no seu tempo'); Matos Soares' idiom 'a seu tempo' is the alternative and drops the preposition 'in'. The ambiguity reader asked whose time — the tree's season or the man's moment: suo is as open, and he heard the tree's first. The verse ends on a colon as the Latin does.

3b

The stylist's best line. fólium ejus → 'a sua folha', singular as in the Latin. défluet → 'cairá' (Douay-Rheims 'fall off', Matos Soares 1932 'cairá'), not 'murchará', which is the Hebrew's verb. ómnia quæcúmque → 'todas as coisas que' in the plural, so that the verse closes on 'prosperarão' and not on 'prosperará', which would rhyme with 'cairá' at the mediant where the Latin has no echo (rule 5). prosperabúntur is passive ('shall be prospered'); Portuguese 'prosperar' is already intransitive, so the passive is grammar that yields (D2); the glossary's 'fazer prosperar' (117:25) is the active of the same verb. Who 'does' — the man or the tree — is left as open as in the Latin. The ambiguity reader heard a shade of material success in 'prosperarão': prosperári has it too.

4

'Non sic … non sic' repeated as in the Latin (the second 'non sic' is the Greek's plus, not in the Hebrew). No verb is supplied in either colon — the Latin has none (Matos Soares 1932 adds 'serão' after 'mas'); see the decision 'nonsic'. 'a fácie terræ' → 'da face da terra' is also the Greek's plus, kept; fácies → face (glossary).

5

Ídeo → 'Por isso'. in judício → 'no juízo' (D15 judícium → juízo; the study names this verse); the ambiguity reader heard the Last Judgment first and 'a judgment' second — judícium without an article is open in the same way. The second colon has no verb in the Latin and none is supplied (Matos Soares 1932 adds '(estarão)'); the reader noticed that 'ressurgirão' is the verb carried over, as resúrgent is in the Latin.

6

novit → 'conhece' (a perfect with present sense); the ambiguity reader heard 'knows how the just live' before 'acknowledges and watches over' — novit is no narrower. períbit → 'perecerá': it is the way that perishes, the Latin's strange and exact image, left alone (DM1962 'leva à perda' explains it); the reader heard 'the wicked will end in destruction', which is what the image means.
