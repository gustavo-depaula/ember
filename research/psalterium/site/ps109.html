1b

fails: plain

“por escabelo dos teus pés”

'escabelo' is a real word but bookish; most who pray it will not know it without a note. 'Estrado' is the plain Brazilian word for a footstool in the familiar liturgical form of this verse, and it keeps the same image.

proposedAté que eu ponha os teus inimigos * por estrado dos teus pés.

kept as an option under Donec ponam inimícos tuos, * scabéllum pedum tuórum — Glossary row (98:5), and the CNBB lectionary itself has *escabelo* in this verse, so the stylist's premise that *estrado* is the familiar liturgical form is not borne out.

2

fails: native, sayable

“domina tu no meio dos teus inimigos”

The imperative with a following 'tu' sounds stiff and a little Portuguese-from-Portugal. The ear hears 'domina tu' as two clashing stresses in a row. The 'tu' is there only so 'domina' is not read as 'ele domina'. Putting 'tu' before the verb keeps it clear and makes the line flow.

proposedO Senhor enviará de Sião a vara do teu poder: * tu, domina no meio dos teus inimigos.

kept as an option under domináre in médio inimicórum tuórum — A comma-set *tu* reads as a vocative. The postposed subject is MS1932's, and the ambiguity reader heard the imperative.

3

fails: sayable

“Contigo está o princípio no dia do teu poder, nos esplendores dos santos”

The mediant colon is too long for one breath (like the Latin, but the Portuguese adds syllables). 'está o' makes a flat hiatus near the start. Leaving out the supplied copula shortens the line and still keeps 'princípio' open, which the Latin leaves open.

proposedContigo, o princípio, no dia do teu poder, nos esplendores dos santos: * do ventre, antes da estrela da manhã, eu te gerei.

kept as an option under Tecum princípium — Saves one syllable, and the commas make *o princípio* an apposition. The Latin colon is as long.

7

fails: sayable

“exaltará a cabeça”

'-rá a' is a hiatus of two identical open vowels right before the final cadence. Sung, it either blurs into one syllable or stalls on the tone. The plainer verb 'erguerá' is still faithful to 'exaltabit caput' and is shorter.

proposedBeberá da torrente no caminho: * por isso erguerá a cabeça.

kept as an option under proptérea exaltábit caput — Glossary row exaltáre → exaltar. The vowels merge in speech.

A sober, faithful rendering that mostly sounds like Portuguese prayer. The future verbs in verses 5 to 7 give the ending a strong, hammering weight, as they should. The weak spots are one bookish noun (escabelo), a stiff imperative in verse 2, and the overloaded mediant of verse 3, which is hard to breathe through when chanted.

best line109:4

5ambiguity

minor

“esmagará as cabeças na terra de muitos” conquassábit cápita in terra multórum

The Latin leaves open whether 'multorum' belongs to 'capita' (the heads of many, in the land) or to 'terra' (the land of many); 'na terra de muitos' fixes it to the land. The reading is defensible (Douay does the same) and the word order is kept, so this is only a note.

proposedKeep as is, or 'esmagará na terra as cabeças de muitos' only if the other reading is preferred; the current wording mirrors the Latin order and is acceptable.

not taken — The Latin's word order is kept, which leaves *de muitos* where the Latin leaves *multórum*. The v1 Latinist named it as unresolved, and DRB does the same. Moving it (*as cabeças de muitos*) would choose MS1932's reading.

A close, faithful rendering: every clause, tense, person and image of the Latin is kept, including the Septuagintal 'tecum principium', 'in splendoribus sanctorum', 'ex utero ante luciferum genui te' and 'de torrente in via bibet'. Verse by verse

1a

Address follows the speaker (D17, which names this verse): God speaks to one addressee, so *tu* in 109:1, 109:3, 109:4. In 109:2 the psalmist speaks to the king: *tu*, as D43 ruled for the Messiah-king of Ps 44. *Disse o Senhor ao meu Senhor* is word for word the Latin, and the wording of MS1932, DM1962 and Mt 22:44 as Brazilians know it. Both *Senhor* capitalised, as the Latin column's *Dómino*. The speech is introduced with a colon, no quotation marks, as 88:3 and 2:7. The CNBB lectionary's *Palavra do Senhor ao meu Senhor* (verified) is a paraphrase from the Hebrew (*ne'um*) and not the Latin's *Dixit*.

1b

*eu* is supplied before *ponha* so that the first person is heard (*que ponha* could be 'that he put'). *escabelo* was listed unknown by the v1 ambiguity reader, as in 98:5. It is kept: it is the glossary row, and it is the word of this very line in the CNBB lectionary (verified).

3

*está* is supplied: the Latin's first colon has no verb (D2, grammar). *eu te gerei* echoes Ps 2:7 *eu hoje te gerei* (the same *génui te*). The first colon is long, as the Latin's is (about 22 syllables against 23).

4

*não se arrependerá* for the impersonal *non pœnitébit eum*, as MS1932 and DM1962.

6

*Julgará entre as nações* (DRB 'among nations'); *judicáre → julgar* bare. *na terra de muitos* is in the Latin's order and leaves *multórum* where the Latin leaves it (with *terra*, or with *cápita* across the phrase). MS1932 resolves it to 'the heads of many'. The v1 ambiguity reader heard *encherá as ruínas* as unclear, "filling or rebuilding ruins": that is the Latin's own openness (*implébit ruínas*, DRB "he shall fill ruins"), kept; *encherá de ruínas* stays an option.

7

*Beberá da torrente no caminho*: natural order (D2); *torrens → torrente* (glossary).

2

Soft check: second colon 3 syllables short of the Latin (13 against 16), accepted. The Latin colon is long because of *domináre* and *inimicórum*, and the Portuguese says every word of it. *de Sião* is placed after the verb, in natural order.
