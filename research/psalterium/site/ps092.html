2

fails: sayable

“O vosso trono está preparado desde então:”

The first colon is long and front-heavy; the article plus possessive 'O vosso' adds a weak syllable at the start of the breath, and the line drags before it reaches the mediant.

proposedVosso trono está preparado desde então: * vós sois desde sempre.

not taken — article before possessives is rule 5

3a

fails: native

“Elevaram os rios, Senhor:”

When a transitive verb comes first and has no object, a Brazilian ear hears 'os rios' as the object: 'they raised up the rivers'. Only the second colon sorts it out, and by then the listener has already misheard. Putting the subject first keeps the Latin's absolute verb and its repetition without the misreading.

proposedOs rios elevaram, Senhor: * os rios elevaram a sua voz.

❧ taken

3b

fails: native

“Elevaram os rios as suas ondas,”

This parses correctly on its own, but if 3a puts the subject first, the anaphora should stay parallel across the three cola.

proposedOs rios elevaram as suas ondas, * com as vozes das muitas águas.

❧ taken

4b

fails: concrete, native

“as elevações do mar”

'Elevações' sounds like geography or engineering (elevations of terrain), not the heaving of the sea. It turns a physical surge into an abstraction, and it half-echoes 'elevaram' from the verses before, though the Latin roots differ.

proposedMaravilhosas são as vagas do mar: * maravilhoso é o Senhor nas alturas.

kept as an option under elatiónes maris — the fault taken, but os vagalhões chosen: vagas heard as vacancies and blurs with ondas (fluctus)

5

fails: plain, sayable

“Os vossos testemunhos se tornaram muito dignos de fé:”

Clunky and prosaic. 'Se tornaram muito dignos' stacks an auxiliary, a flat 'muito' and a three-word predicate, so the colon sounds like a report rather than praise, and 'O vosso' again adds a weak opening syllable. 'Sobremaneira' carries 'nimis' with more weight and fits the liturgical register.

proposedVossos testemunhos se tornaram sobremaneira dignos de fé: * à vossa casa convém a santidade, Senhor, por longos dias.

kept as an option under credibília facta sunt nimis — sobremaneira unknown to earlier blind readers; article is rule 5

Mostly strong and austere. The opening verse and the doubled 'vestiu-se' have real force, and 'vós sois desde sempre' is exactly the right weight. The weak points are the inverted 'Elevaram os rios', which invites a misparse, the abstract 'elevações do mar', and a limp final verse that loses the psalm's momentum just before the end.

Verse by verse

1a

The same Latin opens Pss 96 and 98 (*Dóminus regnávit*) and the antiphon of Christmas and Epiphany *Dóminus regnávit, decórem índuit* (Psalmi major; Nat1-0 missa, grep): *O Senhor reinou, vestiu-se de esplendor* reads alone. *fortitúdo → força* (glossary). The second colon runs a few syllables under the Latin; accepted rather than padded.

1b

*non commovébitur* has a subject here (the relative, antecedent *orbis*), so the participle *abalado* agrees with *o mundo* (as 20:8 *não será abalado*); D43's pronominal *não se abalará* is for the verb without a named subject. The Christmas Offertory (Nat1-0 missa) quotes 92:1b–2 with *Deus* added: *Deus firmávit orbem terræ … paráta sedes tua, Deus, ex tunc*.

2

*a sǽculo → desde sempre* (glossary row, which names 92:2). The Latin's explicit *tu es* keeps its pronoun: *vós sois*.

3a

*Dómine* as vocative *Senhor* between the verb and the second colon, as the Latin places it.

3b

The inline '(4a)' marker is not reproduced. *fluctus → ondas* (D42).

4b

*mirábilis → maravilhoso* (glossary), twice, as the Latin repeats it; copulas supplied (D2), as 47:2 *Grande é o Senhor*. *in altis → nas alturas* (ἐν ὑψηλοῖς).

5

*testimónia → testemunhos* (D15). *decet → convém* (as 64:2 *A vós convém o hino*). *sanctitúdo → santidade* (the Greek ἁγίασμα can be 'sanctuary', but the Latin chose the abstract noun; DRB 'holiness', MS1932 *santidade*). The vocative *Senhor* stays where the Latin has it; the colon reads alone as the Dedication antiphon.
