1draft

Canticle-level draft. parallels.py handles psalm numbers only, so ps226/show_parallels.py builds consult/parallels/ps226.md from: the DO Latin with a line-to-verse map; the Bolls Vulgate, Douay-Rheims and WLC Hebrew of Deut 32:1–43; the Rahlfs LXX from consult/lxx-*.csv; Matos Soares 1932 from the pdftotext layer of the 1932 PDF, printed pp. 361–365; the Diurnal Monástico 1962 (Saturday Lauds, Deut 32:1–18 only, *tu* form); and DO's Portuguese (no authority). The downloaded third-party texts live in consult/ps226/, which is gitignored. Precedent checked with ps226/concord.py and ps226/gloss.py (copies of ps214's). Brazilian circulation fetched and recorded in ps226/circulation.md. Homograph ban applied once: *ouvi* (32:1), decision `audite`. Hard readings: 32:5 *non fílii ejus in sórdibus*, 32:14 *circumdúxit*, 32:33 *inférni novíssima*, 32:63 *de captivitáte, nudáti inimicórum cápitis*.

2checks

Hard checks pass: ids and marks match DO's Latin (only asterisks in this canticle; no daggers or crosses). render.py and checks.py work unchanged with DO number 226, and readVerses skips the heading line. Four flags were fixed in the draft: 32:39 *Onde estão eles?* → *Onde estão?* (the Latin has no pronoun; it also broke the rhyme *eles … deles*); 32:40b dropped *acaso* for *ne forte* (+9 → +6) and put the verb last (the neighbouring finals rhymed *deles / deles*); 32:49 *Acaso não* → *Não* for *Nonne* (+8 → +5); 32:53b *os que restavam* → *os restantes* and one *que* dropped (+8 → +6). Soft flags accepted, all length: +3/+4 at 32:1b, 2a, 2b, 3a, 9b, 10a, 14a (decision `circumduxit`), 36a (*Consuméntur* is one word), 61a, 65a (*adversários*, decision `hostes`); +5 at 32:49a and 32:60a (*relâmpago*); +6 at 32:40b and 32:53b; shorter at 32:22a, 24a–b, 28a–b, 29b, 31a, 32a, 39b, 42b, 44a, 57a, 58a, where the Latin's long verbs (*provocavérunt*, *concitavérunt*, *iracúndiam*) have short Portuguese forms. Rhymes accepted: 32:43 *mil … dez mil* (the Latin's *mille … míllia*); 32:9/32:10 *dirão / Adão*, one colon apart. 32:47b ends on the proparoxytone *amaríssimos*, as the Latin *amaríssimi*; 32:57a *sou eu só* keeps a stressed final before the asterisk (decision `solus`).

32:2

minor

“flua como o orvalho o que eu digo” fluat ut ros elóquium meum

elóquium is a noun (utterance, speech), parallel to doctrína mea; the relative clause 'o que eu digo' turns it into a paraphrase and breaks the noun-for-noun parallel.

proposedflua como o orvalho a minha palavra.

❧ taken — The stylist asked the same. *A minha palavra*, D26's noun.

32:12

minor

“Jacó, a corda da sua herança” Jacob funículus hereditátis ejus

funículus is a diminutive, the small cord or measuring line by which an inheritance is allotted; 'a corda' loses the diminutive and the image of the measuring cord.

proposedJacó, o cordel da sua herança.

not taken — The glossary's *funículus → corda* (104:11 *a corda da vossa herança*, 77:54b, 138:3): one word for the measuring line across the psalter. *Cordel* in Brazil names chapbook verse (*literatura de cordel*) first.

32:16

minor

“o recolheu” assúmpsit eum

assúmere is to take up; 'recolher' leans toward gathering in or sheltering, a slightly different image.

proposede o tomou,

❧ taken — MS1932 and the CNBB LH also have *o tomou*.

32:49

minor

“nos meus depósitos” in thesáuris meis

thesáurus is a treasury or treasure-store; 'depósitos' flattens the word to a storeroom.

proposede seladas nos meus tesouros?

❧ taken — The stylist asked the same.

A close and faithful rendering of the Clementine Canticle of Moses: the Latin's hard readings (32:5 'et non filii ejus in sordibus', 32:45 'inimici nostri sunt judices', 32:63) are kept rather than smoothed toward the Hebrew, and tenses, moods and persons are preserved throughout. Pointing matches the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex or second mediant). Only four minor lexical points, none affecting sense.

32:2

fails: plain, native

“Condense-se como a chuva a minha doutrina”

'Condense-se' sounds like a physics or weather-report word; it is the one clinical verb in an otherwise pastoral opening.

proposedAdense-se como a chuva a minha doutrina

❧ taken — Same sense, plainer.

“o que eu digo”

Flat relative clause for 'elóquium meum'. It breaks the noun-for-noun parallel with 'a minha doutrina' and echoes 'o que eu falo' from v.1.

proposedflua como o orvalho a minha fala.

❧ taken — A noun, as he asks, but D26's *a minha palavra* (with the Latinist) rather than *fala*, which the psalter does not use for *elóquium*; *a minha fala* is an option.

32:4

fails: plain

“Dai magnificência ao nosso Deus”

'magnificência' is a heavy Latinism in the mouth (six syllables, and it sounds like a cognate calque). The plainer faithful word carries the same sense.

proposedDai grandeza ao nosso Deus.

not taken — The glossary's *magnificéntia → magnificência* (8:2b, 28:4, 144:5 …); *grandeza* is *magnitúdo*'s, and DO's own antiphon for this canticle, *Date magnitúdinem Deo nostro*, uses that other word. *grandeza* stays an option in `magnificentia`.

32:5

fails: cadence

“e não são filhos seus nas imundícies:”

The verse ends on a proparoxytone ('imundícies'). A plural of 'imundície' also sounds odd.

proposede em sua imundície não são filhos seus:

❧ taken — The singular taken; the reordering and the possessive refused (they decide the Latin's loose construction).

32:7

fails: native, sayable

“que te possuiu”

In current Brazilian usage 'possuir' a person has an unwanted sexual or demonic overtone. The hiatus 'po-ssu-iu' also stumbles after 'que te'. 'Possédit' here means acquired, owned as his own.

proposedque te adquiriu, e te fez, e te criou?

❧ taken — The ambiguity reader heard the same.

32:9

fails: plain

“Interroga o teu pai”

'Interroga' sounds like a courtroom. The plain verb is 'pergunta'.

proposedPergunta ao teu pai, e ele te anunciará:

not taken — The glossary's *interrogáre → interrogar* (10:5–6, 136:3a, 138:23); *perguntar* would merge it with *rogáre* / *quǽrere*. The CNBB LH itself pairs *pergunta … interroga* in this verse, so the verb is known here.

32:14

fails: native

“Conduziu-o ao redor”

'Conduzir ao redor' is not idiomatic; the ear expects a place after 'ao redor'.

proposedConduziu-o em volta, e o ensinou:

❧ taken — The same sense, and *em volta* stands without a place.

“como a menina do seu olho”

The Portuguese idiom is fixed as 'a menina dos olhos'. The singular sounds like a mistake, not a choice.

proposede o guardou como a menina dos seus olhos.

not taken — The Latin is singular (*pupíllam óculi sui*), and 16:8 *como a menina do olho* set the singular for the glossary row *pupílla (óculi)*; no reader remarked there.

32:15

fails: native

“que provoca os seus filhotes a voar”

'Provocar' in Brazilian Portuguese means to taunt or annoy. The tender image of the eagle becomes a quarrel.

proposedComo a águia que incita a voar os seus filhotes,

kept as an option under Sicut áquila próvocans ad volándum pullos suos — The ambiguity reader heard "urges them to fly" first, so the tender sense reaches the ear. *Provocar* keeps the canticle's one verb for *provocáre* (32:24, 28, 31, 32), and *incitar* is *concitáre*'s here (32:24, 28). *incita* (the CNBB LH) and *estimula* stay options for Gustavo.

32:19

fails: cadence

“e azeite do rochedo duríssimo,”

The verse ends on a proparoxytone superlative, which drags at the final cadence.

proposede azeite do duríssimo rochedo,

not taken — The Latin ends on the same word (*saxo duríssimo*); the superlative after the noun is the plain order, and fronting it is literary.

32:36

fails: cadence, plain

not taken — The Latin ends *morsu amaríssimo*, as the draft does; the fronted adjective is a literary order. 32:47 *amaríssimos* is the same superlative and keeps the echo.

The canticle mostly reads as sober, rhythmic Portuguese that holds the Latin's concrete images, repetitions and harsh catalogues without softening them. The main weaknesses are a few Latinate or technical words ('condense-se', 'magnificência', 'depósitos', 'interroga') and some unintended colloquial tones ('possuiu', 'provoca', 'o que mama'). There are also a few proparoxytone endings, and moving one word fixes each of them.

32:7

minor

“que te adquiriu” qui possédit te

possidere is 'to possess, hold as one's own'; 'adquiriu' leans on the LXX/Hebrew 'acquired/bought' rather than the Latin verb's own sense. Defensible, but 'possuiu' is closer to the Latin.

proposedque te possuiu, e te fez, e te criou?

kept as an option under qui possédit te — He calls it defensible. Both v1 readers who hear the Portuguese (the stylist and the ambiguity reader) heard *que te possuiu*, with a person as object, as sexual or demonic possession, and that misreading costs more in a prayer than the step from "hold as one's own" to "got as one's own" does. *possuiu* stays option 2 in `possedit`, held for Gustavo.

A close, faithful rendering of all 65 verses: tenses, moods, persons and the Latin's hard readings (32:5 'et non filii ejus in sordibus', 32:45 'inimici nostri sunt judices', 32:63 'nudati inimicorum capitis') are kept, not smoothed toward the Hebrew. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex). Only one minor lexical point, in 32:7.

Verse by verse

32:1

DO's first line, *(Canticum Moysis * Deut 32:1-65)*, is a heading with the book reference. It is not a prayed verse and is not translated (D7; readVerses skips it, as for 233, 210–216, 220, 221). DO's ids 32:1–32:65 are the canticle's own line numbers, not Deuteronomy's verses: the text is Deut 32:1–43 (the map is at the head of consult/parallels/ps226.md). The Latin is Jerome's Deuteronomy, not the Gallican psalter. Address: God is not addressed in this canticle; Moses speaks to the heavens and earth (*vós*, plural), to Israel as one people (*tu*: 32:6–9, 32:27), and God speaks in 32:31–65, turning to the peoples in the plural in 32:56–57 (*vos*, *Vede*); 32:64 *Laudáte, gentes* is *vós*. *Quæ loquor → o que eu falo*, kept apart from *elóquium* (32:2, decision `eloquium`).

32:2

*Doctrína → doutrina*; *plúvia → chuva*; *ros → orvalho*. The order of the Latin (verb, simile, subject) is kept in both colons.

32:3

*Stillæ → gotas*; *herba → erva*; *grámina → relva* (collective singular). The second colon begins a new sentence in the Latin (*Quia*), as in the Portuguese.

32:4

*Dei perfécta sunt ópera*: the genitive fronted in the Latin; the Portuguese fronts *Perfeitas*, keeping the predicate first.

32:5

*Absque ulla iniquitáte → sem iniquidade alguma*. *Peccavérunt ei → Pecaram contra ele*.

32:6

*Prava atque pervérsa → depravada e perversa* (77:8 *generátio prava → geração perversa* had one adjective; here two stand together and need two words). *Hǽccine reddis → É isto que pagas* (*réddere → pagar*). *Stultus → tolo*, *insípiens → insensato* (D43). The people is addressed in the singular: *tu*.

32:7

*Numquid non → Acaso não* (the *numquid* row). *Fecit* has no object in the Latin; the Portuguese needs *te* before each verb.

32:8

*Meménto → Lembra-te*; *cógita → pensa em* (the *cogitáre* row); *generatiónes síngulas → cada uma das gerações*.

32:9

*Intérroga → Interroga* (the open row); *annuntiábit → anunciará*.

32:10

*Dividébat … separábat*: imperfects kept. *Gentes → nações*; *fílios Adam → os filhos de Adão*.

32:11

*Constítuit → Estabeleceu* (the *constitúere* row); *términos → os limites*; *juxta → conforme* (the row).

32:12

*Funículus hereditátis → a corda da sua herança* (104:11). *Pars → parte*.

32:13

*Invénit → Encontrou* (88:21). *Horróris → de horror*; *vastæ solitúdinis → de vasta solidão*.

32:14

*Dócuit → o ensinou* (*docére → ensinar*); *custodívit → o guardou*; *pupíllam óculi sui → a menina do seu olho* (16:8; *sui* kept: his own eye).

32:15

*Pullos → filhotes* (83:4); *vólitans → esvoaça*. The simile's main clause is 32:16.

32:16

*Expándit → Estendeu*; *húmeris → ombros*; *portávit → o levou*.

32:17

*Dux → guia*; *deus aliénus → deus estranho* (lower case: a false god).

32:18

*Constítuit eum → Estabeleceu-o* (32:11's verb); *fructus agrórum → os frutos dos campos*.

32:19

*Petra → rocha* (the row); *saxum → rochedo*, kept apart from *lapis → pedra*.

32:20

*Butýrum → manteiga*; *lac → leite* (118:70); *ádipe → gordura* (80:17); *aríetum → carneiros*; *Basan → Basã*. The list is the object of *comesse* in 32:18.

32:21

*Hircos → bodes* (D44), without the article, as the list's other items. *Meracíssimum → puríssimo* (unmixed); *sánguinem uvæ → o sangue da uva*.

32:22

*Diléctus → o amado* (the Hebrew *Jeshurun*; the Latin translates the name). Order turned so that *o amado* is heard as the subject; *Engordou o amado* could be heard as 'he fattened the beloved'. *Impinguátus → cevado*.

32:23

*Salutári suo → seu Salvador* (the glossary: *salutáris* in apposition, 23:5). *Recessit → se retirou* (43:19).

32:24

*Provocavérunt → Provocaram-no* (decision `provocans`, one verb across the canticle); *ad iracúndiam concitavérunt → o incitaram à ira* (77:40); *in diis aliénis → com deuses estranhos* (*in* instrumental); *abominatiónibus → abominações*.

32:25

*Immolavérunt dæmóniis → Imolaram aos demônios* (105:37); *quos ignorábant → que não conheciam* (canticle 212).

32:26

*Coluérunt → prestaram culto*.

32:27

*Génuit → gerou*; *dereliquísti → Abandonaste*; *oblítus es → te esqueceste*; *creatóris tui → teu criador* (apart from *factor*, decision `factor`).

32:28

*Ad iracúndiam concitátus est → foi incitado à ira* (32:24's phrase).

32:29

*Abscóndam fáciem meam → Esconderei … a minha face*; *considerábo → considerarei* (*consideráre* of things); *novíssima eórum → o fim deles* (72:17).

32:30

*Infidéles → infiéis*.

32:33

*Succénsus est → se acendeu* (77:21 *um fogo se acendeu*); *furor → furor*.

32:34

*Móntium fundaménta → os fundamentos dos montes* (17:8); *combúret → queimará*.

32:35

*Congregábo → Juntarei* (32:7, 40:7, of things).

32:36

*Morsu amaríssimo → com mordida amaríssima*: the superlative kept, as in 32:47.

32:37

*Immíttam → Enviarei* (216 36:2 *enviai*); *bestiárum → feras*; *trahéntium super terram → dos que se arrastam sobre a terra*; *serpéntium → das serpentes*.

32:38

*Pavor → pavor*; *júvenem … vírginem → o jovem … a virgem* (148:12). *Foris … intus → Fora … dentro*: the stylist feared *Fora* heard as "Out!"; the ambiguity reader heard outside and inside, and the Latin fronts the adverb too; kept.

32:39

*Dixi: Ubinam sunt? → Eu disse: Onde estão?* (no pronoun, as the Latin; it also avoids *eles … deles* in one verse); *memóriam → a memória*. *Cessáre fáciam → Farei cessar*.

32:40

*Dístuli → adiei* (77:21 *differre*); *ne forte → para que não* (the conjunction *ne forte*, lest; *acaso* was tried and made the colon nine syllables longer than the Latin); *superbírent → se ensoberbecessem*, at the end of the colon (subject before the verb, so that the colon does not end *deles* after 32:39 *a memória deles*).

32:41

*Manus nostra excélsa* (decision `excelsa`, one word).

32:42

*Absque consílio → sem conselho* (D33); *prudéntia → prudência*; *sáperent → fossem sábios* (the *sápere* row); *intellégerent → entendessem*; *novíssima providérent → previssem o fim*.

32:43

*Persequátur → perseguiria*; *fugent → poriam em fuga*; *mille … decem míllia → mil … dez mil* (90:7).

32:44

*Nonne ídeo, quia → Não foi por isto: porque* (v2). *Ídeo … quia* is a correlative ("for this reason, that"); v1's *por isso, porque* was heard as a false start by the stylist and as a muddle of question and negation by the ambiguity reader. The colon makes *isto* point forward to the *porque*-clause, as *ídeo* does. *Véndidit → vendeu* (43:13); *conclúsit → encerrou* (30:9, 77:50, 77:62).

32:45

*Júdices → juízes*: the Latin's riddle (the enemies themselves are judges of it) kept.

32:46

*Sodomórum* is the city's name (neuter plural); *suburbánis → dos arredores*.

32:47

*Botri → cachos*; *fel → fel*. Ends on the proparoxytone *amaríssimos*, as the Latin *amaríssimi*.

32:48

*Dracónum → de dragões*; *venénum áspidum → veneno de áspides* (13:3, 139:4); *insanábile → incurável*.

32:49

*Nonne → Não* (a question that expects yes, as 32:44 *Não foi*; *acaso* is *numquid*'s, 32:7); *signáta → seladas*; *thesáuris → tesouros* (v2, decision `thesauris`).

32:50

*Últio → vingança*; *retríbuam → retribuirei*; *in témpore → a seu tempo* (the idiom; not a possessive). *Labátur → resvale*.

32:51

*Perditiónis → perdição*; *adésse festínant → se apressam a chegar* (the *festináre* row names this line).

32:52

*Judicábit Dóminus pópulum suum* word for word as 134:14a, and the Portuguese copies it. *In servis suis miserébitur → terá piedade dos seus servos*.

32:53

*Infirmáta sit → enfraqueceu*; *manus* without possessive (D2); *clausi → os encerrados* (*conclúdere*'s family); *defecérunt → desfaleceram*; *resídui → os restantes*; *quoque → também*, and the Portuguese does not repeat the *que* of *quod*; *consúmpti sunt → foram consumidos* (32:36).

32:54

*In quibus habébant fidúciam → em quem tinham confiança* (v2, the stylist: *nos quais* was stiff; the gods are persons to those who trusted them). *Fidúciam → confiança*.

32:55

*Víctimis → vítimas*; *ádipes → as gorduras*; *libáminum → das libações*.

32:56

*Surgant → Levantem-se*; *opituléntur → vos socorram*; *prótegant → vos protejam*; *necessitáte → necessidade*. Plural *vos*: God speaks to the people as many.

32:57

*Vidéte → Vede* (plural, as 32:56).

32:58

*Occídam → matarei*; *vívere fáciam → farei viver*; *percútiam → ferirei*; *sanábo → curarei*; *erúere → arrancar* (the row, with a source: *da minha mão*). The Latin's *ego* is repeated three times and so is *eu*.

32:59

*Levábo → Levantarei*; *in ætérnum → para sempre* (D23).

32:60

*Acúero → afiar*; *fulgur → relâmpago*; *judícium → o juízo* (32:4).

32:61

*Reddam ultiónem → Pagarei a vingança*; *retríbuam → retribuirei*, at the end, as the Latin.

32:62

*Inebriábo → Embriagarei*; *carnes → carnes*.

32:63

The line depends on 32:62 (*devorará carnes … do sangue …*).

32:64

*Laudáte, gentes → Louvai, ó nações*; *ulciscétur → vingará*; *sánguinem servórum suórum → o sangue dos seus servos*.

32:65

*Vindíctam → a vingança* (the glossary: *últio / vindícta → vingança*); *propítius → propício* (98:8).
