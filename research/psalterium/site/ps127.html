“Porque comerás dos trabalhos das tuas mãos” Labóres mánuum tuárum quia manducábis

Latin has a direct accusative object (you shall eat the labours); the partitive 'dos' makes it 'eat of/from the labours', a slight shift toward a portion. Idiomatic, but the direct object is closer.

proposedPorque comerás os trabalhos das tuas mãos

kept as an option under Labóres mánuum tuárum quia manducábis — The partitive is how Portuguese says eating from a store — grammar under D2, Matos Soares 1932's wording; "comer os trabalhos" is heard as eating the labour itself. The Latinist called it idiomatic; his wording is option 1.

minor

1

fails: plain

“os que andam nos seus caminhos”

With a plural subject ('os que'), a native ear hears 'seus' first as 'their own ways', the opposite of the Latin's 'his ways' (ejus = the Lord's). You only get the right sense on reflection, which is too late when you are reciting it.

proposedos que andam nos caminhos dele

kept as an option under in viis ejus — The ambiguity reader heard the Lord's ways first; 118:3 has the same words for the same Latin.

2

fails: native, sayable

“Porque comerás”

Starting on 'Porque' with nothing before it sounds like a clause that has lost its main verb. Spoken Portuguese uses 'pois' at the head of a sentence and keeps the causal link.

proposedPois comerás dos trabalhos das tuas mãos:

kept as an option under quia manducábis — D42 keeps porque; the ambiguity reader heard "because" at once.

“bem-aventurado és, e te irá bem”

The inverted 'bem-aventurado és' is a Latinate order, and 'és, e' makes a hiatus that trips the voice at the comma. The ordinary order is smoother and puts the stress in the right place before the comma.

proposedés bem-aventurado, e te irá bem.

❧ taken

3a

fails: native, sayable

“A tua esposa, como videira abundante,”

Without a verb the line hangs, so the reader has to guess what the tone is doing. This half-verse also opens a new stanza, unlike 3b, which the ear can take as a continuation. A supplied present copula takes nothing from the Latin. Supply it in 3b as well so the two halves stay parallel.

proposedA tua esposa é como videira abundante,

❧ taken

“nos lados da tua casa”

'Nos lados' sounds colloquial and spatial ('lá pelos lados de…'), and 'lados da' runs together. The Latin image is the sides or inner walls of the house, and a nobler equivalent that still means 'sides' keeps it without resolving anything.

proposedaos lados da tua casa

kept as an option under in latéribus domus tuæ — "aos lados" moves the vine beside the house; the Latin has "in", and the ambiguity reader heard "nos lados" as the walls.

3b

fails: native

“Os teus filhos, como rebentos de oliveira,”

This has the same verbless hang as 3a. If 3a gets its copula, this line must match it, or the pair stops being parallel.

proposedOs teus filhos são como rebentos de oliveira,

❧ taken

5

fails: plain, sayable

“e vejas as coisas boas de Jerusalém”

'As coisas boas' is flat, and it adds syllables to what is already the longest colon in the psalm. That colon is hard to say in one breath. 'Os bens' is the plainer and nobler faithful word for 'bona', and it saves three syllables.

proposede vejas os bens de Jerusalém todos os dias da tua vida.

❧ taken

The psalm is sober and prayable, and it follows the Latin closely without sounding stiff; 127:4 and 127:6 have exactly the simple nobility that is wanted. The weak points are small Latinate habits: the bare 'Porque' at the start of a sentence, the inverted 'bem-aventurado és', verbless comparisons that hang, and one ambiguous 'seus'. Fixing these would make it sound written in Portuguese throughout.

worst line127:3a

best line127:6

minor

“Porque comerás dos trabalhos das tuas mãos” Labóres mánuum tuárum quia manducábis

The Latin has a direct accusative object (labores manducabis: 'thou shalt eat the labours'); 'comerás dos' introduces a partitive ('some of the labours') not in the Latin. Defensible idiom, but the direct object is closer.

proposedPorque comerás os trabalhos das tuas mãos

kept as an option under Labóres mánuum tuárum quia manducábis — The partitive is how Portuguese says eating from a store — grammar under D2, Matos Soares 1932's wording; "comer os trabalhos" is heard as eating the labour itself. The Latinist called it idiomatic; his wording is option 1.

A faithful rendering: the Septuagintal 'quia' is kept as 'porque', copulas in verse 3 are legitimately supplied, the subjunctives of verses 5-6 are preserved, and 'novellae olivarum' is rendered concretely. The only point is the partitive 'dos' in verse 2. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse.

Verse by verse

1

No title line: the Latin of DO has none, and D7 keeps the tituli out (as Pss 119–122, 133). 'qui timent Dóminum' → 'os que temem o Senhor' (113:19, 117:4). The second 'qui' is resumed as 'os que' (118:1's build), with no 'e' (Matos Soares 1932 adds one). ámbulant → 'andam' (row: not 'caminhar' beside 'caminho'); 'nos seus caminhos' as 118:3. The Monastic None antiphon and the Vespers antiphon are the first colon's first words, 'Bem-aventurados todos * os que temem o Senhor' reads alone.

2

The singular 'tu' begins here: the psalm turns to one man (rule 3: a singular human addressee is 'tu'). 'Porque' for quia at the head of the clause. manducáre → comer (row).

3a

uxor → 'esposa': 'mulher' is heard first as 'woman'. The article before the possessive (rule 5). 3a and 3b are built alike, as in the Latin. The half-verse is the Gradual of the Nuptial Mass (fetched) and reads alone.

3b

in circúitu → 'ao redor de' (row; the row names this verse). 'mensa' → 'mesa'. The Corpus Christi antiphon is made of this line.

4

homo → 'o homem' (not 'todo homem': 'omnis' is the Nuptial Communion's addition, not the psalm's). 'qui timet Dóminum' → 'que teme o Senhor', as 111:1. No comma before the relative: in Portuguese it would make it non-restrictive.

5

ex Sion → 'de Sião', as 133:3 (the ps133 note asked 127:5 to follow). 'ómnibus diébus vitæ tuæ' → 'todos os dias da tua vida', 22:6's 'todos os dias da minha vida'. The ambiguity reader heard "O Senhor te bendiga de Sião" as "the Lord of Zion" first; the formula is 133:3's and is left as it is (the Latin's ex Sion is as close to Dóminus); "e vejas" can be heard as an imperative — "e que vejas" is option 1 of videas.

6

fílios filiórum → 'os filhos dos teus filhos' (102:17b 'os filhos dos filhos'; the pronoun is the Latin's tuórum). The ambiguity reader heard "a paz sobre Israel" as a second object of "vejas" — the Latin's accusative, as intended.
