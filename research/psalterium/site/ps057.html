minor

“armou o seu arco” inténdit arcum suum

'intendit' is formally present or perfect; the Portuguese preterite commits to the perfect, and the surrounding verbs (devenient, auferentur, conteret) are future, which makes the present the more natural reading of the Latin (LXX has a future here). The Portuguese resolves the tense one way the Latin does not require.

4

fails: sayable, native

“Os pecadores tornaram-se estranhos desde o ventre, extraviaram-se desde o seio materno”

The first colon is far too long for one breath (about 25 syllables), and the two enclitic '-se' verbs set side by side ('tornaram-se ... extraviaram-se') make a clunky, bookish pattern. 'seio materno' adds a syllable-heavy tail the Latin doesn't need. Fronting the two 'desde' phrases gives the parallel a chiastic shape the ear can follow and shortens the colon.

proposedDesde o ventre os pecadores se fizeram estranhos, desde o seio se extraviaram: * falaram mentiras.

kept as an option under a vulva, … ab útero — Order and *se fizeram estranhos* taken; bare *seio* refused — the *úterus* row: bare *seio* is the breast.

5

fails: native, sayable

“O furor deles é à semelhança da serpente”

'é à semelhança' is a stiff calque, and the 'é à' hiatus trips the tongue.

proposedO furor deles é semelhante ao da serpente

❧ taken

“como a da áspide surda”

'como a da' is three weak monosyllables in a row, and the feminine 'a' leaves the listener unsure what it refers back to. 'o da' picks up 'furor', which is what the verse is comparing.

proposedcomo o da áspide surda, e que tapa os seus ouvidos,

❧ taken

10

fails: sayable

“como vivos, assim na ira os engole”

The broken 'como ... assim' rhythm makes the reader stop after 'vivos' and again after 'assim'. It is faithful to 'sicut ... sic', but a supplied 'a' before 'vivos' ties the comparison to the object and smooths the line.

proposedcomo a vivos, assim na ira os engole.

❧ taken

9

fails: plain

“serão tirados”

'tirados' is flat and slightly colloquial next to the image of melting wax. 'levados' is just as faithful to 'auferentur', and it is the plainer and more liturgical word.

proposedComo a cera que escorre, serão levados:

not taken — *auferre → tirar* is settled (D41) and *levar* is addúcere's (D42); already an option.

The psalm reads cleanly and keeps the Latin's hard, strange images (the deaf asp, the lions' molars, the thorns and the bramble) without softening them. The cadences at the marks are consistently good. The weak spots are two over-literal constructions in the first half (4 and 5), which sound more like a translation than like prayer. The closing verses (11–12) are strong, plain and sayable.

Verse by verse

2

Plural humans (the *fílii hóminum*, judges) are *vós* (decided); God is not addressed anywhere in this psalm. *fílii hóminum → filhos dos homens* (row *fílius hóminis*), vocative without *ó* as the Latin has none. *justítiam loquímini → falais justiça*: the content object kept, as the *loqui mendácium* row does. Matins antiphon for this psalm in DO is *Juste judicáte * fílii hóminum* (Psalmi matutinum) — other words from the verse, not a text of the psalm; not rendered here.

3

*Étenim → Pois* (row). *operári iniquitátem → praticar a iniquidade* (row), plural kept. *concinnáre → tramar* (D44; its row names 57:3). *injustítia → injustiça*, apart from *iníquitas* (row) — the two stand in one verse here, as ἀνομίας / ἀδικίαν do.

4

*erráre → extraviar-se* (row, which names 57:4). *peccátor → pecador* (row).

5

*obturáre → tapar*, the plain verb for stopping ears; *aures → ouvidos* (row *inclína aurem*). *et obturántis* keeps its *e*: the asp is deaf *and* stops her ears.

6

*exaudíre → escutar* (D3), future kept (*escutará*). The verse continues the sentence of 57:5 (relative *Que*).

11

*lætári → alegrar-se* (row), in the natural order rather than the mesóclise; *lavar as mãos* may ring as Pilate's idiom to the blind reader — it is the Latin's image; *vindícta → vingança* (the row's plural at 17:48 is that verse's own number); *sanguis* singular here as in the Latin.

12

*homo* generic: *o homem*, as the Latin and DRB ('man shall say'). *os* (the Latin's *eos*) has no near antecedent, as in the Latin — the sinners of 57:10–11.

10

The blind reader found *os vossos espinhos entendessem o espinheiro* incomprehensible, as the Latin and the Greek are; kept (decision `intellegerent`). The subject of *engole* is unnamed, as in the Latin.
