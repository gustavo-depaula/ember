13:4

minor

“entre as nações que não o conhecem” inter gentes, quæ ignórant eum

The Latin clause, set off by a comma, reads most naturally as descriptive: the Gentiles, who do not know him. Without the comma the Portuguese becomes restrictive and implies only some of the nations do not know him.

13:8

minor

“sobre uma nação pecadora” in gentem peccatrícem

The Latin has no article. The indefinite 'uma' leans toward 'some other sinful nation', but in context the nation may be Israel itself. The Latin leaves this open, and the Portuguese should too.

proposedsobre a nação pecadora

kept as an option under osténdit majestátem suam in gentem peccatrícem — Latin gentem without a determiner is indefinite (DRB, MS1932, 42:1 'uma nação'); 'a' decides for Israel, 'uma' leaves it open.

This is a faithful, close rendering of the Tobit canticle. Tense, mood, person and imagery are kept throughout (flagellas/salvas, deducis/reducis, Regem sæculorum, agite dies lætitiæ), and the pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only points worth raising are two small ones: a relative clause and an article.

13:4

fails: sayable, native

“Porque foi por isso que vos dispersou”

The cleft 'foi por isso que' piles five unstressed monosyllables at the start of a colon that is already the longest in the canticle. The reader runs out of breath before 'nações', and with no named subject the 'vos' and the 'o' that follows blur together.

proposedPorque por isso ele vos dispersou entre as nações que não o conhecem,

❧ taken — Taken with 'para isto' rather than the proposed 'por isso', which stutters after 'Porque' and is heard as pointing back (ambiguity); the proposal stays as an option.

“para que vós narreis”

'vós narreis' is a hard sibilant-nasal clash (s-n-r-r-s) and sounds stiff when it is not a vocative. The emphasis of the Latin 'vos' can stay, but it lands better after the verb.

proposedpara que narreis vós as suas maravilhas,

❧ taken — Taken with 'para isto' rather than the proposed 'por isso', which stutters after 'Porque' and is heard as pointing back (ambiguity); the proposal stays as an option.

13:9

fails: plain, sayable

“fazei a justiça diante de Deus”

In current Brazilian usage 'fazer a justiça' suggests carrying out a sentence, as in 'fazer justiça com as próprias mãos'. The plainer word for doing what is right is 'praticar'.

proposede praticai a justiça diante de Deus,

kept as an option under et fácite justítiam coram Deo — 'praticar' is operári's verb (14:2) and 105:3 has 'fazem a justiça'; the article already keeps off 'avenge', and the ambiguity reader heard 'act justly'.

“crendo que ele usará convosco da sua misericórdia”

'convosco' sits between 'usará' and its complement 'da sua misericórdia', which breaks the set phrase 'usar de misericórdia'. The ear trips on 'convosco da'.

fails: sayable

The translation is adequate throughout: every clause of the Latin is carried with its sense, tense, person and number intact, the concrete images (flagellas, ad inferos, manum tuam) are kept, and the pointing marks match the Latin verse by verse (one mediant in each, no flex or second mediant). Verse by verse

13:1

DO's Latin file opens with a heading line, '(Canticum Tobiæ * Tob. 13:1-11)', and DO's Portuguese file has its own heading ('(Cântico de Tobias * Tob. 13:1-11)'). It is DO's label, not a prayed verse and not a titulus of the book (the Clementine's 13:1 begins 'Aperiens autem Tobias senior os suum, benedixit Dominum, et dixit', which DO leaves out); render.py and checks.py skip it, as for the Nunc dimittis (233). Under D7 it is not translated here; if it is wanted for DO, 'Cântico de Tobias' is the plain rendering. DO's verse ids are its own: 13:1–13:11, where the Clementine numbers these lines 13:1–13:10 (DO splits Clementine 13:4 into 13:4 and 13:5) — DO's are kept. in ætérnum → 'para sempre' (D23). The Latin text is Jerome's Vulgate of Tobit, translated from an Aramaic text, not the Gallican psalter; the Greek (LXX 13:1–8) is a different text of the same prayer and not a witness of this Latin's sense. DRB and MS1932 (Tob 13:1b–10 in his numbering) translate this same Latin.

13:2

Quóniam → Porque; 'vós' kept because the Latin has tu. flagelláre → flagelar (the flagéllum row, D39; 72:14 'fui flagelado'): kept apart from castigáre → castigar, which the canticle has four verses on (13:6); MS1932 'castigas' merges them. effúgere (not in the psalter) → 'escapar de', the Brazilian regency; MS1932 'subtrair-se à tua mão', DRB 'escape thy hand'. The first colon is 22 syllables for the Latin's 21.

13:3

Confitémini Dómino → 'Dai graças ao Senhor' (D5). fílii Israël → 'filhos de Israel' (102:7, 148:14b), vocative without 'ó' (the Dóminus row's rule). in conspéctu géntium → 'à vista das nações', exactly 97:2. The Latin order is kept in the second colon (the nations before the verb, the verb last, a chiasm with the first colon); it is natural Portuguese, and it puts the nations where the next verse picks them up. The second colon is 3 syllables short of the Latin's 13 (checks) — accepted: nothing is dropped, Portuguese is simply terser here.

13:4

enarráre → narrar (the open row; 47:14 ut enarrétis → 'para que narreis'); the Latin's own 'vos' is kept, after the verb (decision narretis). mirabília ejus → 'as suas maravilhas' (104:2, 95:3). gentes → 'nações' (twice in 13:3–4, as the Latin repeats géntium / gentes). The verse ends on a comma and runs on into 13:5, as the Latin does.

13:5

faciátis scire eos → 'lhes façais saber' — 'fazer saber' is the Portuguese idiom for 'make know that'; scire here takes a 'that'-clause, which 'conhecer' (the open scire row) cannot, and notum fácere → 'fazer conhecer' is another Latin phrase. eos is masculine (the peoples, ad sensum); 'lhes' shows no gender. quia after scire is 'that' → 'que'. The first colon is 3 syllables short of the Latin's 9 — accepted: 'E façais que eles saibam' (−2) is heavier and adds a subjunctive the Latin does not have.

13:6

castigáre → castigar (the working row castigáre / trádere, 117:18). propter → 'por causa de' (the row; 6:5 'por causa da vossa misericórdia'). iniquitátes nostras → 'as nossas iniquidades' (102:10). The two cola are built alike, as in the Latin.

13:7

Aspícite → 'Olhai' (the aspícere row 'olhar para'; with a clause object Portuguese says 'olhai o que'). ergo → 'pois'. fecit nobíscum → 'fez conosco' (125:3 'fez conosco'); 'ele' supplied as subject after a string of imperatives addressed to the hearers. cum timóre et tremóre → 'com temor e tremor', the pair and its rhyme kept as in 2:11 (the working row). confitémini illi → 'dai-lhe graças' (D5, dative kept). Regem sæculórum → 'o Rei dos séculos': sæcula in the plural genitive, as 144:13a 'de todos os séculos'; not the D23 formula. exaltáre → exaltar (the row). Natural order in the second colon (the Latin fronts Regem with -que); the cola are 6 and 3 syllables short of the Latin's 28 and 18 (checks), accepted: every word is rendered, and 'confitémini' / 'Aspícite' shrink to 'dai graças' / 'Olhai'. The second colon's order also matches DO's antiphon for this canticle, 'Exaltáte Regem sæculórum in opéribus vestris' (Psalmi major.txt 47, 325), so the antiphon can be cut from the verse. The stylist heard a hiatus in 'e exaltai' after the asterisk and proposed 'exaltai também'; refused: 'também' is not in the Latin (the -que is 'e'), and the two e-sounds of 'e exaltai' run together in speech and song without loss.

13:8

Ego autem → 'Eu, porém,' (the row; 58:17, 74:10). captívitas → cativeiro (the row). majéstas → majestade (28:3, 71:19). osténdere → mostrar (4:6, 77:11). peccatrix → 'pecadora', the feminine of peccátor → pecador. confitébor illi → 'lhe darei graças' (D5). The first colon is 3 syllables short of the Latin's 20 (checks), accepted.

13:9

Convertímini → 'Convertei-vos', as 89:3 (D25's exception: men told to turn to God, where 'voltai-vos' is also God's imperative in this psalter). ítaque → 'portanto', kept apart from ergo → 'pois' two verses before. peccatóres vocative, without 'ó'. credéntes → 'crendo' (the participle as a gerund, agreeing with the addressed sinners). In draft 2 the colon is 'crendo que ele usará da sua misericórdia convosco' (decision faciat).

13:10

The Latin's plural verb with a double subject ('I and my soul … we shall rejoice') is kept: 'nos alegraremos'. lætári → alegrar-se (the row). 'nele' before the verb, as the Latin's 'in eo' before lætábimur, and it keeps the unstressed 'nos' from opening the colon after the pause (65:6 'ali nos alegraremos nele'). MS1932 recasts ('eu me regozijarei nele com toda a minha alma'); DRB keeps the double subject.

13:11

Benedícite Dóminum (accusative) → 'Bendizei o Senhor', as 133:1a 'bendizei o Senhor, todos os servos do Senhor'. elécti → 'escolhidos' (the open eléctus row; 104:6, 104:43). ágite dies lætítiæ → 'celebrai dias de alegria' — the diem festum ágere row's verb, 'celebrar' (75:11); lætítia → alegria (104:43 'com alegria'). confitémini illi → 'dai-lhe graças', the same words as 13:7, as the Latin repeats them; the canticle ends as it has run, on thanksgiving (confitéri four times: 13:3, 7, 8, 11). The CNBB LH / Lectionary text has 'seus eleitos' here (circulation.md); 'escolhidos' is kept for the open eléctus row.
