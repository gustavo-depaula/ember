15:7

minor

“ao sopro do vosso furor” in spíritu furóris tui

Spiritus is rendered 'sopro' here but 'espírito' in 15:11 (Flavit spíritus tuus), so the Latin's one word becomes two in Portuguese and the link between the two verses is lost. 'Sopro' is a defensible concrete sense, but it settles for the reader a reading the Latin leaves open.

proposedE no espírito do vosso furor se juntaram as águas:

kept as an option under in spíritu furóris tui — Held. 'espírito do furor' is heard as a temper, not the blast. The ambiguity reader heard 'sopro' as the wind of God's anger. 17:16 is the precedent ('ao sopro do espírito da vossa ira'). The one-word reading stays option 2.

A faithful, close rendering of the Latin throughout: tenses (futures in 15:19, perfect possedisti, jussive subjunctives in 15:17-18) and the Vulgate's own readings (Omnipotens nomen ejus, eques Pharao, habitatores Philisthiim) are kept. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only point worth noting is the inconsistent rendering of spiritus in 15:7 against 15:11.

fails: plain, native

“que operastes”

'Operar' is a Latinism here. In current Brazilian speech it means surgery or running a machine, and the ear trips on it just before the cadence.

15:7

minor

“ao sopro do vosso furor” in spíritu furóris tui

Latin 'spiritus' keeps both 'breath' and 'spirit'; 'sopro' settles it on 'breath', and the canticle uses the same word four verses later ('Flavit spíritus tuus'), where it is rendered 'espírito'. The Latin's one word becomes two Portuguese words. Defensible, but the concordance is lost.

proposedE no espírito do vosso furor se juntaram as águas:

kept as an option under in spíritu furóris tui — Held. 'espírito do furor' is heard as a temper, not the blast. The ambiguity reader heard 'sopro' as the wind of God's anger. 17:16 is the precedent ('ao sopro do espírito da vossa ira'). The one-word reading stays option 2.

15:22

minor

“no meio do mar” in médio ejus

The Latin has a pronoun ('of it'), and the translation spells out what it refers to. The sense is clearly the sea, so no meaning changes, but this names something the Latin leaves unnamed.

proposedno meio dele.

kept as an option under in médio ejus — Held. The v1 ambiguity reader could not hear who owns 'dele'. The sense is the sea (the reader agrees). 'no meio dele' is option 2.

A faithful, close rendering of the Canticle of Moses: the tenses, moods (the jussives in 15:17-18, the futures in 15:9-10 and 15:19), persons and concrete images are all kept, and every verse has the same pointing marks as the Latin (a single * each). The only notes are minor: 'spiritus' is rendered 'sopro' in 15:7 but 'espírito' in 15:11, and the pronoun 'ejus' is spelled out as 'mar' in 15:22. Neither is a major fault.

Verse by verse

15:1

DO's Latin file opens with '(Canticum Moysis * Exod. 15:1-22)'. It is DO's heading for the canticle, not a titulus, and not a prayed verse: under D7 it is not translated (as 214, 233). Were it shown: '(Cântico de Moisés * Ex 15,1-19)'. DO's ids 15:1–15:22 count its own lines, not Exodus verses; kept (rule 4). cantáre → cantar; the cohortative → 'Cantemos'. enim → pois. The Lauds antiphon 'Cantémus Dómino * glorióse' → 'Cantemos ao Senhor * gloriosamente'.

15:2

Copied word for word from Ps 117:14 (the same Latin) and canticle 221's 12:3 (less its 'Porque'). It is also the antiphon of Thursday Lauds II and reads alone.

15:3

Iste → Este é (the copula supplied). glorificáre → glorificar, exaltáre → exaltar (117:28a 'e eu vos exaltarei'). 'eu' supplied twice, as 117:28a, so the futures are heard as vows. pater → pai.

15:4

Pharao → Faraó (134:9, 135:15, no article). exércitus → exército. projícere → lançar (30:23, 49:17, canticle 222 38:21), kept apart from 15:1 dejícere. The responsory V. 'Dóminus quasi vir pugnátor, Omnípotens nomen ejus' is the first colon and reads alone.

15:5

Mare Rubrum → Mar Vermelho (105:7b, 105:9, 135:13). submérgere → submergir (68:3b). abýssus → abismo (D42). operíre → cobrir (77:53, 105:11). profúndum (βυθός) → profundeza (68:3, 68:16), kept apart from abýssus. quasi lapis → como uma pedra, identical at 15:18.

15:6

déxtera → a vossa direita (glossary; the Lectionary's 'braço' is the Hebrew paraphrase's). magnificáta est → se engrandeceu, as 15:1. in fortitúdine → em força (fortitúdo → força). percútere → ferir. in multitúdine → na multidão (5:7b, 68:14). depónere (people) → abater (58:12b row). adversárius → adversário. Only the one '*' the Latin has.

15:7

míttere iram → enviar (the psalter's 'enviar' for míttere; 'Derramai' is effúndere's). devoráre → devorar. stípula → palha (82:14). congregári of waters → juntar-se (32:7 'Ele junta … as águas do mar'); the same at 15:8.

15:8

in médio mari → no meio do mar. congregátæ sunt abýssi → juntaram-se os abismos (as 15:7).

15:9

The enemy speaks in the first person, all futures: 'Perseguirei e prenderei' (pérsequi → perseguir; comprehéndere → prender, the row, twice after pérsequi at 17:38, 70:11). dívidere spólia → repartir os despojos (67:13).

15:10

evagináre → desembainhar (36:14). gládius → espada. interfícere alone → matar (9:29, 100:8).

15:11

operíre → cobrir, as 15:5. plumbum → chumbo. vehemens → impetuoso (D43; 47:7 'vento impetuoso'). The Latin repeats 'submérsi sunt' from 15:5; the Portuguese repeats 'foram submersos'.

15:12

Quis símilis tui → quem é semelhante a vós (34:10, 70:19, 88:9, and 85:8 'semelhante a vós' for 'símilis tui'). in fórtibus → entre os fortes (fortes row; LH the same; the Hebrew/85:8 'gods' is not the Latin). magníficus → magnífico (no row; the adjective of magnificéntia, 8:2b). sanctitas → santidade. terríbilis → terrível (D43). laudábilis → digno de louvor (47:2, 95:4, 144:3). atque → e. fáciens mirabília → que fazeis maravilhas (76:14b, 85:10).

15:13

extendísti manum tuam → estendestes a vossa mão (137:7; LH the same). Dux fuísti → fostes o guia (79:10 'Fostes guia'); v2 puts 'in misericórdia tua' first so 'guia' meets 'do povo' (the stylist: draft 1's parenthesis broke the construction). redímere → resgatar (73:2b 'Resgatastes', 76:15).

15:14

portáre → levar (90:12 'Nas mãos te levarão'). 'vós' supplied as subject (checks −4 without it). in fortitúdine tua → na vossa força. habitáculum → morada (D44); the adjective after, as the Latin ('habitáculum sanctum'). ad → 'à'.

15:15

ascéndere → subir (121:4). irásci → irar-se. dolor → dor (47:7).

15:16

Tunc → Então. conturbáre → perturbar (47:6, 63:9). Edom, Moab (82:8, 136:7a), Canaã (104:11, 134:11). tremor → tremor (47:6). habitatóres → habitantes.

15:17

See 'formido'.

15:18

Fiant immóbiles → Fiquem imóveis. donec → até que. pertransíre → passar (104:13 row). pópulus tuus iste → este vosso povo. possidére → possuir (73:2 'que possuístes'). The Latin's repeated 'donec pertránseat pópulus tuus' is repeated.

15:19

mons hereditátis tuæ → o monte da vossa herança (heréditas → herança).

15:20

Sanctuárium → santuário (72:17, 73:7). firmáre → firmar (92:1b, 135:6). manus tuæ → as vossas mãos. regnábit → reinará (9:37).
