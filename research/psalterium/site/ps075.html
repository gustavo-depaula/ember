5

fails: sayable, plain

“Vós iluminais maravilhosamente desde os montes eternos”

The first colon is long (about 18 syllables), and the six-syllable 'maravilhosamente' sits in the middle and takes all the breath. The ear loses 'iluminais' before it gets to the mountains.

proposedVós iluminais admiravelmente dos montes eternos

❧ taken — 'dos' taken; 'admiravelmente' refused and kept as an option in decision illuminans — admirável is admirábilis's, maravilhosamente is mirabíliter at 44:5b.

6

fails: sayable, native

“e todos os homens de riquezas nada encontraram nas suas mãos”

The long subject gets in before the verb, so the colon sags in the middle. 'nas suas mãos' is also a little heavy. Following the Latin order (nihil invenerunt ... in manibus suis) puts the verb up front and reads more naturally.

proposede nada encontraram em suas mãos * todos os homens de riquezas — or, keeping the mark where it is and changing only the order after it: e nada encontraram todos os homens de riquezas em suas mãos

kept as an option under omnes viri divitiárum — With the verb first, 'os homens de riquezas nas suas mãos' is heard as men with riches in their hands.

7

fails: native

“À vossa repreensão”

The crasis 'À' first sounds like a dative ('to your rebuke'). 'Ab' here means 'before' or 'at', and the ear has to work to hear it that way.

proposedAnte a vossa repreensão, Deus de Jacó, * dormitaram os que montavam cavalos.

kept as an option under Ab increpatióne tua — Formula row (17:16b); the ambiguity reader heard the cause first. If taken, all four places change.

11

fails: native, sayable

“vos celebrará um dia de festa”

With the clitic 'vos' right after the verb, it sounds as if the remnant 'celebrates you as a feast day'. The second colon is also long and ends heavily.

proposede o resto do pensamento celebrará para vós um dia de festa.

❧ taken

12

fails: cadence

“trazeis dádivas”

The verse ends on a proparoxytone, 'dá-di-vas', so the final cadence falls onto a weak syllable. The order 'ao redor dele trazeis' is also a little stiff.

proposedtodos vós que ao seu redor trazeis ofertas.

kept as an option under affértis múnera — 'ao seu redor' taken (order). 'ofertas' refused: oblátio's family (D41); the múnera row stands, the proparoxytone is the Latin's own (múnera).

13

fails: cadence, native

“o espírito dos príncipes, *”

The mediant falls on the proparoxytone 'príncipes', and 'espírito' just before it is proparoxytone too, so the half-verse ends on two dactyls in a row.

proposedAo terrível, e ao que tira aos príncipes o espírito — this still ends proparoxytone. If a paroxytone at the mark is required: Ao terrível, e ao que aos príncipes tira o alento (only if 'alento' is accepted for spiritus; otherwise keep 'príncipes' and accept the accommodation).

kept as an option under qui aufert spíritum príncipum — spíritus → espírito; the proparoxytones are the Latin's (spíritum príncipum); he accepted the accommodation himself.

“ao terrível para os reis da terra”

'para os reis' makes 'apud' sound like 'terrible for the kings' (as in 'in their opinion') instead of 'among' or 'before' them.

proposedao terrível diante dos reis da terra.

❧ taken

The psalm is mostly plain, sober and sayable, with a good martial concreteness in vv. 4 and 9. It weakens where it follows the Latin syntax too closely (vv. 6, 11) and in two proparoxytone cadences at the close (dádivas, príncipes), which blunt the ending of the psalm.

Verse by verse

2

The Latin is verbless in both cola; the copula 'é' is supplied twice (grammar, D2). Natural order in the first colon (Deus first); the Latin's order kept in the second, which ends on 'nome' as the Latin ends on nomen ejus. 75:2b alone is a Psalter antiphon (In Israël * magnum nomen ejus) and reads alone. Notus → conhecido (the notus row).

3

'Em paz': the Latin translates the Hebrew place name Salem as peace; the Latin's sense is kept, and the Holy Saturday use (In pace factus est locus ejus) depends on it. Habitátio → habitação as at 68:26 (morada is habitáculum's, D44). The second colon is verbless, borrowing the first colon's verb, as the Latin.

4

Confríngere → quebrar (row). No subject is supplied (God is the subject from 75:2–3), as MS1932. 'E a guerra' kept: the Latin breaks war itself along with the weapons.

5

Second colon: turbáre → perturbar (row); turbáti sunt as the pronominal 'perturbaram-se', as the finished psalms render (con)turbáti sunt (45:4, 47:6, 63:9) — 'foram perturbados' (the compound passive the Latinist asked for at 118:60) is four syllables longer; insípiens → insensato (row), with 'de coração' as reto de coração. First colon ~19 syllables to the Latin's 17 (accepted: the adverb is long in both languages).

6

Dormíre somnum: the cognate object kept (a repetition the Latin has). Inveníre → encontrar, as in every finished psalm (9:36, 36:10, 45:2, 118:143, 118:162).

7

The formula row Ab increpatióne tua → 'À vossa repreensão' (17:16b; decision increpatio), with the vocative Deus Jacob → 'Deus de Jacó' as 45:8.

8

Terríbilis → terrível (settled, D43). Resístere + dative → 'vos resistirá' (16:8a has resistem à).

9

Tremere → tremer; the trepidáre row also has tremer, but the two never share a verse. The verse ends with a comma, as the Latin, running into 75:10. 75:9–10 is the Easter Offertory (with resúrgeret) and a Holy Thursday antiphon (dum exsúrgeret in judício Deus).

10

Cum + subjunctive → 'Quando … se levantou' (exsúrgere → levantar-se, row). In judícium → 'para o juízo', echoing 75:9. Salvos fácere → salvar (row). Mansuétus → manso (row).

11

Cogitátio → pensamento (row), twice, as the Latin repeats it. Quóniam → Porque. The verse is obscure in Latin and Greek; nothing is explained.

12

Plural human audience → vós; 'todos vós que' names the subject so that the plural is not heard as said to God (the Ps 10:2 remedy). In circúitu ejus → 'ao seu redor' (the row's ao redor; the possessive form taken from the stylist in draft 2), placed before the verb so it is not heard as 'bring gifts around him'. Final 'dádivas' is a proparoxytone, as múnera is (accepted, as the row did at 44:13).

13

The datives continue 75:12 (to whom the gifts are brought); the repetition terríbili … terríbili kept. Auferre → tirar (settled, D41). Spíritus → espírito (the Latin's word; 'breath' and 'pride' are readings). 'E ao que tira' rather than 'e àquele que tira' (two syllables lighter). Final 'príncipes' at the mediant is a proparoxytone, as príncipum.
