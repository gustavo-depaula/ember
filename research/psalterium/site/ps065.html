3

fails: sayable, cadence, no rhyme

“na multidão do vosso poder, os vossos inimigos vos mentirão.”

'os vossos inimigos vos' piles up three sibilant v-syllables before the verb; the colon ends on the oxytone 'mentirão', which chimes with 'multidão' at the head of the same colon. Putting the subject last gives a paroxytone close and breaks the -ão echo.

proposedna multidão do vosso poder, vos mentirão os vossos inimigos.

❧ taken — also the Latin's order

5

fails: native

“terrível nos desígnios sobre os filhos dos homens.”

The bare adjective after the mark hangs without anchor in Portuguese; the ear doesn't know whom 'terrível' qualifies (the works? God?). A supplied copula and subject, allowed here, make it sound like prayer and not like a gloss.

proposedele é terrível nos desígnios sobre os filhos dos homens.

kept as an option under terríbilis in consíliis super fílios hóminum — the Latin is verbless and the singular can only be God

fails: native

“Ele pôs a minha alma na vida:”

'pôs ... na vida' sounds like 'put into life' as a place; 'ad vitam' is direction/purpose, and 'para a vida' is both more literal and more natural.

proposedEle pôs a minha alma para a vida:

fails: native, sayable

“Fizestes-nos entrar no laço,”

Opening a verse with an enclitic on a second-person-plural preterite ('Fizestes-nos') is stiff and a little heavy on the tongue; it is also inconsistent with 65:12, which has 'nos fizestes'. A named subject allows natural proclisis.

proposedVós nos fizestes entrar no laço,

❧ taken — 63:3 precedent (*Vós me protegestes*)

fails: native

“E que a minha boca falou,”

'falar' does not take 'votos' as a direct object comfortably ('votos que a boca falou'); the ear stumbles on the relative. A verb that takes an utterance as object keeps the chain with 65:13.

proposedE que a minha boca proferiu,

❧ taken — answered by the Latinist's *E falou a minha boca*, which drops the relative

15

fails: sayable

“Eu vos oferecerei holocaustos cheios de tutano, com incenso de carneiros:”

Very long first colon, and 'Eu vos oferecerei' is a tongue-twister of -ere- syllables; the explicit 'Eu' adds a syllable the verb doesn't need. Dropping it (in both halves, keeping the repetition) lightens the breath. 'tutano' should stay (concrete, faithful), though it sits on the homely edge.

proposedVos oferecerei holocaustos cheios de tutano, com incenso de carneiros: * vos oferecerei bois com bodes.

not taken — a colon opening on a clitic; the mesóclise is the option

16

fails: native, sayable

“Vinde, escutai, e eu narrarei, todos vós que temeis a Deus: * quanto ele fez pela minha alma.”

The vocative wedged after 'eu narrarei' breaks the verb from its object across the mark, and bare 'quanto ele fez' sounds truncated in Brazilian Portuguese ('o quanto' is the native form). Put the vocative with the imperatives and let 'narrarei' lead into the second half.

proposedVinde, escutai, todos vós que temeis a Deus, e eu narrarei: * o quanto ele fez pela minha alma.

❧ taken — order yields (D2); *o quanto* refused — *narrarei quanto ele fez* is standard

18

fails: native, plain

“Se eu contemplei a iniquidade no meu coração,”

'contemplei' carries a strong positive, mystical connotation in Portuguese devotional speech (contemplação); the ear hears 'contemplate iniquity' as a jarring collision. 'aspexi' is simply to look upon; the plainer verb keeps the image without the false resonance.

proposedSe eu olhei para a iniquidade no meu coração,

❧ taken — the ambiguity reader heard the same

The psalm moves well and keeps the Latin's concreteness (laço, costas, tutano, sob a minha língua) without explaining it; the imperatives at the opening ring cleanly and the repeated 'entoe'/'oferecerei'/'escutou' structures are honoured. The rough spots are a few calqued word orders (65:3, 65:16), one bare predicate (65:5), and a verb choice in 65:18 whose Portuguese connotation works against the sense.

Verse by verse

1

The psalm starts at 65:1 with the titulus dropped (D7; DO's 65:1 has *Jubiláte* already). *jubiláre → aclamar* (D43; the row names 65:1). *omnis terra* as vocative: *toda a terra*. 65:1 is the Introit of the third Sunday after Easter and an Offertory (Epi2-0), grep.

3

*Dícite Deo: Quam …* — the quoted speech turns to God (*vós*) while the frame speaks of him. *Quam terribília* → *Como são terríveis* (8:2a *como é admirável*); *terríbilis → terrível* (D43; the row names 65:3). The second colon is lowercase after the *!*, as the Latin.

4

*Omnis terra adóret te* — the subjunctive of wish (*adore*, *entoe*). Each colon reads alone (the Epiphany versicle). Both cola +3 on the Latin: the D25 cost (*entoar salmos* for *psállere*); accepted.

5

*Veníte, et vidéte ópera* — *Vinde, e vede as obras* word for word 45:9 (*ópera Dómini* there, *Dei* here).

6

*in flúmine pertransíbunt pede* → *pelo rio passarão a pé* (*in* of passage; MS1932 *pelo rio*). The Latin's futures kept, the subject 'they' left unnamed as the Latin leaves it. *ibi lætábimur in ipso* → *ali nos alegraremos nele*.

7

*in virtúte sua* → *com o seu poder* (instrumental here; 20:2 *no vosso poder* is where the king rejoices). *in ætérnum → para sempre* (D23). *non exalténtur in semetípsis* → *não se exaltem em si mesmos* (the jussive; *exaltar* row).

8

*audítam fácite vocem* → *fazei ouvir a voz* (the *vocem dare* row, 17:14 *fez ouvir a sua voz*). The Latin ends the verse on a comma, carried into 65:9; so does the Portuguese.

10

Two verbs kept two, as the *examináre / probáre* row asks for this very verse: *provastes … examinastes … se examina*; *no fogo* as 16:3, 11:7, 17:31.

11

*in dorso nostro* → *nas nossas costas* (17:41 *dar as costas*; *dorso* is an animal's). *posuísti … imposuísti* → *pusestes … pusestes* (*impónere super → pôr sobre*, D44): the Latin's two verbs share their root, so the near-repetition is the Latin's; the Greek has two verbs (ἔθου, ἐπεβίβασας 'made to ride').

12

*per ignem et aquam* → *pelo fogo e pela água* (the preposition repeated, as Portuguese needs).

13

*in holocáustis* → *com holocaustos* (DRB, MS1932). *holocáustum → holocausto* (row, which names 65:13, 65:15).

15

*boves cum hircis* → *bois com bodes* (the *hircus* row asks 65:15 to copy 49:9, 13). The Latin's object-first order is not kept in the first colon: the verb first is natural, and the two colons then open alike, as they do in meaning. The first colon is still +3 on the Latin (the future *oferecerei* and *cheios de tutano* for two Latin words); accepted — the marrow is the Latin's image.

16

*omnes, qui timétis Deum* → *todos vós que temeis a Deus* (21:24 *Vós que temeis o Senhor*); *e eu narrarei* names the speaker as the call turns to his own story. The Offertory (Pasc3/Epi) uses 65:16 with *narrábo vobis* and *fecit Dóminus*; the psalm has neither. The second colon +3: *ele* names the subject so that *fez* is not heard as the speaker's; kept.

17

*Ad ipsum ore meo clamávi* → *A ele clamei com a minha boca* — the Latin's order kept at the head (*A ele*), the colon ending on *boca*, which answers *língua* at the end of the verse, as *ore … lingua* do.

19

*Proptérea → Por isso* (17:50). *atténdit voci deprecatiónis meæ* → *atendeu à voz da minha prece* (D3 *atender*; D35 *prece*; 27:6).

20

*amovére → afastar* (row). *et misericórdiam suam a me* → *nem a sua misericórdia de mim*: *et* under a negation is *nem* (grammar); *a me* at the end, where the Latin has it, so that it governs both nouns as in the Latin.
