45:18

minor

“que criou os céus ... que formou a terra e a fez” creans cælos ... formans terram, et fáciens eam

The Latin has present participles (creating, forming, making); the translation puts them in the past tense. Defensible as timeless creation, but the present is closer.

proposedo Senhor, que cria os céus, * o próprio Deus que forma a terra e a faz, ele próprio a moldou:

❧ taken — The present is as timeless as the participle; the perfects of the same acts follow in 45:19, as in the Latin.

“ele próprio a moldou” ipse plastes ejus

'plastes' is a noun (its moulder, its fashioner); the translation turns it into a finite verb, which also puts a fourth past-tense act into the list.

proposedele próprio, o seu modelador:

❧ taken — The apposition is the Latin's build, and with the present tense the verb would have to be "a molda", a fourth act in the list. v1's verb stays option 2.

45:24

minor

“desde o início, e desde então o predisse” ab inítio, ex tunc prædíxit illud

The Latin is asyndetic; the translation adds 'e'. Slight, but it joins what the Latin leaves as two parallel questions.

proposeddesde o início, desde então o predisse?

❧ taken — The two phrases (ab inítio, ex tunc) stand side by side in the Latin; the asyndeton is sayable.

A faithful translation: sense, tense, voice, person and number are kept throughout, the Latin's order and ambiguities (e.g. the placement of 'frustra' in 45:21) are preserved, and every verse carries the same single mediant as the Latin. The only faults are small: the present participles and the noun 'plastes' in 45:18 are rendered as past-tense finite verbs, and 45:24 adds a conjunction. 'Semen' as 'descendência' (45:21, 45:30) is standard and not flagged.

“Foram envergonhados, e coraram todos: * juntos se foram para a vergonha”

'Foram' (passive auxiliary) and 'se foram' (went away) chime within one verse; the ear hears a pun on two different verbs, and 'coraram todos' with the subject trailing sounds translated.

❧ taken — "partiram" keeps the going-away of ab-íre without the chime. The reordering of "todos" is refused: "coraram todos" is good Portuguese and keeps omnes where the Latin puts it.

45:25

fails: sayable

“Deus justo, e que salve, não há senão eu.”

The commas chop the colon into three gasps, and 'não há senão eu' ends abruptly after the smoother 'além de mim' of the first colon.

proposedDeus justo e que salve não há fora de mim.

❧ taken — The commas are dropped. "fora de mim" is refused (option 2): it would lose "senão" for præter (17:32) and can be heard as "beside myself".

45:29

fails: sayable, native

“Portanto, no Senhor, dirá, estão as minhas justiças e o domínio:”

The parenthetical 'dirá' wedged between commas is almost impossible to chant: four short breaks before the verb arrives. Moving the speech verb to the front keeps every word and gives one clean breath.

❧ taken — Order yields to the ear (D2); every word kept, the speaker still unnamed; DRB does the same.

fails: sayable

The comma before 'e louvada' forces a pause right before the mediant, so the voice stops twice in three words.

proposedNo Senhor será justificada e louvada * toda a descendência de Israel.

❧ taken — Punctuation only.

45:20

minor

“Não falei às escondidas” Non in abscóndito locútus sum

'in abscondito' is a local phrase ('in a hidden place'), paired with 'in loco terræ tenebroso'; 'às escondidas' turns it into manner (secretly), weakening the parallel of place.

proposedNão falei em lugar escondido,

kept as an option under Non in abscóndito locútus sum — With a verb of speaking, the adverbial sense is DRB's and MS1932's; 9:30a has the same phrase adverbially; the place is named in the next colon, and "em lugar escondido, num lugar tenebroso" doubles "lugar". His form is option 2.

45:23

minor

“Nada souberam” Nesciérunt

'Nescierunt' is simply 'they did not know / were ignorant'; 'Nada souberam' adds an intensifying 'nothing'.

proposedNão souberam

kept as an option under Nesciérunt — Held for Gustavo: the v1 stylist asked for "Nada" because bare "Não souberam" sounds unfinished in Portuguese; the v2 Latinist asks it back. The Latin's absolute verb has no object in either; "Não souberam" (81:5) is option 2.

“o seu lenho esculpido” lignum sculptúræ suæ

The genitive 'the wood of their carving' is turned into an adjective; defensible, but the possessive in the Latin belongs to the carving, not the wood.

proposedo lenho da sua escultura

kept as an option under levant lignum sculptúræ suæ — He calls it defensible. "escultura" is heard as art (the sculptília row), and his form is +2 syllables on a colon already long; option 3.

A close and faithful rendering: tenses, voices and persons track the Latin throughout, the Latin's own readings (fabricatores errorum, plastes, justitiae plural, dicet) are kept, and the pointing marks match verse for verse. Only two minor points: the local sense of 'in abscondito' in 45:20 and a slight intensification plus adjectivized genitive in 45:23.

Verse by verse

45:15

DO's file opens with the heading line '(Canticum Isaiæ * Isa 45:15-30)'; it is not a verse (readVerses skips it) and is not translated (D7, as ps233, ps211). If a Portuguese file is made for DO, the heading would read '(Cântico de Isaías * Isa 45:15-30)' — DO's range is its own line numbering; the text is Isa 45:15–25 of the Vulgate. 'Deus abscónditus' → 'um Deus escondido': the article supplied (DRB 'a hidden God'; MS1932, DM1962 'um Deus escondido'), the plain adjective as every Brazilian text has it. 'Salvátor' → 'Salvador' (the salvátor row, 24:5), capitalised as a title.

45:16

confúndi → 'ser envergonhado' (D15), erubéscere → 'corar' (the working row; 6:11, 82:18): 'Foram envergonhados, e coraram'. The Latin's order, subject last ('todos'), is also good Portuguese. The subject is not named in the Latin; not supplied (DM1962 'os seus adversários' follows the LXX). The stylist asked for 'Todos foram envergonhados': not taken — 'coraram todos' is good Portuguese and keeps omnes where the Latin puts it.

45:17

salvátus … salúte: the figura (salvo … salvação) kept; no article before 'salvação eterna', as the Latin (v2, stylist: v1's 'uma' limped). non confundémini, et non erubescétis → the same pair of verbs as 45:16. Addressed to Israel, plural (vós).

45:18

hæc dicit Dóminus → 'isto diz o Senhor' (the Brazilian Lectionary opens the same verse 'Isto diz o Senhor'; 11:6a 'diz o Senhor'). ipse Deus → 'o próprio Deus', and the second ipse 'ele próprio' (decision plastes), so the repetition stands. cælos plural → 'os céus'. formáre → 'formar', fácere → 'fazer' (94:5, 138:5), in the present with creans (decision creans). Both cola end on an oxytone (céus, modelador), which the brief allows. v2: colon 2 is +3 on the Latin, the cost of keeping plastes a noun with its possessive (decision plastes); accepted.

45:19

The Latin sets two clauses back to back without a conjunction (non in vanum creávit eam, ut habitarétur, formávit eam); the natural order puts the purpose after 'formou-a' (D2, order). in vanum → 'em vão' (the working row). Ego Dóminus, et non est álius → 'Eu sou o Senhor, e não há outro', identical at 45:26b ('eu sou Deus, e não há outro'), and kept apart from ultra (45:25, 'mais') and præter.

45:20

in abscóndito → 'às escondidas', as 9:30a (the same Latin phrase, adverbial); it keeps the root of 'escondido' (45:15): the hidden God did not speak in hiding. The glossary's 'no lugar escondido' (26:5, 30:21) is for a hidden place; here the second colon names the place. tenebrósus → 'tenebroso' as 17:12 ('água tenebrosa') and 87:7; a blind reader once listed 'tenebrosos' as unknown — 'escuro' is the plainer word if a reader stumbles.

45:22

congregári → 'reunir-se' (D43); 'Reuni-vos' carries the enclitic, so D13 allows it though 'reuni' is also 'I gathered'. accédite → 'aproximai-vos' (33:6), simul → 'juntos'. The polysyndeton (et … et) kept. qui salváti estis → 'vós que fostes salvos': the subject named. ex géntibus → 'dentre as nações' (gentes → nações).

45:23

rogáre → 'rogar' (121:6). deum lowercase, as the Latin prints it: a god that does not save. 'não salva' against 'Salvador' / 'salvos': the Latin's word-family runs through the canticle (salvátor, salvátus, salus, salváti, salvántem, salvans, salvi) and is kept in one Portuguese family. Colon 1 is +4 syllables on the Latin (v2, with 'Nada'): 'os que levantam' for qui levant, and the genitive sculptúræ made an adjective (decision lignum). Accepted.

45:24

annuntiáre → 'anunciar' (D41); 'Anunciai' ≠ 'anunciei'. Colon 1 is −3 syllables on the Latin, colon 2 +2. Colon 1's −3 is the Latin's long consiliámini against 'tomai conselho'. The stylist asked for 'aconselhai-vos': not taken (decision consiliamini; the ambiguity reader heard 'tomai conselho juntos' as 'deliberate', the sense wanted). The two 'desde' are the Latin's two parallel phrases (ab inítio, ex tunc).

45:25

numquid → 'acaso' (the open row, 7:12; the Lectionary has 'Acaso não sou eu o Senhor?'). 'Deus justo e que salve não há senão eu' (v2: the commas dropped, stylist; one breath, one claim): the subjunctive in a negated relative; the participle salvans kept apart from Salvátor.

45:26

omnes fines terræ → 'todos os confins da terra' (21:28, 66:7b, 94:4). quia → 'porque'.

45:27

juráre in → 'jurar por' (62:12); in memetípso → 'por mim mesmo'. The comma after jurávi is made a colon: what follows is the oath's content (punctuation is grammar). justítiæ verbum → 'a palavra de justiça' (verbum → palavra); the article supplied. Colon 1 is −3 on the Latin (memetípso, egrediétur are long words). Colon 2 ends on an oxytone, 'voltará'.

45:28
