“para que vos lembreis dele […] para que o visiteis” quod memor es ejus […] quóniam vísitas eum

O latim afirma a lembrança e a visita no presente do indicativo. A tradução emprega o subjuntivo em construções com «para que», alterando o modo e tornando menos explícito o caráter factual dessas afirmações.

proposedQue é o homem, pois vos lembrais dele? * ou o filho do homem, pois o visitais?

kept as an option under quod memor es ejus? … quóniam vísitas eum? — Held, for the reasons in the decision: (1) ‘que é X, para que …?’ is the Portuguese idiom for this question of wonder and presupposes the fact — nothing of the Latin’s assertion is lost on a hearer, and neither the stylist nor the blind reader stopped at the verse; (2) mood and conjunction are grammar, where D2 lets the ear lead, and D24 ruled the same way at 118:92 against the same reader; (3) Matos Soares 1932, D2’s outer bound, has the same build (‘para te lembrares dele’); (4) his own fixes show the cost of the letter: bare ‘que vos lembrais dele’ is heard in Brazil as a relative clause (‘the man you remember’), and a ‘pois’ clause hung on a question breaks it in two. Both of his wordings are selectable (options 2 and 3 of the decision).

A tradução conserva o conteúdo latino nos demais versículos. Todas as marcas de divisão são as mesmas e aparecem na mesma ordem que no latim.

Verse by verse

2a

The psalm begins at 8:2a (8:1 is the titulus; D7). 8:2a = 8:10 word for word: both verses are built from the same two slots (decisions domine, quam), so they cannot differ. ‘in univérsa terra’ → ‘em toda a terra’: univérsa and omnis are one Greek word (ἐν πάσῃ τῇ γῇ), and ‘toda a terra’ will serve omnis terra too (95:9 has univérsa terra, 95:1 omnis terra).

2b

DO sets the mediant before ‘super cælos’, a second colon of four syllables; kept so. ‘super’ → ‘sobre’ here and in 8:6 (‘sobre as obras’). ‘Porque’ for Quóniam, as in 8:4.

3

The Latin’s first colon is twenty-five syllables and the Portuguese longer still; it is the psalm’s one heavy line. propter → ‘por causa de’ (glossary). Mt 21:16 quotes the first half. The stylist: even with his verb the first colon ‘concentra a dificuldade de respiração’ — it does in the Latin too.

4

ópera → ‘obras’ (glossary), plural as the Latin; fundáre → fundar (eleven verses: fundásti terram → ‘fundastes a terra’); ‘tu’ is expressed in the Latin, so ‘vós’ is expressed.

5

‘fílius hóminis’ → ‘o filho do homem’, lowercase, as DO prints it: the psalm speaks of man, and Hebrews 2 reads it of Christ; the wording leaves both. The Latin’s question marks kept. Heard: the blind reader took ‘o filho do homem’ first for Jesus, ‘pela familiaridade da expressão no ambiente da igreja’, and so 8:6 as said of him — the reading of Hebrews 2, which the Latin’s own phrase invites. The verse stands against the Latinist’s major on the mood (decision quod).

6

The flex kept; the inline marker (7) is not reproduced. ‘glória et honóre coronásti eum’ → ‘de glória e de honra o coroastes’ (Matos Soares 1932 word for word; coronáre → coroar, glossary Ps 5:13b). constitúere → estabelecer (glossary). ‘Ángelis’ → ‘anjos’, lowercase.

8

‘Ómnia’ → ‘Tudo’. 8:8–9 run on as one list under ‘sujeitastes’, as in the Latin.

9

vólucres → ‘aves’ (103:12 vólucres cæli). The stylist: ‘a repetição de «mar» corresponde à repetição latina e sustenta a oração’. The blind reader heard ‘que percorrem’ of the fishes alone — the Latin’s masculine ‘qui’. ‘Veredas’ (settled, D15) was again listed as unknown.
