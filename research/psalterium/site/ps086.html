5

minor

“Nasceu nela um homem e outro homem” Homo, et homo natus est in ea

'outro' is an interpretive addition: the Latin (and LXX anthropos kai anthropos) simply repeats 'homo'. The repetition is the Latin's idiom and can be kept bare in Portuguese; 'um homem e outro' leans toward resolving it as 'this one and that one'. Defensible, but closer is possible.

proposedAcaso Sião dirá: Homem e homem nasceu nela: * e o próprio Altíssimo a fundou?

kept as an option under Homo, et homo natus est in ea — Bare 'homem e homem' as a subject lacks the determiner Portuguese grammar needs; 'um … outro' is grammar under D2, not an interpretation of the idiom; kept as option 2.

A close, literal rendering that keeps the Latin's Septuagintal oddities (Babylon 'scientium me', the unusual 'sicut laetantium omnium habitatio', 'in scripturis populorum') rather than smoothing them toward the Hebrew, and keeps 'Numquid' as an open question. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex or second mediant). Only 86:5 has a minor point, the added 'outro' in 'homo et homo'.

Verse by verse

1

mons sanctus → monte santo (row, adjective after); tabernáculum → tenda (row); Jacob → Jacó; Sion → Sião. The pointing has one mark; the second colon is long, as the Latin's is.

3

The city is addressed as tu (rule 3); 86:7 'em ti' agrees. Checks: first colon +3 syllables, accepted (see decision dicta).

4

Rahab → Raab and Babylon → Babilônia (MS1932's forms, the Ps 82 names row's rule). Who speaks is left unsaid, as the Latin leaves it. Checks: first colon +3 syllables with 'Eu me lembrarei' (+2 with the mesoclisis); accepted — the two names and their prepositions cannot be shortened.

4b

The polysyndeton 'e … e' kept; 'Eis' for ecce (row). v2: 'estes ali estiveram' (the stylist's order, keeping ali for illic as 13:5): 'estes estiveram' ran es-tes es-ti- together before the cadence.

5

The echo fundaménta (86:1) / fundávit kept in 'fundamentos' / 'fundou'. The quotation mark is the colon, as the Latin prints it; where the quotation ends is left to the Latin's punctuation. The ambiguity reader heard 'Acaso Sião dirá' as a question expecting 'no', which inverts the sense of the verses around it — the known cost of the numquid row (7:12), sharper here; the Latin's numquid has the same formal lean. Held; 'Acaso não dirá Sião' (DRB, MS1932) is option 2 — for Gustavo. He also heard 'um homem e outro homem' as two particular men: the Latin's image, as DRB's 'this man and that man'; kept.

6

narráre → narrar (row). pópuli → povos (row). Checks: the mediant closes on the proparoxytone 'príncipes', as the Latin's 'príncipum' does; accepted rather than reorder the pair.

7

lætári → alegrar-se (row). Checks: first colon +3 syllables, the price of 'os que se alegram' for one participle; accepted.
