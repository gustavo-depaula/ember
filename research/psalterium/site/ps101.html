3

fails: native, sayable

“em qualquer dia em que estou atribulado, inclinai para mim o vosso ouvido”

After 'qualquer dia em que' a native ear expects the future subjunctive, as the next verse does ('em que vos invocar'). The present indicative 'estou' sounds like a translation and breaks the parallel with 3b. The colon is also long, so the lighter verb form helps the breath.

proposedem qualquer dia em que eu estiver atribulado, inclinai para mim o vosso ouvido

❧ taken

fails: plain

“os meus ossos se colaram à minha carne”

'Colar-se' calls up glue and craft paper, so it is too homely for this image. 'Aderir' is the plain noble verb and keeps adhaesit exactly.

11

fails: sayable, native

“porque, elevando-me, me derrubastes”

The gerund with an enclitic, a comma, and then a proclitic 'me' right away ('-me, me') trips the tongue at the most dramatic point of the verse. Two finite verbs keep elevans/allisisti (lifted up, then dashed down) and flow in one breath.

proposedporque me erguestes e me derrubastes

❧ taken

fails: sayable, native

“Vós, levantando-vos, tereis piedade de Sião”

'Vós, levantando-vos' stumbles over the vós/vos echo, and the gerund between commas is stiff and bookish. A finite verb keeps the emphatic 'Vós' and the rising movement.

proposedVós vos levantareis e tereis piedade de Sião

fails: native

“Anunciai-me o pequeno número dos meus dias”

'O pequeno número' is a calque of paucitatem and sounds like an arithmetic report. Native prayer says how few the days are, which keeps the concrete sense of fewness without turning it into an abstraction like 'brevidade'.

“No início”

'No início' sounds like everyday prose. 'No princípio' is the plainer liturgical word and carries the echo of creation that the verse is about.

fails: native

'Dirigida' sounds administrative, like a company being managed or a car being driven. 'Guiada' keeps the sense of being directed and sounds like prayer.

The lament half reads well: it is concrete, austere and sayable, and the fumaça, gravetos, pardal and cinza-como-pão images land cleanly. The problems cluster in the stiff gerunds (vv. 11, 14) and in a few calques that sound translated (pequeno número, reunirem juntos, dirigida). The closing verses (27–28) have real grandeur and a good chanting cadence.

6

minor

“os meus ossos se colaram à minha carne” adhǽsit os meum carni meæ

Latin has a singular noun and verb (os meum ... adhaesit, 'my bone clave'); the translation makes it plural. It is a collective sense, so the meaning barely changes, but the number is changed.

proposedo meu osso se colou à minha carne

kept as an option under adhǽsit os meum carni meæ — the singular is heard as one particular bone; number is grammar (D2, D29)

19

minor

“para outra geração” in generatióne áltera

The Latin has in + ablative ('in another generation'); 'para' reads it as purpose/destination, following the LXX accusative (eis genean heteran) rather than Jerome's ablative. It is defensible, but 'em' is closer to the Latin.

proposedSejam escritas estas coisas em outra geração

kept as an option under in generatióne áltera — em is heard as the time of the writing; the Greek εἰς, DRB 'unto'

This is a faithful, close rendering that keeps the Septuagintal images (the pelican of the wilderness, the night raven, 'elevans allisisti me', 'paucitatem dierum meorum nuntia mihi', 'et terrae ejus miserebuntur') without smoothing them. There are only two small closeness points, one of number (v. 6) and one of preposition (v. 19). The pointing marks match the Latin throughout: one mediant in each verse, no flex.

Verse by verse

2

The formula *Senhor, escutai a minha oração* (D3); the response of the versicle — see decision `veniat`.

3

*Não desvieis de mim a vossa face* = 26:9, 12:1 (the *avértere* row); *inclinai para mim o vosso ouvido* is the formula of 16:6, 30:3, 70:2.

3b

= 55:10's first colon (*Em qualquer dia em que vos invocar*); *escutai-me depressa* = 68:18 (the *velóciter* row). The future subjunctive for *invocávero*.

4

*desfaleceram como a fumaça* agrees with 36:20 (the *defícere* row named this verse); *fumus → fumaça* (row). Natural order, subject first.

5

*percútere → ferir* (row). *oblítus sum comédere* with the infinitive: *esquecer-se de* (the row's genitive form).

7

*factus sum* twice here and once in 101:8: *tornei-me* all three times.

8

*passer → pardal* (row, 101:8 named); *in tecto* → *no telhado* (the roof; *teto* is a ceiling in Brazil).

9

*tota die → o dia todo* (D24); *exprobráre → afrontar* (row, 41:11 *afrontaram-me*). The Latin's order kept. v2: the second colon in the stylist's order (*e juravam contra mim os que me louvavam*), breaking the jingle *louvavam juravam*; order only (D2).

10

*manducáre → comer* (row); *fletus → pranto* (row). *eu* named: *comia* is also 'he ate'.

12

*declinavérunt* of a shadow → *declinaram*: the shadow lengthens at evening (ἐκλίθησαν); the *declináre a* row names this verse as another build.

13

*Tu autem, Dómine* → *Vós, porém, Senhor* (3:4, 40:11); the three *tu autem* of the psalm (13, 27, 28) are all *vós, porém*. *pérmanes → permaneceis* (row), present as the Latin, in 13 and 27 alike. The second colon borrows the verb of the first, as the Latin does; formula *de geração em geração*.

14

*exsúrgens* → *vos levantareis e* (v2, the stylist: *Vós, levantando-vos* stumbles on *vós / vos*; the participle made a finite verb, grammar, D2; the row's *levantar-se*) (row *exsúrgere*); *miseréri → ter piedade* three times in 14–15, as the Latin's one verb.

15

*placére + dative → agradar a* (D44).

16

*gentes → nações* (row). The Latin has *nomen tuum* against the Greek's *κυρίου*; the Latin followed. The rhyme flagged with 101:17 (*glória … glória*) is the Latin's own echo.

17

*ædificávit* is perfect in the Latin (the Greek is future); the perfect kept. *edificar* as 50:20, 68:36, 88:3.

18

*respícere in → olhar para* (row); *prex → prece* (row); *eórum → deles* so that the prayer is not heard as God's.

19

*creábitur* → *que será criado*; the Latin's commas around the relative dropped (Portuguese punctuates a restrictive clause without them).

22

*annuntiáre → anunciar* (D41).

23

*In conveniéndo* (gerund) → *Quando … se reunirem*; *convenire in unum → reunir-se juntos* (2:2, 47:5; the *in unum* row names 101:23). *servíre* is not imperative here, so D18 does not reach it.

26

*ópera mánuum tuárum* → *obras das vossas mãos* (18:2, 91:5), plural as the Latin.

27

*vestiméntum → veste* (D43; the row asked for 101:27 to be checked beside *opertórium*, see decision `opertorium`). *períre → perecer* (row).

29

*habitábunt* absolute, as the Latin (MS1932's gloss *tranquilos em Jerusalém* is his).
