A faithful, close rendering: senses, tenses, moods and persons track the Latin throughout, the Septuagintal readings (e.g. 'Deus osténdet mihi super inimícos meos', 'ad manducándum', 'exsecratióne et mendácio annuntiabúntur') are kept rather than smoothed toward the Hebrew, and every verse carries the same pointing marks in the same order as the Latin. Only two small voice/paraphrase points are worth tightening.

5

minor

“sem iniquidade corri, e segui reto” sine iniquitáte cucúrri, et diréxi

'dirigere' is transitive-active ('I directed', i.e. set my course straight); 'segui reto' turns it into a motion verb of the subject. Defensible, but it could stay closer to the Latin verb.

proposedsem iniquidade corri, e dirigi os meus passos

kept as an option under sine iniquitáte cucúrri, et diréxi — his fix supplies an object the Latin lacks (DRB, MS1932 do); he calls the draft defensible

13b

minor

“e não serão mais” et non erunt

'mais' adds a 'no longer' that the Latin does not have; the Latin says simply 'and they will not be'.

proposede não existirão

kept as an option under et non erunt — e não existirão rhymes with the mediant consumação (rule 5); DRB also says 'no more'

17

minor

“de manhã exultarei na vossa misericórdia” exsultábo mane misericórdiam tuam

In the Latin, 'misericórdiam tuam' is the direct object of 'exsultabo' (I will joyfully sing your mercy), in parallel with 'cantabo fortitudinem tuam'. 'exultarei na' makes the mercy the ground of the joy, not what is sung, and loses the parallel.

proposede de manhã celebrarei com júbilo a vossa misericórdia

kept as an option under et exsultábo mane misericórdiam tuam — D44: exultar takes no object; held there against him twice

17b

minor

“Porque vos fizestes o meu amparo” Quia factus es suscéptor meus

'factus es' is passive/deponent in sense ('you have become'); the reflexive 'vos fizestes' reads as an active 'you made yourself', a slight shift of voice.

proposedPorque vos tornastes o meu amparo

kept as an option under Quia factus es suscéptor meus — fazer-se is Portuguese for 'become' and is the formula already in 60:4, 117:21, 117:28b

This is a faithful, literal translation that keeps the Latin's Septuagintal readings (ostendet mihi super inimicos, ad manducandum, dominabitur ... finium terrae, misericordia mea) and does not bring in the Hebrew. It has no critical or major faults, only four minor points about closeness: direxi, non erunt, the transitive exsultabo, and factus es. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse.

Verse by verse

2

The Passiontide versicle and the Alleluia of Pent09-0: *Arrancai-me dos meus inimigos, meu Deus. R. E livrai-me dos que se levantam contra mim.* *insúrgere in → levantar-se contra* (row; 17:49 has the same colon end). *liberáre → livrar* (row).

3

*vir sánguinum → homem de sangue* (D29, the plural lost). *salvum fácere / salváre → salvar* (row).

4

*fortes → os fortes* (row, 53:5). *irrúere in* 'rush upon' → *lançar-se sobre*.

6

*Exsúrge → Levantai-vos* (row); *in occúrsum meum → ao meu encontro* (MS1932). *Dómine, Deus virtútum → Senhor, Deus dos poderes* (D43). The verse runs on into 58:6b, as the Latin's does.

6b

*non misereáris ómnibus* → *não tenhais piedade de todos* keeps the Latin's scope as it stands (it can be heard as 'not of all'; so can the Latin); DRB *have no mercy on all them*. *operári iniquitátem → praticar a iniquidade* (row).

7

Identical to 58:15 (both follow decision `famem`). *convértere → voltar* (row); *ad vésperam → à tarde* (MS1932); *circuíre* with an object → *rodear* (row, 117:10). The finals *iniquidade* (58:6b) / *cidade* (58:7) rhyme (checks): the Latin has the same echo, *iniquitátem* / *civitátem*; accepted.

8

*loquéntur in ore suo → falarão com a sua boca* (DRB, MS1932). The Latin's second member has no verb; *há* supplied (grammar), since *e uma espada nos seus lábios* after *falarão com* is heard as a second instrument.

9

*Et tu, Dómine* repeats 58:6's *et tu, Dómine*: *E vós, Senhor* both times. *ad níhilum dedúcere → reduzir a nada* (row, which names this verse).

10

*quia, Deus, suscéptor meus es* recurs word for word in 58:18 and is identical there: *porque vós, Deus, sois o meu amparo* (*suscéptor → amparo*, D19). v2: the vocative moved before the verb, as the Latin has it, after the stylist heard *sois, Deus, o* stutter in both verses; his *ó* not taken (the psalter's bare vocative, D44). *vós* supplied so that *Deus* is heard as a vocative.

12

The verse number jumps from 10 to 12: DO's ids are kept. The person switch (*Deus osténdet … ne occídas*) is the Latin's.

12b

*in virtúte tua → no vosso poder* (the virtus row, as 20:2, 53:3). *protéctor → protetor* (row); *Senhor, meu protetor* in the natural order of address.

13

*comprehéndere → prender*: *e sejam presos na sua soberba* is the row's own wording for this verse. *eórum … ipsórum* both *seus*.

13b

*annuntiáre → anunciar* (D41) in the passive. *et non erunt* is decision `nonerunt` (v2).

14

*dominári → dominar* (row; 21:29 *dominará as nações*). *fines terræ → os confins da terra* (row, 47:11). *Jacob → Jacó*.

16

*dispérgere* of people, passive → *serão dispersos* (dissipáre / dispérgere rows; 34:16). *manducáre → comer* (row).

17

The Alleluia of 03-28 (with 58:17b as its verse). *exsultáre + accusative → exultar em* (D44). *mane → de manhã* (row).

17b

*factus es → vos fizestes* (60:4, 117:21). *refúgium → refúgio*, *tribulátio → tribulação* (rows).

18

The respond of a responsory at Epi2-3 (*R. Adjútor meus, tibi psallam … * Deus meus, misericórdia mea*). *adjútor → auxílio* (row, uniform), *psállere → entoar salmos* (D25). The first colon runs long because *entoar salmos* is two words for one (D25's cost).
