Verse by verse

2

The psalm begins at 10:2 (10:1 is the titulus; D7). In Dómino confído → ‘No Senhor confio’: confídere in → confiar em (glossary), in the Latin’s order, which is also natural Portuguese (Matos Soares 1932 has the same words). The first colon is the psalm’s confession; the rest of the verse is the advice of others (plural: dizeis), addressed to the soul (singular: tu). Heard: the blind reader took ‘dizeis’ first as said to the Lord — a cost of vós (decision quomodo); draft 2 names the subject.

3

‘Porque eis que’ for Quóniam ecce: quóniam → porque (as 8:2b, 8:4), ecce → eis (glossary). sagítta → seta (glossary); pháretra → ‘aljava’, only here in the psalter (grep) — the one word for a quiver. 63:4 has the same frame (intendérunt arcum … ut sagíttent in occúltis immaculátum) and should copy the wording of this verse. The blind reader listed ‘aljava’ as unknown; there is no plainer word for a quiver, and the image is the Latin’s; kept.

4

The Latin’s question mark kept.

5a

‘templo santo’ as in Ps 5:8b (‘o vosso templo santo’); 17:7b has ‘de templo sancto suo’ too. The first colon supplies ‘está’ (D2).

5b

pálpebræ → ‘pálpebras’: the concrete image kept (rule 5); the only other place is 131:4. fílii hóminum → ‘filhos dos homens’ (glossary). The mediant word ‘pobre’ is a paroxytone.

6

qui autem → ‘mas quem’; dilígere → amar, odísse → odiar (glossary). iníquitas → iniquidade.

7

The list keeps the Latin’s repeated ‘et’ (fogo e enxofre e …): rule 2. Draft 1 had commas before each ‘e’ (fogo, e enxofre, e …); the stylist heard them as small stops that break a colon already long, and the two ‘e’ carry the weight — taken (punctuation only). The second colon is the psalm’s heaviest (+4 on the Latin’s 20 syllables), as it is in the Latin.

8

justus Dóminus → ‘o Senhor é justo’ (copula supplied; D2). Heard: ‘as justiças’ was understood first as ‘os atos justos’ — the test the decision asked for; the plural stands. ‘o seu rosto’ was heard as the Lord’s.
