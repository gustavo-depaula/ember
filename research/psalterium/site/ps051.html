4

fails: native

“fizeste engano”

The bare noun with no article sounds like a calque. A Brazilian ear expects an article or a fuller verb. Coming after the comma, the colon ends flat and odd.

proposedcomo navalha afiada, fizeste o engano.

❧ taken — Taken as a fault, with another fix: *fizeste o engano* is still heard as 'made the mistake'; *tramaste o engano*. His wording is an option.

“a tua língua pensou em injustiça”

'pensou em' is weak and everyday for cogitavit. It sounds like 'thought about' rather than 'plotted'.

proposedO dia todo a tua língua maquinou a injustiça:

kept as an option under injustítiam cogitávit lingua tua — cogitáre → *pensar em* is the row; the blind reader heard 'plotted' first. *maquinou* stays option 2.

5

fails: native, no rhyme

“mais do que falar a equidade”

'falar a equidade' is not Portuguese usage: you do not 'falar' an abstract noun. The mouth stops on it.

proposeda iniquidade mais do que falar com equidade.

kept as an option under iniquitátem magis quam loqui æquitátem — Manner for content; the loqui + accusative row and 16:9b (a Latinist major when content became manner).

“benignidade: * ... equidade”

The -idade rhyme at the mediant and the final, with 'iniquidade' in between, gives three -dade endings in one verse. It chimes, though the Latin (-tatem) chimes too. If the rhyme stays, at least make the final phrase idiomatic.

proposedAmaste a malícia mais que a bondade: * a iniquidade mais do que falar com equidade.

kept as an option under iniquitátem magis quam loqui æquitátem — Manner for content; the loqui + accusative row and 16:9b (a Latinist major when content became manner).

fails: plain, sayable

“te fará emigrar da tua tenda”

'emigrar' is bureaucratic and modern (passports, emigration). It is the one word in the psalm that needs a footnote in tone.

proposedte arrancará, e te fará sair da tua tenda:

❧ taken — Taken as a fault (the blind reader heard voluntary migration too), fixed with *te desalojará*, which keeps the forced removal from a dwelling; *te fará sair* is an option.

“e a tua raiz da terra dos vivos”

The elliptical final colon runs 'raiz da terra' together, as if the root belonged to the earth. A comma restores the Latin's ellipsis.

proposede a tua raiz, da terra dos vivos.

❧ taken

9

fails: native, plain

“esperou na multidão das suas riquezas”

'multidão' is said of people. Of riches it sounds foreign. 'abundância' keeps the sense of the Latin plainly.

proposedMas esperou na abundância das suas riquezas:

not taken — The multitúdo row keeps one word; *abundância* is abundántia's; 48:7 has this phrase word for word.

“prevaleceu na sua vaidade”

'prevaleceu' needs an object or a rival in Portuguese. On its own it sounds legal.

proposede se fez forte na sua vaidade.

kept as an option under et præváluit in vanitáte sua — The Greek's ἐδυναμώθη, not the Latin's *præváluit* (rule 1).

fails: sayable

“Eu, porém, como uma oliveira frutífera na casa de Deus,”

At about 20 syllables this is a heavy first colon for one breath. The article 'uma' adds a beat and nothing else.

proposedEu, porém, como oliveira frutífera na casa de Deus,

11

fails: plain

“porque é bom à vista dos vossos santos”

'à vista de' leans commercial ('pagamento à vista'). With 'porque' already used in the first half, the final colon feels flat.

proposedpois é bom diante dos vossos santos.

kept as an option under quóniam bonum est in conspéctu sanctórum tuórum — The conspéctus row keeps its noun (*à vista*) in predicate places; *porque* is quóniam's throughout. *diante* is option 3.

The psalm is faithful and mostly speakable, and its polysyndeton and vós address carry well. The indictment in vv. 4–7 is where it sounds like a translation: 'fizeste engano', 'falar a equidade', 'emigrar' and 'multidão das riquezas' are Latin shapes pressed onto Portuguese. The closing verses of trust (10–11) are calm and chantable once the first colon of v. 10 is lightened.

minor

“a tua língua pensou em injustiça” injustítiam cogitávit lingua tua

cogitare with a direct object means to devise or plot. 'Pensou em' (thought about) makes the object a topic and weakens the verb to mere thinking, where the Latin says the tongue plotted injustice.

proposedO dia todo a tua língua planejou a injustiça

kept as an option under injustítiam cogitávit lingua tua — The cogitáre row (*pensar em*, 20:12, 34:4b with an evil object); the second reader to say so, so a split of the row is proposed for the coordinator rather than made in one verse.

minor

6

minor

“palavras de ruína” verba præcipitatiónis

praecipitatio is a casting headlong, a hurling down (LXX katapontismos, drowning). 'Ruína' is the traditional rendering (Douay 'ruin') and defensible, but it softens the image of throwing down into a general abstraction.

proposedAmaste todas as palavras de precipício

kept as an option under ómnia verba præcipitatiónis — *precipício* is a cliff, a place, not the act of hurling down; the reader calls *ruína* defensible; DRB and MS1932 have it.

A faithful and close rendering. Sense, tense, person and number hold throughout, 'quia fecisti' and the ambiguous 'bonum est' are handled well, and the pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only faults are minor: a weakened verb in v. 4, an abstraction for 'adjutor' in v. 8, and a softened 'praecipitationis' in v. 6.

Verse by verse

3

*Quid* → *Por que* (τί). *qui potens es in iniquitáte* → *tu que és poderoso na iniquidade*: the subject named, as the Latin's *es* has it; *in iniquitáte* left open between 'mighty in doing iniquity' (MS1932's gloss) and 'mighty by it'. potens → *poderoso* (23:8). The psalm speaks to one man: *tu* (rule 3). The Greek joins *ὅλην τὴν ἡμέραν* to this verse; the Latin puts *Tota die* in 51:4, and is followed.

4

*Tota die* → *o dia todo* (D24). *lingua tua* → *a tua língua*, subject after the time phrase in natural order (D2). *sicut novácula acúta* left open between the tongue and the man as what is like a razor, as DRB and the Latin leave it.

5

*Dilexísti* → *Amaste* (dilígere → amar), twice at the head of 51:5 and 51:6 as in the Latin. See `benignitatem` for the two comparatives and the kept rhyme.

7

*Proptérea* → *Por isso* (17:50, 44:3, 44:8). destrúere → *destruir* (row; not an imperative). *te* repeated with each verb, as the Latin's *te … te … te*. The verse is long (one colon of 22 Latin syllables after the asterisk) and has no flex in the Latin, so none here.

8

*Vidébunt justi, et timébunt* → *Os justos verão, e temerão*, the build of 39:4b *Muitos verão, e temerão*. *super eum ridébunt* → *rirão dele* (the irridére row's *rir de*; γελάσονται, the simple verb). The speech runs on into 51:9, so the verse ends on a colon, as the Latin does; no quotation marks, as elsewhere in this psalter.

9

*in multitúdine divitiárum suárum* → *na multidão das suas riquezas*, word for word 48:7 (multitúdo row). *sperávit in* → *esperou em* (D36). The stylist (v1) asked for *abundância*; refused — the multitúdo row keeps one word (5:7b/5:11b set them against each other), *abundância* is abundántia's, and 48:7 has this phrase word for word.

10

*Ego autem* → *Eu, porém,* (29:7, 30:7b, 30:15, 39:18). *olíva fructífera* → *como oliveira frutífera* (κατάκαρπος); draft 1 had *uma*, dropped in v2 after the stylist (the colon is long, the article adds a beat and nothing else). *in domo Dei* → *na casa de Deus* (domus row). *in ætérnum: et in sǽculum sǽculi* → *para sempre: e pelos séculos dos séculos* (the settled formula, D23, D27). The verse is the Alleluia verse of a Mass (missa/Sancti/10-09, from *Sicut olíva*), and Ambrose quotes it (horas Commune/C1); the colons read alone.

11

*Confitébor tibi in sǽculum* → *Eu vos darei graças para sempre* (D5; 29:13, 43:9). exspectáre → *aguardar* (D24/D39), kept apart from *esperei* of 51:10 as the Latin keeps sperávi / exspectábo apart. *quóniam* → *porque*.
