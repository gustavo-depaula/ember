3checks

3:61

minor

“bendizei, todos os ventos de Deus, ao Senhor” benedícite, omnes spíritus Dei, Dómino

Latin 'spiritus' carries both 'winds/breaths' and 'spirits'. The weather setting favours 'winds', but 'ventos' settles an ambiguity the Latin leaves open, and it hides that the Latin uses the same word again in 3:73 ('spíritus, et ánimæ justórum' → 'espíritos'). 'Sopros' keeps the physical image and stays closer to the word's double sense.

proposedbendizei, todos os sopros de Deus, ao Senhor

kept as an option under omnes spíritus Dei — Kept. The reader itself calls it defensible and asks for no change. *sopro* is the psalter's word for wind and breath at once (102:16). *espíritos* would be heard as angels, and *ventos* is the other option.

The translation is faithful, verse by verse. It keeps the Latin's repeated 'æstus' in 3:62, the jussive subjunctives in 3:66 and 3:71, the hortatives in 3:75, and the plurals throughout. The pointing matches the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex). The only point worth raising is the minor one at 3:61, where 'spiritus Dei' is narrowed to 'ventos'.

3:57

fails: cadence

“louvai-o e exaltai-o sumamente pelos séculos”

The refrain ends on the proparoxytone 'séculos', so the final stress falls three syllables from the end. It also returns in 3:66, 3:71, 3:74, 3:75 and 3:56. It matches the Latin 'sǽcula' and is the traditional formula, so the tone can absorb it. But 'sumamente pelos séculos' drags in the mouth because two unstressed syllables follow the stress at each end of the phrase: 'suMAmente', 'SÉculos'.

proposedlouvai-o e exaltai-o sumamente para sempre

not taken — D43 settles *in sǽcula* → *pelos séculos*. The proparoxytone is the Latin's *sǽcula*, and the stylist granted that the tone can absorb it.

fails: concrete

'spíritus Dei' leaves open whether it means winds or spirits (compare 3:73 'spíritus'). 'ventos' settles that question, and it also breaks the echo with 'espíritos' in 3:73 that the Latin keeps.

3:69

fails: sayable

“Bendizei, baleias, e tudo o que se move nas águas, ao Senhor”

This is the longest first colon in the canticle. The comma after 'baleias' makes the voice stop for a moment and then start a second, longer clause before 'ao Senhor'. It is hard to say in one breath in choir.

proposedBendizei, baleias e tudo o que se move nas águas, ao Senhor

❧ taken

3:73

fails: sayable

“espíritos, e almas dos justos”

The comma before 'e' follows the Latin punctuation. Spoken aloud, it separates 'espíritos' from its genitive and turns it into a bare vocative, so the mouth stops where the sense runs on.

proposedBendizei, espíritos e almas dos justos, ao Senhor

❧ taken — Also taken at *santos, e humildes* for the same reason. The Clementine has no comma in either place.

3:75

fails: native

“louvemo-lo e exaltemo-lo sumamente”

Two enclisis forms in a row with the '-mo-lo' contraction sound bookish in current Brazilian speech, and 'exaltemo-lo' is awkward to say. The line sounds like grammar-school Portuguese, not prayer.

proposeda ele louvemos e exaltemos sumamente pelos séculos

❧ taken

The canticle sounds right: a steady, litany-like 'Bendizei ..., ao Senhor' whose oxytone 'Senhor' gives a clean cadence at every mark, in plain, concrete nouns. The weak spots are the refrain 'exaltai-o sumamente pelos séculos', which is heavy with its proparoxytone close, the bookish enclitics in the doxology verse, and a few commas copied from the Latin that break the spoken flow.

3:61

minor

“todos os sopros de Deus” omnes spíritus Dei

Latin spiritus keeps open 'winds' and 'spirits'; 'sopros' settles it on the physical breath/wind reading, although the surrounding list of weather makes that the natural sense. In 3:73 the same word is rendered 'espíritos', so the reader loses the chance to see that the Latin uses one word in both places. Defensible, since 'espíritos' here would read as angels only.

proposedKeep 'sopros', or 'todos os ventos de Deus' if a more concrete image is wanted; no change strictly needed.

kept as an option under omnes spíritus Dei — Kept. The reader itself calls it defensible and asks for no change. *sopro* is the psalter's word for wind and breath at once (102:16). *espíritos* would be heard as angels, and *ventos* is the other option.

3:64

minor

“gelos e neves” glácies et nives

'gelos' also renders gelu in 3:63 ('gelo e frio'), so the Latin's two distinct words (gelu, frost/cold; glacies, ice) become one Portuguese word. Plural 'gelos' is defensible as glacies can be read plural beside nives.

proposedBendizei, gelos e neves, ao Senhor — acceptable; optionally 'bendizei, gelo e frio' in 3:63 could become 'bendizei, geada e frio' only if pruína were rendered differently, so leaving as is is reasonable.

kept as an option under rores et pruína … gelu et frigus … glácies et nives — Kept, as the reader advises. *Pruína* already has *geada* (77:47, 118:83), and Portuguese has no fourth plain frost word. The number keeps the two apart, as the CNBB and DM1962 do (*Geada e frio … Gelos e neves*). *regelo … gelo* stays an option.

A faithful, close rendering of the canticle: every vocative, the repeated æstus in 3:62, the jussive subjunctives in 3:66 and 3:71, the hortatives of 3:75 and the rubric are all kept, and no content is added or dropped. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (a single mediant each). The only points are lexical: 'sopros' for spiritus in 3:61, and gelu/glacies collapsing into 'gelo(s)' across 3:63-64, both minor.

Verse by verse

3:57

*ópera* → *obras* (the *narráre / ópera* row). *Dómini, Dómino* → *do Senhor, ao Senhor*: the doubling is the Latin's. *in sǽcula* → *pelos séculos* (D43, settled); the proparoxytone at the final is the Latin's own (*sǽcula*). The refrain *louvai-o e exaltai-o sumamente pelos séculos* is identical in 3:57 and 3:74, and in the third person (3:66, 3:71) and first person plural (3:75) only the verb's person changes. DO's heading line *(Canticum Trium Puerorum * Dan 3:57-88,56)* is not a verse (see the audit).

3:58

ángelus → anjo (lowercase, glossary).

3:59

*aquæ omnes, quæ super cælos sunt* is 148:4 word for word → *todas as águas acima dos céus* (copied, rule 6). *omnes virtútes Dómini* → *todos os poderes do Senhor*, as 148:2 *omnes virtútes ejus* → *todos os seus poderes* and *Dóminus virtútum* → *o Senhor dos poderes* (D43).

3:60

*sol et luna* as 148:3. *stellæ cæli* → *estrelas do céu* (CNBB and DM1962 have *astros*).

3:61

*omnes spíritus Dei* → *todos os sopros de Deus* (decision `spiritus`, v2). *omnis imber et ros* → *toda chuva e orvalho*: *imber → chuva* (77:44 *imbres → chuvas*), *ros → orvalho* (132:2b). *omnis* in the singular, 'every', which Brazilian *toda chuva* says.

3:62

See decision `aestus`. *ignis → fogo*, *frigus → frio* (147:6 *frígoris → frio*).

3:63

*rores* is plural, so *orvalhos*, against 3:61's singular *ros → orvalho*. *pruína → geada* (77:47, 118:83).

3:64

*noctes et dies* → *noites e dias*, the Latin order.

3:65

*lux → luz* (collides with *lumen*, as the glossary notes); *fúlgura → relâmpagos* (17:15, 96:4); *nubes → nuvens*.

3:66

See decision `jussive`. The second colon changes person with the first (*laudet et superexáltet*): *louve-o e exalte-o*.

3:67

*montes et colles* → *montes e colinas* (148:9, the *collis* row). *univérsa germinántia in terra* → *tudo o que germina na terra*: 64:11's *gérminans → o que germina*, with *univérsa* as *tudo*.

3:68

*mária et flúmina* → *mares e rios* (23:2).

3:69

No comma after *baleias* (v1 stylist: the pause breaks the colon in two); the Clementine has one after *cete*, DO too, but it is print, not sense. *ómnia, quæ movéntur in aquis* → *tudo o que se move nas águas*. *vólucres cæli* → *aves do céu* (8:9, 103:12). The CNBB has *pássaros*.

3:70

*béstiæ → feras* (148:10, the *béstiæ* row). *fílii hóminum → filhos dos homens* (formula).

3:71

See decision `jussive`. *Israël → Israel*.

3:72

*sacerdótes → sacerdotes*, *servi Dómini → servos do Senhor* (133:1a).

3:73

*spíritus* here means spirits (of the just, beside their souls) → *espíritos*. Compare 3:61. The commas after *espíritos* and *santos* are gone in v2. The Clementine prints *spíritus et ánimæ justórum* and *sancti et húmiles corde* with no comma. Spoken, the comma cannot be heard anyway (v1 ambiguity reader), and without it *dos justos / de coração* may govern one noun or both, as in the Latin. *húmiles corde* → *humildes de coração* (*húmilis* D27; *retos de coração*).

3:74

The three names as the Portuguese Bibles have them (Ananias, Azarias, Misael — DM1962 and the CNBB, fetched). The refrain = 3:57b.

3:75

DO's rubric *(Fit reverentia:)* is kept as a rubric in parentheses, translated: *(Faz-se reverência:)*; checks.py ignores parentheses, and a Portuguese file for DO needs it (DO's Portuguese has *(inclina cabeça)*). *Patrem* accusative → *o Pai* (the *benedícere* row). *eum* singular after three Persons is the Latin's → *a ele louvemos e exaltemos* (decision `eum75`, v2). The Diurnal Monástico 1962 notes that no Gloria Patri is said after this canticle: this verse is its doxology.

3:56

*Benedíctus es* → *Bendito sois* (the CNBB has the same words, *Bendito sois, Senhor, no firmamento dos céus*). *firmaméntum cæli* → *firmamento do céu* (D31: the sky). *laudábilis* → *digno de louvor*, the formula of 47:2 / 95:4 / 144:3 (*muito digno de louvor*) and 112:3. *gloriósus → glorioso* (86:3 *Gloriósa → Coisas gloriosas*). The three *et* are kept.
