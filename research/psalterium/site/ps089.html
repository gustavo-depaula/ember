minor

“desde sempre e para todo o sempre” a sǽculo et usque in sǽculum

The Latin keeps the concrete 'from age to age' (saeculum). 'Desde sempre / para todo o sempre' flattens it into a generic adverb of eternity. That is defensible but could be closer.

proposeddesde os séculos e até os séculos vós sois, Deus.

kept as an option under a sǽculo et usque in sǽculum — settled formula (D37, D41; 40:14 = 105:48); the glossary row names 89:2

8

minor

“o nosso tempo” sǽculum nostrum

Saeculum here means our lifetime or age, the span of our life. 'O nosso tempo' is vaguer and can be read as 'our era'. 'A nossa vida' or 'a nossa idade' is closer to the sense.

proposeda nossa vida à luz do vosso rosto.

❧ taken

A faithful, close rendering that keeps the Latin's hard readings, including the Septuagintal 'aranea meditabuntur', 'supervenit mansuetudo et corripiemur' and the doubled 'dirige' of v. 17, without smoothing them toward the Hebrew. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only notes are two minor ones on how 'saeculum' is rendered.

5

minor

“coisas tidas por nada” quæ pro níhilo habéntur

The Latin has a relative clause with a finite present passive verb. The Portuguese adds the noun 'coisas' and turns the verb into a participle. The sense survives, but the syntax could stay closer to the Latin.

proposedE como a vigília na noite, * os que são tidos por nada, tais serão os seus anos.

kept as an option under quæ pro níhilo habéntur, eórum anni erunt — *coisas* is DRB’s and MS1932’s (“things that are counted nothing”), the neuter made audible; the bare relative form is option 2.

8

minor

“a nossa vida à luz do vosso rosto” sǽculum nostrum in illuminatióne vultus tui

'Sæculum' means age or lifetime, not 'vida'. 'Illuminatio' means the act of lighting up, not simply 'luz'. Both are mild normalizations.

proposedo nosso tempo na iluminação do vosso rosto.

❧ taken

10

minor

“Mas, se houver forças” Si autem in potentátibus

The Latin is a plural prepositional phrase ('among the strong', 'in states of strength'). 'Se houver forças' makes it an existential clause. The sense is defensible, but the Latin's concrete 'in potentatibus' is loosened.

proposedMas, se nos fortes, oitenta anos:

kept as an option under Si autem in potentátibus — as at the v2 gate: *potentátus* is power, not persons (L&S); *nos fortes* is option 1.
