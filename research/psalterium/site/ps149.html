fails: native

“entoem-lhe salmos”

The enclisis on a third-person plural subjunctive ('entoem-lhe') sounds stiff and bookish in the mouth, and it lands at the end of the colon where the ear wants the plainest possible close.

proposedcom tamborim e saltério cantem-lhe salmos:

kept as an option under psallant ei — The stiffness is answered by proclisis 'lhe entoem salmos'; 'cantem' refused because psállere is entoar salmos (D25) and cantáre stands in 149:1.

5

fails: native

“alegrar-se-ão nos seus leitos”

The mesoclisis 'alegrar-se-ão' is correct but antiquated in current Brazilian speech. At the start of a colon it trips the reciter and shows off its grammar, which cuts against simple nobility.

proposedhão de alegrar-se em seus leitos.

❧ taken — Taken with the article kept: 'hão de alegrar-se nos seus leitos'.

6

fails: plain, sayable

“As exaltações de Deus na sua garganta”

With the singular 'sua garganta' directly after 'Deus', the ear first hears 'God's throat'. Latin 'gútture eórum' belongs to the saints. The opening 'As exaltações' is also a heavy, slow start.

proposedAs exaltações de Deus em suas gargantas: * e espadas de dois gumes em suas mãos.

❧ taken — The fault taken, by the Latinist's fix 'na garganta deles'; the plural 'gargantas' refused (changes gútture's number), kept as option.

9

fails: plain

“Para fazerem sobre eles o juízo escrito”

'fazer sobre eles' is a loose calque of 'in eis' and makes the colon wordy. 'neles' is plainer, shorter, and closer to the Latin.

proposedPara fazerem neles o juízo escrito:

not taken — 'fazer neles o juízo' leaves the relation unclear; 'sobre eles' is 118:84's build and was heard rightly by the ambiguity reader.

The psalm sounds bright and processional. Its opening verses are plain and well cadenced, and each colon fits in one breath. The martial second half keeps its hard Latin edge ('vingança', 'repreensões', 'grilhões') without softening it. The main faults are a few bookish clitic forms, and verse 6, where the possessive makes the reciter hear God's throat instead of the saints'.

7

minor

“Para fazer vingança entre as nações: * repreensões entre os povos.” Ad faciéndam vindíctam in natiónibus: * increpatiónes in pópulis.

'in' here marks the object on which vengeance and rebukes fall (as in v. 9 'in eis', rendered 'sobre eles'); 'entre' reads as mere location, as if the vengeance were done among the nations rather than upon them. Defensible, but 'nas'/'sobre' is closer and keeps the parallel with v. 9.

proposedPara fazer vingança nas nações: * repreensões nos povos.

kept as an option under in natiónibus … in pópulis — Held: 'entre' is the Portuguese idiom with plural peoples, the ambiguity reader heard vengeance upon the nations, 'nas nações' is heard as place; the Latinist called it defensible.

A close and faithful rendering: every clause, image and grammatical feature of the Latin is carried over, including the Septuagintal 'exaltationes Dei in gutture eorum', 'in cubilibus suis', 'in salutem' and the dative 'omnibus sanctis ejus'. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each). The only point worth tightening is the preposition in v. 7, where 'entre' blurs the sense of vengeance executed upon the nations.

Verse by verse

1

No title: DO's Psalm149.txt opens on 'Cantáte Dómino' and has no 'Allelúja' line (the LXX and MS1932 have one; DRB ends the psalm with 'Alleluia'). Nothing to translate under D7, as Pss 145–148. 'Cantai ao Senhor um cântico novo' = 95:1 and 97:1 (concord.py); 'na assembleia dos santos' = 88:6 'in ecclésia sanctórum' (D34 ecclésia → assembleia; sanctus ὅσιος → santo). The whole verse is a versicle in the Missal and Office (06-15, 10-31, 11-05 Reliquiae) and reads alone.

2

'naquele que o fez': *fácere → fazer* as in 'que fez o céu e a terra' (120:2 etc.); MS1932 'criou' is *creáre*'s word. 'Alegre-se … exultem' as 47:12 'Alegre-se o monte Sião, e exultem as filhas de Judá'. *in rege suo → no seu rei*.

3

*týmpanum → tamborim* (67:26, 80:3), *psaltérium → saltério* (32:2 'com o saltério'), *psállere → entoar salmos* (D25; 32:2 'entoai-lhe salmos'). The Latin's order, instruments before the verb, is sayable and kept. Final colon as the Latin. 'com tamborim e saltério' without articles, as the Latin: the colon was +3 syllables with them (checks.py). 'saltério' and 'tamborim' were unknown to the ambiguity reader; kept (glossary rows, instruments the Latin names).

5

*exsultáre → exultar*, *sancti → os santos* (glossary). The first colon is also the versicle 'Exsultábunt Sancti in glória' and reads alone.

6

*gládii ancípites → espadas de dois gumes* (plural kept; *gládius → espada*; DM1962 and the CNBB 'de dois gumes', MS1932 'de dois fios'). *guttur → garganta* (5:11a, 113:15; the Common of Martyrs antiphon has *fáucibus* here, also *garganta* by the fauces row). The two *eórum* are 'deles … deles' (v2; decision eorum). Not softened: the swords stay in their hands.

7

*vindícta → vingança* (57:11, 139:13 'a vingança dos pobres'); 'fazer vingança' keeps *fácere*, repeated in 149:9. *in natiónibus … in pópulis → entre as nações … entre os povos* (MS1932, DRB 'upon … among'); gentes/pópuli → nações/povos. Not softened: MS1932's bracketed '(a divina)' is a gloss and is not taken. The second colon is verbless as in the Latin; the ambiguity reader found it could sound incomplete but heard it rightly ('castigar os povos'). 'vingança' was heard as 'vingar-se das nações' — the Latin's word, kept unsoftened.

8

'os seus reis … os seus nobres': the *eórum* is the nations', as in the Latin; *nóbiles → nobres* (DRB 'nobles'; MS1932 'príncipes' is *príncipes*' word). 'em grilhões … em algemas de ferro' keeps the Latin's *in* twice. 'grilhões' was unknown to the ambiguity reader; kept (104:18, the concrete image).

9

No 'Alleluia' at the end: not in DO's Latin.
