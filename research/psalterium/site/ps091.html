4

fails: sayable

“com cântico, com a cítara”

The articles are uneven across a parallel run: 'as dez cordas', 'o saltério', 'a cítara' all carry an article, but 'cântico' has none. The mouth hits a small snag at the one bare noun.

proposedcom o cântico, com a cítara.

❧ taken

5

fails: concrete, no rhyme

“me alegrastes, Senhor, com o que fizestes”

'com o que fizestes' turns the noun 'factura' into a relative clause, which is vaguer than 'your work' and less concrete. It also sets up an internal chime -astes / -estes that the ear hears within one colon.

proposedPorque me alegrastes, Senhor, com a vossa obra: * e nas obras das vossas mãos exultarei.

kept as an option under in factúra tua — The clause is gone (the Latinist's 'feitura'), but 'obra' is refused: it merges factúra with ópera.

11

fails: sayable

“e a minha velhice, numa misericórdia fecunda”

'numa' is conversational, and with the verb left out after the comma the colon sounds clipped. 'em' is plainer and more liturgical, and keeps 'fecunda' so it echoes v.15.

proposede a minha velhice, em misericórdia fecunda.

❧ taken

“como o do unicórnio”

'o do u-' runs three vowels together. It is sayable but clumsy on the mediant.

proposedE será exaltado o meu chifre como o do unicórnio (moving the verb first eases the build-up to the mark).

❧ taken

12

fails: native, no rhyme

“o meu olho olhou de cima”

'olho olhou' is a same-root jingle that the Latin (oculus despexit) does not have. 'olhar de cima' is also a colloquial idiom for looking down on someone. Together they make the worst stumble in the psalm.

proposedE o meu olho viu do alto os meus inimigos.

kept as an option under Et despéxit óculus meus inimícos meos — Identical Latin to 53:9, which has 'o meu olho olhou de cima'; rule 6. Flagged for Gustavo in the despícere row.

“ouvirá o meu ouvido”

The same kind of echo again, which makes the verse sound like a doubled pun. Putting the subject before the verb softens it and ends the colon on a clean oxytone.

proposede o meu ouvido ouvirá dos malvados que se levantam contra mim.

❧ taken

15

fails: native, plain

“e estarão bem”

In current Brazilian speech 'estarão bem' means 'they'll be fine/ok', which is flat and casual for 'bene patientes erunt'. 'numa velhice' is conversational too.

proposedAinda se multiplicarão em velhice fecunda: * e serão bem tratados, para que anunciem:

kept as an option under et bene patiéntes erunt — 'em velhice' taken; 'bem tratados' refused (supplies an agent, makes them passive); 'passarão bem' taken instead from the Latinist.

The psalm mostly sounds like dignified, plain Portuguese: the tree images in vv.13-14 and the opening praise flow well, and the long repetition in v.10 is kept faithfully and can be said. The weak spots are v.12's accidental 'olho olhou' / 'ouvirá o ouvido' jingle and some small conversational choices ('numa', 'estarão bem'). These pull the register down from simple nobility.

5

minor

“me alegrastes, Senhor, com a vossa feitura” delectásti me, Dómine, in factúra tua

'in' + ablative is rendered as instrumental 'com', which narrows it; the Latin 'in' (in/by your work) is parallel to 'in opéribus' in the second half, which the translation renders 'nas obras'. Using 'na' keeps the parallel and the Latin's openness.

proposedme alegrastes, Senhor, na vossa feitura

kept as an option under in factúra tua — 'me alegrastes na …' is not the Portuguese build of the causative; 'com' as 29:2, 64:8.

12

minor

“o meu ouvido ouvirá dos malvados que se levantam contra mim” in insurgéntibus in me malignántibus áudiet auris mea

'ouvir dos malvados' reads most naturally in Portuguese as 'hear from the wicked' (they speak), whereas 'audire in' here means to hear about or concerning them (their fate), in parallel with the eye looking down on the enemies. 'acerca de' would be clearer, though 'de' can carry that sense.

proposedo meu ouvido ouvirá acerca dos malvados que se levantam contra mim

kept as an option under et in insurgéntibus in me malignántibus áudiet auris mea — He grants 'de' can carry 'about'; the Latin's in leaves both readings open and the draft keeps them.

The translation is faithful to the Latin throughout. It keeps the Septuagintal features (the unicorn's horn, 'bene patientes' as 'passarão bem', 'despexit' as 'olhou de cima'), and tense, person and number are preserved. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only points are two minor prepositional nuances.

Verse by verse

2

confitéri → dar graças a (D5); psállere → entoar salmos (D25). 'É bom' for Bonum est (not 'Bom é'), the natural order. The verse is the Offertory / Gradual of several Masses and a Responsory (R. Bonum est confitéri Dómino, allelúja: * Et psállere, allelúja); it reads alone. The switch from 'Dómino' (third person) to 'nómini tuo' (second) is the Latin's and is kept.

3

annuntiáre → anunciar (D41); mane → de manhã (row); per noctem → durante a noite (the nox row's build, singular here). véritas → verdade (row), not the Hebrew-based 'fidelidade'. Read alone as a Gradual/Alleluia verse (Ad annuntiándum mane …): it does.

4

in + instrument → com, as 32:2 and 48:5; cum cántico → com cântico. The Latin's in / cum distinction is lost (grammar). The colon ends on 'cítara', a proparoxytone, as the Latin's ends on 'cíthara' — accepted as at 56:9. cíthara → cítara, psaltérium → saltério (rows). Draft 2: 'com o cântico' (the stylist — the article, as with the three instruments around it).

5

delectáre (causative, εὔφρανας) → alegrar, as 29:2 and 64:8 (the delectári row). exsultáre in → exultar em (row). The Latin's verb-last order kept in the second colon: it ends on an oxytone.

6

cogitátio → pensamento (row; this verse named there).

7

vir → homem; insípiens → insensato, stultus → tolo (D43; 48:11 has the pair: 'o insensato e o tolo'); intellégere → entender.

8

apparére → aparecer; operári iniquitátem → praticar a iniquidade (row, = 91:10). The verse ends on a colon: its sentence runs into 91:9.

9

in sǽculum sǽculi → pelos séculos dos séculos (D27); in ætérnum → para sempre (D23).

10

The Latin's repetition 'quóniam ecce inimíci tui … quóniam ecce inimíci tui' kept whole, vocative and all. ecce → eis (que); períre → perecer; dispérgere (people) → dispersar, passive, as 58:16. Long verse, as the Latin's.

11

cornu → chifre (row), exaltáre → exaltar (row: 74:11 'serão exaltados os chifres do justo'). senéctus → velhice. The ambiguity reader heard the cuckold's 'chifre' and a mythical unicorn: both rows keep the Latin's image unexplained (as 74:11, 21:22).

12

óculus singular → o meu olho (row); auris → o meu ouvido (singular, as the Latin). The tenses are the Latin's: perfect despéxit, future áudiet. The past / future sequence the ambiguity reader found jarring is the Latin's (despéxit … áudiet).

13

cedrus Líbani → o cedro do Líbano (36:35 'os cedros do Líbano').

14

'na casa do Senhor … nos átrios da casa do nosso Deus' = the formula of 133:1b / 134:2 (átrium → átrio, domus → casa).

15

senécta → velhice (as senéctus in 91:11; 70:18). adhuc → ainda (41:6b). ut annúntient → para que anunciem; the verse ends on a colon, its object is 91:16.

16

rectus → reto; iníquitas → iniquidade (row), where the Greek has ἀδικία.
