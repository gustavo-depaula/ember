Laudáte Dóminum

Draft 2 — reviewed. Pray it aloud first; then decide below. Every choice you make rewrites the psalm here, so what you pray is what you chose. Nothing is final until you say so.

Dotted gold marks words still waiting for your decision — touch one to choose its wording where it stands. A gold wash marks where your choice departs from draft 2.

Each verse from the Latin down to what is prayed. Between one draft and the next, words that left are struck through and words that came carry the gold wash; beside them, who objected and what the audit says. A verse no draft touched folds to one line — open it to see its strata.

4

fails: sayable

“e todas as águas que estão sobre os céus louvem o nome do Senhor”

The second colon is long for one breath, and the relative clause 'que estão sobre os céus' puts a flat, prosy verb in the middle. The ear waits a long time for the main verb 'louvem'.

proposedLouvai-o, céus dos céus: * e todas as águas acima dos céus louvem o nome do Senhor.

❧ taken — grammar: a relative clause with copula becomes a prepositional phrase

5

fails: plain, native

“e as coisas foram feitas”

The supplied subject 'as coisas' sounds like a paraphrase and breaks the tight parallel with the second half, 'e foram criadas'. The Latin is bare, 'facta sunt / creata sunt', and the Portuguese can be bare too.

proposedPorque ele disse, e foram feitas: * ele ordenou, e foram criadas.

kept as an option under Quia ipse dixit, et facta sunt: * ipse mandávit, et creáta sunt — 32:9 is the same Latin and took 'as coisas' from its own stylist; rule 6

6

fails: plain

“pôs um decreto”

'Pôs um decreto' sounds bureaucratic and a little clumsy in the mouth (pôs um de-). 'Lei' is plainer and just as faithful to 'praeceptum', and it lands more firmly before 'e não passará'.

proposedFirmou-as para sempre, e pelos séculos dos séculos: * pôs uma lei, e não passará.

kept as an option under præcéptum pósuit, et non præteríbit — præcéptum → decreto settled (D19); lei is lex's

8

fails: native

“vento de tempestades”

With no article and a plural noun, this sounds like a label ('a tempest kind of wind'), not a named power of creation. The definite genitive reads naturally and keeps 'spiritus procellarum'.

proposedFogo, granizo, neve, gelo, vento das tempestades: * que cumprem a sua palavra:

kept as an option under spíritus procellárum — 10:7 has the same Latin words, bare; rule 6

14a

fails: native, plain

“A sua confissão está sobre o céu e a terra”

In current Portuguese, 'confissão ... sobre' first reads as 'a confession about heaven and earth', and 'confissão' pulls toward the confessional. 'Acima de' removes the 'about' reading and keeps the Latin word.

proposedA sua confissão está acima do céu e da terra: * e exaltou o chifre do seu povo.

❧ taken

“o chifre do seu povo”

In Brazilian speech 'chifre' carries a comic connotation (cuckoldry), and a choir may hear it. The image has to stay, so the only remedy is to keep it firmly inside the solemn phrase. Swapping it for 'força' or 'poder' would turn the image into an abstraction.

proposedKeep 'e exaltou o chifre do seu povo' (no better faithful option). Note it for the ruling rather than change it.

not taken — he asks for no change; the cornu row keeps the image everywhere

14b

fails: cadence, native

“o povo que se aproxima dele”

The colon ends on a weak pronoun, 'dele', after a flat proclitic 'se', so the final cadence dies away. Moving the pronoun forward gives a stressed paroxytone close and a more prayerful order.

proposedHino para todos os seus santos: * para os filhos de Israel, o povo que dele se aproxima.

❧ taken

The litany of creatures sounds good in Portuguese: short vocative lists, the repeated 'Louvai-o', and clean cadences carry the chant well. The weak spots are the few places where the translation turns into prose, like the supplied 'as coisas', 'que estão sobre', and 'pôs um decreto'. The closing doxology (v. 14) is where the ear trips most, on 'confissão sobre' and the exposed 'chifre'.

worst line148:14a

best line148:3

12

minor

“porque só o seu nome é exaltado” quia exaltátum est nomen ejus solíus

In the Latin, 'solius' agrees with 'ejus': it is 'the name of him alone', which contrasts God with other beings. The Portuguese puts 'só' on 'nome' ('only his name'), which contrasts his name with other things. The sense is close but the restriction lands on the wrong word.

proposedporque é exaltado o nome só dele

kept as an option under (13) quia exaltátum est nomen ejus solíus — *só o seu nome* already contrasts him with others in Portuguese; the fix is not sayable; DRB and MS1932 have the same build

(13)

The Latin keeps the marker '(13)' before the second half, showing where verse 13 begins. The translation drops it. This is not a pointing mark and changes no content, but the verse boundary is lost.

proposedJovens e virgens: † os anciãos com os mais jovens louvem o nome do Senhor: * (13) porque é exaltado o nome só dele.

kept as an option under (13) quia exaltátum est nomen ejus solíus — *só o seu nome* already contrasts him with others in Portuguese; the fix is not sayable; DRB and MS1932 have the same build

The translation is faithful and literal throughout: the Septuagintal features (dracones, ligna fructifera, cornu, confessio, populo appropinquanti sibi) are kept and not smoothed, and the pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only point worth fixing is 148:12, where 'solius' should restrict 'ejus' and not 'nomen', and where the '(13)' boundary marker is missing.

Verse by verse

1

*in excélsis → nas alturas* (the excélsa row; D42), as the Gloria is said in Brazil. No title line: DO's Psalm148.txt has none — the Greek's αλληλουια Αγγαιου καὶ Ζαχαριου is not in the working text (D7), as Pss 145–147 also begin at the first prayed verse.

2

96:7b's build: *Louvai-o, todos os seus anjos* (the object *-o* expressed, so the noun is heard as the vocative). *ángelus → anjo* lowercase (row).

3

Vocatives without articles, as the Latin.

4

*cæli cælórum → céus dos céus* (MS1932, DRB). *super cælos → sobre os céus* (8:2b, 56:6, 107:5). *laudent* → the jussive *louvem*, 3rd person, as the Latin. *et aquæ omnes* → *e todas as águas* (plain order). The second colon is long, as the Latin's; no split possible without a mark.

6

*in ætérnum, et in sǽculum sǽculi → para sempre, e pelos séculos dos séculos* (D23, D27; = 9:6).

9

*ligna fructífera → árvores frutíferas* (lignum row); *colles → colinas* (collis row, which names 148:9).

10

*béstiæ → feras* (row); *vólucres pennátæ → aves aladas* (77:27 *volatília pennáta*; the pennæ row warns against *penas*); *serpentes → serpentes* (57:5, 139:4).

11

*reges terræ → os reis da terra* (2:2, 71:11, 137:4), here without article as vocatives; *júdices terræ → juízes da terra*.

12

*Júvenes, et vírgines* → *Jovens e virgens* (77:63 has the pair: *os seus jovens … as suas virgens*). The Latin's comma before *et* dropped (Portuguese punctuation). The Latin's *(13)* marker not reproduced.

14a

*exaltávit cornu pópuli sui → exaltou o chifre do seu povo* (cornu row, open, kept at every place; 88:25, 111:9 have the same verb with *chifre*). The ambiguity readers of 74, 91 and 111 heard the cuckold's horn; the image stands unexplained, as the Latin.

14b

No closing *Aleluia*: the DO Latin has none.
