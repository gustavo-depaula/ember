3b

minor

“para todo o sempre” usque in sǽculum

'usque in saeculum' is the plain formula 'unto the age / for ever'; 'para todo o sempre' is the intensified idiom that usually answers 'in saeculum saeculi' or 'in aeternum et ultra'. Defensible, but slightly heightened.

proposede a vida para sempre.

not taken — D37 settles usque in sǽculum → para todo o sempre and lists 132:3b; usque is carried by todo, and para sempre is kept for bare in sǽculum (D23).

A faithful, close rendering: every image (the ointment on the head, the beard of Aaron, the hem of the garment, the dew of Hermon descending on Sion) is kept concrete, and 'in unum' as 'juntos' is adequate. The only point is the slight intensification of 'usque in saeculum'. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each).

fails: native

“os irmãos habitarem juntos!”

The personal infinitive with its subject placed before it, straight after the mediant, reads like a grammar exercise. Said aloud it stalls on 'os irmãos habitarem'. A finite clause brought in with 'que' is the natural Brazilian way to finish 'como é bom...', and it keeps the same words and the same stress on the final 'juntos'.

proposedEis como é bom e como é agradável, * que os irmãos habitem juntos!

❧ taken

3b

fails: sayable

“Porque ali o Senhor”

'ali o' makes a hiatus right at the start of the verse. The mouth has to choose between running the two together and pausing between them, and 'Porque' is heavy for a short opening. 'Pois' is lighter, the clause runs smoothly, and it still carries 'quoniam'.

proposedPois ali o Senhor ordenou a bênção, * e a vida para todo o sempre.

kept as an option under Quóniam illic — porque is the psalter's default for quóniam; pois is kept for quia before por que questions (D42); the elision ali‿o is ordinary.

This is a short, clear psalm, and it sounds like Portuguese prayer. The repeated 'desce sobre' stays in, as it should, and gives the middle verses a gentle falling rhythm. Every cadence lands well. The two verses in a row that end on -ão (Aarão, Sião) are a rhyme the proper names force, and they do no harm. Only the infinitive clause in the first verse sounds translated.

Verse by verse

1

No titulus (D7), as Pss 119–127 and 133. quam … quam → como é … como é (the exclamation row; quão is the option there): the repetition is the Latin's, and the et is kept (e como é), where the familiar Brazilian line drops it. Exclamation mark supplied; the Latin's colon at the end of the verse leads into 132:2a. The line is sung alone as a Gradual and antiphon and stands alone. v2: the verse ends que os irmãos habitem juntos (the stylist). checks.py: first colon +3 by the counter (como‿é twice elides: +1 said).

2a

Sicut → Como, verbless as the Latin (the comparison hangs on 132:1); the Gradual verse sings it alone as a fragment in the Latin too. MS1932 and the CNBB supply É como (the verbless-comparison row: decide per psalm) — not supplied here, because the verse continues the sentence of 132:1. in cápite → na cabeça. barbam, barbam Aaron → a barba, a barba de Aarão: the Latin's (and Greek's) doubled noun kept (MS1932 drops it). Aaron → Aarão (row).

2b

The relative que (Latin quod, neuter) is the ointment's, not the beard's; the anaphora que desce … que desce shows it, though Portuguese cannot mark the gender — to be tested on the ambiguity reader. ejus → sua (Aaron's). ros Hermon → o orvalho do Hermon (the Hermon row, D42). in montem Sion → sobre o monte Sião: the Latin's singular kept against the Greek and Hebrew plural (τὰ ὄρη, the CNBB's os montes); sobre three times for the Latin's three in + accusative. (3a) marker not reproduced. checks.py: second colon +5 by the counter, which does not elide (co-mo‿o, so-bre‿o: about +3 said); accepted — que desce ao monte Sião would save two syllables at the cost of the threefold sobre. The finals Aarão / Sião rhyme (flag): the Latin has the same echo, Aaron / Sion, both names kept at the ends where the Latin has them; accepted.

3b

Quóniam → Porque. illic → ali (row). benedictiónem → a bênção, with the article the Greek has (τὴν εὐλογίαν); no sua added (MS1932, the Diurnal add it). vitam → e a vida, the article matching a bênção; e vida (MS1932) is the bare Latin. usque in sǽculum → para todo o sempre (D37; the row lists 132:3b). Natural order (o Senhor before the verb). checks.py: first colon −4, accepted: nothing is dropped; benedictiónem is six syllables, bênção two. usque in sǽculum → para todo o sempre is D37, which names 132:3b; the Latinist's para sempre (v1 minor) is bare in sǽculum's (D23) and was refused for that reason. ali is left open between Sion and the brothers' dwelling, as illic is (the ambiguity reader heard Sion first).
