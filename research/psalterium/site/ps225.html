1draft

Canticle-level draft. parallels.py handles psalm numbers only, so ps225/show_parallels.py builds consult/parallels/ps225.md (gitignored) from: the DO Latin; the Bolls Vulgate, Douay-Rheims and WLC Hebrew of Hab 3 (book 35); the Rahlfs LXX from consult/lxx-*.csv (a different text of the prayer, only a distant witness); Matos Soares 1932 from the pdftotext text layer of PDF pp. 1873–1874 = printed pp. 856–857; the Diurnal Monástico 1962 (Friday Lauds, ferial canticle; Portuguese from the Hebrew, *tu* address); DO's Portuguese (no authority). The third-party texts are in consult/ps225/ (gitignored). The Latin is Jerome's Habakkuk from the Hebrew, translated as it stands: *córnua* (horns), *diábolus*, *Jesu meo*, *usque ad collum*, *pelles* are the Latin's and are kept against the Hebrew-family readings of MS1932's glosses, the DM1962 and the CNBB. Precedent checked with ps225/concord.py and ps225/gloss.py (copies of ps216's). Brazilian circulation fetched and recorded in ps225/circulation.md. Hardest readings: *auditiónem tuam*, *Súscitans suscitábis*, *in flumínibus*, *ab itinéribus*, *Jesu meo*, *victor … canéntem*. The first-person *Audívi* at the head of 3:16a would be heard as the *vós* imperative 'Hear!', so *eu* is supplied in both places (decision `ouvi`).

3:19a

minor

“como os das corças” quasi cervorum

cervorum is the masculine genitive plural of cervus, meaning stags or deer in general. 'Corças' means does, which is the Hebrew's hinds, not the Latin.

proposede fará os meus pés como os dos cervos.

not taken — The glossary row *cervus*: 17:34 has the same phrase (*pedes meos tamquam cervórum*) and chose *das corças* because the blind reader heard *cervos* as *servos*. 28:9 held *corças* against a Latinist major. The feminine is the cost, recorded in the row; *dos cervos* stays an option in `ponet`. The stylist raised the same point.

3:10b

minor

“O abismo fez ouvir a sua voz” Dedit abyssus vocem suam

'Fez ouvir' is a paraphrase of dedit ('gave'). It is defensible but less close than the Latin's plain image.

proposedO abismo deu a sua voz:

not taken — The glossary row *vocem dare → fazer ouvir a voz* (17:14, 45:7, 76:18), where *deu a sua voz* was refused before: it is not said in Portuguese. He calls the draft defensible.

This is a faithful and close rendering. It keeps the Latin's oddities: diabolus, Christo tuo, Deo Jesu meo, the pelles of Madian and 'mentietur opus olivae'. It keeps the Latin tenses and moods, including the jussives in 16b. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The Latin's embedded verse numbers, (5a) and (10a), are not in the Portuguese, but they are not pointing marks. The only real departure is 'corças' for cervorum in 19a, which follows the Hebrew hinds rather than the Latin.

3:2a

fails: native, plain

“eu ouvi a vossa notícia”

'notícia' now means news, a newspaper word; in the mouth it sounds like a bulletin, not a word from God. The subject pronoun 'eu' also adds a syllable the verb already carries.

proposedSenhor, ouvi o vosso anúncio, * e temi.

not taken — *Audítio → notícia* is the glossary's row (111:7a), and it keeps *tuam* open between a report from God and a report about him. *Anúncio* is no less a newspaper word in Brazil (the classifieds) and leans to God as the speaker. *Eu* stays: *Senhor, ouvi* after a vocative is the *vós* imperative 'Lord, hear' (decision `ouvi`); the CNBB LH has *Eu ouvi* too. Options kept in `auditio`.

3:2b

fails: sayable

“vivificai-a”

vi-vi-fi-cai-a: three identical i-syllables and then a hiatus onto the clitic. The tongue stumbles at the close of the verse, and when chanted the final 'a' floats.

proposedSenhor, a vossa obra, * no meio dos anos, dai-lhe vida:

kept as an option under opus tuum … vivífica illud — The glossary row *vivificáre → vivificar* (Ps 118 nine times, 40:3, 70:20). *Dai-lhe vida* is the row's own option and stays there. The row's verb is kept for the concord; the ambiguity reader did not stop at it.

3:5b

fails: no rhyme

“diante dos seus pés. * Pôs-se de pé”

'pés' at the mediant, then 'pé' at once after it. The echo is not in the Latin (pedes / stetit) and sounds like a pun.

proposedE o diabo sairá diante dos seus pés. * Parou, e mediu a terra.

❧ taken — *Deteve-se* (not *Parou*): the 1:1 row, and one verb with 3:11 *detiveram-se*. Decision `stetit`.

3:6b

fails: native, plain

“os montes seculares”

In current Brazilian Portuguese 'secular' first means 'lay, non-religious'. The ear hears 'the secular mountains' before it hears 'age-old'.

proposedOlhou, e dissolveu as nações: * e foram quebrados os montes dos séculos.

❧ taken — Decision `saeculi`. The ambiguity reader heard the same.

3:6c

fails: native

“Foram curvadas as colinas do mundo”

The analytic passive at the head of the verse is stiff and sounds translated. The reflexive is the native form for 'incurvati sunt', and it avoids a heavy opening.

proposedCurvaram-se as colinas do mundo, * pelos caminhos da sua eternidade.

not taken — With the reflexive, *pelos caminhos* is heard as 'along the roads'; the passive keeps it as the agent, which is what *ab* with *incurváti sunt* says. The ambiguity reader, even with the passive, found the phrase unclear, so the reflexive would cost more. Option in `incurvati`.

3:8b

fails: native

“montareis sobre os vossos cavalos”

'montar sobre' is a calque. In Portuguese one 'monta o cavalo'. The extra preposition also slows the colon.

proposedVós que montareis os vossos cavalos: * e as vossas quadrigas são salvação.

❧ taken — New decision `ascendes`.

3:11

fails: sayable

“na luz das vossas setas, irão no esplendor da vossa lança fulgurante”

The final colon is overlong, and the bare 'irão' arrives with no subject in view: after 'setas' the ear briefly takes the arrows as the ones that go. A light supplied auxiliary would give the verb weight and a clear onset.

proposedO sol e a lua detiveram-se na sua morada, * na luz das vossas setas; hão de ir no esplendor da vossa lança fulgurante.

not taken — The Latin's *ibunt* has no expressed subject either, and it is open (the sun and moon, or others). *Hão de ir* lengthens the Latin's longest colon and still names no subject. Held; the ambiguity reader found the same openness the Latin has.

3:14b

fails: native, sayable

“A exultação deles * como a de quem”

Five syllables of subject, then the mediant, then a comparison with no verb. Spoken aloud it sounds like a fragment. A supplied copula makes it a sentence without changing the sense.

proposedA exultação deles * era como a de quem devora o pobre às escondidas.

❧ taken — Decision `sicut`. The ambiguity reader heard the same.

3:16b

fails: sayable, native

“Que entre a podridão”

At the head of a verse, 'entre' is heard first as the preposition 'between'. 'Que entre a podridão…' sounds like 'that among the rot…' until the line is half over.

proposedQue a podridão entre nos meus ossos, * e que fervilhe debaixo de mim.

❧ taken — New decision `ingrediatur`. The draft's own worry, confirmed by the ambiguity reader.

3:17c

fails: native

“O rebanho será cortado do aprisco”

A flock 'cortado' sounds like it is being sliced; the idiom for 'abscindetur de' with living things is 'arrancado' or 'tirado'.

proposedO rebanho será arrancado do aprisco: * e não haverá manada nos estábulos.

not taken — *Abscíndere → cortar* (76:9). *Arrancar* is the glossary's verb for erípere / éruere / evéllere, so it would merge a fourth Latin verb into it. The ambiguity reader heard 'taken from the fold' first, the sense intended.

3:19a

fails: concrete

“como os das corças”

'cervorum' is masculine (stags). 'Corças' is equally plain and sayable, but it quietly swaps the image. 'Cervos' keeps it with no loss of rhythm.

proposedDeus, o Senhor, é a minha força: * e fará os meus pés como os dos cervos.

not taken — As the Latinist's remark: the glossary row *cervus* (17:34, 28:9). Option in `ponet`.

3:19b

fails: sayable, native

“ele, vencedor, me guiará, * a mim, que canto com salmos”

The first colon is chopped by commas ('alturas ele, vencedor, me'), so a choir cannot take it in one breath. In the second, 'canto com salmos' is unidiomatic: one sings psalms, or sings in psalms.

proposedE sobre as minhas alturas, vencedor, me guiará, * a mim, que canto em salmos.

❧ taken — Half: *em salmos* taken (decision `canentem`). *Ele* kept, so *vencedor* is not heard of the singer; the ambiguity reader, with *ele*, heard God as the victor.

The canticle sounds sturdy and austere. The storm imagery (rivers, abyss, sun and moon standing still) lands plainly, and the closing lament and joy (17-18) is genuinely prayable Portuguese. The weak spots are a few calques and stiff passives ('montar sobre', 'foram curvadas', 'cortado'), two words whose modern sense misleads ('notícia', 'seculares'), and a choppy, verbless or overloaded colon here and there (14b, 11, 19b).

minor

“os montes dos séculos” montes sǽculi

proposede foram quebrados os montes do século.

3:14b

minor

“A exultação deles * era como a de quem” Exsultátio eórum * sicut ejus

The Latin has no verb. The copula is allowed, but the past tense 'era' fixes a time the Latin leaves open. The neutral present 'é' would be closer.

proposedA exultação deles * é como a de quem devora o pobre às escondidas.

kept as an option under Exsultátio eórum * sicut ejus, qui dévorat páuperem in abscóndito — He allows the copula. *Era* follows the enemies' *vinham* in the same strophe, as DRB and MS1932; *é* would turn the line into a maxim. Held for Gustavo, *é como a* recorded as an option.

3:19a

minor

“como os das corças” quasi cervórum

cervórum is masculine ('of stags/deer'). 'corças' (hinds) follows the Hebrew 'ayyalot' against the Latin.

proposede fará os meus pés como os dos cervos.

not taken — The glossary row *cervus*: 17:34 has the same phrase (*pedes meos tamquam cervórum*) and chose *das corças* because the blind reader heard *cervos* as *servos*. 28:9 held *corças* against a Latinist major. The feminine is the cost, recorded in the row; *dos cervos* stays an option in `ponet`. The stylist raised the same point.

The translation follows the Latin closely throughout and keeps the Septuagintal/Vulgate oddities: diabolus, cornua, pelles terræ Madian, Christo tuo, Deo Jesu meo, victor in psalmis canentem. No verse adds or loses content. The issues are small: one number change, one idiomatic paraphrase, one supplied tense, and 'corças' following the Hebrew rather than the Latin cervorum. Pointing marks (* and †) match the Latin in every verse. The parenthetical verse numbers in the Latin are references, not marks.

Verse by verse

3:2a

DO's first line, *(Canticum Habacuc * Hab 3:2-19)*, is a heading with the book reference. It is not a prayed verse and is not translated (D7; readVerses skips it, as for 233, 211–216). The Latin is Jerome's Vulgate of Habakkuk, from the Hebrew, not the Gallican psalter. This line is also DO's antiphon for the canticle (Friday Lauds, `Psalmi major.txt` [Daya5 Laudes], [Monastic Laudes] feria 6). *Timére → temer*.

3:2b

The Latin's anacoluthon (object first, taken up by *illud*) is kept. *Medium annórum → o meio dos anos*, twice, as the Latin repeats it.

3:2c

The second colon, with *Dómine*, is DO's antiphon *Cum irátus fúeris, * Dómine, misericórdiæ recordáberis* ([Day5 Laudes2]): *Quando estiverdes irado, Senhor, vos lembrareis da misericórdia* reads alone. *Misericórdiæ recordári → lembrar-se da misericórdia* (97:3); no possessive supplied (MS1932 puts *(tua)* in parentheses). *Irátus fúeris → estiverdes irado*: the copula build of 79:5, 84:6.

3:3a

*Auster → sul* (the glossary's open row, 125:4; the region). *Sanctus* is God, parallel to *Deus*: *o Santo*, capital, as *o Santo de Israel* (70:22, 77:41, canticle 221). The verb is gapped in the second colon as in the Latin. *Pharan → Farã* (the Portuguese Bible form; the CNBB LH has *Farã*).

3:3b

Natural order (D2) for the Latin's *Opéruit cælos glória ejus … laudis ejus plena est terra*. *Laus → louvor*.

3:4a

Verbless second colon, as the Latin. *Splendor → esplendor* (89:17).

3:4b

DO's line crosses into Vulgate verse 5 at the inline marker *(5a)*, which is not reproduced. *Fortitúdo → força* (glossary). *Ante fáciem → diante da face* (glossary).

3:5b

Crosses into verse 6 at *(6a)*, not reproduced. *Egrédi → sair*, echoed by 3:13a *Egréssus es → Saístes*.

3:6b

*Dissólvere → dissolver* (DRB 'melted'). *Contérere → quebrar* (glossary).

3:6c

*Collis → colina* (64:13). *Mundus* here is the world (Habakkuk's Latin); D30 says the psalter has no *mundus* 'world', so nothing collides. *Æternitas → eternidade*.

3:7

*Tentórium → tenda* (the only tent word here; *tabernáculum → tenda* does not occur in the canticle). *Turbáre → perturbar* (glossary). *Æthiópia → Etiópia*, *Mádian → Madiã*.

3:8a

*Numquid → Acaso* (glossary). The second and third questions stay verbless, as the Latin. *Furor → furor*, *indignátio → indignação* (glossary).

3:9b

DO's line crosses into verse 10 at *(10a)*, not reproduced. The pointing † and * kept.

3:10b

*Dedit vocem suam → fez ouvir a sua voz*, copied from 17:14 and 45:7. *Altitúdo → altura* (glossary, concrete). *Leváre → levantar* (glossary).

3:11

DO's comma after *tuárum* is kept, so *na luz das vossas setas* hangs between the two verbs as in the Latin. *In luce … in splendóre → na luz … no esplendor*, the same preposition twice. *Sagítta → seta* (glossary).

3:13a

*Salus → salvação* (D6), twice, as the Latin repeats it. *Christus → Cristo* (D19), as 2:2 *o seu Cristo*.

3:13b

*Percútere → ferir*. *Caput de domo ímpii → a cabeça da casa do ímpio*. *Collum → pescoço* (the *cervix* row's word, 128:4; the two never meet).

3:14a

*Maledícere → amaldiçoar* (D44). The Latin's plural *sceptris* kept. *Bellátor → guerreiro*. *Dispérgere → dispersar* (glossary).

3:15

*Lutum → lama* (39:3, 17:43). *Aquæ multæ → as muitas águas* (92:3b).

3:16a

*Conturbáre → perturbar* (glossary; 30:10 *o meu ventre*). *Contremíscere → estremecer* (17:8, 76:19). *A voce → à voz* (DRB 'at the voice').

3:16b

*Putrédo → podridão*. The jussives are carried by *Que … que*.

3:16c

*Requiéscere → repousar* (glossary). *Ascéndere → subir*. *Accínctus → cingido* (64:7). *Tribulátio → tribulação*.

3:17a

*Enim → Pois*. *Víneæ → vinhas* (79:9, 104:33). *Florére → florescer* (91:13).

3:17b

*Arva → os campos*; *cibus → alimento* (64:10b). *Afférre → trazer*.

3:17c

Natural order (D2): *O rebanho será cortado do aprisco* for *Abscindétur de ovíli pecus*.

3:18

*Exsultáre → exultar* (glossary).

3:19b

*Excélsa → alturas* (D42; 17:34 *sobre as alturas*); the Latin's *mea* kept: *as minhas alturas*.
