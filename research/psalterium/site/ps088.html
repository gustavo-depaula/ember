Misericórdias Dómini

minor

“Os céus darão graças pelas vossas maravilhas ... e também pela vossa verdade” Confitebúntur cæli mirabília tua ... étenim veritátem tuam

mirabilia and veritatem are direct objects of confitebuntur (the heavens will confess/praise your wonders); 'dar graças por' turns them into the cause of thanksgiving. 'étenim' is 'and indeed' rather than 'e também'.

proposedOs céus celebrarão as vossas maravilhas, Senhor: * e deveras a vossa verdade na assembleia dos santos.

kept as an option under Confitebúntur cæli mirabília tua … étenim veritátem tuam — D5 is settled; 70:22 already renders confitéri + accusative with *por*.

13

minor

“vosso é o braço com potência” tuum brácchium cum poténtia

The Latin is a verbless noun phrase ('your arm with might'); supplying 'é' turns it into a predication of ownership ('the arm is yours'), a sense the Latin does not assert.

proposedo vosso braço com potência.

kept as an option under tuum brácchium cum poténtia — The Greek σὸς ὁ βραχίων fixes the predicate reading; it echoes 88:12 *Tui … tua*.

minor

“não mentirei a Davi” Si David méntiar

The Latin keeps the elliptical oath formula ('if I lie to David...'); converting it to a plain negative explains rather than translates the Septuagintal idiom.

proposedUma vez jurei pela minha santidade: se eu mentir a Davi! *

minor

“Destruístes-lhe a purificação” Destruxísti eum ab emundatióne

The object of destruxisti is 'eum' (him), with 'ab emundatione' as separation (from his cleansing); the translation makes the purification itself the thing destroyed.

proposedDestruístes-o, apartando-o da purificação:

❧ taken

A faithful, close rendering of the Latin throughout this long psalm, with Septuagintal features (distulisti Christum, commutationem Christi, cornu, non apponet nocere) kept and pointing marks identical to the Latin in every verse. No critical or major faults; the minor points concern a few objects/constructions altered (88:6, 88:13, 88:45), one resolved oath formula (88:36), and a couple of word senses drifting toward the Hebrew (88:15, 88:34).

fails: plain, native

“no vosso beneplácito será exaltado o nosso chifre”

'beneplácito' is chancery Latin carried over, a word that needs a footnote; together with 'chifre' at the cadence the line risks a smile in a Brazilian choir. The image must stay, so at least make the rest plain.

fails: sayable, plain

“repelistes e desdenhastes”

'desdenhastes' is a harsh cluster (-nh-, -st-) right before the mediant and is the rarer, more literary word; 'desprezastes' is the plain one and flows.

proposedMas vós repelistes e desprezastes: * adiastes o vosso Cristo.

not taken — *desprezar* is *spérnere*'s word; *despícere → desdenhar* (row, 21:25 keeps both apart).

minor

“vosso é o braço com potência” tuum brácchium cum poténtia

The Latin is a verbless clause whose subject is 'tuum brachium' and whose predicate is 'cum potentia' (your arm [is] with might). Making 'vosso' the predicate ('yours is the arm') shifts the statement from the arm's might to its ownership.

proposedo vosso braço é com potência.

kept as an option under tuum brácchium cum poténtia — The Greek σὸς ὁ βραχίων fixes the predicate reading; it echoes 88:12 *Tui … tua*.

'Assumptio' (a taking-up, being taken under protection) is rendered with the same word used for 'susceptor' in 88:27, so two different Latin terms collapse into one. Defensible, but the concrete sense of 'being taken up' is lost.

The Latin keeps the elliptical oath formula ('if I lie to David…'), a conditional; the translation resolves it into a negative future, explaining the idiom rather than rendering it.

'Sanctum meum' is a substantive neuter/masculine ('my holy one / my holy place'), not the abstract 'sanctitas'; the translation turns it into an abstraction. Traditional rendering, hence minor.
