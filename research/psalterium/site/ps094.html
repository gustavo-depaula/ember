1

fails: no rhyme

“ao Senhor: * ... nosso Salvador:”

Senhor / Salvador rhyme in -or at mediant and final; on a psalm tone the two cadences chime and the verse sounds like a jingle.

proposedVinde, exultemos ao Senhor: * aclamemos a Deus, nossa salvação:

kept as an option under Deo salutári nostro — 'salvação' is the neuter salutáre's word (D6); the salutáris row gives the person 'Salvador' (64:6, 78:9, 84:5). The rhyme falls on the verse's two names of God and is accepted.

2

fails: native, sayable

“Adiantemo-nos à sua face com ação de graças”

'Adiantemo-nos' is a heavy, bookish enclitic form that no one says; the tongue trips on '-mo-nos à sua', and 'adiantar-se' sounds like hurrying ahead in a queue rather than coming before God's face.

proposedCheguemo-nos primeiro à sua face com ação de graças: * e com salmos o aclamemos.

❧ taken

9

fails: no rhyme, sayable

“com aquela geração, * ... no coração.”

geração / coração is a full rhyme at mediant and final, the most audible in the psalm; the mediant clause also runs long before the breath with 'estive ofendido com' in the middle.

proposedCom aquela geração estive ofendido por quarenta anos, * e disse: Estes sempre se extraviam no coração.

❧ taken

The psalm reads as plain, dignified Portuguese prayer and moves well on the tone; the anaphoric 'Porque' of vv. 3-5 carries the Latin's momentum nicely. Its weaknesses are two accidental -or and -ção rhymes at the cadences and the stiff 'Adiantemo-nos' that opens v. 2.

worst line94:2

best line94:7

Verse by verse

1

Deo salutári nostro → 'a Deus, nosso Salvador' (the salutáris row: τῷ σωτῆρι, the person; 64:6, 78:9, 84:5). jubiláre → aclamar (D43). The first colon is the Alleluia of Pentecost XIV (missa Pent14-0), the Introit verse of Ember Saturday of September (missa 093-6) and an Invitatory antiphon of the Dominican rubrics (Matutinum Special, 'Invit 1', under 'rubrica praedicatorum'); each colon reads alone. Soft flag accepted: Senhor / Salvador rhyme at mediant and final. The Latin's two datives end alike (Dómino … nostro), and the rhyme falls on the two names of God that the parallel sets side by side; the only cure would be an inversion ('ao Senhor exultemos') or dropping the title, both worse.

2

jubilémus ei → 'o aclamemos' (aclamar takes a direct object in Portuguese; D43's verb). The whole verse is the Septuagesima Invitatory antiphon with 'Dómini' for 'ejus': 'Cheguemo-nos primeiro à face do Senhor: * e com salmos o aclamemos.'

4

Quia → 'Porque', as quóniam in 94:3 and 94:5 (one Greek ὅτι; 'porque' the default). fines terræ → 'os confins da terra' (fines row); altitúdines → 'alturas' (altitúdo row, which names 94:4). The Ps 93 agent noted that the Invitatory quotes 93:14a ('quóniam non repéllet Dóminus plebem suam' → 93:14 'Porque o Senhor não repelirá o seu povo'). That clause is only in DO's Invitatorium.txt (the other wording, inserted after 94:3); the Gallican Psalm94.txt translated here has no such line, so nothing in this psalm repeats 93:14a. Whoever translates Invitatorium.txt should copy 93:14's wording there.

5

ipse fecit → 'ele mesmo o fez': the Latin repeats the pronoun of ipsíus (and 94:4 ipsíus sunt), and the emphatic form also brings the first colon to within two syllables of the Latin (checks: 'e ele o fez' was −4). formáre → 'formar', kept apart from plasmáre → moldar.

7

DO's Psalm94.txt is the Epiphany Matins form: it lacks 94:6 and 94:7a ('Veníte adorémus, et procidámus: et plorémus ante Dóminum, qui fecit nos. * Quia ipse est Dóminus Deus noster:', which is in Psalm94C.txt), because the Epiphany antiphon sung in the $ant slots is that verse (collation/report.md; Sancti/01-06: 'Veníte adorémus eum: * quia ipse est Dóminus Deus noster'). Only DO's ids are translated. For the record, a rendering of the missing verse by this psalm's rules: 'Vinde, adoremos e prostremo-nos: e choremos diante do Senhor que nos fez. * Porque ele é o Senhor nosso Deus:' (prócidere → prostrar-se, the row 71:9). 'somos' is the copula Portuguese needs. páscua → 'pastagem' (the row names 94:7: 'o povo da sua pastagem'). The people are addressed as vós (plural humans). Soft flags accepted: the first colon is +5 (the supplied 'somos'), the second −3; the verse as a whole is within two syllables of the Latin. Without 'somos' the line hangs verbless in Portuguese (in the Latin it hangs on the antiphon's 'quia ipse est Dóminus Deus noster'). 'endureçais' was listed as unknown by the ambiguity reader; kept (the Portuguese prohibition; 'não queirais endurecer' is the option).

8

irritátio → 'provocação' (D27, the row names 94:8). tentáre → 'tentar' where men tempt God (the tentáre row names 94:8); probáre → 'provar'. The ambiguity reader heard 'tentação' as the people being tempted (or Christ's temptation) and 'provaram' possibly as 'tasted': both are the rows' words (tentáre, probáre), and the Latin's tentátio is as open as the Portuguese; kept. The speaker's change to God ('me') is the Latin's, unannounced.

9

hi and 94:10 isti are both 'estes', as DRB 'these … these men'. Soft flag accepted: the second colon is +6 ('se extraviam' is the erráre row's word; 'erram' would save three syllables and is an option). v2: the rhyme geração / coração removed by word order (decision order9). The ambiguity reader heard 'se extraviam no coração' rightly (hearts wandering from God) but listed 'extraviam' as unknown; kept, the row's word.

10

réquies → 'repouso' (MS1932). cognóscere → 'conhecer'; via → 'caminho'. The ambiguity reader found the link of 'como jurei' unclear (as / so): that is the Latin's ut, kept open on purpose (decision ut).
