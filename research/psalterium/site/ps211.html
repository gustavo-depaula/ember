29:12

minor

“vós dominais todas as coisas” tu domináris ómnium

ómnium is a genitive plural that can be either masculine (all people, picking up omnes príncipes in 29:11c) or neuter (all things). 'todas as coisas' settles it as neuter. The same thing happens in 29:12b with 'impérium ómnium' rendered 'o domínio de todas as coisas'. This is defensible, but it resolves an ambiguity the Latin keeps.

proposedvós dominais sobre todos, (and in 29:12b: o domínio sobre todos), or keep as is if the project has already ruled on ómnium

kept as an option under tu domináris ómnium … impérium ómnium — Held for the neuter: 29:12b's Greek τὰ πάντα is neuter, 29:11b has just said cuncta, and DRB reads "all things" at 29:12b. Portuguese must choose a gender; the masculine is option 4. His "sobre" in 29:12b is taken.

29:13

minor

“nós vos damos graças” confitémur tibi

Confitéri means to acknowledge or praise openly. 'Damos graças' follows the Hebrew and the Douay rather than the Latin sense. This is common in the liturgy and defensible, but it could be closer.

proposednós vos louvamos (or: nós vos confessamos), then keep 'e louvamos' or adjust to avoid repetition

not taken — D5 (settled): confitéri to God is "dar graças a", ruled precisely because it stands beside laudáre, as here — "nós vos louvamos, e louvamos" is the collision D5 avoids; "confessamos" is heard as professing faith or confessing sin.

“o vosso nome glorioso” nomen tuum ínclitum

Ínclitus means renowned or illustrious, not 'glorious'. 'Glorioso' also repeats the glória of 29:11 and 29:12 as if it were the same word.

proposede louvamos o vosso nome ínclito (or: insigne)

kept as an option under nomen tuum ínclitum — The echo was the cost named in the draft. Held: L&S gives "glorious" among its senses, DRB has "glorious", and it is the wording of both the CNBB Liturgia das Horas and the Brazilian Lectionary at this line (circulation.md). "ínclito" and "insigne" are words needing a footnote (rule 5); "ilustre" (option 2) sounds social.

A faithful rendering that keeps every element of the Latin, including the doubled 'in manu tua' and the full list of divine attributes. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only issues are minor: ómnium is resolved as neuter, confitémur is rendered as thanksgiving, and ínclitum is flattened to 'glorioso'.

5stylist

Draft 1, claude-opus-5-5, fresh context, with latin.json. Two remarks; one taken, one kept as an option. Best line 29:12, worst 29:11c. It judged the polysyndeton and the "vossa… na vossa mão" anaphora to carry well and the -ência/-ória echoes of 29:11 to be the Latin's litany, not rhyme.

claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-23 · critic/v1.stylist.json · read prayed.vos.json

29:11c

fails: cadence

“e vós estais sobre todos os príncipes”

The final word 'príncipes' is proparoxytone (PRÍN-ci-pes), so the verse ends on a stress two syllables back and the tone's final cadence falls on weak syllables; the colon also trails off after its strong words.

proposede sobre todos os príncipes estais vós

kept as an option under et tu es super omnes príncipes — The Latin ends on the same word with the same stress, so the tone fits both columns alike; the inversion to "estais vós" is literary (rule 5).

29:12b

fails: sayable

“na vossa mão, a grandeza e o domínio de todas as coisas”

The second colon is noticeably longer than the first and piles up 'e o domínio de todas as coisas' after the elided verb, which is hard to take in one breath in choir. It is still faithful, and the echo of 'todas as coisas' from 29:12 should stay because the Latin repeats ómnium.

proposedna vossa mão, a grandeza e o domínio sobre todas as coisas

❧ taken — A preposition (grammar, D2); it also removes the ambiguity reader's third reading.

The psalm sounds solemn and clean: the Latin's polysyndeton ('e a potência, e a glória, e a vitória') and the anaphora of 'vossa/vosso' and 'na vossa mão' come across with real liturgical weight and without strain. The -ência/-ória echoes in 29:11 come from the Latin and read as litany, not accidental rhyme. The only real stumble is the proparoxytone final in 29:11c.

worst line29:11c

best line29:12

29:13

minor

“o vosso nome glorioso” nomen tuum ínclitum

ínclitus means renowned, illustrious (famed), not 'glorious'. The chosen word also blurs it with glória, which the canticle has already used twice (29:11, 29:12) as a distinct term.

proposede louvamos o vosso nome ilustre.

kept as an option under nomen tuum ínclitum — Held a second time, and flagged for Gustavo because two readings raised it. "glorious" is among L&S's senses of inclutus and is DRB's word; it is what the CNBB Liturgia das Horas and the Brazilian Lectionary say at this line; "ilustre" (option 2, his fix) is heard as social rank in current Brazilian use. The echo with glória is the cost the draft named; ínclitus and gloriósus never meet in the Latin.

This is a faithful, complete rendering of 1 Par 29:10-13. Every item in the Latin lists is kept, in order and in number, and the pointing marks match the Latin throughout (one * per verse). The only point that could be closer is ínclitum; 'confitémur' as 'damos graças' is a legitimate sense and was not flagged.

Verse by verse

29:10

DO's file opens with the heading line '(Canticum David * 1 Par. 29:10-13)'; it is not a verse (render.py/readVerses skip it) and is not translated, as the Nunc dimittis heading was not (D7). Benedíctus es, Dómine → 'Bendito sois, Senhor', word for word 118:12 (identical Latin). The DO text agrees with the Clementine Vulgate at 1 Chr 29:10–13 (Bolls VULG, consult/bolls-VULG-13-29.json; only spelling inclytum/ínclitum). First colon 3 syllables short of the Latin, accepted to keep 118:12's wording.

29:11

Tua est → 'Vossa é', as 73:16 (Tuus est dies, et tua est nox → Vosso é o dia, e vossa é a noite). poténtia → potência (glossary row, open; 64:7, 70:19, 79:3). victória → vitória (not a psalter word). atque → 'e': it is only a weightier 'and', and a 'e também' would add emphasis the ear hears as more. The Latin's polysyndeton (et… et… atque) kept.

29:11b

enim → pois (glossary). in cælo… et in terra: the Latin's second member hangs after a comma; Portuguese joins it without one.

29:11c

Tuum, Dómine, regnum → 'Vosso, Senhor, é o reino': the copula supplied, as 21:29 'do Senhor é o reino'. príncipes → príncipes (2:2, 46:10). The colon ends proparoxytone, as the Latin's does.

29:12

Built as 73:16 and 88:12 (Vossos são… vossa é…). divítiæ → riquezas (36:16, 48:11b, 111:3). domináris → dominais (88:10 Tu domináris → Vós dominais), transitive as 21:29.

29:12b

In manu tua … in manu tua: the repetition kept, 'Na vossa mão … na vossa mão'. virtus → poder, poténtia → potência, the pair of 64:7. The Latin is verbless twice; 'estão' is supplied in the first colon (grammar), and the second colon gaps it, as Portuguese does naturally in a parallel. magnitúdo → grandeza (70:8, 144:3, 144:6, 150:2). checks.py flags the finals of 29:12 and 29:12b as rhyming (-oisas): the Latin has the same echo, ómnium closing both verses, and the rhyme is that repetition kept (rule 5 allows it where the Latin has it).

29:13

Nunc → 'Agora', as the Nunc dimittis; ígitur → 'pois'. The vocative Deus noster → 'nosso Deus', without 'ó', as 98:8 'Senhor, nosso Deus'. laudáre → louvar; nomen tuum → o vosso nome.
