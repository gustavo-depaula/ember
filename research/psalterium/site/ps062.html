2b

fails: sayable, native

“de quantos modos, por vós, a minha carne!”

The verb is left out and 'por vós' is boxed in by commas, so the colon comes out as three stalled pieces. When you say it aloud you can't tell what 'por vós' belongs to until the line ends. The exclamation sounds suspended rather than finished.

proposede de quantos modos a minha carne, por vós!

❧ taken

6

fails: plain, native

“Como de banha e de gordura”

'Banha' is a kitchen and butcher's word (lard) in Brazil. Chanted in the Office, it draws a smile. Having both 'banha' and 'gordura' also sounds like the same thing said twice. Two concrete words for richness are enough.

proposedComo de tutano e de gordura

kept as an option under Sicut ádipe et pinguédine — tutano is DRB's gloss (marrow), not adeps (lard); the head-of-verse problem met by reordering

“seja repleta a minha alma”

'Seja repleta' is a stiff, bookish passive, and the subjunctive is hard to hear on the mediant. A reflexive with a plain verb carries the wish more naturally.

proposedse farte a minha alma

❧ taken

7

fails: plain

“na minha cama”

'Cama' is flat and domestic next to 'madrugadas' and 'meditarei'. 'Leito' is still current Portuguese, keeps the concrete image of stratum, and suits the register better. 'Leito' also stresses its second-to-last syllable, which is right before the flex.

proposedno meu leito

kept as an option under super stratum meum — leito is lectus's word; the row's pair (6:7, 40:4)

12

fails: plain

“dos que falam coisas iníquas”

'Coisas' is a filler word that weakens the last colon. The line also ends on a heavy, rare adjective after two passive verbs ('serão louvados', 'foi fechada'), so the verse sags at its close.

proposedporque foi tapada a boca dos que falam o que é iníquo

kept as an option under loquéntium iníqua — coisas iníquas = 25:4 (rule 6); tapada heard as a hand over the mouth

On the whole this is dignified and sounds natural: the verses of longing and trust (4, 5, 8b) move in a plain, even rhythm, and the Latin repetitions stay audible. The weak spots are where the translation is either too literal to sound native (the elliptical exclamation in 2b) or reaches for the wrong register (the lard of 6, the 'cama' of 7). The closing verse runs a little long and wordy.

worst line62:6

best line62:4

5ambiguity

31 items, 3 unknown words (*iníquas*, *exultação*, *auxílio* — all glossary rows, kept). One real mishearing mended: 62:6 *Como de banha* heard as "I eat lard" — the colon reordered. The rest are the Latin's own openness (62:3 *sic*, 62:12 *rex*, *in eo*) or understood rightly.

2b

minor

“e de quantos modos a minha carne, por vós!” quam multiplíciter tibi caro mea

The Latin has no conjunction; the second member is asyndetic exclamation after the mediant. The added 'e' is harmless but not in the text.

proposedde quantos modos a minha carne, por vós!

kept as an option under quam multiplíciter tibi caro mea — the conjunction makes the verbless colon sayable (stylist); grammar, D2

8b

minor

“a minha alma apegou-se a vós, seguindo-vos” adhǽsit ánima mea post te

'post te' is a single spatial phrase (clung on behind you, following after you); the Portuguese renders it twice, as the object of 'apegou-se a' and again as 'seguindo-vos', which slightly expands the Latin.

proposeda minha alma apegou-se atrás de vós

kept as an option under adhǽsit ánima mea post te — *apegar-se atrás de* is not Portuguese; the participle was his own v1 fix

A faithful rendering of the Gallican text: Septuagintal readings (de luce, quam multipliciter, super vitas, in sancto apparui, partes vulpium, in manus gladii) are kept, and tenses, voices and moods match throughout. Pointing marks agree with the Latin in every verse. Only two small expansions are noted, neither altering sense.

Verse by verse

2a

*Deus, Deus meus → Deus, meu Deus*, as 21:2 and 42:5 (the vocative without *ó*, as the finished psalms). The colon is the Lauds antiphon. checks: the second colon is +4 by the counter, which does not elide *desde‿a‿aurora*; the first colon is −3, so the verse is level with the Latin.

2b

*sitíre in → ter sede de*, the perfect kept (*teve*), as the settled row (D42; 41:3). checks (v2): *vós … vós* at mediant and final is flagged as rhyme — the Latin has the same echo (*ánima mea … caro mea*) and the same object (*in te … tibi*); accepted.

3

*virtus → poder* (row). The ‡ after the first colon kept. The first colon is verbless, as the Latin. checks: the second colon +4 (*me apresentei a vós*, decision `apparui`); *vos apareci* would save it at the cost of an apparition — accepted. The ambiguity reader did not hear the comparison *as in the desert, so in the holy place*: it is the Latin's *sic* with the comparison unstated; not supplied (D2: an ambiguity the Latin leaves).

4

*laudáre → louvar*. *Quóniam → Porque*.

5

*benedícere* (man → God) *→ bendizer* with the accusative (*vos bendirei*). *leváre → levantar* (row; 118:48 *levantei as minhas mãos*). *in nómine tuo → no vosso nome* (53:3).

6

*exsultátio → exultação* (row; 41:5b *com voz de exultação*). *exultação* listed as unknown again by the ambiguity reader (the row records it; kept).

7

*Si memor fui* kept conditional (*Se me lembrei*), as the Latin and DRB; MS1932 flattens to a statement. *memor esse → lembrar-se de*, *meditári in → meditar em* (rows). *adjútor → auxílio* (row, 14/14); *fuísti → fostes*. The versicle *In matutínis, Dómine, meditábor in te. R. Quia fuísti adjútor meus* reads from the two colons. The ambiguity reader found the conditional *Se me lembrei* odd and heard "whenever" as a second reading; the Latin's *Si* is kept (DRB *If*). He listed *auxílio* as unknown (the row, 14/14; kept).

8b

*suscípere* (persons) *→ amparar* (D19); *a vossa direita me amparou* = 17:36a word for word. The colon *Me suscépit déxtera tua* is a Lauds antiphon (grep). *exsultáre → exultar*. checks (v2): the flex colon +5 and closing on the enclitic *seguindo-vos* (stress on *-guin-*) — the cost of keeping *post te* (decision `post`); accepted.

10

*quǽrere ánimam → buscar a minha alma* (row; 53:5), with the row's known cost (heard as the spiritual soul before 'my life'). *vero → porém*, repeated in 62:12 as the Latin repeats it. *trádere → entregar* (row). checks: the last colon +4; it carries two Latin members (*tradéntur in manus gládii · partes vúlpium erunt*), neither can go — accepted.

12

*lætári in → alegrar-se em*; *laudári → ser louvado* (row; 33:3): the Latin's passive (ἐπαινεσθήσεται), not MS1932's *se gloriará*. checks: the last colon +4 (*coisas iníquas* for one neuter plural) — accepted. *iníquas* listed as unknown by the ambiguity reader; kept (the *iníquus* row). The ambiguity reader heard *por ele* first as God — the Latin leaves God and the king open.
