Verse by verse

1

The Latin follows the LXX ('in the help', not 'in the shelter'); kept. protectióne → proteção, so that prótegam in 90:14 (protegerei) answers it, as in the Latin. 'Altíssimo' before the mediant is proparoxytone, exactly like 'Altíssimi' in the Latin.

2

The quoted man says 'es tu' to God — so 'Vós sois' — and then 'in eum' of God in the third person: the jump is the Latin's and is kept. sperábo in → esperar em (glossary). The ambiguity reader heard 'nele esperarei' (and 'esperarás' in 90:4, 'esperou em mim' in 90:14) first as waiting for God to act, and only second as trusting: that is the known cost of the glossary's 'esperar em', not of this psalm — flagged in glossary.md for whoever rules on speráre.

3

liberávit is a perfect in the Latin (future in the LXX): 'livrou'. 'a verbo áspero' is a hard Septuagintal reading (the LXX read dabar 'word' for deber 'pestilence'); kept as 'da palavra áspera', with MS1932, pt-PT and DRB ('sharp word'). Both cadences are proparoxytone in Portuguese as in Latin (áspero / áspera).

4

From here the just man is addressed: tibi, sperábis → te, esperarás (tu). 'sob' rather than 'debaixo de' only for length. sperábis kept as 'esperarás' (not 'te abrigarás', which is the Hebrew).

5

Subject moved to the front (the Latin has Scuto … véritas ejus); 'com um escudo' is the ablative of means — MS1932's 'como um escudo' makes it a comparison. véritas → verdade, not the Hebrew-based 'fidelidade'. First colon +3 by checks.py; 'com‿um' runs together in the mouth. 'temor noturno' and not 'terror noturno', which is a medical term. The verse ends on a comma, as in the Latin, because 90:6 depends on it.

6

Three hard readings kept: the unnamed 'thing' that walks in the dark, the 'investida' as a terror of its own (the Latin's comma and 'et' kept), and the noonday demon. The ambiguity reader heard 'a coisa' as 'a threatening presence that walks in the dark', which is the effect wanted; it heard 'a investida, e o demônio' either as two things or as the demon's attack — the Latin's 'ab incúrsu, et dæmónio' is open in the same way. † colon −4 after 'perambula' → 'anda' (see the decision). Rhyme flag dia / meio-dia accepted (see 'volante' and 'meridiano').

7

The subject of appropinquábit is unexpressed in the Latin (MS1932 supplies '(a morte)'); Portuguese leaves it unexpressed too. appropinquáre is the same verb in 90:7 and 90:10 → 'aproximar-se' in both. The first two cola run 2 syllables short of the Latin, within range.

8

considerábis → contemplarás (MS1932, pt-PT): 'considerar' in Portuguese is to think over, not to look. Verb moved before its object in the second colon.

9

The turn in mid-verse is kept: 'vós sois' to God, 'tu puseste … teu' to the man. In Latin both halves are 'tu' and nothing marks the change; the vós/tu system forces the translator's hand here, and the project's reading (second half to the man) is the one that makes sense of 'refúgium tuum'. MS1932 bridges it with '(disseste)'; not taken. The ambiguity reader noted that, the change of addressee being unannounced, both halves may still be heard as spoken to the Lord: that is how the Latin sounds too, and 'tu … teu' against 'vós' is as much marking as a translation may add.

10

Natural order (subject first) in the first colon; the Latin has the verb first. flagéllum → flagelo (DRB 'scourge'): in Portuguese it is both the whip and the calamity, as wide as the Latin.

11

mandávit is a perfect (future in the LXX): 'deu ordem'. custodíre → guardar; via → caminho.

12

'In mánibus' has no possessive in the Latin; none supplied (MS1932 and pt-PT add 'suas'). portábunt → levarão ('carregarão' is the more concrete alternative). First colon −2. Second colon revised in draft 2 on the Latinist's and the stylist's remarks (see the decision).

13

áspide and basilisco kept, with leão and dragão: the Latin's four beasts, not the Hebrew's. Verb moved to the front. leão / dragão echo inside the colon as leónem / dracónem do. The ambiguity reader listed 'áspide' and 'basilisco' as words a hearer may not know; they are kept on purpose — the Latin names two strange beasts, and the verse itself says they are things one treads on.

14

God speaks (first person) to the end of the psalm, of the man in the third person. 'eu o livrarei' rather than the mesóclise 'livrá-lo-ei' of MS1932: the brief asks for mesóclise only where it is natural, and with six futures in three verses it would not be. liberábo → livrarei matches liberávit → livrou in 90:3.

15

Both pointings respected: it sings with the mediant at ‡ (the app) or at *. exáudiam → escutarei (glossary). '-rei' at ‡ and at the end is flagged as a rhyme by checks.py: accepted, because the Latin has the identical echo (exáudiam eum … glorificábo eum). 'cum ipso sum' keeps its order: 'com ele estou'.

16

salutáre meum → 'a minha salvação' (glossary, open entry; MS1932, pt-PT, DRB agree). It is the neuter τὸ σωτήριον, 'the saving thing', which the tradition also hears as a person; 'salvação' leaves that open and is not smoothed into 'socorro' or 'Salvador'. It must come out the same in the Nunc dimittis (salutáre tuum). replébo → encherei (not the Hebrew 'saciarei'). The ambiguity reader noted 'a minha salvação' could be taken as the speaker's own being saved; the Latin 'salutáre meum' has the same possessive, and the first person of God since 90:14 settles it for the hearer.
