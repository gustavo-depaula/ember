“Os meus olhos estavam nos fiéis da terra, para se sentarem comigo” Óculi mei ad fidéles terræ ut sédeant mecum

The Latin clause is verbless; the supplied copula 'estavam' fixes a past tense the Latin leaves open (the purpose clause has present subjunctive 'sédeant'). Allowed as a supplied copula, but a tenseless rendering would keep the Latin's openness.

proposedOs meus olhos, sobre os fiéis da terra, para se sentarem comigo

kept as an option under Óculi mei ad fidéles terræ ut sédeant mecum — The copula is allowed (he says so); the second colon is past; his tenseless line is an option.

2

fails: sayable

“Entoarei salmos, e entenderei no caminho imaculado,”

The comma before 'e' breaks the colon into two short gasps and makes 'entenderei' sound like a new clause starting cold. The mediant colon is long, and the pause lands in the wrong place.

proposedEntoarei salmos e entenderei no caminho imaculado, * quando vierdes a mim.

❧ taken

6

fails: native, sayable

“Os meus olhos estavam nos fiéis da terra, para se sentarem comigo”

'estavam nos fiéis' is flat and odd: eyes aren't 'in' people. The personal infinitive 'para se sentarem' is heavy and bureaucratic. The colon also runs past one breath.

proposedOs meus olhos estavam postos nos fiéis da terra, para que se sentassem comigo: * quem andava no caminho imaculado, esse me servia.

❧ taken — 'postos' taken; 'para que se sentassem' refused for length (option) — the blind reader heard the infinitive rightly.

7

fails: native, plain

“não seguiu reto à vista dos meus olhos”

'seguir reto' reads like a street direction ('keep straight on'), not a moral image. The ear stops on it. 'Andar reto' is the natural Portuguese idiom and keeps the image of a straight course without choosing a reading.

proposedquem fala coisas iníquas não andou reto à vista dos meus olhos.

kept as an option under non diréxit in conspéctu oculórum meórum — 'andar' is ambuláre's verb in 100:6; 58:5 'segui reto' for the same Latin.

3

fails: plain

“odiei os que cometiam transgressões”

'cometiam transgressões' is a long, legal-sounding ending with a heavy nasal final. It is acceptable but stiffer than the rest of the verse.

proposedEu não punha diante dos meus olhos coisa injusta: * odiei os que praticavam transgressões.

kept as an option under faciéntes prævaricatiónes — 'praticar' is operári's verb (100:8).

A plain, sober psalm that mostly speaks like native Portuguese prayer. The short verses (2b, 5, 5b) are sturdy and chant well, with good paroxytone cadences. The weak spots are the few places where a literal preposition or verb ('estavam nos fiéis', 'seguiu reto') sounds translated, not prayed.

6

minor

“Os meus olhos estavam postos nos fiéis da terra” Óculi mei ad fidéles terræ

The Latin clause is verbless; supplying a copula is allowed, but 'postos' adds a participle beyond the bare copula. Past tense is defensible from the surrounding imperfects (ministrábat).

proposedOs meus olhos estavam sobre os fiéis da terra, para se sentarem comigo:

kept as an option under Óculi mei ad fidéles terræ ut sédeant mecum — The participle belongs to the copula and adds no sense; it answers the stylist's fault ('eyes in people').

7

minor

“não seguiu reto à vista dos meus olhos” non diréxit in conspéctu oculórum meórum

Intransitive dírigo (LXX κατεύθυνεν) means 'went well, prospered, held straight/firm' — 'seguiu reto' reads as a path of movement and is a little opaque; the sense is that such a one did not succeed/stand before the speaker.

proposedquem fala coisas iníquas não prosperou à vista dos meus olhos.

kept as an option under non diréxit in conspéctu oculórum meórum — 58:5 'segui reto' for the same Latin; the image and both senses kept.

A faithful, close rendering: tenses (imperfects of habitual conduct, future habitábit, perfect odívi/diréxit) and the Septuagintal readings (psallam et intéllegam, declinántem a me malígnum non cognoscébam, in matutíno) are kept. Only two minor points, the verbless clause of v. 6 and the sense of intransitive diréxit in v. 7. Pointing marks match the Latin throughout (a single * in each verse, no † or ‡).

Verse by verse

1

The article supplied before both nouns (the object fronted, as in the Latin). 'eu vos cantarei': the subject named so the colon does not open on a clitic. cantáre → cantar here, psállere → 'entoar salmos' in the next verse (D25) — the two verbs kept apart as the Latin keeps them. Responsory R. of Epi2-6 opens with this verse.

2

psállere → 'Entoarei salmos' (D25). immaculátus → 'imaculado' (glossary; 17:33, 118:1); 'no caminho imaculado' is repeated identically at 100:6, as the Latin repeats 'in via immaculáta'. The Lauds antiphon (Psalmi major, Day3 Laudes1) 'Tibi, Dómine, psallam, * et intéllegam in via immaculáta' would read 'A vós, Senhor, entoarei salmos, * e entenderei no caminho imaculado'. v2: the comma after 'salmos' removed at the stylist's request, so the colon is said in one run (the two verbs are one sentence).

2b

perambuláre → 'andar' (the row names 100:2 for L&S's Church sense, 'walk, conduct oneself'). The versicle of Epi2-6 reads it alone; it stands alone.

3

'Eu' named: 'punha' is also third person. rem injústam → 'coisa injusta', no article (MS1932's wording). prævaricatiónes → 'transgressões', the noun of 118:119 'transgressores'.

4

adhærére → 'apegar-se a' (24:21, 62:9, 72:28, 93:20). The two colons open 'Não … / eu não …', the Latin's two negatives.

5

detráhere + dative → 'difamar' (settled, D39; the row names 100:5). 'o seu próximo' moved off the mediant (a proparoxytone at a cadence — glossary row próximus), and 'em segredo' (secréto) takes the cadence. The preposed 'a esse' is the Latin's fronted 'hunc'.

5b

édere → 'comer' (glossary). The -ia imperfects at the finals of 100:5, 5b, 6 (perseguia, comia, servia) echo as the Latin's -bar / -bam / -bat do: accepted as the Latin's own echo.

6

'os fiéis da terra' as 118:119 'os pecadores da terra', and 100:8 below. ministráre → 'servir' (DRB, MS1932 'esse me servia'); servíre's imperative is 'Sede servos' (D18), and the two Latin verbs never meet in a verse. 'quem andava' for the participle 'ámbulans', in step with 'ministrábat'. The ambiguity reader heard 'esse me servia' also as colloquial 'that one suited me'; the servant sense came first, and the second colon's 'hic mihi ministrábat' is plain enough in context — kept.

7

fácere supérbiam → 'agir com soberba' (row, 30:24). loqui iníqua → 'falar coisas iníquas' (the neuter plural as 25:4 'coisas iníquas'). The future 'Não habitará' kept: the Latin moves from past to future here. The ambiguity reader found the switch from 'habitará' to the past 'seguiu' confusing: it is the Latin's (habitábit / diréxit), kept.

8

in matutíno → 'Pela manhã', as 48:15b (in matutíno), kept apart from mane → 'de manhã'. dispérdere → 'exterminar' (glossary). operántes iniquitátem → 'os que praticam a iniquidade' (formula, 5:7a, 93:16). 'omnes peccatóres terræ' = 118:119 'todos os pecadores da terra'. 'para exterminar' for ut + subjunctive with the same subject (grammar).
