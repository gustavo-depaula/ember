minor

“Todos os dias” Per síngulos dies

The distributive 'síngulos' means each day, one by one. 'Todos os dias' turns it into a collective and loses that sense.

proposedCada dia vos bendirei

kept as an option under Per síngulos dies — The row's 'todos os dias' (7:12, 41:11b); the three places change together or not at all.

minor

“o poder dos vossos feitos terríveis” virtútem terribílium tuórum

'terribílium tuórum' is a substantive adjective ('your terrible things'). Adding 'feitos' names what the Latin leaves unnamed. It is defensible, but closer renderings are available.

proposedE dirão o poder das vossas coisas terríveis

kept as an option under virtútem terribílium tuórum — The sense is acts (DRB), heard rightly by the ambiguity reader; 'coisas' vaguer.

The translation is faithful and complete throughout. Tense, mood and person are preserved, including the future 'fáciet' in v.19, the jussives in v.10 and the subjunctive 'benedícat' in v.21. The image in 'eructábunt' is kept. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse, including the ‡ and * in v.19. There are only two minor points, both about closeness: the distributive 'per síngulos dies' in v.2 and the supplied noun 'feitos' in v.6.

4

fails: sayable

“e declararão a vossa potência”

'declararão' stacks -ra-ra-rão right after 'louvará'. The tongue trips on it and the colon opens on a clatter of r's. 'Declarar' also sounds like a notary's verb for pronuntiabunt.

proposede anunciarão a vossa potência

kept as an option under et poténtiam tuam pronuntiábunt — anunciar is annuntiáre's (D41).

5

fails: sayable

“Falarão da magnificência da glória da vossa santidade”

Three 'da' genitives in a row on top of the five-syllable 'magnificência' make a long, flat run that is hard to fit in one breath at recitation pace. The Latin chain has to stay, so the only relief is in rhythm. If an adjective for magnificentia is judged too free, keep the chain as it is. It is heavy but correct.

proposedFalarão da glória magnífica da vossa santidade

not taken — Turns the Latin's noun into an adjective and breaks the mirror with 144:12; recorded in the decision's why.

12

fails: native

“Para fazerem conhecer”

'fazer conhecer' is a calque of 'notam facere'. The Brazilian idiom is 'dar a conhecer', which is plainer and falls more easily into the tone.

proposedPara darem a conhecer aos filhos dos homens a vossa potência

kept as an option under Ut notam fáciant — The notum fácere row (97:2, 76:15, 15:11), rule 6.

19

fails: plain

“e escutará a prece deles”

The colloquial 'deles' lands flat on the flex. It is correct, but it lowers the register just where the verse turns.

proposede atenderá a sua prece

kept as an option under et deprecatiónem eórum exáudiet — atender is inténdere's (D3); 'sua' could be the Lord's.

20

fails: sayable, plain

“e exterminará”

The hiatus e-ex jars. 'Exterminar' also carries modern pest-control and genocide overtones that are louder than dispérdet.

proposede destruirá todos os pecadores

kept as an option under et omnes peccatóres dispérdet — The dispérdere row, fifteen verses.

21

fails: native

“A minha boca falará o louvor do Senhor”

'falar' with a direct abstract object ('falar o louvor') is not Portuguese usage. The ear expects 'falar de' or a different verb.

proposedA minha boca dirá o louvor do Senhor

kept as an option under Laudatiónem Dómini loquétur os meum — loqui / dícere kept apart in this psalm (λαλέω / ἐρῶ); 121:8 row.

The psalm mostly sounds good in the mouth. The short antithetical verses (8, 13b, 14, 16, 17, 18) are plain and fall well to the tone, and the vós address is steady. The weak spots are the Latin genitive chains in 5 and 7, which are long but faithful, and a few calqued verb constructions (fazer conhecer, falar o louvor, declarar) that give away the translation.

Verse by verse

1

No titulus: DO's Latin file begins at 'Exaltábo te' (D7); the Greek's αἴνεσις τῷ Δαυιδ and MS1932's 'Louvor do mesmo David' are not rendered. 'in sǽculum, et in sǽculum sǽculi' → 'para sempre, e pelos séculos dos séculos' = 20:5 and the formula row (D23, D27); the same in 144:2, 144:21. 'Exaltábo te' → 'Eu vos exaltarei' = 29:2, 117:28a.

2

'Per síngulos dies' → 'todos os dias' (the row; 7:12, 41:11b). The -éculos rhyme of 144:1/144:2 finals is the Latin's own (both end 'in sǽculum sǽculi') — accepted.

3

Copied from 47:2 = 95:4 ('Grande é o Senhor, e muito digno de louvor'; D43). 'non est finis' → 'não tem fim': a verb Portuguese prefers (grammar, D2); the Latin's 'finis' stays 'fim'.

5

'loquéntur' + accusative → 'falarão da …' (Portuguese needs the preposition). 'mirabília' → 'maravilhas' (70:17). 'sanctitátis' → 'santidade' (29:5).

6

'dícere' → 'dizer' (with an object, as 28:9 'dirão glória'), kept apart from 'loqui → falar' and 'narráre → narrar' — the Latin varies three verbs of telling across 144:5–6 and 144:11, and so does the Portuguese. 'virtus' → 'poder' (the row); 'magnitúdo' → 'grandeza' (70:8, 78:11).

7

'abundántia' → 'abundância' (the row). 'justítia tua exsultábunt' (ablative) → 'exultarão na vossa justiça' (the D44 build, 50:16).

8

Copied from 102:8 word for word, as the miserátor row asks — though the Latin differs by one word: 102:8 has 'longánimis', 144:8 'pátiens'. Both are μακρόθυμος and both are 'paciente' by the row (D15's Greek test), so identical Portuguese is right here.

9

'univérsis' → 'para com todos' (MS1932): the dative of interest. 'miseratiónes' → 'compaixões' (the row); the copula 'estão' supplied.

10

The build of 137:4 ('Que vos deem graças, Senhor, todos os reis da terra'); D5. 'sancti' (ὅσιοι) → 'santos' (the row).

12

'notam fácere' → 'fazer conhecer' (the notum fácere row, 97:2). 'poténtiam tuam': the Latin's 'your' kept against the Hebrew's 'his'.

13a

'Regnum tuum regnum' — the repeated noun kept, 'um reino' supplied with the copula. 'in omni generatióne et generatiónem' → 'de geração em geração', copied from 44:18a as the formula row asks ('omni' absorbed). 'dominátio' → 'domínio' (102:22). Soft check: the mediant word 'séculos' is proparoxytone (Latin 'sæculórum' paroxytone) — accepted: the noun is the Latin's, and every rewording that moves it (e.g. 'de todos os séculos é o vosso reino') breaks the repeated 'reino … reino'. The second colon is 9 syllables short of the Latin because it copies the 44:18a formula.

13b

The Gallican verse that the Hebrew (MT) lacks: the Hebrew file above has no line for it; the Greek (13a in Rahlfs) has it; the Latin follows the Greek. Built exactly as 144:17 so that the identical second colons read identically ('e santo em todas as suas obras'). 'verbis' → 'palavras' (verbum).

15

144:15–16 are the grace before meals (Benedictio mensae, DO appendix) and the Gradual of Corpus Christi (with 'illis'): circulation.md. 'escam illórum' → 'lhes dais o seu alimento' — the genitive as a dative pronoun plus possessive (grammar); 'alimento' is the esca row's word for food God gives (135:25). The explicit 'tu' kept as 'vós'. 'Os olhos de todos esperam em vós, Senhor' is also the wording of every Brazilian text seen (fetched).

16

'Áperis tu' → 'Vós abris' (pronoun kept; = the CNBB lectionary's words, fetched). 'abris' is indicative, so the ouvi/abri ban does not apply.

17

'Justus' → 'justo' (the row); 'viis' → 'caminhos'. Second colon = 144:13b.

18

The repetition 'ómnibus invocántibus eum … ómnibus invocántibus eum' kept word for word. Latin order ('Prope est Dóminus') kept.

19

Pointing: the Latin has ‡ and * with no †; reproduced. Subject unexpressed, as in the Latin (the Lord of 144:18). 'salvos fáciet' → 'salvará' (the salvum fácere row).

20

'dispérdere' → 'exterminar' (the row; 142:11b). 'dilígere' → 'amar'.

21

'laudátio' → 'louvor' (110:10b).
