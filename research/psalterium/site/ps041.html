4

minor

“As minhas lágrimas foram o meu pão” Fuérunt mihi lácrimæ meæ panes

The Latin has the plural 'panes' (against the LXX singular ἄρτος); the Portuguese singularizes it, changing number and smoothing a Latin peculiarity the rule says to keep.

proposedAs minhas lágrimas foram os meus pães de dia e de noite

not taken — *comer / ter por pão* is the Portuguese idiom; *os meus pães* is heard as loaves; number is grammar (D2, D29), as 40:10. Option 3.

7

minor

“e do Hermon” et Hermóniim

'Hermoniim' is a plural transliteration (the Hermons / Hermon heights), a Septuagintal oddity; reducing it to the singular 'Hermon' smooths it and changes number.

proposede dos Hermons, desde o pequeno monte

not taken — The Latin transliterates a name (Ερμωνιιμ); it does not translate a plural, and a Latin reader hears a name. *dos Hermons* is option 3, *de Hermoniim* option 2.

A close, faithful rendering: tenses (perfects mandavit, exprobraverunt; futures transibo, memor ero, confitebor), persons and the Latin's images are preserved, and the pointing marks (: or , plus a single * in each verse, no † or ‡) match the Latin throughout. The only faults are two number changes that smooth Latin peculiarities: 'panes' made singular and the plural 'Hermoniim' reduced to 'Hermon'.

“quando chegarei, e aparecerei diante da face de Deus?”

Two future verbs ending in -rei stand side by side (chegarei / aparecerei), and 'aparecerei' is five syllables that roll into 'diante'. The mouth stumbles on -rei, e a-pa-re-ce-rei di-. 'Chegarei' is also a weaker match for 'véniam' than the plainer 'irei'.

minor

Verse by verse

2

quemádmodum … ita → *Assim como … assim* (the comparison; the quemádmodum row). The vocative *Deus* → *ó Deus*, as elsewhere. fons → *fonte*; *as fontes das águas* as 17:16a. Uses: a Mass Gradual (missa/Sancti/06-04), a versicle (04-16 Labre), Hildegard's office.

3

apparére → *aparecer* (16:15). *ante fáciem Dei* → *diante da face de Deus* (fácies row). The Easter Vigil tract sings 41:2–4 (missa/Tempora/Quad6-6).

4

die ac nocte → *de dia e de noite*. *Ubi est Deus tuus?* → *Onde está o teu Deus?* — the scoffers speak to one man: *tu*. Same words in 41:11b.

5

recordári → *lembrar-se de*; *Hæc* (plural) → *destas coisas* (MS1932), put after the verb (order, D2). transíre in → *passar a*; the verb returns in 41:8b (*passaram sobre mim*), as *transíbo … transiérunt*. tabernáculum → *tenda* (row, open; 14:1); admirábilis → *admirável* (row, which names this verse). usque ad → *até*. quóniam → *porque* (the Latin's causal; DRB 'for'). The blind reader heard *tenda* as tent, helped by *casa de Deus*, with the shop sense possible; the row stands.

5b

in voce → *com voz de*: the sound that goes with the procession of 41:5. vox → *voz* both here and in 41:8.

6

The refrain; 41:11b's second colon and 42:5's are the same words. contúrbas → *perturbas* (conturbáre row).

6b

speráre in → *esperar em* (D36); the imperative is *tu* (to the soul). adhuc → *ainda*. confitéri → *dar graças a* (D5), with *lhe* for *illi*. 41:6b = 41:12 = 42:6; it closes the prayers at the foot of the altar (missa/Ordo/Ordo.txt).

7

memor ero → *me lembrarei de* (future, as the Latin; MS1932's *me lembrei* is not the Latin's tense). *de terra … a monte* → *desde … desde*. *módicus* → *pequeno*: *módico* in Portuguese is 'modest in price'; the adjective before the noun (order, D2). The blind reader heard *desde* as place first (time second).

8

cataráctæ → *cataratas* (the floodgates, as 'as cataratas do céu' of the Flood); *tuárum* → *vossas*.

8b

fluctus → *ondas* (new row). *super me transiérunt* → *passaram sobre mim* (natural order).

9

In die … nocte → *De dia … de noite* (as 41:4). *cánticum ejus* → *o seu cântico*: 'his' left open as the Latin (the Lord's song, or a song to him — DRB 'to him').

9b

suscéptor → *amparo* (D19). *dicam* → *direi*. The words said to God are *vós*; *Vós* named as subject so the short line has its weight. Deus vitæ meæ → *o Deus da minha vida*.

10

oblivísci → *esquecer* with a direct object (row; 12:1). *contristátus incédo* → *ando entristecido* — 37:7 *contristátus ingrediébar → andava entristecido*. 42:2 has *tristis incédo* (another adjective) and should say *ando triste*. afflígere → *afligir* (row); *dum* → *enquanto* in all four verses (41:4, 10, 11, 11b). Ps 42 v2 has *e por que ando triste, enquanto me aflige o inimigo?* — the *dum* clause identical.

11

confríngere → *quebrar* (row). exprobráre → *afrontar* (row; 34:7, 118:42). tribuláre → *atribular* (row). *qui tríbulant me inimíci mei* in natural order: *os meus inimigos que me atribulam*. The Latin's tenses (present, then perfect) kept.

11b

per síngulos dies → *todos os dias* (row, which names this verse). After a question mark inside the verse the next word stays lower-case, as the Latin prints it.
