Draft 2 — draft. Pray it aloud first; then decide below. Every choice you make rewrites the psalm here, so what you pray is what you chose. Nothing is final until you say so.

Dotted gold marks words still waiting for your decision — touch one to choose its wording where it stands. A gold wash marks where your choice departs from draft 2.

Each verse from the Latin down to what is prayed. Between one draft and the next, words that left are struck through and words that came carry the gold wash; beside them, who objected and what the audit says. A verse no draft touched folds to one line — open it to see its strata.

The translation follows the Latin closely in every verse: tenses (sperabam, finies, clamabo, meditabor, recogitabo, corripies, vivificabis), persons, the subjectless succidit and contrivit, the doubled vivens vivens, and Jerome's inferi/infernus/lacum are all kept without Hebrew smoothing. fails: sayable

“Não olharei mais para o homem, * nem para o habitante do repouso.”

The doubled 'para o' makes both cola sound padded and prepositional; 'olhar para' is weaker than the Latin's plain transitive 'aspiciam'.

proposedNão verei mais o homem, * nem o habitante do repouso.

kept as an option — “verei mais … o”, under Non aspíciam hóminem ultra, * et habitatórem

38:13

fails: native

“e enrolada para longe de mim”

'enrolada para longe' is a motion phrase stuck to a verb that doesn't move; the ear hears a calque of 'a me'. 'para' also adds a slack syllable before the mediant.

proposedA minha geração foi tirada, e enrolada longe de mim, * como uma tenda de pastores.

❧ taken

38:14

fails: sayable

“A minha vida foi cortada como por um tecelão: enquanto eu ainda urdia, ceifou-me:”

The first colon is two full clauses without a breathing mark; the passive opening 'A minha vida foi cortada' front-loads weight. Following the Latin order (verb first, 'vita mea' after the simile) makes the image land and eases the breath.

proposedFoi cortada como por um tecelão a minha vida: enquanto eu ainda urdia, ceifou-me: * desde a manhã até a tarde acabareis comigo.

kept as an option — “acabareis comigo”, under de mane usque ad vésperam fínies me

Verse by verse

38:10

DO's first line, *(Canticum Ezechiæ \* Isa 38:10-24)*, is a heading with a book reference, not a prayed verse and not a titulus; readVerses skips it, as for 233, 211, 213, and D7 keeps it untranslated. For delivery to DO, its Portuguese heading can be *(Cântico de Ezequias \* Is 38:10-24)*. DO's ids 38:10–38:24 are the canticle's own line numbers; the text is Isa 38:10–20 of the Vulgate (map at the head of `consult/parallels/ps222.md`). The Latin is Jerome's Isaiah, from the Hebrew, not the Gallican psalter; where the Hebrew is obscure and Jerome read it otherwise (38:11 *quæsívi*, 38:12 *quiétis*, 38:13 *generátio*, 38:20 *in pace*), the Latin is translated as it stands. *Vadam* → *irei* (DRB *I shall go*; MS1932 *irei*). The Latin has no quotation marks and neither does the Portuguese: where Hezekiah's remembered words end is left as open as the Latin leaves it.

38:11

*Dóminum Deum* → *o Senhor Deus*. *In terra vivéntium* → *na terra dos vivos*, the formula of 26:13, 51:7 and 141:6 (the LH's *terra dos viventes* not taken).

38:12

*Ultra* → *mais* after the verb (82:5 *non memorétur … ultra → não seja mais lembrado*).

38:13

*Tabernáculum → tenda* (the glossary). *Quasi tabernáculum pastórum* → *como uma tenda de pastores* (MS1932's words; the plural *pastórum* kept against the LH's *de um pastor*).

38:14

DO marks this verse with the mediant only (no flex); DO's pointing is followed. v2 follows the Latin's order, verb first and *a minha vida* after the simile (the stylist). *Velut a texénte → como por um tecelão* (MS1932). *Dum adhuc ordírer → enquanto eu ainda urdia*: *ordíri* is to lay the warp, begin a web (L&S), and *urdir* is its Portuguese; the weaving image stays whole. The first colon is long (the Latin's is too, ~27 syllables), with a natural breath at the colon.

38:15

*Sperábam* → *Eu esperava* (the *speráre* row without its *in*; no object in the Latin). DO's comma before the mediant becomes a colon, so that *até a manhã* is not read with *como um leão*. *Contérere → quebrar* (3:8, 104:16, 106:16). *Quasi leo sic* → *como um leão, assim*, both words kept; the subject of *contrívit* is not named, as in the Latin.

38:16

The first colon is 38:14's second, word for word. *Pullus → filhote* (the glossary; 83:4). *Clamábo → clamarei*; *colúmba → pomba* (54:7).

38:17

Eight and eight syllables against eleven and nine; the Latin's words are long, the line is not thin.

38:18

*Vim pátior → sofro violência* (37:12b *vim faciébant → faziam violência*). *Respónde* → *respondei* — the *vós* imperative of an *-er* verb, not the homograph of *respondi* (rule 3). The person shifts from *vós* (God, addressed) to *ele* (God, spoken of: *ipse*), as the Latin does; kept.

38:19

*Amaritúdo → amargura* (9:28). *Ánima mea → a minha alma*.

38:20

The antiphon DO draws from this verse (Tuesday Lauds II: *Corrípies me, Dómine, \* et vivificábis me*) can be said as *Vós me castigareis, Senhor, \* e me vivificareis*. *Corrípere → castigar* (D21); *vivificáre → vivificar* (137:7, 142:11a *vós me vivificareis*). *Si sic vívitur* → *se assim se vive* (the impersonal passive as the Portuguese impersonal). The verbless *et in tálibus vita spíritus mei* takes a supplied *se … está* (the *si* governs both clauses; grammar). The subject *vós* is named because the preceding verse ended in the third person (*ipse*). DO has no flex in this verse, so the long first colon (the Latin's is 32 syllables) keeps its one breath at the comma after *espírito*. v2: the comma before *e me vivificareis* dropped (the stylist): the Latin's pair runs as one; the *-areis* echo is the Latin's own parallel.

38:21

*Tu autem → Vós, porém* (3:4, 39:12, 101:13). *Éruere* without a source → *libertar* (the row's rule: 6:5 *éripe ánimam meam → libertai a minha alma*, 68:19, 118:153); the Lectionary's refrain says *livrastes*, which is *liberáre*'s. *Ut non períret → para que não perecesse*. *Projícere → lançar* (30:23, 49:17 *lançaste para trás*); *post tergum tuum → para trás das vossas costas* (MS1932, with *tergum* the back).

38:22

*Confitéri → dar graças a* (D5), *laudáre → louvar*, *exspectáre → aguardar* (D24, D36), *descendéntes in lacum → os que descem à cova* (the *lacus* formula: 27:1, 29:4, 87:5, 142:7b), *véritas → verdade*. The Latin fronts *non inférnus* and ends on *veritátem tuam*; the Portuguese keeps the object at the end (*a vossa verdade*), which is where the Latin has it.

38:23

*Sicut et ego hódie → como também eu hoje*: *et* = also. *Notam fácere → fazer conhecer* (the glossary; 76:15, 105:8, 144:12 *para fazerem conhecer aos filhos dos homens*). *Pater fíliis* → *o pai … aos filhos*. v2: the stylist asked *dará a conhecer*, the fourth stylist to ask it (97, 144, 221); the row *notum fácere → fazer conhecer* keeps it, rule 6.

38:24

*Salvum me fac → salvai-me* (the formula). *Psalmos … cantábimus → cantaremos os nossos salmos*: here the Latin itself says *cantáre psalmos*, so *entoar salmos* (D25, for *psállere*) is not wanted. *Cunctis diébus vitæ nostræ → todos os dias da nossa vida* (22:6, 26:4, 127:5 for *ómnibus diébus*, with *meæ/tuæ*; the Latin's two words for 'all' meet no Portuguese pair). *In domo Dómini → na casa do Senhor*. The mediant colon is short (5 against 7): the formula; the antiphon *Cunctis diébus vitæ nostræ salvos nos fac, Dómine* (Tuesday Lauds, 1911 and Monastic) can be said as *Todos os dias da nossa vida salvai-nos, Senhor*.
