ambiguities (3)

2

“se firmou sobre nós”

1his mercy has been made strong / prevailed over us (was confirmed toward us) · 2his mercy settled / came to rest upon us (was placed over us) · 3his mercy was established concerning us (sobre = a respeito de)

likely heardhis mercy was established / firmly placed upon us

not taken — The heard sense is the Latin's (made firm upon us); the range of readings is the Latin's own and not closed (rule 2). The feared hearing of *confirmada* as 'proved' did not arise.

2

“a verdade do Senhor”

1the Lord's truth (true doctrine, what is true) · 2the Lord's faithfulness to his word (fidelidade)

likely heardthe Lord's truth in the doctrinal sense

not taken — *véritas → verdade* (row; 53:7 has the same finding); *fidelidade* is the Hebrew-based reading and would close what the Latin leaves open.

2

“Porque”

1causal: praise him because his mercy... · 2start of a new independent statement (for / indeed)

likely heardcausal: the reason for the praise in 116:1

not taken — Heard causal, which is *quóniam*'s sense here; no change needed.

2

minor

“se firmou” confirmáta est

The Latin is a perfect passive ('has been confirmed / made firm'), keeping God as the implied agent. The pronominal 'se firmou' reads as middle voice, meaning the mercy grew firm by itself. It is defensible Portuguese for the result state, but it shifts the voice.

proposedPorque foi confirmada sobre nós a sua misericórdia: * e a verdade do Senhor permanece para sempre.

kept as an option under confirmáta est super nos misericórdia ejus — Held: the Portuguese pronominal is the ordinary way to say the Greek/Latin passive of state without a named agent (87:8 *Sobre mim se firmou o vosso furor* = *confirmátus est super me*, the same build, passed its readers); the proposed *foi confirmada* is the cognate the decision refuses because Brazilian ears hear 'proved' (the CNBB's own *comprovado*). *foi firmada* (passive, the row's verb) and *foi confirmada* stay options 2 and 1 — for Gustavo if he wants the voice over the idiom.

The translation is faithful and complete. Every word of the Latin is accounted for, and 'in ætérnum' and 'manet' are rendered exactly. Each verse has one mediant (*), as in the Latin. The only point is the passive 'confirmáta est', which becomes a pronominal middle voice; that is minor.

Verse by verse

1

*laudáre → louvar* (kept apart from *confitéri → dar graças*, D5), repeated in both cola as the Latin repeats *laudáte* — the Greek varies (αἰνεῖτε / ἐπαινέσατε) and DM1962, from the Hebrew, varies (*celebrai*); the Latin's repetition is kept (rule 2). *gentes → nações*, *pópuli → povos* (glossary rows; *todas as nações* as 48:2, 46:2). *Louvai* and *louvai-o* are safe at the *vós* imperative (past *louvei*). Plural humans → *vós*. The Latin's colon at the end is kept (the sentence runs on into 116:2).

2

*quóniam → porque*. The Latin's order is kept: the mercy stands at the mediant and the truth at the close, as in the Latin; *sobre nós* after the verb as *super nos*. *misericórdia → misericórdia* (row; the CNBB's *amor* and Almeida's *benignidade* are the Hebrew's). *véritas Dómini → a verdade do Senhor* (row: not the Hebrew-based *fidelidade*; the CNBB's *ele é fiel*). *manet → permanece* (as 32:11, 110:3). checks: first colon −3, second +3 on the Latin (heuristic): accepted — the first cannot grow without supplying a word, the second has nothing to cut (*e a verdade do Senhor* is the Latin's four words; *para sempre* is D23's phrase). Both antiphons of Monday Lauds are cut from this psalm (*Laudáte * Dóminum omnes gentes*; *Laudáte * Dóminum quóniam confirmáta est super nos misericórdia ejus*, DO Psalmi major): *Louvai * o Senhor, todas as nações* and *Louvai * o Senhor, porque se firmou sobre nós a sua misericórdia* read alone.
