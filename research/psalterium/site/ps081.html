minor

“e no meio deles julga os deuses” in médio autem deos dijúdicat

The adversative 'autem' is flattened to 'e', and 'deles' is supplied where the Latin has bare 'in medio'. The added pronoun is a harmless clarification, but the contrast is lost.

proposede no meio, porém, julga os deuses

not taken — the assembly is the assembly of the gods, so *no meio deles* and *in médio* name one place; bare *no meio* hangs in Portuguese, and at the end of the stylist's order (*julga os deuses no meio*) worse; δέ continues the scene. His fix *e no meio julga os deuses* is option 4, *porém* option 5.

minor

“Libertai o pobre” Erípite páuperem

'Eripite' means snatch away or rescue. 'Libertai' merges it with 'liberate' in the second half, so the Latin's two distinct verbs become near-synonyms.

proposedArrancai o pobre

❧ taken

The translation is faithful and close throughout. It keeps the Septuagintal 'aceitais as faces', the future passive 'serão abalados' and the dative 'julgai em favor do'. Only two small losses of nuance ('autem' in verse 1, 'eripite' in verse 4). The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse.

claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-22 · critic/v1.stylist.json · read prayed.vos.json

1

fails: no rhyme

“na congregação dos deuses: * e no meio deles julga os deuses.”

The same word, 'deuses', lands on both the mediant and the final, so the two halves chime flatly on the same sound. The Latin avoids this: deórum sits at the mediant and dijúdicat at the end.

proposedDeus se pôs de pé na congregação dos deuses: * e julga os deuses no meio deles.

❧ taken

2

fails: native

“e aceitais as faces dos pecadores?”

'Aceitar as faces' is a Latin calque. No Brazilian ear hears it as a phrase, and the plural 'faces' makes it sound like cheeks. The image of the face has to stay, but the verb and number can be made to sit naturally.

proposede acolheis a face dos pecadores?

kept as an option under et fácies peccatórum súmitis? — singular taken; *acolher* refused — it is the welcoming verb, and the ambiguity reader already heard welcome.

6

fails: native, sayable

“e todos filhos do Excelso.”

'Todos filhos' with no verb sounds clipped and a little telegraphic. It reads as a gloss rather than speech. Repeating the copula from the first half fills it out and keeps the paroxytone cadence on 'Excelso'.

proposede sois todos filhos do Excelso.

❧ taken

8

fails: native

“porque vós herdareis entre todas as nações.”

'Herdar entre' is an odd verb and preposition pairing, since in Portuguese one inherits something, not 'among' something. Coming right after 'Levantai-vos', the 'vós' also repeats the pronoun too closely. 'Em' keeps the Latin 'in' and is more natural.

proposedporque sois vós que herdareis em todas as nações.

kept as an option under quóniam tu hereditábis in ómnibus géntibus — *em* taken; the cleft refused — an emphasis construction for the plain *tu*.

A sober, well-weighted psalm that is mostly easy to say, with strong cadences and a clean, austere second half (vv. 4-7). The problems are local: one calque (the 'faces' of sinners), an echoed 'deuses' at mediant and final in the first verse, and a clipped, verbless half-line in v. 6.

worst line81:2

best line81:7

1

minor

“e julga os deuses no meio deles” in médio autem deos dijúdicat

'autem' is flattened to 'e', losing its light adversative/transitional force, and 'deles' is added: the Latin says only 'in the midst' (of the assembly just named), not 'in the midst of them' (the gods). The addition narrows the reference.

proposede no meio julga os deuses.

not taken — the assembly is the assembly of the gods, so *no meio deles* and *in médio* name one place; bare *no meio* hangs in Portuguese, and at the end of the stylist's order (*julga os deuses no meio*) worse; δέ continues the scene. His fix *e no meio julga os deuses* is option 4, *porém* option 5.

A close and faithful rendering: sense, tense, person and number are kept throughout, Septuagintal idioms ('aceitais a face', 'herdareis em todas as nações', 'Arrancai') are preserved rather than smoothed, and supplied copulas stay within the working rule. The only point is in 81:1, where 'deles' is added and 'autem' weakened. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse.

Verse by verse

1

v2: the stylist's order (*e julga os deuses no meio deles*) ends on *deles*; draft 1 had *deuses* at both cadences, the Latin's repetition (*deórum … deos*) put where the Latin does not put it.

3

Both cola run long (+4, +3): the first is 9:39's build (*julgar em favor do órfão*), the second the forensic *fazer justiça a* for one Latin verb; each still one breath. Accepted.

4

*liberáre → livrar* (row); the Latin's chiasm in this verse (*erípite páuperem : egénum … liberáte*) is not kept — verb-last in the second colon would read inverted; the chiasm of 81:3 is kept because there it reads naturally.

5

*movéri → ser abalado* (row, 12:5b; one Greek σαλεύω). *Nescíre → não saber*, *intellégere → entender* (row), *ambuláre → andar* (row), present *andam* as the Latin. The second colon is three syllables over the Latin; accepted.

6

*e sois todos filhos do Excelso* (v2, the stylist's): the copula of the first colon repeated, which D2 allows; draft 1's verbless *e todos filhos do Excelso* sounded clipped to him. *omnes* before *filhos* (MS1932's order). *Excelso* was listed as unknown by the ambiguity reader; kept under the row (decision `excelsi`).

8

*Surge → Levantai-vos*, as *exsúrgere* (glossary; the Greek is ἀνάστα in both); 131:8 *Surge, Dómine* should follow. *Deus* bare in the vocative (row). *julgai* is safe (past *julguei*).
