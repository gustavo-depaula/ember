claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-22 · critic/v2.latinist.json · read prayed.vos.json

1

minor

“como cabana de vigia do pomar” in pomórum custódiam

The Latin says 'into a keeping of fruits' (LXX ὀπωροφυλάκιον, a fruit-watcher's hut). 'cabana' makes the image explicit, and 'pomar' (orchard) replaces 'poma' (fruits). The image is defensible, but it could stay closer to the Latin words.

proposedpuseram Jerusalém como guarda de frutos

kept as an option under posuérunt Jerúsalem in pomórum custódiam — 'guarda de frutos' is not heard as a place at all; ὀπωροφυλάκιον is the hut; both v1 readers wanted a named thing.

10

minor

“entre as nações ... entre as nações” in géntibus ... in natiónibus

The Latin uses two different words, gentes and nationes. Rendering both as 'nações' loses that variation, though the meaning is unchanged.

proposedPara que não digam entre as gentes: Onde está o Deus deles? * e seja conhecida entre as nações, diante dos nossos olhos,

kept as an option under in géntibus … in natiónibus — one Greek word (D15); 'da gente' is barred in Brazil (the gentes row, 42:1); the Latinist himself says the meaning is unchanged.

10b

minor

“chegue à vossa presença” intróeat in conspéctu tuo

'introeat' means 'enter', not 'arrive'. 'chegue' weakens the verb slightly.

proposedentre na vossa presença o gemido dos acorrentados.

kept as an option under intróeat in conspéctu tuo — 'entre' after two prepositional 'entre' was misheard by two v1 readers; a wrong first hearing outweighs a slight weakening (D2).

The translation is faithful throughout. No content is added or dropped, and tenses, persons and the Septuagintal readings (posside, mortificatorum, septuplum in sinu) are kept. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only points worth raising are three small ones: the fruit-keeper's-hut image, gentes and nationes both becoming 'nações', and 'introeat' rendered as 'chegue'.

Verse by verse

1

Bare vocative *Deus* (D44). *gentes → nações* (glossary). *templum sanctum tuum → o vosso templo santo* (as the 5:8b formula row). *vieram … à vossa herança*: *veníre in*, not *intráre*; MS1932 the same. The verse is also the Common of Martyrs' reading (Commune/C3).

2

*escas volatílibus cæli* → *como comida às aves do céu*; the esca row names *comida* for food given (68:22). The second colon keeps the Latin's ellipsis of *posuérunt*, with *às* matching *às aves*. *carnes* plural kept for men's flesh (the carnes row). *sanctórum* (ὁσίων) → *santos* (glossary). *béstiis → feras* (glossary). The Innocents' responsory (12-28) reads this verse with *mortália*. The ambiguity reader heard *santos* as canonized saints; the glossary's *sanctus → santo* kept (as 30:24, 31:6).

3

*eórum → deles* (not *o seu sangue*, which could be heard as their own or God's). *in circúitu → ao redor de* (glossary). *effudérunt → derramaram*, echoed by *que foi derramado* in 78:10b (*effúsus est*), as the Latin echoes it. The Innocents' responsory (12-28) sets this verse.

4

Near-twin of 43:14 (*oppróbrium vicínis nostris … his, qui sunt in circúitu nostro*): the shared words are copied (*uma afronta para os nossos vizinhos*, *zombaria … para os que estão ao nosso redor*); the Latin varies *derísum / illúsio* and so does the Portuguese (*riso / escárnio*; D39 names 78:4). *Facti sumus → Tornamo-nos*, as 30:12 *tornei-me uma afronta*.

5

The settled formula *Até quando, Senhor, … para sempre?* (D27; 12:1). *irascéris* future → *vos irareis*. The second colon continues the question without repeating *até quando*, as the Latin (and as 73:10). v2: the second colon in the stylist's order (*como fogo se acenderá*), so it no longer opens on a clitic. *zelus → zelo* (the zeláre row keeps the noun; 68:10, 118:139): the ambiguity reader heard 'care' first; *zelo* beside *irareis* and *fogo* is kept as the Latin's word.

6

*novérunt* (perfect of knowing, present sense) → *conhecem* (D44). *effúnde → derramai*; *invocáre → invocar*.

8

The Lenten Tract (Quadp3-3 and ferias) sets 78:8–9 with *Dómine* before it; the verse reads alone. *iniquitátum nostrárum antiquárum → das nossas iniquidades antigas* (adjective after, as the Latin). *Ne memíneris → Não vos lembreis* (the reminísci row, 24:7).

9

*Ádjuva nos → Auxiliai-nos* (the adjuváre row; 43:26). *salutáris noster → nosso Salvador* (64:6, the salutáris row). *propter … propter* both → *por causa de*, keeping the Latin's repetition (*propter nomen tuum → por causa do vosso nome*, 43:26). *líbera → livrai* (liberáre). The first colon is long (it is long in the Latin: two clauses before the asterisk). The Thursday Matins antiphon *Ádjuva nos, * Deus, salutáris noster: et propítius esto peccátis nostris* reads: *Auxiliai-nos, Deus, nosso Salvador: e sede clemente com os nossos pecados*.

10

*Ne forte dicant → Para que não digam* (*forte* absorbed, as MS1932). *Ubi est Deus eórum? → Onde está o Deus deles?* (41:4 *Onde está o teu Deus?*). *coram óculis nostris → diante dos nossos olhos*. The verse ends on a comma: its subject opens 78:10b.

10b

*últio → vingança* (the vindíctæ row; ἐκδίκησις is the Greek of both). *intróeat in conspéctu tuo → entre na vossa presença*, as 118:170 *Intret postulátio mea in conspéctu tuo → Entre o meu pedido na vossa presença* (a verb of motion: *à vossa vista* does not take one). *gémitus → gemido*.

11

*magnitúdo → grandeza*; *bráchium → braço*. The ambiguity reader heard *tomai posse* as 'seize' rather than 'claim as yours'; kept — it is the Latin's *possidére*, and the Greek's 'preserve' is MS1932's reading, not the Latin's.

12

*réddere → pagar* (the réddere row; 27:4b *pagai-lhes*). The ambiguity reader did not know *seio* and heard 'in their midst'; kept, as 73:11 (*do meio do vosso seio*).

13

*Nos autem → Nós, porém,* (the ego autem row). *oves páscuæ tuæ → as ovelhas da vossa pastagem* (the páscua row; 73:1 the same). *confitébimur tibi → vos daremos graças* (D5; 74:2). *in sǽculum → para sempre* (D23).

13b

The formula *De geração em geração* (118:90). *annuntiáre → anunciar* (D41); *laudem tuam → o vosso louvor* (50:17).
