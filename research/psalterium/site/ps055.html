4

minor

“Desde a altura do dia temerei” Ab altitúdine diéi timébo

'Desde' makes 'ab' purely temporal (from that hour onward). The Latin 'ab' is open: it can mean the time from which, or the thing feared or its cause. Plain 'da' keeps that openness.

proposedDa altura do dia temerei

❧ taken

7

minor

“se esconderão” abscóndent

'Abscondent' is active and transitive with no object ('they will hide [something]', like LXX katakrypsousin). The reflexive 'se' changes the voice and supplies an object the Latin does not have.

proposedHabitarão e esconderão

❧ taken

11

minor

“louvarei a palavra, no Senhor louvarei a palavra” laudábo verbum, in Dómino laudábo sermónem

The Latin varies its nouns (verbum / sermonem) in the parallel lines. The Portuguese uses 'palavra' twice, which flattens that into plain repetition.

proposedEm Deus louvarei a palavra, no Senhor louvarei o discurso

❧ taken — a second noun taken; the stylist's *a fala* chosen over *o discurso*, which stays an option

This is a close, faithful rendering. It keeps the Septuagintal oddities ('altura do dia', 'por nada os salvareis', 'a carne'), the tenses and the future perfect in 55:10. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only faults are small: a temporal 'desde' in 55:4, a reflexive that changes the voice in 55:7, and the verbum/sermonem pair collapsed into one word in 55:11.

fails: sayable, native

“que pagarei, louvores a vós”

'Pagar votos' sounds commercial; in Portuguese one 'cumpre' vows. The appositive 'louvores a vós' hangs off a comma and comes out as a mumble. A colon makes the apposition sayable.

proposedque cumprirei: louvores a vós

The psalm reads plainly and has its verbal weight in the right places. The refrain verses (5, 11) mostly carry well, and the vós forms sit naturally. Where it stumbles, the cause is idiom rather than sense: the crossed 'pisar aos pés', 'conheci que', the doubled 'palavra', and a mediant/final rhyme in verse 8 that turns sing-song when chanted.

10

minor

“eis que eu soube: vós sois o meu Deus” ecce, cognóvi, quóniam Deus meus es

The subordinator 'quóniam' (that) is turned into a colon, so the object clause becomes direct speech. 'Soube' also reads as 'I found out (once)', a narrower sense than cognóvi's perfect-with-present-force 'I have come to know'. Both are defensible, but a closer rendering is available.

proposedeis que conheci que vós sois o meu Deus

kept as an option under ecce, cognóvi, quóniam Deus meus es — his own note calls both defensible; draft 1's *conheci que* was refused by the v1 stylist as not current and the bare *eis,* as clipped; *soube* is the verb 19:7a took for *cognóvi* + *quóniam* after a Latinist major. *eis, conheci que vós sois* stays option 2.

5

minor

“louvarei as minhas palavras” laudábo sermónes meos

'Sermónes' is rendered 'palavras', the same word used for 'verba' in 55:6 and 'verbum' in 55:11, while 'sermónem' in 55:11 becomes 'fala'. The Latin keeps sermo and verbum apart within the psalm, and this rendering blurs that distinction. The sense is not wrong.

proposedlouvarei as minhas falas

not taken — D15 rules sermo → palavra and refused the plural *falas* (the Ps 118 stylist, three times); 55:5 *sermónes meos* and 55:6 *verba mea* are the same Greek (τοὺς λόγους μου), so there the Latin's variation carries no sense; 55:11 took *fala* only because the Greek differs there too (ῥῆμα / λόγον) and both v1 readers heard the doubled noun as a slip.

This is a faithful, literal rendering that keeps the Latin's hard spots as they are: 'Da altura do dia', 'Habitarão e esconderão' without an object, 'vossos votos', and the dangling 'Assim como também na vossa promessa'. It does not smooth them toward the Hebrew. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, and no flex or second mediant anywhere). Only two small points could be closer: 55:10 and 55:5.

Verse by verse

2

*Miserére mei, Deus* → *Tende piedade de mim, Deus* (formula row; no *ó*: the Latin has none). The Lenten Gradual (Quad3-1, Quad5-1) sings this verse with *bellans*.

4

*ego vero* → *eu, porém* (118:70). *timébo* (future) kept against MS1932's *Estou temendo*; 55:5's *não temerei* answers it.

5

The refrain: 55:5 and 55:11 share *Em Deus louvarei … em Deus esperei … não temerei o que me faça …*; they differ where the Latin differs (*sermónes meos / verbum … sermónem*, *caro / homo*). *In Deo laudábo sermónes meos* kept as the Latin has it — 'in God I will praise my words' — against MS1932's gloss (*as palavras que me dirigiu*).

6

*verba mea* → *as minhas palavras*, the same words as 55:5's *sermónes meos*: the Greek has τοὺς λόγους μου in both (D15).

9b

*Sicut et* → *Assim como também*, as 55:8's *Sicut* → *Assim como*. *converténtur retrórsum* → *voltarão para trás* (the *retrórsum* row, which named 55:9). Natural order, subject before verb; the trailing colon of the DO text kept.

10

*invocávero* (future perfect) → *em que vos invocar* (19:10 *no dia em que vos invocarmos*).

12

*laudatiónes tibi* in apposition to *vota*, as the Latin (αἰνέσεως σοι); *réddere vota → pagar os votos* (row; 21:26). v2: a colon before the apposition, from the stylist.

13

*lapsus* only here and 114:8 (grep) → *queda*.

8

*fácies … confrínges* → *salvareis … quebrareis*: the stylist (v1) heard a chime of *-areis* across the asterisk; the Latin has the same echo (two futures in *-es*), so it is kept (rule 5). His move (*quebrareis na ira os povos*) would put the two futures side by side across the mediant. *por nada* keeps the Latin's open *pro níhilo*: the ambiguity reader heard it split between "in no way" and "freely" — so is the Latin (DRB "for nothing"); *de nenhum modo* stays option 2.
