1draft

Draft 1 from the parallels. Liturgical use and Brazilian circulation fetched into circulation.md: every Brazilian text found has 'suba' for Dirigátur; refused for the Latin's verb (decision dirigatur). Hard readings: 140:6 (in beneplácitis, juncti petræ), 140:7 (potuérunt, crassitúdo), all left as obscure as the Latin is; the Hebrew's senses (DRB and MS1932's 'contra', 'precipitados', 'delícias') not imported.

2checks

Hard checks pass. 3

minor

“e aos meus lábios uma porta que os cerque” óstium circumstántiæ lábiis meis

The Latin has a noun genitive, 'a door of enclosure'. The translation turns it into a relative clause with a verb ('que os cerque') and adds an object pronoun. The sense survives, but the Latin's noun phrase could be kept.

proposede aos meus lábios uma porta de cerco

kept as an option under et óstium circumstántiæ lábiis meis — 'cerco' is heard as a siege; a noun made a clause is grammar (D2).

4

minor

“para se desculpar com desculpas nos pecados” ad excusándas excusatiónes in peccátis

The Latin gerundive means 'to make excuses in sins', with no reflexive. 'se desculpar' adds a reflexive subject, and 'com desculpas' recasts the figura etymologica as an instrumental.

proposedpara dar desculpas nos pecados

kept as an option under ad excusándas excusatiónes in peccátis — His fix drops the Latin's figura etymologica, which D2 keeps; the reflexive only gives the infinitive a Portuguese build.

5

minor

“mas o óleo do pecador não unja a minha cabeça” óleum autem peccatóris non impínguet caput meum

'impinguet' means 'to fatten, to make rich with oil', which is more than 'anoint'. 'unja' flattens the concrete image of fattening.

proposedmas o óleo do pecador não engorde a minha cabeça

kept as an option under non impínguet caput meum — The impinguáre row (22:5b) is ungir, and the 22:5b Latinist withdrew this very request.

The translation is faithful to the Latin throughout. It keeps the Septuagintal difficulties of vv. 6-7 without resolving them, and every verse has the same pointing marks as the Latin in the same order. The three items are all minor: two places where a Latin noun or gerundive construction becomes a looser verbal paraphrase, and one concrete image ('impinguet') that is flattened.

2

fails: sayable, native

“Que a minha oração seja dirigida como incenso na vossa presença”

The periphrastic passive 'Que ... seja dirigida' makes a long, flat first colon with the verb buried in the middle; it reads like a translated optative, not like a prayer.

proposedDirija-se a minha oração como incenso em vossa presença

kept as an option under Dirigátur orátio mea — Heard aloud as 'dirija-se à minha oração' (go to my prayer).

4

fails: native, sayable

“para se desculpar com desculpas nos pecados”

The impersonal 'se desculpar' has no clear subject (the heart? someone?) and the ear stalls on it before the repeated root arrives; the line sounds like a gloss.

proposedpara me desculpar com desculpas nos pecados

kept as an option under ad excusándas excusatiónes in peccátis — The gerundive names no person; the ambiguity reader heard the first person anyway.

6

fails: sayable, native

“Porque ainda a minha oração também está no que lhes agrada”

'ainda ... também' split around the subject is clumsy; two adverbs pulling in different places, and the mouth doesn't know where to lean.

proposedPois até a minha oração ainda está no que lhes agrada

❧ taken

8

fails: sayable

“Foram dispersos os nossos ossos”

A run of sibilants and o-s syllables ('dispersos os nossos ossos') hisses and blurs when recited in choir; the passive auxiliary adds one more heavy beat before it.

proposedDispersaram-se os nossos ossos junto ao inferno

❧ taken

10

fails: plain

“a sós estou eu, até que eu passe”

The second 'eu' is redundant after 'estou eu' and weighs down the final cadence.

proposeda sós estou eu, até que passe

kept as an option under singuláriter sum ego — Without it 'passe' is heard as 'until it passes'; tránseam is first person.

The psalm is faithful and mostly plain, with good concrete images (incense, the door of the lips, the oil, the snare) and clean cadences at the marks. Its weak spots are the obscure middle verses (4-8), where the translation leans on passives and stacked adverbs and begins to sound like a word-for-word gloss rather than Portuguese prayer; small reorderings would make them sayable without losing any of the Latin.

Verse by verse

1

No titulus: DO's file has none (D7), though the LXX and MS1932 print 'Salmo de David'. clamáre → clamar with the perfect kept ('clamei a vós', as 129:1); exáudi → escutai (D3), inténde → atendei (D3; the glossary lists 140:1 among the four places where the two stand parallel). 'eu' supplied in the second colon so that 'quando clamar' has its subject. The first colon reads alone as the antiphon 'Dómine, clamávi ad te, exáudi me'.

2

The verse is said at the incensation and as a Gradual; each colon stands alone. The versicle split (after 'orátio mea') falls after 'seja dirigida'. in conspéctu → 'na vossa presença', the conspéctus row's split for an act of worship (137:1b, 87:3).

4

The Latin's two cola run on into 140:4b ('Cum homínibus …'), so 140:4 ends with a comma as the Latin does.

4b

operári iniquitátem → 'praticar a iniquidade' (glossary). 'os homens' with the article: 'com homens que praticam' would be heard as 'together with such men' only; the article keeps the class.

6

absorbére → engolir (row open; 123:3b's 'tragado' was a local departure where two verbs stood together). The ambiguity reader found 'no que lhes agrada' contradictory on first hearing: the Latin is as obscure (decision beneplacitis); not clarified.

8

The verse has one asterisk and an inner colon; the colon is kept as punctuation. 'Dómine, Dómine' repeated as the Latin. The copula 'estão' supplied (D2). auferre → tirar (D41). quia → porque (the default).

9

scándalum → tropeço (row; 139:6 has the same word); custódi me → Guardai-me (custodíre row). 'tropeços' listed unknown by the ambiguity reader, as at 48:14; kept under the row. The stylist named 140:9 the best line.

3

'guarda' is the noun, as 38:2b; the ambiguity reader noted it could be heard as the verb after 'Ponde', and took the noun. Kept.

7

'espessura' was listed unknown by the ambiguity reader; it is crassitúdo's plain word and no plainer faithful one exists (decision crassitudo). Kept.
