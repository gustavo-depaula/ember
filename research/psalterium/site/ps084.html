4

fails: native, sayable

“vos afastastes da ira da vossa indignação”

Opening a colon with an unstressed clitic ('vos afastastes') is colloquial in this elevated register and makes the second half start on a weak, hissing syllable. It also sounds briefly like 'vós afastastes', as if God were the object pronoun's subject.

proposedafastastes-vos da ira da vossa indignação

❧ taken

7

fails: sayable, plain

“voltando-vos, vós nos vivificareis”

'vos, vós nos viv-' piles up v- and s- sounds that the tongue trips on, and the stressed 'vós' that repeats the address adds nothing to what 'voltando-vos' already says. 'vivificareis' has five syllables and is heavy before the mark.

proposedDeus, voltando-vos, nos dareis a vida

kept as an option under tu convérsus vivificábis nos — Drops the Latin's emphatic *tu* and the *vivificáre* row's verb (79:19, 70:20).

9

fails: sayable

“Ouvirei o que o Senhor Deus fala em mim … falará paz para o seu povo”

'o que o' is a small hiatus snag. In the second colon, 'para o seu povo' is slacker than a plain dative, and it pads the cadence.

proposedOuvirei o que o Senhor Deus fala em mim: * porque ele falará paz ao seu povo.

❧ taken

10

fails: sayable

“para que habite a glória na nossa terra”

'na nossa' stutters (na-no-); 'em nossa terra' flows better and suits the register.

proposedpara que habite a glória em nossa terra

not taken — Rule 5: the article stays before possessives (*na nossa terra*, as 84:13 *a nossa terra*).

13

fails: native

“o Senhor dará a benignidade”

The definite article before an abstract object makes it sound like translationese, as if some particular benignity already mentioned were meant. Without the article the sentence reads naturally and still repeats 'dará' as the Latin does.

proposedPois o Senhor dará benignidade: * e a nossa terra dará o seu fruto.

❧ taken

14

fails: native, cadence

“e porá no caminho os seus passos”

This word order is a needless inversion that copies the Latin. The natural Portuguese order puts the paroxytone 'caminho' at the close and gives a better final cadence. Whose steps they are stays as open as in the Latin.

proposede porá os seus passos no caminho

❧ taken

A plain, dignified, mostly native text. The great central verses (11–12) ring clearly, and the Latin's repetitions (afastastes, ira, dará) are kept without strain. The weak spots are a few Latinate word orders and clitic placements (vv. 4, 7, 14) and a sibilant pile-up in v. 7 that a choir would stumble on.

worst line84:7

best line84:11

Nothing to correct in any verse.

The translation follows the Latin closely throughout: tenses (perfects of vv. 2-4, futures of vv. 6-7, 13-14), the Septuagintal 'in me' and 'ad cor' of v. 9, and the concrete images (osculatae sunt, prospexit, gressus) are all kept. The reflexive 'afastastes-vos' in v. 4 only supplies what Portuguese needs for the intransitive 'avertisti ab', and the pointing marks match the Latin (a single mediant in every verse).

Verse by verse

2

Introit of Advent III; each colon reads alone. *captívitas → cativeiro*, *Jacob → Jacó* (13:7).

3

*remíttere → perdoar* (row, 31:5b); *opérire → cobrir* (the *tégere* row names this verse, 31:1). *plebs → povo* (row). *eórum → os seus*: after *vosso povo* the possessive can only be theirs. The ambiguity reader noted *cobristes* is near *cobrastes* ('you charged'); kept — the *tégere / opérire* row's verb (31:1), and the context (*Perdoastes*) settles it.

4

*ira … ira* is the Latin's repetition, kept. *indignátio → indignação* (row).

5

The Compline versicle: *V. Convertei-nos, Deus, nosso Salvador. R. E afastai de nós a vossa ira.* Natural order in the second colon (*de nós* before the object).

6

*numquid → acaso* (row). *a generatióne in generatiónem → de geração em geração*, as 76:9 and the formula row.

7

*convérsus* of God → *voltando-vos*, as 70:20 (*voltando-vos, me vivificastes*). *lætári → alegrar-se* (row).

8

The Preces and Advent versicle, which must stand alone as V and R: *V. Mostrai-nos, Senhor, a vossa misericórdia. R. E dai-nos a vossa salvação.* *salutáre tuum → a vossa salvação*, = the Nunc dimittis (D6). Natural order (*dai-nos* before the object). *Mostrai* and *dai* are safe under rule 3 (past *mostrei*, *dei*). The glossary had no formula row for it; one is proposed.

9

*audíre → ouvir* (not an imperative, D3 allows it).

10

The copula *está* supplied (D2). *timéntes eum → os que o temem* (33:8). *ut inhábitet → para que habite*, the purpose clause kept (22:6b row); MS1932 turns it into a statement. The stylist's *em nossa terra* (v1) refused: rule 5 keeps the article before possessives. The ambiguity reader heard *Todavia* as a contrast (the cost named in decision `verum`).

11

*obviáre sibi → encontrar-se* (L&S cites this verse: 'to meet'); *uma com a outra* not added — *se encontraram* is already reciprocal. Soft check: rhyme *encontraram / beijaram* accepted — the Latin sets two perfects at the two cadences (*obviavérunt / osculátæ sunt*), and every Portuguese perfect plural ends in *-aram* (the option *oscularam* too); reordering to avoid it would be inversion for its own sake.

12

*prospícere → olhar* (row, which names this verse; 13:2 *olhou do céu*). The Christmas antiphon is the first colon.

13

*étenim → pois* (row). *dabit … dabit* kept as *dará … dará* (MS1932 varies the second, *produzirá*); 66:6 *a terra deu o seu fruto*. The Advent versicle (*V. Dóminus dabit benignitátem, et terra nostra dabit fructum suum*) is this verse without *Étenim*.

14

*ambuláre → andar* (row); *gressus → passos* (16:5). *os seus passos* keeps the Latin's open reference (justice's own steps or the Lord's: *suos* against the Greek αὐτοῦ).
