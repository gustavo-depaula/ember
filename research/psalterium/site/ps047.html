3

fails: native, sayable

“Com a exultação de toda a terra é fundado o monte Sião”

The prepositional phrase is fronted and the subject comes last, after a passive verb. The ear has to hold 'com a exultação de toda a terra' in suspense before it learns what is being founded. That word order is Latin, not Portuguese.

proposedO monte Sião é fundado com a exultação de toda a terra, * os lados do norte, a cidade do grande Rei.

kept as an option under Fundátur exsultatióne univérsæ terræ mons Sion — the appositions after the asterisk would follow *toda a terra* and be heard as the earth's

fails: native, plain

“Porque eis que”

'Porque eis que' stacks two connectives and sounds heavy at the head of the verse. 'Pois' says the same with a lighter onset.

proposedPois eis que os reis da terra se congregaram

kept as an option under Quóniam ecce — quóniam → porque is the default and 47:15 has it; 42:2's *Pois* was forced by two *por que* questions

“reuniram-se juntos”

This sounds like the schoolroom pleonasm 'reunir juntos'. 'Convenerunt' means 'came together', and the plain verb avoids the redundancy.

proposedvieram juntos.

kept as an option under convenérunt in unum — the in unum formula of 2:2, identical Latin (rule 6)

6

fails: sayable, native

“Eles, ao verem, assim se admiraram, foram perturbados, foram abalados”

The first colon is too long for one breath. The two analytic passives, 'foram perturbados, foram abalados', add syllables and weaken the three-beat Latin run ('admiráti sunt, conturbáti sunt, commóti sunt'). Three parallel reflexive verbs keep the triple and shorten the colon.

proposedEles, vendo, assim se admiraram, perturbaram-se, abalaram-se:

kept as an option under conturbáti sunt, commóti sunt — *perturbaram-se* taken; *abalaram-se* refused (movéri row *ser abalado*; colloquial "take off")

“o tremor apoderou-se deles”

In Brazilian speech the clitic comes before the verb here. The enclitic form sounds like European or bookish Portuguese.

proposedo tremor se apoderou deles.

❧ taken

10

fails: native

“Acolhemos, Deus,”

A bare 'Deus' as a vocative in the middle of a sentence sounds abrupt in Portuguese. Liturgical Portuguese uses 'ó Deus', which also gives the colon a better rhythm.

proposedAcolhemos, ó Deus, a vossa misericórdia, * no meio do vosso templo.

kept as an option under Deus (vocative, twice) — the vocative rule: no *ó* unless the Latin has *O*

11

fails: native, sayable

“Como o vosso nome, Deus, assim também o vosso louvor, até os confins da terra”

The same bare vocative 'Deus' appears, and the comma before 'até' strands the phrase so it seems to hang without a verb. If the comma is dropped, 'até os confins' reads as reaching out with 'o vosso louvor'.

proposedComo o vosso nome, ó Deus, assim também o vosso louvor até os confins da terra:

❧ taken — comma dropped; the *ó Deus* half is the 47:10 option `deus`

The Portuguese is sober and faithful and mostly prays well. The hymnic verses (2, 9, 12, 15) have real weight. Where it stumbles, it follows Latin word order or analytic passives too closely (3, 6), or uses small bookish habits: enclisis, a bare vocative 'Deus', and 'reuniram-se juntos'. Each of these can be fixed without touching the Latin's images.

Verse by verse

2

magnus → *grande*, the copula supplied. *in civitáte Dei nostri* → *na cidade do nosso Deus*; mons sanctus → *monte santo* (row; 47:2 is named there). The verse is the Matins antiphon *Magnus Dóminus * et laudábilis nimis in civitáte Dei nostri* (Psalmi matutinum), the Gradual of Pent08-0, a verse of the Holy Thursday Mandatum (Quad6-4 files), and a Pent01-0 responsory (grep): *Grande é o Senhor* reads alone before an asterisk placed after it.

3

Rex magnus → *o grande Rei*, capital as *Rei da glória* (23:7). The appositions are the Latin's; no *situada* (MS1932's supplement). The ambiguity reader could not place *os lados do norte* (unknown to him as a phrase). The Latin apposition is itself obscure (a famous crux); kept, with *nos lados do norte* an option in `latera`.

4

*in dómibus ejus* → *nas suas casas* (domus → casa, row); the Greek βάρεσιν is towers or palaces, the Latin says houses, and 47:14 *domos ejus* repeats it — the same word both times. cognoscétur → *será conhecido* (cognóscere → conhecer, row).

5

Quóniam ecce → *Porque eis que* (ecce → eis, row).

6

conturbári → *perturbar* (row; now *perturbaram-se*, see `passives`); commovéri → *ser abalado* (movéri row; σαλεύω). The Latin's three verbs are kept three. *ao verem* leaves the object unstated, as *vidéntes* does (the ambiguity reader heard the city).

7

contérere → *quebrar* (row). The sudden *vós* (*quebrareis*) is the Latin's second person. The ambiguity reader could not tell who *quebrareis* addresses (God or the hearers): the Latin turns to God with no vocative, and so does the Portuguese; kept. *Társis* was unknown to him — a proper name, kept.

9

*Sicut … sic* → *Como … assim*; the echo *ouvimos / vimos* is the Latin's (*audívimus / vídimus*). in ætérnum → *para sempre* (D23). fundáre → *fundar* (row). The Purification Gradual quotes a variant with *ita et* (missa Sancti/02-02, grep) — the psalm keeps its own words.

10

*in médio templi tui* → *no meio do vosso templo*. See `suscepimus`: this verse and 47:11 are the Introit *Suscépimus* (02-02; Pent08-0), where the Missal has *ita et* for the psalm's *sic et* — the psalm keeps its own.

11

laus → *louvor* (row). The vocative *Deus* without *ó*, as in 47:10.

12

Sion → *Sião*, *o monte Sião* (2:6, 9:12). lætári / exsultáre → *alegrar-se / exultar* (row); fíliæ Judæ → *as filhas de Judá*; judícia → *juízos* (D15). The verse is a versicle at the Purification (horas SanctiM/02-02, grep).

13

circumdáre → *cercar* (row). The two imperatives of 47:13 and the three of 47:14 are -ar or irregular verbs; none is a rule-3 homograph (*cercai, abraçai, narrai, ponde, partilhai*).

14

*Pónite* → *Ponde* (past *pus*, no homograph). The ambiguity reader heard *partilhai as suas casas* as hospitality, owner unclear; the Latin *distribúite domos ejus* is as open (see `distribuite`). *seu poder*: God's or Sion's, as *ejus*.

15

in ætérnum et in sǽculum sǽculi → *para sempre e pelos séculos dos séculos* (D23, D27). The first *Deus* bare, the second *o nosso Deus* in apposition, as the Latin repeats the noun. *regerá* was unknown to the ambiguity reader; it is the régere row (22:1, 27:9), kept.
