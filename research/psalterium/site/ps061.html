Voice changed: emigrare is active and intransitive ('I shall not move away / depart'); the passive 'serei desalojado' makes the speaker the object of someone else's action and adds the idea of being dislodged by force.

proposednão emigrarei

Agent noun (helper) rendered as an abstraction (help); the Latin distinguishes adjutor from auxilium (cf. 61:8 'Deus auxílii mei').

fails: native

“Pois também ele é o meu Deus”

'Pois também ele' sounds like a clumsy word-for-word rendering of 'Nam et ipse'. 'também' makes the ear ask 'also, besides whom?' In Portuguese the emphatic 'ele mesmo' carries the 'et ipse' naturally.

proposedPois ele mesmo é o meu Deus, e o meu Salvador:

❧ taken — the blind reader heard 'he too' as well; local departure from the row, recorded.

4

fails: sayable, no rhyme, concrete

“como a uma parede inclinada e a uma cerca empurrada?”

The vowels run together in 'como a uma' and again in 'e a uma'. 'inclinada / empurrada' makes an accidental rhyme inside the colon, right at the final. 'cerca empurrada' is flat: a fence someone has shoved is not the image of a wall pushed down (depulsæ).

proposedmatais todos vós, como a parede que pende e a cerca derrubada?

❧ taken — *que pende* taken (rhyme gone); the dative *a uma* and *empurrada* kept (decision depulsae: *derrubada* says already fallen).

“vos lançais sobre um homem”

The reflexive 'vos lançais' is stiff, and in a psalm that keeps vós for God it takes a moment to hear that this is addressed to the enemies. 'vos atirais' is more physical and more current.

proposedAté quando vos atirais sobre um homem?

kept as an option under Quoúsque irrúitis in hóminem? — = 58:4 (rule 6); the blind reader heard it plainly.

5

fails: native, sayable

“pensaram em repelir o meu preço”

'pensaram em' is conversational ('they thought about') and too weak for cogitavérunt, which means scheming. The phrase also sits heavily before the mediant.

proposedTodavia, tramaram repelir o meu preço, corri sedento:

kept as an option under cogitavérunt repéllere — *tramar* is concinnáre's.

“corri com sede”

'com sede' reads like everyday prose ('I ran while thirsty'). 'sedento' is one word and has the right weight.

proposedcorri sedento

kept as an option under cucúrri in siti — the Latinist pulled the other way (the locative); *com sede* held between them.

“com a sua boca bendiziam, e com o seu coração maldiziam”

The doubled articles and possessives ('com a sua … com o seu') weigh down the parallel. Dropping the articles keeps suo in both halves and lightens the line.

proposedcom sua boca bendiziam, e com seu coração maldiziam.

not taken — rule 5, the article before possessives.

fails: sayable, native

“Todavia, sê sujeita a Deus, minha alma”

'sê sujeita' is an awkward imperative, and the 'sê su-' sibilants blur together when spoken. Moving the vocative forward lets the imperative land more softly and still echoes 'sujeita' from v. 2.

proposedTodavia, minha alma, sê sujeita a Deus: * porque dele vem a minha paciência.

fails: plain, native

“não serei desalojado”

'desalojado' sounds bureaucratic, as in evictions and floods. It is also passive, while non emigrábo is active: 'I will not move away'.

fails: sayable

“o Deus do meu auxílio, e a minha esperança está em Deus”

The colon starts with a bare noun phrase that is then joined to a full clause, so the ear loses the grammar. A supplied copula steadies it.

proposedele é o Deus do meu auxílio, e a minha esperança está em Deus.

kept as an option under Deus auxílii mei — the Latin's apposition; the blind reader heard it; +3 syllables.

10

fails: sayable, native, no rhyme

“vãos são”

The nasal 'ãos' clashes straight into 'são', a real cacophony at the head of the verse.

proposedTodavia, são vãos os filhos dos homens, mentirosos os filhos dos homens nas balanças:

❧ taken — order (D2).

“para eles, pela vaidade, enganarem juntos”

The personal infinitive with the subject placed first ('para eles … enganarem') sounds at first like 'for them'. The verb arrives only at the end, after a parenthesis, so the colon cannot be said in one breath with any sense.

proposedpara que eles mesmos, por vaidade, enganem juntos.

❧ taken — the finite clause and *mesmos* (= ipsi) taken; *por vaidade* refused (conceit), option 3.

11

fails: plain

“não cobiceis rapinas”

'rapinas' in the plural and without an article is rare in Brazilian speech outside 'ave de rapina', so it sounds like a Latinism. The singular with an article keeps the concrete noun and sounds native.

proposedNão espereis na iniquidade, e não cobiceis a rapina:

kept as an option under rapínas — the Latin's plural, the things seized.

12

fails: sayable

“estas duas coisas eu ouvi”

The supplied 'eu' adds a syllable to an already very long first colon and makes it sound like prose. The verb alone is firmer.

proposedUma vez falou Deus, estas duas coisas ouvi: que o poder é de Deus, e vossa, Senhor, é a misericórdia:

not taken — bare *ouvi* is the *vós* imperative (rule 3).

The psalm mostly sounds grave and plain, and its repeated anchors (Todavia, sujeita a Deus, Salvador, auxílio) come through well for chant. The weak spots are the enemy verses (4, 5 and 10), where the syntax follows the Latin too closely: vowel clashes, an inner rhyme, and one colon (10b) that does not parse aloud. A few words are too flat or bureaucratic (com sede, desalojado, rapinas).

Verse by verse

2

*enim → pois* (row). *salutáre → salvação* (D6). The colon *Nonne Deo * subjécta erit ánima mea* is a Matins antiphon (grep).

3

*salutáris meus* (masculine, of God) → *o meu Salvador* (the *salutáris · salvátor* row: one Greek word, σωτήρ, here and at 61:7). *suscéptor → amparo* (D19; the row names 61:3). 61:3 and 61:7 are one Greek line with two Latin variations (*salutáris / salvátor* merged by the row; *suscéptor / adjútor* kept apart as the glossary keeps them; *non movébor ámplius / non emigrábo* kept apart). v2: *Pois ele mesmo é* (decision namet), a local departure from the *nam et* row.

4

The whole verse is one question in DO's Latin (the second colon ends with *?*). *interfícitis univérsi vos* → *matais todos vós*: no object supplied, as the Latin; *como a uma parede* then says what is done to the man, the dative's sense. *univérsus → todo* (row). v2: *que pende* removes the inner rhyme (decision inclinato).

5

*repéllere* (no *a*) → *repelir* (settled, D42). *ore suo … corde suo* → *com a sua boca … com o seu coração* (the article before possessives, rule 5). The Greek has no object 'me' for the verbs, and none is supplied (MS1932 adds *me*).

6

*súbditus / subjéctus esse → ser sujeito a* (36:6 *sê sujeito ao Senhor*); the soul addressed is *tu*, as the refrain 41:6 *minha alma* (bare vocative). *patiéntia → paciência* (9:19).
