minor

“fiquei em silêncio sobre coisas boas” sílui a bonis

'a bonis' is separative ('kept silent from good things', i.e. withheld even good words); 'sobre' makes the good things the topic of the silence, which the Latin does not say.

proposede calei-me das coisas boas

❧ taken — In substance: the separative with *longe do bem* (30:21, 30:12b); his *calar-se* is tacére's verb and would break the echo with 38:13b; *sobre coisas boas* stays an option.

7

minor

“o homem passa como uma imagem” in imágine pertránsit homo

Latin says 'in an image', not 'as an image'; 'como' turns the Latin's locative/modal phrase into a simile. Defensible, but closer is possible.

proposedo homem passa em imagem

not taken — *passa em imagem* is not Portuguese; *como* is the build the sense takes (DRB 'as an image'); his wording is option 2.

“para quem os juntará” cui congregábit ea

❧ taken — Mended the other way: *Acumula tesouros* gives *os* its antecedent (the treasures are what *ea* means), which also answers the blind reader's unknown *Entesoura*.

3

minor

“fiquei em silêncio longe do bem” sílui a bonis

'longe do' makes a spatial sense the Latin does not have, and the plural 'bona' (good things) turns into the abstract singular 'o bem'. The Latin 'a bonis' can mean 'kept silent even from good things' or 'about good things', and the translation should leave both open.

proposede fiquei em silêncio quanto às coisas boas

❧ taken — His fix keeps the Latin's two readings open (D2) and the plural.

7

minor

“o homem passa como uma imagem” in imágine pertránsit homo

The Latin has 'in imagine' (in an image), not a comparison. 'Como' settles a phrase that the tradition also reads as 'in the image (of God)'. That is resolving an ambiguity the Latin keeps.

proposedo homem passa em imagem

not taken — *passa em imagem* is not Portuguese; *como* is the build the sense takes (DRB 'as an image'); his wording is option 2.

9

minor

“fizestes de mim a afronta do insensato” oppróbrium insipiénti dedísti me

'Dedisti' means 'gave' and 'insipienti' is a dative ('to the fool'). The genitive 'do insensato' reads more naturally as 'the fool's own reproach' than as 'a reproach handed over to the fool'.

proposeddestes-me como afronta ao insensato

not taken — *afronta ao insensato* is heard as an insult to the fool; the blind reader could not tell the direction with *para*; he had passed *para o* on draft 1. Option.

14

minor

“Perdoai-me” Remítte mihi

'Remitte mihi' can mean 'forgive me' or 'let up on me / relent' (LXX ἄνες, which follows naturally from 'ámove a me plagas tuas'). 'Perdoai-me' keeps only the first sense. 'Relevai-me' keeps both.

proposedRelevai-me, para que eu seja reanimado antes de partir, * e já não existirei.

❧ taken

The translation is faithful and close. Tenses (exardéscet as future, congregábit), person, number and the Septuagintal readings (the spider, 'in imagine', 'substantia') are all kept, and the pointing marks match the Latin throughout (only the mediant *, in the same places). The remaining points are minor: in a few places the translation settles something the Latin leaves open ('a bonis', 'in imagine', 'Remitte') or loses a dative construction ('insipienti').
