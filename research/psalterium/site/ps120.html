3

fails: native, sayable

“Que ele não deixe vacilar o teu pé”

The opening 'Que ele' is a soft hiatus (que-e-le) and the explicit 'ele' sounds like written grammar, not prayer; the Latin names no subject, and the next colon already supplies 'aquele que te guarda' as the one who acts.

proposedQue não deixe vacilar o teu pé: * nem dormite aquele que te guarda.

❧ taken

“nem dormite”

'dormite' is correct (subjunctive of dormitar) but on the ear it can momentarily sound like 'dormi-te'; it is saved by the parallel with v. 4 'dormitará', so keep it, but the cleaner first colon helps the listener hear the subjunctive chain.

proposedkeep 'nem dormite aquele que te guarda'

not taken — Nothing to change: the stylist himself asks to keep it; noted in the dormitare decision.

5

fails: sayable

“O Senhor te guarda, o Senhor é a tua proteção,”

The first colon is the longest of the psalm and crowds articles (o... o... a tua); in choir it strains one breath. Dropping the article before 'tua proteção' lightens it without losing anything of the Latin.

proposedO Senhor te guarda, o Senhor é tua proteção, * sobre a tua mão direita.

kept as an option under Dóminus protéctio tua — Rule 5 keeps the article before possessives; 'é‿a' elides, so the colon is within a syllable of the Latin.

A plain, quiet, very singable version: short cola, clean paroxytone and oxytone cadences, and the Latin repetitions (dormitará nem dormirá; guarda / guarde) are kept and ring well. Only the opening of v. 3 sounds translated, and v. 5 is a little heavy in its first half.

best line120:6

Verse by verse

1

No titulus: the psalm's heading 'Canticum graduum' is not in DO's psalm file, and the tituli are out of the first edition (D7); Ps 133, the other Gradual Psalm already done, carries none either, so the two match. leváre → levantar (glossary row, Ps 24:1); 122:1 'Ad te levávi óculos meos' should say 'levantei os meus olhos' too. The perfect 'Levávi' kept ('Levantei', not the CNBB's present). The verse is also read as a text in the Office of Our Lady Mediatrix (aliquibus locis, 05-31). The prayer is in the first person; from 120:3 the psalm speaks to one person, so tu (rule 3). The ambiguity reader heard the relative reading first (help from the mountains), with 120:2 then seeming to answer it — the Latin's own openness as printed; kept (decision unde).

2

'que fez o céu e a terra' is the formula row (113:23, 123:8, 133:3, 145:6) and stays word for word; here the relative follows 'Senhor' directly, so no 'ele' is needed (133:3 needed it only because of 'Sião'). Sung alone as the Monday Vespers antiphon of the older psalter (Psalmi major, Day1 Vespera): it reads alone.

3

'dare in commotiónem' = 'deixar vacilar', by the fluctuátio row (54:23, 65:9; the same Greek σάλος). 'pedem' is singular: 'o teu pé' (65:9 had the plural). 'qui custódit te' → 'aquele que te guarda', and 120:4 'aquele que guarda Israel' — the same build for the same Latin. custodíre → guardar (glossary). Draft 2 opens 'Que não deixe vacilar' (stylist). The ambiguity reader heard the wish ('may he not doze') as a faint doubt that the keeper stays awake; that is the Latin's own jussive ('neque dormítet'), answered by 120:4 'Eis que não dormitará'; kept. 'vacilar' carries a faint colloquial 'blunder' for him; it is the fluctuátio row's word (54:23, 65:9), kept.

4

ecce → 'Eis que' before a clause, as 7:15, 38:6, 118:40. 'dormitará … dormirá' echo each other at mediant: the Latin's own pairing (dormitábit / dórmiet), accepted. Second colon +3 by checks.py ('aquele que guarda Israel', 10 against 7), accepted: 'aquele que' matches 120:3 'aquele que te guarda' for the same Latin 'qui custódit', and 'o que guarda' can be heard as 'what guards'.

5

The copula 'é' is supplied in 'o Senhor é a tua proteção' (rule 2). protéctio → proteção (glossary). Present 'te guarda' for the Latin's present 'custódit' (the Greek has a future; the Latin is followed).

6

úrere → queimar, as 25:2 'queimai os meus rins'. The verb is gapped for the moon ('nem a lua de noite'), as in the Latin. 'De dia … de noite' departs from the working row per diem → durante o dia for length (see the decision; proposed in glossary.md).

7

'custódit' (present) then 'custódiat' (wish): 'te guarda' then 'guarde', as the Latin moves. ánima → alma (glossary; 'vida' is the row's option, not taken: nothing here is the idiom for seeking a life). The whole verse is the Vespers antiphon of the Office of the Dead (Commune C9, Ant Vespera) and reads alone.

8

'intróitum … éxitum': the Latin's order, entering then going out (the Hebrew and the Diurnal have going out first). 'ex hoc nunc, et usque in sǽculum' → 'desde agora e para todo o sempre', identical to 112:2b and 113:26 (the ex hoc nunc row and D37). Second colon +3 by checks.py, accepted: it is the fixed formula (D37). 'a tua entrada e a tua saída' was heard as comings and goings, somewhat literal — the Latin's concrete pair; kept.
