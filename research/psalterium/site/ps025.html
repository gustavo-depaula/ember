Verse by verse

1

Júdica me → 'Julgai-me', bare (glossary judicáre; 7:9b opens the same way). quóniam ego → 'porque eu': the pronoun kept for the Latin's emphasis, which 25:11 'Ego autem' answers ('Mas eu'). The Latin's order of the participle kept ('e, esperando no Senhor, …').

2

renes → rins (glossary; the organ named).

3

ante óculos meos → 'diante dos meus olhos'.

4

sedére cum → 'sentar-se com'; introíre → entrar (glossary íngredi / intráre).

5

malignántes → malvados (D24). sedébo → 'não me sentarei', the verb of 25:4 again, as the Latin repeats it.

6

The Lavabo (25:6–12) begins here; each verse of it reads alone as well as in the psalm. The colon's final ':' is DO's (25:7 is its purpose clause).

7

ut áudiam … et enárrem → 'Para ouvir … e narrar': the purpose infinitives (the subject is the speaker's own). vocem laudis → 'a voz do louvor' (laus → louvor). enarráre → narrar (glossary), mirabília → 'maravilhas' (the family of mirábilis).

8

dilígere → amar (glossary).

10

In quorum mánibus iniquitátes sunt → 'Em cujas mãos há iniquidades' ('há' for sunt, as Portuguese says existence). déxtera → 'a direita' (glossary); 'deles' rather than 'sua', so that it is not heard as God's (named in 25:9). repléta est → 'está cheia'. The natural order ends the verse on the proparoxytone ‘dádivas’, as the Latin ends on munéribus (draft 1 had turned the colon; the stylist's worst line).

11

Ego autem → 'Mas eu' (glossary; 16:15). The first colon is 25:1's words, the verse ending on 'inocência' in both.

12

benedícere (man → God), accusative → 'vos bendirei' (glossary). The Tract of Monday in the second week of Lent adapts this verse with other words ('Rédime me … pes enim meus stetit in via recta: in ecclésiis benedícam Dóminum') — not this psalm's Latin.
