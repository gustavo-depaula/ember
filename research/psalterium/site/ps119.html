5

minor

“muito tempo foi forasteira a minha alma” multum íncola fuit ánima mea

Latin 'multum' is a bare adverb ('much, greatly'); adding 'tempo' narrows it to duration, resolving an ambiguity the Latin keeps (though duration is the likely sense given 'prolongátus est').

proposedmuito foi forasteira a minha alma

kept as an option under multum íncola fuit ánima mea — bare 'muito' is heard as degree, a wrong narrowing after 'se prolongou'; duration is what DRB and MS1932 hear and what the Latinist calls likely.

A faithful rendering: sense, tense, person and the concrete images (arrows, desolating coals, Cedar) are all kept, and the pointing marks match the Latin in kind and order in every verse. The only point is the supplied 'tempo' in 119:5, which slightly narrows 'multum'; it is defensible and could stand.

4stylist

5 remarks; 3 taken (119:1 'e me escutou', 119:5 'porque', 119:7 'eles me combatiam'), 2 kept as options (119:3 'à', 119:4 'brasas'). Best line 119:2; worst 119:7.

claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-23 · critic/v1.stylist.json · read prayed.vos.json

1

fails: sayable

“e ele me escutou”

The 'e ele' hiatus is a small snag at the very close of a short colon, and the stressed pronoun makes the ending sound conversational. The subject is already clear from 'Ao Senhor'.

proposedAo Senhor clamei, quando estava atribulado: * e me escutou.

❧ taken

3

fails: sayable

“para a língua enganadora”

'para a' gives a heavy vowel hiatus (pa-ra-a) right after the mark, and 'para' sounds like direction or purpose, so the ear hesitates. Contracting it keeps the Latin's openness and moves more smoothly.

proposedQue te será dado, ou que te será acrescentado, * à língua enganadora?

kept as an option under ad linguam dolósam — after 'te', 'à língua' is heard as apposition (you, the tongue), leaning to the vocative reading and closing the Latin's open 'ad'.

4

fails: concrete

“carvões devastadores”

In Brazilian ears 'carvões' is charcoal for the grill: cold, black, a commodity. 'carbones' here are burning coals, and 'brasas' is the plain, concrete word for them. It also scans better before the final.

proposedSetas agudas do poderoso, * com brasas devastadoras.

kept as an option under carbónibus — glossary row carbónes → carvões (Ps 17); a change should be made for the whole row, and 'devastadores' already says they burn.

5

fails: plain, sayable

“Ai de mim, que a minha peregrinação se prolongou”

Causal 'que' sounds slightly archaic and can be misheard as an exclamative or a relative. The colon is also long, and the article-possessive 'a minha' adds a syllable the breath doesn't need.

proposedAi de mim, porque se prolongou minha peregrinação: † habitei com os habitantes de Cedar: * muito tempo foi forasteira a minha alma.

❧ taken — 'porque' taken; dropping the article before the possessive and moving the verb refused (rule 5; order change gains nothing once 'porque' is in).

7

fails: native, cadence

“quando lhes falava, me combatiam sem motivo”

Opening a clause with a clitic after the comma ('me combatiam') is everyday speech. In this register it jars, and it stacks two unstressed clitics ('lhes', 'me') close together. Naming the subject fixes the placement without enclisis sounding stiff.

proposedCom os que odiavam a paz, eu era de paz: * quando lhes falava, eles me combatiam sem motivo.

❧ taken

A sober, faithful, and mostly sayable psalm. The Latin's repetitions ('habitei com os habitantes', 'que te será... que te será') are kept and sound natural. The slips are small: one colloquial clitic, one archaic causal 'que', and a few hiatuses where the breath catches. 'Carvões' is the one word that weakens an image.

worst line119:7

best line119:2

7

minor

“os que odiavam a paz” qui odérunt pacem

odérunt is a preteritive perfect with present sense ('who hate peace'), describing the enemies' abiding character; the imperfect 'odiavam' ties it to the past narrative of eram/loquébar. Defensible by sequence of tenses, but the Latin keeps the present-state force.

proposedCom os que odeiam a paz, eu era de paz: * quando lhes falava, eles me combatiam sem motivo.

kept as an option under qui odérunt pacem — beside eu era / falava Portuguese needs the past for sequence (the Latinist calls it defensible); odeiam against eu era jars in the ear; MS1932 odiavam.

A faithful rendering: every verse carries the Latin's content, including the Septuagintal 'Quid detur tibi... ad linguam dolosam' and 'carbonibus desolatoriis', without smoothing toward the Hebrew. Pointing marks match the Latin in kind and order throughout (the omitted '(6)' is a verse-number insertion, not a pointing mark). Only a slight tense point in v. 7 is worth considering.

Verse by verse

1

Titulus: the Latin (DO) has no 'Canticum graduum' line (D7; collation/report.md lists tituli as omitted), and Ps 133 — also a Gradual Psalm — carries none; so Ps 119 has none, and Pss 120–133 should have none. For the day tituli are added, the formula is proposed in glossary.md (Formulas) as 'Cântico dos degraus' (open). The verse opens on 'Ao Senhor', as the Latin opens on 'Ad Dóminum'. The Brazilian Liturgia das Horas (CNBB) has 'e ele me escutou' here — fetched, ps119/circulation.md; it is a Hebrew-family text and counted only as circulation, not sense. v1 ambiguity reader listed 'atribulado' as unknown; kept (the glossary's family; 'na minha tribulação' is option 1 of tribularer).

2

líbera → livrar (glossary). lábia iníqua → 'lábios iníquos' (iníquos is paroxytone: i-NÍ-quos). lingua dolósa → 'língua enganadora', as 108:3 and the dolósus row (which lists 119:2–3); the same two words are in the CNBB Liturgia das Horas line of this psalm (circulation only). v1 ambiguity reader listed 'iníquos' as unknown and heard 'a minha alma' as the spiritual soul first; both kept — glossary rows iníquus and ánima mea, which note the same cost.

3

The repeated 'língua enganadora' of 119:2–3 repeats as the Latin's does.

4

Verbless, as the Latin: the line stands as an answer to 119:3. 'Setas agudas' is 44:6's build (acútus of a point → agudo, D44 row novácula). No article, as Matos Soares 1932.

5

DO's inline (6) is not reproduced. Cedar kept as the name (Qedar), no gloss. 'Ai de mim, que …' renders 'Heu mihi, quia': the exclamative 'que' is the natural Portuguese causal after 'Ai de mim' (grammar, D2). 'Cedar' was listed as unknown by the v1 ambiguity reader; a proper name, kept without gloss.

7

The Latin's 'Cum his, qui' is 'with these, who'; 'Com os que' is the Portuguese form (grammar). No subject supplied for 'impugnábant' — 'os que odiavam a paz' is the only candidate.
