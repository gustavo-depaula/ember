Doublet (rule 6): 59:7–14 = 107:6b–14, and Ps 107 is not yet translated, so nothing to copy from it; Ps 56 overlaps 107:2–6 only, not this psalm. 59:12b copies 43:10b (D43). Liturgical uses found by grep in content/do/horas/Latin: the Matins antiphon *Da nobis * Dómine auxílium de tribulatióne* (Psalmi matutinum), versicles from 59:13 in a local office of Our Lady (aliquibus locis/08-SanteDultim), 59:6 in the office of St Emygdius (Urbis/08-09). 'in sancto suo' is left open in the Latin (his holy place / his sanctuary / his holiness / his holy one); supplying 'lugar' resolves it toward the place. Defensible (Douay reads 'holy place'), but a noun that keeps the ambiguity is closer.

minor

Only two minor points, 'lugar santo' resolving 'in sancto suo' and 'contra' for 'de'. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex or second mediant).

fails: sayable, native

“vos irastes, e tivestes piedade de nós”

Opening the second colon on the unstressed clitic 'vos', right after 'Deus, vós nos' in the first colon, makes a blur of vós/vos/nos for the ear; in choir 'vos irastes' is heard as 'vós irastes' (wrong) or just stumbles. Enclisis gives the colon a firm stressed start.

proposedirastes-vos, e tivestes piedade de nós.

fails: plain

“no seu lugar santo”

'lugar' is supplied and settles 'in sancto suo' as a place, which the Latin leaves open (holy place / holiness / holy one); it also adds a syllable of padding before the mark.

proposedDeus falou no seu santo: * eu me alegrarei, e repartirei Siquém: e medirei o vale das tendas.

❧ taken

The psalm sounds plain, firm and genuinely Portuguese; the vós forms are handled cleanly, the cadences land well on almost every mark, and the concrete images (fraturas, calçado, panela, cidade fortificada) are kept without softening. The only real stumbles are the proclitic 'vos' in the opening verse and the heavy 'de diante do' in 59:6. 'Panela' is accurate but homely to a Brazilian ear; it should stay for the image, but a cantor will feel it.

minor

“Dai-nos auxílio na tribulação” Da nobis auxílium de tribulatióne

'de tribulatione' is help out of / against the tribulation (Douay 'from trouble'), not help given while in it; 'na' shifts the preposition's sense from source/deliverance to circumstance.

proposedDai-nos auxílio contra a tribulação

Verse by verse

3

*irásci → irar-se*, *miseréri → ter piedade de* (rows). The Latin's two perfects, anger and mercy, are kept as the past (the Hebrew's imperative 'restore us' is not the Latin's). *et … et* kept as *e*; MS1932's *porém* interprets. Bare vocative *Deus*, as in the finished psalms; the pronoun *vós* supplied at the head (D2), as 60:4 *Vós me guiastes*. **v2:** *irastes-vos* (the stylist): enclisis gives the second colon a stressed start and clears the *vós / vos / nos* blur.

4

*commovére → abalar* (17:8, 45:7), *conturbáre → perturbar* (row), *sanáre → curar* (row; the row names 59:4).

5

*dura* (neuter plural) → *coisas duras* (DRB, MS1932). *potáre* → *dar a beber*.

6

*metuéntes te* → *os que vos temem*: the same Greek (φοβούμενοι) as *timéntes te* (118:63 row). The verse ends on a colon because it runs on into 59:7 (*ut … ut*), and the Portuguese repeats *para que … Para que* as the Latin repeats *ut*.

7

*liberáre → livrar* (row), passive *sejam livrados*. *dilécti tui → os vossos amados* (the *diléctus* row). *déxtera → a direita* (row). *exaudíre → escutar* (D3). 107:6b–7a has the same words and should copy them.

8

*lætábor → eu me alegrarei*: the pronoun supplied (D2) so the colon does not open on a clitic and so that the oracle is heard to begin. *partíri → repartir* (= 21:18 *repartiram*, where the Latin is divídere; 107:8 *dívidam* is the same Greek, διαμεριῶ, and can copy it). *metíri → medir*. The Latin's second colon has an inner colon (*Síchimam:*), kept.

9

*fortitúdo → força* (row). Names in their Vulgate-family Portuguese forms, as MS1932 prints them (Galaad, Manassés, Efraim, Judá, Moab, Idumeia, Siquém), not the Hebrew-based ones (Gileade). 107:9 differs here (*suscéptio*).

11

*cívitas muníta → cidade fortificada* (row; the row names 59:11). *usque in → até*.

12

The second colon is 43:10's word for word (*e não saireis, Deus, com as nossas forças*, D43), with the Latin's question mark; *virtútes nostræ → forças* (D43 names 59:12b). The ambiguity reader heard *Não sereis vós … que nos repelistes?* as a reproach first, the link to 59:11 not obvious; the Latin is as bare. *Quem, senão vós* (MS1932) stays the option in `nonne`.

13

*quia vana salus hóminis → porque é vã a salvação do homem*: *salus → salvação* (D6); the copula supplied. The ambiguity reader heard *é vã a salvação do homem* possibly as 'man's salvation is in vain'; the genitive is as open in the Latin (*salus hóminis*) and in DRB — kept.

14

*ad níhilum dedúcere → reduzir a nada* (row; names 59:14). *tribulántes nos → os que nos atribulam* (row; unknown to several blind readers, kept for the family of *tribulação* — and *tribulátio* stands in 59:13 just before). *ipse → ele mesmo* (MS1932), the emphasis kept.

9b

*panela*: the stylist kept it for the image ('a cantor will feel it'); the ambiguity reader noted the slang *panela* = a clique, and that the contempt is not grasped. Kept: the Latin's image, concrete (rule 5).
