14

minor

“revestida de variedade” circumamícta varietátibus

The Latin plural 'varietatibus' is made singular. That blurs the deliberate contrast with 'circumdata varietate' (singular) in 10b and changes number.

proposedrevestida de variedades

kept as an option under circúmdata varietáte … circumamícta varietátibus — As 44:10b; *variedades* is a variety show.

“Toda a glória da filha do Rei” Omnis glória ejus fíliæ Regis

The resumptive 'ejus' ('her glory, of the King's daughter') is dropped. It is a pleonastic Septuagintal construction that the Latin keeps, and the Portuguese can keep it too.

proposedToda a sua glória, da filha do Rei, é de dentro

kept as an option under Omnis glória ejus fíliæ Regis — Grammar (D2); reads as a mid-sentence correction in Portuguese; DRB and MS1932 drop it.

18a

minor

“de geração em geração” in omni generatióne et generatiónem

Replaces the Latin formula with the stock idiom, which drops 'omni' and the preposition 'in' with its odd ablative/accusative doubling.

proposedem toda geração e geração

kept as an option under in omni generatióne et generatiónem — Not Portuguese; the formula row (and MS1932) holds.

12

minor

“o teu esplendor” decórem tuum

'decor' means comeliness or beauty of form, not radiance. 'esplendor' shifts the sense slightly.

proposeda tua formosura

kept as an option under Et concupíscet Rex decórem tuum — *formosura* is *spécies*' in 44:5a; the decor row keeps the three words of beauty apart.

The translation is faithful and close to the Latin throughout, keeps the Septuagintal oddities (gutta, ex quibus, vultum deprecabuntur, pro patribus tuis), and has every pointing mark in the right order. The only faults are small: a number change in 14 (varietatibus), the dropped resumptive ejus in 14, the idiomatic flattening of 'in omni generatione et generationem' in 18a, and a slight sense shift for decor in 12.

The psalm keeps the Latin's ceremonial shape and mostly sounds like dignified Portuguese, especially in the bride's section (11, 15b–18). It stumbles where literalism produces abstract or foreign-sounding nouns ('gota', 'variedade', 'com prosperidade') and where relative or comparative constructions go stiff ('das quais', 'mais que aos'). Two mediant cadences (3, 13) fall on proparoxytones that can be avoided by reordering or by the plainer word.

5a

minor

“avança próspero” próspere procéde

próspere is an adverb qualifying the going forward; 'próspero' as a predicative adjective shifts it to a quality of the king. Defensible, but the adverb is closer.

proposedatende, avança prosperamente, e reina.

kept as an option under próspere procéde — *prosperamente* is heavy and rare; *próspero* keeps the root and the three quick imperatives (stylist, v1).

9

minor

“de onde” ex quibus

ex quibus is a plural relative whose antecedent the Latin leaves open (the ivory houses, or the spices/garments); 'de onde' leans to a place reading. 'das quais' keeps the relative and the ambiguity.

proposeddas quais te deleitaram as filhas dos reis na tua honra.

kept as an option under ex quibus — Both possible antecedents (garments, ivory houses) are sources, which *de onde* keeps open; *das quais* made *das* three times running and was hard to parse aloud (stylist, v1).

minor

varietas is 'variety/diversity'; 'cores' supplies the interpretation (many-coloured garment) that the Latin leaves implicit.

kept as an option under circúmdata varietáte … circumamícta varietátibus — *variedade* is heard as an assortment, not a variegated cloth (stylist, blind reader); the cost of *cores* is recorded in the decision.

Verse by verse

2a

*dico ego* → *eu digo* (the pronoun is the Latin's). *Regi* capitalised as DO has it; *Rei* throughout (6, 12, 14, 15b, 16). *verbum bonum* → *uma boa palavra* (verbum → palavra, D15).

2b
