1:70

minor

“que são desde sempre” qui a sǽculo sunt

'a saeculo' means 'from of old / from the beginning of the age', not 'from always'; 'desde sempre' edges toward eternity, which the Latin does not claim of the prophets.

❧ taken

1:71

minor

“Salvação dos nossos inimigos” Salútem ex inimícis nostris

'ex' is unambiguously 'from/out of'; Portuguese 'dos' at the head of the line most naturally reads as a genitive ('our enemies' salvation'). The parallel 'da mão de todos' helps, but the first half on its own can say what the Latin does not.

proposedSalvação contra os nossos inimigos

❧ taken — Fixed with "Salvação que nos livra dos" rather than "contra", which leaves "e da mão" hanging (decision salutem).

A close and faithful rendering: every clause of the Latin is present, tenses and moods are kept (vocaberis, praeibis, serviamus), 'cornu salutis', 'viscera misericordiae' and 'oriens ex alto' keep their concrete images, and the pointing marks (+ and * in 68, a single * elsewhere) match the Latin exactly. The only points are two minor ones: 'a saeculo' slightly overstated as 'desde sempre', and 'ex inimicis' rendered with a 'dos' that can be misread as a genitive.

5stylist

claude-opus-5-5, fresh context, read latin.json. Five remarks: three taken, two kept as options. Worst line 1:71, best 1:78.

claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-23 · critic/v1.stylist.json · read prayed.vos.json

1:70

fails: native, plain

“dos seus profetas, que são desde sempre”

'que são desde sempre' makes it sound as if the prophets exist eternally. 'Desde sempre' is a colloquial 'forever' and sounds careless after 'pela boca dos santos'. The Latin 'a saeculo' means 'from of old', and 'saeculum' can stay concrete.

proposeddos seus profetas, desde os séculos antigos:

❧ taken — Taken as "desde os tempos antigos" with the Latinist; the stylist's form is an option.

1:71

fails: native

“Salvação dos nossos inimigos”

Heard aloud with no context, the genitive reads first as 'our enemies' salvation': the ear takes 'dos' as possessive, not as 'from'. The parallel 'da mão de todos' in the second half fixes this only after the listener has already misheard.

proposedSalvação, livres dos nossos inimigos, * e da mão de todos os que nos odeiam.

❧ taken — Fixed with the relative "que nos livra dos".

1:74

fails: native, sayable

“livrados ... sirvamos a ele”

'sirvamos a ele' is a stiff way to use the stressed pronoun. After 'que', a proclitic 'o sirvamos' is the natural Brazilian liturgical order. 'livrados' is correct but heavy. 'livres' is shorter and helps the long first colon fit in one breath.

proposedQue, sem temor, livres da mão dos nossos inimigos, * o sirvamos.

kept as an option under serviámus illi — livrados is the liberáre row's participle (123:7b); "sirvamos a ele" keeps the Latin's dative and length. Both kept as options for Gustavo (decisions liberati, serviamus).

1:77

fails: native

“dar o saber da salvação”

'o saber' used as a noun sounds academic and slightly off here, as if it were a body of knowledge. Portuguese prayer normally says 'conhecimento' (or the traditional 'ciência') for 'scientia salutis'.

proposedPara dar ao seu povo o conhecimento da salvação: * para a remissão dos seus pecados:

kept as an option under sciéntiam salútis — The sciéntia row (18:3, 72:11, 93:10, 138:6) has passed readers in four psalms; conhecimento is its named option. For Gustavo with the row.

1:79

fails: native

“dirigir os nossos pés ao caminho da paz”

'dirigir ... ao caminho' is an unidiomatic preposition. With a verb of motion, the ear expects 'para o caminho' ('in viam', motion toward).

proposedpara dirigir os nossos pés para o caminho da paz.

❧ taken

The canticle mostly sounds dignified and prayable: it keeps the concrete images (the horn of salvation, the bowels of mercy, the Dawn from on high) and its cadences fall well at the marks. The weak spots are small idiomatic slips: an ambiguous 'dos' in 71, a stiff pronoun in 74, 'o saber' in 77, and a preposition in 79. These make a few lines sound translated rather than native.

worst line1:71

best line1:78

1:70

minor

“pela boca dos santos, * dos seus profetas, que são desde os tempos antigos” per os sanctórum, * qui a sǽculo sunt, prophetárum ejus

The Latin hyperbaton most naturally reads sanctorum ... prophetarum as one phrase ('his holy prophets'); splitting it into 'the saints, his prophets' makes 'santos' a separate noun with 'profetas' in apposition, a reading the Latin allows but does not favour. The relative clause is also moved after 'profetas', though it stays in the second half-verse, so the pointing is unaffected.

proposedPela boca dos seus santos, * que são desde os tempos antigos, os seus profetas:

❧ taken

1:77

minor

“ao seu povo: * para a remissão dos seus pecados” plebi ejus: * in remissiónem peccatórum eórum

The Latin separates ejus (God's people) from eorum (the people's sins, plural). Using 'seu' twice blurs that distinction, and the second 'seus' could be read as referring to the same possessor as the first.

proposedpara a remissão dos pecados deles

kept as an option under in remissiónem peccatórum eórum — The v1 ambiguity reader heard "seus" as the people's, so the blur is not heard; "deles" is a heavy close.

The translation is faithful to the Latin throughout. It keeps the Vulgate perfect 'visitávit' in 1:78 against the Greek future, renders 'óriens' as a noun ('o Oriente'), and preserves the concrete images (chifre, entranhas, face, pés). The marks match the Latin in every verse: '+' and '*' in 1:68, a single '*' elsewhere. The only notes are two minor ones: how 'sanctorum ... prophetarum' is split in 1:70, and how the possessive pronouns in 1:77 are distinguished.

Verse by verse

1:68

DO keys the canticle by Luke's own verses (1:68–1:79) and prints '+' after the first word; both reproduced, as ps233 did (the '+' is where the sign of the cross is made, not a flex; it stands where the Latin has it, after the first word). Heading line (Canticum Zachariæ * Luc. 1:68-79) not translated (D7). The head is the settled formula of 40:14 / 71:18 / 105:48 (Bendito o Senhor, Deus de Israel), which the glossary row says the canticle should copy; verbless, as the Latin. quia → porque. 'visitou' is left without an object, as the Latin and Greek leave it (the CNBB and DM1962 make 'o seu povo' its object). plebis → povo (233; plebs row). The CNBB and Nova Vulgata futures elsewhere do not concern this verse; our Latin has perfects throughout.

1:69

'para nós' before the object (see cornu). David → Davi (the psalter's form). The comma before 'seu servo' marks the apposition, as 104:42.

1:70

sicut → como; locútus est → falou; per os → pela boca. v2: "desde os tempos antigos" (decision asaeculo). The Latin's hyperbaton (sanctórum … prophetárum ejus) is kept across the asterisk: 'dos santos, * dos seus profetas' — the ambiguity whether sanctórum is a noun or the adjective of prophetárum is the Latin's own; moving 'dos seus profetas' ahead of the relative clause is natural Portuguese order inside the second colon.

1:71

odérunt → 'odeiam' (the perfect of state, present sense: 24:19, 43:11 'os que nos odeiam'). de manu → da mão. v2: "Salvação que nos livra dos" (decision salutem).

1:72

fácere misericórdiam cum → 'usar de misericórdia com', 17:51's build (fáciens misericórdiam Christo suo → usando de misericórdia com). memorári + genitive → 'lembrar-se de' (memor esse row; 104:8, 105:45 'lembrou-se da sua aliança'). testaméntum → aliança (the row; 'testamento' is a will in Brazil). 'santa aliança': the adjective before the noun, as the CNBB and MS1932 ('santo pacto'); 'aliança santa' would end on a stressed pair of short words.

1:73

Jusjurándum is an accusative in apposition to the covenant; 'O juramento' stands in apposition in the same way (DRB 'The oath, which'). ad Ábraham → 'a Abraão' (104:42). patrem nostrum → 'nosso pai', the article dropped in apposition as 104:42 'seu servo'.

1:74

ut → 'Que' after 'conceder' (1:73). sine timóre is placed first as in the Latin, where it can go with 'livrados' or with 'sirvamos' (the Greek ἀφόβως goes with serving); the commas leave both open. serviámus illi → decision serviamus. checks.py flags length −5 on the first colon: the Latin's own long colon (21 syllables, five-syllable words inimicórum nostrórum) against compact Portuguese; nothing is dropped, so left.

1:75

coram ipso → 'diante dele'. One preposition for the pair (In sanctitáte, et justítia), as the Latin; the CNBB repeats it ('em santidade e em justiça'). sanctitas → santidade (29:5). ómnibus diébus nostris → 'todos os nossos dias', ablative of time with no preposition (89:14 has 'em todos os nossos dias', where the Latin has in); the CNBB paraphrases.

1:76

A singular human addressee → tu (rule 3). puer → 'menino' (112:1; παιδίον). paráre → preparar (paráre row, 233). enim → pois. 'para preparar' supplies the preposition Portuguese needs before an infinitive of purpose. checks.py flags +3 and a proparoxytone mediant ('Altíssimo'); the Altíssimus row tolerates it (90:1), and the Latin-order option (decision vocaberis) cures the cadence at the same length.

1:77

plebi ejus → 'ao seu povo'; peccatórum eórum → 'dos seus pecados': with the people just named, 'seus' is heard as theirs.

1:78

víscera → entranhas (D44). 'entranhas de misericórdia' (misericórdiæ as a genitive of quality, the Portuguese idiom), then 'do nosso Deus'; MS1932 has 'da misericórdia'. visitávit → visitou (the visitáre row; the same verb as 1:68, the repetition the Latin has).

1:79

Illumináre is a bare infinitive of purpose continuing 1:78; Portuguese needs 'Para'. his (dative) → 'os que' (iluminar takes a direct object). The Latin's verb comes last in the colon; natural order puts it first so that the colon ends on 'morte'. ténebræ, umbra mortis → trevas, sombra da morte (106:10, 106:14, 87:7). v2: "para o caminho" (decision inviam). pedes → pés; pax → paz. checks.py flags +4 on the first colon: 'Para' and 'estão sentados' (106:10's periphrasis for the one word sedent) are what Portuguese needs; 'se sentam' (option) saves one syllable, 'jazem' two, at the cost the sedent decision records.
