2:4

minor

“Não multipliqueis palavras altivas” Nolíte multiplicáre loqui sublímia

The Latin multiplies the act of speaking (infinitive loqui) lofty things; the translation turns the verb into a noun 'palavras'. Sense survives, but the construction is flattened.

6stylist

Reader: claude-opus-5-5, fresh context, with latin.json. 8 remarks: 3 taken, 5 refused or kept as options. Worst line 2:7, best 2:10.

claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-23 · critic/v1.stylist.json · read prayed.vos.json

2:1

fails: native

“e foi exaltado o meu chifre no meu Deus”

The inverted passive (verb before subject) reads as translation, and the second colon piles up 'meu ... meu' behind a delayed subject. The plain order keeps the exultou/exaltado echo and sounds more like Portuguese.

proposede o meu chifre foi exaltado no meu Deus.

not taken — The Latin's order, and the psalter's for this very phrase: 74:11, 88:18, 88:25, 91:11 all *será exaltado o … chifre*. The verb next to *exultou* also keeps the echo at the colon's head.

2:2

fails: native, plain

“Abriu-se bem a minha boca”

'bem' is a filler standing in for 'dilatatum'. 'Abriu-se bem' sounds like a dentist's instruction, and the ear trips on the weak monosyllable after the clitic.

proposedDilatou-se a minha boca contra os meus inimigos

❧ taken — With the Latinist.

2:5

fails: plain, native

“o Deus dos saberes”

'saberes' as a plural noun belongs to the vocabulary of pedagogy and academic prose, so it sounds modern and jargony in prayer. Something plainer keeps the plural 'scientiarum'.

proposedporque o Senhor é o Deus dos conhecimentos

not taken — *sciéntia → o saber* is the glossary's row (18:3, 72:11); *conhecimento* would be a new word for it, and no plainer. *o Deus do saber* stays option 2 if the plural should go.

2:7

fails: native, concrete

“se empregaram por pão”

'empregar-se' calls up a modern job search and blurs the image of hiring oneself out for bread. 'estavam fartos ... se empregaram' also lands flat before the mediant.

proposedOs que antes eram fartos alugaram-se por pão:

kept as an option under pro pánibus se locavérunt — *alugar-se* is the literal verb but in Brazil is heard of renting property; the ambiguity reader heard *se empregaram* as intended, 'hired themselves out for bread'. *se alugaram* stays option 2. *estavam fartos* kept: the state before the fall.

2:9

fails: native, cadence

“conduz aos infernos e traz de volta”

'traz de volta' is colloquial and loses the paired prefix of deducit/reducit. The verse ends on a weak two-word phrase instead of a single stressed verb.

proposedconduz aos infernos e reconduz.

kept as an option under dedúcit ad ínferos et redúcit — The Latinist's *faz descer* took the verb; *reconduzir* is heard of officials. Option 3.

2:12

fails: plain

“e ocupe um trono de glória”

'ocupe' sounds administrative (someone occupies a post), whereas 'teneat' means to hold or possess.

proposede tenha um trono de glória.

not taken — *tenha um trono* is weak and possessive; *ocupar um trono* is the ordinary Portuguese for a king in his seat (MS1932). *tenha* stays option 3.

2:13

fails: concrete, native

“os polos da terra”

'polos' first suggests the North and South Poles, a geography-class image, so the Latin's sense of hinges or pivots is lost.

proposedPorque do Senhor são os eixos da terra,

❧ taken — The ambiguity reader heard the same; *eixos* is the pivot, cardo's own sense.

2:14

fails: sayable

“porque não é pela sua força que o homem se fortalecerá”

The cleft 'não é ... que' makes the final colon long and prosaic, so it runs to the end of the breath before reaching its stressed close.

proposedporque o homem não se fortalecerá pela sua força.

❧ taken — Order only (D2); every word kept.

Overall the canticle sounds sober and rhythmic, and the paired antitheses of 2:9-2:11 make it strong for the choir. Most of the weak spots are single words with a modern or technical colour (saberes, se empregaram, polos, ocupe) or small fillers ('bem', 'traz de volta'), not structural faults. The images (chifre, esterco, arco) are kept concrete, which is good.

2:2

minor

“Dilatou-se a minha boca contra os meus inimigos” Dilatátum est os meum super inimícos meos

super is 'over, above' (the mouth opened wide in triumph over the enemies); 'contra' narrows it to hostility against them, a slight interpretive shift.

proposedDilatou-se a minha boca sobre os meus inimigos

❧ taken — 21:14 *sobre mim a sua boca*, 58:12 *sobre os meus inimigos*; the triumph is the sense.

2:4

minor

“Não multipliqueis palavras altivas” Nolíte multiplicáre loqui sublímia

The Latin has the infinitive loqui (to speak) with sublímia (lofty things) as its object; the translation turns the act of speaking into a noun 'palavras', and 'altivas' leans toward 'arrogant' where sublímia is 'lofty, high things'. Defensible, but closer is possible.

proposedNão multipliqueis o falar de coisas altivas

kept as an option under Nolíte multiplicáre loqui sublímia — Portuguese *multiplicar* takes no infinitive; one noun for verb and object is grammar (D2), MS1932's. His wording is option 2. *altivas* for *sublímia* beside *gloriántes* is the haughty talk the context means (L&S).

2:7

minor

“se empregaram por pão” pro pánibus se locavérunt

pánibus is plural (loaves); the translation makes it singular.

proposedse empregaram por pães

not taken — D42 (settled): *panes → pão*, the idiom.

A faithful and close rendering of the Canticle of Anna: the Septuagintal features of the Latin (the horn, the God of knowledges, ad ínferos, Christi sui) are kept, and no verse adds or drops content. Verse by verse

2:1

Heading *(Canticum Annæ * 3 Reg 2:1-16)* not translated (D7, as 211, 213, 233). *exsultáre / exaltáre → exultar / exaltar* (the row); 12:6b *O meu coração exultará* is the same subject-first build. *in Dómino … in Deo meo* kept as two phrases.

2:2

*lætáta sum* is feminine (Anna); Portuguese *me alegrei* has no gender, nothing lost. *in salutári tuo → na vossa salvação* (D6; 9:16a). The sudden *tuo* (to God) is the Latin's.

2:3

*extra te → fora de vós* (MS1932 *fora de ti*). No † in DO, so the first colon carries two clauses.

2:7

*repléti* → *fartos*: those full of bread (the repléri row gives *estar farto* where the fullness is the point, 122:3). *famélici → famintos* (as *esuriéntes*, 106:5; the two never meet here). *saturáre → saciar*, passive as the row.

2:8

*infirmári → enfraquecer* (the row: 9:4, 106:12).

2:11

*suscitáre → levantar*, *stercus → esterco*, *pauper → pobre* (112:7's build), *egénus → necessitado* (34:10b, 40:2, 69:6, 81:3–4, 108:22). No subject named: the Lord of 2:10 goes on.

2:13

*orbis → o mundo* (D30). *pónere super → pôr sobre*.

2:14

*serváre* of feet (a person kept safe) → *guardar*; the row's *observar* is for a law. MS1932 *guardará os pés*.

2:15

*tonáre → trovejar* (17:14, 28:3 *intonáre → trovejar*). *in cælis → nos céus*.
