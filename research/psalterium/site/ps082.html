1draft

Psalm-level draft from the parallels (no Diurnal: not a day-hour psalm). Hard readings: 82:4 malignavérunt consílium (transitive, only here), 82:5 de gente (ἐξ ἔθνους), 82:19 nomen tibi Dóminus. Glossary departures: cogitáre → maquinar (absolute and hostile, 82:4, 82:6; decision cogit); fácies plural → rostos (82:17). Names as MS1932 prints them, but Madiã.

2checks

Hard pass. Soft: 82:6b −5 (the Latin's long list colon; fine), 82:11b +5 and 82:18b +3, both accepted (see choices). Proparoxytone mediants at 82:10 (Sísara, as the Latin's Sísaræ), 82:13 (príncipes, as the Latin's own colon runs) and 82:18 (the formula) accepted. No rhyme flags.

3latinist

claude-opus-5-5, fresh context, with latin.json. No remarks: tenses, moods, persons, images and names all found faithful; 'formaram com malícia um desígnio' and 'como nação' judged within the Latin's sense.

Nothing to correct in any verse.

The translation follows the Latin closely throughout: tenses (future persequeris/turbabis/quaerent), moods (jussive erubescant/conturbentur/confundantur/pereant/cognoscant) and persons are preserved, the concrete images (rota, stipula, stercus terrae, ignis/flamma) are kept, and the proper names are all present. Paraphrastic touches such as 'formaram com malícia um desígnio' (malignaverunt consilium) and 'como nação' (de gente) are within the sense of the Latin, and the pointing marks match the Latin (one mediant per verse, in the same place).

4

fails: plain, native

“formaram com malícia um desígnio”

'formar um desígnio' reads like a calque; the verb is flat and the colon is heavy before the mediant. Portuguese says one 'trama' or 'urde' a design.

proposedContra o vosso povo tramaram com malícia um desígnio: * e maquinaram contra os vossos santos.

kept as an option under malignavérunt consílium — tramar is concinnáre's verb (D44); MS1932 has 'Formaram desígnios'.

5

fails: native, sayable

“e exterminemo-los como nação”

'como nação' is clipped and sounds ambiguous, as if it meant 'in the manner of a nation'. The enclitic '-mo-los' followed by 'como' also chimes (mo-los co-mo) right at the mediant.

proposedDisseram: Vinde, e exterminemo-los, que deixem de ser nação: * e não seja mais lembrado o nome de Israel.

❧ taken

10

fails: cadence

“e a Sísara: *”

The mediant falls on a proparoxytone ('Sísara'), which the tone cannot land cleanly. Swapping the two names puts the oxytone 'Madiã' at the mark.

proposedFazei-lhes como a Sísara, e a Madiã: * como a Jabin na torrente de Cisson.

kept as an option under Mádian — The order of the names is the Latin's (and Judges'), not a matter of Portuguese grammar; the Latin's own mediant is proparoxytone.

15

fails: plain, native

“que queima a selva”

To a Brazilian ear 'selva' means jungle, an exotic tropical image. 'Floresta' is the plain word for silva, and it has the same cadence at the mediant.

proposedComo o fogo que queima a floresta: * e como a chama que queima os montes:

kept as an option under combúrit silvam — silva → selva is settled (D44); evidence passed to the glossary.

19

fails: sayable

“só vós sois”

Three sibilant monosyllables in a row ('só vós sois') hiss and blur in choir at the start of the final colon.

proposedE conheçam que o vosso nome é Senhor: * vós só sois o Altíssimo em toda a terra.

kept as an option under quia nomen tibi Dóminus: * tu solus Altíssimus in omni terra — The proposal is as sibilant and can be heard 'you are only the Most High'.

The psalm reads with a sober, dignified energy. The vós forms are handled cleanly, and the long catalogue of names moves well to the tone. Only a few spots show the translation through: a calqued collocation in v.4, a clipped phrase in v.5, and one mediant that lands on a proparoxytone. Otherwise the imprecations sound plain and strong, as they should.

5

minor

“exterminemo-los, para não serem nação” disperdámus eos de gente

The Latin is a plain prepositional phrase ('destroy them from [being a] nation'); the Portuguese recasts it as a purpose clause, which slightly explains rather than renders. Defensible as the sense, but it could stay closer to the bare 'de gente'.

proposedexterminemo-los de ser nação

kept as an option under disperdámus eos de gente — His fix is not Portuguese; he calls the clause defensible as the sense (DRB 'so that they be not a nation'); draft 1's 'como nação' was the stylist's worst line and double-read by the ambiguity reader.

16

minor

“com a vossa tempestade … na vossa ira” in tempestáte tua … in ira tua

The Latin uses the same 'in' in both halves; the Portuguese splits it into instrumental 'com' and locative 'na', breaking the parallel the Latin keeps.

proposedAssim os perseguireis na vossa tempestade

❧ taken

A faithful rendering of the Latin throughout: tenses (future persequéris/turbábis, jussive subjunctives in 17-19), persons and the Septuagintal forms (Agarenos, Gebal, Endor, stercus terrae) are kept, and the pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. Only two small closeness points: the purpose-clause paraphrase of 'de gente' in v. 5 and the uneven treatment of the repeated 'in' in v. 16.

2

3

Quóniam ecce → 'Porque eis que'. extóllere → erguer (row); odérunt present in sense → 'os que vos odeiam' (odísse row, 73:4, 73:23). The ambiguity reader listed *ressoaram* unknown and heard the uproar only by inference; kept for D43, the periphrases are options of decision sonuerunt.

5

Veníte → 'Vinde' (33:12, 45:9); dispérdere → exterminar (row); memorári (passive) → 'não seja mais lembrado' — MS1932 'não haja mais memória' turns the verb into a noun. v2: the first colon is +3 syllables (checks) with the clause; accepted for a clear hearing (decision degente).
