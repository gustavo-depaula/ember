proposedAdonai, Senhor, vós sois grande, e esplêndido em vosso poder, * e ninguém vos pode superar.

❧ taken

16:17

fails: plain

“e as coisas foram feitas”

The supplied subject 'as coisas' is prosy and flat right where the colon should land firmly. 'Dissestes, e as' also runs into a hiatus.

proposedToda a vossa criatura vos sirva: * porque dissestes, e tudo foi feito:

kept as an option under quia dixísti, et facta sunt … et creáta sunt — *as coisas* is the glossary's formula for this Latin (32:9 = 148:5); *tudo foi feito* turns the plural into a singular and leaves 16:18 *foram criadas* without a subject.

16:19

fails: sayable, native

“Os montes serão abalados desde os fundamentos com as águas”

'Com as águas' trails after 'fundamentos' as an afterthought, and the ear hangs it on the foundations. The colon is also long for one breath. Moving it forward keeps the image and makes the phrase land on 'fundamentos'.

proposedOs montes, com as águas, serão abalados desde os fundamentos: * as rochas, como cera, se derreterão diante da vossa face.

❧ taken

16:21

fails: native, sayable

“contra a minha raça”

In current Brazilian speech 'raça' has a strong ethnic or racial sense, and in slang it means grit ('ter raça'). It jars in prayer. 'Genus' means kin or stock.

proposedAi da nação que se levanta contra a minha linhagem: pois o Senhor todo-poderoso se vingará deles, * no dia do juízo os visitará.

❧ taken

“Ai da nação que se levanta contra a minha raça: pois o Senhor todo-poderoso se vingará deles”

The first half is very long and has no flex to breathe at. Keeping the colon lean helps: 'que se ergue' is a syllable shorter than 'que se levanta'.

proposedAi da nação que se ergue contra a minha linhagem: pois o Senhor todo-poderoso se vingará deles,

not taken — *Insúrgere → levantar-se contra* is the psalter's verb (3:2, 17:40, 58:2, 108:28); a single syllable does not justify a second verb.

16:22

fails: no rhyme, native

“Pois porá fogo”

'Pois porá' puts two p-syllables together and sounds like a stammer at the start of the verse. 'Porá' also flattens 'dabit'.

proposedPois dará fogo e vermes às carnes deles, * para que ardam, e sintam para todo o sempre.

❧ taken — *dará* taken with the Latinist; *às carnes* refused, since *in carnes* is 'into', so *nas carnes* is kept.

The psalm moves with a plain, dignified pace and keeps the Latin's images, and the opening hymn verses sing well. The weak spots are a few prosy fillers ('as coisas', 'excelente'), a trailing phrase in the mountains verse, and 'raça', which carries the wrong modern charge. The long curse in 16:21 is heavy to say in one breath.

16:21

minor

“contra a minha linhagem” super genus meum

genus here is 'my race / my people' (the nation set against 'genti'); 'linhagem' narrows it to line of descent, a family pedigree, and loses the collective 'people' sense.

proposedcontra o meu povo

kept as an option under genus meum — *Povo* is *pópulus*'s word throughout the psalter; DRB and MS1932 merge the two, and the gate on v1 passed *raça*. *Genus* is stock, a people seen as a descent, and *linhagem* keeps that without the ethnic charge two readers heard in *raça*. *O meu povo* stays option 3, the retreat if Gustavo prefers the ear.

A faithful and close rendering: content, tense, mood, person and the concrete images (visitabit, fire and worms in the flesh, mountains moved with the waters) are all kept, and nothing is added or explained. The only point worth tightening is 'genus meum' in 16:21. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (a single mediant each).

Verse by verse

16:15

DO's first line, *(Canticum Judith * Judith 16:15-22)*, is a heading with the book reference, not a prayed verse and not a titulus. readVerses skips it, as it did for the Nunc dimittis (ps233), and D7 keeps tituli out anyway. When the canticle is delivered to DO, its Portuguese heading can stay as DO has it: *(Cântico de Judite * Jdt 16:15-22)*. The verse numbers are DO's, not the Clementine's: DO splits Vulgate 16:17 into 16:17–18 and so runs to 16:22 where the Vulgate ends at 16:21. The Latin is Jerome's Judith (from an Aramaic text), not the Gallican psalter. The LXX is a different recension and explains little. *Hymnum cantémus* → *Cantemos um hino* (natural order; *cantar* is cantáre's and *hino* hymnus's in the glossary). The Latin's repetition *hymnum … cantémus / hymnum … cantémus* is kept whole.

16:16

*Magnus es tu* → *vós sois grande* (75:8 *Vós sois terrível*). *In virtúte tua* → *no vosso poder* (the glossary; 20:2).

16:18

*Misísti spíritum tuum, et creáta sunt* echoes 103:30 *Emíttes spíritum tuum, et creabúntur → Enviareis o vosso espírito, e serão criados*: the same verb, *enviar*, and *espírito*. *Foram criadas* agrees with 16:17's *as coisas* (32:9). *Resístere → resistir* (16:8a, 75:8). The second colon is the Latin's eleven syllables.

16:19

*Petræ → as rochas* (the glossary's *petra → rocha*; plural and definite, since the Latin's plural names rocks in general). *Sicut cera → como cera*, as 96:5 *Os montes, como cera, escorreram*. *Liquéscere → derreter-se*, as 21:15b *cera que se derrete*. *Ante fáciem tuam → diante da vossa face*, the row's form (Nunc dimittis 2:31). *À face de* is kept for *a fácie* (67, 96:5). The second colon is +4 syllables on the Latin; accepted, since every word is a settled one.

16:20

*Qui autem timent te* → *Mas os que vos temem* (118:74 *Os que vos temem*). Six syllables for six.

16:21

The only mark is the mediant; the colon break the Latin prints as a colon after *meum* is punctuation, kept as *:*. *Vindicábit in eis* → *se vingará deles* (98:8 *vos vingastes de*). The plural *eis* after the singular *genti* is kept, as the Latin has it (MS1932 normalises to *dela*). *In die judícii* → *no dia do juízo* (*judícium → juízo*, D15). *Visitábit* → *visitará* (the glossary's *visitáre*). Here the visit is punitive (as 88:33 *Visitarei com a vara*), and that is the Latin's word.

16:22

*Enim → pois*, as in 16:21: the Latin repeats it, and so does the Portuguese. *Et vermes* follows *ignem* under the one verb, as in the Latin.
