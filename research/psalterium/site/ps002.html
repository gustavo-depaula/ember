Verse by verse

1

gentes → nações, pópuli → povos (glossary, Nunc dimittis). meditáti sunt → 'meditaram': the same verb as 1:2 (meditábitur), the link that joins the two psalms — the just man meditates on the law, the peoples meditate empty things. inánia → 'coisas vãs' (Douay-Rheims 'vain things', Matos Soares 1932), not 'vaidades', which is vánitas's word. The Latin's colon before the asterisk is kept inside the question. DM1962 has this psalm (monastic Prime); it was heard for diction only.

2

'advérsus … et advérsus' → 'contra … e contra', the repetition kept. First colon +1 by the checker in draft 2. The ambiguity reader asked whose Christ 'o seu Cristo' is — the Lord's or the kings': ejus is as open in form, and he heard the Lord's.

3

No 'they said' and no quotation marks: the Latin has none (decision 'v3voice'). dirúmpere → romper. jugum → 'jugo' and 2:9 fíguli → 'oleiro' were listed by the ambiguity reader as words a hearer may not know: both are the Latin's concrete images and have no plainer name (rule 5).

4

'in cælis' plural → 'nos céus'. 'Qui hábitat' → 'Aquele que habita' (habitáre → habitar, Ps 90:1).

5

ira → ira, furor → furor. The Latin's chiasm kept (falará … na sua ira / no seu furor … conturbará); 'lhes' for ad eos.

6

'Ego autem' → 'Eu, porém'. 'Rei' with the Latin's capital. 'super Sion montem sanctum ejus' → 'sobre Sião, o seu monte santo'. The speaker is the Anointed, as in the Latin and the Greek; nothing marks the turn but 'Eu, porém', as in the Latin — the ambiguity reader heard 'a king begins to speak of himself; who he is is not said', which is exactly the Latin's state. The article stays in 'o seu monte santo' (glossary) against the stylist's 'seu monte santo'. The first colon is the longest of the psalm (23 syllables in Latin, no flex): the stylist found it heavy for one breath, and nothing can be dropped from it.

7

'dixit ad me' → 'me disse' (Brazilian proclisis). The second colon is the Latin word for word: ego hódie génui te → 'eu hoje te gerei'.

8

postuláre → pedir ('Pede-me'): the plain verb; pétere will share it. 'dabo tibi' → 'eu te darei': the pronoun supplied for the rhythm (Matos Soares 1932 does the same). The double accusative → 'como tua herança … como tua posse', without the article, which a predicate complement does not take. Draft 2 gives up the Latin's chiasm in the second colon for the ear (decision 'v8order').

9

'vas fíguli' → 'vaso de oleiro'. Both verbs of the verse keep the object before them where the Latin has it after; the verse closes on the verb.

10

'reges' → 'reis', bare vocative (no 'ó' unless the Latin has 'o'). intellégite → 'entendei': safe under rule 3 (past 'entendi'). 'qui judicátis' → 'vós que julgais'.

11

See the decisions 'servite', 'ei' and 'v11b'. 'exultai' safe under rule 3. The ambiguity reader heard 'no temor' as fear of punishment before reverence, and 'com tremor' as bodily trembling: timor and tremor are no gentler.

12

disciplína → disciplina: the ambiguity reader heard 'a regulated, obedient conduct' first, 'instruction and correction' second, 'punishment' third — the range of disciplína (παιδεία). períre → perecer (Ps 1:6). He also asked whether 'e não pereçais' is a second purpose or a new command: he heard the purpose, rightly.

13

beáti → 'bem-aventurados', as ruled in Ps 1:1 — the two psalms are framed by the word. confídere in → confiar em (glossary, Ps 117:8). Both cola +3 by the checker: 'Quando‿em' and 'se‿inflamar' elide in the mouth, and 'bem-aventurados' is the price ruled on in Ps 1.
