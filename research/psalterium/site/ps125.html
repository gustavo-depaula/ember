5

minor

“com exultação ceifarão” in exsultatióne metent

The Latin sets 'in lácrimis' against 'in exsultatióne' with the same preposition, and keeps 'cum exsultatióne' for 6b; rendering the first 'in' as 'em' and the second as 'com' loses the parallel and merges the two constructions.

proposedem exultação ceifarão

❧ taken

A faithful, close rendering: the future 'dicent', the Septuagintal 'facti sumus sicut consoláti', the pleonastic 'Eúntes ibant' / 'Veniéntes vénient', and 'torrens in Austro' are all kept without smoothing. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only point is the preposition in v. 5, which is defensible as it stands.

5

fails: cadence, native

“Os que semeiam em lágrimas, *”

The mediant falls on a proparoxytone (LÁ-gri-mas), so the stress sits three syllables back and the reciting tone has nowhere to land; the colon also ends on a weak trailing '-mas'. The second colon, 'com exultação ceifarão', is inverted too and ends in a double -ão (exultação ceifarão) that clangs.

proposedOs que em lágrimas semeiam, * ceifarão com exultação.

❧ taken

6a

fails: sayable, native

“Indo, iam e choravam”

'Indo, iam' makes the mouth stumble over the i-i-a hiatus and the comma, and a participle set off in front of its own finite verb does not sound like Portuguese. The repeated root can stay in a form Portuguese already uses, the progressive 'ir indo'.

proposedIam indo e choravam, * lançando as suas sementes.

❧ taken

4

fails: sayable

“como uma torrente no sul”

'como uma' gives a slack run of unstressed syllables (co-mo-u-ma) just before the image, and the indefinite article weakens it. The definite article is lighter and more lapidary, and it does not change the image.

proposedFazei voltar, Senhor, o nosso cativeiro, * como a torrente no sul.

❧ taken

3

fails: native

“tornamo-nos alegres.”

The pronominal 'tornamo-nos' sounds bookish in a colon this short, and the verse ends flatly after the full first colon. 'Ficamos' is the plain Brazilian way to say 'facti sumus' and says the same thing. The same fix would work in 125:1 ('ficamos como consolados').

proposedGrandes coisas fez o Senhor conosco: * ficamos alegres.

❧ taken

The psalm is plain and dignified and keeps the Latin's repetitions ('Grandes coisas fez o Senhor', 'exultação', the fourfold return) without strain. The weak spots are the Hebraic participle doublets in the last strophe and one proparoxytone mediant, which read like a translation rather than prayer. Most cadences land cleanly on oxytones and paroxytones.

worst line125:6a

best line125:2a

claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-23 · critic/v2.latinist.json · read prayed.vos.json

Nothing to correct in any verse.

The translation follows the Latin closely throughout: the gerund 'in convertendo' is rendered as a temporal clause, the Latin idiom 'magnificavit facere' is rendered idiomatically as 'grandes coisas fez' without losing sense, and the Latin's futures ('dicent', 'metent', 'venient') and the pleonastic participle-plus-verb pairs ('euntes ibant', 'venientes venient') are kept. 'Facti sumus laetantes' as 'ficamos alegres' flattens the participle slightly but is defensible. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse.

Verse by verse

1

No title line (*Canticum graduum*): D7, as in ps120 and ps133. The Latin's colon ends with ':' and so does the Portuguese.

2b

*dicent* is future in the Latin and the Greek (ἐροῦσιν) — 'then they will say among the nations' — after the past of 125:1–2a; kept (DRB 'Then shall they say'). The CNBB (from the Hebrew) has *se dizia*. *gentes → nações* (glossary). The quoted words are not set in quotation marks, as the Latin has none; the capital after the colon marks them. The ambiguity reader heard the future *dirão* as clashing with the past of 2a; it is the Latin's own tense shift, kept.

3

The whole verse is the Tuesday Vespers antiphon (Psalmi major, Day2 Vespera) and reads alone.

4

*Convérte* → *Fazei voltar*: the causative the captívitas row prescribes for 125:4; *fazei* is not a homograph of a first-person past (*fiz*). *torrens → torrente* (glossary; 7 lines), singular as the Latin, with an indefinite article as a simile needs. The ambiguity reader heard *no sul* as possibly southern Brazil and the image as unclear; the wind reading stays option 2 of `auster`. He also heard *Fazei voltar … o nosso cativeiro* (and 125:1) as possibly 'make captivity come back upon us', while naming the restoration as the likely hearing — the risk the captívitas row already records; *os cativos* stays the option. Not changed: the glossary row governs 13:7, 52:7, 84:2 too, and a change belongs to the row.

5

v2: the mediant is now *semeiam* (paroxytone); the proparoxytone *lágrimas* of draft 1 moved inside the colon.

6a

*mitténtes sémina sua* → *lançando as suas sementes*: the sower's cast (MS1932 *lançavam*). *semen* → *sementes* here, not *descendência*: D32's exception, the verse itself is a sowing.

6b

*autem* → *porém*, after the participle, where the Latin has it. *manípulos → feixes* (MS1932, CNBB).
