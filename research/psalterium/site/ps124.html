3

fails: plain, sayable

“sobre a sorte dos justos”

To a Brazilian ear 'sorte' means luck first; 'lot, allotted portion' needs a footnote, and the line briefly suggests the 'luck of the just'. The first colon is also long (over 20 syllables) and uses up the breath before the mediant.

proposedPorque o Senhor não deixará a vara dos pecadores sobre a partilha dos justos:

❧ taken — the fault taken, with 'o quinhão' rather than 'a partilha' (the portion received, not the act of dividing; partilhar is distribúere's). 'a partilha' kept as an option.

“estendam as suas mãos à iniquidade”

'as suas mãos' adds an extra unstressed syllable just before the long final word, so the end of the colon drags.

proposedpara que os justos não estendam suas mãos à iniquidade.

not taken — rule 5 keeps the article before possessives.

5

fails: plain, sayable, native

“os que se desviam para as amarras”

'Desviar-se para as amarras' is an odd picture in Portuguese. 'Amarras' suggests mooring ropes, and the ear cannot tell what it means. 'Laços' is the word the psalter tradition uses for bonds and snares, is just as concrete, and keeps the Latin as open as it is.

proposedMas os que se desviam para os laços

❧ taken

“Mas os que se desviam para as amarras, o Senhor os levará com os que praticam a iniquidade:”

At about 27 syllables this is the longest colon in the psalm and cannot be said in one breath. Two 'os que' clauses around a resumptive 'os' blur which group is which.

proposedMas os que se desviam para os laços, o Senhor os levará com os que obram a iniquidade:

not taken — the Latin colon is as long (30 syllables) and cannot be split (rule 4); 'praticar a iniquidade' is the glossary row.

The psalm is short, plain and steady. The circling repetitions ('ao seu redor' / 'ao redor do seu povo') and the quiet blessing of v.4 sit well in the mouth, and the cadences land cleanly on oxytones and paroxytones. Only two things weaken it: the final verse runs long with an opaque 'amarras', and 'sorte' in v.3 pulls toward 'luck'.

Verse by verse

1

No titulus: 'Canticum graduum' is not in DO's psalm file (D7), and Ps 133 and Pss 119–120 carry none; the glossary's proposed formula if tituli are ever added is 'Cântico dos degraus' (open, Ps 119). The DO verse runs into biblical 2a ('(2a)' before 'in Jerúsalem'); the marker is not reproduced. confídere in → confiar em (row); Sion → Sião, 'o monte Sião' (47:12, 73:2b, 77:68). 'qui hábitat' → 'quem habita': the Latin (after the Greek ὁ κατοικῶν) makes the dweller in Jerusalem the subject of 'non commovébitur'; the Hebrew's sense (the mountain abides for ever; the Diurnal's 'estável para sempre') is not followed. 124:1–2b is the Tract of the 4th Sunday of Lent (missa Quad4-0) and of the Dedication (Commune C8), so both verses are made to read alone. v1 ambiguity reader: unsure whether 'quem habita em Jerusalém' is the mountain, the one who trusts, or the city's people — the Latin's own openness (Greek ὁ κατοικῶν); kept.

2b

'et Dóminus in circúitu' → 'e o Senhor está ao redor': 'in circúitu' → 'ao redor (de)' (row), repeated as the Latin repeats it. 'ex hoc nunc, et usque in sǽculum' → 'desde agora e para todo o sempre', identical to 112:2b, 113:26 and 120:8 (ex hoc nunc row; D37); third colon +3 syllables, the fixed formula.

3

relínquere → deixar (the plain verb; Douay-Rheims 'leave', Matos Soares 1932 'deixará', without his gloss 'por muito tempo'); derelínquere keeps 'abandonar'. virga → vara (row), peccátor → pecador, justus → justo (row). 'Quia' → 'Porque' (default, D42). 'manus suas' → 'as suas mãos' with the article (rule 5). v1: the stylist asked 'suas mãos' without the article (a lighter end); refused under rule 5 (the article before possessives). The ambiguity reader heard 'vara' as a stick for beating more than a sceptre: kept, the Latin's concrete image (virga row; 'cetro' is the row's option).

4

rectus corde → 'os retos de coração' (row; the row already lists 124:4).

5

'autem' → 'Mas'. The Latin puts the object first ('Declinántes … addúcet Dóminus'); kept first with a resumptive 'os', which is natural Portuguese; Matos Soares 1932's mesóclise 'levá-los-á' refused as bookish. addúcere → levar (D42; Greek ἀπάξει 'lead away'). operári iniquitátem → 'praticar a iniquidade' (row). v1: the stylist found the first colon too long (about 30 syllables) and asked 'obram' for 'praticam'; refused — the length is the Latin's own (30), the colon cannot be split (rule 4), and 'praticar a iniquidade' is the glossary row. The ambiguity reader noted that 'levará' does not say where; neither does 'addúcet' (Greek ἀπάξει, 'lead away'); kept.
