9

minor

“ficarei mais branco do que a neve” super nivem dealbábor

dealbabor is passive (I shall be whitened), parallel to the passive mundabor, which the translation keeps as 'serei purificado'; 'ficarei mais branco' drops the passive voice and the verbal act of whitening.

proposedserei branqueado mais do que a neve

kept as an option under et super nivem dealbábor — *ficar* + adjective is the Portuguese resultative; *branqueado* is laundry and bleach

16

minor

“do sangue” de sanguínibus

Latin plural (sanguines, 'bloods', i.e. bloodguilt) reduced to singular; the number change is allowed only for need, and the plural is sayable.

proposedLivrai-me dos sangues

not taken — D29 (settled)

“a minha língua exultará na vossa justiça” exsultábit lingua mea justítiam tuam

justitiam is an accusative object: the tongue joyfully proclaims/celebrates God's justice. 'na vossa justiça' turns the object into the place or ground of rejoicing, changing the construction's sense.

proposeda minha língua exultará a vossa justiça

kept as an option under et exsultábit lingua mea justítiam tuam — *exultar* is never transitive in Portuguese nor in the fourteen finished uses; *na* is 12:6b, 34:9

20

minor

“Sede benigno ... com Sião” Benígne fac ... Sion

benigne fac is an action (deal kindly, do good to Sion), not a state; 'sede benigno' turns the imperative of doing into one of being.

proposedTratai benignamente, Senhor, na vossa boa vontade, a Sião

❧ taken

A close, faithful rendering of the Miserere; tenses (future in 9, 10, 17, 18, 19, 21), moods and the Latin's key images are preserved, and every verse carries the same single mediant as the Latin with no flex or second mediant missing or added. The only faults are small: a lost passive in 9, the plural 'sanguinibus' and the accusative object of 'exsultabit' in 16, and 'benigne fac' rendered as a state rather than an act in 20.

The mediant and the final both end in -astes, and it is the heavy 2nd-person-plural preterite both times. You hear the rhyme at every recitation. In the Latin 'justítiam tuam' is the direct object of 'exsultábit' (my tongue will extol with joy your justice); 'na vossa justiça' makes justice the ground of rejoicing rather than what the tongue proclaims.
