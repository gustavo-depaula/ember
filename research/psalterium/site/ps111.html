1

minor

“quererá muito os seus mandamentos” in mandátis ejus volet nimis

The Latin keeps the preposition 'in' (volet in mandatis = he will will / take pleasure in his commandments, LXX en tais entolais autou thelēsei); making 'mandamentos' the direct object of 'quererá' turns the delight-in into a plain wanting of the commandments. Defensible, but the prepositional phrase can be kept.

proposednos seus mandamentos quererá muito

❧ taken — The preposition is back, with the stylist's *se comprazerá*; his own *nos seus mandamentos quererá muito* is kept as an option.

A close, faithful rendering: tenses (futures in 1, 2, 5, 7, 9, 10; perfects in 4 and 9), the triple 'misericors, et miserator, et justus', the concrete 'chifre' and 'olhe de cima' for despiciat are all kept, and supplied copulas in 3 and 7b are within the rule. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse; the only point worth touching is the loss of the preposition 'in' with 'volet' in verse 1.

claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-23 · critic/v1.stylist.json · read prayed.vos.json

1

fails: native

“quererá muito os seus mandamentos”

'quererá muito' sounds colloquial to a Brazilian ear, like 'will want a lot'. The future 'quererá' is also heavy at the start of the colon, and the Latin 'in mandatis' is flattened into a direct object.

proposednos seus mandamentos muito se comprazerá

❧ taken

5

fails: native, sayable, plain

“Agradável é o homem”

'Agradável' is the vocabulary of small talk ('a pleasant person'), not of the psalm; it deflates the opening of the verse.

proposedAmável é o homem que tem piedade e empresta

❧ taken

“porque para sempre não será abalado”

The order 'para sempre não' is foreign and makes the reciter hesitate. Moving it after the verb would be ambiguous ('not shaken forever').

proposedporque jamais será abalado

kept as an option under (6) quia in ætérnum non commovébitur — The formula row (14:5b, 29:7) waits on the main session's ruling; *jamais* drops *in ætérnum*'s word (D23). Third stylist to ask — reported.

7a

fails: native, cadence

“não temerá notícia má”

Putting the adjective after the noun in 'notícia má' is unnatural in Portuguese, and the half-verse ends on an abrupt monosyllable after a heavy proparoxytone.

proposednão temerá a má notícia

❧ taken

7b

fails: native

“olhe de cima os seus inimigos”

'olhar de cima' is a Brazilian idiom for arrogant condescension, a social sneer. It does not suggest the just man looking down on defeated foes.

proposedaté que olhe do alto os seus inimigos

kept as an option under donec despíciat inimícos suos — The *despícere* row keeps both height and contempt, as the Latin does; 53:9, 91:12, 117:7 move with it.

9

fails: native

“o seu chifre será exaltado”

In spoken Brazilian Portuguese, 'chifre' carries a strong cuckold connotation, and here it sits in the final colon beside 'exaltado'. The image must stay, but the phrasing should not add emphasis to the word. The definite 'em glória' also reads slightly clipped.

proposede o seu chifre será exaltado na glória

not taken — *e* is not in the Latin; *na glória* narrows *in glória*; the horn is the row's (he agrees the image stays).

10

fails: no rhyme, native

“definhará: * ... perecerá.”

The mediant and final both end in stressed future '-rá' ('definhará' / 'perecerá'), which gives an accidental rhyme. 'rangerá' and 'verá' in the same verse make the '-rá' chime monotonous.

proposedperecerá o desejo dos pecadores.

❧ taken

“rangerá os seus dentes”

'os seus dentes' is a redundant possessive for a body part in Portuguese, and it sounds translated.

proposedrangerá os dentes, e definhará

kept as an option under déntibus suis fremet — The Latin has *suis*; 36:12 (the same Greek) has *os seus dentes*.

The psalm reads with dignity and mostly good cadence. The 'Nasceu nas trevas a luz' line and the doubled 'o seu coração' are strong. Its weak spots are a handful of colloquial or translated-sounding turns ('quererá muito', 'Agradável', 'olhe de cima', 'notícia má') and a pile of stressed '-rá' futures that chime at the end.

worst line111:5

best line111:4

Verse by verse

1

*Beátus vir → Bem-aventurado o homem* (D19; Ps 1:1's words). *timére → temer* with the Lord as object; the Brazilian LH's *respeita* (fetched) is refused — *temer* is the row, and the psalm's first line is also the Introit and Gradual of the Commons (Latin there *cupit nimis*).

2

*semen → descendência* (D32; no plant image in the verse). The whole verse is a versicle of the Commons (*V. Potens in terra erit semen ejus: generátio rectórum benedicétur*, grep in content/do); each colon reads alone. The Latin's order (*Poderosa … será*) kept: it is also natural Portuguese (MS1932 has it). *rectórum → dos retos* (the *rectus* row).

3

*manet in sǽculum sǽculi → permanece pelos séculos dos séculos* (D27; *permanére* row). **The second colon is word for word 110:3b and 111:9b**: the Portuguese is identical in both verses here and in Ps 110:3b, checked against ps110/prayed.vos.json once it was finished (identical). 111:4b's pair matches 110:4 *misericordioso e compassivo*, each in its own Latin's order.

4

Also a versicle of the Transfiguration (horas/Sancti/08-06), with *rectis corde*; this colon ends at *retos*, as the psalm does.

5

*miserétur et cómmodat → tem piedade e empresta* — copied from 36:26 (the *mutuári / commodáre* row names this verse). The DO verse joins 111:5 and 111:6; the inline (6) is not reproduced. The ambiguity reader heard *no juízo* as the court or the Last Judgment, meaning unclear — the Latin's own range (decision `judicio`); *tem piedade* was heard as compassion first, as in 36:26.

7a

The versicle of confessors and the Commons (C4, C9: *V. In memória ætérna erit justus. R. Ab auditióne mala non timébit.*); each colon reads alone. *memória ætérna → memória eterna*: the adjective, not D23's adverbial phrase. *estará* supplied for *erit* (the 'be in' of a place).

7b

*despíciat inimícos suos → olhe de cima os seus inimigos*: the *despícere (enemies)* row, accusative as 117:7 (*olharei de cima os meus inimigos*). *non commovébitur → não será abalado* (the *movéri* row). The Latin follows here the Gallican *non commovébitur* where the Greek has οὐ μὴ φοβηθῇ; the Latin is followed (rule 1).

9

*justítia ejus manet in sǽculum sǽculi*: = 111:3b, identical. The first colon is 2 Cor 9:9 and a versicle of St Lawrence (08-10). *paupéribus → aos pobres* (D38 row). The ambiguity reader could not hear what was scattered, nor for certain who scattered it: neither can the Latin (*Dispérsit* has no object and no subject named). The horn was heard with its cuckold overtone, as in Pss 74 and 91; kept by the *cornu* row.

10

*desidérium → desejo* (37:10, 77:29); *períre → perecer*. *definhará* was unknown to the ambiguity reader again (the *tabéscere* row; kept).
