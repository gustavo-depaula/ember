8

minor

“e não há nada como as vossas obras” et non est secúndum ópera tua

The Latin has no subject. 'nada' makes it neuter ('nothing'), but the Latin could just as well mean 'no one', continuing 'non est símilis tui'. The Latin leaves this open; the translation settles it.

proposede não há igual às vossas obras

❧ taken

16

minor

“dai o vosso poder” da impérium tuum

'impérium' means command or dominion, not strength in general. 'poder' makes it more abstract.

proposeddai o vosso império

❧ taken

“ao vosso servo” púero tuo

'puer' (boy, child, servant) is rendered by the same word as 'servum' in 85:2. The Latin uses a different word here, and it pairs with 'fílium ancíllæ'. The translation loses that distinction.

proposedao vosso criado

kept as an option under púero tuo — The púer row (68:18) gives servo; "servo … serva" in one colon is the pair the Latin builds with "fílium ancíllæ tuæ"; "menino" was heard wrongly at 68:18. Kept as option 3; "criado" (the v1 ask) is option 2.

This is a faithful, close translation. It keeps the Septuagintal readings ('quóniam sanctus sum', 'inférno inferióri', 'synagóga poténtium'), and every verse has the same pointing marks in the same order as the Latin. The only faults are minor: 85:8 decides a subject the Latin leaves open, and 85:16 renders 'impérium' and 'púer' more loosely than it could.

1

fails: native, plain

“porque eu sou carente e pobre”

'carente' in current Brazilian usage leans toward emotional neediness (a 'carente' person lacks affection); it sounds colloquial and psychological beside 'pobre', not the plain material want of 'inops'.

proposedporque eu sou necessitado e pobre

not taken — D38 (inops → carente); "necessitado" is egénus's. Evidence added to the row.

13

fails: plain

“do inferno mais baixo”

'mais baixo' is flat and spatial-technical at the final cadence; the ear expects the traditional depth image. It keeps the 'inferno inferiori' echo weakly and lands on a limp close.

proposede arrancastes a minha alma do inferno mais profundo.

❧ taken

14

fails: native

“diante da sua vista”

'diante da sua vista' is a calque of 'in conspectu suo'; no Brazilian says it, and the possessive after 'diante da' sounds bureaucratic. The idiom for this Latin phrase is 'diante dos olhos', same image of sight.

proposede não vos puseram diante dos seus olhos.

kept as an option under et non proposuérunt te in conspéctu suo — Twin of 53:5; olhos is óculus's word.

17

fails: native

“Fazei comigo um sinal para o bem”

'para o bem' dangles; the ear hears 'for the good (in general)' and stumbles before the comma. 'In bonum' is a sign favourable to me.

proposedFazei comigo um sinal de bem, para que vejam os que me odeiam, e sejam envergonhados:

kept as an option under Fac mecum signum in bonum — "de bem" changes "in bonum" (for good) into a sign of good.

The psalm reads cleanly and prayably: the vós forms are handled naturally, the mediants and finals mostly land on good paroxytone or oxytone stresses, and the Latin repetitions (alma, misericórdia, vosso) are kept without strain. The few weak spots are calques at the edges of verses (diante da sua vista, inferno mais baixo) and one word, carente, whose modern connotation pulls the register down.

16

minor

“dai o vosso domínio ao vosso servo” da impérium tuum púero tuo

The psalm distinguishes 'servus' (vv. 2, 3: 'servo') from 'puer' here; rendering both as 'servo' flattens the Latin's distinct word (puer = boy/young servant, LXX paidi). Defensible, since puer commonly means servant, but a closer rendering keeps the difference.

proposeddai o vosso domínio ao vosso menino

kept as an option under púero tuo — The púer row (68:18) gives servo; "servo … serva" in one colon is the pair the Latin builds with "fílium ancíllæ tuæ"; "menino" was heard wrongly at 68:18. Kept as option 3; "criado" (the v1 ask) is option 2.

A faithful, close rendering that keeps the Latin's sense, tense, mood and person throughout, including the Septuagintal 'quoniam sanctus sum', 'synagoga potentium' and 'inferno inferiori'. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only point worth weighing is the collapse of 'puer' into 'servo' in v. 16.

Verse by verse

1

inclína aurem → inclinai … o vosso ouvido (formula row; no 'ad me' here, so no 'para mim'); exáudi me → escutai-me (D3; the enclitic makes it safe anyway). inops et pauper → carente e pobre (D38), 'porque eu sou' as 24:16 'porque eu sou só e pobre' (the same 'sum ego'). The missa texts of Quad3-5 and Pent16-0 (the latter with 'mihi') sing this verse alone. v2: the stylist (asking "necessitado") and the ambiguity reader both heard "carente" as emotional neediness first. Held under D38 ("necessitado" is egénus's, which 85:1 does not have but the psalter pairs with inops in 34:10); the evidence is added to the D38 row for Gustavo.

2

sanctus (ὅσιος) → santo (row). Matos Soares 1932 'porque sou santo' (his note glosses it as God's intimate); Douay-Rheims 'for I am holy'. The Hebrew's 'faithful/devout' is not imported (rule 1). v2: the ambiguity reader heard "porque sou santo" as a boast — the Latin's word, kept (rule 1); Douay-Rheims says the same.

3

DO joins Vulgate 3 and 4 in one prayed verse with one mediant. Latin perfect 'clamávi' kept (the Greek has a future, κεκράξομαι). 'a vós clamei … a vós, Senhor, levantei' keeps the Latin's twice-fronted 'ad te'. The second half is the formula of 24:1 'A vós, Senhor, levantei a minha alma'. tota die → o dia todo (D24). lætificáre → alegrar (row). Long verse, as the Latin.

5

'Quóniam tu' → 'Porque vós' (the pronoun kept). mitis → manso (mansuétus / mitis row; the Greek here is ἐπιεικής, not πραΰς). 85:12 + 85:5 are the Offertory of Nat2-0.

6

D4 (áuribus pércipe → dar ouvidos a; its row names 85:6), D3 (inténde → atendei), D35 (deprecátio → prece). 'a voz da minha prece' as 27:6 and 65:19.

7

quia exaudísti me → porque me escutastes, as 16:6 'Eu clamei, porque me escutastes, Deus' (the same Latin, the same odd causality: the psalmist cries because he has been heard). Matos Soares 1932 inserts '(sempre)'; not taken.

8

'in diis' → 'entre os deuses' (as 81:6 'Sois deuses', 49:1 'o Deus dos deuses'). 'Não há semelhante a vós' as 70:19 'quem é semelhante a vós?'

9

'quascúmque fecísti' → 'que fizestes' (fácere kept; MS1932 'criaste' is creáre's). coram te → diante de vós. The first colon is also a responsory (Pent02-5: 'Omnes gentes quascúmque fecísti, vénient'). v2: comma after "fizestes", as the Latin after "fecísti": the ambiguity reader could run "fizestes virão" into "fizestes vir".

10

'fáciens mirabília' → 'e fazeis maravilhas', the participle made a finite verb beside 'sois' (76:14 'que fazeis maravilhas').

11

ingrédi (walking) → andar (row; πορεύσομαι). lætári → alegrar-se, jussive 'alegre-se'.

12

Copies 9:2 'Eu vos darei graças, Senhor, de todo o meu coração' with 'meu Deus' added (the Dómine, Deus meus formula → Senhor, meu Deus). confitéri → dar graças (D5); in ætérnum → para sempre (D23). The Latin's closing colon kept.

13

éruere with a source → arrancar (row; 21:21, 38:9).

14

Near-twin of 53:5: insúrgere super → se levantaram contra (row; hostile 'super me' → 'contra mim', as Ps 34). iníqui → os iníquos (26:12 'testemunhas iníquas'). synagóga → congregação (row, 7:7b, 81:1). quæsiérunt ánimam meam → buscaram a minha alma (53:5; ánima row: 'alma' kept, 'vida' the option there). The Latin's plural verb after the collective 'synagóga' is made singular ('buscou') — grammar (D2). 'puseram' is plural, the iníqui and the synagoga together. poténtes → os poderosos (κραταιῶν), kept apart from 53:5's 'fortes'.

15

pátiens → paciente (row); verax → verdadeiro (ἀληθινός; DRB 'true', MS1932 'verdadeiro'); proposed as a row. 'misericordioso … misericórdia' repeats the root as the Latin's 'miséricors … misericórdiæ' does.

16

Réspice in me, et miserére mei → Olhai para mim, e tende piedade de mim (formula, 24:16). ancílla → serva (the word of Lk 1:38 in Brazilian use; MS1932 'escrava'). The ambiguity reader heard Mary and Christ in "o filho da vossa serva" — the Latin carries the same echo (Lk 1:38 "ancílla Dómini"); kept.

17

odérunt (present sense) → odeiam (odísse row). confúndi → ser envergonhado (D15). adjuváre → auxiliar (row); consolári → consolar. The whole verse is the Introit verse / Gradual of Quad3-5 (with 'Dómine' added after 'mecum'); each colon stands alone.
