The verbless second colon is faithful to the Latin ellipsis but reads as a fragment; the long 'retribuição' followed by 'na minha' makes the colon lurch. Moving the prepositional phrase forward lets the colon fall on the key noun and still leaves the Latin open (no verb supplied).

minor

“para todo o sempre” usque in sǽculum

'todo o' is an intensifying amplification not in the Latin; 'usque in saeculum' is plainly 'for ever'.

proposeddesde agora e para sempre.

not taken — D37 settled *usque in …* → *para todo o sempre* (the *usque* is the 'todo'); the formula is word for word 112:2b, 113:26, 120:8, 124:2b, and D23 keeps bare *para sempre* for *in ætérnum / in sǽculum* without *usque*.

A faithful rendering that keeps the Latin's oath-ellipsis in 2a-2b (the unexpressed apodosis 'so be the retribution in my soul') without resolving it, and correctly reads 'ablactatus' as the substantive 'weaned child' (as the LXX's τὸ ἀπογεγαλακτισμένον supports), keeping the Septuagintal 'super matre sua' and 'retributio' against the Hebrew. Tenses (imperfect 'sentiebam', perfect 'exaltavi') and the jussive 'Speret' are preserved. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse; only a minor amplification in 130:3.

Verse by verse

1a

No title line (*Canticum graduum*): D7, as Pss 119–125 and 133. *Dómine* → *Senhor* at the head. 130:1a's first colon is the Wednesday Vespers antiphon (*Dómine, * non est exaltátum cor meum*) and reads alone: *Senhor, o meu coração não se exaltou*. The ambiguity reader heard the colloquial *exaltar-se* ('get worked up') as available but not first (decision `exaltatum` weighed it); kept.

1b

*Neque … neque* → *Nem … nem*, both kept. *super me* → *acima de mim* (above me, ὑπὲρ ἐμέ), apart from *super me* 'against / upon me' elsewhere.

2a

The Latin's colon at the end of the verse is kept: the sentence runs into 130:2b. The conditional is a true one with its apodosis in 2b, not the Hebraic oath *si* of 94:10 (the *si* of oath row): there the Latin has no apodosis and the plain negative is the Portuguese; here the Latin (and the Greek) build the requital, so *Se* stays.

2b

The Latin follows the Greek against the Hebrew (retribution, not a child at rest); the Portuguese follows the Latin (rule 1). The CNBB's *criança … no regaço* is the Hebrew picture. v2: the ambiguity reader listed *desmamada* as a word he did not know; kept — it is the everyday verb (*desmamar*) and Almeida's word; *a criança que deixou o peito* would explain an image the Latin gives in one word.

3

*speret* → *Espere* (D36 *esperar em*), as 129:6 in the parallel draft. *ex hoc nunc et usque in sǽculum* → *desde agora e para todo o sempre*: the ex hoc nunc row and D37, = 112:2b, 113:26, 120:8, 124:2b. *Israel* without article, as the psalter's usage.
