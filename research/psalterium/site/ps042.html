The audit trail

1source

Latin = DO Psalm42.txt, 6 prayed verses (42:1–42:6; no titulus, D7). One mediant per verse, no flex. Uses by grep (ps032/uses.py): the whole psalm is said at the foot of the altar in the traditional Mass, verse by verse alternately between priest and server, with the antiphon *Introíbo ad altáre Dei. — Ad Deum, qui lætíficat juventútem meam* split at the mediant of 42:4 (missa Ordo.txt, Ordo67, OrdoA, OrdoS, Holy Saturday Quad6-6); Passion Sunday Introit and Gradual (Quad5-0, with *éripe me* and an added *Deus meus*), Quad5-2 gradual verse, Sexagesima Communion (Quadp2-0: 42:4 whole), Transfiguration versicle (horas Sancti/08-06: 42:3a), Corpus Christi antiphon (horas Pent01-4, adapted). So every colon must read alone. Pss 41 and 42 are one psalm in the Hebrew (Heb 42–43): 42:5b = 41:6a = 41:11b; 42:6 = 41:6b = 41:12; 42:2b is 41:10b with *tristis* for *contristátus*. Ps 41 had no prayed.json when this draft was made, so the refrain is proposed here as a glossary formula row.

1

fails: no rhyme, sayable

“de uma nação não santa”

'nação não' collides two nasal diphthongs back to back, and 'de uma' adds a limp syllable to an already long first colon; the phrase lurches just before the mediant.

proposedJulgai-me, Deus, e separai a minha causa de um povo não santo, * arrancai-me do homem iníquo e enganador.

kept as an option — “de um povo não santo”, under de gente non sancta

2

fails: native

“Porque vós sois, Deus, a minha força: * por que me repelistes? e por que ando triste”

The ear hears 'porque… por que… por que' three times: the causal 'porque' sounds identical to the interrogative, so the verse seems to open with a question. The Latin distinguishes quia from quare.

proposedPois vós sois, Deus, a minha força: * por que me repelistes? e por que ando triste, enquanto o inimigo me aflige?

kept as an option — “enquanto o inimigo me aflige”, under dum afflígit me inimícus

4

fails: native, plain

“E entrarei até o altar de Deus: * até o Deus que”

'entrar até o altar' is not idiomatic Portuguese; 'entrar até' reads as a calque of 'ad'. Repeating 'até' at both colons makes it heavier. The familiar Portuguese form of this verse, the one people know from the prayers at the foot of the altar, is 'Entrarei ao altar de Deus'.

proposedE entrarei ao altar de Deus: * ao Deus que alegra a minha juventude.

kept as an option — “entrarei ao … ao”, under Et introíbo ad altáre Dei: * ad Deum, qui lætíficat

6

fails: sayable

“a salvação do meu rosto, e o meu Deus”

After the mediant the second colon floats free. Nothing ties it to 'lhe', so it sounds like a fragment. The article in 'e o meu Deus' also adds a hiatus at the close.

proposedEspera em Deus, porque ainda lhe darei graças: * a ele, salvação do meu rosto e meu Deus.

kept as an option — “a ele, salvação do meu rosto e meu Deus”, under salutáre vultus mei, et Deus meus

The psalm sounds plain and prayerful, with good cadences at almost every mark. The vós register holds without strain, and the turn to 'tu' for the soul in verses 5–6 comes across naturally. The weak spots are small idiom slips (the 'entrar até' calque, the triple 'porque/por que'), not problems of register.

best line42:3

4

minor

“E entrarei até o altar de Deus: * até o Deus” Et introíbo ad altáre Dei: * ad Deum

'ad' with 'introibo' marks the goal of entry (to/unto), not extent; 'até' reads as 'as far as' and slightly shifts the sense. Plain 'a/ao' keeps the Latin's direction.

proposedE entrarei ao altar de Deus: * ao Deus que alegra a minha juventude.

kept as an option — “entrarei ao … ao”, under Et introíbo ad altáre Dei: * ad Deum, qui lætíficat

A faithful rendering: the perfects 'deduxérunt, adduxérunt' are rightly kept as past ('guiaram', 'levaram'), 'tabernácula' stays plural, 'salutáre vultus mei' is kept concrete, and the pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only point is the preposition in 42:4, where 'até' is looser than the Latin 'ad'.

Verse by verse

1

judicáre → *julgar*, bare (row; 5:11a *julgai-os, Deus*; 7:9b *Julgai-me, Senhor*); the vocative *Deus* without *ó*, as 5:11a and 21:21. *causa* keeps its own word (the judicáre row notes 42:1). éruere with a source → *arrancar* (row; 31:7, 36:40, 38:9); the verb moved first (natural order, D2), so that the first word of the second colon is the imperative. dolósus → *enganador* (row; 5:7b *o homem de sangue e enganador*), iníquus → *iníquo* (kept apart from *ímpio*). The Introit of Passion Sunday (Quad5-0) quotes the verse with *éripe me* and adds *quia tu es Deus meus et fortitúdo mea* — another wording; the psalm keeps its own.

2

Quia tu es → *Porque vós sois* (30:4 *Porque vós sois a minha força*). fortitúdo → *força* (row; 17:2, 30:4, 117:14). quare → *por que* (2:1, 21:2). incédere → *andar* (37:7 *contristátus ingrediébar* → *eu andava entristecido*); tristis → *triste*, kept apart from 41:10's *contristátus* (→ *entristecido*, as 34:14, 37:7), which otherwise has the same second colon. afflígere → *afligir* (row).

3

emíttere → *enviar* (MS1932 *Envia*; DRB 'send forth'); the Transfiguration versicle (Sancti/08-06) quotes the first colon alone, and Passion Sunday's Gradual and Quad5-2's the whole — it reads alone. véritas → *verdade* (row). mons sanctus → *monte santo*, adjective after (row; 14:1 *no vosso monte santo*). The blind reader heard *tendas* as market stalls or camping tents, and the past *guiaram* as odd after *Enviai*; both are the row's and the Latin's (see `tabernacula`, `deduxerunt`), kept.

4

lætificáre → *alegrar* (row, open; 18:9, 29:2). juvéntus → *juventude* (24:7 *Das faltas da minha juventude*). Corpus Christi's antiphon (horas Pent01-4) adapts the verse (*sumam Christum, qui rénovat juventútem meam*) — not the psalm's wording.

5

confitéri → *dar graças* (D5); *Confitébor tibi* → *Eu vos darei graças* (9:2, 34:18, 117:21). cíthara → *cítara* (row; 32:2). *Deus, Deus meus* → *Deus, meu Deus* (21:2 formula). conturbáre → *perturbar* (row). The second colon is the refrain of Ps 41 (41:6, 41:11b) — see decision `anima` and the glossary formula row.

6

= 41:6 (second line) and 41:12, word for word; Ps 41 should copy it. *Spera* (to the soul, *tu*) → *Espera em* (speráre in → *esperar em*, D36; 36:3 *Espera no Senhor*). adhuc → *ainda*. *confitébor illi* → *lhe darei graças* (27:7b *lhe darei graças*). The blind reader: *Espera* can be heard as 'he waits' — the same in 41:6b and 41:12; the soul was named one line before (42:5), so it stands.
