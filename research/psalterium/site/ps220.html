1source

Latin = DO Psalm220.txt (canticle; Sunday Lauds II, Psalmi major [Day0 Laudes2]). The heading line *(Canticum Trium Puerorum * Dan 3:52-57)* is not a verse (D7; readVerses skips it, as in 210, 211, 213, 233); for a DO Portuguese file it would read *(Cântico dos Três Jovens * Dn 3,52-57)* — proposed, not in `verses`. **DO splits the Clementine's 3:52 into 3:52 and 3:53, so its ids run one ahead of the Clementine, the Greek, DRB and MS1932**: DO 3:54–3:57 = Vulg 53–56 and DO 3:58 = Vulg 57 (the heading's 52–57 is the Clementine numbering). DO's ids are kept. render.py and checks.py read the file by DO number and ran unchanged.

2draft

parallels.py 220 gives only the Latin and DO's Portuguese for a canticle, so `ps220/show_parallels.py` (modelled on ps213's) builds `consult/parallels/ps220.md` from: the Clementine (Bolls VULG, fetched by ps210), Rahlfs Theodotion and Old Greek (consult/lxx-*.csv), DRB from drbo.org (`consult/ps220/drbo-daniel3.htm`), and Matos Soares 1932 from the PDF text layer, printed pp. 766–767 (`consult/ps220/ms1932-dan3-p766-767.txt`; ps210 said no MS1932 Daniel was available — it is in the same 1932 PDF, PDF pp. 1784–1785). The Latin is the Vulgate of Daniel from Theodotion's Greek, not the Gallican. Hardest: the prefix *super-* (7 places), *intuéri* (no psalter precedent), 3:53's verbless line and its genitive.

3:52

fails: cadence

“e sumamente exaltado pelos séculos”

The final word 'séculos' is proparoxytone, so the verse ends on a dactyl and the tone's final cadence falls on a weak syllable. The same ending comes back in every verse, so this one flaw is repeated seven times. The final colon is also long, and 'sumamente exaltado' is a mouthful at the end of the breath.

proposede digno de louvor, e glorioso, e sumamente exaltado para sempre.

not taken — D43 settles *in sǽcula* → *pelos séculos*, and *para sempre* is *in ætérnum*'s (D23): the Latin's final is itself the proparoxytone *sǽcula*. The same remark was refused in canticle 210.

3:53

fails: native, cadence

“E bendito o santo nome da vossa glória”

Without a verb the line reads like a caption, not a sentence of praise. In a choir it sounds like a fragment. A supplied copula is allowed.

proposedE bendito é o santo nome da vossa glória:

kept as an option under Et benedíctum nomen … (no verb) — The Latin has no verb, and the Latinist named the elision as kept on purpose. 71:19 (*E bendito o nome da sua majestade*) is the same build, verbless. *é* would decide statement against wish.

“por todos os séculos”

It ends on the proparoxytone 'séculos', which is the same cadence problem as in 3:52.

proposede digno de louvor, e sumamente exaltado por todas as eras.

not taken — *sǽculum* is *século* throughout the psalter (D27, D43); *eras* is a new word for it. The form is canticle 212's (13:1).

3:54

fails: sayable, cadence

“e sumamente digno de louvor, e sumamente glorioso pelos séculos”

Two heavy four-syllable adverbs plus 'digno de louvor' make the colon long and lumpy, and it ends on a proparoxytone. The super- doubling must stay, so the fix is only in the ending.

proposede sumamente digno de louvor, e sumamente glorioso para sempre.

not taken — The stylist itself keeps both *sumamente*; its only change is *para sempre*, refused above.

3:55

fails: cadence

“e sumamente exaltado pelos séculos”

It ends on the proparoxytone 'séculos'.

proposede sumamente digno de louvor, e sumamente exaltado para sempre.

kept as an option — “prefix dropped”, under superexaltátus / superexaltátum / superexaltáte · superlaudábilis · supergloriósus

3:56

fails: sayable, cadence

“Bendito sois, vós que olhais os abismos, e estais sentado sobre os querubins”

The first colon is the longest in the canticle. 'sois, vós' bumps two stressed monosyllables together, and 'e estais' makes an awkward hiatus. It cannot be said comfortably in one breath.

proposedBendito sois vós, que olhais os abismos e vos sentais sobre os querubins:

❧ taken

“pelos séculos”

It ends on a proparoxytone.

proposede digno de louvor, e sumamente exaltado para sempre.

kept as an option under et sedes super Chérubim — 79:2b has the same Latin as *estais sentado* (rule 6); the hiatus elides.

3:57

fails: cadence

“e glorioso pelos séculos”

It ends on the proparoxytone 'séculos'.

proposede digno de louvor, e glorioso para sempre.

not taken as proposed

3:58

fails: sayable, cadence

“louvai-o e exaltai-o sumamente pelos séculos”

'louvai-o e exaltai-o' is a chain of vowels in hiatus (-ai-o e ex-ai-o) that blurs when sung. The two enclitic -o's also jingle, and the line ends on a proparoxytone.

proposedlouvai-o e sumamente exaltai-o para sempre.

not taken — 3:58 is canticle 210's 3:57 word for word, and the refrain stands four times there; it moves only with 210. The order *exaltai-o sumamente* is 210's, passed by two Latinist gates there.

The canticle sounds dignified and plain, and 'Bendito sois' carries the litany well. The recurring weakness is the ending 'pelos séculos': every verse closes on a proparoxytone, which fights the psalm-tone cadence. The long chains of 'sumamente' make several final colons heavy, and the one fragmentary verse (53) and the crowded 56 break the flow.

Verse by verse

3:52

The second colon is canticle 210's 3:56b word for word (*et laudábilis, et gloriósus, et superexaltátus in sǽcula*): copied (rule 6). *Deus patrum nostrórum* → *Deus dos nossos pais* (21:5, 43:2, 77:3 *patres nostri* → *os nossos pais*). *laudábilis* → *digno de louvor* (47:2 formula; 112:3 *laudábile* → *digno de louvor*). *in sǽcula* → *pelos séculos* (D43). The three *et* are kept, as in 210.

3:53

Built as 71:19 *Et benedíctum nomen majestátis ejus* → *E bendito o nome da sua majestade*. *laudábile* (neuter, with *nomen*) → *digno de louvor*. See decisions `e53`, `nome53`, `omn53`.

3:54

*in templo sancto* → *no templo santo* (10:5a, 17:7b *templo santo*). *glóriæ tuæ* → *da vossa glória*. The soft length flag on the second colon (+4/5) is the prefix said twice, as the Latin says it (decision `super`); accepted.

3:55

*regni tui* → *do vosso reino* (144:11). Second colon long for the same reason as 3:54.

3:56

*et sedes super Chérubim* → *e estais sentado sobre os querubins*: 79:2b *Qui sedes super Chérubim* → *Vós que estais sentado sobre os querubins* is the same Latin (second person), copied (rule 6); 98:1's *aquele que se senta* is third person. *abýssos* → *os abismos* (D42 row). *Benedíctus es, qui* → *Bendito sois vós, que* (v2, the stylist's order: *sois, vós* bumped two stresses together): the pronoun is supplied so the relative clause is heard as said to God (grammar, D2). See decision `sed56`.

3:57

The Latin differs from canticle 210's 3:56 (no *Dómine*, no *superexaltátus*), so only what is identical is copied: *Bendito sois … no firmamento do céu* and *e digno de louvor, e glorioso … pelos séculos*. *firmaméntum cæli* → *firmamento do céu* (D31: the sky).

3:58

Canticle 210's 3:57 word for word (the Latin differs only by a comma after *laudáte*, which is print). 210's decisions stand: the order *Bendizei, X, ao Senhor* (decision `order` there) and *sumamente* (here decision `super`). This is the first verse of the Benedicite, which DO appends to close the canticle (Clementine 3:57).
