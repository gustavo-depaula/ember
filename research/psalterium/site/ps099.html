minor

“sede servos do Senhor com alegria” servíte Dómino in lætítia

The Latin is a plain imperative verb, 'serve'. 'Sede servos' turns the action into a status (be servants). It is defensible, but the direct verb is closer.

proposedservi ao Senhor com alegria

not taken — D18 is settled: the bare 'servi' is also 'I served'; kept as option 2 for Gustavo.

3b

minor

“Somos o seu povo e as ovelhas da sua pastagem:” Pópulus ejus, et oves páscuæ ejus:

The Latin has no verb, and the nominative phrase is ambiguous. It can stand in apposition to 'nos' in the previous verse ('we, his people'), or it can be a vocative addressed to those told 'introite' ('his people, enter...'). Adding 'Somos' settles this in favour of the Hebrew reading. Supplying a copula is allowed, but here it also removes an ambiguity the Latin keeps.

minor

“e de geração em geração a sua verdade” et usque in generatiónem et generatiónem véritas ejus

'Usque in' means 'even unto', stressing that the truth lasts as far as the generations reach. The idiom 'de geração em geração' flattens this. The meaning is kept, but the wording could be closer.

proposede até geração e geração a sua verdade

not taken — not a Portuguese phrase; the formula row 'de geração em geração'; option.

The translation is accurate, with no additions or omissions of substance. The pointing marks (* ; ‡ * ; † *) match the Latin exactly. The only points are minor: a periphrasis for 'servite', a copula in 3b that settles the apposition-or-vocative ambiguity, and the flattening of 'usque in'.

'pastagem' sounds agricultural or technical. 'pasto' is the plainer word and the traditional one for this image.

The same word 'graças' lands at the mediant and at the final, so the verse chimes at both cadences. The root repetition (confessióne/confitémini) has to stay, but it doesn't have to sit on the mark. The three possessives in the middle colon ('suas portas', 'seus átrios') are also heavy.

2a

minor

“sede servos do Senhor” servíte Dómino

The Latin is a plain imperative verb, 'serve'. The Portuguese turns it into a copula plus predicate noun ('be servants'), which shifts the action onto a status. The meaning is close but not the same construction.

proposedservi ao Senhor com alegria

not taken — D18 is settled: the bare 'servi' is also 'I served'; kept as option 2 for Gustavo.

3b

minor

“com ação de graças ... dai-lhe graças” in confessióne ... confitémini illi

In the psalter's Latin, confessio/confiteri means acknowledging and praising God, not only thanking him. 'Ação de graças' narrows it toward the Hebrew todah. This is defensible, and it is rendered the same way in both places. 'Louvor/louvai-o' would be closer to the Latin, if the project has no fixed decision on it.

proposedentrai com louvor pelas suas portas, * com hinos nos seus átrios: louvai-o.

kept as an option under in confessióne … confitémini illi — D5 gives confitéri to God 'dar graças'; the confitéri row names 99:3b for 'ação de graças', as 94:2; 'louvor / louvai' is laus / laudáre's, and 99:4b has 'Laudáte' in the next verse.

The translation is faithful and complete. Nothing is added or dropped, 'et non ipsi nos' is kept as the Latin has it, and 'suávis' is kept as 'suave'. The pointing marks (*, ‡ *, † *) match the Latin in kind and order. Only two minor points: 'servíte' is recast as 'sede servos', and confessio is narrowed to thanksgiving.
