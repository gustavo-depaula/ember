9

minor

“E o inclinou deste para aquele” Et inclinávit ex hoc in hoc

The Latin gives no expressed object; 'o' supplies one (the cup). This is a defensible reading, since inclinavit is transitive and calix is the obvious referent, but it names what the Latin leaves implicit.

proposedE inclinou deste para aquele

not taken — without it *inclinou* is heard as intransitive ('leaned'), a sense the Latin lacks; the pronoun is grammar.

“a sua borra não se esgotou” fæx ejus non est exinaníta

The Latin is a passive perfect ('has not been emptied out'), but the Portuguese uses a middle reflexive ('has not run out'). The voice shifts slightly, and the sense of emptying something is softened.

proposeda sua borra não foi esvaziada

❧ taken — voice taken (*não foi esgotada*); his verb *esvaziada* kept as option 3.

The translation is close and literal throughout. It keeps the Septuagintal oddities (cum accepero tempus, justitias judicabo, vini meri plenus misto, the elliptical verse 7), and the singular 'do justo' in verse 11 is correct. Only verse 9 has small points: a supplied object and a passive rendered as a reflexive. Every verse has the same single mediant (*) as the Latin, in the same place.

3

fails: sayable, native, plain

“quando eu tomar o tempo, eu julgarei as justiças”

The doubled 'eu ... eu' clunks inside one short colon; the Latin names the subject only once (ego, at the verb of judging). Also, 'tomar o tempo' in current Brazilian usage leans toward 'take up / use up time', so the ear hesitates before it settles on 'seize the appointed time'.

proposedquando tomar o tempo, eu julgarei as justiças

❧ taken — first *eu* dropped; *tomar o tempo* kept as the Latin's verb.

4

fails: cadence

“e todos os que nela habitam:”

'habitam' is proparoxytone (HA-bi-tam), so the mediant falls on a dactyl and the tone's cadence has nowhere to land.

proposede todos os que nela moram:

not taken — *habitam* is ha-BI-tam, a paroxytone (checks.py agrees); *habitar* is the Latin's verb.

6

fails: native

“não faleis iniquidade contra Deus”

'falar' with a bare noun object ('falar iniquidade') sounds calqued; 'dizer' takes the object naturally, and it keeps the Latin's noun.

proposednão digais iniquidade contra Deus

kept as an option under nolíte loqui advérsus Deum iniquitátem — the *loqui* row keeps *falar* with a content object; *dizer* is dícere's.

7

fails: sayable

“Porque nem do oriente ... * porque Deus é juiz.”

'porque ... porque' at the head of both cola sounds like a stutter; the Latin itself varies the word (quia / quóniam), so the vernacular may vary it too.

proposedPorque nem do oriente, nem do ocidente, nem dos montes desertos: * pois Deus é juiz.

❧ taken

The psalm reads plainly and in good cadence overall. It keeps the Latin's hard images (the horn, the cup of pure wine full of mixture, the dregs) without softening them, and the closing verses ring with real nobility. The weak spots are small ones: a doubled pronoun, one proparoxytone at a mediant, and a pair of calqued turns. One note, not a fix: in Brazilian ears 'chifre' carries a comic cuckold connotation, so the choir should be ready for it, but the image has to stay.

best line74:10

9

minor

“E o inclinou deste para aquele” Et inclinávit ex hoc in hoc

The Latin has no expressed object; the pronoun 'o' ties the verb explicitly to the cup of v. 8, where the Latin leaves the object implicit. It is the natural reading, but the pronoun still resolves it.

proposedE inclinou-o deste para aquele — or, closer still, E inclinou deste para aquele

not taken — without it *inclinou* is heard as intransitive ('leaned'), a sense the Latin lacks; the pronoun is grammar.

The translation is close and faithful throughout: tenses (future confitebimur/narrabimus, future perfect accepero, perfect liquefacta est/confirmavi), numbers (justi singular) and the Septuagintal images (cornu, calix vini meri plenus misto, faex non exinanita) are kept. The only point worth noting is the supplied object pronoun in 74:9. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (a single * each).

Verse by verse

2

*invocáre → invocar* (row); *nomen tuum → o vosso nome*. The repeated *confitébimur* is repeated.

3

*narráre → narrar*, *mirabília → maravilhas* (9:2, 25:7). The switch from 'we' to 'I' is the Latin's and is not smoothed.

4

*omnes qui hábitant in ea → todos os que nela habitam*. *ego → eu*, emphatic as the Latin.

5

*Dixi → Eu disse* (38:2). *delinquéntibus → aos que cometem faltas* (row *delínquere*, 24:8). The colon ends on ':' as the Latin, which runs on into 74:6. *cornu* has no possessive here (74:6 adds *vestrum*), so *o chifre*.

6

*loqui iniquitátem* → *falar iniquidade*: the content object kept (the *loqui mendácium* row; 57:2 *falais justiça*); *contra Deus* moved after the object, natural order.

7

*judex → juiz*; *Deus judex est → Deus é juiz*, no article, as 49:6.

9

The Latin's inner ':' without a mark is kept. *bibent* has no object; none is supplied (MS1932 adds *delas*). *peccatóres terræ → os pecadores da terra*.

10

*cantáre → cantar*: the Latin has *cantábo* where the Greek has ψαλῶ; the Latin's verb is followed, so not *entoarei salmos* (D25). *Deo Jacob → ao Deus de Jacó* (19:2, 23:6, 45:8).

11

*confríngere → quebrar* (row). Object first in the Latin; natural order in Portuguese. *justi* singular kept: *do justo*.
