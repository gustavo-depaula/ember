1source

Latin = DO Psalm45.txt, 11 prayed verses (45:2–45:12; no titulus, D7). One mediant per verse, no flex. Not a day-hours psalm: no DM1962 column. Uses by grep (ps032/uses.py): 45:5 whole as a versicle (C6-1, missa 02-05, Brasilia/08-30), 45:5b and 45:6b as the Marian antiphon of the Immaculate Conception and Our Lady Aparecida, 45:6a in the Commons of Virgins and of the BVM, 45:8a as the Matins versicle, 45:2 whole for the Protomartyrs (Urbis/06-27). So these colons must read alone.

2draft

Psalm-level draft from consult/parallels/ps045.md (Latin, LXX, WLC, DRB, MS1932). DO's Portuguese not used (D12). Formulas checked with ps038/xref.py. Rules applied: D19 (suscéptor → amparo), D41 (auferre → tirar), rows virtus → poder (God's), fortitúdo → força, adjútor → auxílio, (con)turbáre → perturbar, (com)movére → abalar, vocem dare, tabernáculum → tenda, mane / dilúculo, gentes → nações, *Dóminus virtútum* → *o Senhor dos poderes* (formula row, unruled). Local rulings: contérere / confríngere kept apart here (two Greek verbs), *não se abalará* (keeps the Latin's open subject), *Parai* for vacáte. Refrain 45:8 = 45:12 built from one slot. Tests for the readers: *refúgio e poder*, *sem medida nos encontraram*, *as suas águas*, *com a sua força*, *a sua tenda*, *tirando as guerras até o fim da terra*, *Parai*.

4

minor

“as suas águas ... com a sua força” aquæ eórum ... in fortitúdine ejus

The Latin distinguishes a plural possessor (eorum, 'their' waters) from a singular one (ejus, 'his' strength, i.e. God's). Portuguese 'suas ... sua' flattens both into one ambiguous possessive, so the reader can no longer see that the waters belong to 'them' while the strength belongs to 'him'.

proposedRessoaram, e se perturbaram as águas deles: * os montes se perturbaram com a sua força.

❧ taken

A close, faithful rendering of the Latin: no content added or dropped, tenses (future timebimus/turbabitur/transferentur, perfects in v. 4 and 7, future conteret/confringet/comburet) and voices kept, the Septuagintal 'Vacate' rightly given as 'Parai', and 'mane diluculo' kept doubled. The only point worth weighing is the eorum/ejus contrast in v. 4, which 'suas ... sua' blurs. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex or second mediant).

2

fails: native

“nas tribulações que sem medida nos encontraram”

The adverbial 'sem medida' wedged between the relative and the verb reads like syntax built to follow the Latin. The ear has to wait for the verb, and the clitic cluster 'nos encontraram' comes too late to land cleanly.

proposedauxílio nas tribulações que nos encontraram sem medida.

❧ taken

3

fails: sayable, plain

“e os montes forem transportados para o coração do mar”

The second colon is long and heavy. 'Transportados' is a freight word: four syllables with a bureaucratic flavour, and chanting it makes the recto tono drag before the cadence.

proposede os montes forem levados ao coração do mar.

kept as an option under et transferéntur montes in cor maris — *levar* is addúcere's (D42) and loses the carrying across.

fails: sayable

“Deus a auxiliará”

'a au-' is a vowel clash, and the proclitic 'a' disappears into 'auxiliará'. In choir it comes out as 'Deus auxiliará', so the object is lost.

proposedDeus há de auxiliá-la de manhã, ao amanhecer. This keeps the adjutor/adjuvabit root link with 'auxílio' in v. 2.

❧ taken

9

fails: native, sayable

“tirando as guerras até o fim da terra”

A bare gerund opening the second colon dangles, since nothing in the verse clearly governs it. 'Tirar as guerras' is also flat and colloquial, like taking something off a shelf.

proposedele afasta as guerras até o fim da terra. Supplying a subject is negotiable, and 'afastar' keeps the concrete sense of removal.

❧ taken — The finite verb with *ele* taken; his *afasta* kept as an option — *afastar* is amovére's, D41 gives *tirar*.

fails: native

“Parai, e vede”

'Parai' sounds like a shout to halt a crowd or a cart. Vacate is a call to stillness and leisure, not an order to stop moving, so in the Office the word jars.

kept as an option under Vacáte, et vidéte — vacáte / σχολάσατε is ceasing, beside the wars made to cease; stillness is the Hebrew's reading.

A sober, well-paced rendering: the refrain (8/12) is strong and the cadences at mediant and final land correctly almost everywhere. The weak spots are a few heavy Latinate verbs (transportados, auxiliará) and the dangling gerund in v. 9, which is the only place it sounds like translation rather than prayer.

9

minor

“as obras do Senhor, os prodígios que pôs sobre a terra” ópera Dómini, quæ pósuit prodígia super terram

In the Latin, quæ is the object of pósuit and prodígia is its predicate: the works that he set as wonders on the earth. The translation makes prodígia a second object in apposition, so the works and the wonders become a list of two things instead of one.

proposedas obras do Senhor, que ele pôs como prodígios sobre a terra

kept as an option under ópera Dómini, quæ pósuit prodígia super terram — The Latin allows the relative with its noun drawn in (MS1932's reading), and his build is heavier; kept as option 3.

“ele tira as guerras” áuferens bella

The Latin has a present participle that depends on Dómini. The translation turns it into a new main clause with a finite verb. The meaning survives, but the Latin's syntax is lost.

proposedtirando as guerras até o fim da terra

not taken — The gerund was heard by the blind reader as 'except for the wars'; making a participle finite is grammar (D2), and the verb (D41) stays. *tirando* is option 2.

7

minor

“fez ouvir a sua voz” dedit vocem suam

'Fez ouvir' is a paraphrase of the concrete idiom 'gave his voice'. It is defensible and idiomatic, but 'deu a sua voz' keeps the Latin image.

proposeddeu a sua voz, a terra se abalou

not taken — The glossary row *vocem dare → fazer ouvir a voz* (17:14, passed there); *deu a sua voz* is not said in Portuguese.

2

minor

“que nos encontraram sem medida” quæ invenérunt nos nimis

Nimis is an intensifier meaning exceedingly or greatly. 'Sem medida' is acceptable but adds a slight shading of 'beyond measure'. 'Grandemente' is closer.

proposedque nos encontraram grandemente

not taken — *sem medida* is 37:9's rendering of the same *nimis* (L&S 'beyond measure'); *grandemente* is valde's option (D38 review).

This is a faithful, close rendering. It keeps the ambiguity of 'non commovébitur', and the Septuagintal readings ('Flúminis ímpetus', 'mane dilúculo', 'Vacáte') are left as they stand. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only points worth raising are syntactic, mainly the construction of 'quæ pósuit prodígia' and the finite rendering of the participle 'áuferens' in 45:9.

Verse by verse

2

*Deus noster* → *O nosso Deus* (article before the possessive). refúgium → *refúgio* (row; 31:7, 90:2). *nas tribulações* for *in tribulatiónibus* (plural kept). The whole verse is sung for the Protomartyrs of Rome (Sancti/Urbis/06-27).

3

*Proptérea* → *Por isso* (17:50, 41:7). timére → *temer* (row). dum → *enquanto* (29:10, 42:2). turbáre → *perturbar* (row). See `transferentur`.

4

turbáre and conturbáre both → *perturbar* (rows; ταράσσω both times) — the Latin's near-repetition kept as one verb twice. Natural order in the second colon (the subject first), a copy of 17:8 *os fundamentos dos montes se perturbaram*.

5

*Flúminis ímpetus* → *O ímpeto do rio* (ὁρμήματα, 'rushings'; the cognate is current; DRB 'stream' and MS1932 *um rio caudaloso* take the force out of the noun). lætificáre → *alegrar* (row; 42:4). *cívitas Dei* → *a cidade de Deus*. Altíssimus → *o Altíssimo* (row); the verb-subject order of the Latin turned to natural order so the colon ends on a paroxytone. Both colons are sung alone as versicles (C6-1, 02-05 missa, Brasilia/08-30) and read alone.

6

*Deus in médio ejus* → *Deus está no meio dela* (copula supplied, D2); *ejus* is the city. adjuváre → *auxiliar* (row); see `adjuvabit`. The second colon is the Marian antiphon *Adjúvit eam Deus mane dilúculo* (with a perfect there — the psalm keeps its future).

7

conturbáre → *perturbar* (row). *dedit vocem suam* → *fez ouvir a sua voz* (row; 17:14); the subject left unnamed, as the Latin (MS1932 supplies *Deus*). *mota est terra* → *a terra se abalou* (movére → *abalar*, as 17:8 *Commóta est … terra* → *A terra se abalou*); the Latin's asyndeton kept.

8

suscéptor → *amparo* (D19); *noster* → *o nosso*. *Deus Jacob* → *o Deus de Jacó* (19:2, 23:6). The Latin's order (the title first, the name last) kept, with *é* supplied: the colon ends on the name, as the Latin does. = 45:12 word for word (a refrain; the brief).

9

*Veníte, et vidéte* → *Vinde, e vede* (33:12 *Vinde*; 33:9 *vede*). *ópera Dómini* → *as obras do Senhor* (27:5, 117:17). *super terram* → *sobre a terra*. The first colon is long in the Latin too.

10

*scuta* plural → *os escudos*; *combúret igni* → *queimará … no fogo* (DRB 'in the fire'); the Latin puts the object first, Portuguese order puts the verb first (D2). *arma* → *as armas*.

11

God speaks: *eu sou Deus* (no article; MS1932 *o Deus* follows the Greek's ὁ θεός; the Latin has none). exaltáre → *exaltar* (row); *exaltábor … et exaltábor* → *serei exaltado … e serei exaltado*, the repetition kept. *in géntibus* → *entre as nações* (gentes → nações, row); *in terra* → *na terra*.

12

= 45:8, the refrain (one slot, `virtutum`).
