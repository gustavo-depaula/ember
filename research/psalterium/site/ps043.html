minor

“à voz do que me afronta e me insulta” A voce exprobrántis, et obloquéntis

Two participles joined by et; the translation fuses them into a single person, while the parallel second half ('do inimigo e do que me persegue') keeps two. It also supplies 'me' twice, which the Latin leaves unexpressed. Treat both halves alike.

proposedà voz do que afronta e do que insulta: * diante do inimigo e do que persegue.

❧ taken — the article repeated (two persons, parallel to 43:17b); the supplied *me* held as grammar — *afrontar* and *perseguir* want an object

minor

“Não pedirá Deus contas disto?” Nonne Deus requíret ista?

proposedNão investigará Deus estas coisas?

not taken — the requírere row (9:34 *Não pedirá contas*), where God requires sin; DRB 'search out' is the option there

23

minor

“não nos repilais para sempre” ne repéllas in finem

The Latin has no object; 'nos' is supplied. Defensible from context (v.10 repulisti nos) but it adds a word the Latin leaves open.

proposede não repilais para sempre.

not taken — *repelir* wants an object in Portuguese (grammar, D2); DRB 'cast us not off'; the object is the *nos* of 43:10 and 43:24

A faithful, close rendering: the Septuagintal features (ventilabimus cornu, declinasti semitas nostras with its positive 'et', multitudo in commutationibus, venter conglutinatus) are all kept, tenses and persons are right, and the pointing matches the Latin (one mediant per verse, no flex or second mediant in either). The faults are minor: one preposition (post) bent toward the Hebrew, a few slight narrowings (illuminatio, virtutes, requiret) and an inconsistent handling of the paired participles in v.17.

'Carência' is clinical and modern for inopia. In the unlinked second colon, the non-reflexive 'esqueceis' also sounds clipped. The reflexive form with 'de' is the native construction, and 'pobreza' is the plainer word.

fails: plain

“auxiliai-nos”

'Auxiliai-nos' is Latinate and heavy on the tongue next to 'Levantai-vos'. 'Ajudai-nos' is the prayer word everyone already says.

The psalm mostly sounds like prayer: the lament in the second half (vv. 20–26) is plain, grave and rhythmically sure, and the Latin figures ('com os nossos ouvidos ouvimos', 'a obra que operastes') are kept without strain. Verse by verse

2

The vocative *Deus* without *ó* (the Latin has none). áuribus … audívimus kept as the Latin has it (*com os nossos ouvidos ouvimos*). annuntiáre → *anunciar* (D41); its object is 43:2b, so the verse ends on a colon. Used alone as the Sexagesima Introit verse and at Candlemas (missa Quadp2-0, Sancti/02-02); it reads alone.

2b

Opus is the object of 43:2's *anunciaram*; DO starts it with a capital after a full stop, Portuguese needs it joined (lower case after 43:2's colon). *in diébus eórum* → *nos dias deles* (*seus* is ambiguous beside *vós*).

3

dispérdere → *exterminar* (row), afflígere → *afligir* (row), expéllere → *expulsar* (row, 43:3 named). *plantásti eos* keeps the image (MS1932 *estabeleceste … em seu lugar* explains); *eos* is the fathers, unnamed as in the Latin. v2: *e vós os plantastes* (stylist) marks the change of subject from *a vossa mão*; the ambiguity reader had heard the nations planted. The rhyme *plantastes … expulsastes* is the Latin's parallel (plantásti … expulísti); held.

4

*enim* → *pois* (row). *in gládio suo … brácchium eórum*: the Latin's own change of pronoun kept (*suo* reflexive → *a própria espada* in v2, the stylist: *sua* could be heard as God's; then *o braço deles*). possidére → *possuir* (MS1932's *conquistaram* is the Hebrew's sense). *et … non* kept as *e … não*.

4b

déxtera → *a direita* (row).

6

*In te … in nómine tuo* → *Em vós … no vosso nome*, the Latin's preposition kept both times (DRB *Through thee*). spérnere → *desprezar*, insúrgere in → *levantar-se contra* (rows; 43:6 named).

7

speráre in → *esperar em* (D36). gládius → *espada*, arcus → *arco* (7:13, 36:14).

8

The subject *vós* supplied after 43:7's first person. confúndere (active) → *envergonhar* (D15; 13:6 *vós envergonhastes*). *affligéntibus* present, as *os que me atribulam*.

9

laudári → *ser louvado* (row, 33:3: the Latin's passive; MS1932 *nos gloriaremos* is the Hebrew's). tota die → *o dia todo* (D24). confitéri → *dar graças* (D5); in sǽculum → *para sempre* (D23). The Alleluia verse of Pent23 (missa) has *in sǽcula*; the psalm keeps its own.

10

*Nunc autem* → *Mas agora*. repéllere (without *a*) → *repelir* (row, 42:2; 43:10 named). egrediéris is a future → *saireis*. The same colon is 59:12 and 107:12 (*exíbis*): they should copy it (*com as nossas forças*, v2).

11

*odérunt* has present sense (24:19 *me odeiam*); *diripiébant* imperfect → *saqueavam* (new row diripere).

12

dispérgere → *dispersar* (row); the natural order.

13

*sine prétio* → *sem preço*.

14

pónere + predicate → *fazer de* (row, 17:12). oppróbrium → *afronta* (row; 30:12 *tornei-me uma afronta … para os meus vizinhos*). in circúitu → *ao redor* (row); *nostro* → *ao nosso redor*.

15

géntibus → *para as nações* (dative, as 43:14 *vicínis*); *in pópulis* → *entre os povos*.

16

fácies → *face* (vultus is *rosto*). coopéríre → *cobrir*, the same verb as 43:20, kept one. The verse runs on into 43:17 (the cause of the shame), so it ends on a comma.

17

18

*Hæc ómnia* → *Tudo isto*. *nec* → *e não*, as the Latin (MS1932 *e, ainda assim*, explains). *iníque ágere* → *agir iniquamente* (118:86 *iniquamente*). testaméntum → *aliança* (row; 43:18 named); *in* kept as *na*.

20

humiliáre → *humilhar* (row). afflíctio → *aflição*. *umbra mortis* → *a sombra da morte*.

21

oblivísci → *esquecer*, direct object (row). aliénus → *estranho* (row). expándere → *estender*.

22

requírere (God, of sin) → *pedir contas* (row; 9:34 *Não pedirá contas*). *Nonne* is a question that expects yes; *Não pedirá Deus…?* does that. *ista* → *disto*. novit → *conhece* (row).

22b

æstimári → *ser tido* (v2, the stylist; draft 1 *considerados*, MS1932). occísio → *matança*; *ovelhas de matança* keeps the genitive (v2; draft 1 *para a matança*). Rom 8:36 = this verse (see `mortificamur`).

23

Exsúrge → *Levantai-vos* (row), twice as the Latin. obdormíre → *adormecer* (row, which names 43:23). *ne repéllas* has no object in the Latin; *repelir* needs one, so *nos* is supplied (DRB 'cast us not off'). in finem → *para sempre* (D27, perpetuity). The Sexagesima Introit (missa Quadp2-0) is 43:23–24 and 25b.

24

avértere fáciem → *desviar a face* (row, 12:1). *oblivísceris* is a present (the accent; the oblivísci row names 43:24) → *esqueceis*. The Latin has no *et* before it; the asyndeton is kept. inópia → *carência*, of the family of D38's *carente* (inops); tribulátio → *tribulação* (row).

25

*humiliáta est … conglutinátus est* as states (*está humilhada … está colado*). *in púlvere* → *no pó*. v2: *Quóniam* → *Pois* (draft 1 *Porque*), which the ambiguity reader heard as *por que* after the two questions of 43:23–24 — the same mend as Ps 42:2.

26

adjuváre → *auxiliar* (row), redímere → *resgatar* (row; *resgatai-nos* safe). *propter nomen tuum* → *por causa do vosso nome* (24:11, 30:4). The Litany and Candlemas antiphon (*Exsúrge, Dómine, ádjuva nos, et líbera nos propter nomen tuum*) has another verb in its second half; the psalm keeps its own.
