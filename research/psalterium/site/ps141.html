4

minor

“vós conhecestes as minhas veredas” et tu cognovísti sémitas meas

The Latin's paratactic 'et' (a Hebraism after the temporal clause) is dropped; keeping it preserves the Latin's odd but deliberate connective rather than smoothing it.

proposedQuando o meu espírito desfalecia em mim, * e vós conhecestes as minhas veredas.

kept as an option under et tu cognovísti — After quando, Portuguese e opens no main clause and would be heard as a second dangling clause; conjunction is grammar (D2); MS1932 drops it.

A close and faithful rendering: tenses, voices and persons track the Latin, the concrete images (laço, fuga, prisão, porção) are kept, and the pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only point is the dropped 'et' in 4a, a minor smoothing of the Latin's parataxis.

3

fails: no rhyme

“a minha oração, * ... a minha tribulação”

Mediant and final both end on '-ação' after the same 'a minha', so the verse chimes like a jingle when chanted to the tone.

proposedDerramo a minha oração na sua presença, * e declaro diante dele a minha tribulação.

❧ taken — Order only: na sua presença moved after the object.

4b

fails: native

“esconderam um laço para mim”

'para mim' tacked on at the end is flat and a little prosaic; the clitic is what a Brazilian ear expects in a prayed register.

proposedNeste caminho por onde eu andava, * esconderam-me um laço.

❧ taken

5

fails: plain

“Eu observava à direita”

'observava' is clinical, a word of reports; the plainer verb of the eyes serves the image better, and the pronoun is not needed.

proposedOlhava à direita, e via: * e não havia quem me conhecesse.

kept as an option under Considerábam ad déxteram, et vidébam — olhar is plain looking; κατενόουν is looking closely and the row gives observar for the absolute use (93:9); the pronoun is kept because observava is also third person.

5b

fails: native, sayable

“Perdeu-se para mim a fuga”

Inverted, with the subject left dangling at the end; it reads as a calque and the mouth stumbles on 'para mim a fuga'.

proposedNão há mais fuga para mim, * e não há quem procure a minha alma.

kept as an option under Périit fuga a me — The inversion was fixed by order (A fuga se perdeu para mim); his wording drops périit and doubles não há.

6

fails: sayable

“eu disse: Vós sois a minha esperança, a minha porção na terra dos vivos”

The second colon is very long for one breath; the supplied 'eu' and the doubled 'a minha' add weight with no gain.

proposedClamei a vós, Senhor, * e disse: Vós sois a minha esperança, minha porção na terra dos vivos.

not taken — Article before possessives is a style rule; e is a supplied conjunction; eu disse as 118:57. The length is the Latin colon's (19).

8

fails: sayable

“até que me retribuais”

The vowel run '-bu-ais' at the final cadence is awkward to chant and the form feels bookish.

proposedTirai da prisão a minha alma, para dar graças ao vosso nome: * os justos me aguardam, até que me recompenseis.

kept as an option under donec retríbuas mihi — retribuir is the row (115:3, 137:8); recompensar only at the imperative (118:17) and it decides for reward.

A plain, sober rendering that mostly sounds like Portuguese prayer; the repetitions of v.2 carry well and the vós address is steady. The weak spots are a calqued inversion in 5b, an accidental -ação rhyme in v.3, and a couple of bookish words (observava, retribuais) that stiffen the cadence.

worst line141:5b

best line141:2

4

minor

“Quando o meu espírito desfalecia em mim” In deficiéndo ex me spíritum meum

'ex me' is 'out of me / from me' (the spirit ebbing away from the speaker, LXX ἐξ ἐμοῦ); 'em mim' shifts it to 'within me'.

proposedQuando o meu espírito desfalecia de mim

kept as an option under In deficiéndo ex me spíritum meum — desfalecer de is heard as the cause of fainting; the motion is available as se esvaía de mim.

“vós conhecestes” et tu cognovísti

The connective 'et' is dropped. It is an apodotic Hebraism, so the omission is defensible, but it is a Latin word left out; 'então' would carry it without adding meaning.

proposedentão vós conhecestes as minhas veredas

kept as an option under et tu cognovísti — então sets the knowing after the fainting in time, a reading the Latin leaves open; the conjunction is grammar (D2).

7b

minor

“se fortaleceram mais do que eu” confortáti sunt super me

'super me' is open. It can be 'beyond me' (stronger than I, the Douay reading, following the Hebrew) or 'over/against me'. The comparative settles the ambiguity in the Hebrew's favour. 'sobre mim' keeps the Latin's openness.

proposedporque se fortaleceram sobre mim

kept as an option under quia confortáti sunt super me — Identical to 17:18b, which reads mais do que eu; the doublet is kept (rule 6).

A faithful, close rendering. Sense, tense, person and number hold throughout, and every verse has one mediant (*) matching the Latin. The only points are small: 'ex me' rendered as 'em mim' and the dropped apodotic 'et' in v. 4, and 'super me' in v. 7b settled as a comparative where the Latin stays open.

Verse by verse

2

No title: DO's Latin file starts at 141:2 and has no titulus (D7); the Vulgate title Intellectus David, cum esset in spelunca, oratio is not translated. The whole verse is the Introit psalm verse of 09-17.

3

oratiónem → oração (row), tribulátio → tribulação (row).

4b

via → caminho, ambuláre → andar (rows); eu supplied because andava is also 'he walked'.

5

cognóscere → conhecer (row); the imperfect subjunctive conhecesse as MS1932.

6

Clamei a vós as 118:146; eu disse as 118:57, whose Pórtio mea is also a porção; terra vivéntium → terra dos vivos (26:13, 51:7). The monastic Vespers antiphon Pórtio mea, Dómine, sit in terra vivéntium is built from this colon. The second colon is long (the Latin's is 19 syllables); accepted. The stylist asked e disse and minha porção without the article, for breath: refused — the article before possessives is a style rule, and e supplies a conjunction the Latin lacks (the same reason the apodotic et of 141:4 is not supplied); eu disse as 118:57.

7

inténde → atendei (D3), deprecátio → prece (D35); humiliátus sum nimis → fui humilhado sem medida, as 37:9 and 115:1. The ambiguity reader heard humilhado as 'shamed' first, 'brought low' second; the row is shared with 37:9 and 115:1, kept.

7b

The second colon is 17:18's word for word (porque se fortaleceram mais do que eu, super me as a comparison). liberáre → livrar (row).

8

ad confiténdum nómini tuo → para dar graças ao vosso nome (D5; 121:4's build). retribúere → retribuir; retribuais is the subjunctive, not the imperative the open row is about.

4

The ambiguity reader listed veredas and desfalecia as unknown: both are rows (sémita → vereda, D15, often reported unknown and kept; desfalecer, 76:4) and kept.
