6

minor

“e nos dareis a beber lágrimas por medida?” et potum dabis nobis in lácrimis in mensúra?

The Latin keeps 'in lacrimis' as a prepositional phrase ('give us drink in tears'), not tears as the direct object of the drink. Making tears the thing drunk is a small reshaping, though near the sense.

proposede nos dareis de beber em lágrimas, por medida?

❧ taken — *e nos dareis de beber em lágrimas* — *dar de beber* is *potum dare*, and *em* keeps the Latin's openness.

11

minor

“e os seus ramos, os cedros de Deus” et arbústa ejus cedros Dei

'arbusta' means trees or a planted grove, not branches. 'ramos' goes along with Douay's 'branches', but that narrows the image. It also blurs the difference from 'pálmites' and 'propágines' in the next verse.

proposede o seu arvoredo, os cedros de Deus

❧ taken

17

minor

“Queimada a fogo, e escavada, * à repreensão do vosso rosto perecerão.” Incénsa igni, et suffóssa * ab increpatióne vultus tui períbunt.

'incensa' and 'suffossa' can be read as feminine singular (the vine, as in the LXX) or as neuter plural ('things burned and dug up', as in Douay), and 'peribunt' is plural. The feminine singular settles an ambiguity that the Latin leaves open. Portuguese can hardly keep both readings, and the LXX supports this choice, so it is acceptable. The reason is to be noted, not a change forced.

not taken — he asks no change; the reason (the Greek's feminine singular) is in the decision.

The translation stays close to the Latin throughout: tenses, persons, the passive-reflexive 'convertere', the Septuagintal 'singularis ferus' and 'virum dexterae tuae' are all kept, and the present 'non discedimus' is right. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. Only minor points remain: the construction of 'in lacrimis', the sense of 'arbusta', and the gender and number chosen in v. 17.

17

fails: sayable, native

“Queimada a fogo, e escavada, * ... perecerão”

The singular participles run straight into a plural verb that has no stated subject, so the ear hunts for who is perishing and the verse feels broken at the mark.

proposedQueimada a fogo e escavada: * à repreensão do vosso rosto perecerão. (the pause at the mark then lets the plural begin a new thought; the translators should check the number of the participles)

not taken — it cuts the participles off from *perecerão* as a sentence of their own, closing what the Latin runs on; the number clash is the Latin's.

'Seja ... sobre' is calqued on Latin 'Fiat ... super'; in Portuguese a hand resting on someone 'está' there.

proposedEsteja a vossa mão sobre o homem da vossa direita

kept as an option under Fiat manus tua super — the *fíeri* row (*Seja* for jussive *Fiat*, 118:173 held twice against the same request); *Esteja* makes it a state.

fails: sayable, native

The vine section is the weak stretch: small calques ('à vista dela', 'Seja a vossa mão'), clitic snags ('e a vindimam', 'a pastou') and the number clash in v.17 make it sound translated rather than prayed.

5

6

*transférre → transportar* (D43). *víneam* without an article in Latin: *uma vinha* (DRB 'a vineyard'); put at the end of the colon, the natural place for the new thing.
