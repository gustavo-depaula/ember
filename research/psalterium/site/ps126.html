2a

fails: sayable, native

“levantai-vos depois de vos terdes sentado, vós que comeis o pão da dor”

The compound personal infinitive 'de vos terdes sentado' is heavy and bookish. It stacks a third 'vos' before 'vós que', and the colon runs past one breath. The ear stumbles on 'terdes sentado'.

proposedlevantai-vos depois de vos sentardes, vós que comeis o pão da dor.

❧ taken

“É vão para vós levantar antes da luz”

The reflexive is missing here but present in the next colon ('levantai-vos'). The two halves of the verb pair then sound mismatched.

proposedÉ vão para vós levantar-vos antes da luz:

❧ taken

2b

fails: sayable

“eis a herança do Senhor, os filhos: a recompensa, o fruto do ventre”

With a comma before 'os filhos' and a colon after it, the ear hears 'os filhos: a recompensa' as a pair. That breaks the two parallel appositions (herança = filhos; recompensa = fruto do ventre). The punctuation gives no guide for pausing when reciting.

proposedeis a herança do Senhor: os filhos; a recompensa: o fruto do ventre.

kept as an option under ecce heréditas Dómini fílii — The draft's punctuation is the Latin's; the ambiguity reader heard the pairs rightly; the Latin leaves the grouping open.

4

fails: native

“assim os filhos dos que foram sacudidos”

Without a verb the second colon sounds like a fragment. 'Sacudidos' already strikes the ear as strange, and with no copula it falls flat. A supplied copula makes the comparison land.

proposedassim são os filhos dos que foram sacudidos.

❧ taken — A copula is grammar (D2).

5

fails: native, plain

“que encheu o seu desejo com eles”

'Encher o desejo com' is not idiomatic Portuguese; one fills a container, not a desire. Ending the colon on 'com eles' puts a weak pronoun right before the mark, and 'eles' is left dangling.

proposedBem-aventurado o homem que com eles saciou o seu desejo:

kept as an option under qui implévit desidérium suum ex ipsis — *saciar* is satiáre's; 102:5 already has *enche … o teu desejo*; the filling is the Latin's image. The weak pronoun at the mediant is removed by the Latinist's fix (*deles* before the object).

“não será envergonhado”

The passive 'ser envergonhado' is stiff. 'Confundido' is the plainer, established liturgical word for 'confundétur', and it echoes 'non confundar' elsewhere in the Office.

proposednão será confundido quando falar aos seus inimigos à porta.

not taken — D15 (settled): *confundido* is heard as 'confused'.

The opening pair of verses is excellent: parallel, austere and easy to chant, with good cadences at every mark. The middle of the psalm gets heavy. The personal-infinitive construction in 2a and the unclear punctuation in 2b slow the mouth. The close uses a non-idiomatic collocation ('encheu o seu desejo com eles') that sounds translated rather than prayed.

worst line126:5

best line126:1b

Verse by verse

1a

No titulus: *Canticum graduum* (Salomonis) is not reproduced (D7; as Pss 119, 120, 133). *in vanum → em vão*; *domus → casa* (row).

1b

*custodíre → guardar* (row), twice as the Latin has it (*custodíerit … custódit*). *frustra → em vão* (38:7).

2a

*súrgere / súrgite → levantar / levantai-vos*: the Latin's one verb twice. *manducáre → comer* (row); *panis dolóris → o pão da dor* (MS1932; *dolor → dor*, row). *qui manducátis* → the vocative *vós que comeis*. The second colon is long, as the Latin's is (19 syllables).

2b

*Cum déderit* → *Quando ele der*: the subject hidden in the verb named (D2); lowercase. *diléctis suis → aos seus amados* (the *diléctus* row; 59:7 *os vossos amados*). *somnus → sono*. Natural order puts *o sono* before the dative; the colon still ends on a paroxytone.

4

*sagítta → seta* (row); *potens → o poderoso*, the row Ps 119:4 set up for this verse (*Setas agudas do poderoso*): God or a warrior left open. *in manu → na mão*. v2: the copula *são* supplied (the stylist: without it the colon sounds a fragment; D2 allows a copula). Colon now +5 on the Latin's 9. *setas* was listed unknown by the ambiguity reader: the *sagítta* row keeps it (14 verses). The ambiguity reader heard *o poderoso* as a warrior — the row leaves it open.

5

*Beátus vir → Bem-aventurado o homem* (D19). *confúndi → ser envergonhado* (D15); singular as the Latin (the Greek is plural). *loqui* + dative → *falar aos*; *in porta → à porta* (68:13: the city gate, where *na porta* is heard as 'in the doorway'). The second colon is a Vespers antiphon ([Daya3 Vespera], DO Psalmi major) *Non confundétur * cum loquétur inimícis suis in porta* and reads alone: *Não será envergonhado * quando falar aos seus inimigos à porta*. The ambiguity reader heard *à porta* as the door of the house: kept, the Latin's singular (68:13); *às portas* (72:28b, for *in portis*) would change the number. The stylist asked *confundido*: refused, D15 (settled).
