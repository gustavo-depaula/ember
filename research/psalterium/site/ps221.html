The audit trail

1draft

Canticle-level draft. parallels.py handles only psalm numbers, so ps221/show_parallels.py (adapted from ps213's) builds consult/parallels/ps221.md from: the DO Latin; the Bolls Vulgate, WLC Hebrew and DRB of Isaiah 12 (consult/ps221/bolls-*-23-12.json); the Rahlfs LXX from consult/lxx-*.csv; drbo.org's Isaias 12 page; and Matos Soares 1932 from the pdftotext text layer of the 1932 PDF, p. 1458 (consult/ps221/ms1932-isaiah12.txt). There is no Diurnal: the canticle is not in it. Precedent was checked with ps221/concord.py (ps213's, renamed) and glossary rows with ps221/gloss.py. Brazilian circulation was fetched and recorded in ps221/circulation.md: the CNBB LH prays it at Thursday Lauds of Week II, and the Lectionary uses it at the Easter Vigil, on the Sacred Heart (B) and on Advent III (C), in a paraphrase from the Hebrew. Copied from finished psalms: 117:14 (12:3), 104:1 (12:4b), 11:6b (*agirei com confiança*), the *Confitébor tibi, Dómine* formula. Hard readings: the causal *quóniam irátus es*, *convérsus est furor*, *Salvatóris* against the Hebrew's 'salvation', *adinventiónes* of God, *magnífice fecit*.

Hard checks pass: ids and marks match DO's Latin. render.py and checks.py ran unchanged with the DO number 221, and readVerses skips the heading line. 12:4

minor

“Com júbilo” in gáudio

gaudium is joy/gladness, an inner state; 'júbilo' leans toward jubilatio/exsultatio (outward shouting), a word the Latin reserves elsewhere. Defensible, but 'alegria' is the plain equivalent.

proposedCom alegria tirareis águas das fontes do Salvador: * e direis naquele dia: Dai graças ao Senhor, e invocai o seu nome:

kept as an option under in gáudio — The glossary row gáudium → júbilo is open for a ruling and has been followed in five places, including canticle 214, which refused the same remark. Alegria is lætítia's word.

A faithful rendering of the Latin canticle: tenses, persons, and moods are kept, concrete images (fontes, habitação de Sião, invenções) are preserved, and the only additions are copulas Portuguese requires. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex or second mediant). The single note is lexical: 'júbilo' for gaudium is slightly stronger than the Latin.

12:1

fails: native

“o vosso furor voltou atrás”

'voltar atrás' is colloquial Brazilian for 'back down / retract a decision'; in the mouth it sounds like God changed His mind in a quarrel rather than His wrath turning away. It also lands a little flat after the solemn first colon.

proposedo vosso furor se afastou, e me consolastes.

❧ taken

12:5

fails: native, plain

“as suas invenções”

In current Portuguese 'invenções' means gadgets or, worse, fabrications ('isso é invenção'); a congregation will hear either technology or lies. It needs a footnote, which the register forbids.

proposedDai a conhecer entre os povos os seus desígnios: * lembrai-vos de que o seu nome é excelso.

kept as an option under adinventiónes ejus — This is the row for all six places, and it was held against the same remark at 76:13. Desígnios is consílium's word (D33). The question goes to Gustavo with the row.

“Fazei conhecer”

Slightly calqued; 'Dai a conhecer' is the native liturgical idiom for making known.

proposedDai a conhecer entre os povos

kept as an option under Notas fácite — The notum fácere row, repeated across psalms (rule 6).

12:6

fails: plain, sayable

“porque agiu com magnificência”

'magnificência' is a five-syllable abstract noun that clogs the mediant; 'agiu com' is bureaucratic. The Latin 'magnifice fecit' is a simple verb + adverb about doing.

proposedCantai ao Senhor, porque fez coisas magníficas: * anunciai isto em toda a terra.

❧ taken

12:7

fails: plain

“habitação de Sião”

'habitação' sounds administrative (housing); 'morada' is the plainer faithful word for habitatio and is the natural prayer word.

proposedExulta e louva, morada de Sião: * porque é grande no meio de ti o Santo de Israel.

kept as an option under Exsúlta, et lauda, habitátio Sion — Morada is habitáculum's word (D44). Habitação is the row's word (75:3, 86:7, 131:13), and it was held against the same remark at 86:7.

The canticle reads cleanly and moves with good breath: the imperatives of 12:4-12:7 give it a steady liturgical pulse and the cadences all fall well. Its weaknesses are lexical rather than rhythmic: a few Latinate or colloquial choices ('invenções', 'magnificência', 'voltou atrás', 'habitação') make it sound translated at exactly the points where it should sound most native.

12:1

minor

“o vosso furor se afastou” convérsus est furor tuus

conversus est means 'was turned'; the Latin leaves open whether the anger turned away or turned back. 'se afastou' picks the Hebrew sense ('turned away'), which is the likely meaning but narrows the Latin.

proposedo vosso furor se desviou

kept as an option under convérsus est furor tuus — His fix narrows just as much (desviar = turn aside), and it borrows avértere's row verb. Afastar was taken because two readers misheard the draft-1 voltou atrás.

12:6

minor

“porque fez coisas magníficas” quóniam magnífice fecit

magnifice is an adverb qualifying fecit with no object; the Portuguese supplies an object ('coisas') and turns the adverb into an adjective.

proposedporque agiu magnificamente

kept as an option under quóniam magnífice fecit — Supplying an object that Portuguese needs is grammar (D2). His adverb brings back the heaviness the stylist refused, and a collision with agam in 12:2.

A faithful rendering of the Vulgate Isaiah 12 canticle; no content is dropped or added, and the pointing matches the Latin (one mediant per verse, no flex). Two minor points only: 'se afastou' narrows the neutral 'conversus est', and 'fez coisas magníficas' recasts the adverb 'magnifice' with a supplied object.

Verse by verse

12:1

DO's first line, *(Canticum Isaiæ * Isa 12:1-7)*, is a heading with the book reference. It is not a prayed verse and not a titulus. readVerses skips it, as it did for 213 and the Nunc dimittis, and D7 keeps tituli out anyway. The Vulgate's lead-in *Et dices in die illa* is not part of DO's text. *Confitébor tibi, Dómine* → *Eu vos darei graças, Senhor* (D5; the formula of 9:2, 110:1, 137:1a). The DO ids run 12:1–12:7 because DO splits Vulgate 12:2 in two, so from 12:3 on they run one ahead of the Vulgate. The Latin is Jerome's Isaiah from the Hebrew, not the Gallican psalter. In DO the canticle is sung at Monday Lauds II (Psalmi major.txt [Day1 Laudes2]; also [Daya1 Laudes] and the monastic feria 2), and its antiphon is 12:1b: *Convérsus est furor tuus, Dómine, et consolátus es me* → *O vosso furor se afastou, Senhor, e me consolastes*. So the 12:1b wording is the antiphon's too.

12:3

The same words as 117:14 (*Fortitúdo mea, et laus mea Dóminus: * et factus est mihi in salútem*), with *Quia* in front. Copied: *O Senhor é a minha força e o meu louvor: * e se fez salvação para mim*, where *Dóminus* is moved up so that the subject comes first (the 117:14 ruling; the glossary's formula row names this canticle id). *Quia → Porque*. Canticle 224 (Ex 15:2) has the same line.

12:4

*Et dicétis in die illa: Confitémini Dómino, et invocáte nomen ejus* copies 104:1 *Dai graças ao Senhor, e invocai o seu nome* (D5). *In die illa → naquele dia* (145:4). Like the Latin, the line has no quotation marks: where the people's speech ends is left open (the CNBB closes it at the end of the canticle).

12:5

*Notas fácite → Fazei conhecer* (the *notum fácere* row: 97:2, 144:12). *In pópulis → entre os povos* (56:10, 107:4). *Mementóte → lembrai-vos* (104:5), with *de que* for *quóniam*, the standard build. *Excélsum → excelso* (D43).

12:6

*Cantáte Dómino → Cantai ao Senhor* (95:1). *Annuntiáte → anunciai* (D41). *In univérsa terra → em toda a terra* (8:2a).

12:7

*In médio tui → no meio de ti* (115:9, a place addressed). *Sanctus Israël → o Santo de Israel* (70:22, 77:41). *Porque é grande no meio de ti o Santo de Israel* keeps the Latin's order and both readings of it: 'the Holy One is great in your midst' and 'great is he who is in your midst' (DRB). The CNBB has the same order.
