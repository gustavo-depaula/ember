1

fails: native, sayable

“irem-se os povos”

'irem-se' is a rare enclitic form of irar-se, and the ear first hears 'irem', from 'ir' (to go), then has to stop and parse it; it snags right at the opening of the psalm.

proposedO Senhor reinou, enfureçam-se os povos:

❧ taken

“o que está sentado sobre os querubins”

'o que' at the start of a colon reads first as 'that which', and 'está sentado' is a flat participle phrase; the Latin's substantive 'qui sedet' wants a named subject.

proposedaquele que se assenta sobre os querubins, abale-se a terra.

❧ taken — taken as *aquele que se senta* (the current form of the verb)

4

fails: native, plain

“as retidões”

The plural 'retidões' is not current Portuguese; the ear stops on a word that sounds invented, and it sits right on the mediant.

proposedVós preparastes as vias retas: * vós praticastes o juízo e a justiça em Jacó.

kept as an option under Tu parásti directiónes — the fault taken (singular *a retidão*); his wording refused because it adds *vias*

8

fails: native, sayable

“e vingando-vos de todas as invenções deles”

The loose gerund after 'fostes propício' has no finite verb to hang on, so the colon trails off; the clitic 'vingando-vos' is heavy, and 'invenções deles' closes on a weak pronoun. The Latin sets 'propitius' and 'ulciscens' side by side as descriptions of God, and a noun parallel to 'propício' keeps that.

proposedDeus, vós lhes fostes propício, e vingador de todas as invenções deles.

kept as an option under et ulcíscens in omnes adinventiónes eórum — the fault taken (finite verb *vos vingastes de*); *vingador de* refused — heard as 'avenger on behalf of'

The psalm moves with a plain, grave step, and the refrain of verses 5 and 9 ('Exaltai o Senhor, nosso Deus... porque é santo') lands well both in the mouth and against the tone. The stumbles come in a few spots: an opening verb the ear mishears, one coined plural, and a gerund in verse 8 that leaves the second colon hanging.

4

minor

“Vós preparastes a retidão” Tu parásti directiónes

directiones is plural (LXX euthutētas, concrete 'right ways/directions'); the singular abstract 'a retidão' changes number and slightly abstracts the image.

proposedVós preparastes as retidões

not taken — the plural was unknown to the ambiguity reader and heard as coined by the stylist, at the mediant; number is grammar (D2), and *a retidão* is the psalter's word for *diréctio* (44:7, 118:7). *as retidões* stays option 2.

7

minor

“e o decreto que lhes deu” et præcéptum quod dedit illis

praeceptum is a command/precept; 'decreto' is defensible but shifts toward a legal-edict nuance. 'preceito' is the direct equivalent.

proposede o preceito que lhes deu

not taken — D19 (settled) lists this verse: *preceitos* is *justificatiónes*' word in Ps 118, *decreto* is *præcéptum*'s across all eight places.

8

minor

“e vos vingastes de todas as invenções deles” et ulcíscens in omnes adinventiónes eórum

The Latin has a present participle coordinated with propitius fuisti (you were propitious and avenging), not a second finite past verb; the finite perfect narrows it to a single past act.

proposede vingando-vos de todas as invenções deles

not taken — the gerund was the stylist's worst line and the ambiguity reader heard its link as loose; Portuguese has no participle to coordinate with *fostes propício*, and the finite verb keeps the verb, the object and the coordination with *fostes* (same time frame); the gerund is option 2 (draft 1).

A faithful rendering that keeps the Latin's hard readings (the 'honor regis judicium diligit' clause, neuter 'sanctum est' ambiguity at v.5, 'adinventiones') without smoothing them toward the Hebrew. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. Only small closeness issues: the plural of directiones, praeceptum as 'decreto', and the participle ulciscens made finite.

Verse by verse

2

*excélsus → excelso* (D43). *Super omnes pópulos → sobre todos os povos*: the *super* (place) row. The copula is supplied once, in the first colon, and understood in the second, as the Latin.

3

The Latin has a colon inside the first half, with no pointing mark; the Portuguese keeps it. *Quóniam terríbile, et sanctum est*: the neuters agree with *nómini* — the name is terrible and holy; *terríbilis → terrível* (D43). *Honor regis judícium díligit* is kept word for word, subject then verb then object, so that it cannot be heard the other way round; MS1932's *a honra do rei está em amar a justiça* interprets; the Hebrew 'the strength of the king' is not the Latin. *Dilígere → amar* as 32:5 *Ele ama a misericórdia e o juízo*. v2: the first colon is +2 after naming *os povos* (decision confiteantur).

4

The Latin says *tu* twice, at the head of the verse and at its end; both are kept as *vós*, at the head of each colon.

6

No verb, as the Latin (MS1932 supplies *estavam*). *In sacerdótibus … inter eos* both *entre*: *in* with a plural of persons is 'among'. *Invocáre → invocar*; *ínvocant* present, kept. Names: *Moisés, Aarão* (glossary), *Samuel*.

6b

*Exaudíre → escutar* (D3), imperfect *escutava*, and again in 98:8 *escutáveis* — the Latin's repetition across the two verses. *Ipse* is plain 'he' here (αὐτός); *ele mesmo* would stress what the Latin does not.

7

*Testimónia → testemunhos* (D15); *præcéptum → decreto* (D19, which lists this verse). *Dedit* perfect → *deu*.

8

*Dómine, Deus noster* → *Senhor, nosso Deus*; the bare vocative *Deus* in the second colon, as the psalter keeps it.

9

*Mons sanctus → monte santo* (the row). *Adoráte in monte* → *adorai no seu monte santo* (the Latin's ablative, not the Greek's εἰς). 98:5 and 98:9 open with the same words, as the Latin; *santo é o Senhor* puts the adjective first as *sanctus Dóminus*.

1

Second colon flagged +6 by the counter, which does not elide; with *aquele‿que*, *sobre‿os*, *abale-se‿a* it is about +3. Accepted: the relative clause and the jussive are both needed, and *aquele* was asked for by the stylist and the ambiguity reader. *Moveátur → abale-se*: the movéri row and 95:9's jussive *Abale-se*.
