3

fails: cadence

“levanta-te, saltério e cítara: *”

The mediant falls on the proparoxytone 'cítara', so the flex lands on its third-last syllable and the tone drags. Swapping the two instruments puts a paroxytone at the mark, keeps both nouns, and word order is free.

proposedLevanta-te, minha glória, levanta-te, cítara e saltério: * eu me levantarei ao amanhecer.

kept as an option under psaltérium et cíthara — Refused as in Ps 56: the Latin's own mediant is the proparoxytone *cíthara*, and 107:3 = 56:9 word for word.

6

fails: no rhyme

“para que sejam livrados os vossos amados”

'livrados' and 'amados' chime inside the final colon, making a jingle in the closing words of the verse. 'livrados' is also a little flat in this spot.

proposedpara que sejam libertos os vossos amados.

kept as an option under ut liberéntur dilécti tui — Identical Latin to 59:7a (rule 6); *libertar* is the erípere row's verb; the chime is inside one colon, not mediant/final.

9

fails: sayable

“e Efraim é o amparo”

'e E-fra-im é o a-' strings five vowels together, and in choir the voices smear them. The copula is supplied (the Latin has none), so it can go.

proposede Efraim, amparo da minha cabeça.

kept as an option under et Éphraim suscéptio cápitis mei — Keeps 59:9b's build; without the copula the colon is heard as an apposition to *Manassés*.

10

fails: plain

“a panela da minha esperança”

'panela' belongs to the kitchen stove and makes people smile when it is chanted. The image is a cooking vessel, and a slightly older word keeps it concrete without the domestic comedy.

proposedMoab é a caldeira da minha esperança.

❧ taken — Taken as *o caldeirão* (L&S's gloss of *lebes*, the glossary row's proposal); *caldeira* itself refused (boiler / crater) and kept as an option.

7

fails: native

“Deus falou no seu santo”

The nominalized 'no seu santo' first sounds like 'in his saint', meaning a person. The ambiguity belongs to the Latin, and every plain fix (santuário, santidade) would resolve it, so I suggest keeping the wording and only letting 'santo' carry the pause.

proposedDeus falou no seu santo:

not taken — No change proposed; the reading is one of the Latin's (Ps 59 v2's ruling).

The psalm reads cleanly and moves with good energy: the doubled 'pronto' and 'levanta-te' come across strongly, and the questions in 11-12 have real momentum. The rough spots are small: a vowel pile-up in 9, a homely 'panela' in 10, an internal chime in 6, and a dactylic mediant in 3.
