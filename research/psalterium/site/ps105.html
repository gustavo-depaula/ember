minor

“Para vermos a bondade dos vossos escolhidos” Ad vidéndum in bonitáte electórum tuórum

The Latin preposition 'in' is dropped: the Latin reads 'to see in the goodness' (the Hebraism for enjoying the good things of the elect), parallel to 'ad lætándum in lætítia'. Rendering it as a direct object makes the goodness something merely looked at.

proposedPara vermos na bondade dos vossos escolhidos

❧ taken

13

minor

“Depressa o fizeram” Cito fecérunt

The pronoun 'o' adds an object the Latin does not have. 'Cito fecerunt' is absolute (they soon did it / they were quick).

proposedDepressa agiram

kept as an option under Cito fecérunt, oblíti sunt óperum ejus — agir is ágere's (105:6); fazer needs its object; the colon makes it point forward.

32

minor

“Moisés foi maltratado por causa deles” vexátus est Móyses propter eos

'Vexatus est' means he was afflicted or troubled. 'Maltratado' implies that someone mistreated him, which the Latin does not say.

proposedMoisés foi afligido por causa deles

kept as an option under et vexátus est Móyses propter eos — vexáre → maltratar row (93:5); afligir is afflígere's.

33

minor

“E pronunciou com os seus lábios” Et distínxit in lábiis suis

'Distinxit' means he uttered distinctly, set forth or decreed (LXX diésteilen). 'Pronunciou' is weaker and leaves out the note of distinct or deliberate utterance.

proposedE proferiu distintamente com os seus lábios

kept as an option under Et distínxit in lábiis suis — 65:13 row, the same Greek.

39

minor

“a terra foi manchada de sangue” infécta est terra in sanguínibus

The Latin plural 'sanguinibus' (bloodshed, blood-guilt) is flattened to a singular, and 'in' becomes 'de'.

proposeda terra foi manchada com sangues

not taken — D29 settled the singular.

The translation is faithful and literal throughout the psalm. It keeps the Septuagintal oddities (laudavérunt laudem, initiáti sunt Beélphegor, dedit eos in misericórdias) and does not follow the Hebrew against the Latin. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. Only minor points remain: two small dropped or added elements (the 'in' in v.5, the object 'o' in v.13) and three places where the wording could be closer (vv.32, 33, 39).

fails: native, sayable

“e dominaram-nos os que os odiavam”

A Brazilian ear hears 'dominaram-nos' as 'they dominated US'. The assimilated '-nos' (from -m + os) is textbook grammar but is dead in Brazilian speech. Followed by 'os que os', it also stumbles.

proposede os dominaram aqueles que os odiavam.

❧ taken

fails: native

“E atribularam-nos os inimigos deles”

This has the same trap as v.41. 'atribularam-nos' is heard as 'they afflicted us', which is the wrong people in a psalm that alternates between 'we' and 'they'.

proposedE os inimigos deles os atribularam, e foram humilhados sob as mãos deles: * muitas vezes os livrou.

❧ taken

43

fails: native

“provocaram-no”

'-no' sits one letter away from '-nos'. In choir it blurs into 'provocaram-nos' ('provoked us'), and the enclisis is European. The same issue is in vv.29 and 32.

proposedEles, porém, o provocaram com o seu desígnio: * e foram humilhados nas suas iniquidades.

❧ taken

The psalm is faithful and mostly sayable, and the frame (vv.1, 4-6, 47-48) has the right simple nobility. The long narrative middle, however, leans on European enclisis after 'E' (salvou-os, provocaram-no, dominaram-nos), which makes it sound translated and, in vv.41-42, makes a Brazilian ear hear 'us' where the Latin says 'them'. A handful of calques and filler nouns (fazer conhecer, ter por nada, coisas, lugar, petição) could give way to plainer native idiom with no loss to the Latin.

13

minor

“Depressa o fizeram: esqueceram-se das suas obras” Cito fecérunt, oblíti sunt óperum ejus

'o' supplies an object the Latin does not have; 'cito fecerunt' is absolute ('they did quickly', i.e. they soon...). The colon also sets the two verbs further apart than the Latin comma.

proposedDepressa fizeram, esqueceram-se das suas obras

kept as an option under Cito fecérunt, oblíti sunt óperum ejus — agir is ágere's (105:6); fazer needs its object; the colon makes it point forward.

33

minor

“E pronunciou com os seus lábios.” Et distínxit in lábiis suis

'distinxit' (LXX διέστειλεν) is 'distinguished / spoke distinctly', not simply 'pronounced'; 'pronunciou' flattens the verb and loses the sense of distinguishing (which the Latin keeps obscure). Pointing mark (*) matches.
