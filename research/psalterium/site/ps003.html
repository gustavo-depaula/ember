Verse by verse

2

The psalm begins at 3:2 (3:1 is the title; D7). 'quid' → 'por que', with the Latin's question mark (Douay-Rheims 'Why'); the lower case after '? *' follows the Latin, as in Ps 4:3. multiplicáti sunt → 'se multiplicaram' (as Ps 4:8). tribuláre → 'atribular', of the family of tribulátio → tribulação (glossary). 'multi … Multi' (3:2b, 3:3a) → 'muitos … Muitos', the anaphora kept. The Diurnal Monástico 1962 does not have this psalm.

3

ánimæ meæ → 'à minha alma' (glossary). salus → salvação (D6). 'in Deo ejus' → 'no seu Deus'. The capital after the colon follows the Latin (Non est…).

4

'Tu autem, Dómine' → 'Vós, porém, Senhor' (God is vós). Possessives with the article throughout: 'o meu amparo, a minha glória … a minha cabeça'.

5

'Voce mea' → 'Com a minha voz', kept first as in the Latin. clamáre → clamar (glossary). 'de monte sancto suo' → 'do seu monte santo', the same words as 2:6 (montem sanctum ejus → 'o seu monte santo').

6

'Ego' is said in the Latin and is kept: 'Eu dormi'. dormíre → dormir, as 4:9 'dormirei'. The three verbs are three: dormi / fui tomado pelo sono / me levantei (draft 3; decision 'soporatus'). The second colon's close is 117:13b word for word.

7

'salvum me fac' → 'salvai-me' (glossary, Ps 53:3). 'Deus meus' → 'meu Deus', the living vocative (Matos Soares 1932 'Deus meu'). Lower case after the colon, as the Latin.

8

'tu' is said in the Latin: 'vós feristes'. percútere → ferir (Matos Soares 1932 'tens ferido'; the perfect is a simple past here). peccatóres → pecadores (glossary, Ps 1).

9

benedíctio → bênção.
