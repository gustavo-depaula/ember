2

fails: native

“Deus, atendei em meu auxílio”

'atender em' is not a Portuguese construction; the ear expects 'vinde em meu auxílio' (the formula every Brazilian knows) or 'atendei a', and the hybrid sounds like a calque of 'in adjutorium intende'. Keeping 'atendei' (for inténde) needs a verb that governs 'em' or a different preposition.

proposedDeus, voltai-vos em meu auxílio: * Senhor, apressai-vos para auxiliar-me.

kept as an option under Deus, in adjutórium meum inténde — D40 is deferred to Gustavo; voltar-se is convértere's verb.

fails: no rhyme

“Voltem para trás, e corem, * os que me querem mal”

'Voltem' here and 'Voltem-se' in 4b render the same avertántur with two different forms, so the repetition the Latin makes is blurred. There is also a faint echo 'corem / querem' across the mediant.

fails: plain, native, sayable

“imediatamente”

A long, bureaucratic adverb (six syllables) that weighs down the colon; statim is a short word and wants a short word.

proposedVoltem-se logo, corando, * os que me dizem: Bem feito, bem feito!

“Que bom, que bom”

In Brazilian ears 'que bom' is warm and approving, so the mockery of Euge, euge is lost; it sounds like a friend being pleased. 'Bem feito!' is the native cry of gloating over another's misfortune and keeps the doubled exclamation.

proposedos que me dizem: Bem feito, bem feito!

kept as an option under Euge, euge — Rule 6 (= 39:16); proposed for the row.

6

fails: plain

“Deus, auxiliai-me”

'auxiliai-me' is heavy and clerical at the most urgent cry of the psalm; the short final colon wants a short, strong verb. 'socorrei-me' is the plainer faithful word for ádjuva.

proposedEu, porém, sou necessitado e pobre: * Deus, socorrei-me.

kept as an option under Deus, ádjuva me — Swaps adjuváre's family and breaks the psalm's ádjuva/adjútor echo.

The psalm is short and mostly moves well in the mouth, with a good urgent close ('Senhor, não demoreis'). The weak points are two calque-sounding joints (2 and the 'em vós todos' tangle in 5), and 4b, where 'imediatamente' and 'Que bom, que bom' drain the scene of its bite. Some Latinate words ('auxílio/auxiliar/auxiliai') recur heavily; the repetition of root in 2 is faithful, but in 6 a plainer verb would pray better.

Verse by verse

2

The Portuguese of the Hour versicle in DO (Common/Prayers.txt) is a separate text this psalm does not touch; whatever D40 rules should reach it too. The Rahlfs line for 69:2 in the parallels file reads εἰς τὸ σῶσαί με κύριον ὁ θεός εἰς τὴν βοήθειάν μου πρόσχες and looks damaged or reordered (no verb for *festína*); it was not used for anything.

3

= 34:4 (*Confundántur et revereántur, * quæréntes ánimam meam*; here *qui quærunt*), whose words are copied: *Sejam envergonhados e desonrados, * os que buscam a minha alma* (formula row). 39:15 has the same line with *simul* and *ut áuferant eam*. Latin *ánimam* kept as *alma* (MS1932 and DM1962 turn it into 'my life'). **Draft 2:** the comma before the subject removed (stylist: Portuguese does not part predicate and subject; the asterisk is the pause). 34:4 and 39:15b still have it; proposed in the glossary that they follow.

4

*Avertántur retrórsum* → *Voltem para trás* (retrórsum row: one Portuguese phrase under avértere and convértere); *qui volunt mihi mala* → *os que me querem mal*, word for word 39:15b. The comma before the subject removed in draft 2, as 69:3. The first colon is four syllables under the Latin (checks); accepted, nothing omitted.

5

Identical in Portuguese to 39:17. The Latin differs in two small things: *in te* for 39:17's *super te* (39:17 already chose *em vós*, so nothing changes) and *qui quærunt te* for *quæréntes te*. The subject *os que amam a vossa salvação* moved before the quotation, as 34:27 and 39:17, so it is not heard as part of what is said. The first colon is long, as the Latin's is.

6

*Ego vero … pauper sum* → *Eu, porém, sou … e pobre*, the build of 39:18 (*Ego autem mendícus sum*). egénus → *necessitado* (D38 row; 34:10b, 40:2), where 39:18 has mendícus → *mendigo*: the one difference of the two lines kept. *ádjuva me* → *auxiliai-me* (adjuváre row; 118:86), safe at the *vós* imperative (*auxiliei* is the past).

6b

*es tu* → *Vós sois* first, the natural order (39:18b).
