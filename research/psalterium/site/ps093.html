13

minor

“Para que lhe abrandeis os dias maus” Ut mítiges ei a diébus malis

In the Latin, 'mitigare ei a diebus malis' means to give him relief from the evil days: 'a diebus malis' is a separative ablative, not the object of the verb. The Portuguese makes 'os dias maus' the direct object, so the days themselves are softened rather than the man being relieved from them.

proposedPara que lhe deis alívio dos dias maus

❧ taken

“insensatos do povo” insipiéntes in pópulo

'in populo' is locative ('among the people'). 'do povo' is close to a partitive or possessive reading, so it is slightly looser than the Latin.

proposedEntendei, insensatos no povo

❧ taken

The translation is faithful and close throughout. It keeps the Septuagintal readings ('líbere egit', 'fingis laborem in praecepto' as addressed to God, 'captabunt in animam'), and the pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only points are two small ones: the construction in 93:13, and 'in populo' in 93:8.

17

minor

“Se o Senhor não me tivesse auxiliado” Nisi quia Dóminus adjúvit me

The Latin protasis is a perfect indicative stating a fact ('were it not that the Lord helped me'); the counterfactual is carried only by 'habitásset'. Recasting the protasis as a pluperfect subjunctive hypothesis is idiomatic but slightly shifts the mood.

proposedSe não fosse que o Senhor me auxiliou,

kept as an option under Nisi quia Dóminus adjúvit me — Mood is grammar (D2); DRB and MS1932 build it the same way; 118:92 ruled so (D24). His wording is option 2.

20

minor

“no decreto” in præcépto

'Præceptum' is a precept or commandment (the law), not a decree; 'decreto' drifts toward a juridical sense the Latin does not specify.

proposedvós que formais a fadiga no preceito?

kept as an option under qui fingis labórem in præcépto — D19 settles præcéptum → decreto and names 93:20; 'preceitos' belongs to justificatiónes (D15).

A faithful rendering that keeps the Latin's hard readings (líbere egit, the unresolved syntax of v. 15, fingis labórem in præcépto) without smoothing them toward the Hebrew. Tenses, persons and moods track the Latin closely, and the pointing marks match verse for verse. Only two minor points: the factual 'nisi quia' of v. 17 turned counterfactual, and 'præcépto' rendered 'decreto'.

Verse by verse

1

The versicle of 11-30 reads 93:1–2 as one (Sancti/11-30); nothing in the wording depends on it.

2

Exaltáre → 'Exaltai-vos' (glossary row, which names 93:2). 'redde retributiónem' → 'pagai a retribuição', as 27:4b ('pagai-lhes a retribuição deles'); safe at the vós imperative (past 'paguei').

3

The Latin ends 93:3 with a colon and closes the question at the end of 93:4. Each verse is chanted alone, so 93:3 closes its own question (as DRB); 93:4 is a second question. gloriári → 'gloriar-se' (glossary: the same verb for the sinners' boasting, 93:3 named).

4

effári (φθέγγομαι, to utter) → 'proferir', kept apart from loqui → 'falar', whose repetition across the asterisk is kept. operári injustítiam → 'praticar a injustiça' (injustítia → injustiça, apart from iníquitas).

5

Natural Portuguese order (verb first) for the Latin's fronted object 'Pópulum tuum' (D2).

6

ádvena → 'forasteiro' (glossary; here the Greek is προσήλυτος, not πάροικος, but the Latin word is the same); pupíllus → 'órfão' (glossary).

7

'Deus Jacob' → 'o Deus de Jacó'; intellégere → 'entender' (glossary).

10

corrípere → 'castigar', árguere → 'repreender' (both settled, D21; the glossary names 93:10). sciéntia → 'o saber' (glossary names 93:10). The second colon is a relative with no main verb, as in the Latin; left hanging.

12

13

fóvea → 'fosso', fódere → 'cavar' (glossary; 56:7b 'Cavaram um fosso').

14

repéllere → 'repelir' (settled D42); plebs → 'povo'; derelínquere → 'abandonar'. The first colon is quoted in the Invitatory (Psalterium/Invitatorium.txt, 'quóniam non repéllet Dóminus plebem suam') — Ps 94 should copy it.

16

consúrgere → 'levantar-se'; malignántes → 'os malvados' (D24; the glossary names 93:16); 'mihi' → 'por mim' (DRB 'for me', MS1932). The second colon is long (+4): the formula 'os que praticam a iniquidade' is fixed.

17

19

22

'factus est mihi … in refúgium' → 'se fez refúgio para mim', as 117:14 'se fez salvação para mim' (in + accusative of result, no preposition in Portuguese). The first colon is a responsory (Tempora/Epi2-5, 'R. Factus est mihi Dóminus in refúgium') and reads alone. adjutórium → 'auxílio'; spes → 'esperança'.

23

reddere → 'pagar' (27:4b, 61:12); 'ipsórum … eórum' → 'deles … deles', so that 'sua' is not heard as the Lord's; malítia → 'malícia' (glossary names 93:23).
