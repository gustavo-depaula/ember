“o que entende o necessitado e o pobre”

'entender' with a person as direct object reads as 'understands him' in a psychological sense ('entende o pobre' = empathizes, gets him); the Latin 'intellegit super' is attending to, taking thought for. The ear hesitates on what is meant.

proposedBem-aventurado o que atende ao necessitado e ao pobre: * no dia mau o livrará o Senhor.

kept as an option under qui intéllegit super egénum, et páuperem — The fault is mended the Latinist's way (*acerca de*); *atender* is inténdere's (D3). His line is option 3.

fails: native, plain

“Ele saía para fora”

'sair para fora' is the textbook redundancy Brazilian ears are trained to wince at; the Latin pleonasm 'egrediebatur foras' can be kept with a native idiom that also stays concrete.

proposedSaía porta afora, * e falava no mesmo sentido.

kept as an option under Egrediebátur foras — The pleonasm is the Latin's (*egrédi foras*) and carries the inside/outside contrast with 40:7; *porta* is an image the Latin lacks.

“falava no mesmo sentido”

Slightly bureaucratic, flat close; acceptable, but 'do mesmo modo' is plainer to the mouth.

proposede falava do mesmo modo.

kept as an option under et loquebátur in idípsum — Manner, where *in idípsum* is direction or purpose.

fails: native, sayable

“pensavam em males para mim”

'pensar em males para mim' is a clumsy chain of prepositions (em ... para); the colon limps after the clean first half.

proposedcontra mim pensavam o mal para mim.

❧ taken — In part: the redundant dative left unsaid; the row's *pensar em* and the plural kept.

fails: plain

“não tornará a ressurgir”

'tornar a' + 're-surgir' doubles the 'again', which the ear hears as redundant.

proposedAcaso o que dorme não tornará a levantar-se?

kept as an option under Numquid qui dormit non adíciet ut resúrgat? — The Latin doubles it too (*adíciet ut resúrgat*); *ressurgir* is the row's word, apart from 40:11's *ressuscitar*.

10

fails: native, sayable

“engrandeceu contra mim a sua rasteira”

'rasteira' is colloquial (passar a rasteira) and collides with the elevated 'engrandeceu'; the collocation 'engrandecer uma rasteira' does not exist, so the line ends in a jolt of register. The second colon is also long for one breath.

proposedque comia o meu pão, fez grande contra mim a sua rasteira.

The psalm reads as faithful, sober and mostly sayable, with the vós address handled cleanly and good oxytone/paroxytone endings at nearly every mark. Its weak points are a handful of calques that a Brazilian ear hears as translation ('saía para fora', 'disseram males', 'pensavam em males para mim') and one register slip in 'engrandeceu a sua rasteira'. 2

3

beátum fáciat → *o faça bem-aventurado* (D19; the beátus row names 40:3). consérvet → *conserve* (15:1, 36:28). tradere → *entregar* (9:35a, 26:12, 118:121). The versicle for the Pope (Preces.txt) — it reads alone. v2: the blind reader heard *à alma dos seus inimigos* vaguely and did not know *vivifique*; both kept (26:12; the vivificáre row), with *à vontade* and *lhe dê vida* as options.

4

lectus → *leito*, dolor → *dor*, infirmitas → *enfermidade* (15:4a). *super lectum* → *sobre o leito* (MS1932).

5

Ego dixi → *Eu disse* (38:2). *Miserére mei* → *tende piedade de mim* (formula). sanáre → *curar* (6:3, 29:3). *peccávi tibi* → *pequei contra vós* (DRB, MS1932). *Sana ánimam meam, quia peccávi tibi* is a short responsory (Major/Minor Special, Preces — grep): *Curai a minha alma, porque pequei contra vós* reads alone.

6

períre → *perecer* (row). The quoted question keeps its capital, as the Latin.

7

vana loqui → *falar coisas vãs* (11:3). congregáre → *juntar* (32:7, 34:15, 38:7b); the perfect kept (MS1932's imperfect *acumulava* not taken). ingrédi here is entering a room, not walking (14:2, 37:7 *andar*): the verse sets *ingrediebátur* against 40:7b *egrediebátur foras*.

8

The anaphora *Contra mim … contra mim* kept at the head of both colons, as the Latin.

9

constitúere → *estabelecer* (2:6, 4:10, 8:6, 9:21, 17:44, 117:27b). iníquus → *iníquo*. Natural order: the verb first.

10

homo pacis meæ → *o homem da minha paz*: the Hebraism kept (MS1932 explains: *da minha intimidade*); 36:37 *hómini pacífico* → *homem de paz*. speráre in → *esperar em* (D36). édere → *comer* (row).

11

Tu autem, Dómine → *Vós, porém, Senhor* (3:4). retribúere → *retribuir* (17:21, 30:24); *eu* supplied so the first person is heard after two imperatives. Holy Saturday's responsory (Quad6-6) quotes the second colon with *Et resúscita me*.

12

cognóvi quóniam → *conheci que* (118:75). *gaudébit … super me* is ἐπιχαρῇ ἐπ᾿ ἐμέ, the Greek of *supergaudére* (34:19, 34:24, 37:17 — read in consult/parallels/ps034.md, ps037.md), so the same Portuguese: *não se alegrará à minha custa* (also 29:2). The second *quóniam* → *porque* (DRB 'because'). v2: the Latinist asked *sobre mim* for *super me* (minor); refused — the Greek is supergaudére's (ἐπιχαρῇ ἐπ᾿ ἐμέ), and 29:2, 34:19, 34:24, 37:17 all say *à minha custa*; *se alegrar sobre mim* is not Portuguese.

13

Me autem → *Mas a mim* (the fronted object; *mas eu* is ego autem's). suscípere → *amparar* (D19; 29:2 *suscepísti me → me amparastes*).

14

Benedíctus Dóminus → *Bendito o Senhor* (27:6, 30:22). *Deus Israël* → *Deus de Israel*, in apposition. The same verse closes Book IV (105:48, with *et dicet omnis pópulus*) and the Benedictus opens with *Benedíctus Dóminus Deus Israël*: whoever translates them should copy this wording.
