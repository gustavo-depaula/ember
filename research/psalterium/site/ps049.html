The audit trail

1draft

Psalm-level draft from parallels (no Diurnal: not a day-hours psalm). God speaks in the first person from 49:7: to Israel (tu) and then to the sinner (tu); the heavens / the court (49:5) and the forgetful (49:22) are plural (vós). Hard readings: 49:5 *órdinant testaméntum super sacrifícia*; 49:21 *iníque* (adverb or vocative) and the object-less *státuam*; 49:17 the tense of *odísti*; 49:1b the verbless *spécies decóris*.

2checks

Hard checks pass. Soft length flags accepted: 49:3 second colon +4 (*ficar em silêncio* is the silére row's, kept apart from tacére → *calar-se* in 49:20); 49:7 −3, 49:20 −3, 49:22 −3 (Portuguese shorter, no loss); 49:11 −3/+3 (the Latin order *mecum est* kept at the end); 49:21 +4/+3 (*iniquamente* and *semelhante a ti* are the words; *como tu* would save three syllables but lose símilis's word, which 48:13 keeps as *semelhante*); 49:23 +5 (the supplied *está*; the verbless option is 2 syllables shorter).

1

fails: native

“O Deus dos deuses, o Senhor, falou”

The opening 'O' is spoken exactly like the vocative 'Ó', so for a moment the ear hears 'O God of gods...' as an address. Then 'falou' turns it into a statement and the reciter has to re-parse. Without a verb near the start, the line feels like it is calling out rather than telling.

proposedFalou o Senhor, o Deus dos deuses: * e chamou a terra,

❧ taken

3

fails: plain, native

“Deus virá manifestamente”

'manifestamente' is a heavy six-syllable adverb that sounds like a legal document. It swallows the colon and is hard to point to a tone.

proposedDeus virá abertamente

❧ taken

“e não ficará em silêncio”

The periphrasis is flat and wordy. The plain verb is 'calar-se', which also closes on a clean oxytone.

proposedo nosso Deus, e não se calará.

kept as an option under Deus noster et non silébit — silére / tacére kept apart (49:20).

3b

fails: plain

“Um fogo se inflamará na sua presença”

'se inflamará' is clinical, and the reflexive clitic in the middle of the colon makes it lumpy. 'Arder' is the plain word for fire that blazes up.

proposedUm fogo arderá na sua presença: * e ao seu redor, uma tempestade violenta.

kept as an option under Ignis in conspéctu ejus exardéscet — exardéscere row; arder is concaléscere's in 38:4.

fails: native

“Congregai para ele os seus santos”

'para ele' sits awkwardly between the verb and its object. 'Congregai' next to 'para ele' also sounds stiff.

proposedReuni para ele os seus santos

not taken — bare Reuni = I gathered (rule 3); already option 3 of congregate.

fails: native

“Não receberei da tua casa bezerros”

Putting the object after 'da tua casa' copies the Latin order. It sounds translated, and the object arrives last, like an afterthought.

proposedNão aceitarei bezerros da tua casa: * nem bodes dos teus rebanhos.

❧ taken — order taken; the verb refused (accípere → receber), kept as option 3 of order9.

13

fails: native

“as carnes dos touros”

'as carnes' in the plural sounds like a butcher's counter. In Portuguese, flesh as food is naturally singular.

proposedAcaso comerei a carne dos touros? * ou beberei o sangue dos bodes?

❧ taken

16

fails: sayable

“Por que tu narras as minhas justiças, e tomas a minha aliança na tua boca?”

The second colon is very long for one breath. 'tu narras' is two short stressed syllables that jolt, and 'narras' is a storyteller's word, weaker than the Latin 'enarras'.

proposedPor que proclamas as minhas justiças, e tomas a minha aliança na tua boca?

kept as an option under Quare tu enárras justítias meas — enarráre row; proclamar is prædicáre's.

18

fails: native, concrete

“punhas a tua porção”

The phrase is opaque in Portuguese. 'Pôr a porção' is not an idiom, and the ear hears a portion of food being set down. 'parte' is the plainer faithful word and keeps 'punhas'.

proposede com os adúlteros punhas a tua parte.

kept as an option under et cum adúlteris portiónem tuam ponébas — pórtio row; parte makes the idiom no more native.

20

fails: native, sayable

“punhas tropeço”

The bare noun without an article sounds clipped and not Portuguese. The first colon is also long, and the missing article makes the reciter stumble right at the flex.

proposedSentado, falavas contra o teu irmão, e contra o filho da tua mãe punhas um tropeço: * isto fizeste, e eu me calei.

❧ taken

21

fails: plain, native

“Pensaste iniquamente”

'iniquamente' is a clumsy five-syllable adverb right after the verb. It sounds bookish.

proposedPensaste, ó iníquo, que eu seria semelhante a ti

kept as an option under Existimásti, iníque, quod ero tui símilis — the other reading of iníque; DRB and MS1932 read the adverb.

“e porei diante da tua face”

'pôr' with no object leaves the ear waiting for a word that never comes, so the final cadence feels cut off. A neutral pronoun keeps the Latin as open as it is.

proposedeu te repreenderei, e o porei diante da tua face.

kept as an option under árguam te, et státuam contra fáciem tuam — o supplies an object heard as him.

22

fails: native

“para que não arrebate, e não haja quem liberte”

The verb has no subject, which in Portuguese reads almost like a misprint. 'liberte' is weaker and less natural here than 'livre'.

proposedpara que ele não arrebate, e não haja quem livre.

kept as an option under nequándo rápiat, et non sit qui erípiat — the rápere row (no subject) and erípere row (libertar).

The psalm reads with dignity and generally good cadences. The divine speech from verse 7 on is strong and direct. Its weak spots are Latinate adverbs ('manifestamente', 'iniquamente'), a few calqued orders and bare nouns ('punhas tropeço', 'punhas a tua porção'), and object- or subject-less verbs that are clear in Latin but leave the Portuguese ear hanging.

Verse by verse

1

*Deus deórum, Dóminus*: the two names in apposition, set off with commas (DRB *The God of gods, the Lord*). *locútus est* → *falou*.

3

silére → *ficar em silêncio* and tacére (49:20) → *calar-se*: the silére / tacére row keeps two Latin verbs apart; here the Greek differs too (παρασιωπήσεται / ἐσίγησα).

3b

exardéscere → *inflamar-se*, *um fogo se inflamará* as 38:4. in circúitu → *ao seu redor* (17:12).

4

*desúrsum* → *lá do alto*.

6

annuntiáre → *anunciar* (D41). *judex est* → *é juiz*, no article, as the Latin.

7

God speaks to Israel, one people: *tu* (rule 3; D17). *Deus, Deus tuus* → *Deus, o teu Deus*, as 44:8.

8

árguere → *repreender* (D21), the same verb again in 49:21 as the Latin repeats *árguam te*. *in sacrifíciis* (ἐπί, 'concerning') → *pelos*. holocáustum → *holocausto* (the row).

9

accípere → *receber* (the row). vítulus → *bezerro* (the row). Latin order kept (*da tua casa bezerros*), so that the mediant falls on *bezerros*.

10

juménta → *os animais* (the row; *jumento* is a donkey).

11

volatília cæli → *as aves do céu*; pulchritúdo → *beleza* (D43).

12

orbis terræ → *o mundo* (D30); *et plenitúdo ejus* → *e a sua plenitude*, the formula of 23:1. *Si esuríero* → *Se eu tiver fome*.

13

numquid → *acaso* (the row). carnes → *as carnes* (the row).

14

vota réddere → *pagar os votos* (21:26). Altíssimus → *Altíssimo* (the row).

15
