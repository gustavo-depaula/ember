5b

minor

“derrubar os meus passos” supplantáre gressus meos

supplantare is to trip up, to catch the feet from under (the heel image); 'derrubar' (knock down) blurs the image, and 'derrubar os passos' is awkward. 'Fazer tropeçar' keeps the concrete sense of tripping.

proposedOs que maquinaram fazer tropeçar os meus passos: * os soberbos esconderam um laço para mim:

not taken — v1 had *fazer tropeçar*; the stylist heard *tropeçar* (5b) and *tropeço* (6b, scándalum) as one root twice, which merges two Latin words the psalm keeps apart. *Derrubar* is the supplantáre row's verb, and with *gressus* it matches 36:31 *os seus passos não serão derrubados*, which passed its gate. Readers pulled both ways on a minor, so the row is held; *fazer tropeçar* stays as option 1.

A close, literal rendering that keeps the Latin's hard readings intact (the 'a desiderio meo peccatori' construction in v. 9, 'caput circuitus eorum' in v. 10, 'cum vultu tuo' in v. 14) without smoothing them toward the Hebrew. Tenses, persons and moods are preserved, and every verse carries the same single mediant as the Latin. The only fault is a slight blurring of the tripping image in 'supplantare'.

Verse by verse

2

No title line (*In finem, Psalmus David*): DO's Latin has none; D7. *homo malus → homem mau*; *vir iníquus → homem iníquo* (17:49 *do homem iníquo me arrancareis*). The Latin's *homo / vir* variation cannot be kept (*varão* would need a footnote). Each colon reads alone (the Passiontide versicle).

3

*Qui* → *Os que*, a substantive relative after the singular *homem iníquo*, as the Latin's plural *qui* turns to the whole band. *tota die → o dia todo* (D24). *in corde → no coração*.

4

The second colon copies 13:3b exactly (glossary formula row, = Rom 3:13). The ambiguity reader listed *áspides* unknown; the formula (13:3b, Rom 3:13) is kept.

5

= the Offertory of Tuesday in Holy Week (Quad6-2), word for word. *de manu peccatóris → da mão do pecador* (70:4).

5b

The Latin's colon at the end is kept; the sentence runs into 139:6. *abscondérunt … láqueum mihi → esconderam um laço para mim* (30:5, 34:7 *esconderam para mim*; 118:110 *puseram um laço para mim*). *supérbi → os soberbos* (118:51, 78, 122).

6

*juxta iter → junto do caminho* (*juxta* of place → *junto de*, 5:6, 37:12b; iter → caminho). *scándalum → tropeço* (the row); *mihi → para mim*, as 5b. v2: 5b's *derrubar* removes the *tropeçar / tropeço* echo.

7

*Dixi Dómino: Deus meus es tu* = 15:1 (*Eu disse ao Senhor: Vós sois o meu Deus*); *vocem deprecatiónis meæ → a voz da minha prece* (27:2, 27:6, 65:19, 85:6, 129:2b; D35).

8

*Dómine, Dómine* both kept. *obumbrásti super caput meum → fizestes sombra sobre a minha cabeça* (obumbráre → fazer sombra a, 90:4; *super* kept). *in die belli → no dia da guerra* (77:9); *bellum → guerra* apart from *prǽlium → batalha* (139:3).

9

Three clauses in the second colon, as the Latin; a semicolon after *contra mim* so that *não me abandoneis* is heard as a new plea. *ne derelínquas me → não me abandoneis* (26:9b, 37:22). *trádere → entregar* (118:121 *não me entregueis aos que me caluniam*).

10

The first colon verbless, as the Latin; *seu* is theirs (God is *vosso* in this psalter). *opériet → cobrirá*.

13

*Eu* supplied so the colon does not open on a bare verb that could be heard as a question; the subject is in the Latin's ending.

14

*Verúmtamen → Todavia* (the row). *confitebúntur nómini tuo → darão graças ao vosso nome* (D5). *cum vultu tuo → com o vosso rosto*, word for word 15:11, 20:7 (the Latin's *cum*, σὺν τῷ προσώπῳ; CNBB *junto a vós*, Ave Maria *em vossa presença* explain). *recti → os retos* (the rectus row). This verse closes the Good Friday Tract.

11

The second colon in the natural order (*não subsistirão nas misérias*; D2): in the Latin's order it ended on *subsistirão*, which rhymed with *destruição* at the end of 139:12, an echo the Latin (*subsístent / intéritu*) does not have. *in misériis → nas misérias* (ταλαιπωρίαις). The ambiguity reader listed *subsistirão* unknown; kept (102:16; the Latin's word).
