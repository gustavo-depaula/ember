Salmo 143

Benedíctus Dóminus

Draft 2 — reviewed. Pray it aloud first; then decide below. Every choice you make rewrites the psalm here, so what you pray is what you chose. Nothing is final until you say so.

Dotted gold marks words still waiting for your decision — touch one to choose its wording where it stands. A gold wash marks where your choice departs from draft 2.

Each verse from the Latin down to what is prayed. Between one draft and the next, words that left are struck through and words that came carry the gold wash; beside them, who objected and what the audit says. A verse no draft touched folds to one line — open it to see its strata.

The literal tier: the Latin’s words and order kept as far as Portuguese grammar allows — the scaffold the prayed text was made from, not a text to pray.

1draft

Psalm-level draft from consult/parallels/ps143.md, with precedent from ps143/concord.py over all finished psalms (Ps 17's twin lines, 8:5, 48:13, 32:12, 74:9, 101:12, 103:32, 127:3b) and the glossary rows. Hard readings: the three rescue verbs (7, 10, 11); fulgura coruscatiónem; the Latin's 'quorum' making 12–14 the strangers' prosperity against the Hebrew's wish; boves … crassæ (feminine).

2checks

Hard checks pass. Soft flags accepted: 143:3 +6 (the counter does not elide; sung, 'que‿é‿o … dado‿a … a‿ele' is ~18 against 16, and the 8:5 build needs the auxiliary; 'vos deis a conhecer' is the shorter option); 143:15 +5 (same elisions; the anaphora kept); 143:9 +3 (the 32:2 formula); 143:12b +3; short second cola at 143:7, 143:14 and first cola at 143:11, 143:12 (-3 to -6), where the Latin's long words have short Portuguese equivalents. 3

minor

“para que vos tenhais dado a conhecer a ele? … para que o leveis em conta?” quia innotuísti ei? … quia réputas eum?

Latin 'quia' is a plain 'that' (the fact that you have made yourself known), not a purpose clause; 'para que' reads as 'in order that' and so adds a telic shade the Latin does not have.

proposedSenhor, que é o homem, que vos tenhais dado a conhecer a ele? * ou o filho do homem, que o leveis em conta?

kept as an option under quia innotuísti ei? … quia réputas eum? — 8:5's build, held under D25; his fix is not a Portuguese question

10

minor

“libertai-me” éripe me

'éripe' is rendered 'arrancai-me' in 143:7 and 'líbera' is 'livrai-me' there; here 'éripe' becomes 'libertai-me', blurring the eripere/liberare distinction the psalm itself keeps side by side.

proposedVós que dais a salvação aos reis: * que resgatastes Davi, vosso servo, da espada maligna: arrancai-me.

kept as an option under éripe me, et líbera me de aquis multis (7) · éripe me (10) · Et érue me de manu filiórum alienórum (11) — no source named: 'arrancai-me' hangs; merges éripe with érue at 10/11

A faithful, close rendering throughout; Septuagintal features (homo vanitati similis, eructantia ex hoc in illud, circumornatae ut similitudo templi, beatum dixerunt) are kept rather than smoothed. Only two minor points: the purpose-sounding 'para que' for 'quia' in v. 3 and an inconsistent verb for 'eripe' in v. 10. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse.

1

fails: native

“que ensina as minhas mãos para a batalha”

'ensinar' does not take a direct object plus 'para' in Brazilian Portuguese; the ear hears a calque. 'adestrar' is the verb that naturally governs hands trained for combat and keeps the parallel with 'os meus dedos para a guerra'.

proposedBendito o Senhor, meu Deus, que adestra as minhas mãos para a batalha, * e os meus dedos para a guerra.

kept as an option under qui docet manus meas ad prǽlium — 17:35 is the same Latin; docére → ensinar

2b

fails: native

“que sujeita o meu povo sob mim”

The bare 'que' after the colon has lost its antecedent (the list of titles is two cola back), and 'que sujeita' can momentarily be heard as 'que [é] sujeita'. Naming the subject restores the line.

proposedO meu protetor, e nele esperei: * ele, que sujeita o meu povo sob mim.

❧ taken

3

fails: sayable, native

“para que vos tenhais dado a conhecer a ele?”

Heavy compound-tense chain with the pronoun trailing at the mediant: 'tenhais dado a conhecer a ele' is a mouthful and ends on a weak 'a ele'. Fronting the pronoun lets the colon land on the stressed last syllable of 'conhecer'.

proposedSenhor, que é o homem, para que a ele vos tenhais dado a conhecer? * ou o filho do homem, para que o leveis em conta?

❧ taken

'fulgurar' is bookish and doubles the sense of 'relâmpago' with a rare verb; a plainer verb keeps the image of lightning flashing.

proposedFazei brilhar o relâmpago, e os dispersareis: * enviai as vossas setas, e os perturbareis.

'adornadas em volta' is stiff, and 'volta à' clumps; 'em redor' is the natural liturgical phrase and a comma gives the breath.

13

fails: native, concrete

“jorrando deste para aquele”

'deste para aquele' dangles with no noun for the demonstratives to point at; 'de um para outro' is the Portuguese idiom for 'ex hoc in illud'. 'jorrar' of grain is also odd; 'transbordar' keeps the image of overflowing stores.

proposedOs seus celeiros estão cheios, * transbordando de um para outro.

❧ taken — idiom taken; 'transbordando' refused for the eructáre row (option in eructantia)

14

fails: native

“Não há ruína de cerca”

'ruína de cerca' sounds like a calque and 'cerca' suggests a fence rather than the stone wall of 'maceria'.

proposedNão há ruína de muro, nem passagem: * nem clamor nas suas praças.

kept as an option under ruína macériæ — macéria → cerca, 61:4, 79:13

The psalm moves well in its war-hymn opening and the theophany (vv. 5-6) is strong and sayable; the closing blessing is clean and ends with a fine cadence. The weak spots are calques that the ear registers as translation: 'ensina ... para', 'filhos estranhos', 'falou vaidade', and the idyll of vv. 12-14, where several phrases ('em volta', 'deste para aquele', 'ruína de cerca') read stiffly.

10

minor

“libertai-me” éripe me

éripe means to snatch or tear away, and 143:7 renders the same verb 'arrancai-me' while using 'livrai-me' for líbera. 'Libertai-me' makes éripe sound like líbera, so the concrete image of pulling out is lost and the psalm's wording is no longer consistent. (Érue in 143:11 is a different verb, so a different Portuguese word there would keep the two apart.)

proposedarrancai-me (and in 143:11, if you want to keep érue distinct, 'E tirai-me da mão…')

kept as an option under éripe me, et líbera me de aquis multis (7) · éripe me (10) · Et érue me de manu filiórum alienórum (11) — the erípere row's source rule; 'tirar' is edúcere's and auferre's; 'arrancai-me' hangs without a source

The translation follows the Gallican Latin closely all the way through. Tenses, moods and persons are kept, the Septuagintal images are preserved (the vanity simile, the jutting-out storehouses, the ruin of the wall, 'nem passagem'), and the only supplied words are copulas Portuguese needs. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only fault is minor: éripe in 143:10 is rendered 'libertai-me', which clashes with the psalm's own 'arrancai-me' in 143:7.

Verse by verse

1

Head as the formula 'Bendito o Senhor, Deus de Israel' (40:14, 71:18; D41) — no 'seja'. The rest copies 17:35 'Que ensina as minhas mãos para a batalha' (prǽlium → batalha, bellum → guerra, as 17:40, 75:4). Also the Gradual of 03-27 (missa Sancti). No titulus (D7: DO's Psalm143.txt has none; the Greek's τῷ Δαυιδ πρὸς τὸν Γολιαδ and MS1932's 'Salmo de David, contra Golias' are not in the working text).

2

Verbless appositions, as 17:3c 'O meu protetor, e o chifre da minha salvação, * e o meu amparo'; article kept before each possessive. suscéptor → amparo (D19). The first Vespers antiphon (Benedíctus Dóminus * suscéptor meus et liberátor meus) is built from these words: 'Bendito o Senhor, * o meu amparo e o meu libertador'.

2b

'qui subdit pópulum meum sub me' follows 17:48 'sujeitais os povos sob mim' (subdere takes subjícere's verb; the row says 143:2 should follow). 'nele esperei' as 27:7.

4

'como a sombra', with the article, as 101:12 and 108:23. prætéreunt → 'passam'.

5

17:10 'Inclinou os céus, e desceu' and 103:32 'que toca os montes, e eles fumegam' — the same verbs. 'descei', 'tocai' are safe at the vós imperative (past 'desci', 'toquei').

6

dissipáre with people as object → 'dispersar' (the row's proposal, 17:15 'e os dispersou', 67:31b 'Dispersai'); conturbáre → 'perturbar' (17:15). emíttere → 'enviar', one verb for 143:6 and 143:7 (the emíttere row; 17:15, 17:17 'enviou'). The ambiguity reader found no antecedent for 'os' (dispersareis, perturbareis): the Latin's 'eos' has none either (the enemies appear in 143:7); kept.

7

'de muitas águas' without the article, as 17:17 'de muitas águas'; 'da mão dos filhos estranhos' as the aliénus row (17:46 'Os filhos estranhos'). The Latin order 'a vossa mão do alto' kept.

8

Relative 'Cuja' opens the verse and hangs on 143:7, as the Latin's 'Quorum'. A copula is supplied in the second colon (the Latin has none); the repeated 'direita' kept. déxtera → 'direita' (row). 143:11 repeats 143:8 word for word, as the Latin does. The ambiguity reader heard 'direita' without 'mão' as 'what is right' or the political right: the déxtera row keeps the bare noun, as 17:36a, 117:16; recorded, not changed.

9

The responsory of Pasc4-0 (R. Deus, cánticum novum cantábo tibi … In psaltério decem chordárum psallam tibi) reads from this verse. psaltério decachórdo → 'o saltério de dez cordas' (32:2; the decachórdum row names 143:9 for this). psállere → 'entoar salmos' (D25), 'vos entoarei salmos' as 137:1b. Bare vocative 'Deus' (D44).

10

salus → 'salvação' (D6); MS1932's 'saúde' is the older sense. redímere perfect → 'resgatastes' (the row: 30:6, 70:23, 76:15). 'Davi, vosso servo' as 131:10. The LXX ends 143:10 at πονηρᾶς and has ῥῦσαί με in 143:11; the Latin moves 'éripe me' to the end of 10 — kept there.

12

'Quorum' refers back to the 'filhos estranhos' (143:11), as in the Greek (ὧν): in the Latin, 143:12–14 describe the prosperity of the strangers, and 143:15a reports that men called such a people happy. The Hebrew (and so the CNBB and Ave Maria, circulation.md) reads 'our sons … our barns' as a wish; not followed. Copulas supplied at the head of 12, 12b, 13, 13b (grammar, D2); the second cola stay verbless as in the Latin.

14

macéria → 'cerca' (61:4, 79:13); platéa → 'praças' (54:12 'das suas praças'); clamor → 'clamor' (row).

15

The second colon is the Saturday Vespers antiphon 'Beátus pópulus * cujus Dóminus Deus ejus' (Psalmi major) and reads alone: 'Bem-aventurado o povo * cujo Deus é o Senhor'. beátus → 'bem-aventurado' (D19). The ambiguity reader could not tell who 'disseram' are and heard both lines as one approving statement: the Latin leaves it just so (no 'sed'); kept.
