1draft

Canticle-level draft. parallels.py handles psalm numbers only, so ps216/show_parallels.py builds consult/parallels/ps216.md from: the DO Latin; the Bolls Vulgate of Sirach 36 (book 71); Douay-Rheims from drbo.org, since Bolls DRB has no deuterocanonicals; the Rahlfs LXX from consult/lxx-*.csv (the Latin's source text, with some lines of another text); Matos Soares 1932 from the pdftotext layer of the 1932 PDF, pp. 386–387; the Diurnal Monástico 1962 (Saturday Lauds, Eclo 36,1-13, a Vulgate-family text close to MS1932, *tu* address); and DO's Portuguese (no authority). The downloaded third-party texts live in consult/ps216/, which is gitignored. No Hebrew is at hand. Precedent checked with ps216/concord.py and ps216/gloss.py (copies of ps210's and ps212's). Brazilian circulation fetched and recorded in ps216/circulation.md: the CNBB LH (Monday Lauds, Week II, Eclo 36,1-7.13-16, a metrical paraphrase from the Greek that omits the verses of wrath) and the Lectionary (Wednesday, Week 8, Year I, Eclo 36,1-2a.5-6.13-19). Homograph ban applied twice: *afligi* (36:8) and *reuni* (36:12) are refused, decisions `affige` and `congrega`. Hard readings: *Deus ómnium*, *qui salvátur*, *hereditábis eos*, *coæquásti*, *immúta mirabília*.

Hard checks pass: ids and marks match DO's Latin. render.py and checks.py work unchanged with DO number 216, and readVerses skips the heading line. Soft flags, all length, all accepted. 36:1b −5: Portuguese is shorter than the Latin's *miseratiónum tuárum*, and the colon is the antiphon. 36:6a +3: the object *vos* and *como também*. 36:6b −4: *Dómine → Senhor*. 36:10a +3: *quem se salva* (decision `salvatur`). 36:13a −3. 36:14a +6: the Latin's idiom kept (decision `invocatum`). 36:14b +4. 36:1

fails: native, plain

“a luz das vossas compaixões”

'Compaixões' in the plural is not used in Brazilian prayer and sounds coined; the ear expects the usual liturgical word for miserationes.

proposede mostrai-nos a luz das vossas misericórdias:

not taken — *Miseratiónes → compaixões* is the glossary's row (24:6, 50:3b, 102:4), kept apart from *misericórdia* because the Greek has two words too. The stylist asked *clemências* at 50:3b, also refused. *Misericórdias* would merge the two families.

36:3

fails: plain, native

“e narrem as vossas grandes coisas.”

'Grandes coisas' is flat and a little colloquial for magnalia, and it ends the verse on a weak noun. 'Grandes obras' keeps the 'great' image and stays separate from 'maravilhas' (mirabilia) in 36:9.

proposede narrem as vossas grandes obras.

not taken as proposed

36:4

fails: plain, native

“nações estranhas”

In current Portuguese 'estranhas' first means 'strange/odd'. 'Estrangeiras' is the plain sense of alienas.

proposedLevantai a vossa mão sobre as nações estrangeiras,

not taken — *Aliénus → estranho* is the glossary's row, wider than 'foreign' (anyone not one's own; 17:46). The ambiguity reader heard 'foreign nations' first.

“a vossa potência”

'Potência' sounds technical (physics, geopolitics). 'Poder' is the plainer faithful word and ends the verse on an oxytone.

proposedpara que vejam o vosso poder.

not taken — *Poténtia → potência* is the glossary's row (64:7, 70:19, Ps 144). *Poder* is *virtus*'s word.

36:6

fails: sayable

“como também nós conhecemos, * que não há Deus”

'Conhecemos' has no object and is followed by a comma and 'que', so the listener hears a dangling verb and then a stray clause. Moving 'também' makes the phrase flow into what follows.

proposedPara que vos conheçam, como nós também conhecemos, * que não há Deus senão vós, Senhor.

❧ taken — Order only (D2); decision `cognovimus`.

36:12

fails: plain

“e narrem as vossas grandes coisas:”

Same as 36:3. The refrain should match whatever is chosen there.

proposede narrem as vossas grandes obras:

not taken as proposed

36:13

fails: native

“como desde o início.”

'Início' is everyday and administrative. 'Princípio' is plainer in prayer and echoes 'como era no princípio'.

proposedcomo desde o princípio.

not taken — *Ab inítio → desde o início* is the glossary's row (73:2, where the stylist asked for *princípio* and was refused). *Princípio* is *princípium*'s.

36:14

fails: cadence

“que igualastes ao vosso primogênito.”

The verse ends on a proparoxytone (pri-mo-GÊ-ni-to), which drags the final cadence. The Latin does the same, so this is tolerable. The only fix is inversion, which costs some naturalness.

proposede de Israel, que ao vosso primogênito igualastes.

not taken — He calls it tolerable, since the Latin has the same stress. The inversion costs more naturalness than the cadence costs sound.

The psalm sounds sober and firm. The bare imperative pairs in 36:7-9 have real liturgical force, and keeping 'inenarráveis' rightly echoes 'narrem'. Where it slips, it's through a few words that sound translated or flat ('compaixões', 'grandes coisas', 'estranhas', 'potência') rather than through syntax.

36:10

minor

“quem escapa” qui salvátur

The Latin is passive ('he who is saved, who is being kept safe'); 'escapa' makes it active and narrows it to escaping. The sense, the survivor, is right, but the voice is changed.

proposedNa ira da chama seja devorado quem se salva:

kept as an option under In ira flammæ devorétur qui salvátur — He grants that the sense, the survivor, is right. *Quem se salva* is reflexive and so is not the passive either. The ambiguity reader heard it as 'whoever is saved' in the religious sense, a curse on the saved, and that misreading costs more in a prayer than the voice does. The v1 Latinist had passed *se salva*, so the two readers split. Held for Gustavo, with *quem se salva* as option 2.

36:11

minor

“a cabeça dos príncipes inimigos” caput príncipum inimicórum

'inimicorum' can be the adjective ('hostile princes') or the noun ('the princes of the enemies'). The Latin leaves both open; 'príncipes inimigos' reads mostly as the adjective. It is defensible, and Portuguese keeps some of the ambiguity, so this is noted only.

proposedQuebrai a cabeça dos príncipes dos inimigos,

not taken — He calls it defensible and noted it only. *Príncipes inimigos* keeps some of the openness: they are the enemies' princes, and they are hostile. *dos príncipes dos inimigos* stacks two *dos* in a short colon.

A faithful, close rendering of Sirach 36:1-16 that keeps the Latin's imperatives, tenses, and concrete images (the hand, the right arm, the flame), including the Septuagintal 'qui salvatur'. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex or second mediant). The only points are two minor ones: the passive 'salvatur' is rendered as the active 'escapa', and the genitive 'inimicorum' is read as an adjective.

Verse by verse

36:1

DO's first line, *(Canticum Ecclesiastici * Sir 36:1-16)*, is a heading with the book reference. It is not a prayed verse and is not translated (D7; readVerses skips it, as for 233, 211, 213). DO's ids are the canticle's own line numbers, not the Clementine verses (see the head of consult/parallels/ps216.md). The Latin is the Vulgate of Sirach (the Old Latin, which Jerome did not revise), not the Gallican psalter. *Miserére nostri → Tende piedade de nós* (122:3). *Réspice nos → olhai para nós* (21:2, 24:16 *olhai para mim*). *Miseratiónum → das vossas compaixões* (24:6, 50:3b). The second colon is the antiphon *Osténde nobis, Dómine, * lucem miseratiónum tuárum* (Saturday Lauds): *Mostrai-nos, Senhor, a luz das vossas compaixões* reads alone.

36:2

No comma before the mediant: the relative is restrictive (the nations that have not sought you), and the Latin's comma at the asterisk is DO's punctuation, not a pause the Portuguese needs. *Exquisiérunt → procuraram* (118:155 *não procuraram*). *Timor → temor*.

36:3

*Enárrent → narrem* (the glossary's *enarráre → narrar*, 18:2). *Magnália → as vossas grandes coisas* (70:19, 105:21), kept apart from *mirabília → maravilhas* in 36:7 and 36:9. 36:12 repeats this verse word for word, and so does the Portuguese.

36:4

*Álleva → Levantai* (the *alleváre* row, 144:14). *Gentes aliénas → nações estranhas*. *Poténtiam → potência* (64:7, 70:19). *Super* kept as *sobre*: 'lift your hand over', the gesture of power; *contra* (the CNBB LH) decides.

36:5

*Sicut enim … sic → Pois, assim como … assim*. *Sanctificátus es in nobis → fostes santificado em nós*; *magnificáberis in eis → sereis engrandecido neles* (*magnificáre → engrandecer*, 103:1). The two passives kept, as the Latin's mirror.

36:6

*Cognóscant te → vos conheçam*: with an object, *conhecer* (the row). *Dómine → Senhor*, vocative without *ó*.

36:7

*Glorífica manum, et brácchium dextrum → Glorificai a mão, e o braço direito*: the Latin has no possessive; kept bare (D2). *Brácchium → braço*; *dexter* as an adjective → *direito*.

36:8

*Éxcita furórem → Despertai o furor*: the glossary's row for *excitáre* names this verse (79:3). *Effúnde iram → derramai a ira* (68:25, 78:6, there with *vossa*; here bare, as the Latin).

36:9

*Festína tempus → Apressai o tempo*: transitive here, so not the reflexive of 69:2b. *Meménto finis → lembrai-vos do fim*. *Ut enárrent mirabília tua → para que narrem as vossas maravilhas*: the same verb as 36:3, and the other noun.

36:10

*Devorétur → seja devorado* (the *devoráre* row). *Invéniant perditiónem → encontrem a perdição* (*inveníre → encontrar*, 20:9, 88:21; *perdítio → perdição*, 87:12).

36:11

*Cóntere caput → Quebrai a cabeça* (9:36 *Quebrai o braço*). *Príncipum inimicórum → dos príncipes inimigos*. *Dicéntium* agrees with the princes → *que dizem*. The boast mirrors the confession (see decision `praeter`).

36:12

DO prints a colon before *ut cognóscant*, kept. No flex mark in the Latin, so none here.

36:13

*Sicut ab inítio → como desde o início* (73:2, the *inítio* row). The last line of the shorter form (`216(1-13)`).

36:14

*Miserére plebi tuæ → Tende piedade do vosso povo*: the dative after *miseréri* becomes *de*. *Plebs → povo* (row 101). *Israël → Israel*.

36:15

*Civitáti … civitáti*: the Latin repeats the noun in the dative, so *da cidade … da cidade*. *Réquies → repouso* (131:8, 131:14). *Jerúsalem* stands in apposition after the first *cidade*, as in the Latin.

36:16

*Reple → Enchei* (active, as 102:5 *enche*). The second colon borrows the verb: *e da vossa glória o vosso povo* (DRB 'and thy people with thy glory'). *Verbum → palavra*.
