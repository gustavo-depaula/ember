minor

“até o dia de geração em geração” usque in diem generatiónis et generatiónis

The Latin has 'the day of generation and generation'; 'de geração em geração' turns the genitive pair into a succession idiom, producing a hybrid that neither keeps the Latin's construction nor reads cleanly.

proposedos seus anos, até o dia de geração e geração

❧ taken

A faithful, close rendering: content, tense, person and voice are preserved throughout, the Septuagintal readings (exaltásti me in petra, deduxísti me, quis requíret) are kept against the Hebrew, and the pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only points are minor: 'a facie' flattened to 'diante', 'velamentum' abstracted to 'abrigo', and the genitive 'generationis et generationis' recast as an idiom.

3

fails: sayable

“quando se angustiava o meu coração, sobre uma rocha me exaltastes”

The second colon carries two clauses and is long for one breath. The inverted 'se angustiava o meu coração' adds syllables before the turn to the main verb, and the ear has to wait for 'exaltastes' to learn what the clause is attached to.

proposedenquanto meu coração se angustiava, sobre a rocha me exaltastes.

❧ taken — order taken; enquanto, bare meu and a rocha refused (length, rule 5, = 26:6).

4

fails: native

“Guiastes-me”

An enclitic on a second-person-plural preterite at the head of the verse sounds bookish and slightly clipped ('-tes-me'). Current Brazilian liturgical usage puts the pronoun first.

proposedVós me guiastes, porque vos fizestes a minha esperança: * torre de força diante do inimigo.

❧ taken

6

fails: plain

“destes uma herança”

The indefinite 'uma' is weak and falls on the stress right after the mediant pause, so the colon opens flat. The Latin has no article, and the definite article reads as more natural and more solemn.

proposeddestes a herança aos que temem o vosso nome.

kept as an option under dedísti hereditátem timéntibus nomen tuum — the definite names a known inheritance the Latin does not name.

7

fails: native, plain

“Acrescentareis dias sobre os dias do rei”

'Acrescentar sobre' is not idiomatic: one adds something 'a' something. 'Sobre' calques 'super' and makes the reciter stumble.

proposedAcrescentareis dias aos dias do rei:

❧ taken

“os seus anos, até o dia de geração em geração”

'De geração em geração' is a fixed idiom meaning 'down the generations'. Hung on 'o dia', it becomes nonsense ('the day from generation to generation'), and a native ear hears the idiom rather than the Latin's 'day of generation and generation'. It also loses the Latin 'et'.

proposede os seus anos, até o dia de geração e geração.

❧ taken — the added e refused (the Latin has none); kept as an option.

8

fails: sayable, plain

“Permanece para sempre”

The verse opens on a subjectless verb right after the king's years, and in the second colon the ear briefly takes 'a sua misericórdia' as the subject of 'Permanece'. A named pronoun steadies it.

proposedEle permanece para sempre na presença de Deus:

❧ taken

“quem as procurará?”

The run 'pro-cu-ra-rá' after 'as' is a tongue-twister at the final cadence. 'Buscar' is the plainer faithful word for 'requiret'.

proposeda sua misericórdia e verdade, quem as buscará?

kept as an option under quis requíret? — buscar is quǽrere's; requírere → procurar in four psalms.

fails: cadence, sayable

“ao vosso nome pelos séculos dos séculos: *”

The mediant falls on 'séculos', a proparoxytone, so the tone's cadence has nowhere to land. The first colon is also too long for one breath.

proposedAssim, pelos séculos dos séculos, cantarei um salmo ao vosso nome: * para cumprir os meus votos de dia em dia.

❧ taken — order taken; cantarei refused (psalmum dícere → entoar um salmo, 17:50, 26:6b), kept as an option.

“pagar os meus votos”

'Pagar' sounds commercial in Portuguese. 'Cumprir os votos' is the native collocation and still renders 'reddam'.

proposedpara cumprir os meus votos de dia em dia.

The psalm is mostly sober and sayable, and the vós forms sit naturally in 2, 5 and 6. The weak spots are in the king section: 60:7 lets a Portuguese idiom override the Latin and confuse the sense, and 60:8 has an unclear subject and a clumsy final verb. 60:9 breaks the cadence at the mediant with 'séculos' and needs reordering.

5

minor

“serei protegido ao abrigo das vossas asas” protegar in velamento alarum tuarum

'velamentum' is a concrete covering or veil; 'abrigo' (shelter) shifts slightly toward the abstract. Also, 'protegar' can be read as future or as subjunctive ('may I be protected'); the future is defensible and matches 'inhabitabo', but it does settle the ambiguity.

proposedserei protegido sob a cobertura das vossas asas

kept as an option under in velaménto alárum tuárum — σκέπη = 35:8 tegmen → abrigo (D15); the tegmen row refuses sob a cobertura (heard as a roof or insurance). The future matches inhabitábo and the Greek future σκεπασθήσομαι, which is not a wish.

9

minor

“pelos séculos dos séculos” in saeculum saeculi

The Latin is singular ('unto the age of the age'). The plural is the usual Portuguese liturgical idiom, but it changes the number. Contrast v.5 'in saecula', which is plural and is correctly rendered 'pelos séculos'.

proposedAssim, pelo século do século, entoarei um salmo ao vosso nome

not taken — D27 settled pelos séculos dos séculos; the Latinist has asked the singular before and it is held (number is grammar, D2).

This is a faithful and close rendering. It keeps the Septuagintal readings ('dies super dies regis adjicies', 'usque in diem generationis et generationis', 'quis requiret?') and leaves the referent of 'ejus' in v.8 open, as the Latin does. The only points are two minor ones: 'velamentum' in v.5 and the number of 'in saeculum saeculi' in v.9. Every verse carries a single mediant '*' and no flex or second mediant. That matches the Latin as I know it, but I could not check it against latin.json because I was limited to the two files.

Verse by verse

2

Rule 6: the opening is 54:2's (*Exáudi, Deus, … → Escutai, Deus, …*), where 53:4 *Deus, exáudi* keeps its own order (*Deus, escutai*). *exaudíre → escutar* (D3), *inténdere → atender* with *a* (D3; 5:3, 16:1a *atendei à minha prece*), *deprecátio → prece* (D35). The two nouns change places against 16:1a because the Latin's do.

3

*fines terræ → os confins da terra* (glossary; 21:28, 47:11). *ad te clamávi → a vós clamei*, the Latin's order, so the colon ends on the oxytone *clamei*. *In petra exaltásti me* copies 26:6 *Sobre uma rocha me exaltou* (the *petra* row asks for it), indefinite as the Latin. checks: the second colon is +8 by the counter (which does not elide *-ção se‿an-*, *o‿meu*, *ro-cha‿me*); it carries four Latin members (*dum anxiarétur · cor meum · in petra · exaltásti me*) and none can go; *numa rocha* would save a syllable at the cost of 26:6's wording — accepted. The Latin verse division is DO's, not DRB's (which ends v3 with 'Thou hast conducted me').

4

*dedúcere → guiar* (D22; this verse is one of the row's places). *factus es → vos fizestes* (the *fíeri* row, *fazer-se*; 117:21, 117:28b). *quia → porque*. The Tract of 01-31 begins *Factus es spes mea, Dómine*.

5

The colon is one of the two places of *velaméntum* (with 62:8b, where Ps 62 should copy *ao abrigo das vossas asas*). The blind reader heard *tenda* as a camping tent as well as God's dwelling — the cost the *tabernáculum* row already records.

6

*Deus meus* vocative → *meu Deus*, as in every finished psalm (7:2, 21:3, 34:24). *tu* explicit → *vós*. *exaudísti → escutastes* (D3).

7

*regis* → *do rei*, lower case as the DO Latin. *annos ejus → os seus anos* — *seus* is the king's here and cannot mislead.

9

*psalmum dícere → entoar um salmo* (17:50, 26:6b; D25's family); the blind reader listed *entoarei* as a word he did not know — the D25 cost, noted, the settled verb kept. *in sǽculum sǽculi → pelos séculos dos séculos* (D27), moved to the head of the verse in v2 (decision `v9order`) so the mediant is not a proparoxytone. checks: the first colon is still long, the formula being eight syllables for five.
