In éxitu

Draft 2 — reviewed. Pray it aloud first; then decide below. Every choice you make rewrites the psalm here, so what you pray is what you chose. Nothing is final until you say so.

stylist

claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-23 · critic/v1.stylist.json · read prayed.vos.json

1

fails: cadence, sayable

“a casa de Jacó, de um povo bárbaro:”

The final word 'bárbaro' is proparoxytone, so the cadence falls on the third-last syllable and the tone has to drag the ending. On top of that, the second colon has no verb and hangs, and a listener only takes it as a parallel to 'saiu' once the phrase is over.

proposeda casa de Jacó, do meio de um povo bárbaro: (the verb is still elided, but 'do meio de' brings back the motion of 'de pópulo'. The cadence stays proparoxytone, which matches the Latin 'bárbaro'. If the ear is to win, use 'de um povo estrangeiro', though that loses the colour of 'barbarus')

kept as an option — “estrangeiro”, under de pópulo bárbaro

8

fails: native

“Que converteu a rocha”

A verse that opens on a bare 'Que' is heard at first as the interrogative 'What converted…?', since the antecedent is in the previous verse. In Portuguese recitation a relative that starts a line needs something to lean on.

proposedAquele que converteu a rocha em lagoas de água, * e o penhasco em fontes de água.

❧ taken

16

fails: sayable, native

“os que os fazem”

'os que os' is a stutter of the same monosyllable twice (article, then clitic), right before the mediant. Together with 'Façam-se semelhantes a eles' the colon also gets heavy in sibilants and hard to say in one breath.

proposedSejam semelhantes a eles aqueles que os fazem: * e todos os que confiam neles.

kept as an option — “Sejam”, under Símiles illis fiant qui fáciunt ea

21

fails: native

“Bendisse todos os que temem”

With a personal object, 'bendizer' reads more naturally with 'a' ('bendisse a todos'). The bare 'Bendisse todos' sounds clipped, and the ear takes 'todos' as the start of a new clause.

proposedBendisse a todos os que temem o Senhor, * os pequenos com os grandes.

❧ taken

22

fails: native, concrete

“O Senhor acrescente a vós: * a vós, e aos vossos filhos.”

'acrescente a vós' is not Portuguese: 'acrescentar a alguém' needs an object. It also drops the spatial image of 'super vos', the blessing laid upon them, which the Latin repeats three times.

proposedAcrescente o Senhor sobre vós: * sobre vós e sobre os vossos filhos.

kept as an option — “Acrescente o Senhor sobre vós”, under Adíciat Dóminus super vos: * super vos, et super fílios vestros.

25

fails: plain

“Não são os mortos que vos louvarão”

The cleft 'Não são… que' adds an emphatic contrast the Latin doesn't have and puts two syllables of scaffolding in front of the image. The direct order is shorter and more austere, and it keeps the Latin's 'Non mórtui' as the opening.

proposedNão vos louvarão os mortos, Senhor: * nem todos os que descem ao inferno.

❧ taken

The psalm sounds good: the Exodus section (vv. 3-7) is short and vivid and can be sung, and the idol litany keeps its hammering parallelism. The rhymes in -ão in vv. 13-15 come from the Latin futures and read as structure, not accident. The weak spots are grammatical, not in the diction: a few calqued constructions (v. 22 'acrescente a vós', v. 21 'bendisse todos') and one stutter (v. 16 'os que os').

Verse by verse

1

domus Jacob → a casa de Jacó (domus → casa). The commas keep 'de um povo bárbaro' with 'saiu' (from a foreign people).

2

Judǽa → Judeia (75:2 'na Judeia'). 'Facta est' → 'tornou-se' (a state come upon the subject, fíeri row). The second colon's verb is understood, as in the Latin. The ambiguity reader heard 'a sua' / 'o seu' as Judea's and Israel's own; that is the Latin's ejus (God is not yet named), kept by D2.

3

retrórsum → voltar para trás (row; 9:4, 43:11). Jordánis → Jordão (41:7).

4

aríes → carneiro (64:14 'carneiros das ovelhas'); collis → colina (row); agni óvium → cordeiros das ovelhas, the Latin's genitive kept.

5

'Quid est tibi' → 'Que tens tu' (the Portuguese idiom for 'what ails you'); the sea and the Jordan are singular non-divine addressees, so tu (rule 3). quia after convérsus es is 'that', so 'que voltaste' parallels 'que fugiste'.

7

A fácie → À face de (row, 96:5 'à face do Senhor'); movéri → abalar-se (45:7). The repeated 'a fácie' repeats.

8

v2: 'Aquele que' opens the verse for the Latin relative Qui (stylist; the ambiguity reader heard a bare 'Que' as 'What…?'). The antecedent is the Deus Jacob of 113:7; 'Aquele' is the pronoun Portuguese needs to hang a relative on at the head of a line (grammar, D2). stagna aquárum → lagoas de água (106:35, identical Latin). fontes aquárum → fontes de água, so the Latin's repeated aquárum repeats.

9

The familiar head 'Não a nós, Senhor, não a nós' is kept word for word from the Brazilian use (CNBB LH, Mass refrain; see liturgical-use-brazil.md), without 'ó' by the vocative rule. da glóriam is an imperative → 'dai glória' (safe: not a first-person past).

10

nequándo → para que não (row). Deus eórum → o Deus deles, since 'o seu Deus' would be heard as the nations' own god or as ours ambiguously (41:4 'Onde está o teu Deus?' is the same question).

11

autem → porém (row). The Latin copula is supplied ('está'). 'ómnia quæcúmque vóluit, fecit' → 'tudo o que quis, ele fez', the Latin order kept with the object first; 134:6 has the same Latin with Dóminus and should match.

12

simulácrum → ídolo (row, open; 134:15 is identical Latin and should copy). The copula 'são' supplied. ópera → obras (plural kept).

13

os → boca; verbs in the Latin's future (decision futures). Order 'Têm boca' puts the verb first as Portuguese does; the Latin's object-first order is an inversion Portuguese does not need (D2 order). 113:13–15 is identical Latin with 134:16–17 in part and should copy.

14

aures → ouvidos. nares → narinas (decision nares).

15

palpáre → apalpar (feel with the hands); 'in gútture suo' → 'com a sua garganta' (the Latin in of instrument; 'na garganta' would be where, not with what).

16

'ea' (the idols, neuter) → 'os'. v2: 'aqueles que os fazem' (stylist) avoids the 'os que os' stutter before the mediant; the Latin 'qui' has no article either way. confídere in → confiar em (row).

17

speráre in → esperar em (D36). The comma at the end of 113:17 and 113:18 follows the Latin.

19

Qui timent Dóminum → Os que temem o Senhor (117:4). The Latin comma between subject and verb is not reproduced, since Portuguese does not put one there.

20a

memor esse → lembrar-se de (row); proclisis 'se lembrou' after the subject (Brazilian order). benedícere God→man: the dative is made direct (row): 'nos bendisse'.

20b

dómui Israël → a casa de Israel, the dative made direct (row); 'a casa de Aarão' (117:3).

21

v2: 'Bendisse a todos' (stylist): with 'todos' the direct object takes the preposition in Portuguese (objeto direto preposicionado), so the row (the dative made direct) still holds; bare 'Bendisse todos' sounded clipped.

23

'qui fecit cælum et terram' → 'que fez o céu e a terra' (formula; 133:3 has 'ele que fez' because the verse there begins with a vocative clause; here the relative follows its noun directly).

24

'Cælum cæli' → 'O céu do céu' (67:33b); the copula 'é' supplied; autem → porém (row). 'deu-a' takes up the fronted 'a terra' with the pronoun (the Latin fronts terram; Portuguese needs the resumptive), as MS1932 'mas a terra deu-a'.

25

te → vos (God is vós); 'Senhor' in the same colon fixes the address after 113:22–23's vós to the people. inférnus → inferno (row, open; the ambiguity reader heard 'hell of the damned', the row's known cost). The Latin comma after omnes is not reproduced.

26

benedícere man→God keeps the dative: 'bendizemos ao Senhor' (row, Ps 102). 'ex hoc nunc et usque in sǽculum' → 'desde agora e para todo o sempre', identical to 112:2 (sibling psalm, same Latin) and D37.
