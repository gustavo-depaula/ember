4b

minor

“A minha alma se sustentou na sua palavra” Sustínuit ánima mea in verbo ejus

Sustinere here is the same verb just rendered 'esperei por vós' (sustínui te) in 4a: to wait on, hold out for. The reflexive 'se sustentou' reads as 'was upheld / supported itself', which shifts the sense from the soul's waiting toward being sustained. It also breaks the link the Latin draws between 'sustínui te' and 'sustínuit ánima mea'. Douay's 'relied on' shows the reading is defensible, but it could be closer.

proposedA minha alma aguardou a sua palavra

not taken — His fix is the stylist's v1 proposal, already refused: "aguardar" is exspectáre's verb (D24, D36) and it drops the Latin's "in", which the v1 Latinist asked to keep. The two runs pull opposite ways (v1: keep "in", the word as ground; v2: keep the waiting); "se sustentou na" keeps the preposition and the root, and he calls DRB's "relied on" — the sense it gives — defensible. Minor, not a major. For Gustavo: "esperou pela" (draft 1, the 4a link) and "esperou na" (v1 Latinist) are options 1 and 2 of decision inverbo.

The translation is faithful throughout. Tense, mood, and person are kept: jussive 'speret' is 'espere', future 'rédimet' is 'redimirá', and the Septuagintal 'propter legem tuam' and 'a custódia matutína' are preserved. The only point worth tightening is 4b, where 'se sustentou' blurs the waiting sense of sustinere. All pointing marks match the Latin: one mediant per verse and no flex or second mediant.

Verse by verse

1

No titulus: 'Canticum graduum' is in the LXX and MS1932 ('Cântico gradual') but not in DO's psalm file (D7; as Pss 119–123, 133). The inline '(2a)' marker is not reproduced. The vocative repeated across the mark ('Senhor: * Senhor'), as the Latin; bare, no 'ó'. exáudi vocem meam → 'escutai a minha voz' (D3; 26:7 'Escutai, Senhor, a minha voz'). The Office of the Dead says the verse alone as a versicle; it reads alone.

2b

deprecátio → prece, and 'a voz da minha prece' as 27:2, 27:6, 65:19, 85:6 (D35). Second colon 7 syllables against the Latin's 11 — the Latin's long genitive; accepted, as at 85:6. The Septuagesima Tract reads 'in oratiónem servi tui' here; the psalm's text is followed.

3

The Latin's repeated vocative kept across the mark. 'as iniquidades' with the article only (iníquitas → iniquidade, row); no 'nossas' supplied. The line is the Office of the Dead antiphon and reads alone.

4a

'Quia' → 'Porque' (default; MS1932's 'Mas' is freer). propter → 'por causa de' (row). 'sustínui te' → 'esperei por vós' (D36, God as object). 'Porque junto de vós está a …' and 129:7 'Porque junto do Senhor está a …' keep the Latin's parallel (Quia apud te … Quia apud Dóminum).

4b

Subject-first order (D2) in both colons, so that 'a minha alma … a minha alma' stands as the Latin's repetition does. The DO id '129:4b' covers biblical 4b–5; the '(5)' marker is not reproduced. speráre in → 'esperar em' (D36). The finals of 129:4a, 4b and 6 all end on 'Senhor' as the Latin ends on Dómine / Dómino (checks flag it as rhyme; accepted).

6

custódia → vigília, as 89:5 ('a vigília na noite'; the custódia row); matutína → 'da manhã' (MS1932 'Desde a vigília da manhã até à noite'). The jussive 'speret' → 'espere' (D36 esperar em). 'Israel' without article, as the psalter's usage. The Latin (with the Greek) makes the watch the time of Israel's hoping, not the Hebrew's watchmen waiting for dawn (CNBB 'mais que o vigia pela aurora') — the Latin is followed. The ambiguity reader heard "vigília" as a vigil service, or vaguely "early morning" — the same hearing as at 89:5 (custódia row); kept for the row.

7

A copula supplied in the first colon (D2); misericórdia → misericórdia (row). "copiosa" was listed unknown by the ambiguity reader; kept — it is the Latin's word and the one Brazil already prays here (circulation.md).

8

'ejus' → 'suas' (Israel's): the Portuguese hears it so. +2 syllables in the first colon (ele mesmo for ipse), accepted: the emphasis is the Latin's.
