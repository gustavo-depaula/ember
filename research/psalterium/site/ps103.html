minor

“de largos braços” spatiosum manibus

Latin image is 'hands'; 'braços' changes the concrete image (though 'braço de mar' is idiomatic Portuguese). Also illic reptilia has no finite verb: 'rastejam seres' turns the noun 'creeping things' into a verb with a generic subject.

proposedEste mar, grande e espaçoso de mãos: * ali há répteis sem número.

not taken — *espaçoso de mãos* is not Portuguese (option 1); *braços de mar* keeps a limb. *répteis* is zoological (option 2).

minor

“todos aguardam de vós” omnia a te exspectant

omnia is neuter 'all things'; 'todos' narrows to persons/animals. Same in 103:28 'omnia implebuntur' -> 'todos se encherão'.

proposedtodas as coisas aguardam de vós

not taken — *todas as coisas aguardam … que lhes deis o alimento* makes things eat; option 1 keeps the neuter.

minor

“o que eu digo” eloquium meum

eloquium is a noun ('my speech, my utterance'); the relative clause paraphrases it.

proposedSeja-lhe agradável a minha palavra

not taken — *palavra* is *verbum / sermo*'s (glossary, D16, D26); *eu* dropped instead.

A faithful, close translation of the Latin throughout: Septuagintal readings (confessionem, herodii domus dux est eorum, draco quem formasti ad illudendum ei, spatiosum manibus) are kept rather than smoothed toward the Hebrew, and no verse adds or drops content. Remaining points are minor: passive magnificari rendered reflexively, a few softened lexical choices (decor, stabunt, confirmet, possessio, manibus), and neuter omnia rendered as 'todos'. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex or second mediant).

5ambiguity

60 readings; most are the Latin's own (subject of 103:7, 9, 14, 15; 103:27 *ele*; third person 103:16, 19). Acted on: 103:1 *engrandecestes-vos* as self-aggrandizement (→ passive, with the Latinist); 103:3 *como* as simile (→ *por*, with the stylist). Recorded, kept: 103:1b *confissão* as the sacrament (the known cost, 95:6); 103:12 *Sobre elas* heard with *feras*; 103:17 *ouriços* as sea urchins; 103:23 *tarde* as afternoon (58:7); 103:29 *desfalecerão* as fainting (row). Unknown words (transporão, garça, covis, iníquos …) are the settled vocabulary.

Verse by verse

1

The frame of the psalm (103:1a = 103:35b) fills both places from one decision. *Dómine, Deus meus → Senhor, meu Deus* (formula row). The soul is *tu*, God is *vós*, both in one verse as the Latin has it. Checks: The remaining ±3–4 flags (103:1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 11, 12, 14, 14b, 15, 21, 22, 23, 24, 28, 30, 32) come from Portuguese articles and analytic verbs; none moves a cadence off oxytone/paroxytone.

2

The participles of 103:1b–2 (*amíctus … Exténdens*) and the relatives *qui* (103:2b–5, 103:10) hang on *Dómine* of 103:1 without a main verb; kept so. *Qui* → *vós que* each time: the anaphora is the Latin's. *tégere → cobrir* (row); 103:9 *operíre* also becomes *cobrir* (a different Greek verb: στεγάζω / καλύπτω) — they do not meet in one verse.

4

*urens* → *ardente*; MS1932 *abrasador*.

5

*inclináre* → *inclinar-se* (settled row, of kingdoms); DRB 'moved'. *in sǽculum sǽculi → pelos séculos dos séculos* (D27).

7

*Ab increpatióne tua → À vossa repreensão*, the formula of 17:16b and 75:7. *tonítruum → trovão* (row *in rota*).

8

The Latin's ':' after *montes* and the mediant after *campi* are both kept. *campi → campos*, as 95:11 *campi gaudébunt → os campos*; *ager* (103:11) also becomes *campo*, so two Latin words (πεδίον, ἀγρός) share one Portuguese — they are eight verses apart. *fundásti* repeats 103:5's *fundastes*.

9

*transgrédi → transpor* (row); *convérti → voltar* (row).

10

*pertransíre → passar* (38:7), kept one verb in all three places of the psalm (103:10, 20, 26).

11

*Potábunt* — in Vulgate Latin *potáre* can mean 'give to drink' (the Greek ποτιοῦσιν does), but *béstiæ* is nominative, so in the Latin the beasts drink. *ónager → asnos selvagens* (*jumento* is a donkey in Brazil, *onagro* unknown). *exspectáre → aguardar* (D39).

12

*Super ea* is neuter in the Latin (Greek αὐτά, the waters, ὕδατα neuter); *Sobre elas* refers the ear to *as águas* of 103:10b. *vocem dare → fazer ouvir a voz* (row; 17:14); the plural kept; no possessive, as the Latin (*dabunt voces*; v2 gate). *petra → rocha* (row). **v2 (ambiguity reader):** *Sobre elas* may be heard with *feras* (103:11); the Latin *ea* is equally a bare pronoun (the waters of 103:10). Kept, not glossed.

13

*rigáre → regar* (row). *satiáre / saturáre → saciar* (row; one Greek verb, χορτάζω, in 103:13 and 103:16), passive as in the Latin. **v2:** *se saciará* (stylist: the analytic passive was heavy); the Latin passive is medio-passive (D2). 103:16 takes the same form, so one Greek verb (χορτάζω) keeps one shape.

14

*fænum → feno* here (fodder for the herd; the row's split: *erva* where it flowers), *herba → erva* (row), so the two words stay apart in one verse. *servitúti hóminum* kept as *o serviço dos homens* (DRB 'the service of men').

14b

*edúcere → fazer sair* (row). The subjunctive of the second colon is announced with *que* (*e que o vinho alegre*), because *e o vinho alegre …* is first heard as 'the cheerful wine'. *lætificáre → alegrar* (row). 103:15 repeats the build.

15

Checks: the rhyme 103:14b/103:15 (*homem*) is the Latin's own repetition (*cor hóminis* twice); kept. v2 checks: 15b +5 with *fortaleça* (the Latinist's verb); accepted.

16

*que ele plantou*: the Latin's third person (God), inside a psalm that addresses him. *cedri* is feminine in Latin, *cedros* masculine; nothing hangs on it. *passer → pardal* (row, which names this verse). *nidificáre → fazer ninho*. **v2:** *As árvores do campo se saciarão* — the stylist asked a pronominal (his *Saciam-se* changes the tense; refused); the subject is put first to avoid a proclitic at the head of the verse. Checks: +6 (the Portuguese articles and the supplied *ele*); accepted.

17

*excélsus → excelso* (D43). *cervus → cervo* kept: the row allows it where the animal's context is clear, as here between the heron and the hedgehog. *herinácius* (hedgehog; χοιρογρύλλιος, the rock-badger) → *ouriços*, MS1932. The second half is verbless in the Latin; one copula is supplied (*são*), the second clause keeps the Latin's ellipsis. Checks: +6 — the supplied *são* and prepositions; accepted. **v2 (ambiguity reader):** *ouriços* may be heard as sea urchins; *ouriço* is also the land animal (MS1932), and *porco-espinho* is another animal. Kept.

19

*in témpora → para os tempos* (MS1932 adds *marcar*).

20

*Posuísti* repeats 103:9 *Pusestes*. *silva → selva* (D44), though readers have objected to it in three psalms; left for the coordinator.

21

The verse has no main verb in the Latin: the lion cubs are in apposition to the beasts of 103:20b. The gerund keeps that. *cátulus → filhote* (row; 16:12 *um filhote de leão*); *rugíre → rugir*; *rápere → arrebatar* (row); *quǽrere → buscar* (row); *esca → alimento* (the row foresees *alimento* or *comida* for food given; 103:27 has the same word, and *alimento* serves both).

22

*oríri → nascer* (row); *congregári → reunir-se* (D43); the subject pronoun *eles* supplied (D2), otherwise the sun seems to gather. *cubíle → covil*.

23

*opus → obra* (as 103:13, 24, 31); *operátio → trabalho* (a different noun of the same root; *operação* is not said of a man's labour). *usque ad vésperum → até a tarde* (58:7 *à tarde*).

24

The formula of 91:6a: *Como se engrandeceram as vossas obras, Senhor!* *implére → encher*, in 103:24 and 103:28 alike. *in sapiéntia → com sabedoria*.

26

*navis → navio* (D43).

27

*formáre → formar* (94:5). *in témpore → a seu tempo*, the Portuguese idiom for 'in due time' (Greek εὔκαιρον).

28

The two absolute ablatives stay gerunds, each with its subject *vós* (MS1932's build). *bónitas → bondade* (row). *aperíre* is not an imperative here, so rule 3 does not apply.

29

*avértere fáciem → desviar a face* (row, 12:1); the Latin has no *tuam* and the Portuguese none. *turbári → perturbar-se* (row). *auferre → tirar* (D41). *spíritum eórum … spíritum tuum* (103:30): *espírito* both times, so the play between their spirit taken and yours sent is heard; lower case, as the DO Latin and 50:13 (D44). *defícere → desfalecer* (row); *revérti → voltar*; *pulvis → pó*.

30

Lower case *espírito*, as the DO Latin prints *spíritum*. The Pentecost versicle's words (*Emítte Spíritum tuum*) are an imperative in the liturgy, a future here; the psalm's future is kept.

31

*Sit … in sǽculum → Seja … para sempre* (D23). *lætári → alegrar-se* (row).

32

*respícere → olhar para* (row). *Ele, que* opens the verse: the Latin's *Qui* hangs on *Dóminus* of 103:31b, and a verse cannot open on a bare *Que*. *eles fumegam*: the subject supplied (D2).

33

*Cantábo … psallam*: *cantar* and *entoar salmos* side by side, as 12:6b (D25). *in vita mea → na minha vida* (62:5). *quámdiu sum → enquanto eu existir* (*esse* absolute → *existir*, D39). Checks: +8 syllables — *psallam → entoarei salmos* (D25) and *sum → eu existir* (D39) each cost a word; accepted for the formulas.

34

*delectári → deleitar-se* (D44). *ego vero → eu, porém*.

35

*ita ut non sint → de modo que já não existam* (*esse* absolute → *existir*, D39; *já* as 38:14 *e já não existirei*). *peccátor / iníquus → pecador / iníquo* (row). Checks: the rhyme 103:34/103:35 (*Senhor*) — the Latin ends both on *Dómino*; kept. **v2:** *ita ut non sint → até não existirem mais* (stylist: *de modo que* was the register of an explanation); *até* keeps the result, *não … mais* the *já* of 38:14. v2 checks: +6; accepted.
