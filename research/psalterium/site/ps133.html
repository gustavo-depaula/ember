Verse by verse

1a

benedícite → bendizei (safe at the vós imperative: the past is 'bendisse'). 'Senhor' closes both cola, and the first colon of 133:1b too: the Latin does the same (Dóminum / Dómini / Dómini). 'omnes servi Dómini' is a vocative; no 'vós' or 'ó' is supplied (Matos Soares adds 'vós', the Diurnal 'ó'). servus → servo, as in the Nunc dimittis (servum tuum).

1b

The verse hangs from the vocative of 133:1a, as in the Latin. 134:2 is the same verse word for word (checked in the DO Latin): whoever translates Ps 134 copies this one. 'átrios' kept (átrium; the courts of the Temple): a word still used of churches. 'domus' → casa both times. The ambiguity reader heard 'a casa do Senhor' as the church where the hearers are — which is how the verse is prayed. The Hebrew has no second colon here and puts 'in the nights' in this verse; the Latin follows the LXX and so does the translation.

2

'In nóctibus' is plural (LXX ἐν ταῖς νυξίν; DRB 'In the nights') and stays plural; draft 2 has 'Durante as noites' for draft 1's 'Nas noites' (see the decision). First colon +4 by checks.py, accepted: nothing in it is added, and the two elisions bring it to about 17 in the mouth. Second colon −2, within range.

3

The psalm turns from the plural servants (vós) to a singular 'te': 'te', with tu-forms nowhere else needed. The ambiguity reader heard the 'te' as each hearer singly, a particular person, or the community as one — the Latin's 'te' is as open, and nothing is done about it. It also listed 'Sião' as a word a hearer may not know: a proper name, kept. 'ex Sion' → 'de Sião' (MS1932, pt-PT, Diurnal); 'desde Sião' would mark the origin more plainly but is heavier. 127:5 has the same blessing with the dative (Benedícat tibi Dóminus ex Sion) and should follow whatever is ruled here.
