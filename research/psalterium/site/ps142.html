3b

minor

“como os mortos de outrora” sicut mórtuos sǽculi

'sæculi' is a bare genitive ('of the age / of the world'); 'de outrora' reads it one way (the long-ago dead, following the LXX νεκροὺς αἰῶνος) and narrows a phrase the Latin leaves open. Defensible, but a closer rendering keeps the genitive.

proposedcomo os mortos do século

kept as an option under sicut mórtuos sǽculi — 'do século' is heard as 'of the century'; both Vulgate-family witnesses (DRB, MS1932) read 'of old'.

A faithful, close translation: tenses (perfects, the imperfect 'meditábar', the futures of 10b-12b), moods and persons are kept, the Septuagintal readings (terra recta, audítam fac, in lacum) are preserved, and no content is added or dropped. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (a single mediant each). The only point worth noting is the slightly interpretive 'mortos de outrora' for 'mórtuos sǽculi'.

4stylist

claude-opus-5-5, fresh context, read latin.json. 8 remarks: 2 taken (142:3b order, 142:8b 'por onde'), 6 refused (kept as options where they are renderings). Best 142:7a, worst 142:10b.

3b

fails: plain, sayable

“Colocou-me em lugares escuros”

'Colocou-me' is flat and office-like, and 'lugares escuros' spends four syllables on a paraphrase of 'in obscúris'; the first colon drags before it reaches the image of the dead.

proposedPôs-me nas trevas como os mortos de outrora:

not taken — collocáre → colocar (row; 'pôr' is pónere's) and 'trevas' is ténebræ's; 'nas trevas' is already option 2 of 'obscuris'.

“e o meu espírito se angustiou sobre mim, em mim se perturbou o meu coração”

The second half is long for one breath and 'se angustiou' puts the proclitic 'se' in a stiff pre-verbal slot right after 'espírito'; the ear stumbles on 'espírito se angustiou'.

proposede angustiou-se sobre mim o meu espírito, em mim se perturbou o meu coração.

❧ taken

10b

fails: plain, sayable, cadence

“por causa do vosso nome”

'por causa de' is prosaic and adds two syllables to an already long second half.

proposedpelo vosso nome, Senhor,

not taken — the psalter's formula for 'propter nomen tuum' (24:11, 30:4, 43:26, 108:21); 'pelo' would also be heard as an oath.

“vós me vivificareis, na vossa equidade”

'vivificareis' is a five-syllable Latinism that needs to be read, not prayed, and it piles on 'equidade', another bookish word; the whole colon is crowded with long, abstract forms.

proposedpelo vosso nome, Senhor, vós me dareis a vida, na vossa equidade.

kept as an option under vivificábis me — held for identity with 137:7 until the vivificáre row is ruled; the evidence is added to the row.

“me guiará à terra reta”

'à terra reta' clashes vowels ('guiará à') and 'reta' ends the half on a thin, clipped sound.

proposedO vosso espírito bom há de guiar-me à terra reta:

not taken — the Latin's plain future; the auxiliary adds a syllable to a colon already over the Latin, and 'guiará à' elides.

12b

fails: plain, native

“E fareis perecer todos”

The periphrasis 'fareis perecer' is weaker than the single Latin verb and breaks the root echo disperdes/perdes that 11b sets up with 'exterminareis'; the two verses no longer answer each other.

proposed11b: e na vossa misericórdia destruireis os meus inimigos. / 12b: E destruireis todos os que atribulam a minha alma:

not taken — D24 settled pérdere → fazer perecer, apart from dispérdere → exterminar (the pérdere row names 142:12b); the Latin's two verbs are two (ἐξολεθρεύσεις / ἀπολεῖς), and merging them into 'destruir' loses one; 'destruir' also fails rule 3 at the imperative elsewhere.

A sober, faithful psalm that mostly sounds like prayer: the opening pleas and the short verses 7a-8a are clean and breathe well. It grows heavy toward the end, where Latinate words ('vivificareis', 'equidade', 'fareis perecer') and long second halves make the final petitions harder to say than to read.

worst line142:10b

best line142:7a

3b

minor

“como os mortos de outrora” sicut mórtuos sǽculi

saeculi (LXX aiōnos) is 'of the age / of long ago', and can carry the sense of those dead for ever; 'de outrora' narrows it to past time only. Defensible, but 'de há muito' or 'do século' keeps more of the genitive's ambiguity.

proposedcomo os mortos de há muito

kept as an option under sicut mórtuos sǽculi — both runs minor and asking different fixes ('do século', 'de há muito'); 'de há muito' narrows the same way (to time past) and is European in ear. For Gustavo.

9

minor

“em vós me refugiei” ad te confúgi

ad te with confugere is motion toward: 'I have fled to you'; 'em vós' turns it into rest in a place of refuge.

proposeda vós me acolhi

kept as an option under ad te confúgi — 'acolher' is suscípere's word; 'junto de vós me refugiei' (MS1932) is option 1 for whoever wants the direction back. The colon is the Thursday Lauds antiphon and reads alone either way.

A close and faithful rendering: tenses (meditatus sum / meditabar), the future indicatives of 10b-12b, and the literal 'e serei semelhante' in 7b are all kept, and the pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. Only three minor points of nuance (conspectu, saeculi, ad te confugi), none of which changes the sense.

Verse by verse

1

The opening formula as 101:2 'Senhor, escutai a minha oração' (D3) and 53:4/83:9 'dai ouvidos' (D4). 'in veritáte tua' → 'na vossa verdade' (véritas row, 53:7), not the Hebrew-based 'pela vossa fidelidade'. The Latin's colon mark after 'meam' is a comma of sense, not a pointing mark; only the asterisk is reproduced.

2

'E não entreis em juízo com o vosso servo' is word for word the wording Brazilian Catholics know (Ave Maria Bible, fetched — circulation.md); the Latin's 'Et' kept. The first colon is also said alone as a versicle (Epi2-3 responsory).

3a

'humiliávit in terra vitam meam' follows 7:6 'in terra vitam meam' → 'na terra a minha vida' (the same three words, the same order); humiliáre → humilhar (row). MS1932 'até ao chão' follows the Greek εἰς γῆν.

3b

collocáre → colocar (row, 22:1). 'anxiári' → 'angustiar-se' (row, 60:3); 'super me' → 'sobre mim' kept, as MS1932. 'in me' → 'em mim' (D42). The Latin's '(4)' marker not reproduced.

5

'Memor fui' → 'Lembrei-me' (memor esse row; 76:12, 118:52, 118:55); 'dias antigos' as 76:6; 'meditei em todas as vossas obras' = 76:13's words; 'meditábar' imperfect → 'eu meditava' (118:47 'meditava'), the pronoun supplied so the imperfect is not heard as third person.

6

expándere → estender (43:21, 87:10). 87:10 has 'estendi para vós as minhas mãos' because its Latin puts 'ad te' first; here the Latin order is kept.

7a

= 101:3b and 68:18 'escutai-me depressa' (velóciter row); 'defécit spíritus meus' = 76:4 'desfaleceu o meu espírito' (defícere row allows things as subject).

7b

'Non avértas fáciem tuam a me' = 26:9, 101:3 'Não desvieis de mim a vossa face'; 'descendéntibus in lacum' = 27:1, 29:4, 87:5 'os que descem à cova'.

8a

'Audítam fac' → 'Fazei-me ouvir' (65:8 'fazei ouvir'); mane → 'de manhã' (row); 'in te sperávi' → 'em vós esperei' (7:2, 30:15).

8b

'Notam fac' → 'fazer conhecer' (row, 15:11, 89:12); 'Ad te levávi ánimam meam' → 'a vós levantei a minha alma' (24:1, 85:3 formula).

9

'Éripe me de inimícis meis' → 'Arrancai-me dos meus inimigos' (58:2 exactly; erípere row: a source named). 'doce me fácere voluntátem tuam' → 'ensinai-me a fazer a vossa vontade' — what Brazilians know (Ave Maria: 'Ensinai-me a fazer vossa vontade, pois sois o meu Deus', fetched); the article kept (rule 5). 'quia Deus meus es tu' → 'porque vós sois o meu Deus' (15:1, 30:15, 117:28 formula). The Latin's '(10a)' not reproduced. The first colon is the Thursday Lauds antiphon; the whole verse the Offertory of Monday in Holy Week — each half reads alone.

10b

dedúcere → guiar (D22). 'propter nomen tuum' → 'por causa do vosso nome' (24:11, 30:4). 'in æquitáte tua' → 'na vossa equidade' (118:40), the Latin's comma kept before it. The subject 'vós' supplied, as 137:7.

11b

edúcere + de → 'tirar de' (row; 29:4, 30:5 'Vós me tirareis deste laço'); tribulátio → tribulação. dispérdere → exterminar (row, which names this verse).

12b

pérdere → fazer perecer (D24; the row names 142:12b and keeps it apart from dispérdere in the verse before). tribuláre → atribular (row). 'ego servus tuus sum' → 'eu sou o vosso servo' (118:125).
