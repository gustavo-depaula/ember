4

minor

“Que os povos vos deem graças, Deus: * que todos os povos vos deem graças.” Confiteántur tibi pópuli, Deus: * confiteántur tibi pópuli omnes.

In the psalter's Latin, confiteri tibi means to acknowledge or praise God publicly (confession of praise), and it is broader than thanksgiving. 'Deem graças' narrows it to gratitude. The Latin also has no motive for thanks in this verse, since the reason comes only in 66:5, where it is God's judging. 'Louvem' keeps the Latin's breadth better.

proposedQue os povos vos louvem, Deus: * que todos os povos vos louvem.

kept as an option under Confiteántur tibi pópuli, Deus: confiteántur tibi pópuli omnes — D5 settled: louvar is laudáre's (they meet in 34:18, 108:30).

6

minor

“Que os povos vos deem graças, Deus, que todos os povos vos deem graças” Confiteántur tibi pópuli, Deus, confiteántur tibi pópuli omnes

Same narrowing of confiteri as in 66:4. Whatever rendering is chosen, it should be the same in both verses.

proposedQue os povos vos louvem, Deus, que todos os povos vos louvem: * a terra deu o seu fruto.

kept as an option under Confiteántur tibi pópuli, Deus: confiteántur tibi pópuli omnes — D5, as 66:4.

The translation is faithful and close. Tenses (dedit rendered as 'deu', the present-tense judicas and dirigis), the jussive subjunctives, the image 'ilumine o seu rosto sobre nós', and 'salutáre tuum' rendered as 'a vossa salvação' are all correct. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (a single * each). The only point worth raising is the rendering of confiteantur as 'deem graças', which is defensible but narrower than the Latin's sense of acknowledging or praising.

4

fails: native

“vos deem graças, Deus: *”

A bare vocative 'Deus' dropped after the verb, with only a comma, sounds clipped in Portuguese. The mouth expects 'ó Deus' in direct address, and here the mediant arrives on a monosyllable the reciter has no run-up to.

proposedQue os povos vos deem graças, ó Deus: * que todos os povos vos deem graças.

kept as an option under pópuli, Deus — the Deus (vocative) row: bare unless the Latin has o.

6

fails: native

“vos deem graças, Deus, que todos”

Same bare vocative as 66:4. Mid-colon it's worse: 'graças, Deus, que' reads as a list, not as an address.

proposedQue os povos vos deem graças, ó Deus, que todos os povos vos deem graças: * a terra deu o seu fruto.

kept as an option under pópuli, Deus — the row, as 66:4; the two verses must stay identical.

7b

fails: native, sayable

“Bendiga-nos Deus, o nosso Deus, bendiga-nos Deus”

Starting the verse with the enclitic 'Bendiga-nos' is correct by the grammar books, but it sounds like a translation, and the hyphenated '-nos Deus' twice in one breath is heavy. The same verb is already proclitic in 66:2 ('e nos bendiga'), so this also breaks the echo inside the psalm. The Latin's triple 'Deus' and the repeated verb are kept.

proposedDeus nos bendiga, o nosso Deus, Deus nos bendiga: *

❧ taken

“e o temam todos os confins da terra”

'e o temam' puts a bare clitic right after the conjunction. The ear stumbles on 'e o' (a hiatus that blurs into one vowel) and misses the 'que' that a jussive subjunctive wants in Brazilian speech.

proposede que o temam todos os confins da terra.

❧ taken

The psalm sounds plain, steady and prayable: the short jussive cola and the refrain of 66:4/66:6 carry well, and nothing needs a footnote. The only roughness is in the forms of address and the clitics: the bare vocative 'Deus' and the enclitic opening of the last verse, which sound translated rather than native.

best line66:3

Verse by verse

2

*miseréri* → *tenha piedade de* (the row); the repetition of *misereátur nostri* at both ends of the verse is kept word for word. The subject *Deus* first, as the Latin. The first colon is a Lauds antiphon and reads alone.

3

The psalm turns from *suum* (66:2) to *tuam / tuum* (66:3) without warning; the Latin does, and so does the Greek — kept, not smoothed. *cognoscámus* is first person plural in the Latin (the Greek and Hebrew have an infinitive; DM1962's passive *Sejam conhecidos* is from the Hebrew) — *conheçamos*. *salutáre* → *salvação* (D6). v2: the ambiguity reader heard the second colon three ways (a second object of *conheçamos*; a verbless wish; the nations as knowers) — the Latin is as open, and the likeliest hearing is the right one; kept.

5

*júdicas … dírigis* are presents in the Latin (the Greek has futures, κρινεῖς … ὁδηγήσεις) — kept present. *in æquitáte* → *com equidade*, as 9:8b *julgará o mundo com equidade* (the ǽquitas row; *equidade* was unknown to blind readers before and is kept). *dirígere* → *dirigir* (row; 7:10 *dirigireis o justo*); the indicative *dirigis* is untouched by the imperative ban. *Alegrem-se e exultem*: the Latin's order, the reverse of 34:27 = 39:17 *Exultem e alegrem-se*, where the Latin is reversed too. *gentes in terra dírigis* → *dirigis as nações na terra*: the verb moved up, natural order (D2); *na terra* as in 66:3.

6

The first colon is 66:4 entire, as in the Latin. *terra dedit fructum suum* → *a terra deu o seu fruto* (1:3a *dará o seu fruto*); the perfect kept. v2: the ambiguity reader heard *a terra deu o seu fruto* as a reason for the thanks or as a separate statement — the Latin joins it with no conjunction; kept.

7b

*Deus, Deus noster* → *Deus, o nosso Deus* (the apposition takes the article, as possessives do). Both cola read alone (the blessing, the responsory). *métuere* (only here) → *temer* by D15's test (φοβέομαι, as *timére*).
