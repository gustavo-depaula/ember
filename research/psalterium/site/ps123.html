3b

minor

“talvez a água nos teria engolido” fórsitan aqua absorbuísset nos

The Latin uses two different verbs in the parallel pair: 'deglutíssent' (2b, swallowed down) and 'absorbuísset' (3b, engulfed/sucked under, the proper word for water). The translation renders both as 'engolido', flattening a distinction the Latin keeps and weakening the water image.

proposedtalvez a água nos teria absorvido

❧ taken — the distinction taken, with the stylist's 'tragado' rather than 'absorvido' (a sponge's word); 'absorvido' kept as an option

“Quando o furor deles se irou contra nós” Cum irascerétur furor eórum in nos

Imperfect subjunctive describes an ongoing rage ('was raging'); the punctual 'se irou' makes it a single outburst. Defensible, but the durative sense is closer.

proposedQuando o furor deles se irava contra nós

❧ taken — taken, and 123:2b 'se levantavam' moved with it to keep the two cum-clauses parallel

5

minor

“A nossa alma passou a torrente: * talvez a nossa alma teria passado” Torréntem pertransívit ánima nostra: * fórsitan pertransísset

'pertransire' is 'to pass right through, cross over'; 'passar' is weaker and can read as 'go past'. 'Atravessar' keeps the per- sense.

proposedA nossa alma atravessou a torrente: * talvez a nossa alma teria atravessado a água insuportável.

❧ taken

The translation is faithful throughout: no content is added or lost, the counterfactual structure, persons, voices and the concrete images (teeth, sparrow, snare of the hunters) are all preserved, and the pointing marks match the Latin (a single mediant in every verse, no flex or second mediant). The only points are minor: the collapse of deglutire/absorbere into one verb, the aspect of 'irascerétur', and 'passar' for 'pertransire'.

2b

fails: native

“quando os homens”

Starts lowercase, while the parallel 123:3b starts with a capital 'Quando'. Latin has 'Cum' capitalized in both. The two matching verses look inconsistent on the page.

proposedQuando os homens se levantaram contra nós, * talvez nos teriam engolido vivos:

❧ taken

“talvez nos teriam engolido vivos”

'Talvez' followed by the conditional is spoken usage, and a trained ear notices it as slightly off. It is tolerable, and the Latin 'forte' has to stay, but the ear catches on it. The fix is not to switch to the subjunctive ('tivessem'), which would change the sense from counterfactual to uncertain past. So I keep the construction and only flag it. Keep 'engolido' here for 'deglutissent'; see 123:3b for the other verb.

proposedtalvez nos teriam engolido vivos: (keep; flagged only)

not taken — no change proposed; the subjunctive would make the counterfactual a real possibility

3b

fails: no rhyme, concrete

“talvez a água nos teria engolido”

'Engolido' already translates 'deglutissent' in 123:2b, and here it also translates 'absorbuisset'. So the two verses end in the same word ('engolido vivos' / 'engolido'), which creates an echo the Latin does not have and loses the change of image from mouth to flood. 'Tragar' is a plain, concrete verb for water swallowing something up.

proposedtalvez a água nos teria tragado.

❧ taken

“o furor deles se irou”

'Se irou' is stiff in the mouth, but it keeps the Latin's 'furor... irasceretur'. The word order is fine. No change proposed. It is noted only because it is the most bookish phrase in the psalm.

proposedQuando o furor deles se irou contra nós, (keep)

not taken — the stylist proposed no change; the verb became 'se irava' on the Latinist's remark, the word kept

5

fails: sayable

“talvez a nossa alma teria passado a água insuportável”

This colon is about 18 syllables, the longest in the psalm, and it is hard to sing in one breath on the reciting tone. The article in 'a nossa alma', together with 'a água', piles up open a-vowels ('a nossa alma... passado a água'). Brazilian Portuguese can drop the article before the possessive. Doing so in both cola shortens the line and removes the vowel collision, while keeping the repeated 'nossa alma' and 'passou / teria passado'.

proposedNossa alma passou a torrente: * talvez nossa alma teria passado a água insuportável.

not taken — rule 5 keeps the article before possessives; the Latin colon is as long; the vowel run is eased by 'atravessou / atravessado' in draft 2

The psalm is plain, quick and faithful. It keeps its repetitions ('se o Senhor não estivesse conosco', 'nossa alma') and ends on a strong, familiar close. The main weaknesses are in the middle: 'engolido' is used for two different Latin verbs, and 123:5 is long and full of a-sounds. The deliverance verses (6-8) read well.

claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-23 · critic/v2.latinist.json · read prayed.vos.json

Nothing to correct in any verse.

The translation is adequate throughout: the pluperfect subjunctives (deglutissent, absorbuisset, pertransisset) are rendered as unreal past conditionals with 'talvez' for forte/forsitan, the imperfect subjunctive cum-clauses are kept as past-durative 'quando' clauses, and the concrete images (torrent, teeth, sparrow, broken snare) are preserved without explanation. 'Erat in nobis' as 'estivesse conosco' is a legitimate rendering of the Latin idiom, and 'in captionem' as 'como presa' keeps the sense. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex or second mediant).

Verse by verse

1

No titulus: DO's Latin has none ('Canticum graduum' is omitted in every Gradual Psalm), and D7 keeps tituli out; Ps 133 has none, and this psalm follows it. The Latin's shape is kept: the protasis, the aside 'diga agora Israel' (glossary row 'dicat nunc Israël' → 'diga agora Israel', as 117:2), the protasis again, and the apodosis only in the next verse. 'Senhor' is not a vocative here. Address: the psalm speaks of God in the third person throughout; there is no 'vós' in it.

2b

The verse opens with a capital, as the Latin's 'Cum' does (draft 1 had a lower case; the stylist pointed at the mismatch with 123:3b). exsúrgere in → levantar-se contra (glossary row exsúrgere). The imperfect subjunctive after 'cum' → 'se levantavam', parallel with 'se irava' in 123:3b (decision 'irasceretur'). 'vivos' at the end: the Latin puts it before the verb, Portuguese idiom after ('engolir vivo'); order yields (D2). Length flags accepted: see the checks steps.

3b

'aqua' singular → 'a água'; the flood comes without introduction, as in the Latin (the ambiguity reader noticed it; nothing is added). The two apodoses ('talvez nos teriam engolido vivos' / 'talvez a água nos teria tragado') are built alike, with two verbs for the Latin's two (draft 2).

5

The first colon is a fact (pertransívit, perfect indicative), the second unreal (pertransísset): the Portuguese keeps the change of mood. The Latin's object-first order ('Torréntem pertransívit') yields to Portuguese order (D2). 'ánima nostra' twice → 'a nossa alma' twice (glossary 'ánima mea' → 'a minha alma'); the repetition is the Latin's. torrens → torrente (glossary). The stylist (v1) asked to drop the articles before 'nossa alma' to shorten the long second colon: refused, rule 5 keeps the article before possessives; the Latin colon is as long (20 syllables by checks.py).

6

'Benedíctus Dóminus' verbless → 'Bendito o Senhor' (27:6; the row 'Benedíctus Deus' keeps 'seja' only for 65:20, 67:36). 'dedit … in' → 'entregou' (the 'dare in oppróbrium' row: dare as a giving-over). 'déntibus eórum' → 'aos dentes deles', not 'aos seus dentes', which could be heard as the Lord's. The ambiguity reader could hear 'presa aos dentes' as 'caught in their teeth' as well as 'prey'; both are the image, and the likely hearing was prey. Kept.

7a

passer → pardal (glossary; this verse is named in the row). erípere with a source named ('de láqueo') → arrancar (glossary row erípere). láqueus → laço (glossary). The passive 'erépta est' is kept (MS1932 turns it active, 'escapou'). venántium → dos caçadores. The verse is also a Mass verse (missa Sancti 09-26n) and, with 7b–8, the Alleluia/Gradual verse of the Holy Innocents and of Common C3a ('Láqueus contrítus est … qui fecit cœlum et terram') — grep in content/do.

7b

contérere → quebrar (glossary). 'O laço foi quebrado' keeps the Latin's passive, parallel with 'fomos livrados'.

8

'que fez o céu e a terra' is the glossary formula (113:23, 120:2, 133:3, 145:6); here the relative follows 'Senhor' directly, so no 'ele' is needed (133:3 needed one because of Sião). The whole verse matches the versicle already said in Brazil (see decision 'adjutorium').
