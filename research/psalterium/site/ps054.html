4

minor

“voltaram iniquidades contra mim” declinavérunt in me iniquitátes

The Portuguese rightly keeps the Latin's ambiguity: 'iniquitates' can be the subject ('iniquities bent upon me') or the object ('they turned iniquities upon me', as in the LXX ἐξέκλιναν ἐπ' ἐμὲ ἀνομίαν). But 'voltaram' most readily means 'returned', which is a sense the Latin does not have. 'declinare in' means to bend or turn aside onto, not to come back.

proposedPorque declinaram iniquidades sobre mim

kept as an option under declinavérunt in me iniquitátes — The fault is taken (the stylist's *as iniquidades se voltaram contra mim*); his cognate is heard first as "declined, refused" in Portuguese. Kept as an option.

10

minor

“a contenda” contradictiónem

'contradictio' means gainsaying or speaking against (LXX ἀντιλογίαν). 'contenda' (strife) is broader and less exact.

proposedporque vi a iniquidade e a contradição na cidade.

not taken — The glossary row (17:44, 30:21b, 54:10 named): the blind reader of Ps 17 heard *contradições* as inconsistencies. *contradição* is option 2.

15

minor

“andávamos” ambulávimus

The Latin has the perfect here, set against the imperfect 'capiebas' in the first half-verse. The Portuguese makes both imperfect and loses the change of tense.

proposedna casa de Deus andamos em concórdia.

not taken — *andamos* is heard as present ("we walk"), saying the friendship stands — the opposite of the verse. Tense is grammar (D2). Option 2.

20b

minor

“para retribuir” in retribuéndo

'in' + ablative gerund means 'in the act of repaying' (LXX ἐν τῷ ἀποδιδόναι). It does not express purpose, so 'para' turns it into a purpose clause.

proposedestendeu a sua mão ao retribuir.

❧ taken

The translation is faithful and close throughout. It keeps the Septuagintal readings (exercitatio, pusillanimitas, 'super oleum', 'non dimidiabunt'), the ejus/illius distinction in 54:21, and the Latin's ambiguities, such as 'inter multos erant mecum'. The faults are minor: one misleading verb in 54:4, one lexical choice in 54:10, and one small tense or construction slip each in 54:15 and 54:20b. Every verse has one mediant, matching the Latin, and there are no flexes on either side.

3

fails: native

“Estou entristecido no meu exercício”

'exercício' in current Brazilian ears means physical exercise or a school task; the participle 'estou entristecido' is heavy for the opening of a lament. The word is faithful but the phrase sounds translated.

proposedEntristeci-me em meu exercício: * e estou perturbado pela voz do inimigo, e pela tribulação do pecador.

kept as an option under Contristátus sum … conturbátus sum … Cor meum conturbátum est — the three perfect passives say one state (as 6:3–4) and must go alike; *Entristeci-me* alone would break the Latin's parallel with *conturbátus sum*. The event reading is option 2. *exercício* kept (decision `exercitatio`).

4

fails: plain, native

“Porque voltaram iniquidades contra mim”

Without the pronoun, 'voltaram' first reads as 'came back, returned', and the ear waits for a place. The bare plural subject after the verb also sounds foreign.

proposedPorque as iniquidades se voltaram contra mim: * e com ira me importunavam.

❧ taken

8

fails: native

“Eis que fui para longe, fugindo”

'fui para longe, fugindo' is flat and a little childish. The trailing gerund also puts a weak word before the mark.

proposedEis que me afastei, fugindo: * e permaneci na solidão.

kept as an option under Ecce, elongávi fúgiens — elongáre keeps the Latin's *longe* (glossary row); *afastar-se* is discédere's.

11

fails: sayable, native

“a iniquidade a cercará sobre os muros dela”

The run 'iniquidade a cercará' makes the article and the clitic collide (a... a), and 'cercará sobre os muros' is a stumble: you surround something along or upon its walls, not simply 'on' them. Then 'dela' comes back in the next colon and again in 54:12, so the passage keeps clicking on -dela.

proposedDe dia e de noite a iniquidade a rodeará por sobre os seus muros: * e no meio dela há fadiga, e injustiça.

❧ taken — *por sobre* and *os seus muros* taken; *rodear* refused (circuíre's; circumdáre → cercar); the Latin's order (subject last) removes the a … a.

12

fails: native

“E não desapareceram das praças dela”

'desapareceram' is long and prosaic for 'defecit'. It is also a third '-dela' in a row (see 54:11).

proposedE não se apartaram de suas praças * a usura, e o engano.

kept as an option under Et non defécit de platéis ejus — *das suas praças* taken; *apartar-se* is declináre a's (glossary), so the verb stays and his is option 2.

13b

fails: native, plain

'falar grandezas' is not idiomatic Portuguese. It sounds like a calque, and the ear hears 'greatnesses' as praise.

proposedE se aquele que me odiava tivesse dito contra mim grandes coisas, * talvez eu me tivesse escondido dele.

not taken — D39 (magna loqui → falar grandezas), settled.

In today's Portuguese 'conhecido' means a mere acquaintance, which is the opposite of the intimacy the verse laments.

kept as an option under et notus meus — *notus* is only "known"; the closeness is said by *de uma só alma comigo* and *meu guia*; 30:12's word.

15

fails: sayable

“Que juntamente comigo tomavas”

The verse opens on a bare relative 'Que', which the ear can take as a conjunction. 'juntamente comigo' is a clumsy four-syllable adverb before the verb.

proposedTu, que comigo juntamente tomavas doces manjares: * na casa de Deus andávamos em concórdia.

kept as an option under Qui simul mecum — the Latin runs on from 54:14's vocative with a bare relative; after 54:14's colon it is heard as one. *Tu* is not in the Latin.

fails: sayable

“ele, que é antes dos séculos”

'que é antes' sounds unfinished in Portuguese because 'ser antes' wants a complement. The comma after 'ele' also makes a hiccup.

proposedDeus escutará, e os humilhará, * ele que existe antes dos séculos.

kept as an option under qui est ante sǽcula — D39 keeps *existir* for absolute esse; *est ante* has its complement. MS1932's *existe* is option 3.

fails: native

“Pois não há para eles mudança”

Splitting 'há ... mudança' with 'para eles' is stiff.

proposedPois para eles não há mudança, e não temeram a Deus: * estendeu a sua mão para retribuir.

23

fails: native, sayable

“para sempre não deixará vacilar o justo”

Putting 'para sempre' in front of the negation is unnatural and makes the reader stumble at the start of the colon.

proposednão deixará o justo para sempre vacilar.

kept as an option under non dabit in ætérnum fluctuatiónem justo — his order tips the colon to "not waver for ever" (but for a while); the draft keeps the Latin's both, and is 29:7's build.

24b

fails: native

“Os homens de sangue e enganadores”

The pairing is lopsided: a prepositional phrase ('de sangue') is set beside an adjective-noun ('enganadores'), and the ear hears 'men of blood-and-deceivers'.

proposedOs homens de sangue e os enganadores não chegarão à metade dos seus dias: * mas eu esperarei em vós, Senhor.

kept as an option under Viri sánguinum et dolósi — the second article picks two groups where the Latin leaves *dolósi* open; the glossary foresaw the draft's wording (D29).

The psalm mostly moves with a plain, grave rhythm, and the terror and dove passage (54:5-7) and the curse (54:16) are strong and sayable. The weak stretches are the city lament (54:11-12), where clitics and the repeated '-dela' clog the line, and the betrayal verses (54:13b-15), where a few calques ('grandezas', 'conhecido', 'uma só alma') sound translated rather than prayed.

minor

“Precipitai-os, Senhor, dividi-lhes as línguas” Præcípita, Dómine, dívide linguas eórum

The Latin gives præcipita no object of its own. 'eórum' belongs to linguas, so it can be read with both verbs ('cast down and divide their tongues'). Adding '-os' settles that question and supplies an object the Latin leaves out.

proposedPrecipitai, Senhor, dividi as suas línguas

not taken — bare *Precipitai* is heard as "hasten"; and bare *dividi* is banned (rule 3 / D13: also "I divided"). Both are options.

'comigo' spells out who the unanimity is shared with, which the adjective leaves implicit. It adds a small gloss.

proposedhomem unânime

not taken — *unânime* is said of groups in Portuguese; bare *de uma só alma* was heard as "single-souled" by two v1 readers. ἰσόψυχε is equal to someone. Options 2 and 3.

minor

“na casa de Deus andávamos em concórdia” in domo Dei ambulávimus cum consénsu

ambulávimus is perfect, and the translation turns it into an imperfect. Assimilating it to the imperfect capiébas in the first half is defensible, but it is not the Latin tense.

proposedna casa de Deus andamos em concórdia

not taken — *andamos* is heard as present ("we walk"), saying the friendship stands — the opposite of the verse. Tense is grammar (D2). Option 2.

minor

“para sempre não deixará vacilar o justo” non dabit in ætérnum fluctuatiónem justo

The Latin says 'will not give wavering to the just man', with a noun object and a dative. The translation turns this into a causative verb phrase. The sense is kept but the construction and the noun are lost.

proposednão dará para sempre ao justo a vacilação

not taken — dare + noun as "allow" is grammar (the glossary row dare + inf. → deixar); *vacilação* is heavy and not sayable; *vacilar* keeps the root. Option.

This is a faithful and careful rendering. It keeps the Latin's Septuagintal readings (exercitatióne, circúmdabit as a future, the singular cor illíus, non dimidiábunt) and its tenses, with no critical or major faults. The remaining points are minor: an object added to præcípita, a small gloss on unánimis, one perfect turned into an imperfect, and fluctuatiónem turned into a verb. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (a single mediant each).
