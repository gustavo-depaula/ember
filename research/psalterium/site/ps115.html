The audit trail

1draft

Psalm-level draft from parallels (Latin, LXX, DRB, MS1932, DM1962). Shared vocabulary with Ps 114 (in progress): *humilhar*, *invocar o nome do Senhor*, *ó Senhor*. Liturgical use in Brazil fetched and recorded in `liturgical-use-brazil.md`: the CNBB wordings are from the Hebrew; the Brazilian Tridentine Ordo's *Que retribuirei ao Senhor … Tomarei o cálice da salvação e invocarei o nome do Senhor* is the one Latin-family wording in circulation and 115:3a / 115:4 keep it. Hard readings: *in excéssu meo*, the doubled *retribúere*, *hóstia*, *quia* in 7a, and the *coram / in conspéctu* split in the repeated vow formula.

2checks

Hard checks pass. 3

fails: plain

“por tudo o que ele me retribuiu?”

The named subject 'ele' adds a weak syllable just before the clitic, and the line thickens into 'o que ele me'. The subject is already clear from 'ao Senhor'.

proposedQue retribuirei ao Senhor, * por tudo o que me retribuiu?

❧ taken

5

fails: native

“é preciosa à vista do Senhor a morte dos seus santos.”

Opening the second colon with the supplied copula 'é' sounds flat and a little like a gloss. Portuguese puts the weight at the front: first the place of judgment, then the predicate. That is also nearer the Latin's word order, which starts with pretiosa.

proposedPagarei os meus votos ao Senhor diante de todo o seu povo: * à vista do Senhor é preciosa a morte dos seus santos.

kept as an option under pretiósa in conspéctu Dómini mors sanctórum ejus — the Latin's head word is *pretiósa*; the versicle said alone would lose it

7b

fails: native, plain

“a vós sacrificarei uma vítima de louvor”

Today 'vítima' mainly means the victim of a crime or an accident, so 'vítima de louvor' sounds odd, almost like 'victim of praise'. The traditional Portuguese liturgical word for hostia is 'hóstia' (as in 'esta hóstia pura, santa e imaculada'). It keeps the Latin image and the sacrificial sense without explaining anything.

proposedRompestes as minhas cadeias: ‡ a vós sacrificarei uma hóstia de louvor, * e invocarei o nome do Senhor.

❧ taken

A sober, plain psalm that says well. The short cola (1, 2, 4) have clean oxytone and paroxytone cadences and read like native prayer. The long first cola of 5 and 9 are long only because the Latin is, and they still breathe. The one real stumble is 'vítima de louvor'. Everything else needs only small adjustments of order and weight.

Verse by verse

1

*ego autem → mas eu* (the *ego autem* row). *humiliáre → humilhar* as 114:6, the psalm this one continues (MS1932 prints Ps 115 as *Continuação do anterior*).

2

*mendax* is an adjective with the copula understood → *é mentiroso*, supplied (D2). *Todo homem* without the article (Brazilian use; CNBB *Todo homem é mentiroso*).

3

*retríbuam* (deliberative subjunctive) → future, as DRB and the Ordo; *pro ómnibus* → *por tudo*, number as grammar. The *retribúere* row: the bare verb is *retribuir* (it names 115:3).

4

Second colon = 115:7b's third colon word for word, as in the Latin (rule 6). *invocáre → invocar* (the row; 114:3b *invoquei o nome do Senhor*).

5

The inline marker (6) is not reproduced. The verse ends with a period where the Latin has a colon (the Latin runs on into 7a; a Portuguese colon at a verse end reads as unfinished).

7a

The repetition *ego servus tuus … ego servus tuus* kept, with the copula supplied both times. *ancílla → serva* and the article, as 85:16 *o filho da vossa serva*. The *servo / serva* echo at mediant and final is the Latin's own (*tuus / tuæ*, *servus / ancíllæ*).

7b

*dirúmpere → romper* (2:3 *Rompamos as cadeias deles*, 106:14 *rompeu as suas cadeias*); *víncula → cadeias* (the row, which names this verse). *sacrificáre → sacrificar*, *tibi → a vós* first as in the Latin (53:8 *sacrificarei a vós*). *laus → louvor*. v2 *hóstia* (decision `hostia`). **Accepted rhyme** *louvor / Senhor* at the second mediant and the final: the Latin has no such echo (*laudis / invocábo*), but both words are fixed — *louvor* by the *laus* row and the final colon by 115:4, which it must repeat word for word; no order of the middle colon removes it without a paraphrase.

9

The inline marker (10) is not reproduced. *átrium → átrio*, *nos átrios da casa do Senhor* as 133:1b / 91:14 (*nos átrios da casa do nosso Deus*). Jerusalem, a single addressee who is not God, is *tu* (rule 3): *no meio de ti* (MS1932 the same). The ambiguity reader listed *átrios* as unknown (the fourth blind reader to do so; the row keeps it, the Latin's word) and heard *no meio de ti* rightly as said to the city.
