6

minor

“entre aclamações” in júbilo

Latin has a singular abstract-collective noun with 'in' (in jubilation, amid the shout of joy); the plural 'aclamações' and 'entre' shift number and slightly concretize it. Defensible, but closer is available.

proposedSubiu Deus com júbilo: * e o Senhor, à voz da trombeta.

kept as an option under in júbilo — *júbilo* breaks the root pair with 46:2 *aclamai* (jubiláre family → aclamar, proposed row) and takes the word the open gáudium row needs; the plural is number, which is grammar (D27), and the reader called it defensible.

A faithful, close rendering: every clause is present, tenses (subjecit, elegit, ascendit perfect; regnabit future; sedet present; congregati/elevati sunt perfect passive) and persons are kept, and the Septuagintal 'speciem Jacob' and 'dii fortes terrae' are preserved rather than smoothed. The only point worth touching is the number of 'in júbilo' in 46:6. Pointing marks match the Latin (a single mediant in each verse, no flex).

4

fails: native

“Sujeitou os povos a nós”

Putting the indirect object last as 'a nós' sounds stiff, like a translation. The mediant also lands on a bare stressed monosyllable after a weak 'a'. 'Sujeitou-nos' doesn't work either, because it would say that we were the ones subjected.

proposedA nós sujeitou os povos

❧ taken — Taken as *Sujeitou a nós os povos* — the dative before the object, the Latin's order, mediant on *povos*; the fronted form stays an option.

9

fails: plain

“Deus está sentado sobre o seu trono santo”

The Latin repeats a root in 'sedet super sedem'. 'Sentado ... trono' drops that echo, and the periphrasis 'está sentado' spends two extra syllables on a simple present.

proposedDeus se assenta sobre o seu santo assento

kept as an option under Deus sedet super sedem sanctam suam — sedes (God's) → *trono* is the row; *assento* is a chair. *está sentado* says the present state (κάθηται). *se assenta* recorded as an option.

10

fails: sayable

“foram muito elevados”

'Muito' is flat and conversational at the psalm's climax. It weakens the final cadence, and 'vehementer' reads as adverb plus participle.

proposedforam sobremaneira elevados

kept as an option under veheménter eleváti sunt — *sobremaneira* was unknown to a blind reader and withdrawn at 30:12 in the review; the valde/veheménter row keeps *muito*.

“Os príncipes dos povos se reuniram com o Deus de Abraão”

The first colon runs about 19 syllables, which is a long breath. The article in 'com o Deus' adds a hiatus the line can do without.

proposedOs príncipes dos povos se reuniram com o Deus de Abraão (keep it, but it can be lightened as 'Reuniram-se os príncipes dos povos com o Deus de Abraão')

not taken — The reader itself says to keep it; the length matches the Latin (18/18), and the verb-first order parts *reuniram-se* from *com*.

It is a clean, sturdy, easily chanted psalm with strong acclamatory openings. The fourfold 'entoai salmos' is heavy, but it is faithful and it rings. Only the verse 46:4 word order and the closing verse slip from prayer into translation.

Verse by verse

2

gentes → *nações* (row); *Omnes gentes* as vocative → *Todas as nações* (9:18, 117:10 *todas as nações*). *in voce exsultatiónis* → *com voz de exultação*, word for word 41:5b. The whole verse is the Introit of Pentecost VII (Pent07-0) and the Alleluia verse of the Ascension Mass (missa Pasc5-4) — grep; each colon reads alone.

3

excélsus → *excelso*, the Latin's adjective, kept apart from *Altíssimo* (Altíssimus) though the Greek has ὕψιστος. *Rex magnus* → *grande Rei*, without article, in apposition as the Latin; Rei capitalized for God (5:3, 23:7). *super omnem terram* → *sobre toda a terra*. First colon −3 syllables against the Latin (the asyndeton kept); accepted.

5

*Elégit nobis* → *Escolheu para nós* (eligere → *escolher*, 32:12, 24:12). *hereditátem suam* is 'his inheritance' in the Latin and Greek (the Hebrew has 'our'); kept. dilígere → *amar* (row).

6

ascéndere → *subir* (17:9, 17:11, 23:3); the Latin's verb-first order kept, as natural in Portuguese. The Ascension's versicle and antiphon (Pasc5-4 etc.) quote the verse with *in jubilatióne*, not the psalm's *in júbilo*; the psalm keeps its own words, and the same Portuguese would serve both (see `jubilate`). The two colons are said as V and R: each reads alone.

7

Four times *entoai salmos*: +4 syllables in each colon, accepted under D25 and D2 (see `psallite`). *Deo nostro*, *Regi nostro* → *ao nosso Deus*, *ao nosso Rei* (article before possessive).

8

*Rex omnis terræ Deus* has God as subject (ὁ θεός with the article; DRB, MS1932): *Deus é o Rei de toda a terra*, natural order (D2). The Palm Sunday procession (missa Quad6-0r) sets this verse as a versicle.

9

*Regnábit* future (the Greek aorist not followed). *Deus … Deus* kept at the head of each colon.

10

*cum Deo Ábraham* → *com o Deus de Abraão* (μετὰ τοῦ θεοῦ Αβρααμ); DM1962's *ao povo do Deus de Abraão* is the Hebrew. *quóniam* → *porque*.
