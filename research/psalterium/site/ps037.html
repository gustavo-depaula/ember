8

fails: plain, native

“os meus lombos estão cheios de ilusões”

In current Portuguese 'ilusões' means illusions or fantasies. Next to 'lombos' it sounds psychological, as if the loins were daydreaming. The Latin 'illusiones' here means mockeries or taunts, and the ear gets the wrong picture without a footnote.

proposedPorque os meus lombos estão cheios de escárnios: * e não há saúde na minha carne.

❧ taken

11

fails: sayable

“O meu coração está perturbado, abandonou-me o meu vigor”

The first colon is long, with two clauses and two 'o meu'. The article on the opening possessive adds a syllable the breath doesn't need.

proposedMeu coração está perturbado, abandonou-me o meu vigor: * e a luz dos meus olhos, nem ela está comigo.

kept as an option under Cor meum conturbátum est — The glossary keeps the article before possessives.

12

fails: native

“Os amigos meus e os próximos meus”

Putting both possessives after the noun copies the Latin word order. It sounds precious and calqued, like a translation, not like prayer. The postposition only exists to rescue the mediant from the proparoxytone 'próximos'.

proposedOs meus amigos e os meus vizinhos * contra mim se aproximaram, e se detiveram.

❧ taken — In part: the plain order taken (proparoxytone mediant accepted); *vizinhos* refused — vicínus's word, and it drops the echo *próximos … aproximaram*; an option.

12b

fails: native

“se detiveram de longe”

'Deter-se de longe' is not idiomatic. One stops 'ao longe' but stands 'de longe'. The ear stumbles on the preposition.

proposedE os que estavam junto de mim se detiveram ao longe: * e faziam violência os que buscavam a minha alma.

❧ taken — Mended otherwise, with the Latinist's *ficaram de longe*.

13

fails: plain, native

“falaram vaidades”

Today 'vaidades' means vanity in the sense of pride or cosmetics, not empty talk. The phrase needs a footnote.

proposedE os que procuravam males contra mim falaram coisas vãs: * e o dia todo tramavam enganos.

kept as an option under locúti sunt vanitátes — vánitas row; *coisas vãs* is inánia / vana's (2:1, 11:3). Evidence to the row.

“meditavam enganos”

To a modern Brazilian ear 'meditar' is devotional, so 'meditar enganos' sounds almost self-contradictory. 'Tramar' is the plain word for plotting and still says that they turned it over in their minds.

proposede o dia todo tramavam enganos.

kept as an option under dolos tota die meditabántur — The meditári row keeps the Latin's one verb for the just man and the plotter (2:1, 35:5); *tramar* explains.

15

fails: plain

“não tem repreensões na sua boca”

'Repreensões' is heavy and sounds like scolding. The Latin means a retort or rebuttal: the man has no answer to give. 'Réplicas' is the plainer faithful word and flows better.

proposedE tornei-me como um homem que não ouve: * e que não tem réplicas na sua boca.

kept as an option under non habens in ore suo redargutiónes — The echo with 37:2 (ἐλέγξῃς / ἐλεγμούς) is the Latin's; the blind reader heard a sense within the word.

17

fails: sayable, native, plain

“Porque eu disse: Para que não se alegrem à minha custa os meus inimigos”

'Para que não' as the opening of direct speech is awkward. 'À minha custa' is colloquial and commercial. The inverted subject piles the whole colon onto its tail. It takes more than one breath.

proposedPorque eu disse: Que os meus inimigos não exultem sobre mim: * e enquanto os meus pés vacilam, falaram grandes coisas contra mim.

❧ taken — In part: *Que os meus inimigos não se alegrem à minha custa* — the wish and the order taken; *exultem sobre mim* refused (super me row; exsultáre's verb), option 3.

“são abalados, falaram grandezas”

'São abalados' is a heavy passive. 'Falaram grandezas' is unidiomatic, since 'grandezas' suggests quantities or splendours. 'Grandes coisas' keeps 'magna' plainly.

proposede enquanto os meus pés vacilam, falaram grandes coisas contra mim.

kept as an option under dum commovéntur pedes mei — The Latin's passive and the movéri row; heard rightly.

18

fails: native

“a minha dor está sempre à minha vista”

'Minha ... minha' in quick succession, and 'à minha vista' is slightly stiff. 'Diante de mim' is the natural Portuguese for 'in conspectu meo' and gives a clean oxytone close.

proposedPorque eu estou pronto para os flagelos: * e a minha dor está sempre diante de mim.

kept as an option under et dolor meus in conspéctu meo semper — The conspéctus row (15:8); *diante de* already renders a fácie three times here.

23

fails: native

“Atendei em meu auxílio”

'Atender em' is not a Portuguese construction. The ear hears a garbled echo of 'Vinde em meu auxílio'. 'Acudir em auxílio' is the natural idiom for turning toward someone to help.

proposedAcudi em meu auxílio, * Senhor, Deus da minha salvação.

kept as an option under Inténde in adjutórium meum — D3 inténdere → *atender*; the blind reader heard 'come to my help' first. The formula (= 69:2) proposed as an open row.

The psalm mostly sounds like prayer in Portuguese. It is plain, heavy and penitential, and the Latin echoes are kept well: 'fardo pesado pesaram', 'não há saúde na minha carne', the run of 'Porque'. The weak spots are a few false friends (ilusões, vaidades, meditavam) and some calqued syntax in the middle verses (12, 17, 23), and those are where the text starts to sound like a translation.

“ficaram de longe” de longe stetérunt

The same verb 'steterunt' is 'ficaram de pé' in 37:12 and here only 'ficaram'; the posture ('stood') of the Latin is weakened and the echo between the two lines is lost.

proposedpuseram-se de pé ao longe

not taken — It was his own v1 fix; *ficar* twice keeps the one verb; *ao longe* is the ambiguous 'offshore / afar' the stylist and D8 warned of, and *de longe* is the Latin's *de longe*.

17

minor

“Que os meus inimigos não se alegrem à minha custa” Nequándo supergáudeant mihi inimíci mei

'à minha custa' is an idiom that interprets 'mihi' (exult over me); 'nequando' ('lest ever / never') is flattened to a plain 'não'.

proposedQue nunca os meus inimigos exultem sobre mim

not taken — The super me row (34:19, 34:24); *exultar* is exsultáre's. *nunca* is option 2 (Matos Soares 1932); the plain *não* follows the nequándo row, which has never spelled out *quando*.

minor

“pensarei no meu pecado” cogitábo pro peccáto meo

'pro' (on behalf of / for, i.e. be anxious for) becomes a simple object 'em'; the Latin's 'pro' is lost.

proposedpensarei pelo meu pecado

not taken — *pensar pelo pecado* is heard as 'think in the sin's place'; the cogitáre row's regency. *por causa do* is option 1.

20

minor

“se firmaram sobre mim” confirmáti sunt super me

Passive 'confirmati sunt' (were made strong / grew strong over me) is given a reflexive active sense; closer is a passive or 'fortalecer-se' which keeps the comparative strength of the enemies.

proposedforam fortalecidos sobre mim

not taken — The reflexive is the Portuguese middle for 'grew firm', and one verb keeps the echo with 37:3 *firmastes sobre mim*; the v1 Latinist passed it; the blind reader heard 'gained the upper hand'.

A faithful, close rendering: no content is added or dropped, tenses and persons track the Latin, and Septuagintal readings (illusionibus, cicatrices, a facie insipientiae, rugiebam) are kept rather than smoothed. The pointing is identical throughout (a single mediant * in every verse, no flex or second mediant). The remaining points are minor nuances: an intensive 'ipsum', the repeated 'steterunt', 'nequando', 'pro peccato', and the passive 'confirmati sunt'.
