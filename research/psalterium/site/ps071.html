The pointing marks match the Latin throughout (one mediant per verse, no flex).

Verse by verse

2

The Latin's colon ends on the imperative (*regi da*); Portuguese order puts it first. *Deus* bare vocative (the row). *dai* is safe under rule 3 (*dei* ≠ *dai*). *judícium → juízo* (D15). The verse ends with a colon, as the Latin, because 71:2b depends on it.

2b

*Judicáre* (the infinitive of purpose or result, κρίνειν) → *Para julgar*. *com justiça* as 9:8b.

3

*pópulo* dative → *para o povo*. *colles → colinas* (64:13). The second colon verbless, as the Latin.

4

*salvos fáciet → salvará* (salvum fácere row). *calumniátor → caluniador* (calumniári row, which names 71:4).

5

*in generatióne et generatiónem → de geração em geração* (formula row; 32:11 has the same ablative form). *cum sole* kept literally (*com o sol*): the king lasts alongside the sun.

6

*stillicídia stillántia* → *gotas que gotejam*: the Latin's figure of one root twice, kept (stillicídium row, *gota*).

7

Advent versicle (Adv1-1, Adv3-0, Adv3-3). *abundántia → abundância* (row). *auferri → ser tirado* (auferre row, D41).

8

*a mari usque ad mare → de mar a mar*, the Portuguese idiom; the second pair keeps *desde … até*. *orbis terrárum → o mundo* (D30). The Latin is a versicle and Alleluia (Adv2-0; 10-DU).

9

*prócidere → prostrar-se* (no row; proposed, open). *Æthíopes → os etíopes*, the people's name in lower case as Portuguese writes it. **v2:** subject first, *coram illo* after the verb (the stylist): draft 1 put the proparoxytone *etíopes* at the mediant; order is grammar (D2). The ambiguity reader could take *os seus inimigos* as the Ethiopians' — as the Latin *ejus* can be; left.

10

*Tharsis → Társis* (D43). *Árabum → dos árabes* (the Latin names the people; MS1932 *da Arábia* the land). *Reges Tharsis, et ínsulæ*: the islands are a second subject, as the Greek αἱ νῆσοι.

11

The Latin's order (verb, then subject) kept: subject-first (*E todos os reis da terra o adorarão*) put *adorarão* at the mediant against *servirão* at the end, a rhyme the Latin does not have (checks). Each colon is said alone: the Epiphany Offertory's second half, an Advent versicle (Adv3-3), 10-DU.

12

The Latin repeats *páuperem* (the Greek varies πτωχὸν … πένητα): the repetition is kept. *erat* → *não tinha* (the imperfect). No comma before *que*: the restrictive reading is the plainer one in Portuguese.

13

*ánimas páuperum salvas fáciet → salvará as almas dos pobres*. The verse and 71:12 are the Epistle-verse of St Peter Claver (09-09n, aliquibus locis).

14

*usúra → usura* (row, names this verse); the plural kept — unknown to the ambiguity reader, kept for the reproach the row wants. *rédimet → resgatará* (redímere row). **v2:** first colon *as suas almas* for *as almas deles* (stylist); *eórum* in the second colon stays *deles*, apart from the king's *dele*.

15

*dábitur ei de auro* → *lhe será dado do ouro* (the partitive *de* kept). *tota die → o dia todo* (D24). *benedícent ei* → *o bendirão* (the dative made direct, as the benedícere row allows for pronouns).

16

One long first colon with no flex in the Latin; the comma kept. *superextollétur super Líbanum* → *acima do Líbano se erguerá* (extóllere → erguer, row open; the *super-* of the verb carried by *acima*). The Latin's order puts *fructus ejus* last before the mediant; kept, so the colon closes on *fruto*. **v2:** comma after *na terra* (the stylist: two locatives ran together).

17

*in sǽcula → pelos séculos* (D43, names 71:17). *nomen ejus* twice, kept.

17b

*magnificáre → engrandecer* (row; the Greek μακαριοῦσιν, 'call blessed', is another verb — the Latin followed). *omnes tribus terræ → todas as tribos da terra*.

18

The close of Book II. *Benedíctus Dóminus, Deus Israël* → *Bendito o Senhor, Deus de Israel*, verbless, as 40:14 (D41). Responsory R. of Pent01-0 and Gradual of Epi1-0a (with *magna*, grep).

19

*in ætérnum → para sempre* (D23). *replébitur → será cheia* (the replére row: passive kept). *fiat, fiat → assim seja, assim seja* (D41). The first colon verbless, carrying on 71:18's acclamation.
