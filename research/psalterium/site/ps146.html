The audit trail

1draft

Drafted at psalm level from the parallels, with lookups in the finished psalms (ps146/concord.py, adapted from ps145's) and circulation checked (ps146/circulation.md: CNBB LH page read verbatim from saved HTML, CNBB Bible fetched). The hard readings: 146:1 'bonus est psalmus' (order, so it is not heard as the Lord's goodness), 'decóra'; 146:3 'contrítos corde' against 50:19 and 'contritiónes' against 59:4 (same Greek both times); 146:7 'præcínite' beside 'psállite'; 146:10 'voluntátem habébit' as θελήσει = 111:1. Uses in the Latin books: the antiphons 'Deo nostro jucúnda sit laudátio' and 'Laudáte Dóminum qui sanat …', the short responsory and Gradual on 146:5.

2checks

Hard checks pass. There are no rhyme flags. The length flags are accepted. 146:3a is +3 because 'de coração contrito' needs a preposition the Latin ablative doesn't. 146:7 is +3/+4: 'entoar salmos' is D25's two words for one, and 'Começai o canto' keeps 'præcínite' apart from it. 146:8a second colon is +4: the Latin dative 'terræ' becomes 'para a terra', and 'plúviam' keeps its article. 146:9a is +3, from the article and possessive. 146:10–11 run 3–4 short, because the Latin's 'beneplácitum est/erit' and 'voluntátem habébit' are periphrases and Portuguese says them with one verb. Every colon ends on a paroxytone or oxytone, except 146:5b 'número' and 146:7b 'cítara'. Both are proparoxytones exactly where the Latin has one ('númerus', 'cíthara').

2

minor

“congregará os dispersos de Israel” dispersiónes Israélis congregábit

The Latin has an abstract collective noun ('the dispersions / scatterings of Israel', LXX tas diasporas), not a personal participle; 'os dispersos' makes it personal. Sense is the same, but the Latin's noun could be kept.

proposedcongregará as dispersões de Israel

kept as an option under dispersiónes Israélis congregábit — Repeated from v1. An abstract noun cannot be gathered in Portuguese, and DRB and MS1932 both make it personal.

3

minor

“os de coração contrito ... as suas fraturas” contrítos corde ... contritiónes eórum

The Latin repeats the root (contritos / contritiones); 'fraturas' loses the echo. Defensible, but 'contrições' or 'quebrantos' paired with 'quebrantados' would keep it closer.

proposede enfaixa as suas contrições

kept as an option under Qui sanat contrítos corde: * et álligat contritiónes eórum. — 'contrições' means acts of contrition, a wrong sense. 50:19 and 59:4 (the same Greek) are kept, and the echo is recorded as lost.

A faithful rendering: tenses (congregabit, habebit, erit future), the Septuagintal readings (psalmus bonus, servituti hominum, pullis corvorum invocantibus eum, tibiis viri) and the concrete images are all kept, and the pointing matches the Latin (one mediant per verse, no flex or second mediant). Only two minor points of closeness: the abstract 'dispersiones' made personal, and the lost contritos/contritiones echo.

3

fails: concrete

“os de coração contrito, * e enfaixa as suas fraturas”

The Latin binds the two cola with one root (contrítos / contritiónes); 'contrito' then 'fraturas' breaks the echo the ear expects, and 'fraturas' sounds clinical beside 'enfaixa'.

proposedQue cura os de coração quebrantado, * e enfaixa os seus quebrantos.

kept as an option under Qui sanat contrítos corde: * et álligat contritiónes eórum. — 'quebranto' is the folk evil-eye malaise in Brazil, and 'quebrantado' loses 50:19's word for the same Greek.

7

fails: sayable, native

“Começai o canto ao Senhor com ação de graças”

Wordy paraphrase of praecínite; 'ao Senhor com ação' chains nasal -ão/ao sounds and the colon runs long for one breath.

proposedEntoai ao Senhor ação de graças: * cantai salmos ao nosso Deus com a cítara.

❧ taken — The first colon is taken: 'entoar' is præcínere's own sense. His second colon ('cantai salmos') is refused, because D25 keeps 'entoai salmos' for psállere.

9

fails: concrete

“dá aos animais o seu alimento”

'animais' is generic where juménta names the working beasts; the plainer concrete word is available.

proposedQue dá ao gado o seu alimento, * e aos filhotes dos corvos que o invocam.

kept as an option under Qui dat juméntis escam ipsórum — The juménta row ('animais'); 'gado' is narrower (cattle).

10

fails: native

“Não se comprazerá na força do cavalo”

'se comprazerá' is bookish and sibilant-heavy in the mouth; the hard -zerá-na hurries into the image.

proposedNão terá gosto na força do cavalo, * nem se agradará das pernas do homem.

kept as an option under Non in fortitúdine equi voluntátem habébit — 111:1's verb for the same Greek θελήσει ἐν. It moves with that row.

The psalm moves well: the chain of relative clauses ('Que cura... Que conta... Que cobre...') gives it a steady litany rhythm and the images stay concrete. Only v. 7 sounds translated rather than prayed, and v. 10 leans slightly bookish; the rest is plain, noble and easy to chant.

2

minor

“os dispersos de Israel” dispersiónes Israélis

The Latin has an abstract noun, 'the dispersions (scatterings) of Israel', a Septuagintal turn (diasporas). The translation replaces it with the concrete persons, which smooths the Latin toward the Hebrew sense.

proposedcongregará as dispersões de Israel.

kept as an option under dispersiónes Israélis congregábit — Repeated from v1. An abstract noun cannot be gathered in Portuguese, and DRB and MS1932 both make it personal.

7

minor

“ação de graças” in confessióne

'in confessione' is adverbial ('with praise/acknowledgement'), not the object of the verb; the translation makes it the object of 'entoai' and narrows confessio to thanksgiving.

proposedEntoai ao Senhor com louvor: *

kept as an option under Præcínite Dómino in confessióne — 'entoar' wants an object, so the adverbial phrase becomes one; that is grammar (D2). 'louvor' is refused: it is laus/laudátio's word (D5), and 'in confessióne' is 'ação de graças' at 94:2 and 99:3b. The adverbial build is added as option 2.

9

minor

“aos animais” juméntis

jumenta are beasts of burden or cattle, not animals in general; 'animais' widens the word.

proposedQue dá ao gado o seu alimento, *

kept as an option under Qui dat juméntis escam ipsórum — The juménta row (35:7b, 48:13). 'gado' is heard as cattle, which is narrower than κτήνη. It is option 2, as the v1 stylist also asked.

A faithful, close rendering: tenses (including the futures in 146:2 and 146:10), persons and images are kept, and every verse has the same single mediant as the Latin. The only points are minor: an abstract Septuagintal noun made concrete in 146:2, 'in confessione' made into an object and narrowed in 146:7, and 'jumenta' widened in 146:9.

Verse by verse

1

The Alleluia title (LXX αλληλουια Αγγαιου καὶ Ζαχαριου; MS1932 'Aleluia.') is not in DO's Latin, which opens on 'Laudáte Dóminum'. As in Pss 110–112 and 134 it is not reproduced (D7). 'laudátio → louvor' (110:10b). 'Deo nostro' stands first in the colon, as in the Latin. Taking 'e belo' out leaves the antiphon text of Thursday Lauds and Saturday Vespers ('Deo nostro jucúnda sit laudátio').

3

Opens as a relative ('Que cura'), hanging on 'o Senhor' of 146:2 as the Latin 'Qui' does. It also joins cleanly to 'Louvai o Senhor' in the Thursday Lauds antiphon.

4

'numeráre → contar' (21:17b 'contaram'). The Latin's echo 'númerat … númerus' (146:4, 146:5) is lost: 'conta … número'. 'Não tem conta' would keep it, but it drops the noun 'número' that 39:13 and 104:34 use for 'non est númerus'.

5

'Magnus Dóminus' as the formula 'Grande é o … Senhor' (47:2, 95:4). Each colon is also a short responsory text ('Magnus Dóminus noster, * et magna virtus ejus. V. Et sapiéntiæ ejus non est númerus') and a Gradual versicle (St Raphael), and each reads alone. 'non est númerus → não tem número' (39:13). 'virtus → poder' (the working row).

8a

'operíre → cobrir' (108:19); 'paráre → preparar' (88:3). 'terræ' is a dative → 'para a terra'.

11

'timéntes eum → os que o temem' (84:10, 102:17). 'speráre super → esperar em' (32:18b, word for word).
