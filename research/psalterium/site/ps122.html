3

minor

“estamos muito fartos de desprezo” multum repléti sumus despectióne

'repleti' is 'filled, sated'; 'fartos' is literally right, but in current Portuguese 'estar farto de' is heard first as 'to be fed up with', which turns being filled with contempt into weariness of it. Defensible (the Hebrew image is satiety), but the reading can tip away from the Latin.

proposedporque estamos muito repletos de desprezo:

kept as an option under multum repléti sumus despectióne … multum repléta est ánima nostra — 'repletos de desprezo' is heard as contempt we feel, the fault 'fartos' was chosen to avoid; 'fed up with being scorned' keeps the suffering and the excess.

4

minor

“a afronta aos prósperos, e o desprezo aos soberbos” oppróbrium abundántibus, et despéctio supérbis

The Latin nouns are bare, in apposition to the soul's filling: we are a reproach to the rich and a contempt to the proud. The added definite articles ('a afronta', 'o desprezo') make them read as specific things, and 'a afronta aos prósperos' can be heard as an affront directed at the prosperous. Datives and the sense of 'abundantibus' ('those who have plenty') are otherwise kept; 'repleta est' with 'farta' carries the same caveat as v. 3.

proposedPorque a nossa alma está muito repleta: * afronta para os prósperos, e desprezo para os soberbos.

❧ taken

The translation is faithful and complete: tenses, persons, the subjunctive 'donec misereatur', and the dative structure of v. 4 are all kept, and the pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only points are minor: 'fartos/farta' risks the colloquial 'fed up' sense, and the articles in v. 4 slightly shift the bare appositional nouns 'opprobrium' and 'despectio'.

2b

fails: sayable

“assim os nossos olhos para o Senhor, nosso Deus, até que tenha piedade de nós”

The second colon is long and has no verb. By the time the reader reaches 'até que tenha piedade' the subject has slipped away, and 'tenha' could momentarily be heard as going back to 'os nossos olhos'. It needs a breath in the middle and a verb to hold it together.

proposedassim estão os nossos olhos no Senhor, nosso Deus, até que tenha piedade de nós

kept as an option under sicut óculi servórum in mánibus … ita óculi nostri ad Dóminum — 'no' loses ad (toward); supplying the verb fills what the Latin leaves verbless; the ambiguity reader heard the colon rightly.

3

fails: native

“estamos muito fartos de desprezo”

In current Brazilian speech 'estar farto de' means 'to be fed up with'. Said aloud, the line lands as annoyance ('we are sick of being despised') instead of the Latin 'filled to the full'. The words are acceptable here, but the colloquial echo is strong.

proposedporque estamos muito saciados de desprezo

kept as an option under multum repléti sumus despectióne … multum repléta est ánima nostra — saciar belongs to satiáre / saturáre (glossary); 'fed up with being scorned' is the Latin's sense, not against it.

4

fails: native, sayable

“a nossa alma está muito farta”

Coming right after v.3, the colloquial 'fed up' sense of 'farta' is even stronger with 'alma' as its subject ('our soul is really fed up').

proposedPorque a nossa alma está muito saciada

kept as an option under multum repléti sumus despectióne … multum repléta est ánima nostra — One verb twice, so it goes with 122:3.

“a afronta aos prósperos, e o desprezo aos soberbos”

The definite articles make the colon read like a detached pair of headings. The ear can't tell how it connects to the soul being filled. It also stumbles on 'a afronta aos', where the vowels run together ('a-a-fron-ta-aos'). Without the articles the colon still keeps the Latin dative and its ambiguity, and it reads as the content of the filling.

proposedafronta aos prósperos, e desprezo aos soberbos

❧ taken — Articles dropped as asked; 'para' (the Latinist's) preferred to 'aos'; 'aos' kept as an option.

Plain, faithful and dignified. The first three verses move well to the tone, and the repeated 'tende piedade de nós' carries the psalm's plea cleanly. The weak points are the colloquial 'fed up' echo of 'fartos/farta' and the loose, article-heavy last colon, which ends the psalm on a stumble.

Nothing to correct in any verse.

The translation follows the Latin closely throughout: the vocatives, the double 'miserere nostri', the perfect passive 'repleti sumus' / 'repleta est', and the ambiguous datives 'abundantibus' and 'superbis' (kept open with 'para') are all rendered without adding, dropping or resolving anything. 'Prósperos' for 'abundantibus' and 'fartos' for 'repleti' are fair equivalents. The pointing marks match the Latin in kind and order in every verse.

Verse by verse

1

No titulus: 'Canticum graduum' is not in DO's psalm file and tituli are out of the first edition (D7) — as Ps 133 and Ps 120. 'in cælis' plural → 'nos céus' (2:4 'Aquele que habita nos céus'). habitáre → habitar. The perfect 'levávi' → 'levantei' (as 24:1 and 120:1; the CNBB has the present).

2a

dóminus of the masters → 'senhores', and of God (122:2b) → 'Senhor': one Latin word, one Portuguese word, so the image of the servant's master and the Lord is heard as the Latin has it. Second colon 7 syllables against 11 (checks.py), accepted: nothing is dropped, Portuguese is shorter here.

2b

'Dóminum, Deum nostrum' → 'o Senhor, nosso Deus' (apposition). 'misereátur nostri' → 'tenha piedade de nós' (the miseréri row), so that the next verse's 'Miserére nostri' is heard as the same words — the Latin's echo. The Missal's Tract (Quad3-0) has 'Et sicut' and later 'nobis'; the psalm's text is followed.

3

'Miserére nostri, Dómine, miserére nostri' → 'Tende piedade de nós, Senhor, tende piedade de nós' (the miseréri formula, plural; the same words as the Te Deum and the Preces versicle in the Latin). First colon +3 on the Latin, accepted for the formula. multum → muito, in both verses. despéctio → desprezo (see v4b). The vocative 'Dómine' bare, as the glossary's rule (no 'ó' where the Latin has none; the CNBB adds one).

4

'Quia multum repléta est ánima nostra' repeats 122:3's 'quia multum repléti sumus'; the Portuguese repeats 'porque … muito farto'. Subject-first order in the first colon (rule 2). ánima nostra → 'a nossa alma'. Draft 2: 'afronta para os prósperos, e desprezo para os soberbos' (the Latinist). The ambiguity reader noted 'soberbos' could be heard as 'superb'; beside 'desprezo' it heard 'the proud', and 93:2 has the same word. 'prósperos' heard as 'those who do well', neutral: the Latin's word is as neutral (72:12).
