major

“e há firmeza no flagelo deles” et firmaméntum in plaga eórum

The Latin second colon is verbless and elliptical; whether 'non est' carries over (no firmness in their stroke) or not is left open. Supplying a positive 'há' resolves the ambiguity toward the opposite of the reading the Vulgate tradition usually takes.

proposede firmeza no flagelo deles

not taken as proposed

“flagelo” plaga

'plaga' is a blow/wound, distinct from 'flagellabúntur' in the next verse; 'flagelo' assimilates the two words.

proposede firmeza na chaga deles

not taken as proposed

The one substantive fault is 72:4b, where an added 'há' resolves the elliptical colon; the rest are minor number/image/locative points. Verse by verse

1

*Quam bonus* → *Como … é bom* (the *quam* row: *Como …!*). *his, qui recto sunt corde* → *para os que são retos de coração* (the *rectus corde* row, with the Latin's relative clause). Palm Sunday Tract (Quad6-0) and the Graduals of 02-27, 03-27, 06-04.

2

*Mei autem* is fronted for contrast after 72:1; *Mas os meus pés* carries it without *quanto a mim*. The two colons keep the Latin's *pæne … pæne* (*quase … quase*).

3

*videns* → *vendo*; *pax → paz*.

5

*labor → fadiga* (the row; 72:16 has the same noun). The final colon ends on a colon, as the Latin, since 72:6 draws the consequence (*Ídeo*).

6

*Ídeo → Por isso* (row), as 72:10.

7

*prodíre → sair* (row, which names 72:7); *adeps → gordura* (row, which names 72:7: the concrete image kept; MS1932 glosses *coração sensual*). *quasi* → *como que*. The subject first, for the ear (D2).

8

*cogitáre* shares its object with *loqui*; Portuguese says *pensar maldade* (content accusative, as 16:9b *falou soberba*), so the row's *em* is not used. *nequítia → maldade* (row, which names 72:8 beside *iniquitátem*). *locúti sunt* twice, *falaram* twice.

9

*transívit in terra* → *passou pela terra* (διῆλθεν ἐπὶ τῆς γῆς); *transíre → passar* as in 72:7.

10

*dies pleni → dias cheios* (DRB *full days*, MS1932); *inveníri → ser achado*; *in eis → neles*.

11

*scit … sciéntia* → *sabe … saber*: the root repeats as in the Latin (*sciéntia → o saber*, the row; *scire* is used absolutely, so *saber*, not the row's *conhecer*). 72:21 *nescívi* → *não soube* keeps the same verb.

13

*Et dixi* → *E eu disse* (the pronoun so that *disse* is not heard as 'he said'). *sine causa → sem causa* (row). *et lavi inter innocéntes manus meas* copies 25:6 (*Lavarei as minhas mãos entre os inocentes*) as the Lavabo row asks.

14

*tota die → o dia todo* (D24).

15

The first second-person pronoun of the psalm (*filiórum tuórum*): God, so *vossos*.

17

*Sanctuárium → santuário* (kept apart from *sancta → o lugar santo*, as that row notes).

19

*propter iniquitátem suam* → *por causa da sua iniquidade*, as 72:18 *propter dolos* → *por causa dos enganos*.

20

*imágo → imagem*; *ad níhilum redígere → reduzir a nada* (row, which names 72:20 and 72:21: the same phrase twice in two verses, kept identical). *Dómine* → bare *Senhor*.

21

*inflammáre → inflamar-se* (MS1932). *nescívi → não soube*, as 72:11. DO joins 72:21–22 in one prayed verse; 72:22 is not a separate id.

24

The Gradual of Palm Sunday (Quad6-0) and of 03-27.

25

*enim → pois* (row); *velle → querer* (row), perfect *quis*.

26
