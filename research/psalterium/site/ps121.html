2draft

Hard readings: in idípsum (D9, sixth place), participátio, testimónium Israël, sedérunt sedes, quæ ad pacem sunt Jerúsalem, virtus of a city. Where the Latin follows the Greek against the Hebrew (121:1 things said, not sayers; íbimus future; 121:2 átria, not gates; 121:3 participátio … in idípsum), the Latin is followed. No title: DO's Latin has no titulus for Ps 121 (nor for Ps 133, whose 'title formula' the brief asked to match — there is none in ps133/prayed.json), and D7 keeps tituli out.

1

minor

“com o que me foi dito” in his, quæ dicta sunt mihi

The Latin is plural ('in these things which were said to me'); the singular 'o que me foi dito' collapses it into a single utterance. Defensible as idiom but the number shifts.

proposedAlegrei-me com as coisas que me foram ditas: * Iremos à casa do Senhor.

kept as an option under in his, quæ dicta sunt mihi — the neuter clause carries a plural quæ with no noun (as D26); the plural adds three weak syllables to a line also sung alone

2

minor

“Os nossos pés estavam parados” Stantes erant pedes nostri

'Stantes' is 'standing', not 'stopped/halted'; 'parados' adds a nuance of having come to a stop. 'de pé' is closer.

proposedOs nossos pés estavam de pé, * nos teus átrios, Jerusalém.

not taken — 'os pés estavam de pé' says the feet stood on their feet; the ruling moved to 'se detinham' for the other readers' reasons

6

minor

“Rogai o que é para a paz de Jerusalém” Rogáte quæ ad pacem sunt Jerúsalem

'quæ' is neuter plural ('the things that are for peace'); rendered as singular 'o que é'. Also the Latin leaves 'Jerusalem' indeclinable (LXX has it accusative: ask the things for peace of/for Jerusalem); 'da paz de Jerusalém' is acceptable but 'o que é' should be plural.

proposedRogai as coisas que são para a paz de Jerusalém: * e abundância para os que te amam:

kept as an option under quæ ad pacem sunt — as 121:1; +3 syllables to a colon already +2

A faithful, close rendering that keeps the Septuagintal oddities (the pleonastic 'cujus participatio ejus in idipsum' as 'cuja participação é em conjunto', 'sedes... sedes' as thrones, the imperfect 'loquebar' as 'eu falava') without importing the Hebrew. Only minor points of number (v1, v6) and one slight nuance ('parados' for 'stantes', v2). Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex).

2

fails: native

“Os nossos pés estavam parados”

'parados' makes you hear stalled or idle feet, like traffic, not pilgrims who have arrived and stand in the courts. It is flat in the mouth.

proposedOs nossos pés se detinham, * nos teus átrios, Jerusalém.

❧ taken

3

fails: native, plain, concrete

“cuja participação é em conjunto”

'em conjunto' is office language, and 'participação é em' makes the ear stop. The line sounds like a translation note. It needs a plainer word for idipsum, one that still leaves open whether this means unity or 'the same thing'.

proposedcuja participação é num só todo.

kept as an option under in idípsum — supplies a noun and closes in idípsum on a whole; 'em conjunto' keeps the Greek's together and the juntos family

4

fails: sayable

“testemunho de Israel, para dar graças ao nome do Senhor”

This second colon is too long for one breath, and 'para dar graças ao' is heavy with little words. The shorter verb works for the psalm tone and still translates confitendum.

proposedtestemunho de Israel, para louvar o nome do Senhor.

kept as an option under ad confiténdum nómini Dómini — D5: louvar is laudáre's

6

fails: plain, native

“Rogai o que é para a paz de Jerusalém”

'o que é para a' is a chain of weak syllables and sounds like a gloss, not a prayer.

proposedRogai o que serve à paz de Jerusalém: * e abundância para os que te amam:

kept as an option under quæ ad pacem sunt — supplies a verb of usefulness for the bare sunt ad

7

fails: concrete

“no teu poder”

'poder' is abstract and political. Virtus here goes with the towers of the next colon: the strength of the walls. 'força' is more concrete and sits better next to 'torres'.

proposedHaja paz na tua força: * e abundância nas tuas torres.

❧ taken

8

fails: native

“eu falava paz sobre ti”

'falar paz sobre' is heard as a calque. 'sobre ti' can sound like 'about you' or like 'over you', and the ear hesitates between them.

proposedeu falava de paz a teu respeito:

kept as an option under loquébar pacem de te — pacem is the accusative of content, kept as 84:9 'falará paz' and 16:9b; 'falar de paz' is talking about peace

9

fails: plain

“busquei coisas boas para ti”

'coisas boas' is colloquial and weak for the psalm's closing cadence. A plain noun in final position ends the psalm with more weight.

proposedbusquei para ti os bens.

kept as an option under quæsívi bona tibi — the bona tríbuere row: 'bens' is heard as property

The psalm moves simply and its pilgrim joy comes through, especially at the opening. The middle (vv. 3, 6, 8) falls into gloss-like phrasing, with weak strings of small words and a couple of calques, so there it sounds translated. Tightening those colons and giving v. 9 a firmer final word would carry the ear through to the end.

worst line121:3

best line121:1

2

minor

“Os nossos pés se detinham” Stantes erant pedes nostri

'stantes erant' is a periphrastic of state, 'were standing'. 'se detinham' ('were halting / stopping') adds a sense of stopping or coming to rest that the Latin does not have.

proposedOs nossos pés estavam parados

kept as an option under Stantes erant pedes nostri — the gate contradicts itself: on draft 1 it marked 'parados' for the same fault (a stop). Two other readers heard 'parados' as stuck or idle; 'se detinham' is the deter-se row, DM1962's verb, and the Latin's length. Draft 1's wording stays option 1

4

minor

“testemunho de Israel” testimónium Israël

Israël is indeclinable, and the Latin leaves it open whether it is genitive or dative. The LXX has the dative, τῷ Ισραηλ, 'a testimony for/to Israel'. 'de Israel' picks the genitive and closes off the other reading.

proposedtestemunho para Israel

kept as an option under testimónium Israël — 'para' closes it the other way, on the Greek's dative; 'de' is the Latin surface and Douay-Rheims, and in Portuguese it is also 'concerning'. Raised only on the second reading

This is a faithful, close rendering. It keeps the Latin's odd phrasing: 'cujus participatio ejus in idipsum', the imperfect 'loquebar', and 'sedes' as seats in judgment. The only issues are two minor ones: 'se detinham' shades 'stantes erant', and 'de Israel' decides the case of an indeclinable noun. All nine verses have the same pointing marks as the Latin, a single mediant each.

Verse by verse

1

lætári → alegrar-se (glossary). 'Alegrei-me' at the head of the verse; 'Eu me alegrei' (Diurnal Monástico 1962) is as good and one syllable longer. No titulus: DO's Latin has none for this psalm or for Ps 133, and D7 keeps tituli out, so there is no title formula to match.

2

Jerusalem is spoken to: a singular addressee, tu (rule 3), here and in 121:6–9. átrium → átrio (glossary; unknown to several blind readers, kept). No 'ó' supplied before the vocative.

3

The resumptive ejus is not translated ('cuja participação' already says it). See the decisions participatio and idipsum. The ambiguity reader listed 'se edifica' and 'participação' as possibly unknown and heard a possible moral sense of 'edificar'; kept — edificar is the psalter's verb (50:20).

4

enim → pois (glossary). 'tribus, tribus Dómini' — the repetition kept. confitéri → dar graças a (D5); 'ad confiténdum nómini Dómini' keeps its dative. Dómini closes both cola, in Latin and Portuguese.

5

Quia → porque; illic → ali. The ambiguity reader heard 'thrones were set up for judging' first — the sense — and listed 'se assentaram' as possibly unknown; kept.

6

rogáre → rogar (new; 'Rogai' is also the CNBB wording of this verse). 'o que é para a paz' carries quæ ad pacem sunt; the plural 'as coisas que são' is the literal. abundántia → abundância (glossary; 121:6 and 121:7 are named in the row). The sudden 'te' (Jerusalem) inside a verse spoken to 'vós' is the Latin's. The ambiguity reader heard this 'te' (and the 'ti' of 121:9) as God. Nothing is supplied: the turn to Jerusalem is the Latin's, 121:2 has already made her 'tu' by name, and in this psalter God is 'vós', so the pronoun itself says who is meant.

7

turris → torre (glossary, 60:4).

8

'meus próximos' at the mediant is a proparoxytone, accepted as at 37:12 ('Os meus amigos e os meus próximos *'). próximus → próximo (glossary).

9

quǽrere → buscar (glossary). bona → 'coisas boas' (the bona tríbuere row, 12:6b; 'bens' is heard as property). 'do Senhor, nosso Deus' as 112:5.
