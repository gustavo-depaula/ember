31:14

minor

“Ao trigo, e ao vinho, e ao azeite” Super fruménto, et vino, et óleo

'Super' with ablative ('over, on account of') marks what they rejoice/stream in about, not a further goal parallel to 'ad bona Domini'; 'ao' turns it into an apposition to 'aos bens', making the list the definition of 'bona Domini', which the Latin does not state.

proposedPelo trigo, e pelo vinho, e pelo azeite

❧ taken

“crias das ovelhas e das vacas” fœtu pécorum et armentórum

'pecora' is flocks/small cattle in general and 'armenta' herds of large cattle; 'ovelhas' and 'vacas' narrow both to single species.

proposede pelas crias dos rebanhos e das manadas

❧ taken

31:17

minor

“os alegrarei depois da sua dor” lætificábo a dolóre suo

'a dolore' is 'from their sorrow' (away from / out of it); 'depois da' resolves it as purely temporal. Defensible (Douay reads 'after'), but narrower than the Latin.

proposede os alegrarei, livres da sua dor

kept as an option under et lætificábo a dolóre suo — Held: the fix supplies a verb and closes the other way; DRB reads 'after'. 'livres da sua dor' is the separative option for Gustavo.

“em júbilo” in gáudium

'gaudium' is joy; 'júbilo' leans toward exultatio/jubilatio, a louder register than the Latin word.

proposedem alegria

kept as an option under in gáudium — It chimes with the two 'alegrar' of different Latin root; the glossary row (29:12, same build) is open for a ruling.

A faithful, close rendering: no content is added or dropped, tenses and persons are kept, and the single mediant in each verse matches the Latin. The only points are small precision matters: 'super' in 31:14 flattened into an apposition, the species narrowed in 'pecorum et armentorum', and 'a dolore' read temporally in 31:17.

claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-23 · critic/v1.stylist.json · read prayed.vos.json

31:11

fails: sayable, native

“e o guardará como um pastor guarda o seu rebanho”

The supplied verb echoes 'guardará' at once ('guardará ... guarda'), which makes the line jingle and stretches the colon past one easy breath. Portuguese can leave the verb out after 'como', as the Latin does.

proposede o guardará como o pastor o seu rebanho.

❧ taken

31:12

fails: plain

“da mão do mais poderoso”

'Mais poderoso' is four syllables of comparative that slow the final cadence. 'Mais forte' is the plainer faithful word for potentior and closes more firmly.

proposede o livrou da mão do mais forte.

kept as an option under de manu potentióris — 'forte' is fortis's word; potens → poderoso is the glossary row.

31:14

fails: plain, native

“e às crias das ovelhas e das vacas”

'Vacas' is barnyard-homely and drops out of the register. Pecorum et armentorum are the flock and the herd, and the collective nouns are both closer and more dignified.

proposede às crias dos rebanhos e do gado:

❧ taken — Taken as the Latinist's 'rebanhos e manadas'; 'gado' is pecus's glossary word.

31:17

fails: cadence, plain

“em júbilo, *”

The word before the mediant, 'júbilo', is stressed on its third-to-last syllable, so the mediant cadence falls on an unstressed syllable. 'Alegria' would fix the stress but would clash with 'alegrarei' in the next colon, where the Latin uses two different roots (gaudium / laetificabo).

proposedE converterei o luto deles em regozijo, *

kept as an option under in gáudium — 29:12 has the same Latin build as 'em júbilo'; the glossary row decides both at once.

“depois da sua dor”

'Depois da' reads flat and prosaic. It also narrows the Latin 'a dolore suo' to a purely temporal sense, where the Latin leaves room for 'away from' or 'out of'. 'Após' keeps the same length and is less chatty, but ideally the preposition should stay as open as 'a'.

proposede os consolarei, e os alegrarei após a sua dor.

kept as an option under et lætificábo a dolóre suo — Just as temporal, and 'após' is post's word.

31:18

fails: sayable

“E embriagarei”

The vowel clash 'E em-' at the very start of the verse makes the mouth stall on a double nasal onset. Moving the object ahead of the verb softens it and keeps the 'E'.

proposedE a alma dos sacerdotes embriagarei de gordura: *

kept as an option under Et inebriábo ánimam sacerdótum pinguédine — An inversion for its own sake (rule 5); the elision is natural.

The psalm sounds plain, concrete and prayable. The chain of 'e ... e ...' carries the Latin's Hebrew parataxis without strain, and most cadences land well. The weak spots are the proparoxytone 'júbilo' at a mediant, a self-echoing 'guardará ... guarda', and the homely 'vacas', which are the few places where the register drops or the tone stumbles.

worst line31:17

best line31:15

31:17

minor

“e os alegrarei depois da sua dor” et lætificábo a dolóre suo

The preposition 'a' is separative ('from their sorrow'); 'depois da' makes it strictly temporal, narrowing what the Latin leaves open (away from / after). Defensible (it matches the traditional 'after their sorrow'), but a separative rendering would sit closer to the Latin.

proposede os alegrarei, [tirando-os] da sua dor — or keep 'depois da sua dor' if the temporal sense is preferred as in the Douay tradition

kept as an option under et lætificábo a dolóre suo — Held: the fix supplies a verb and closes the other way; DRB reads 'after'. 'livres da sua dor' is the separative option for Gustavo.

A faithful, close rendering of the canticle: every clause of the Latin is present, tenses, persons and numbers are kept, 'super frumento' as 'pelo trigo' and 'virgo in choro' as 'a virgem em coro' are adequate and keep the Latin's concreteness. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex or second mediant). The only point worth weighing is the temporal 'depois da' for separative 'a dolore' in 31:17.

Verse by verse

31:10

DO's Latin file opens with '(Canticum Jeremiæ * Jer 31:10-18)'. It is DO's heading for the canticle, not a titulus, and it is not a prayed verse. Under D7 it is not translated, as the Nunc dimittis did with its '(Canticum Simeonis …)'. Were it ever shown, it would be '(Cântico de Jeremias * Jr 31,10-14)'. DO's '31:10-18' counts its own nine lines, not Jeremiah's verses. DO's ids 31:10–31:18 are kept as they stand (rule 4), though the text is Jer 31:10–14. gentes → nações (glossary). ínsulæ → ilhas (71:10, 96:1).

31:11

dispérgere (people) → dispersar (43:12, 88:11; Tob 13:4 in ps212). congregáre active → congregar (15:4b, 106:2, and 146:2 'congregará os dispersos de Israel', the same promise). custodíre → guardar (glossary). Israel has no article, as in the Latin. 'congregará' was listed as unknown by the ambiguity reader. It is the glossary's congregáre (146:2, same promise), kept.

31:12

enim → pois, the psalter's use (9:27, 43:8, 49:12). The Latin opens with the verb; the order is natural (D2). Jacob → Jacó (77:71). liberáre → livrar (glossary; 105:10 has 'da mão do inimigo' in the same build).

31:13

laudábunt is absolute in the Latin → 'louvarão', with no object (MS1932 glosses '(a Deus)', not followed). mons Sion → o monte Sião (124:1). bona Dómini → os bens do Senhor (26:13). The chain virão / louvarão / Sião / afluirão is the Latin's own -ent / -unt / -ent chain in Portuguese dress. Mediant and final ('Sião' / 'Senhor') do not rhyme. 'afluirão' was listed as unknown by the ambiguity reader. It is kept: it is the CNBB's word in this very verse, and 'acorrerão' is the option.

31:15

Erítque → 'E … será'. eórum → 'deles', against the 'sua' that follows in 31:17 for suo: the Latin's two pronouns stay apart, and 'deles' cannot be heard as 'your'. ultra non esúrient → 'e não terão mais fome' (MS1932's words; ultra → mais, as 9:39, 82:5). The Hebrew / CNBB 'de sede e fome' adds thirst, which the Latin does not have.

31:16

Tunc → Então (125:2a). júvenes → jovens, senes → anciãos (148:12). simul → juntos (glossary). The articles are supplied (grammar). virgo is singular, as in the Latin (the Greek has plural παρθένοι): 'a virgem'. The ambiguity reader heard 'a virgem' as possibly the Virgin Mary and 'em coro' as singing in chorus. That is the Latin's own openness in a Christian ear, and the dance reading is the option 'na dança'.

31:17

convértere in (a change of state) → converter em (glossary row, 29:12, 65:6). consolári → consolar (70:21, 85:17). lætificáre → alegrar (glossary). suo → 'sua'. The mediant 'júbilo' is proparoxytone, and so is the Latin 'gáudium'. Accepted.

31:18

This colon, with 'ait Dóminus', is the canticle's antiphon (Thursday Lauds; circulation.md). As an antiphon: 'O meu povo, * diz o Senhor, será cheio dos meus bens.' pópulus meus → o meu povo (72:10, 80:12). The colon is 4 syllables under the Latin; nothing in the Latin is left out.

31:14

óleum as food → azeite (4:8). fœtus (singular, collective) → 'as crias'. The ambiguity reader listed 'crias' as unknown; it is the plain rural word for young animals, kept. 'filhotes' is childish.
