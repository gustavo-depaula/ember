7

minor

“e examinava o meu espírito” et scopébam spíritum meum

scopere means 'to sweep'. 'Examinava' turns the concrete image into an abstraction (Douay-Rheims keeps 'I swept my spirit').

proposede varria o meu espírito

kept as an option under et scopébam spíritum meum — The form scopébam is an -ēbam imperfect, the conjugation of scopo, -ere (= σκοπέω, 'search'), which L&S gives for this very verse; the sweeping verb scopáre would make scopábam. The Greek ἔσκαλλεν is a searching verb. 'varria' (DRB) stays option 3.

9

minor

“para sempre” in finem

'in finem' means 'to the end'. Rendering it 'para sempre' merges it with 'in ætérnum' in v. 8, a distinction the Latin keeps.

proposedOu até o fim cortará a sua misericórdia

❧ taken

minor

'in sancto' (LXX ἐν τῷ ἁγίῳ) can mean either 'in the holy place' or 'in holiness'. Adding 'lugar' picks one reading.

proposedDeus, no santo está o vosso caminho

❧ taken

18

minor

“as nuvens fizeram ouvir a voz” vocem dedérunt nubes

'fizeram ouvir' paraphrases 'dederunt' ('gave'). The Latin says 'gave voice'.

proposedas nuvens deram voz

kept as an option under vocem dedérunt nubes — The row vocem dare → fazer ouvir a voz (17:14, 45:7): 'dar a voz' is not how Portuguese says a sound is made.

The translation is faithful and literal throughout. It keeps the Septuagintal oddities ('na roda', 'invenções', 'Agora comecei'), the tenses (the imperfects in v. 7, the future passive in v. 20) and the voices, and every verse has the same pointing marks in the same order as the Latin. The only issues are minor: one flattened concrete image (scopebam), one merged time phrase (in finem), one resolved ambiguity (in sancto) and one light paraphrase (dederunt).

3

fails: sayable

“No dia da minha tribulação procurei a Deus, com as minhas mãos, de noite, diante dele”

The first colon is very long for one breath, and 'procurei' (three syllables before a heavy 'a Deus') makes it heavier. The Latin colon is long too, so the fix is to trim syllables, not content.

proposedNo dia da minha tribulação busquei a Deus, com as minhas mãos, de noite, diante dele: * e não fui enganado.

not taken — exquírere → procurar (row; 26:8), kept apart from quǽrere → buscar; the gain is one syllable.

4

fails: native

“e me exercitei”

To a Brazilian ear 'exercitar-se' first means working out or drilling. Next to 'me deleitei' it sounds like a gym, not like an anguished mind. The same thing happens in 76:7 ('me exercitava') and 76:13 ('me exercitarei'). If the root has to stay consistent across the three verses, keep it, but know that this is the psalm's most translated-sounding thread.

proposedlembrei-me de Deus, e me deleitei, e me agitei: e desfaleceu o meu espírito (with 'me agitava' in 76:7; 76:13 would need its own solution)

not taken — The row across Ps 118 (five places) and 54:3; 'agitar-se' is another verb's meaning and the stylist has no form for 76:13. Not added as an option for that reason; the row's own option 'ocupar-se' is there.

5

fails: native

“fui perturbado”

The passive 'fui perturbado' sounds as if someone disturbed the speaker. A Brazilian says 'fiquei perturbado' or 'perturbei-me' for an inner state.

proposedOs meus olhos anteciparam as vigílias: * fiquei perturbado, e não falei.

❧ taken

6

fails: native

“tive na mente”

'Tive na mente' is slightly off-idiom. The living phrase is 'tive em mente', which says the same and flows better.

proposedPensei nos dias antigos: * e tive em mente os anos eternos.

❧ taken

8

fails: native, sayable

“lançará fora para sempre”

'lançará fora' hangs without an object, and 'fora para' makes a clumsy -ra pa- run. 'Rejeitar' is the plainer word for proicere and stands on its own without an object.

proposedAcaso Deus rejeitará para sempre

not taken — proícere → lançar (row, which names 76:8); 'rejeitar' is reprobáre's (117:22). The missing object is the Latin's.

“ou não tornará ainda a ser mais favorável?”

'tornará ainda a ser' piles up three small words, and the ear trips before 'mais favorável'. 'Voltar a' is the current Brazilian auxiliary.

proposedou não voltará ainda a ser mais favorável?

❧ taken

9

fails: plain

“Ou para sempre cortará”

This repeats 'para sempre' from 76:8, but the Latin changes from 'in aeternum' to 'in finem'. The ear hears a duplicate where the text moves on. 'Até o fim' gives the variation and a better rhythm.

proposedOu cortará até o fim a sua misericórdia, * de geração em geração?

❧ taken

11

fails: native, concrete

“E disse”

Without a subject, 'E disse' is heard as 'and he said'. The speaker is 'I'.

proposedE eu disse: Agora comecei:

❧ taken

“a mudança da direita do Excelso”

In current Portuguese, 'a direita' alone means the right-hand side, or the political right. The concrete image of the right hand is lost. 'Excelso' also sounds precious next to the usual 'Altíssimo'.

proposedesta é a mudança da mão direita do Altíssimo.

not taken — déxtera → a direita (row, readers of 15:11, 17:36a heard the hand); Excélsus kept apart from Altíssimus (D43). Both options; both flagged in the glossary.

12

fails: sayable

“porque me lembrarei desde o início das vossas maravilhas”

'desde o início' splits the verb from its complement, so the ear hears 'the beginning of your wonders' before it can take in the sentence. Putting the phrase last keeps the Latin's ambiguity and reads in one line.

proposedporque me lembrarei das vossas maravilhas desde o início.

❧ taken

13

fails: native, plain

“nas vossas invenções”

In Brazilian Portuguese 'invenções' means gadgets, or worse, fabrications ('isso é invenção'). Said of God this is almost comic, even unsettling. 'Desígnios' is a plain liturgical word for God's contrived works and plans.

proposede nos vossos desígnios me exercitarei.

not taken — desígnios is consílium's word (D33); the row serves six places; flagged for Gustavo.

14

fails: native

“que Deus é grande como o nosso Deus?”

'Que Deus é grande' is first heard as the exclamation 'how great God is!', and the question only comes clear at the end. 'Qual' makes it a question from the first word.

proposedqual Deus é grande como o nosso Deus?

❧ taken

17

fails: native

“Viram-vos as águas, Deus,”

A bare 'Deus' as a vocative in mid-line sounds abrupt. Portuguese prayer uses 'ó Deus', which also gives the pause a syllable to rest on.

proposedViram-vos as águas, ó Deus, viram-vos as águas:

not taken — The glossary rule: bare vocative, no ó.

18

fails: native, plain

“Muito estrondo das águas”

'Muito estrondo' is colloquial ('what a lot of noise'), which is far from the Latin 'multitudo'. 'Grande' keeps the sense of mass and sounds noble.

proposedGrande estrondo das águas:

❧ taken

“as nuvens fizeram ouvir a voz”

'a voz' with no possessive sounds clipped. The Latin 'vocem dederunt' is also more direct.

proposedas nuvens deram a sua voz.

not taken — The vocem dare row (17:14, 45:7).

20

fails: native

“em muitas águas”

'em muitas águas' sounds counted, like several waters. With the article it becomes the vast waters, which is the image.

proposedNo mar o vosso caminho, e as vossas veredas nas muitas águas:

not taken — aquæ multæ without the article in 28:3 and 17:17; the phrase kept one.

The psalm reads faithfully and mostly has a sober, prayable rhythm. The theophany verses (17-21) sound strongest. What most betrays it as a translation is a handful of word choices that carry the wrong modern overtones: 'exercitar-se', 'invenções', 'a direita', 'muito estrondo'. There are also a few spots where word order makes the ear misparse the line on first hearing (76:12, 76:14).

minor

“e examinava o meu espírito” et scopébam spíritum meum

scopebam is concrete: 'I swept' (from scopae, broom; Douay 'I swept my spirit'). 'Examinava' turns the image into an abstraction and follows a modern gloss rather than the Latin word.

proposede varria o meu espírito

kept as an option under et scopébam spíritum meum — The -ēbam imperfect belongs to scopo, -ere (= σκοπέω, 'search'), which L&S gives for this verse; 'to sweep' would be scopábam; the Greek ἔσκαλλεν searches. 'varria' stays option 3 for Gustavo.

18

minor

“Grande estrondo das águas” Multitúdo sónitus aquárum

multitudo is a multitude/abundance, not 'great'; 'grande estrondo' flattens the noun into an adjective of intensity.

proposedMultidão do som das águas

kept as an option under Multitúdo sónitus aquárum — 'multidão' of a sound is heard as a crowd, and draft 1's 'Muito' was heard as colloquial; of a mass noun the multitude is its greatness. The row's 'A multidão do estrondo das águas' is option 3; sónitus stays 'estrondo' (9:7b).
