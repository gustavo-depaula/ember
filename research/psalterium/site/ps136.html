3a

fails: sayable, native

“Porque ali os que nos levaram cativos nos pediram”

The subject clause wedged before the verb makes a long, top-heavy first colon, and the two clitics 'nos levaram ... nos pediram' collide. The ear waits too long for the verb and loses the thread before the mediant.

proposedPorque ali nos pediram os que nos levaram cativos * palavras de canções:

❧ taken

8

fails: native

“Filha da Babilônia, miserável:”

In current Brazilian speech 'miserável' leans toward 'scoundrel' or 'stingy', a colloquial insult, rather than 'wretched, doomed'. 'Mísera' would be exact but its stress falls too early for the mediant. 'Infeliz' is plain, stressed on the last syllable, and carries the pity and doom of mísera.

proposedFilha da Babilônia, infeliz: * bem-aventurado quem te retribuir a tua retribuição, que nos retribuíste.

❧ taken

9

fails: plain, sayable

“quem agarrar, * e arremessar”

'Agarrar' sounds grabby and colloquial, and it dangles without an object at the mediant. The comma before 'e' adds a second, unnatural halt right after the chant pause. 'Tomar' is the plainer, nobler verb for tenébit, and it carries the suspended object just as well.

proposedBem-aventurado quem tomar * e arremessar os teus pequeninos contra a rocha.

❧ taken

The psalm reads plainly and gravely, in a native lament voice. Verses 4 to 7 are especially clean, with good stresses at every mark, and the retribuir/retribuição chain in verse 8 keeps the Latin's hammering repetition without sounding strained. The weak spots are a top-heavy word order in 3a and two word choices (miserável, agarrar) that drop into colloquial speech.

1

minor

“lembrando-nos de Sião” cum recordarémur Sion

Temporal cum-clause with imperfect subjunctive ("when we remembered") is flattened into a participial gerund; defensible, but the finite imperfect is closer and keeps the time frame explicit.

proposedquando nos lembrávamos de Sião

kept as an option under cum recordarémur Sion — Clause into gerund is grammar (D2); the time frame is carried by 'sentamos e choramos'; the gerund keeps the Latin colon length (7 against 8, the clause 10) and is MS1932's wording.

6b

minor

“Se eu não puser Jerusalém” Si non proposúero Jerúsalem

proponere is 'set before / put first', not simply 'put'; the prefix pro- carries the sense of preferring, which 'puser' drops (though 'no princípio' partly restores it).

proposedSe eu não propuser Jerusalém

kept as an option under Si non proposúero Jerúsalem, in princípio lætítiæ meæ — 'propor' is heard as 'to propose', a wrong first hearing; the before is carried by 'no princípio', as he concedes; 'antepuser' is the other option.

A faithful, close rendering that keeps the Latin's concrete images (organa, fauces, retributio triad, parvulos ad petram) and its tenses and moods; no additions or omissions. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. Only two minor points of nuance: the temporal cum-clause in v.1 and the force of proposuero in v.6b.

Verse by verse

1

illic → ali (row). The Latin's comma and colon kept. Sion → Sião. The whole of 136:1 is the Offertory of Pentecost XX and Thursday in Passion Week in the form 'dum recordarémur tui, Sion' — the Missal's wording, not the psalm's; the psalm keeps its own.

2

salix → salgueiro (every version). 'in médio ejus' → 'no meio dela': Babylon (feminine), as the Latin's ejus after Babylónis. The ambiguity reader could attach 'dela' to Sião, the last name heard. So can the Latin: Sion is indeclinable and 'ejus' can point to either; the Latin leaves it to the context, and so does this.

3a

Latin order changed only to put the subject before the verb (D2). 'captívos duxérunt' → 'levaram cativos' (the captívitas row's family). The first half is the verse of Responsory 6 of Easter IV (Pasc4-0).

3b

hymnus → hino, cantáre → cantar, cánticum → cântico (rows). 'Cantai-nos': the captors address the exiles, plural humans → vós. 'Hymnum cantáte nobis * de cánticis Sion' is the Monastic Vespers antiphon (Psalmi major, feria 4) and the respond of Responsory 6 of Easter IV: the words 'Cantai-nos um hino * dos cânticos de Sião' read alone.

4

'Como cantaremos' — the Latin's plain future (the CNBB's 'Como havemos de cantar' is the option a stylist may want; not taken, the future is what the Latin says). The respond of Responsory 6 of Easter IV.

6a

memínisse → lembrar-se (row). 'se eu não me lembrar de ti' = MS1932.

6b

The Latin's comma after Jerúsalem is dropped: in Portuguese it would cut the verb from its complement (puser … no princípio).

7a

Memor esto → 'Lembrai-vos' (memor esse row; 73:2 'Lembrai-vos, Senhor, da vossa congregação'). 'in die Jerúsalem' → 'no dia de Jerusalém', unexplained, as the Latin (MS1932's '(da ruína)' is his gloss). Edom as a name, unchanged.

8

The curse is the Latin's and is translated as it stands: beátus → bem-aventurado (D19), not softened to 'feliz'. The daughter is 'tu' (rule 3). The second colon is long (the Latin's is 26 syllables); the triple root is the reason.

9

Translated faithfully, as the brief requires for this verse: 'Bem-aventurado' (D19), 'tomar' for tenébit (row), 'arremessar … contra a rocha' for allídet ad petram, 'os teus pequeninos' (row) — the Latin's words, no gloss, no softening, no added violence. v2: no comma after the mediant (the stylist). The ambiguity reader listed 'arremessar' as unknown; it is kept — the everyday word of the throw in Brazil ('arremesso'), and 'lançar' (option) is weaker than 'dash'.
