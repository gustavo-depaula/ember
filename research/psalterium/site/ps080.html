minor

“e um juízo do Deus de Jacó” et judícium Deo Jacob

Latin has a plain dative 'Deo Jacob' parallel to 'in Israël'; the genitive 'do Deus de Jacó' settles it as possessive (the God's ordinance) where the Latin can also mean an ordinance for/belonging to God. Defensible (possessive dative), but a dative rendering would keep the Latin's shape.

proposede um juízo para o Deus de Jacó

not taken — as in v1: the possessive dative, parallel to *in Israël*, is the reading the ambiguity reader heard; *para o* is heard as 'in God's favour'. Option 2.

A close, faithful rendering: tenses (perfect 'cibávit', future 'ibunt', pluperfect subjunctives in 14-15), the Septuagintal images (cóphino, aqua contradictiónis, deus recens, ádipe fruménti) and the ambiguous subject of 'cum exíret' are all kept. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. Only a minor point on the dative in 80:5.

5

minor

“e juízo do Deus de Jacó” et judícium Deo Jacob

Deo is dative (a judgment for/belonging to the God of Jacob, parallel to 'in Israël'); the genitive 'do' makes God the author of the judgment, a narrower reading than the Latin's dative.

proposedPorque é decreto em Israel: * e juízo para o Deus de Jacó.

not taken — as in v1: the possessive dative, parallel to *in Israël*, is the reading the ambiguity reader heard; *para o* is heard as 'in God's favour'. Option 2.

A faithful, close rendering: tenses (pluperfect subjunctives in 14-15, futures in 9 and 13, perfects in 17), the Septuagintal images (cophinus, aqua contradictionis, deus recens, adeps frumenti) and the pointing marks all match the Latin. Only two minor points: the agent noun adjutor rendered as an abstraction, and the dative Deo Jacob in v. 5 rendered as a genitive.

Verse by verse

2

*jubiláre → aclamar* (D43); *Deo Jacob → ao Deus de Jacó* (74:10). The people are addressed as *vós* (plural humans).

3

*psaltérium → saltério*, *cíthara → cítara* (open rows, 32:2). The verse ends on the proparoxytone *cítara*, as the Latin ends on *cíthara*.

4

*buccináte … tuba → Tocai a trombeta*: the verb 'blow the trumpet' and its instrument are said by one Portuguese verb and its object; *tuba → trombeta* (46:6). *solemnitátis vestræ → da vossa solenidade* (73:4 *solenidade*): the Latin's *vestræ* (the Greek has 'our'), said to the people.

5

*præcéptum → decreto* (D19; this verse is in its list). *in Israël* → *em Israel*, the Latin's preposition (the Greek has a dative). The subject of *est* (the feast of 80:4) is left unspoken, as in the Latin. v2: *Porque é decreto … e juízo* without articles, as the Latin (stylist: the hiatus *é um*).

6

*cum exíret* has no named subject: God (the Hebrew's reading) or Joseph (the people); *quando saía* keeps it open. *linguam, quam non nóverat, audívit*: the subject again unnamed; the Latin's object-first order is put in Portuguese order. *nóverat* (pluperfect of *nosco*, 'had come to know' = knew) → *não conhecia*.

7

*ejus … ejus → as suas … as suas*: Joseph's, as the Latin.

8

God speaks from here to the end, and speaks to Israel as *tu* (D17). *invocáre → invocar*, *liberáre → livrar*, *exaudíre → escutar* (D3), *probáre → provar* (16:3, 65:10). v2: *eu te escutei … eu te provei* with the pronoun before the verb, as in the first colon (stylist: *escutei-te … provei-te* stiff after *me invocaste … te livrei*); the subject *eu* named to carry the proclisis (rule 2).

9

*Audi, pópulus meus → Ouve, meu povo*, word for word 49:7's opening. The DO verse joins the Vulgate's 9 and 10 into one prayed verse; the long second colon is the Latin's. *aliénus → estranho* (row; 43:21 *um deus estranho*).

11

*Ego enim sum → Pois eu sou*. *Dóminus Deus tuus → o Senhor, teu Deus* (the comma for the apposition).

12

*inténdere → atender* (D3; 39:2, 76:2 *e ele me atendeu*).

13

*dimíttere* (let go) *→ deixar ir* (row, from the Nunc dimittis). *cordis eórum* singular *→ do seu coração*.

14

The Latin's second *si* stands inside its colon (*Israël si*); Portuguese puts it first. *ambuláre → andar* (row), *via → caminho*.

15

The counterfactual (*humiliássem … misíssem*) → *teria humilhado … teria lançado*, answering *tivesse ouvido* of 80:14. *tribulántes eos → os que os atribulam* (row *tribuláre*). The Latin order *super tribulántes … manum meam* is turned so that the colon ends on the verb phrase's natural close.

16

*mentíri* + dative *→ mentir a* (17:46). *in sǽcula → pelos séculos* (D43; this verse is in its list). *tempus eórum* is left as open as the Latin: the enemies' time, most readers say.

17

The speaker returns to the third person (*cibávit*), as the Latin has it, against the Hebrew. *petra → rocha* (row); definite here, *da rocha*, because it is the rock of the desert and Portuguese says *de uma rocha* only of any rock. *saturáre → saciar* (row), with *de*, as 16:14c *saciados de filhos*. *cibáre → alimentar*.
