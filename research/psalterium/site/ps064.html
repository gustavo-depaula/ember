The gerund makes the sprouting adverbial and leaves the verb without a clear subject. In the Latin, 'gérminans' is the subject (what sprouts will rejoice). 'O que germina' would end on a proparoxytone, but 'o que brota' keeps the cadence.

❧ taken — as *o que germina*, keeping the Latin root; *o que brota* is an option

“da vossa benignidade”

'Benignidade' is a long Latinate word that clogs the end of the mediant colon. 'Bondade' is the plainer faithful word and has a clean paroxytone cadence.

proposedBendireis a coroa do ano da vossa bondade: * e os vossos campos serão cheios de fartura.

13

fails: native, plain

“Engordarão as belezas do deserto”

In Brazilian ears 'engordar' belongs to livestock and diets. Opening a verse with it sounds comic, not like fruitfulness. 'Fartas' keeps the image of richness and fatness in concrete words and picks up 'fartura' from v.12.

proposedFicarão fartas as belezas do deserto: * e as colinas serão cingidas de exultação.

kept as an option under Pinguéscent — fat is the Latin's image (rule 5); *fartos* is satiety and echoes *fartura* (ubértas). With the subject now *os lugares formosos*, the farming sense of *engordar* is clearer

fails: no rhyme, sayable, native

“Os carneiros das ovelhas estão vestidos”

With subject-first order the verse opens on the odd genitive and then deflates on 'estão vestidos'. Putting the verb first makes the image land and sounds like Portuguese poetry, not a gloss.

proposedVestidos estão os carneiros das ovelhas, e os vales abundarão em trigo:

❧ taken — as plain verb–subject *Estão vestidos*, avoiding the inversion

“clamarão, pois entoarão um hino”

'Abundarão / clamarão / entoarão' makes three -arão endings in one verse. The two in the final colon jingle audibly.

proposedclamarão, pois hão de cantar um hino.

The psalm is mostly sayable and keeps the Latin's concrete harvest imagery with dignity. The first half (vv.2-6) sounds especially clean and prayerful. The agricultural close is where the ear trips: 'engordarão', the subject-first sheep line and the -arão rhyming make the ending, which should be the most jubilant part, sound flat or faintly comic.

Verse by verse

2

The Lauds antiphon divides after *hymnus* (*Te decet hymnus, * Deus, in Sion*): *A vós convém o hino, * Deus, em Sião* divides the same way. Bare vocative *Deus*, as the finished psalms. The blind reader half-heard *voto* as a ballot; *pagar os votos* is the psalter's established phrase (21:26, 49:14, 60:9), kept.

4

*super nos → sobre nós* (the Latin's preposition; 12:4b's *contra* is *advérsus*). *impíetas → impiedade*, as 5:11b and 31:5b. *vós sereis propício às nossas impiedades* puts the verb before its dative so the verse ends on a paroxytone. **The blind reader heard *propício às nossas impiedades* as God favouring our sins** — the second reader to hear the row's adjective that way (24:11). Kept for the row; the main session should look at *propitiári* before 77:38, 78:9.

5

*aquele que* rather than MS1932's *o que*, which a Brazilian ear can take as 'what'. *inhabitáre → habitar*. *átrio* kept (the row), though the blind reader did not know it (the third). The first colon is +8 against the Latin in v2 (*Bem-aventurado*, D19, and *tomastes para vós*); one breath still.

5b

*os bens da vossa casa* as 26:13 *os bens do Senhor*. *mirábilis → maravilhoso* (row), *æquitas → equidade* (36:37a, 51:5).

7

*profúndum maris → o fundo do mar* (67:23 has the same phrase). *sonum* is in apposition to *profúndum*, and stays so: *o fundo do mar, o som das suas ondas* (MS1932 adds a verb in a gloss). *sonus → som*, as 18:5. *conturbáre → perturbar* (row).

8

*Turbabúntur gentes → As nações se perturbarão*: *turbáre → perturbar*, the middle as 47:6 *perturbaram-se*. The first colon runs long (+3 against the Latin in v2) because Portuguese needs *os que* for *qui*; it is one breath. *éxitus … véspere*: the blind reader found *as saídas da manhã* opaque (exits, departures). It is the Latin's own crux, left concrete and unresolved on purpose (decision `exitus`).

10

*visitáre → visitar* (row).

10b

*parásti … præparátio* and 64:7 *Prǽparans*: one root, *preparar … preparação*, as the Latin. *ejus* (the Greek has σου) → *a sua*, whose antecedent is left as open as the Latin's. *cibus → alimento*. *illórum → deles*, to keep it off the river. First colon +5 (accepted: *preparastes-lhes o alimento* would save one syllable but *lhes* lands on the river's *águas*; *o seu alimento* could be the river's). The blind reader found *deles* without antecedent and *a sua preparação* opaque; the Latin's *illórum* and *ejus* are just as unanchored (and the Greek has σου), so neither is resolved.

12

*Benedíces → Bendireis* (the *benedícere* row), *corónæ → a coroa* (the crown kept, as the *coronáre* row), *benignitátis → benignidade* (D44 names this verse), *ubertáte → fartura* (the *ubértas* row names this verse).

13
