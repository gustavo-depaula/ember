The audit trail

1source

Latin = DO Psalm232.txt. It is the Clementine Vulgate of Luke 1:46–55, not the Gallican, and it matches the Bolls VULG text word for word (consult/ps232/bolls-VULG-42-1.json). The Diurnal Monástico 1962 prints the same Latin (Sunday Vespers, OCR p. 199). Verse ids are Luke's, as in 231 and 233.

2draft

parallels.py builds only Latin + pt-PT for a canticle, so ps232/show_parallels.py (adapted from ps231) builds consult/parallels/ps232.md. Its sources are: Bolls VULG, TISCH and DRB of Luke 1 (copied from consult/ps231); Challoner's notes from drbo.org; MS1932 from the text layer of the 1932 PDF (pdftotext, consult/ps232/ms1932-luke1-46.txt); and the Diurnal Monástico 1962, Sunday Vespers, retyped from its OCR (consult/ps232/dm1962-magnificat.txt). DO's Portuguese of this canticle is the Diurnal's text. The CNBB LH (Vespers) and Lectionary wordings were fetched and are recorded in circulation.md. Copied from the glossary and finished texts: magnificáre (33:4), exsultávit (223 2:1), salutáris of God (94:1), respícere + humílitas (30:8), ancílla (85:16), beátus (D19), magna (magnália row), sanctum nomen (110:9b), progénies (48:12), timéntibus (30:20), in brácchio (76:15), dispérgere (88:11), supérbus (118:21), sedes → trono, húmiles (D27), esuriéntes + bonis (106:9), suscípere (D19), puer → servo (231, 104:42), recordári (97:3), patres nostri (231), semen (D32), in sǽcula (D43). 3checks

The shared checks.py crashes on this Latin: "Magníficat + *" leaves an empty colon between the cross and the mediant, and measure() indexes the last word of an empty list. It was not edited; ps232/checks232.py is a local copy whose only change is that an empty colon measures 0. Hard checks pass: verse ids and marks (+ and * in 1:46) match the Latin. Soft flags: 1:47 second colon -3 (meu Salvador is shorter than salutári meo); 1:48 second colon +4 (desde agora, bem-aventurada); 1:51 second colon +5 (no pensamento do seu coração keeps mente cordis sui whole); 1:55 second colon +4 (descendência, pelos séculos). The finals of 1:46 and 1:47 rhyme (Senhor / Salvador): it is the pair both Brazilian books have, and the words are the Latin's; kept, flagged for the stylist.

1:51

minor

“Com o seu braço agiu com potência” Fecit poténtiam in brácchio suo

'Fecit potentiam' has potentia as the direct object of fecit (he wrought might, did a mighty deed); 'agiu com potência' turns the object into a manner adverbial, so the might becomes how he acted rather than what he made. Defensible, but the transitive construction can be kept.

proposedCom o seu braço fez potência

kept as an option under Fecit poténtiam in brácchio suo — Row 135 agir com poder was held against the same request at 107:14; *fazer potência* is not Portuguese. It is recorded as an option.

A close, faithful rendering of the Clementine Magnificat: tenses, persons and images are kept (exsultavit as past, dimisit inanes, mente cordis sui, the Abraham/semini construction left as open as the Latin). Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (+ and * in 1:46, * alone elsewhere). The only point worth a look is 1:51, where 'fecit potentiam' is softened from an object into a manner phrase.

5stylist

claude-opus-5-5, fresh context, read latin.json. Four remarks: two taken, two held as options. Worst line 1:51, best 1:53.

claude-opus-5-5 (fresh context, with latin.json) · 2026-09-23 · critic/v1.stylist.json · read prayed.vos.json

1:48

fails: sayable

“pois eis que desde agora todas as gerações me chamarão bem-aventurada”

The second colon is long for one breath, and 'pois eis que' puts three unstressed particles together before the line gets going. It comes out as 'poiseisque'. The comma-less 'desde agora' also runs into 'todas'.

proposedeis que, desde agora, todas as gerações me chamarão bem-aventurada (if 'enim' must show, keep it: pois eis que, desde agora, todas as gerações me chamarão bem-aventurada, with the commas giving the voice a place to rest)

❧ taken — Commas around desde agora; pois kept for enim, as the stylist allowed.

1:49

fails: native, plain

“Porque me fez grandes coisas quem é poderoso”

'me fez grandes coisas' can be heard as 'made me into great things'. Leaving the subject 'quem é poderoso' to the end makes the listener hold an unresolved clause, which sounds like a translation. The object pronoun needs a preposition to be clear.

proposedPorque fez por mim grandes coisas aquele que é poderoso: * e santo é o seu nome.

❧ taken — fez por mim taken. aquele que refused: +2 syllables, and quem é poderoso was heard rightly by the ambiguity reader. The subject stays last, as in the Latin.

1:51

fails: native, plain, sayable

“Com o seu braço agiu com potência”

'com … com' repeats within five syllables. 'agiu com potência' is flat and technical: 'potência' belongs to physics, not prayer, and 'agiu' is weak for 'fecit'. The line also has no punctuation before the asterisk, where every other verse pauses, so it runs straight into 'dispersou'.

proposedCom o seu braço mostrou o seu poder: * dispersou os soberbos no pensamento do seu coração.

kept as an option under Fecit poténtiam in brácchio suo — The pause is taken (a colon before *). potência is the poténtia row, refused to stylists at 88:13 and 216; mostrou adds a verb and spends virtus's poder.

1:55

fails: sayable, native

“a Abraão e à sua descendência”

'a Abraão' makes an a-A hiatus right at the start of the colon, and 'e à' adds another run of vowels. The mouth stumbles on 'a-a-bra-ão e-à'. In the Latin, Abraham stands in apposition to 'patres', and the Portuguese does not need the preposition to show this.

proposedComo falou aos nossos pais, * Abraão, e à sua descendência pelos séculos.

kept as an option under Ábraham, et sémini ejus — The dative pair is the Latin's likelier construction, and the hiatus has precedent (104:42; 231 1:73).

The Magnificat reads mostly clean and dignified. 1:52 and 1:53 are especially good: tight parallel cola with natural Portuguese word order and firm cadences. The weak spots are 1:49 and 1:51, where Latin word order and a technical noun ('potência') make it sound translated. 1:48 and 1:55 need small fixes for breath and vowel clashes.

worst line1:51

best line1:53

1:49

minor

“fez por mim grandes coisas” fecit mihi magna

mihi is a plain dative (to/for me, as recipient); 'por mim' can be heard as 'through me' or 'in my place', which adds a nuance the Latin does not carry.

proposedPorque fez em mim grandes coisas quem é poderoso: * e santo é o seu nome.

❧ taken — Only this preposition changes, to the MS1932 and Diurnal wording; checks re-run and they pass.

A faithful rendering of the Clementine Magnificat: tenses, persons, the concrete images (arm, seat, the hungry filled, the rich sent away empty) and the ambiguous apposition of Abraham in 1:55 are all kept. The pointing marks (+ and * in 1:46, a single * elsewhere) match the Latin in every verse. The only point worth touching is the preposition for mihi in 1:49.

Verse by verse

1:46

DO keys the canticle by Luke's own verses (1:46–1:55), as 231 and 233. The '+' (where the sign of the cross is made; not a flex, see 233) is kept directly before the mediant, as in the Latin. The heading line (Canticum B. Mariæ Virginis * Luc. 1:46-55) is not translated (D7). ánima mea → 'a minha alma' (the row).

1:47

salutári meo → 'meu Salvador': the salutáris-of-God row (Greek σωτῆρι, a person), as 94:1 'a Deus, nosso Salvador'. D6's 'salvação' is for the neuter salutáre. spíritus meus → 'o meu espírito' (76:4, 142:7a). The colon after 'exultou' in the Latin is not reproduced: Portuguese syntax runs on into 'em Deus'. Second colon −3 on the Latin; the name 'Salvador' is shorter than salutári meo.

1:48

quia → 'Porque' (the default), in both 1:48 and 1:49, keeping the Latin's repetition. respícere → 'olhar para' (30:8, 101:18). ancílla → 'serva' (the row; 85:16, 115:7a). ecce enim → 'pois eis que' (the ecce and enim rows). ex hoc (ἀπὸ τοῦ νῦν) → 'desde agora', as the formula ex hoc nunc (112:2); the Lectionary's 'Doravante' is one word, but it is literary. beátam → 'bem-aventurada' (D19) against the LH's 'bendita', which is benedícta's word. v2: commas around *desde agora*, as the v1 stylist asked, so that the voice rests after *pois eis que*; *pois* stays for enim.

1:49

'e santo é o seu nome': the copula supplied, as 110:9b 'Santo e terrível é o seu nome'. The LH ends on the same words.

1:50

misericórdia → misericórdia (the LH's 'amor' refused). eum → 'o' (God, third person, as the canticle speaks of him).

1:51

dispersou the soberbos: 'soberbos' is the row (118:21 'Repreendestes os soberbos'); the LH's 'orgulhosos' refused. First colon −2, second +3/+4 on the Latin: the phrase mente cordis sui is kept whole. v2: a colon before the mediant, as the Latin has.

1:52

Derrubou (v2; decision deposuit). exaltáre → 'exaltar' (26:6); húmiles → 'os humildes' (D27, 101:18). The hiatus 'e exaltou' is accepted: there is no natural order that avoids it, and the LH's 'e os humildes exaltou' is an inversion.

1:53

esuriéntes → 'famintos' (106:36, 145:7a); impléri bonis → 'encher de bens', as 106:9 'saciou de bens'. dívites → 'os ricos' (33:11). The two cola mirror each other (verb + complement + noun), as the Latin's chiasm does in its own way; the Lectionary's first colon is the same words.

1:54

Israël → Israel. The commas around 'seu servo' mark the apposition, as 104:42.

1:55

sicut → 'Como' (231, 1:70). locútus est ad → 'falou a'. patres nostros → 'os nossos pais' (21:5, 43:2; 231). Ábraham → 'Abraão' (104:42). semen → 'descendência' (D32). The Latin leaves open whether 'Ábraham, et sémini ejus' depends on 'locútus est' (he spoke to Abraham) or on 'misericórdiæ' in 1:54 (mercy to Abraham). 'a Abraão' after the mediant keeps both readings open. First colon −3 on the Latin.
