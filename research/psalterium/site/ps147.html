minor

“Ele envia à terra o que diz” Qui emíttit elóquium suum terræ

The noun 'eloquium suum' (his utterance, his word) becomes a relative clause 'o que diz'. The sense holds, but the Latin has a concrete noun object parallel to 'sermo ejus', and a noun would be closer.

proposedEle envia à terra a sua fala: * depressa corre a sua palavra.

❧ taken

6

minor

“quem subsistirá?” quis sustinébit?

'Sustinere' means to bear or endure something (here the cold), with an implied object. 'Subsistirá' means to hold out or survive and loses the idea of bearing the cold.

proposeddiante da face do seu frio, quem o suportará?

❧ taken — taken without the pronoun 'o': the Latin leaves the object implied

9

minor

“a todas as nações” omni natióni

The Latin is singular ('to every nation'); the translation makes it plural. The meaning is nearly the same, but the number changes.

proposedNão fez assim a toda nação: * e não lhes manifestou os seus juízos.

❧ taken

The translation is faithful and close. It keeps the Septuagintal images (crystallum as ice, buccellas as morsels, adeps frumenti, spiritus), and the neuter plural 'ea' is handled correctly with 'os'. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse. The only points are three minor ones: 'eloquium' is turned from a noun into a clause, 'subsistirá' is weaker than 'suportará' for 'sustinebit', and the singular 'omni nationi' is made plural.

“o que diz”

'o que diz' is a thin paraphrase for eloquium. It sounds like a relative clause, not a noun. It is flat at the mediant and reads like a gloss beside 'sua palavra' in the second colon.

proposedEle envia à terra a sua fala: * depressa corre a sua palavra.

fails: plain, native

“como bocados”

'bocados' for buccellas is ambiguous in Brazilian ears (bites or chunks), and hail as 'bocados' does not call up a picture. 'migalhas' is the ordinary word for morsels or crumbs and keeps the same image.

proposedLança o seu gelo como migalhas

❧ taken

“quem subsistirá?”

'subsistirá' is bookish and slightly abstract. People say 'resistir' when they mean standing up against cold. The stress still falls on the last syllable.

proposeddiante da face do seu frio, quem resistirá?

kept as an option under quis sustinébit? — the Latinist's 'suportar' taken instead: 'resistir' is opposition, 'sustinére' here is bearing; the sustinére row's verb

9

fails: native

“Não fez assim a todas as nações”

'fazer assim a' is not idiomatic. Brazilian Portuguese says 'fazer assim com'. With 'a', the ear half-hears 'did not do this to all of them' (to only some). 'com' keeps the Latin's wording and sits better in the mouth.

proposedNão fez assim com todas as nações

❧ taken — 'com' taken; the number follows the Latinist (singular); the plural is an option

The psalm is sober and rhythmic. Its short parallel cola suit the tone well, and the run of futures in v. 7 flows nicely. The few weak spots are single word choices ('o que diz', 'bocados', 'subsistirá') and one Latinate inversion in v. 3. These are where the text still sounds translated.

3

minor

“Ele pôs a paz nas tuas fronteiras” Qui pósuit fines tuos pacem

The Latin is a double accusative: he made (set) your borders [to be] peace, i.e. the borders themselves are made peace. 'Pôs a paz nas tuas fronteiras' turns the predicate accusative into a locative ('put peace in your borders'). The sense is close and the rendering is traditional, but the Latin's image (the frontier become peace) is slightly flattened.

proposedEle fez das tuas fronteiras a paz

kept as an option under Qui pósuit fines tuos pacem — his fix is already option 2 of 'fines', which v1 weighed: 'fez das tuas fronteiras a paz' is not a sentence Portuguese says, while the locative keeps both words and the image and is how DRB and MS1932 read it; the gate itself calls the sense close and the rendering traditional

A faithful rendering: tenses (present in 147:3-6, future in 147:7), the Septuagintal 'crystallum ... sicut buccellas' and the neuter 'ea' are kept, and 147:9 preserves the Latin's 'non ... omni nationi' without resolving it. Only 147:3 recasts the double accusative as a locative, a minor point. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse.

'ea' (neuter plural: the snow, mist and ice) → 'os'. liquefácere → derreter (row of 74:4). Soft flag: second colon +4 by the counter, which does not elide; sung, 'seu‿espírito' and 'e‿correrão' close it to about +2. Accepted: nothing can go without losing a word of the Latin. The ambiguity reader found 'os' without a clear antecedent ('gelo' is singular); the Latin's 'ea' is as loose (neuter plural for snow, mist and ice together). Held. The futures are the Latin's.

manifestáre → manifestar (row of 79:2b). The object moved after the verb (natural order, D2); the Latin puts 'judícia sua' first. Soft flag: second colon -3 (the Latin's long 'manifestávit'); accepted. The ambiguity reader heard 'os seus juízos' as God's, rightly. v2 soft flag: first colon -3 ('com toda nação', 9 against the Latin's 12); accepted: nothing is missing, the Latin's words are longer.
