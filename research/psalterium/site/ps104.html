6

major

“Descendência de Abraão, seus servos” Semen Ábraham, servi ejus

'servi ejus' is genitive singular in apposition to Abraham (LXX δούλου αὐτοῦ, 'Abraham his servant'), not a nominative plural addressing the descendants. The plural changes number and referent. (In the second half 'elécti ejus' is plural, LXX ἐκλεκτοὶ αὐτοῦ, so 'seus escolhidos' is right.)

proposedDescendência de Abraão, seu servo: * filhos de Jacó, seus escolhidos.

kept as an option — “seu servo … seu escolhido”, under Semen Ábraham, servi ejus: * fílii Jacob, elécti ejus

9

minor

“Que fez com Abraão” Quod dispósuit ad Ábraham

'dispósuit' means 'appointed/ordained/set out', which is more specific than the colourless 'fez'.

proposedQue dispôs para com Abraão:

kept as an option — “dispôs”, under Quod dispósuit ad Ábraham

minor

“O que o Senhor disse o inflamou” Elóquium Dómini inflammávit eum

The Latin has a noun as subject ('the word/utterance of the Lord'). Turning it into a relative clause is a small paraphrase.

minor

minor

“porque o temor deles pesava sobre eles” quia incúbuit timor eórum super eos

'incúbuit' is perfect ('fell upon / had settled upon'). The imperfect 'pesava' shifts the aspect.

proposedporque o temor deles caíra sobre eles.

not taken as proposed

A faithful, close translation that keeps the Septuagintal oddities, e.g. 'não exacerbou as suas palavras' (v. 28), 'Fez das chuvas deles granizo' (v. 32) and 'mosca canina' (v. 31). The one real fault is v. 6, where 'servi ejus' (Abraham his servant) has become plural 'seus servos'. The other items are minor tightenings. The pointing marks match the Latin in every verse: one mediant each, no flex or second mediant, and v. 18 has its mediant after 'alma'.

Verse by verse

1

D5 (Dai graças), invocáre → invocar, annuntiáre → anunciar (D41), gentes → nações. The Latin's order, 'entre as nações as suas obras', ends on the object.

2

psállere → entoar salmos (D25), the dative 'ei' as 'lhe' in both verbs, as the Latin repeats it.

5

judícia → juízos (D15). v2 (stylist): the comma and 'suas' are dropped, so 'que ele fez' is a plain restrictive clause. The ejus is carried by 'ele fez' (grammar, D2).

7

The copula 'estão' is supplied in the second colon (D2); 'Ipse' → 'Ele'. univérsus → 'toda' (row).

8

in sǽculum → para sempre (D23); testaméntum → aliança (row); mandáre with a thing as object → ordenar (the mandáre row, 32:9, 118:138). The first Saturday Matins antiphon ('Memor fuit in sǽculum * testaménti sui Dóminus Deus noster') reads 'Lembrou-se para sempre da sua aliança o Senhor, nosso Deus'.

10

statúere → firmar (row); præcéptum → decreto (D19). The feminine 'a' takes up 'palavra' / 'aliança' (the Latin's neuter 'illud' points to verbum). ætérnus → eterna.

12

íncola → forasteiro (D39). 'ejus' (the land) → 'nela'.

13

gens → nação (42:1 keeps 'gente' out); 'de nação em nação' is the Portuguese idiom of 'de gente in gentem'.

14

corrípere → castigar (D21); nocére → fazer mal (88:23 row). 'pro eis' → 'por causa deles', since 'por eles' would be heard as agent.

15

malignári → 'fazer mal a'. The row's absolute 'fazer o mal' (36:8) loses its article when there is a dative object, which is the Portuguese idiom (stylist v1). The v1 chiasm with a fronted dative was refused as a lurch. The Latin's order (in prophétis … nolíte malignári) gives way to the ear (D2).

17

servus → servo, so that Joseph, Moses (104:26), Abraham (104:42) and the seed (104:6) carry one word, as they do in Latin. 'escravo' (MS1932) is the idiom for a man sold, but it would break that repetition.

18

One mediant only, as in the Latin; the first colon is long, as the Latin's is. 'a sua palavra' leaves open whose word it is (Joseph's interpretation or God's), as 'verbum ejus' does.

19

DO joins Vulgate 104:19b–20 in one prayed verse (no 104:20 id). misit rex → 'mandou o rei' (v2, stylist): the verb comes first as in the Latin, and 'mandar' is the plain verb for a king who sends word without a stated object. v1's 'o rei enviou' left 'enviou' hanging (the stylist and the ambiguity reader). misit is 'enviou' at 104:17, 26, 28, where it has an object. There is no clash with mandáre, which is 'ordenou' at 104:8. dimíttere (let go) → deixar ir (row).

21

constitúere → estabelecer (row); posséssio (goods) → bens, as 77:48 v2.

22

erudíre → instruir (row); docére → ensinar (row); senes → anciãos (row). 'como a si mesmo' keeps the Latin's open reference.

23

áccola (πάροικος, the Greek of íncola) → forasteiro, by D39's merge. Cham → Cam (77:51).

24

augére → aumentar; veheménter → muito (row).

26

'e' is supplied before Aarão. Without it the second name is heard as an apposition to Moses (grammar, D2). Móyses, Aaron → Moisés, Aarão (row).

29

convértere in → converter em (row); the Latin's order is changed to 77:44's ('converteu em sangue os rios deles') so that the two tellings of the plague sound alike. The rhyme flag on "deles … deles" (104:29, 35, 38, and 29/30) is the Latin's own eórum repeated; it is accepted, as in Ps 77.

30

penetrália → aposentos (inner rooms); MS1932's 'câmaras' is heard as a legislature in Brazil. v2 (stylist): 'ipsórum' → 'seus próprios' before the noun, where the emphasis sits in Portuguese; 'próprios reis deles' piled up. It also removes one 'deles' rhyme against 104:29.

31

cœnomyía → a mosca canina (77:45 row); cínifes (σκνῖπες, gnats) → mosquitos (MS1932); fines → confins (row).

33

lignum → árvore (row), the collective singular made plural (grammar). contérere → quebrar, as 104:16.

34

'cujus non erat númerus' → 'que não tinha número': the number kept (D41 kept 'número' in 'além de todo número').

35

fænum → erva (the row's split: here grass is eaten growing, not dried as hay). The repeated 'comeu' is the Latin's.

36

Identical to 77:51 wherever the Latin is identical: 'E feriu todo primogênito … as primícias de todo o seu trabalho' (rule 6).

37

infírmus → enfermo (row).

39

protéctio → proteção (row); 'per noctem' → 'durante a noite' (91:3). lucére is 'to give light', not illumináre ('iluminar').

40
