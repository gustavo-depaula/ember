The audit trail

1draft

Psalm-level draft from the parallels (Latin, LXX, Hebrew, DRB, MS1932, DM1962) and the glossary. Liturgical use checked by grep (the Tract of Passion Sunday, the Vespers antiphon, St Bernard's reading of 128:3) and the CNBB wording fetched; see circulation.md. Hard readings: 'étenim' (not causal here), 'fabricavérunt' without an object, 'concídit' present or perfect, 'fænum' living grass, and who speaks in 128:8b.

2checks

Hard checks pass. One soft flag, twice: 128:1 and 128:2 first colon +4 syllables (19 against 15). Accepted: 'sæpe' (2) can only be 'muitas vezes' (4), and 'desde a minha juventude' is the Latin's words; dropping 'minha' or taking the CNBB's 'desde jovem' would change the text for the count. The antiphon use (the first colon alone) is not harmed by the length.

3

minor

“trabalharam os pecadores” fabricavérunt peccatóres

fabricare is to build, forge or work a material (LXX etektainon, the smith's or builder's work). 'Trabalharam' is generic and loses the concrete image of craftsmen working on the back like an anvil or a building site.

proposedSobre as minhas costas fabricaram os pecadores: * prolongaram a sua iniquidade.

kept as an option under fabricavérunt peccatóres — The fault is taken (with the stylist's 'forjaram'); the cognate itself refused — without an object it is not Portuguese, and it was heard as factories at 73:16.

The translation is faithful throughout: tenses (perfect concidit, exaruit, benediximus), jussive subjunctives (confundantur, convertantur, fiant), the Septuagintal 'non potuerunt mihi' and the concrete images (cervices, fænum tectorum, sinum) are all kept. The only point is the generic 'trabalharam' for fabricaverunt. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse.

3

fails: concrete, native

“Sobre as minhas costas trabalharam os pecadores”

'trabalharam sobre as minhas costas' is flat and vague in Portuguese. It sounds like the sinners were simply 'working on my back', and the image of the Latin 'fabricavérunt' (wrought, forged, hammered as on an anvil) is lost to a generic verb.

proposedSobre as minhas costas forjaram os pecadores: * prolongaram a sua iniquidade.

❧ taken

4

fails: sayable

“O Senhor justo cortou os pescoços dos pecadores”

'O Senhor justo' said in one run can sound like a tag ('the just Lord' as a title) and slightly stiff. 'os pescoços' in the plural, right after 'cortou', lands heavily in the mouth: 'cortou os pescoços dos pecadores' piles up o/os/dos. It is still acceptable. The comma gives a breath and keeps the Latin's predicate adjective reading.

proposedO Senhor, que é justo, cortou os pescoços dos pecadores: * sejam envergonhados e voltem para trás todos os que odeiam Sião.

❧ taken

7

fails: native, plain, concrete

“Da qual não encheu a sua mão o que ceifa”

'Da qual' at the head of a new verse is translationese: a dangling relative that the ear cannot tie back across the full stop of the chant. 'o que ceifa' wavers between 'that which' and 'he who'. 'a sua mão' has a redundant possessive.

proposedDela não encheu a mão quem ceifa,

❧ taken

“nem o seu seio o que recolhe os feixes”

'seio' in current Brazilian Portuguese means the breast first. The Latin 'sinus' is the fold of the garment where gleanings are gathered, and a choir will hear the wrong body part. 'regaço' is concrete, current and exact.

proposednem o regaço quem recolhe os feixes.

❧ taken

8

fails: sayable

“E não disseram os que passavam: A bênção do Senhor sobre vós:”

The first colon is long and carries a quotation inside it. Without a verb the blessing formula 'A bênção do Senhor sobre vós' is slightly clipped in speech, but it is faithful and it is liturgically familiar. The only real trip is the doubled colon mark (quotation colon plus mediant). Punctuating the greeting as a direct quote helps the reader breathe.

proposedE não disseram os que passavam: «A bênção do Senhor sobre vós»; * «nós vos bendissemos em nome do Senhor».

not taken — This psalter sets speech after a colon with a capital and no quotation marks (4:6); and quoting the second colon would decide who speaks it, which the Latin leaves open (decision benediximus).

A plain, sober rendering that mostly sounds like spoken Portuguese and keeps the Latin's repetitions (the doubled 'Muitas vezes me combateram', the paired 'pecadores'). Cadences fall well throughout. The weak points are the harvest verse, where 'Da qual' and 'seio' make it read like a gloss, and the generic 'trabalharam', which drains the forging image from verse 3.

Nothing to correct in any verse.

The translation follows the Latin closely in every verse: the Septuagintal 'supra dorsum meum fabricaverunt' is kept concretely as 'forjaram', 'concidit cervices' keeps its image, 'fænum tectorum' and the reaper/binder images are intact, and 'benediximus' is correctly rendered in the perfect ('bendissemos'). 'Mas' for 'etenim' in v. 2 and the omission of the possessives 'suam/suum' in v. 7 are within Portuguese idiom and do not alter sense. Pointing marks match the Latin in number and order throughout.

Verse by verse

1

Per D7 the titulus 'Canticum graduum' is not reproduced (Matos Soares prints 'Cântico gradual'). 'juventus → juventude' as in 42:4 and 24:7 (MS1932 'mocidade'; the CNBB 'desde jovem'). 'sæpe → muitas vezes' as in 105:42. The first colon is sung alone as a Vespers antiphon (Psalmi major, Daya3 Vespera and Monastic Vespera feria 2) and reads alone.

2

Identical to 128:1's first colon, word for word, as the Latin repeats. 'non potuérunt mihi' (οὐκ ἠδυνήθησάν μοι) → 'não puderam comigo': the Portuguese idiom 'poder com alguém' is exactly 'to prevail over', and keeps the Latin's bare verb with a pronoun; MS1932's 'prevaleceram contra mim' supplies a stronger verb.

3

'prolongáre → prolongar', as 119:5 'se prolongou' (MS1932 'prolongaram a sua iniquidade'). The Passion Sunday Tract has the plural 'iniquitátes suas'; the psalm's singular is followed. 'os pecadores' after the verb, as in the Latin, puts a paroxytone at the mediant.

4

The second colon is DO's verse 5 run in ('(5)' not reproduced). 'confundántur … convertántur retrórsum' → 'sejam envergonhados e voltem para trás': D15's 'confúndi → ser envergonhado' and the glossary row 'retrórsum', which names 128:5 as a place to copy 34:4b ('Voltem para trás, e sejam envergonhados'); here the Latin's order is confundántur first, so the Portuguese follows it. 'odérunt' → present 'odeiam' (the odísse row: a perfect of a state). 'Sion → Sião'. The colon is long (≈22 syllables against the Latin's 20), as the Latin is. The ambiguity reader heard 'cortou os pescoços' as beheading first, and the breaking of pride (the stiff neck) third; the Latin 'concídit cervíces' (cut to pieces the necks) is as violent, and the image is kept.

6

'Fiant sicut' → 'Sejam como' (MS1932, CNBB 'Sejam eles como'). 'exáruit' → 'secou' (arescere → secar, as 36:2, 101:12), kept a perfect and kept at the end of the colon where the Latin puts it. The verse ends in a colon, as the Latin, because 128:7 continues the sentence.

7

'métere → ceifar' and 'manípulus → feixe' as 125:5–6b. 'et' after a negative → 'nem'. The build is decision relclause; the word for sinus is decision sinum.

8

'qui præteríbant' → 'os que passavam' (imperfect; MS1932). The greeting is not set in quotation marks; a capital after the colon marks it, as elsewhere in this psalter (4:6). The addressees of 'vós' are the reapers of 128:7 (plural humans, vós). 'bendissemos' was listed as unknown by the ambiguity reader and heard as a present; kept for 117:26b's identical Latin (decision benediximus, where the present is option 2). Whether 'nós vos bendissemos' continues what was not said or is the psalmist's own blessing stays open, as the reader found it.
