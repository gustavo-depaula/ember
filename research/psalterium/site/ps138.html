minor

“Maravilhoso se tornou o vosso saber de mim” Mirábilis facta est sciéntia tua ex me

'ex me' marks the knowledge as wonderful from/beyond the speaker's standpoint ('to me', too high for me); 'o vosso saber de mim' most naturally reads as an objective genitive, 'your knowledge about me', a sense the Latin does not state.

proposedMaravilhoso se tornou para mim o vosso saber

kept as an option under Mirábilis facta est sciéntia tua ex me — the v1 gate asked the preposition’s own word (*de mim*, taken), the v2 gate *de mim* moved, this one *para mim*. *de mim* keeps “of / from me” open, as the Latin; *para mim* (DRB) is option 2.

15

major

“Não vos foram ocultos os meus ossos, que fizestes em oculto” Non est occultátum os meum a te, quod fecísti in occúlto

'os meum' is singular (os, ossis; LXX tò ostoûn mou); the plural 'os meus ossos' changes number, and the relative 'quod' likewise refers to a single bone/frame.

proposedNão vos foi oculto o meu osso, que fizestes em oculto

pending — for Gustavo — held as at v1 and the v2 gate, with Ps 101:6’s ruling on the same collective singular: *o meu osso* is heard as one bone. Flagged for Gustavo with 101:6 — both change together or neither; option 1.

16

minor

“Os vossos olhos me viram inacabado” Imperféctum meum vidérunt óculi tui

The Latin object is the substantive neuter 'imperfectum meum' ('my unfinished/imperfect [thing]'), not 'me' with a predicate adjective; the rendering recasts the construction and loses the possessive noun.

proposedOs vossos olhos viram o meu imperfeito

not taken — *o meu imperfeito* is not Portuguese and is heard as “my flaw”; the neuter as a predicate on *me* is grammar (D2). *imperfeito* stays an option.

A faithful, close rendering of the Latin that keeps the Septuagintal oddities (funículum, resurrectiónem, the 17th verse's honoured friends) and matches the pointing marks in every verse. The one real fault is the pluralised 'ossos' in 138:15 against the singular 'os meum'; 138:6 and 138:16 could sit closer to the Latin construction.

Verse by verse

1

No titulus (*In finem, Psalmus David* in the LXX and MS1932; not in DO's Latin): D7. *vós* supplied in 1a for a sayable opening with proclisis; 1b's *vós* is the Latin's *tu*. The verse number (2) inside the Latin is not reproduced. The whole verse is the Easter Introit's psalm verse and the Introit verse of 02-24 (circulation.md). *probáre → provar* (the examináre / probáre row, as 16:3, 25:2). The CNBB's familiar *vós me sondais e conheceis* is from the Hebrew: *sondar* is scrutári's word here.

3

*intellígere → entender*; *de longe* moved to the head (natural order). *sémita → vereda* (D15).

4

*sermo → palavra* (D15). The Latin (with the Greek) says only that there is no word on my tongue; MS1932 and the CNBB add 'before it / you know it all' (the Hebrew) — not added.

5

*Ecce → Eis que* (the ecce row, 127:4). *novíssima … antíqua* → *as últimas e as antigas* with *coisas* supplied once; the *novíssima* row (72:17, *o fim deles*) is another build. 5b is in the Easter Introit.

8

*illic → ali* (row). *ades → estais presente* (the CNBB has the same words, from the Hebrew).

9

*extréma maris* → *os extremos do mar*; plural kept.

10

*Étenim* (καὶ γάρ) → *Pois também*; *illuc* (whither) → *ali*, the row's word. *dedúcere → guiar* (D22; this verse is on the row's list).

12

The middle colon *et nox sicut dies illuminábitur* → *e a noite se iluminará como o dia*: the medio-passive (MS1932 *a noite é clara como o dia*) and Portuguese order; the Latin-order *e a noite, como o dia, será iluminada* was +4 syllables. *sicut ténebræ ejus, ita et lumen ejus* → *como as suas trevas, assim também a sua luz* — *ejus* (the night's) kept as open as the Latin; *lumen → luz* (row). *illumináre → iluminar*.

13

*possidére → possuir* (row); *renes → rins* (row; MS1932's gloss *afectos* not followed).

14
