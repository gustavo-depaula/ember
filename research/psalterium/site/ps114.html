8

fails: cadence

“os meus olhos das lágrimas, * os meus pés da queda”

The mediant falls on 'lágrimas', a proparoxytone. The chant has to land on the antepenult, and the colon trails off on two weak syllables right where it should come to rest.

proposeddas lágrimas os meus olhos, * da queda os meus pés

kept as an option under óculos meos a lácrimis, * pedes meos a lapsu — The Latin's mediant is the proparoxytone *lácrimis*; the inversion is for its own sake and would break 55:13's *os meus pés da queda*.

7

fails: native

“porque o Senhor te fez bem”

In everyday Brazilian speech 'fez bem' means 'was good for you' (a walk or a medicine 'faz bem'), so it sounds therapeutic rather than like God doing good to the soul. Adding the article gives it back its moral, active sense without changing the verb.

proposedporque o Senhor te fez o bem

kept as an option under Dóminus benefécit tibi — The *benefácere* row (56:3, 48:19) says *fazer bem*; changing one place would split the row. Proposed in the glossary as the row's alternative for all three places.

4b

fails: sayable

“o Senhor é misericordioso, e justo,”

The comma before 'e justo' makes a pause that Portuguese does not want between two coordinated adjectives. Reciters will hesitate after the long 'misericordioso' and the line limps.

proposedo Senhor é misericordioso e justo,

❧ taken — Punctuation only (D2).

2

fails: native

“e nos meus dias invocarei”

Fronting the adverbial phrase and ending on the bare, objectless verb sounds translated and cut short. The absolute verb is faithful, but it comes more easily when the time phrase follows it.

proposede invocarei nos meus dias

❧ taken — Order only (D2); the Latin's words and the absolute verb are kept.

Plain, sober and mostly well cadenced. It sounds like prayer and keeps the Latin's repetitions (encontraram/encontrei, Senhor/Senhor) without strain. The only real flaw is the proparoxytone 'lágrimas' at the mediant of v. 8; the rest are small matters of idiom and punctuation.

Verse by verse

1

*vocem oratiónis meæ → a voz da minha oração* (the *orátio* row). *exaudíre → escutar* (D3). The subject moved before the verb so that *escutará* takes the mediant; *porque escutará o Senhor* would hear *o Senhor* as the object.

2

*inclináre aurem* → *inclinou para mim o seu ouvido*, the third person of the formula row (*inclinai para mim o vosso ouvido*, 16:6, 30:3). The Monday Vespers antiphon *Inclinávit Dóminus * aurem suam mihi* (DO Psalmi major, grep) adds *Dóminus*; as an antiphon it would read *O Senhor inclinou * para mim o seu ouvido*.

3a

First colon = 17:5a word for word (*Cercaram-me as dores da morte*), as the Latin is identical (rule 6). *periculum → perigo*, the plain cognate.

3b

The Latin's order (object first, verb at the mediant) is kept: it holds *encontrei* at the same place as *encontraram* at the end of 3a, and it avoids the *dor / Senhor* rhyme at mediant and final that the plain order makes (decision `inven`). *invocávi → invoquei*, the *invocáre* row; the second colon is in natural order.

4b

*líbera → livrai* (the *liberáre* row; safe under rule 3, past *livrei*). *miserétur → tem piedade* (the *miseréri* row); the family link with *misericordioso* is lost, as in every Portuguese version. *O Dómine* → *Ó Senhor*, the interjection kept.

7

*convértere → voltar* (the row; here the soul's turning back, not conversion). *réquies → repouso* (the row, 94:11; MS1932). *benefácere → fazer bem* (the row; 56:3 *ao Deus que me fez bem*; the row names this verse).

8

Near-twin of 55:13 *Quóniam eripuísti ánimam meam de morte, et pedes meos de lapsu*: its words are copied (*arrancar* with a source named, the *erípere* row; *lapsus → queda*, D44, which names this verse), with the person changed to the Latin's third (*erípuit*; the Hebrew has the second — not followed). *lácrimæ → lágrimas* (the row). The proparoxytone *lágrimas* at the mediant mirrors the Latin's own *lácrimis*; accepted.

9

= the Office of the Dead Vespers antiphon (see decision `regione`).
