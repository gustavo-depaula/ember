11b

minor

“Escolhi ser posto de lado” Elégi abjéctus esse

abiectus (from abicere, 'thrown down / cast away') means cast off, lowly, despised; 'posto de lado' (set aside) softens it into mere neglect and loses the sense of abasement.

proposedEscolhi ser rejeitado

kept as an option under Elégi abjéctus esse in domo Dei mei — *rejeitar* is reprobáre's (117:22); *rejeitado na casa do meu Deus* says the house turned him away.

A close, faithful rendering: Septuagintal readings are kept (passer, legislator, de virtute in virtutem, faciem Christi tui, ascensiones ... in loco quem posuit with its ambiguous subject left open), and nothing is added or explained. The only point worth tightening is abiectus in 83:11b. Pointing marks match the Latin in every verse (one mediant each, no flex or second mediant).

fails: sayable

“e a rola um ninho para si, onde ponha os seus filhotes”

The elided verb makes 'e a rola um ninho' run together as if 'a rola um ninho' were one noun phrase; the reader stumbles looking for the verb.

6

fails: native, sayable

“dispôs no seu coração subidas”

The object comes late after the adverbial, which is Latin order; the ear hears 'no seu coração' and then trips on a bare 'subidas' followed by two more 'no' phrases. The second colon is also very long for one breath.

proposeddispôs subidas no seu coração, no vale de lágrimas, no lugar que estabeleceu.

❧ taken

“será visto o Deus dos deuses em Sião”

Verb-first passive sounds translated; natural order lets the subject lead and keeps the stressed final 'Sião'.

❧ taken

11b

fails: native

“Escolhi ser posto de lado ... mais do que habitar”

'Escolhi X mais do que Y' reads like a calque of 'magis quam'; Portuguese prefers a preference construction after 'escolher'.

proposedEscolhi ser posto de lado na casa do meu Deus: * antes que habitar nas tendas dos pecadores.

kept as an option under magis quam habitáre — The point taken as *em vez de*; *antes que* is the conjunction 'before'.

The psalm reads plainly and reverently, and its images (pardal, rola, átrios, tendas) carry the longing well. The rough spots are a few places where the Latin word order shows through (83:6, 83:8) and one weak mediant in 83:11. The rest could be prayed as it stands.

Verse by verse

2

Subject first in the second colon (natural order, D2); the Latin puts the two verbs first. *tabernácula → tendas* (row, plural as 42:3). *concupíscere → ansiar por* (row). *átrium → átrio* (row; two blind readers did not know it — kept, the Latin's word). The first colon is sung alone as a responsory and Gradual (Commune C8, Pent05-0, Epi1-0).

3

*caro → carne*; *Deus vivus → o Deus vivo* (41:3's words without *forte*). First colon +3: the articles before possessives (rule 5).

4

*étenim → pois* (row). *passer → pardal* (row, which names this verse). *turtur → rola* (MS1932). *invénit* perfect → *encontrou*. *ubi ponat* subjunctive → *onde ponha*. Second colon +5 in v2: the second *para si* kept (decision `sibi2`) and *rolinha* (decision `turtur`); accepted.

4b

*Rex meus et Deus meus → meu Rei e meu Deus* (formula row, 5:3, 43:5). Second colon −3: the formula; the Latin's *et* is kept. The blind reader could not tell what is said about the altars: the Latin is verbless and says no more (decision `altaria`).

5

*in sǽcula sæculórum → pelos séculos dos séculos* (D27). The second colon is a responsory R. alone (*In sǽcula sæculórum laudábunt te*) and reads alone. Vocative *Senhor* kept at the end of its colon, where the Latin has it. First colon +3: *bem-aventurados* (D19).

6

*beátus vir → bem-aventurado o homem* (D19). *auxílium → auxílio*. *in valle lacrimárum → no vale de lágrimas* — the Salve Regina's phrase. The two *in* are both kept (*no vale …, no lugar*). Verb moved before its object (*dispôs no seu coração subidas*), natural order. Length: first colon +4 (*bem-aventurado*, D19's known cost); second colon about −2 against the Latin's 28 syllables.

8

*legislátor → legislador* (DRB 'lawgiver', MS1932). *benedictiónem* singular (the Greek has the plural εὐλογίας; the Latin followed). *a bênção*: Portuguese needs an article or *uma*; *a* is lighter. Subject of *ibunt* left unnamed, as the Latin. *Deus deórum → o Deus dos deuses* (D44). The blind reader heard *legislador* first as a lawmaker in the modern sense (and listed it as unknown); the Latin's word, DRB and MS1932 alike — kept. First colon −4 against the Latin, accepted.

9

*exáudi → escutai* (D3); *áuribus pércipe → dai ouvidos* (D4, which names this verse: *dai ouvidos, Deus de Jacó*). *Deus virtútum* follows *Dóminus virtútum* (decision `virtutum`), as 79:8.

10

*protéctor → protetor* (row). *Christus → Cristo* (D19; the row names 83:10). Ends with a colon, as the Latin runs on into 83:11 *Quia*.

11

v2: *é melhor* moved to the mediant (the stylist: *átrios* left two weak syllables before the mark); *melhor do que milhares* now joins across the mark as the Latin's *mélior … super míllia* does. The Introit of Pentecost XIV (*… quia mélior est dies una in átriis tuis super mília*) joins 83:10 and 83:11a. *super míllia → do que milhares*. First colon −3 after the v2 gate removed *só*; accepted.

11b

*tabernáculis peccatórum → nas tendas dos pecadores* (the tabernáculum row names this place). *habitáre → habitar*, as 83:5. Second colon −3, accepted.

12

Natural order (subject before verb in both colons, D2); the Latin ends each colon on the divine name. *díligere → amar* (row). *misericórdia*, *véritas → verdade* (rows). *grátia → graça*.

13

*beátus homo → bem-aventurado o homem*: *homo* and *vir* (83:6) become one word, as in MS1932 and DRB ('the man'); Portuguese has no plain second word (*varão* is archaic). *ambuláre → andar*, *innocéntia → inocência*, *speráre in → esperar em* (rows). Second colon +5: *bem-aventurado* (D19).
