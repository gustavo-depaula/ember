4

fails: cadence

“e sobre os céus a sua glória.”

The verse ends on 'glória', which is proparoxytone in writing and trails off after the stress. The line also closes with a weak article-possessive cluster ('a sua glória') and no verb, so the second colon feels unfinished when chanted.

proposede a sua glória, sobre os céus.

kept as an option under et super cælos glória ejus — the Latin ends on glória ejus; = 56:6's build; glória is paroxytone

5

fails: sayable, native

“e olha para o que é humilde”

'para o que é humilde' piles up vowels in hiatus (o-que-é-hu) and sounds abstract and wordy. The Latin 'humília' is a plain neuter plural, meaning humble things.

proposede olha as coisas humildes no céu e na terra?

❧ taken

7

fails: native, plain

“Levantando da terra o carente, * e erguendo do esterco o pobre:”

Two dangling gerunds with no main verb read like a translation and not like Portuguese prayer. 'Carente' carries a modern, social-work or emotional tone ('uma pessoa carente') that is foreign to the psalm's register.

proposedEle levanta da terra o necessitado, * e do esterco ergue o pobre:

kept as an option under ínopem — D38 settles carente; necessitado is egénus's; evidence passed to the row

8

fails: plain

“Para colocá-lo com os príncipes”

'colocá-lo' is flat and administrative ('colocar' sounds like placing an object or filling a job). The enclitic '-lo' after a stressed oxytone also clips the rhythm.

proposedpara o assentar com os príncipes, * com os príncipes do seu povo.

kept as an option under Ut cóllocet eum — assentar is the Greek/Hebrew 'seat'; collocáre is 'place' (rule 1)

9

fails: native

“Ele, que faz habitar a estéril numa casa,”

'Ele, que…' opens a relative clause that never reaches a main verb, so the ear waits for a predicate that never comes. 'habitar a estéril' also blurs subject and object for a moment.

proposedEle faz a estéril habitar numa casa, * alegre mãe de filhos.

❧ taken

The opening laud (1–3) sounds clean, reverent and truly Brazilian, and it sits well under a psalm tone. The second half, from 7 to 9, slips into translationese: dangling gerunds, a relative with no main clause, and two register lapses ('carente', 'colocá-lo'). Those need finite verbs and plainer, older-sounding words to match the nobility of the opening.

worst line112:7

best line112:2

minor

“* e olha para as coisas humildes” * (6) et humília réspicit

The Latin text carries the inline verse-number marker '(6)' after the mediant, which marks where verse 6 begins inside this prayed verse; the Portuguese drops it. The pointing marks themselves match (a single '*').

proposedQuem é como o Senhor, nosso Deus, que habita nas alturas, * (6) e olha para as coisas humildes no céu e na terra?

❧ taken

The translation is faithful to the Latin throughout: 'púeri' as 'meninos', the neuter 'humília' as 'coisas humildes', and the ambiguous 'in cælo et in terra' left unresolved; the participles of vv. 7 and 9 are rendered as finite verbs with a supplied subject, which the working rule allows. The pointing marks agree with the Latin in every verse; the only fault is the missing '(6)' verse marker in 112:5.
