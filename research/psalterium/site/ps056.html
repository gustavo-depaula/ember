2

fails: native

“Tende piedade de mim, Deus, tende piedade de mim”

A bare vocative 'Deus' in the middle of the line reads as a calque; the Portuguese ear expects the vocative particle. 'ó' is not a word added to the sense, only the native way of calling on someone, and it also gives the line a lift before the repetition.

proposedTende piedade de mim, ó Deus, tende piedade de mim: * porque em vós confia a minha alma.

kept as an option under Deus (vocative, no *o*) — the Latin has no *o*; D44 held the bare vocative in Ps 50 and flagged it for Gustavo; one psalter-wide ruling

3

fails: cadence

“Clamarei ao Deus altíssimo:”

'altíssimo' is stressed on the third-last syllable, right at the mediant, so the tone runs past a dead syllable. Putting the verb at the end of the colon gives a strong final stress and matches 'fez bem' in the second half.

proposedAo Deus altíssimo clamarei: * ao Deus que me fez bem.

❧ taken — the cadence taken by 7:18's order (*ao altíssimo Deus*), not by the inversion (verb last), refused as inversion for its own sake

6

fails: native

“Exaltai-vos sobre os céus, Deus,”

Same bare vocative. Here it falls right on the mediant, so a lone monosyllable ends the colon and it sounds clipped.

proposedExaltai-vos sobre os céus, ó Deus, * e por toda a terra a vossa glória.

kept as an option under Deus (vocative, no *o*) — as 56:2

7b

fails: plain, cadence

“Cavaram um fosso ... e caíram nele.”

'fosso' suggests a moat or a ditch. The plainer and more biblical word for a pit dug as a trap is 'cova'. Ending the verse on 'nele' leaves it falling onto a weak pronoun; moving the pronoun forward puts the stress on the verb.

proposedCavaram uma cova diante da minha face: * e nela caíram.

kept as an option under fóveam — the *lacus / fóvea* row keeps the two words apart (7:16); *nele* is a paroxytone and the plain order needs no inversion

fails: native

“O meu coração está pronto, Deus,”

Bare vocative again, as in 56:2 and 56:6.

proposedO meu coração está pronto, ó Deus, o meu coração está pronto: * cantarei, e entoarei um salmo.

kept as an option under Deus (vocative, no *o*) — as 56:2

fails: cadence

“saltério e cítara:”

'cítara' is stressed on the third-last syllable, at the mediant. Swapping the two instruments keeps both words and the image, and gives a paroxytone ending ('saltério').

proposedLevanta-te, minha glória, levanta-te, cítara e saltério: * eu me levantarei ao amanhecer.

kept as an option under psaltérium et cíthara — the Latin ends the colon on *cíthara*; its order kept for the reader crossing columns and for 107:3

A sober, faithful psalm that sounds mostly like Portuguese prayer: the images stay concrete (wings, lions' cubs, snare, pit) and the refrain comes back with dignity. The main recurring flaw is the bare vocative 'Deus', which sounds like a translation; two proparoxytones at the mediant ('altíssimo', 'cítara') and the ending of 56:7b ('fosso ... nele') are the only real stumbles when chanting. Verse by verse

2

*Miserére mei* formula row, bare vocative *Deus*. *confídere in → confiar em* (24:1 *em vós confio*). The second colon is an antiphon on its own (*Quóniam * in te confídit ánima mea*, Monastic Lauds feria 3 and Wednesday Matins [Daya3]) and a short-responsory versicle (Matutinum Special [MM LB2C]); *porque em vós confia a minha alma* reads alone. The whole verse is a Mass chant (Quadp3-3, grep).

2b

*speráre → esperar* (the future *esperarei*); *donec tránseat iníquitas → até que passe a iniquidade* (row).

4

*e me livrou* — proclisis after *e*, the Brazilian order.

4b

*dormívi conturbátus → dormi perturbado* (*dormíre → dormir*, 3:6; *conturbáre → perturbar*). *erípere de → arrancar* (row: the source is named). The Latin's comma after *misericórdiam suam* is not kept before *e*.

6

The refrain. 56:6 and 56:12 share every slot the Latin shares (*Exaltai-vos sobre os céus, Deus*); they part at *in / super omnem terram* (see `interram`) and keep the Latin's own punctuation (comma / colon at the mediant).

7b

*incidérunt in eam → e caíram nele*; MS1932 adds *eles mesmos*, not in the Latin.

8

*cantábo → cantarei*; the colon copies 26:6b.

9

The mediant falls on *cítara*, a proparoxytone, as the Latin's *cíthara* does; kept (decision `instruments`). The unknown words *saltério* and *cítara* (ambiguity reader) are the rows' (32:2, 48:5), kept as the Latin's instruments.

10

*Confitébor tibi → Eu vos darei graças* (D5; 42:5 *Eu vos darei graças*); *in pópulis … in géntibus → entre os povos … entre as nações* (rows; 17:50). The trailing colon of the DO text kept. **Doublet:** 107:4 shares the first colon exactly and should copy it.

11

*usque ad → até* (35:6 *até as nuvens*), Brazilian *até os* without *a*.
