The audit trail

1draft

Drafted at psalm level from the parallels (consult/parallels/ps150.md), with lookups in the finished psalms (ps150/concord.py, copied from ps149's) and the glossary rows for every instrument. Brazilian circulation was fetched (circulation.md): the CNBB LH text is from the Hebrew, and none of it is taken. Precedent followed: 46:6 (à voz da trombeta → ao som da trombeta), 56:9/107:3 (saltério e cítara), 67:26/80:3 (tamborim), 136:2 (órganum → instrumento), 67:36 (in sanctis suis → nos seus santos), 148:2 (virtútes → poderes), D31 (firmamento, the sky), D43 (jubilátio → aclamação). Hard readings: 150:1 *in sanctis* (persons or places), 150:4 *chorus* (dance, not choir) and *órganum* (instrument, not organ), 150:5 *omnis spíritus*. 150:4 *chorus* was first pencilled 'a dança'. Before the readers it was aligned with Ps 149:3 ('em coro', ruled in parallel and read without remark) as 'o coro'; see decision `choro`.

2checks

Hard checks pass. Every length flag is short, not long: 150:1b −3, 150:2 −4/−4, 150:5a −5, 150:5b −3. They come mostly from the refrain. *Laudáte eum* has five syllables and 'louvai-o' has three, and that comes twice in every verse. 150:5a also loses syllables because *benesonántibus* (six) becomes 'sonoros' (three). All of them are accepted, since a shorter colon still fits one breath. Every mediant and final ends on a paroxytone or oxytone except 150:3b 'cítara', which falls exactly where the Latin has 'cíthara'.

Reader: claude-opus-5-5, fresh context, with latin.json. There was one minor remark, and no majors. Overall: close and faithful. It named in sanctis, virtutibus, multitudinem magnitudinis and generic organum as preserved.

5

minor

“com címbalos sonoros” in cýmbalis benesonántibus

bene-sonantibus is 'well-sounding', a judgment of quality, not merely 'sounding/loud'; 'sonoros' drops the 'bene' element (Portuguese 'sonoro' leans to 'resonant, loud').

proposedcom címbalos bem sonantes

not taken — Dicio gives 'sonoro' as 'que tem som claro e agradável' (sense 2), so *bene* is carried. MS1932, DM1962 and the CNBB all print 'címbalos sonoros'. The calque is bookish, and it stays an option.

A close and faithful rendering: the Septuagintal features (in sanctis kept as the ambiguous 'nos seus santos', virtutibus as plural 'poderes', multitudinem magnitudinis, generic organum as 'instrumento') are preserved rather than smoothed. The only point that could be closer is benesonantibus; pointing marks (* in 1-4; † then * in 5) match the Latin in kind and order.

2

fails: native

“segundo a multidão da sua grandeza”

Faithful to the Latin, but 'multidão' next to an abstract 'grandeza' makes the ear stop for a moment: in Portuguese 'multidão' calls up a crowd of people. It is the Latin image, so it must stay. The fix is to make it sit more easily: dropping 'sua' before the final word shortens the colon and lets it close cleanly.

proposedlouvai-o segundo a multidão de sua grandeza

not taken — Rule 5 keeps the article before possessives. The colon is already 4 syllables short of the Latin.

4

fails: concrete, plain

“e o instrumento”

The only place in the psalm's list of instruments where a named instrument turns into a generic word. It lands flat and prosaic at the final cadence, just where the list should close on something you can see and hear. 'Órgão' keeps the Latin word, is what the Portuguese tradition uses, and gives a good paroxytone close.

proposedlouvai-o com as cordas e o órgão

kept as an option under et órgano — The glossary row and 136:2 (ὄργανον). 'órgão' is heard as the church pipe organ, which the psalm does not name. The Latinist counted the generic word a fidelity.

5

fails: plain

“címbalos de aclamação”

'Aclamação' sounds a bit official, like voting or applause, and it is long. 'Júbilo' is the plainer word, is closer to 'jubilatio', and is what Brazilian liturgical ears know. It also matches 'sonoros' in the first colon better, and the flex still falls on a stressed syllable (JÚ-bi-lo is proparoxytone, which a Gregorian flex handles well).

proposedlouvai-o com címbalos de júbilo

kept as an option under in cýmbalis jubilatiónis — D43 (settled) gives *jubilátio* → 'aclamação' and names 150:5. 'júbilo' belongs to *gáudium*.

A clean, strong doxology: the 'louvai-o' anaphora carries it, and the lines are short and easy to breathe through. The weak spots are in the instrument list, where the words go abstract ('instrumento', 'aclamação') at the moment the psalm most needs concrete, ringing nouns.

worst line150:4

best line150:1

4

minor

“com as cordas e o instrumento” in chordis, et órgano

organum is a concrete instrument (in the Psalter the pipe/wind instrument, Hebrew 'ugab'); 'o instrumento' reduces it to a generic abstraction and blurs the pairing strings / pipe. 'órgão' is anachronistic, but a concrete wind instrument keeps the image.

proposedlouvai-o com as cordas e a flauta

kept as an option under et órgano — Held for the glossary row and 136:2 (ὄργανον, one Greek word). 'instrumento' is what *órganum* says, and 'flauta' decides which instrument. The v1 Latinist named the generic word as preserved. 'a flauta' is option 2, one touch away, and it is the likeliest change if Gustavo wants the list concrete.

5

minor

“com címbalos de aclamação” in cýmbalis jubilatiónis

jubilatio is the shout of joy, jubilation; 'aclamação' narrows it to acclaiming someone and loses the note of exultant joy.

proposedlouvai-o com címbalos de júbilo

kept as an option under in cýmbalis jubilatiónis — D43 (settled) gives the *jubiláre / jubilátio* family 'aclamar / aclamação' and names 150:5. 'júbilo' is kept for *gáudium*. The stylist asked for the same thing on v1. If Gustavo overturns D43, this verse moves with the family.

A faithful, close rendering: every clause, preposition-phrase and plural (virtutibus = 'poderes', cymbalis = 'címbalos') is carried over, and the pointing marks (* in 1-4; † then * in 5) match the Latin exactly. Only two minor lexical points: 'instrumento' for organum is generic, and 'aclamação' for jubilatio is a little off its sense of joyful shout.

Verse by verse

1

No title. DO's Psalm150.txt opens at 'Laudáte Dóminum in sanctis ejus'. It has no 'Allelúja' line, although the LXX and MS1932 have one. As in Pss 146–147, nothing is added (D7). *laudáte* → 'louvai' with a direct object, and *laudáte eum* → 'louvai-o', as 116:1 and 148:1–2. The tenfold imperative is kept word for word (rule 6: repetitions stay).

2

*secúndum multitúdinem magnitúdinis ejus* → 'segundo a multidão da sua grandeza'. These are the *multitúdo* row (which notes that 150:2 has both nouns) and *magnitúdo* → 'grandeza' (70:8, 78:11, 144:3, 144:6). MS1932 has the same words.

3

*psaltérium* → 'saltério' and *cíthara* → 'cítara' (glossary rows; 56:9 = 107:3 'saltério e cítara', in the Latin's order). One 'com' serves both nouns, as the Latin has one *in* (97:5). The colon ends on the proparoxytone 'cítara' where the Latin ends on 'cíthara' (as 91:4, 146:7).

4

*chordæ* → 'as cordas' (DRB 'strings'). MS1932's 'instrumentos de corda' explains the word. One 'com' serves each pair, as the Latin has one *in*.

5

The Latin's '(6)' marker is not reproduced (rule 4). The verse keeps its † and *. The last colon is a jussive subjunctive ('louve'), like *laudet*. This verse, with the whole psalm, is also said in the priest's thanksgiving after Mass (DO missa/Latin/Ordo/Post.txt) and at Lauds of Holy Saturday (missa/Latin/Tempora/Quad6-6r.txt), found by grep.
