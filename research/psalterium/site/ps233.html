Verse by verse

2:29

DO keys the canticle's verses by Luke's chapter (2:29–2:32) and has a '+' after the first words; both are reproduced as they stand. (Main session, after the agent: the '+' is not a flex — in DO's psalter files it occurs only at the opening of the three Gospel canticles, Benedíctus + / Magníficat + * / Nunc dimíttis +, in the Magnificat directly before the mediant; it marks where the sign of the cross is made. It stays where the Latin has it.) 'Nunc' → 'Agora', first word as in the Latin. The order of the Latin's three cola is kept, so that 'in pace' closes the verse as it does in the Latin: 'em paz', the same words as Ps 4:9 (In pace → Em paz), said a few minutes earlier at the same Compline. The comma before 'em paz' is mine: without it the ear could take 'a vossa palavra em paz' as one phrase. Dómine → Senhor (the Greek δέσποτα is not the Latin's word). 'secúndum verbum tuum' → 'segundo a vossa palavra' (verbum → palavra; the phrase is also in 118:25, 118:65, 118:107). First colon +2 by checks.py ('deixais ir' for dimíttis).

2:30

salutáre tuum → 'a vossa salvação', matching Ps 90:16 (salutáre meum → a minha salvação), said just before at Sunday Compline; the neuter τὸ σωτήριον, 'the saving thing', is left as open as 'salvação' leaves it, and is not turned into 'Salvador'. The ambiguity reader heard exactly that openness ('an event in which the Lord saved', or 'someone or something that brings the Lord's salvation'); it did not hear 'a vossa salvação' as God's own being saved, the risk noted at 90:16. Natural order in the first colon (the Latin has the verb first); the verb stays in the first colon and its object in the second, as in the Latin. Quia → Porque.

2:31

The verse is a relative clause hanging from 'salvação', as in the Latin (Quod ← salutáre). parásti → preparastes (ἡτοίμασας). pópuli → povos.

2:32

Lumen and glóriam are accusatives in apposition to salutáre… quod parásti; Portuguese shows no case, so nothing is needed. 'plebis tuæ Israël' → 'do vosso povo Israel', without a comma: with one, 'Israel' would be heard as a vocative. plebs → povo, the same word as pópulus: Portuguese has no second word ('plebe' is contemptuous), so the Latin's pópulus / plebs is not audible — recorded in the glossary. Lumen → luz, as in Ps 4:7 (lumen vultus tui → a luz do vosso rosto).
