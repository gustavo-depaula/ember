# Canticle 223 (1 Samuel 2:1–10, Vulgate — Canticle of Anna) — Brazilian circulation

Checked 2026-09-23 on the web; nothing here is from memory. Saved pages are under `consult/ps223/` (gitignored). The CNBB text is copyrighted, so only short fragments are quoted here.

## Liturgia das Horas (CNBB) — Wednesday Lauds, Week II

Source: Paulus Editora, *Liturgia Diária das Horas*, "Dia 17 – Quarta-feira" (https://www.paulus.com.br/portal/liturgia-diaria-das-horas/dia-17-quarta-feira-19/, saved as `consult/ps223/lh-paulus-dia17-quarta.html`; the canticle extracted to `lh-paulus-dia17-canticle.txt`). Paulus prints the CNBB text. It stands between Ps 76(77) and Ps 96(97), which is Wednesday Lauds of Week II. A second Paulus day (`lh-paulus-dia5-quarta.html`) and Católico Digital (`lh-catolicodigital-laudes-quarta.html`) carry the same text.

Heading: **Cântico 1Sm 2,1-10 – Os humildes se alegram em Deus.** Antiphon: *Exulta no Senhor meu coração. É ele que exalta os humilhados.*

It is a metrical version from the Hebrew-family text, so it is evidence of what Brazilian ears know, not of sense. Points of contact, by DO line (fragments only):

## What this means for the translation

The canticle is well known in Brazil through one CNBB text, used both at Lauds and at Mass, which is made from the Hebrew-family text and paraphrases. So no fixed Brazilian wording of *this* Latin has to be matched. Where the CNBB's words are also faithful to the Vulgate, the draft uses them: *vigor*, *se empregaram por pão*, *dá a morte e dá a vida*, *faz descer* (v2), *dar à luz*, *vossa salvação*. Where they are not, the Latin's word stands: *chifre* (not *fronte*), *aos infernos* (not *sepultura*), *eixos* (not *colunas*; v1 *polos*), *Cristo* (not *Ungido*, D19).
