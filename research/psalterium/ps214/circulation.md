# Canticle 214 (Jer 31:10–14) — liturgical use and Brazilian circulation (checked 2026-09-23)

## In the Latin books (grep of `content/do/`, psalter files excluded)

- **The canticle itself**: Thursday Lauds I (`Psalterium/Psalmi/Psalmi major.txt` [Day4 Laudes1], l. 89) and the monastic ferial canticles ([DaymF Canticles], l. 327).
- **Its antiphon**: *Pópulus meus, * ait Dóminus, bonis meis adimplébitur* (both places above). It is 31:18b plus *ait Dóminus*, which the Vulgate has at the end of Jer 31:14 and DO's canticle drops. The antiphon must read alone.
- **31:18 whole**, as an Alleluia verse: *V. Ego Dóminus inebriábo ánimam sacerdótum pinguédine: et pópulus meus bonis meis adimplébitur. Allelúja.* (`Commune/aliquibus locis/C4c.txt` l. 37, marked *Jer 31:14*).
- **31:10** in the Advent responsory *R. Audíte verbum Dómini, gentes, et annuntiáte illud in fínibus terræ: * Et ínsulis, quæ procul sunt, dícite: Salvátor noster advéniet* (`Tempora/Adv1-0.txt` [Responsory8]). This is an adapted text, not the canticle's words.
- **31:12–15** in a responsory: *R. Redémit pópulum suum et liberávit eum, et vénient et exsultábunt in monte Sion … super fruménto, vino et óleo, * Et ultra non esúrient. V. Erítque ánima eórum quasi hortus irríguus* (`Tempora/111-1.txt` [Responsory1]). Also adapted.
- *quasi hortus irríguus* also comes from Isa 58:11 (missa Quadp3-6, Sancti 07-20; SanctiM 10-03b; St Scholastica, SanctiM 02-10). That is the same image in another book.

## Brazilian circulation (fetched)

- **Liturgia das Horas, CNBB**, Thursday Lauds week II, *Cântico Jr 31,10-14 – A felicidade do povo libertado* (Paulus, `consult/ps214/paulus-lh-dia31-quinta.html`, gitignored, from paulus.com.br/portal/liturgia-diaria-das-horas/dia-31-quinta-feira-3/). The text is Hebrew-based and is not a witness of sense:
  Fragments only (CNBB copyright; the full text is in the gitignored consult file): *Ouvi, nações, a palavra do Senhor*; *Quem dispersou Israel, vai congregá-lo*; *qual pastor a seu rebanho*; *o Senhor remiu Jacó*; *do poder do prepotente*; *afluirão para as bênçãos do Senhor*; *jardim bem irrigado*; *de sede e fome nunca mais*; *a virgem dançará*; *mudarei em alegria o seu luto*; *após a pena*; *Saciarei os sacerdotes de delícias*; *meu povo há de fartar-se de meus bens*.
  The antiphon is *O meu povo há de fartar-se de meus bens.*
- **Lectionary responsorial psalm** *Jr 31,10.11-12ab.13 (R. cf. 10d)*, with the refrain ***O Senhor nos guardará qual pastor a seu rebanho***. The stanzas are the LH text word for word (painossodeus.wordpress.com, 2023-04-01, `consult/ps214/lecionario-painossodeus-jr31.html`; its "após a guerra" is taken as a slip for the LH's "após a pena"). Its days are Saturday of Lent week 5 and Saturday of Ordinary Time week 25, odd years. That comes from a web-search summary of musicasparamissa.com.br titles; I did not read it on a page of the Lectionary.
- **Diurnal Monástico 1962** (consult/diurnal-1.md, Thursday Lauds; Hebrew-family): *Ouvi, nações, a palavra do Senhor, * e anunciai-a nas ilhas longínquas … como um pastor, o seu rebanho … e correr aos bens do Senhor … E será sua alma como jardim regado … Alegrar-se-ão então em côros as donzelas … E trocarei em gôzo o seu luto … e viverá o meu povo na fartura de seus bens.* Antiphon: *O meu povo será cumulado de benefícios, diz o Senhor.*

## What Brazilian ears know

They know ***Ouvi, nações, a palavra do Senhor***, ***Quem dispersou Israel*** + ***congregar***, ***qual pastor a seu rebanho*** (the Lectionary refrain), ***afluirão*** (the LH's word for *cónfluent*, which is also this draft's), ***jardim … irrigado***, ***luto***, ***o meu povo … de meus bens***.

Where this draft departs from them, the rule is the reason:
- *Ouvi* is the bare *vós* imperative, which rule 3 bans. The draft uses *Escutai*, as at Ps 48:2.
- *remiu* comes from the redímere row's *resgatar*.
- *do prepotente* is the Hebrew's sense. The Latin says *potentióris*.
- *dançará* follows the Hebrew; the Latin has *in choro*, rendered from the glossary.
- *de sede e fome* adds thirst, which the Latin does not have.
- *Saciarei … de delícias* is not the Latin's *inebriábo … pinguédine*.

*qual pastor* is archaic-poetic and was not taken. *como um pastor* is plainer. It is noted as a thing heard, not an authority.
