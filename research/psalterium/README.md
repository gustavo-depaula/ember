# Psalterium — Saltério Galicano em Português

A Brazilian Portuguese translation of the Gallican Psalter **meant to be prayed**, for a translation of the Breviarium Romanum (1961/62 books). Built by frontier LLMs working through agentic harnesses, with human philological sign-off. Output is public domain.

[`design.md`](design.md) is the founding design doc: goal, corpus, the style standard ("simple nobility"), a proposed pipeline, the pilot. Read it for the *intent*. Its pipeline, data model and tooling are a proposal, not a contract — this folder is deliberately empty so whoever does the work can find the shape that actually produces a good psalter. Make folders, formats and scripts as you need them, and say why in this README as you go.

**Status (2026-09-20):** pilot Latin collated — no wording divergence from the Clementine ([`collation/report.md`](collation/report.md)). Ps 4 has been through every step the design doc proposes; draft 2 is prayable at [`ps004/sheet.md`](ps004/sheet.md), and [`ps004/retrospective.md`](ps004/retrospective.md) says which steps earned their keep and what the slimmer method is. The pilot is Sunday Prime and Compline.

**Review surface:** the generated site — `python3.13 research/psalterium/site.py`, then serve `research/psalterium/` (`python3.13 -m http.server 8765 --bind 127.0.0.1 --directory research/psalterium`) and open `/site/index.html`: a page per psalm (facing columns, popover decisions, the layers Latin → literal → each draft, the audit trail, other psalters beside), the glossary, word studies, method and every open decision in one list. It grew out of the hand-built [`review.html`](review.html), which is kept as it was and still shows Ps 4 at draft 2. What follows describes that first page: — Ps 4 in facing columns, the *vós*/*tu* pairs and every open decision on one page; choosing an alternative rewrites the psalm so it can be prayed as chosen, and "Copy my decisions" hands the result back as markdown. It is hand-built from `ps004/prayed.json` draft 2 and `vos-tu.md`, not generated; rebuild it if either changes. Touching a dotted word opens its decision in place. The "beside it" switch sets other psalters under each verse (Matos Soares 1932 and 1956, Ave Maria, CNBB; Douay-Rheims, RSV-CE, KJV) — built by `compare.py` into the gitignored `consult/compare-ps004.js`, because most of them are in copyright; without that file the switch hides itself. Only the Douay-Rheims and Matos Soares 1932 are from the Vulgate (his 1956 edition follows the Hebrew). The 1932 text comes from the text layer of `~/Downloads/Vulgata-Padre-Matos-Soares-1.pdf` — `pdftotext -f <page> -l <page>` is cheap, so never read its pages as images; Ps 4 is PDF pages 1021–1022, and it is the nearest Portuguese antecedent (see `ps004/decisions.md`). The Bolls copy of the CNBB lacks verse 9.

**Waiting on a human:** Gustavo's review of the overnight rulings in [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md) (2026-09-20: he delegated decisions on condition that they be deliberated and documented) — first D2, the rule for how far fluency may pull against the Latin, and D5, *confitéri* → *dar graças a*. **The machine, meanwhile:** the psalter psalm by psalm through sub-agents, 40 minutes apart in batches of two — [`PROGRESS.md`](PROGRESS.md) says where it stands, [`HANDOFF.md`](HANDOFF.md) how it is run.

**How a psalm is made** — the method, written for someone who does not know the project, with its drawing — is [`method.md`](method.md), the site's Method page. This file and the others on the Working notes page are for whoever works on it.

## What is here

- `collation/` — `collate.py` diffs the DO pilot Latin against the Clementine Vulgate Project text in `collation/witness/` (public domain, committed so the diff reproduces offline); `diffs.md` is its output, `report.md` the reading of it. Exists because the base text had to be settled before a word was translated. `normalise()` in the script is the orthography fold a lemmatiser will need later.
- [`vos-tu.md`](vos-tu.md) — fifteen pilot verses rendered both ways, made so the address could be heard before it was fixed. Decided: *vós* (2026-09-20); kept as the record of what was weighed.
- `latin.py` — reading the DO psalter: orthography fold, prayed-verse lines, cola, pointing skeleton, and the verse as the app points it. Everything else imports it.
- `lemmas.py` → `lemma-freq.json` — LatinCy lemma counts over Pss 1–150, for the rare-word flag. `lexicon.py` — Lewis & Short lookups from the consult cache.
- `codex.py` — runs one Codex role read-only in a folder and stores the reply verbatim with prompt and target hashes. `checks.py` — the deterministic checks (marks, verse ids, colon length, cadence, rhyme). `render.py` — flat texts for the critics and the praying sheet.
- `words/` — word studies for the verbs and nouns that will be prayed hundreds of times, because those are decided once for the whole psalter. `words.py <name> <regex> --greek … --pt …` generates `<name>.concordance.md` (every occurrence, its place in the verse, the Greek verb behind it from Rahlfs, the pt-PT antecedent's line); `<name>.md` is the study written on top of it. A study covers: counts and forms; the sense (Lewis & Short via `lexicon.py`); **the neighbours it must stay distinct from, especially where they stand in parallel in one verse**; the constraints *vós* puts on its forms; living liturgical usage, looked up on the web and marked verified or not; candidates; test verses to pray; what is not known. First: [`words/exaudire.md`](words/exaudire.md) — recommends *escutar*, reversing Ps 4 draft 2's *atender*.
- `ps004/` — Ps 4 pushed through every step the design doc proposes, each step's output kept apart so its worth can be judged: `dossier.json` (tier 0), `interlinear.md` (1), `literal.json` (2), `prayed.json` (3), `checks.md`, `critic/` (every model verdict, verbatim), `prompts/`, `blind/` (back-translation, run where the Latin is not), `sheet.md` (what gets prayed), `hard-readings.md`.

**Where this is going:** the finished psalter is meant for Divinum Officium, as its Brazilian Portuguese psalter — hence DO's ids, marks and one-verse-per-line shape throughout. DO's present Portuguese (`content/do/horas/Portugues/`) is *not* a witness: parts of it are recent AI-assisted contributions of Gustavo's own, and he says it has many mistakes; it appears in the parallels only as the thing being replaced. **`DECISIONS.md`** is the log of what has been ruled, by whom and why — Gustavo's rulings, and Claude's overnight ones awaiting his review.

### Tooling

- Python is `python3.13`. Scripts that parse Latin need the venv: `python3.13 -m venv research/psalterium/.venv`, then `pip install "spacy>=3.8,<3.9"` and the LatinCy wheel `https://huggingface.co/latincy/la_core_web_lg/resolve/main/la_core_web_lg-3.9.8-py3-none-any.whl` (the unversioned `-any-` wheel name is rejected by current pip).
- `consult/` is gitignored — a cache of things consulted, not redistributed. To rebuild it: `lxx-text_accented.csv` and `lxx-E-verse.csv` from `github.com/eliranwong/LXX-Rahlfs-1935` (`01_wordlist_unicode/` and its `alignment_with_OSSP/`); `bolls-WLC-19-<psalm>.json` from `https://bolls.life/get-text/WLC/19/<psalm>/`; `ls_<LETTER>.json` from `github.com/IohannesArnold/lewis-short-json`; `lagarde-iuxta-hebraeos_djvu.txt` from `archive.org/download/psalteriumiuxta00lagagoog/`. Bolls' "LXX" is lemmas only — useless as a text.
- **The books consulted are kept in `consult/books/`** so that they can be consulted again: `matos-soares-1932-vulgata-vol1.pdf` (Matos Soares' Vulgate translation, 1932; has a text layer — the Psalms are PDF pp. 1015–1300, extracted to `consult/ms1932-salmos.txt` with `pdftotext -f 1015 -l 1300 -layout`), `diurnal-monastico-1962.pdf` (the scan of the *Diurnal Monástico*, Mosteiro de São Bento do Rio de Janeiro, 1962, Latin–Portuguese) and `diurnal-monastico-1962-ocr/` (its OCR, a `markdown.md` and page folders for each half; the two `markdown.md` files are also copied flat as `consult/diurnal-1.md` and `diurnal-2.md`, which is what `diurnal.py` reads). `hetzenauer-1914/` holds the page scans read for the collation (pp. 487, 535, 557–558, 1009, from sacredbible.org/hetzenauer1914) and the two index pages that map verse ranges to pages. `tests/` (tracked) holds the stub-DOM verifiers for `review.html` and the site, and `diurnal-survey.py`. Nothing worth keeping lives in `/tmp`. Both books came from Gustavo's `~/Downloads`. Both are in copyright, so nothing of them leaves `consult/`. **`consult/` exists only in this working tree** — it is gitignored, so deleting the worktree deletes it; copy it out before that.
- `diurnal.py <psalm>` pulls one psalm's Portuguese out of the Diurnal OCR into `consult/diurnal/ps<NNN>.json`, keyed by DO's prayed-verse ids (it recognises the book's Latin column by its words and pairs the Portuguese paragraphs with it). The Diurnal has 78 psalms (the day hours of the monastic office; none of the psalms proper to Matins). Its psalms are Dom Marcos Barbosa's, made "baseada no texto original" (its preface) — from the Hebrew, addressed as *Tu* — so it is a witness for prayed Brazilian diction and cadence, never for sense. It feeds `parallels.py` and the comparison table.
- Bolls files the Douay-Rheims under a chapter count of its own: two stray one-verse chapters (10, a copy of 9:22; 119, "Aleph"), the second half of Ps 9 as chapter 11, and **no chapter at all for Vulgate Ps 10** — its text is in `ps010/drb.txt`, fetched from drbo.org. So Ps 9 is chapters 9 + 11, Vulgate 11–117 are n+1, 118–150 are n+2, and it runs to 152 (`parallels.py: drbChapters`, `drbByHand`). The Ps 10 agent found the Ps 9–10 error on 2026-09-21, before either psalm was translated.
- Codex CLI ≥ 0.155 (older versions cannot run the configured model).

## What does not bend

- Translate the Latin as Latin. LXX and Hebrew explain Jerome; they never correct him.
- It is a text for the mouth: recited daily, chanted to a psalm tone, facing the Latin column. Cola follow the Latin's flex, mediant and final; prayed verses are never merged or split.
- What repeats in the Latin repeats identically in the Portuguese — refrains, formulas, doublet psalms.
- No rendering is final without a human decision, and every rendering can be traced back to how it was made.

## What is already in the repo

Verified against the files, 2026-09-20:

- **The Breviary's Latin psalter is at `content/do/horas/Latin/Psalterium/Psalmorum/`** — a verbatim mirror of Divinum Officium (pinned commit in `content/do/meta.json`; never edit it). `Psalm1.txt`–`Psalm150.txt`, one prayed verse per line, LXX numbering, already pointed: `†` flex, `*` mediant, and `‡` — a second mediant: the app renders DO's `noflexa` default, which moves the asterisk to the `‡` and drops every `†` (13 verses; see the collation report). It is the text Ember's breviary renders, so translating it keeps both columns aligned in the app. The pilot psalms match the Clementine word for word; each psalm is read against its Hetzenauer 1914 page before translation (all of Sunday Compline done: Pss 4, 90, 133, Lc 2:29–32).
- Orthography is liturgical: stress accents, `j`, `æ`/`œ`. Useful for cadence; normalise in memory before any lemmatiser sees it.
- **Verse division is the Breviary's, not the Bible's.** 194 verse lines in Pss 1–150 carry a letter suffix (`4:2a` / `4:2b`, `17:36b`) where a biblical verse is split into two prayed verses, and 74 inline markers (`(20)`, `(26a)`) in 37 files mark where a prayed verse runs across a biblical boundary. The prayed verse is the unit.
- **No tituli and no *diapsalma*** — `Psalm4.txt` opens at `4:2a`. The Clementine witness in `collation/witness/` has every titulus, so translating them is cheap; but DO's Breviary text does not carry them, so the Latin column will never show them.
- Canticles are `Psalm210.txt`–`Psalm273.txt` in the same folder (`Psalm233.txt` = Nunc dimittis). The invitatory form of Ps 94 (long verses with antiphon slots) is `Psalterium/Invitatorium.txt`; `Psalm94.txt` is the Gallican wording but not a plain Ps 94 — it is the Epiphany Matins form, missing 94:6–7a because the antiphon sung in its `$ant` slots is that verse. The nine places where the invitatory's wording parts from the Gallican are tabled in the collation report. Antiphons and hour assignments are in `Psalterium/Psalmi/*.txt`.
- **Pilot psalms per that data:** Sunday Prime `117, 118(1-16), 118(17-32)` (the `[Tridentinum]` scheme prefixes `53`); Sunday Compline `4, 90, 133`.
- *Quoniam in saeculum misericordia eius* is the refrain of Pss 105, 106, 117. Pss 135 and 99 read *in aeternum* — a different formula; whether they share a rendering is a choice to make on purpose.
- A Portuguese antecedent already aligned to the same cola sits at `content/do/horas/Portugues/Psalterium/Psalmorum/` — European Portuguese (*protecção*), so a reference and a contrast, not a base.

## Open questions

- Tituli: in or out of the MVP. A source exists now; the question is only whether an unprayed line belongs in a prayed text.
- What `‡` shows as upstream with flexes switched on, and what `=`, `^`, `_` mean in `Invitatorium.txt`.
- **Latin against the ear.** On Ps 4 the Latinist critic and the judges passed the draft; the stylist called 8 of 10 verses "not native", and most of its fixes leave the Latin (a supplied verb, the Hebrew's sense, a lost repetition). The rule "translate the Latin as Latin" and the priority "it works in the mouth" pull apart here, and only a praying human can say where the line is.
- Whether a phrase like *in idípsum* (six places) must have one rendering everywhere, as refrains do.
- The dossier quotes Rahlfs' LXX from a CCAT-derived repository. Fine for working files; before anything is published as CC0, either confirm that is clean or switch the Greek to Swete.
- A better *iuxta Hebraeos* source than the Lagarde OCR, which needs hand repair verse by verse.

Closed: **God is addressed as *vós*** throughout the corpus (Gustavo, 2026-09-20) — the register Brazilian Catholics already pray in, and it keeps God and a singular human addressee apart where a psalm switches between them (Ps 90:9). Its known costs are to be managed, not reopened: under *vós* the imperative of an *-ir* verb is also the first-person past (*ouvi*), so such verbs are avoided at imperatives (*exaudire* → *atender*); *vosso* is a syllable longer than *teu*; God and a plural crowd share a form (Ps 4). The nearest antecedents (Matos Soares 1932, the CNBB Bible) use *tu*, so *vós* wording cannot simply be borrowed from them.

Closed: the DO mirror is the working text, verbatim; Hetzenauer 1914 (page scans at sacredbible.org) is the printed authority it is read against, psalm by psalm (2026-09-20).
- Who the Latinist reviewer is; whether the translation table is published for public comment.
